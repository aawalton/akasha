Chapter 50Memories of a Wisper – Part Three

By 2044, the global situation was like a pressure cooker about to explode. International conflicts had intensified to the brink of chaos and the hidden world was between a very thin line.

Many countries, fearful of the implications of organizations operating in the shadows of their affairs, decided to sever ties with Wishper. The reason behind was clear thanks to intelligence agencies: powerful companies and criminal groups, notorious for their illicit activities in both worlds, began offering valuable resources to governments in exchange for turning a blind eye to their actions and dismantling the organizations tasked with stopping them. Many countries had small research groups focused on the occult and Wishper became one of those targets.

Over the years, the organization had operated in the shadows, working from within to combat organized crime and the trafficking of magical creatures and items. However, by 2044, the network of corruption and manipulation woven by the world’s great powers had become too strong to confront from the shadows. Influence trafficking had infiltrated even the highest levels of governments, and the forces that once secured a line between the human world and the hidden world, were now allying with those destabilizing the world amid the changing global climate.

The shift was sudden and violent, but not really a surprise.

Wishper found itself the subject of international persecution, and missions that had previously been carried out with caution were now surrounded by mistrust and sabotage. Within weeks and months, several countries ceased providing support to the organization. Governments that had once been allies began closing doors, and international operations grew increasingly complex, if not impossible.

Mai, as part of one the teams, became involved in an investigation that would expose the magnitude of corruption infiltrating the spheres of power.

They discovered that the criminal groups they had been fighting were not only independent and wealthy, with abundant resources, but many were heavily tied to powerful multinational corporations. It was a complex facade: companies controlling key sectors of the global economy used criminals as a smokescreen while operating without restrictions themselves.

The corporations not only backed the criminals, but placed them on the front lines, empowering them to act while keeping their own hands clean and the laws intact. The companies used criminal gangs as their own version of “mercenaries,” orchestrating a cold war in which the layers of world—the human, the criminal and the hidden—were pieces moving like in a chess game.

And Wishper’s team delved deeper and discovered that these multinationals were not merely involved in money laundering, or arms trafficking, their network of influence extended far far further. Their interests ranged from manipulating natural resource, markets, to controlling governments, the media and social networks, ensuring that their operations were overlooked whenever possible. All of this was done in the name of profit and power expansion, while, naturally, the welfare of the people was relegated to second place. Although it was not a surprise, really.

No matter the years, the world was always the same poem. Different people, Same shit, different smell.

Mai, who had always firmly believed she was fighting for a purpose greater than herself, began to question everything she had taken for granted. The missions they had carried out in the past, the battles fought, now had a darker tint not related to creatures, cults or battles against street thugs.

The true enemies again, were not hidden in the shadows of criminality. They sat in the highest echelons of power, disguised as benefactors, successful entrepreneurs, respected figures. They were the ones really pulling the strings behind everything. And among them, even some old enemies that disappeared with Octogonus in the past.

It was a sad paradox: the magical creatures she had fought to protect were also being used as pieces on a global chessboard, manipulated by those who sought only power and control.

As investigations progressed, Wishper’s team began to face external pressures. Some governments, already indebted to the large corporations, started retaliating, and what had once been collaboration turned into a witch hunt—especially in southern China, India, and Russian territory. The situation in South America and the political instability in the Northern Kingdom were even worse. The organization found itself caught between a rock and a hard place, struggling to survive while multinationals harassed them from every front.

The fight had to continue, but now with a goal far greater than before. It was no longer just about eradicating crime. It was about dismantling a corrupt system that had infiltrated the veins of humanity and the hidden world.

The 2044 missions would remain etched in her memory as the turning point of her life and of Wishper’s history. From that moment on, the organization ceased to be merely a clandestine force fighting for good. It became an entity dedicated to exposing and destroying corruption on a large scale, even if it meant putting everything they had achieved at risk.

One year later, Wishper launched a counterattack. It was a global operation, coordinated with several organizations from the hidden world, which would come to be known as the Hydra Mission. During this period, over forty global corporations were simultaneously attacked by trained and specialized teams. The operation was a strategic strike that dismantled the core of the corporations that had been manipulating the world from the shadows.

The boards of the organizations involved were eliminated in a single strike. Wishper teams and allied groups destroyed the criminal networks that maintained direct contact with corrupt governments, seizing critical information that exposed the full extent of the dirty games everyone had been playing. Amid all the tragedy, the news outlets were given a “free lunch” as emails began arriving en masse over an extended period. Photographs, summaries of off-the-books funds used by certain government officials, deals with dictators in the south. Secret commercial agreements. All kinds of clandestine schemes carried out in secrecy.

Wishper and the involved groups, with the help of R&R and ZAIEN, provided some minor assistance. It was vital to implement a complete false network that made it appear no one from the hidden world was involved, only a group previously unknown. That was Hydra.

In Mai’s case, the mission assigned to her was one of the most delicate and dangerous: the assassination of a government leader and his entire entourage. She had been assigned this task because of her previous record, particularly what had occurred in former North Korea.

The mission was carried out after weeks of waiting for the right opportunity, and it finally came during a government meeting in Saint Petersburg. A team of only seven wispers, disguised as humans and equipped with slightly outdated tactical gear, infiltrated the country house where members of the government and a group of technocrats interested in installing a climate control facility in Russian territory had gathered.

And that was when everything unfolded.

Infiltration, cutting communications, eliminating guards and designated targets.

The building was silent, but it was not a natural silence; only five minutes earlier the entire place had been calm, until gunfire began to replace the sound of crickets. The other wispers remained behind, securing the perimeter, while Mai took care of the final part.

Mai moved forward without a sound, crossing the corridors like a shadow that did not belong there, leaving behind bodies that no longer had history or names, eliminated with the same precision with which errors are erased from a report that must never be seen.

At the end of the last corridor, a single door remained closed.

There was no urgency in her movements, there never was, because she knew that time, in situations like this, was no longer a factor that could alter the outcome. After all they knew beforehand how many guards were there and, judging by the ones they had already taken out, she already knew how many had been left.

When she opened the door, she found him there, surrounded by five bodyguards in a wide room filled with screens and a circular table.

Mai moved like a cat through the scene, and the five shots she fired did all the work.

Only the president remained. A tall man, though rather thin, with bony hands. He had come to power barely three years earlier, propelled by media campaigns and the vast funding of corporations interested in Siberia’s resources. Several international accusations had linked him to prostitution networks decades before, but those who had spoken out had disappeared when the matter threatened to reach public attention. Since then, a new reputation had been carefully constructed, driven by a narrative tailored for both the Russian public and the international press.

But that had come to an end.

The man did not attempt to flee, nor seek shelter, nor even pretend surprise, he simply stood beside a table, a screen glowing behind him where numbers, projections, and patterns continuously rearranged themselves, as if the entire world could be reduced to variables someone, somewhere, had decided to optimize. He did not even lower his gaze to look at his subordinates now lying on the floor.

“So it’s true,” he said at last, his eyes fixed on Mai, his expression marked by clear displeasure. “This is what they sent to kill me. A fucking garden gnome?”

Mai did not respond to the insult.

The man let out a faint exhale that was not quite resignation, but something more closer to a lucid acceptance, almost weary.

“Do you know what happens after this?” he continued, turning slowly. “You can eliminate me, erase my name, even make everything I built disappear… but someone else will take my place. They always do. And everything goes on the same.” He paused briefly. “The system doesn’t stop. It never has.”

Mai stepped forward.

“I know,” she replied quietly.

And it was true.

That was the problem.

And even so, she did not hesitate.

The dry sound that followed was brief, almost insignificant compared to the weight of what it represented.

Mai remained still for a second longer than necessary, watching as the screen behind him continued to function, adjusting numbers, recalculating scenarios, adapting without interruption, as if his death had no real impact on the flow of what he had helped build.

As if he had never existed at all.

Finally, she turned and left the room without looking back.

She knew the system would continue.

It always did.

Hydra’s heads would not fall cleanly, and many others would be dragged down in the process, but at that scale, it remained a lesser evil compared to allowing men like him to continue shaping the world unchecked.

In less than six minutes, the Wishper team left the place aboard stealth craft, while the final explosions illuminated the horizon, marking the end of an operation that, in theory, was meant to change everything, or at least… something.

The Hydra Mission was the strongest strike carried out in recent history, both in the human world and in the hidden world. For humanity, the attack was presented as an act of extremist radicalism perpetrated by shadowy groups aiming to destabilize global order. However, the truth was very different. For many countries, the strike was a relief. They finally regained access to their natural resources, which had been controlled by multinationals and large corporations for decades. For some, it was like a second chance to rebuild what the global elites had stripped away.

The world was in chaos, yes, but for certain governments and citizens, that chaos represented a form of liberation. The balance of power was beginning to shift, albeit in a violent and dangerous way. While media were divided and public opinions in various countries described the attacks as a series of unjustified terrorist acts—depending on whether their preferred political party was affected—the true motivations behind the operation remained hidden in the shadows, with the corporations still trying to regain their lost control.

Mai, for her part, had been part of that wave of destruction, and although she carried out her mission with her usual efficiency, a feeling of emptiness began to take hold. It was not the kind of emptiness she felt when alone, it was not a void that could be filled with new objectives or missions. It was the emptiness that comes when, after years of struggle, one realizes they are simply tired.

In March 2046, Shanghai, Mai took a deep breath.

She was tired.

After more than thirty years of service, decades of missions in which the lives of others—both humans and feys—had been in her hands, she had finally made a decision.

It was time to move. She felt it deep down inside, even though she hadn't wanted to do it before so as not to leave Whisper in the lurch when they still needed her.

It was fortunate that three of her friends had just arrived in the city.

That same night, Shanghai did not feel like a battlefield.

There were no orders waiting, no encrypted messages, no distant explosions echoing somewhere across the world. Only the constant murmur of the city, neon lights reflecting on the damp pavement, and a small bar tucked between two narrow streets that no one would remember twice.

Mai sat by the window.

She had not changed much—the same posture, the same calm presence—but without her gear, without the weight of a mission pressing on her shoulders, there was something about her that felt… distant. As if she had taken half a step out of the world and had not fully returned yet.

“You’re late.”

Miria dropped into the seat across from her without asking, with her usual lack of ceremony, placing a glass of whisky on the table with a soft thud before even properly looking at her.

“And you look terrible,” she added, narrowing her eyes.

Mai exhaled softly, but smiled faintly.

“I’ve been worse.”

“Yeah, but usually there’s blood involved,” Miria replied without missing a beat.

A third voice joined before Mai could respond.

“Should I be worried that this is your idea of casual conversation?”

Freya slid into the seat beside them, her tone softer, though her gaze lingered on Mai a second longer than necessary. Nerea arrived right after, carrying drinks for all of them.

For a moment, none of them spoke.

It wasn’t uncomfortable. Just… strange. For weeks, the four of them had been apart, but now they had all ended up in the same place, as agreed after finishing their respective missions.

Then Miria leaned slightly forward, resting her chin on her hand.

“So,” she said, “you call us in the middle of the night after disappearing for weeks.”

“It wasn’t like that.”

Miria blinked.

“…That’s not new,” Nerea added, drinking half her beer in one go.

Freya tilted her head slightly.

“Then what is it?”

Mai’s gaze drifted for a moment toward the window, toward the city that kept moving as if nothing had changed, as if nothing ever did. Then she looked back at them, bringing her hands together, idly playing with her fingers.

Then she spoke.

“I’m leaving.”

Silence.

Nerea was the first to react, her hands tightening slightly around her mug.

“Leaving… as in a reassignment?” she asked carefully.

Mai shook her head once.

“No.”

Miria watched her for a few seconds, then leaned back in her seat.

“Hm.”

Freya, on the other hand, did not look away.

“For how long?” she asked.

Mai considered the question, as if the answer was not something she had fully decided yet.

“Forever. Maybe I can cooperate with things sometimes, but I think it’s time to move on. I’ve spent a long time in Wishper, but I think it’s time to go back to Tokyo.”

That landed differently.

Nerea lowered her gaze.

Miria let out a slow breath through her nose and smiled.

“…About time.”

Mai looked at her, slightly surprised.

Miria shrugged.

“What? You thought no one noticed?” she said, tapping the table lightly with a finger. “It’s been a while since you looked at missions the way you used to.”

Freya nodded silently.

“You were still there,” she added, “but not completely.”

Mai didn’t deny it. Because there was nothing to deny.

Nerea hesitated for a moment before speaking, her voice lower.

“And now?”

Mai looked at her hands briefly, as if searching for something in them that wasn’t blood, or weight, or memories.

“I don’t know,” she admitted. “Maybe I should go back to what I used to do. Investigating cases like I did decades ago. But first, I need to sort things out.”

“That’s actually terrifying.”

Freya let out a small smile. “It’s normal.”

Nerea gently pushed one of the mugs toward Mai. “Then… I think that’s a good place to start, Mothy.”

Mai wet her lips with the foam. It was actually delicious.

Miria raised her glass.

“Well,” she said, “to Mai, who finally decided to behave like a normal person.”

Freya followed. “To whatever comes next.”

Nerea hesitated only a second before joining.

“To rest.”

Mai looked at them.

At the people who had stood by her through decades of war, loss, and silence.

Miria didn’t lower her glass after the toast.

She stared at it for a second longer, slowly turning it between her fingers.

“Still…” she murmured, “you’re not the only one.”

Mai raised her gaze slightly. “What do you mean?”

Nerea rested her elbow on the table, more relaxed now.

“We’ve been thinking the same thing,” Nerea said.

“Eh?” Mai looked at her in surprise, but Freya nodded too.

“I mean… this isn’t the same anymore,” Miria said, bluntly. “After Hydra…I don’t know if I want to keep going at the same pace.”

Freya didn’t speak immediately, but nodded.

“I’ve been thinking about it too,” she added. “Not retiring completely, but… slowing down. Choosing more carefully what to do and what not to.”

Miria let out a short laugh.

“‘Choosing more carefully,’ huh,” she said, glancing at her. “Sounds nice, but we all know how this works.”

“I know,” Freya replied calmly. “That’s exactly why I’m saying it.”

Mai looked them in silence.

“Since when?” she asked.

Nerea shrugged.

“For years, probably,” she said, taking another drink. “It’s just that now it can’t be ignored anymore. I couldn’t step away when things were this bad. But now, after the last operations… it might be a good time.”

Miria finally set her glass down.

“I’m not staying in this for another forty years,” she said flatly. “I’ve done enough.”

“You’ve done more than enough,” Nerea added with a half-smile.

“That too,” Miria replied, puffing her chest slightly.

Freya looked at Mai.

“You…” she said, “you were just the first to say it out loud.”

A small silence followed.

“So,” Mai said, “what are you going to do?”

Nerea shrugged again and brought a hand to her chin as if seriously thinking. “Rest for a while. See what’s left when there are no missions. Travel the world. Maybe I should just have sex nonstop for the next ten years.”

“Ew... That sounds disgusting,” Freya replied, and Mai choked back a laugh as she took a sip from her mug.

“Jealous?” Nerea flashed a double peace sign.

“You don’t need sex, you need vitamin D.”

“You’re really just handing me these jokes, aren’t you?” Nerea said with a smug.

“What do you mean?”

“Vitamin D is just another way of saying I need Vitamin Dick.”

“I’m going to hit you, you damn pervert,” Freya said, pouting.

Mai almost choked on her beer.

Miria crossed her arms and looked up at the ceiling.

“Travel,” she said. “Sleep. Eat without someone trying to kill me every two days. Sounds good. Whatever happens in the world… maybe I’ll just go around for a while.”

Freya smiled faintly.

“Think,” she added. “I’m not sure what I’ll do yet, but I really want to rest.”

Mai nodded slightly.

“Sounds… reasonable.”

Miria looked at her with a raised eyebrow.

“Reasonable?” she scoffed. “That’s the bare minimum.”

Nerea raised her glass again.

“Then let’s toast again,” she said. “But this time, for real.”

Freya followed.

Miria sighed, but raised hers anyway.

Mai did the same.

“To staying alive,” Nerea said.

“To not having to prove anything,” Freya added.

“To not coming back tomorrow if we don’t want to,” Miria finished.

Mai held her glass for a second before speaking.

“To choosing.”

The glasses clinked again.

This time, the sound was a little firmer.

“We’ll have to go to the mountains to talk to Jinat and the others,” Nerea said.

The four of them sighed, tired.

And then, in a low voice, Mai added:

“To something different.”

***

After some weeks, with a mixture of relief and sorrow, Mai and the other three conveyed their decision to the Wishper team when they travel to the mountains. Although her departure marked the end of an era for her, she knew it was time to leave behind the constant struggle and seek something more in her life—something beyond missions, death, and secrets.

Her decision was not easy. She knew that leaving Wishper meant giving up everything that had been her life for three decades. The team had been her family, the only constant in a world that was falling apart around her. But the weight of the years, the endless suffering, the losses, and the coldness of war had left their mark in a way she could no longer ignore. She needed a respite, a break, a time to heal.

As she made her choice, she thought of the comrades she had fought alongside. Not only Freya, Nerea, Miria who had always been her closest friend, but of all the others who, in some way, had been left behind. Many of them had never had the chance to choose, to decide when to retire.

Her hair, now completely silver, was a testament to her years of war and sacrifice. Yet, at that very moment, she knew that even though her life as a fighter was ending, there was still more life left for her to live.

After the reunion with Jinat and the others, they left China and the friends said their “See you later” in Hawaii, and Mai continued her journey toward Tokyo to reunite with her friends before eventually returning home.

And so she did.

Until exhaustion arrived a few months later, even when she was far removed from the conflicts.
