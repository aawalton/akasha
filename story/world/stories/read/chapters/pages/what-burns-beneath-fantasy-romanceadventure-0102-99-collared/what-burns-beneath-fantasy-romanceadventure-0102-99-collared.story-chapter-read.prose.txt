The babble of music and otherworldly voices fell to a fuzzy hum as I stepped back, eyeing Velidan warily.

He didn’t seem surprised or bothered by my sudden retreat. If anything, he just looked amused.

“This item we’re here for,” I said slowly, bracing one palm against the wall. “You were going to use it on me before? After I brought you the shard?”

“Of course.”

My pulse jumped. “Why?”

The Fae’s mouth curled in a wicked smile. “Well, I assumed you’d resist the idea of making a new deal. Enchanted slumber seemed kinder than ripping your tongue out.” He actually chuckled at that, and nausea flooded my stomach at the sheer flippancy of his tone.

Ripping my tongue out. Or worse. Or everything else he threatened me with. Saints, or just murdering me. I took another step back. Every nerve screamed for me to run, but of course there was no escaping him. “How long would it have-”

“Until I saw fit to end it. Sleeping spells are hardly effective if the victim can just wake themselves up.” That awful, endless gaze made my skin crawl. He spent a moment drinking in my expression, then scoffed. “Don’t look so frightened, Brin. If I wanted to keep you here indefinitely I’d have done it by now. I need you to keep the relic close and I want you to warn the other humans.” He paused, letting the words sink in. “You’ll wake tomorrow, as promised.”

The reasoning made sense, but my heart wouldn’t stop pounding. I searched the ground, blood roaring through my ears, for what seemed a very long time. Lavender smoke framed each blue-green blade of grass in a dreamy glow.

I finally managed to find my voice. “What… happens?” He looked puzzled and I closed my eyes, sucking in a steadying breath. “Right now, I mean. If I change my mind. If I… decide I don’t want to go with you after all. What happens right now?”

For several breaths, the only sounds were nearby chatter and the thrumming of my own heart. Then Velidan simply shrugged. “We’ll go peruse the market until you wake.”

I squinted, waiting for more. Searched for the double meaning, the hidden threat, and came up empty. “That’s it?”

He shrugged again. “I doubt we’ll have time for much discussion of runes or magic, but perhaps you’ll find something worth purchasing.”

I looked towards the market, brows knotted in confused suspicion. “But why?”

“I’m told that mortal women enjoy shopping.”

Again, I waited for more. Narrowed my eyes between him and the throng of Fae, gnawing my lower lip. “No, I meant… you’re not going to drag me in there?” I slashed one hand towards the bizarre door and whatever lay beyond.

“Don’t be ridiculous, Brin. We’ve had an enjoyable evening, thus far.” The Fae drew closer, voice as smooth as silk, and I felt every word when he added, “I intend for it to stay that way.”

Right. If I start being defiant, he’s going to… calmly take me shopping in a magical marketplace. Because we’re still on ‘charming.’ The giggling wisps flew past again. I watched their path until a few creatures that resembled short, hairy men further down the wall struck up an off-key song. One of them started to dance, earning a chorus of laughter from his fellows. And saints help me if I’m not a little charmed by this place. But I’m also not an idiot. I swallowed. “Enjoyable. Sure. But then tomorrow night-”

“Will be much less pleasant for you.” He flashed a quick, sharp smile. “It’s your choice.”

There it is. I bristled, wanting to shout in the face of the oh-so-smug threat, but… Saints, at least he’s being honest about it all. Or at least I think he is. He really is letting this be my choice.

My gaze trailed between the Fae and the open door. So I’m going to be trapped here by an indefinite sleeping curse that he’ll be in control over. Ashes. And my mom… wanted this. Right? She must have. Me taking the shard, Velidan being one of my runes, Faer… it all led to here. I’m supposed to be here. I don’t have to trust him, but I can sure as salt trust her. I lifted my chin and marched past Velidan, firmly ignoring the pleased expression on his face, and straight through the doorway.

What lay beyond was a jarring space lit by floating globes of orange light. Damp-looking roots and stone stretched through a long, narrow, and dizzyingly tall interior. The walls had been hollowed and grooved a hundred times over. Each hollow held clay jars, pouches made from moldy-smelling animal hide, bundles of dried plants, animal bones, books, and small iron boxes. Dust floated through the air.

Velidan stepped in behind me, closing the door as I turned in another slow circle, head tilted back, to see that the cramped collection stretched upwards through a labyrinth of winding roots. On and on, with no ladder or stairs to reach any of it, which begged the question-

“Lordling.” The voice was a scratchy wheeze, like brittle leaves scraping against rock. It came from above us.

My gaze latched onto a vaguely humanoid figure perched high amidst the warped roots. She leaned down, emerging more fully into the dim light, and it was all I could do to not turn and rush right back out the door.

Something that resembled a vulture combined with an ancient, naked woman peered down from the twisted canopy. Her face was mostly human, with deeply wrinkled skin and a matted tangle of white hair. Her beak-like nose sloped down at a severe angle and her ears sloped up, creating a sharp cast to the profile. One round black eye gleamed, while the other was a barren mass of scarred skin.

She stood. Two dark wings stretched out from a humped back and both legs shifted atop her perch. Her feet and hands were gnarled claws, like those of a hawk. Her legs- and most of her unclothed body- were bone-thin and sleek with feathers.

“Adara,” Velidan rumbled from just behind me.

The Fae dropped with a flap of her wings and a little trilling noise. She landed in front of me, bringing a puff of dust and the smell of damp earth. “Trrrrl! You’ve brought a human. Your little problem, I assume?”

“Problem?” I gasped, cringing back. The Fae’s good eye blinked as she stepped close, brushing against me.

“Don’t take offense, morsel.” She clucked her tongue.

“Mind your distance, harpy,” the deep voice behind me was flat. “She gets very loud when she’s frightened.”

Nervous heat flooded my face and my back hit the stone wall. The harpy cackled. It was a grating sound, partially muffled by the earth and stone around us.

“And, yes, this is her. My request?”

“Mm. Patience, lordling. Let this old crone sate some curiosity.” The Fae’s head cocked in a distinctly birdlike manner as she craned down, examining every inch of me. “You said she comes in slumber? She’s dreaming now?”

He folded his arms. “Yes.”

“Remarkable. Only half here, yet I feel her, smell her, hear her heartbeat… what a little mystery you’ve found, Velidan.”

“I’m a mystery?” I looked between the two of them.

“In more ways than one,” he answered, still in that flat tone. “She’s physically here, yet her body lies in the mortal realm. You're confident the trinket will work despite the… complication?”

Dark feathers along her narrow shoulders and the slope of her chest ruffled. “Lordling, my magic always works.”

“Good. Then-”

“Is there a door when she comes or goes?”

He looked momentarily annoyed at the interruption. “No. Just a…” he frowned, as if searching for the correct word, before settling on, “distortion.”

The harpy’s head tilted and her beady black eye rolled, examining me once more. “Physical? Temporal?”

Veldian’s frown deepened. “Both. She’s never here for long.”

“I’ve wondered about that,” I said meekly, feeling quite nervous beneath Adara’s scrutiny. My breath caught as I spread both hands wide. “I… even when I sleep all night, I’m not here for the same amount of time. And sometimes I just faint, but then it feels like I’m here for… well, longer.”

“Hm. The twisting of time is a rare thing. More likely it’s a twisting of perception. Curious, indeed. Perhaps it’s you, lordling, and not to do with her. Have you ever bound another human?”

Velidan shook his head.

“A mystery, then. The spell should keep her more grounded.” Adara shuffled forwards, feathers rasping and claws scraping, and it was all I could do not to whimper as she inhaled close to my neck. “Oh. Oh. There’s a scent on her.”

He went very still. “A scent?”

“Like distant smoke. It clings to her skin.” She hissed, then fixed him with a hard stare. “Will my spell incur vengeance? Retaliation? I am weaker than you, lordling. Vulnerable.”

“Less vulnerable than you’d have me believe, I think.” She cackled at that, and he spent a moment in thought before answering smoothly, “You’re smelling a relic. The echo of a dead fool, incapable of seeking vengeance. And,” he added dryly, sparing me a glance, “he seems to be very confused.”

My brows puckered at that. He’s talking about the shard. He must be. And he thinks it’s… confused?

The harpy’s claws twitched. She sniffed me again, then stepped away and croaked in a slow, wary voice, “That scent is a warning, Velidan. I know death when it stands before me.”

I didn’t have a clue what that meant, but I didn’t ponder it. I didn’t even hear Velidan’s response. My mind was echoing the word confused over and over.

Confused. He thinks the shard is confused. But for something to be confused, it has to… I mean, a thing can’t be confused! It would have to be able to… to think. It would have to have a mind, be sentient, it… it…

A numbing sense of clarity fell alongside a flood of memories. The metal’s flickers of heat and light when I spoke to it. The dream of sunlight and safety when I’d woken with it in hand. The indescribable sense that, despite having given it to Velidan, it had wanted to stay with me.

It’s alive. Somehow, perhaps only just a little bit, but… saints, it is. Of course it is! It’s a living thing!

I stared at nothing, wide-eyed and open-mouthed, until their conversation snapped me back to the present.

“-bargain was struck several days ago.” Velidan’s voice was low and even. “Renege and I’ll tear your other eye out.”

The blood drained from my face. I whirled in horror, unsure of which frightened me more; the threat itself or the utter calm with which it was delivered. “No! You said violence isn’t allowed here!”

He ignored my cry, and I half-considered throwing myself in front of the ancient Fae before Velidan decided to act on the threat. Perhaps I could slow him down enough for her to fly away, and surely he couldn’t follow her up into the canopy.

Adara gave a little hiss. Both wings stretched from her back, rising like twin shadows. “A threat, lordling? Trrrl! Your ancestors would have offered to bring me the heads of my enemies or given a taste of their own flesh, first!”

“I like my flesh, and I’m short on time,” he answered coldly. They regarded each other for several heartbeats, then he flashed a wicked smile. “But perhaps I can kill something for you later.”

She laughed at that. The sound curled like dry leaves and smoke, making the air around me tremble. “That’s better. Your generation struggles with respect.” She clucked, as if admonishing a child, then flapped up to one of the hanging roots. When she returned, she clutched a small iron box. One talon scraped a painful keen along the metal. “Only an echo, lordling? Incapable of wrath?” She searched his face, wings stretching up. The motion obscured each orange globe behind her, making her feathers glow as if silhouetted by embers. “Vow it. Swear on your life, and know I’ll curse you for a lie.”

“Is that a threat, harpy? A demand? Careful. I’ve killed for less.”

She hissed at him.

“Fine. On my life.” He sneered down, seemingly unimpressed by her display, then opened the box. A thin silver necklace with a dangling red jewel lay within. “My specifications?”

“Your blood is lock and key. The moon herself would be hard-pressed to snap that cord. But once snapped, it won’t mend.”

The finality of her claim hit me and a little whimper rose in the back of my throat. I sagged against the wall. That’s it, then. A piece of fancy jewelry is going to keep me trapped here until he decides to take it off. Saints, what if I’m making a huge mistake?

Adara turned, eyeing me with obvious amusement. “Upset, mortal? Were you hoping for an escape? Trrrrl! My curses don’t break.”

Velidan turned, too, and spent a long moment looking at me. A crease formed between his brows. “Could you fashion another?”

“What? Why?” The room seemed to grow smaller, narrowing behind him as fresh horror stiffened my spine. “Why would you need another?”

“I’d need more payment,” the harpy said slowly, ignoring me. One talon tapped against her sharp teeth. “I have enough of your blood left over to fashion a new jewel. It’s a draining spell, lordling. But, yes, in time I could fashion another curse.”

I stepped forwards. “Why?”

Velidan just tilted his head, features lit by the dull glow as he watched me, seemingly lost in thought. Motes of dust swirled through the air between us.

“Please don’t.” My gaze found Adara’s and my voice hitched. “Please, don’t make him any-”

“Why, I haven’t heard a mortal plead in ages, lording.” The harpy cackled. Every feather shivered at the shake of her stick-thin shoulders. “How delightful! Perhaps she’ll weep, too.”

My blood ran cold. I clasped a hand to my throat, turning the helpless look towards Velidan. “There’s no reason-

“Make another.”

The very air seemed to thicken, pressing in on me with suffocating weight. I cried out in dismay, earning a shriek of laughter from Adara, then turned and ran out the door.

Endless stars, balmy night air, and the chaotic din of voices greeted me. I sagged against the stone wall, palms pressed against my eyelids, fighting to keep my pulse steady.

“Stop panicking, Brin.”

I ground my teeth together, leveling a glare up at the Fae’s impassive face. “Telling someone to stop panicking doesn’t usually work.”

He snorted. The necklace hung from one hand, its jewel shining like a fresh drop of blood. It looked… cold. Heavy.

“Why do you want another one?”

Broad shoulders lifted in a shrug. “I have no diabolical plans at present. I just like to be prepared for things.”

“Prepared to keep me trapped here.” Every word seethed with bitterness.

“Or prepared in case we need to do this again,” he answered smoothly, unfazed by my anger. “Or in case you want to stay longer, some night.”

“That won’t happen.”

He just shrugged again. “Perhaps not, but in either case, I won’t have to jump through the realm and commission Adara’s services again. I’ll simply be ready.”

Right. Logical, reasonable answers. Because if he did ever intend to use it against my will, he wouldn’t just tell me. And, ashes, it’s not as though I could stop him.

As if reading my mind, he continued, “Mortal, this changes nothing. Our plan for the immediate future is the same, and as for further on… well, either I’ll collar you with your consent and you have nothing to worry about, or it will be against your will and there’s nothing you can do to stop me.” He rumbled an easy laugh, as if that chilling logic dismissed the entire matter. “Either way, there’s no point in worrying.”

I glared at him.

“Or perhaps I’ll never even use the other one, Brin. But right now, I request you focus on the task at hand.”

My gaze fell to the necklace. I spent a moment steadying myself against the ancient stone, breathing in fragrant air and collecting my wits. He’s right. I think I hate him even more for it, but he does make a good point. There’s no point in worrying about the future right now.

I heaved a sigh, tangling both hands through my hair. “Right. I… I suppose I can panic later.”

He flashed a wry grin. “That’s the spirit, mortal.”

The necklace’s red gem shone. It was beautiful, far more beautiful than any ornament I’d ever worn before, and the very sight of it made me shiver. “You’ll… you’ll end it. You’ll take it off tomorrow.” The whisper came out barely audible over the nearby din, and I wasn’t sure if the words were simply for my own sake or an attempt to coax more reassurance from Velidan.

“Of course.”

I met his eyes. “May I have your word?”

The Fae looked surprised at that, and even huffed another little laugh. “My word? Does my word mean anything to you, Brin? I’m shocked.”

I bit my lower lip, searching his face for anything besides amusement and finding nothing. I considered the obvious malice he’d shown just a few moments ago, when Adara had made a similar request, and my heart sank. “I suppose not. But… I don’t know. I’d like to think it means something to you.”

The amusement faded at that, but I couldn’t quite tell what took its place. He spent several heartbeats gazing at me. When he finally answered, the mocking tone lowered to a more solemn pitch. “I’ll let you wake up tomorrow. You have my word.”

I let the vow hang for a moment, wondering if it held any actual weight, then reached for the silver cord.

And was spun around. I scarcely had time to gasp before I heard the hiss of a black knife being drawn and the wet slide of metal against flesh. Cold silver settled around my throat. It grew tight for a moment as his fist closed over the back, apparently sealing the two ends together with sheer force and magic rather than any kind of physical clasp. Heat blossomed along the nape of my neck. I wasn’t sure if it was from his touch or from the freshly drawn blood and instinctively tried to pull away.

Then the necklace’s weight settled as he let go. The world turned oddly fuzzy as a before-unrealized pressure deep within my bones seemed to lessen. I blinked at the curling smoke and sparkling stars as they blurred, then refocused with vivid clarity.

I turned, very aware of the red jewel pressed between my collarbone. It wasn’t particularly heavy, but it felt… wrong. It was too decadent, too exquisite, and too… final. Warm night air brushed against my skin, but I shivered nonetheless.

Silver flickered along his palm. Glittering eyes fell to my throat.

I winced, clasping a hand up as if to hide the jewelry. My eyes fell to the ground, then searched his face warily. “Well. What… h-happens now?”

Velidan gave me a slow smile, then turned sideways and swept one hand towards the throng of Fae.

Excited, nervous heat crept along my skin. So this part was true, then. I’ve agreed, I’m trapped, and… and we’re still going to go explore a magical marketplace. We’re actually going shopping in the Night Court.

The scene behind him was mysterious, mesmerizing, and… apparently, mine, in a small way. I was free to wander through this bizarre, enchanting space at my leisure.

A lovely cage, indeed.

I let the giddy thrill of anticipation carry me forward even as something in the back of my mind screamed out that, if I was indeed in a cage, he’d just locked the door.
