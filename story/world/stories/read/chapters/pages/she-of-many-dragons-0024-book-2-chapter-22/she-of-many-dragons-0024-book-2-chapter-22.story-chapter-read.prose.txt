Preparation for Numi's plan took three days. The first step was getting Alice outside. Prim could effectively hide Alice from the comings and goings of the Protectorate Guild, but it would be much easier if they did not have to go through the main lobby at all and risk discovery. Nobody knew what kinds of possible illusion-breaking effects the guild’s mages might have. That was where Natura came in.

Alice's largest dragon could not fly with a human aboard, but she was comfortable with gliding from their four-story window.

"It’s good practice for me too," she said cheerfully, while Alice tried not to feel sick at the thought.

Thanks to Prim and her invisibility illusion, they could walk out onto one of the courtyard balconies where Alice climbed onto Natura's back. The first time she did this was an extremely harrowing experience. Alice sat only a few feet off the ground, but it still felt very high up, and when she went to the edge of the balcony, looking down four stories was so much worse.

Alice wanted to close her eyes in fear, but Natura had warned her to shift her weight along with any left or right movement she had to make. Alice could not do that while trying to block out everything else. She had to be a present, active rider.

Her nature dragon climbed to the very edge of the balcony, and it was a miracle the railing did not crack under her weight. Natura was lighter than she looked, and no doubt the Protectorate Guild only hired the best people for their construction.

Then, before Alice could back out, Natura leapt into the dark sky. Alice was certain Prim had to cover her yelp of fear with an illusionary sound, but then Natura's wings spread out, looking larger than ever, and she caught the air.

Natura was more than just any plant-based dragon—she was a nature dragon and was able to conjure up a gust of wind to buoy them along. While she was nowhere near the point of flapping her wings and carrying Alice in active flight, she was able to glide quite easily down the block almost all the way to a nearby park.

Once there, Natura was more than happy to restore what trees and plant life she could in the park. Spark and Aconite were set to watch her just in case there was any danger. There never was. This time of night, in the rich part of the city, the park was nearly abandoned.

Alice dismounted on shaky legs. She had lived through the experience, but the night had only begun.

Prim stepped in again, this time making an illusionary version of Alice. She looked older and practically dripped with jewels and necklaces. This version of her would walk about twenty paces in front of the real Alice.

They went fishing for thieves.

Like this, Alice walked along the city streets for hours without meeting up with any problems. The few people who were out gave her odd looks, but no one actually took the bait.

When it happened on the third night, Alice was not prepared. The thief must have had some sort of silencing skill, because she heard the sound of a single footstep before a man seemed to melt out of the shadows in front of the illusionary version of her.

He did not just go for the jewels. With one hand he grabbed at the necklace, the other hand held a knife which slashed down right at the illusion's throat and dispersed it. It happened so quickly, so coldly, that the entire action took Alice's breath away. That man had meant to kill.

Alice's mind reeled. She understood people stealing. She had been desperate and hungry enough to do that herself back when she had been a General Laborer, when Prim had just hatched and she had no other way to feed her. But this thief was willing to coldly kill for just a pretty necklace. She doubted he knew if it was real or just cheap glass. The point was he wanted it, and she had it.

As she stood frozen, the twenty paces in front of her illusion faded away. But she was not as shocked as the thief. In the space between them, Alice faced him full on: tall, willowy, with hazel eyes. And in those hazel eyes she saw a deadly sense of coldness. One look at this man and she knew for a fact that he would kill—and probably had killed—just because he could. And for a moment it seemed that he was staring straight at the real her. Then he turned and ran.

Horror and shock had rooted Alice to the spot, but her dragons were much more prepared.

Without a word, Numi scuttled after him, with Prim in hot pursuit to cloak her in an illusion.

This moment had been a bone of contention between Alice and her dragons. Prim could not cloak both Numi and Alice at the same time at a distance. While they were planning this, Prim had first refused to go along with Numi, insisting that protecting Alice was much more important. Alice had ended up putting her foot down.

"Iggy and Bella will be there to protect me, and anything that can get past those two, you cannot fight," she said.

Finally, Prim had agreed, though not without haranguing Iggy and Bella and ordering them multiple times that they were to absolutely protect Alice with their lives. Both had easily agreed, and Alice had felt overwhelmed by the show of that much loyalty.

The thief was moving fast, surely buoyed by fear, and her dragons had to hurry to catch up. Soon they had reached Prim's limits, and Alice felt her illusionary cloak slide away. She was once again visible and felt exposed, standing out there in the middle of the road.

After all, it had just been proved very viscerally that there were people in the night willing to kill.

"Alice," Bella said, urging her to turn around. "Back to the park."

Feeling a little numb, Alice followed her dragon's directions. The worst part was that there was no guarantee they would not have to keep doing this. After all, even a successful thief would likely not hold a stash of gems for long. He or she would sell them to somebody else, or… well, Alice was not quite sure what thieves did. Anyway, there was every chance that this particular thief would not have anything valuable for Numi to find. So they would have to keep going until they ran across one who did.

Aspect 1, Primordialis "Prim" has gained a level.

Illusion: 9

Mother of All: 6

Well, that was one positive for the night. Prim was getting much practice with her illusionary skills, as well as her Mother of All skill, which she normally received only when laying an egg. Her skill up now was possibly because she was helping to take care of Numi. Valuable training was always good... right?

With effort, Alice put that aside and made herself walk back to the park calmly, as if she belonged within the night. She did not run like she desperately wanted to.

When she returned to the park, she found Natura had been busy on her own. When Alice and the others had left her earlier this evening, her dragon had been in a secluded area that was bare of all but some neatly tended grass. Now it was thriving with shade-loving plants of different emerald-green colors, some of which had blue and purple buds flowering. Her dragon had created a beautiful little glade, an oasis of peace in a random park.

Alice sat on the soft, now very luxurious grass and felt some of the stress leave her body. At least, right up until she started listening to her dragon's conversation.

"Oh yes," Natura said. "I encouraged many of the native seeds. The soil is very rich. I think that's because of the large dragon's skeleton."

"I'm sorry, what?" Alice opened her eyes, not realizing that she had been on the verge of drifting off. Had she just dreamed what she thought Natura had said?

Her nature dragon turned from her conversation with Iggy and blinked back at her. "The large dragon's skeleton underground. It's been placed deep, but the plants are taking a lot of nutrients from it. In fact, it's feeding everything here." She gestured with a wing to indicate the entire park.

So she had not been dreaming. "There is a dead dragon under us?" Alice asked, just to make sure.

"Very far under us," Natura confirmed.

"Do you know what it died from?" Iggy asked.

"How would I know that? I can only tell that the skeleton has been there for many, many years." She sounded quite annoyed, as if they were the unreasonable ones.

Alice, however, felt a thrill of premonition. “Would you say it’s been there for two centuries or so?”

She hesitated for a moment before she nodded. "About that long, I would say. But as you can see, its bones are still quite potent."

Obviously, Natura was not concerned with the thought of a dead dragon. Alice, meanwhile, felt as cold as if she had stepped out into a midwinter's day. Two hundred years ago was about the same time as the Old Kingdom cities had been abandoned. Though, to be fair, two or more centuries was a very broad yardstick to measure against.

The hair standing up on the back of her neck told her it was no coincidence.

"I don't suppose you could encourage the earth to bring up a bone," Bella wheedled. "Perhaps a rib? Oh, actually, maybe a femur? They are much thicker, with more marrow."

Natura gave her an odd look. "Why would I disturb a grave like that?"

"It's not disturbing. Think of it as an energy transfer," Bella said. "The dragon will live on through me."

Natura hesitated again before she looked to Alice for permission.

Alice's gaze swung from one to another before finally landing on her dark dragon. "Just to make sure I know what you're talking about, you want to do an burnt sacrifice on this skeleton?"

"Yes, of course," Bella said easily, "but not the whole skeleton. Just one measly bone."

"But dear, if you're hungry, can you not seek out some mice? Surely there are some in the park."

"It's not about hunger." Her dark claws clenched together into the equivalent of a fist. "It's about power. Think about it: The skeleton has been feeding all these plants for hundreds of years. If it's that potent, I should be able to gain a new power through it as well."

Bella's egg had been found amongst a graveyard of skeletons, left in the arms of a child. Perhaps she had been placed there so she would have plenty to consume when she hatched out.

Alice suddenly missed Prim's comforting presence, but Prim could not decide this for her.

Alice closed her eyes for a moment and thought it out. Like most of Bella's powers, it was slightly disturbing, but if they were standing above a potent wellspring, she would be a fool to deny her dragon. Alice nodded to herself before she reopened her eyes.

"Yes, but to be clear, you are never to kill anything but vermin for your burnt sacrifice."

"Of course," Bella easily agreed. "But you know, the dragon's been dead for a long time..."

Alice frowned. "You're not taking this seriously enough. Bella, look at me." She waited until her dragon's gaze met her own. "The more I learn about your burnt sacrifice, the more I feel that there need to be boundaries set around it. Unless your life is in danger or I have previously approved of it, like with the vermin, you are not to kill to further your burnt sacrifice skill, nor dig up any graveyards without my approval," she added quickly.

All the arrogance drained out of Bella, and for a moment she looked as young and unsure as she had when she first hatched in the forest. "Yes, of course, I agree," she said in her soft, smoky voice. "I would never do that if you did not want me to. But Alice, if this dragon skeleton has the power to feed an entire park for hundreds of years, it's extremely potent. I only want one bone from it."

Alice still felt uneasy about giving approval, but her dragons, especially Bella, could not grow quickly enough with training alone. And she was coming to realize more and more that they were in a world that did not trust them. They needed to be as strong as possible in order to protect themselves.

She looked to Natura. "If it will not disrupt the balance of the park, then please use the earth to bring up a bone."

"A single bone will not disrupt anything," Natura assured her. "Besides, it is up to the lazy gardeners to make sure each plant has the proper amount of light and soil nutrition. I believe they have not questioned why the plants grow so well here and have not been doing their jobs properly."

With that, she moved to a spot a little ways away where the grass seemed to have trouble taking hold and had left behind a bare patch of dirt. It looked like it had been a dried puddle at one time. Natura pressed her claws into the hard patch and they sank down a few inches.

Alice waited, but there was no sign of movement or the feeling of rumbling underfoot. She should have known better. Natura was nothing but considerate of other life and would not do something as traumatic as upset the layers of the soil for no reason.

"This will take some time," Natura said, as if hearing her thoughts. "The flesh and ligaments of the body are well gone, but it was buried very deep, and I do not want to tear any roots on the way up, so I'm encouraging them to move to the side."

"Deep?" Bella repeated. "Then perhaps it was some kind of earth-type dragon and it got stuck underground and was unable to rise again to the air?"

That painted a terrible picture. Alice shivered.

"No," Natura said calmly. "I can feel the layers of the earth, you know. There are several types of life that feed only on the surface and some that live further down below. These layers had been churned up a long time ago. The dragon died, and the soil was added on top at a later point."

Bella perked up. "A battle then, perhaps? I may be able to learn what kind of dragon it was from the flavor of its power."

Iggy had kept silent so far but this seemed to be a bridge too far. He turned to Bella. "You know, to take strength from an enemy that you had not slain yourself could be considered a type of theft."

"So could finding and keeping a coin that was lost on the street, and you don't complain about Numi," Bella snapped back. "This dragon isn't using its body anymore—nothing is using it other than some grass. And who wants that as its legacy? Except for you, Natura," she hastily added.

Natura snapped her mouth shut and continued concentrating on her patch of soil. Bella and Iggy fell silent, though they were looking away from one another. Alice wondered if she should step in, but decided that they needed to work out their differences themselves.

Finally, Natura stepped back and reached out to brush at the top layer of dirt. A gray bone was slowly revealed.

"The femur bone was longer than two of Alice put together," Natura said. "This is a vertebra from the tail."

It was as large as a human head.

Bella was so excited that she could not wait and grabbed the bumpy top to try to drag it away. Unfortunately, it was still half sunk into the ground and much too heavy for her. Alice bade her to be patient, and then, when the bone was finally free, she picked it up, grunting a little bit at the effort.

"Over here, over here!" Bella said, bouncing further into the glen. She looked for all the world like an excited little dog who could not wait for her master to throw a ball.

Alice set down the bone and then looked around. "It is night, and your burnt sacrifice skill can be rather bright."

"No need to worry, I can obscure us," Natura said. More plants immediately shot out of the soil, each with wide leaves. Other plants grew closer, weaving among each other until they were enclosed by a tight canopy. There was an opening at the top to let in moonlight.

"It would be a good idea to send Spark and Aconite out to check for any observers," Iggy said.

Alice agreed and told the sub-aspects to keep watch.

Finally, when they ensured all was secure, Bella was allowed to begin her skill. She drew a circle around the vertebra with her claw, ripping up grass and making Natura wince. When the circle connected, a bright green light flared up. That, along with the unusual shape of the vertebra, cast the entire glade in an eerie green light.

The bone was extremely dry and old, and though it was large, it fell into ash right away, all of which flowed to Bella. The dragon's eyes rolled back and she collapsed.

"Bella!" Alice ran to her, bent to pick her up, but then paused. The skill was still going, and she was afraid to interrupt it. The vertebra was so large that there was a lot of ash to go around, and it all seemed to flow into the little dragon. Reluctantly, she stepped back.

Bella did not move. She just lay there until the last of the ash flowed into her.

"Bella?" Alice said again and carefully picked up her dragon to cradle her. Her scales were hot, almost feverish. Almost immediately, Bella's eyes slid open.

"He was a sun dragon," she murmured.

"What?"

Bella shook herself slightly and seemed to come more awake. "Forgive me for frightening you, Alice. My magic isn't compatible with the bone, so it's taking some time to absorb."

Her eyes went vague and she said in a monotone voice, "His wings stretched so far out that he was able to stay in the sky with just the merest breath of wind. Once he took to the air as a hatchling, he never needed to touch down again. He slept on the wing, drank rain, and collected sunlight for energy. He would travel across the kingdom and whenever he overflew a city, he would beam down any extra energy into magical collectors. That energy would power entire cities. He would leave lights and running machines in his wake."

She shuddered in Alice's arms. "Then the rebellion came, and the armies shot him down and took him from the sky forever."

"What rebellion?" Alice asked with a dry mouth.

"I don't know. He never... There weren't many thoughts in his head. He spent his days in the sky slowly drifting and collecting sunlight. He didn't think very deeply." Bella shuddered again. "I want to sleep, Alice. I need to process this."

"Yes." She put her hand on Bella's head. "We will speak later. Rest, dearest."

The nearby leaves suddenly parted and Alice turned around, startled. But Numi's green snout poked out and Alice let out a breath. Prim was right behind her. Both dragons looked tired, but triumphant.

Seeing Bella's state, Prim rushed up and placed the tip of her muzzle to Bella's forehead. "She will be okay," she said after a moment.

As if the System had been waiting, she received a notification.

Alice did not think she had been that close to leveling again so soon, but then she realized—in all the fuss of raising the dead dragon's bones—that she had missed another important notification: Numi had leveled in her adventure as well.

Aspect 2, Numismatis "Numi" has gained a level:

Lockpicking - 8

Aspect 2, Numismatis "Numi" has reached level 7

Even more surprisingly, both of them together had tipped Alice's own experience over the edge.

Alice was now a level eight.
