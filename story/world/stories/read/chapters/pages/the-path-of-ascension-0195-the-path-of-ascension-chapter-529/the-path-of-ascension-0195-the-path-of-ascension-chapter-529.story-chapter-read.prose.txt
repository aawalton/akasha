Chapter 529

Esther shoved another forkfull of noodles into her mouth as she and Lawrence sat on their ship and watched the recording of their fellow Gladiators laying waste to the Federation.

“I think we’d be best off with a throw to delay Titan. He’s too heavy for a stasis to really work against him, and uses too much mana for a slow to inhibit much. Though maybe a microloop? I could see that working.”

Lawrence, always practical, offered a suggestion about the thing right in front of them. It was a super cut of their fellow Gladiators’ latest raid and it would have captivated her most of the time. Except, she couldn’t care less about the video. She wanted to talk about the much bigger underlying issue of how Maxine ‘Cosmind’ Teslovaka had played them, her, like a puppet.

The instant she’d been muted by Matt in the Gladiator Chat, she’d been sent another message. It just hadn’t come from that system, or even the Gladiator chat, it had come through to her normal inbox, despite her never greenlighting the sender. That also ignored the impossibility of that system finding anything to connect to on a military ship in chaotic space, but Esther hadn’t needed that to know something was wrong when she got the message from Maxine of all people.

Her initial reaction had been that the Gladiator Chat had breached containment, but she’d ruled that out worryingly quickly. It had taken her a frantic and hectic week of tearing apart their ship in discarded futures to figure out the truth, but she’d managed it.

It was just that the answer wasn’t at all helpful.

Cosmind had spliced and injected the personal message throughout their last six months of official Republic military updates. The virus had had a defined timer to send the message at the same moment Matt had muted her, which said far too much about their higher Tier Corporation peer and her ability to predict the future.

Esther was woman enough to admit to herself that she’d been mildly terrified before she’d even read the message.

“I’m more than happy to play, Esther. You don’t need to worry about my delving cycle or anything so obvious. Waiting around won’t make it harder for me to anticipate your next move, quite the contrary. Now, because I’m not entirely mean, I’ll be cruel. Here is a link to something you and Lawrence have been putting together yourselves, since you aren’t entirely sure you can trust the current Republic information systems. Let me save you some trouble. I even promise it's nothing you couldn’t have collected on your own if you weren’t so busy fighting and all that. However, I wanted to make sure you did your absolute best in the end of the war fight. That way, when you get trounced, you can’t fall back on a lack of preparedness as an excuse. Have fun and good luck! I’ll be rooting for you two!”

Then, to add insult to injury, the moment she finished reading the last word, another message had come through perfectly timed once again. “Rooting, not betting. I don’t want you to think I think you are going to win! Okay, byeeee! And remember: Stay out of my back-end systems! Next time I see you playing with them, I’ll force you to be a moderator!”

It was simultaneously one of the most terrifying and arousing things Esther had experienced in her life. Sadly, despite all of her many good qualities, she was keenly familiar with her own weaknesses. Fear, and attraction through competence, easily occupied ten out of the top five spots on her ‘does not handle well’ list, and the message had hit her in both.

Esther wanted to say she hadn’t intended her limit testing to be anything more than a bit of fun, but she was self aware enough to know that a lot of her regret came from both not only getting caught, but being predicted so thoroughly.

Worse yet, the link Max had sent only further proved her preparedness. It contained a super cut of all the active Gladiators teams’ historical fights from Tier 25 to Tier 35, with everything from the smallest public scuffle, to hours of previous wars’ battles. It was all there.

More than that, Max hadn’t only included the Clan and Empire Gladiators, she’d included both Maya’s and their own.

The two of them had spent an inordinate amount of time in discarded timelines as their ship traveled from battlefield to battlefield going over their opponents’ fighting styles.

It was how she’d fallen back into a bad habit in a big way.

In fact…

Esther turned to her brother and probed, “Hey, you know what I realized?”

“Hmm?” was his half hearted reply.

“I hadn’t stress eaten in a hundred thousand years. I should commemorate my accomplishment with a nice dinner.”

She punctuated her comment by taking another bite of her food.

Lawrence wasn’t quite as impressed as she hoped, and his eyes were pulled back to the projection. “Let’s refocus before we have to discard this timeline, we only have fifteen minutes. We still need to figure out more counters to their moves. We have no idea how much longer this war is going to last. It could be over tomorrow for all we know. We are nearing the expected thresholds, so let's not squander the time we have.”

Esther desperately wanted to complain about the notion of wasting time when they were experiencing almost an hour for every real second that passed. Instead, she agreed, even as she pulled out a still steaming loaf of bread in protest.

“Fine. I’m thinking about Legion. My whip should be able to shred her spirit if I can break through her armor. The problem is, the moment there are two of her, that doesn't accomplish anything more than annoying her momentarily. I’ll need your help to restrain her other bodies if we want that to actually finish her off.” Chewing on the heel of the warm bread, she mumbled, “That means you need to take out their support and put Titan on the defensive. Wraith without her Aspect is the closest thing they have to a team weakness, so they’ll be expecting it. What about —”

She could stress eat and plan just fine. The crumbs would vanish along with this possible timeline anyway.

Matt desperately wanted the war to end, and while that was nothing new for him, he was far from alone in that feeling two hundred and forty years later.

The entire Realm had watched while casualty numbers climbed across the warfronts year by year as they approached the two century mark. As the population war reached everyone’s estimated required casualties based on the former population wars, the Realm waited for word to come down from the Tier 50s to start winding the fighting down.

It hadn’t come. Without it, the war continued battle after battle, push after push, day after day, year after year.

Morale plummeted across all the Great Powers as the death toll rose five percent further than anyone had estimated. Rather than slow down, the war front only intensified, with pushes becoming weekly occurrences, as each side dug that little bit deeper. When the collective Realm had slid past ten percent more losses, everyone realized the former estimates had been wildly optimistic as a sense of despair swept through the frontlines.

Matt knew that for many, even immortals, it was their first real reminder that the Tier 50s made the decisions, not public sentiment. Most of the Great Powers faced protests and sometimes even riots in the wake of that revelation. Undoubtedly some of that was driven by the late stage war economy that strained everyone, but others were just upset their loved ones that had managed to survive the brutal culling were expected to continue fighting without any end in sight.

Despite the lower Tiers' feelings, all the protests were put down without any large scale problems. In the Sects and the Federation, that lesson had never been forgotten, and their worlds only had a scattered few isolated problems, giving them a minor but noticeable advantage in the Tier 15 to 20 brackets.

While the Empire had had push back, they hadn’t been the hardest hit. That dubious title went to the Republic, whose enthusiasm for the war was the lowest to begin with. They had protests, demonstrations, and even a few riots that turned ugly, but while they were the worst hit, the other Great Powers all experienced some degree of the same.

Thankfully, the Empire hadn’t suffered on the battlefields either, at least compared to the others. That horrific honor went to the Sects and Guilds, who were a full two percent farther along in their casualties rates than the rest of the Great Powers.

As an ally to the Guilds and having interacted with their people, he didn’t quite understand the ferocity they met the Sects with, despite seeing the historical precedent for it. To him, the enmity seemed to come out of nowhere. But whatever the case, the two sides clashed like hot oil and cold water. The Sects policy to not surrender didn’t help things as a principle, and the Guilds’ cultivators proved their own stubbornness and dedication to punishing such acts at every possible turn.

Worse, Matt could see the Empire and Collective losses were proportionally lighter than others, as they held themselves back around the edges of the warfront, but in absolute numbers the casualties were still staggering as the main battlefields raged day and night. Which is what made it all the more confusing when the war waltzed past a hundred and fifteen percent of the estimated required casualties, without any indication of when the fighting would stop.

The speculation was endless, with everyone who had a method to communicate having a guess, but he didn’t give weight to any of their ideas, no matter how logical they might sound. Some had a grain of truth to them, like the idea that the Collective was holding out for their new Ascenders to finish, but that ignored the reality that they’d lost the Tier 15 and the Tier 35 brackets already.

The trio had been a little too young to influence the war, but they’d be in a strong position for a follow-up one in a few hundred years. That reason was also why Matt was hopeful the wars would end sooner rather than later.

Or that was what he told himself.

As the joint fatality rating slid past a hundred twenty percent of the estimates, everyone paused in rapt anticipation for word from the Tier 50s that they could stop.

It came just as Matt was afraid mass riots would break out.

Peace talks were underway, and an armistice was in effect.

For the warfront as a whole, it meant there was an end in sight.

For Matt, Liz, and Aster, it meant they had a date to keep.

Instead of kicking back and going home, they ignored their new orders to return for an end of war debriefing and rushed to Minkalla at their top speed.

With Matt powering their engines they arrived first, but none of them minded the delay. None of them had been back to Minkalla in a while, and he took a moment to appreciate all that was the same and everything that was different.

It was a new real space star system compared to when he had last visited it, but that was the smallest surprise of all. The energy devouring planet teleported to the nearest Tiered world any time it drained a location of energy, which was why the Great Powers deliberately kept it surrounded by Tier 1 worlds that didn’t already have environments mortals could easily live on, instead of letting it eat something more valuable like an inhabited world.

Despite essence requiring life to form, many low Tier worlds lost their tenuous grip on that claim to fame. While rifts would eventually terraform and repopulate any world with essence, that was an agonizingly slow process reliant on miniscule chances determined by the local rifts. As a result, many such worlds remained in the lowest Tiers as their essence was the only thing making more essence, lacking the generations of life to add mana to the cycle and hurry things along.

After he had the thought, Matt decided to follow it up. He stepped outside the ship, where he [Portal]ed to a spot just above the clockwork planet. He spent a few minutes watching the massive copper gears as they churned and clanked silently below him. Each rotation and twist shifted the large boxes that were theorized to be the environments that made up the floors.

He supported that belief like most who’d experienced the strange environmental boundaries present inside the planet but he also understood how little the inside and outside appearances of high Tier items had in relation to each other.

Matt did the math and realized the post-war meeting would most likely happen just as Minkalla was letting out. That would be a spectacle and he started making plans to avoid getting roped in either by Manny or the military.

That brought his attention away from Minkalla and to the worlds surrounding it. Unlike his time as a Tier 11, his spiritual sense was more than capable of reaching the eight moons that orbited the planet from where he floated next to it. That let him see how they’d changed radically since his time there.

Without the hordes of Tier 14s who needed to enter the world in hopes of a Concept, the approaches to the rearranging planet had shifted. That had been reflected in the Great Power installations more than Minkalla itself.

While the Sects still had mass attendance due to cultural expectations, the other Great Powers had seen steep declines in their entrant numbers across the board. Without those who were simply seeking immortality, it meant less competition for the non-respawning monsters on the top floors, but it also meant the average skill and combat power level of those who entered was far higher.

Statistically, most teams were able to go to far more challenge rooms on average than they’d been able to in their delve, and they had to fight more monsters than people. In turn, their fellow delvers were far more dangerous as a whole, and that was reflected in the services the Great Power offered. The support moons now had more last minute combat trainers, skill modification experts, and crafters who’d packed themselves in the formerly empty spaces than when Matt had come through.

At that time, half of the moons had felt like a place to help the aging Tier 14’s get inside where they could become food for the more skilled cultivators. Now, it seemed more about trying to sell their goods and services to the next round of capable entrants.

Matt wanted to test something and created a liquid mana gem in his hand with a measly two million mana. Once it was formed, he let it slip from his grip, where it fell to the planet he couldn’t interact with below him.

As he tracked its path downward, he watched as the ever-present energy drain picked away at the hard outer shell of his mana stone. It held up surprisingly well; so much so that he was a little afraid of what might happen if it couldn’t eat the present. But before it came close to reaching what constituted ground for Minkalla, the drain found a weak point and unraveled the mass of energy.

The liquid mana quickly evaporated as it turned back into a gas, but it, along with the dissolving harder shell, was swept down and into the world's depths like every other free source of energy.

Matt tried to follow the energy with his Aspect, but his new command over his mana failed to let him violate Minkalla's privacy. He hadn’t expected it to work, given he was only using an Aspect, but he’d been hopeful.

He did learn one thing which interested him. As he was losing his connection, he had enough feedback to tell that his mana had been converted out from under him into some other form of energy rather than absorbed into another container or the like. He assumed that the new form of power was Genesis Energy, the golden essence-like energy unique to the world, but he didn’t know how to prove it.

Before he could form ideas on how to tackle that impossibility, he felt one of the spiritual presences that had been following him poke him in the side. He turned to follow the offensive gesture when he saw who’d done it.

Wun waved vigorously at him from the Clans moon as he saw he’d gotten Matt’s attention. He instinctively checked the Ascender Chat to see if the older Ascender had messaged him, but Wun hadn’t done so and only waved harder with each passing moment.

Matt’s manners demanded he return the gesture, but he didn’t move any closer or try to communicate. He didn’t want anyone from On The Last Line trying to diminish his upcoming win by saying he’d gotten last minute help from Wun.

Thankfully, while the others were slower than their ship, they weren’t actually slow.

Maya was the second to arrive and she sauntered out of her ship with a saucy wink and wave to the floating moons. She was blowing the moons a kiss when the three of them arrived next to her.

“Well, well, well look at you three, all grown up. Almost brings a tear to my eye.”

Matt rolled his own eyes at the comment as he pulled his fellow blonde into a one armed hug before she was beset by Liz and Aster.

“So, are you really leaving the war Tiers?” Liz asked as she and Aster separated from their own greetings.

Maya snorted as her good cheer seemed to vanish. “The second this fight is over, I’m Tiering up. I’ve tried every book in the trick to get Janet to pay me to leave or stay, but she won’t bite. Everything is going to Esther and Lawrence, lucky fuckers.”

“I mean, that only makes sense, given they are expected to linger for a while and they’ve admitted they are onboard with the idea. I’m just surprised Janet didn’t want a second war with two Tier 35 Ascenders. She could have made quite the expansion play.”

Aster's comment earned another shake of the head from Maya. “Nooo wayyyy. Janet isn’t that expansionist, and I don’t think the other Tier 50s would take another double-up well. From what I’ve seen, Janet will be happy if I leave of my own volition. I’m considering going with only a minor fuss; my last good deed of the war.”

Maya lightly clutched at her chest as she put on her most solemn expression. The act only lasted until a phoenix Liz chortled and took Maya down with her.

“Yeah, who am I kidding. Sooo, that was a fun little raid you and On The Last Line did. Any cool goodies that you guys snatched you want to brag about or possibly dispose of in a bet?”

Liz laughed as she hooked a finger at Matt. “Not from that trip, but when we hit the shipping yard earlier, Matt grabbed some shield units and we’ve already retrofitted Lila’s old ship with them. Allie is already talking about a jaunt into the second layer of chaotic space after the royal generation. So if you don’t Tier up too quickly you can probably tag along and see what we find.”

“Ohhhhh, that does sound interesting. Tip me sideways but I am intrigued. I might need to shuffle some things around, as that does sound fun. I’ve always hated that Olivier has more strange realm rewards than I do and this is a great chance to even that out before he can take advantage of Allie’s range too.”

The four of them chatted there next to Minkalla until On The Last Line arrived half a day later. They’d have continued with the larger group, but they brought word that Esther and Lawrence were only hours behind them.

Matt only needed a thought to don his last disposable set of war armor. He was far from alone, as the others all got ready far earlier than they were necessarily required too.

There was a small amount of banter as they waited, but all of them were too amped up to really get into it. When the final ship tore its way from chaotic space and revealed its occupants were also fully armored and ready for a fight, he smiled.

To help things along, Matt opened a [Portal] from near the newest arriving ship to the group as he asked for all of them to hear, “So, how do we want to do this? Free for all?”

Moe rubbed his hands as Esther and Lawrence joined them. “Naaa, that normally works just fine, but if the two from the Republic team up, we are forced to team up as well and that’s not acceptable. I want my chance to knock your head off, Matt! No way I can accept teaming up again with that in mind. I want to fight so bad, I can taste it!” Grabbing the air in front of Matt, he growled. “I don’t want to team up with you! I want to beat you so bad no one ever questions who’s the strongest fighter in the Realm again! It's me!”

Almost immediately, he cocked his head as if he was only considering the words for the first time after saying them. Turning to address the group in general, he asked, “Wait? Would I be happy to know I’m the best or would that suck all the joy out of my life?” After only a moment of contemplation the dwarf shook his head and chortled to himself. “Of course I would! Sounds amazing in fact! Hahahah! I—”

Maya tossed a blanket she created out of light over the cackling dwarf which muted his laughter to unanimous nods of approval. That included the remaining three members of On The Last Line who had no sympathy for their teammate. “As much as I hate to agree with an idiot who’s never had a good thought more complicated than ‘punch hard, punch good’, he’s right. A two verse two isn’t what anyone signed up for.”

Moe tried to interrupt but his voice was completely muffled by the blanket, preventing him from pointing out the flaw in Maya’s comment any more than visually.

Krodag offered a suggestion as well as bottles of beer no one took. “Then which pair wants to fight the most? That should be the last fight so we can give it our all.”

Moe pointed to Matt, proving his hearing hadn’t been affected by the covering along with Ellen who pointed at Liz. That split the Clan team in half between Matt, Liz, and Aster, and Esther and Lawrance, effectively removing their choice.

Things rapidly devolved from there but they eventually argued out a solution everyone could accept.

Matt, Liz, and Aster would fight Maya, then Esther and Lawrence, then On The Last Line. They’d wanted to end with a fight against the ‘revived’ Ascenders as the two closest in power pair, but Maya had claimed it as her right to pass the torch to a Gladiator of her own Great Power. She refused to budge on the topic and was willing to concede in other areas, which meant they ended up giving in to her demands.

As much as he wanted to complain, Matt did understand. He didn’t like the idea of stepping away from their role as their Great Power’s strongest sword and shield without a proper replacement any more than On the Last Line did. Knowing that was exactly how it went most of the time didn’t help take that worry off his shoulders. That, and letting Maya revel in her unexpected chance wasn’t that large of a burden when it meant he got to fight On The Last Line as the final battle, where he wouldn’t have any reason to hold back.

With three battles the first two would, by necessity, be lighter bouts, but that didn’t mean they wouldn’t be seriously trying to kill each other. They all had enough power that a single mistake could lead to such a result, but they were all equally experienced in not dying, which made serious injuries historically unlikely.

Or that was true for the first two fights. The final one promised to be a much more furious battle, and he was looking forward to it. They’d never get the chance to fight their dwarven peers on an equal footing again, where it was very likely they’d find some occasion to battle their Republic ones sooner than later.

With the battles decided, Matt, Liz and Aster looked across the vacuum of space where their lone opponent floated in her heavy armor next to the half of Minkalla they were fighting by.

Aster kept to his rear with a phoenix Liz sitting on her shoulder for additional protection while Liz’s main bodies were next to Matt.

Maya seemed chipper as she called out, “On one. Three.”

Aster didn’t miss a beat as she followed, “Two!”

“One!”

All four of them called out the final number as they sprung into action.

Matt started out with a torrent of mana delivered via [Mana Beam] as a distraction for his real move of flaring out his Domain and extending Minkalla’s gravity well, threatening to pull Maya into and through the world.

Maya didn’t dodge either attack. She let the blue bar of death wash over her and it did so harmlessly, but that only got Matt to start looking for an illusion by activating his strange realm reward, trying to find the flaws in the unreality that undoubtedly surrounded him.

Blood splattered across his face as an instant later an axe was embedded deep inside his sternum.

Maya appeared next to him with a wicked smile on her exposed face,even as his ability highlighted the infinitesimally small flaws that revealed she was an illusion.

“Didn’t you get the memo? We cut out the interference. Armor won’t help you now!”

She wretched the weapon out at an angle, causing even more damage, but Matt didn’t stop his [Mana Beam] or acknowledge the wound in any way. He did create a mana crystal blade and tried to stab the illusory Maya in turn, but she was already gone like morning mist under a noonday sun.

Aster had more luck than him, dispelling the wound Maya had dealt to him like it was an illusion, right before Matt noticed reality firm up. His bond had taken advantage of the same Law which had wounded him to cut through Maya’s anti-healing techniques.

Her interruption must have worked a little too well as Maya ducked Liz’s thrust and returned a lunging rapier towards his bond’s location in return.

As she did so, words spoken in Maya’s voice resonated throughout his head and spirit. “I spy a skinned fox!”

Maya appeared directly next to his bond as her words finished without crossing the intervening distance, but her thrust carried all of the momentum she should have gathered. It let her start with a massive alpha strike that caused a wave of ice and snow to shoot out as she battered through the hidden defences his bond had hastily erected.

They’d expected her to target Aster to remove their support, so when the image of Aster was spontaneously flayed, the illusion simply dropped to reveal a Liz in the cloud of ice and cold left behind.

Rather than try to cover the distance normally, Matt activated his teleportation ring and swapped places with the fox-shaped blood ooze. Without army support to stabilize space, he and Liz had more than enough strength to use their rings under the Domain spatial layering Maya was using. It took more mana from the shared pool to activate but they’d never cared about efficiency, only that it could activate through the suppression.

They were two and she was one, which made all the difference on such a small scale.

His left hand caught and twisted the sword she thrust at his face, but the act still shredded his physical armor’s gauntlet. Her enhanced blade carved through both it and the second layer of [Cracked Phantom Armor], where it skittered across his invulnerable skin.

In return, his right hand came down with his lattice sword aimed directly at the frosty Maya’s head, ready to cleave her in half before she could shake off the debuffs Aster had layered her illusion with.

Instead of being hit, the Republic Ascender seemed to ride the blow with the sword. The weapon carried her along without ever making contact to her slowed form, despite the impressive gravitational attraction he was channeling through his sword with [Cosmic Pressure] and [Gravity Manipulation] in an attempt to prevent her from running away. However, the harder Matt pushed his magic and sword, the faster she moved, as if she was riding a wave of water.

Surf’s up.

His [AI] noted and cataloged the anomaly as one he’d seen her use in fights against Aiden. Using that law, she could ride any kind of waveform he created, so he looked to deny it instead of fighting around the ability.

He moved to activate his Intent and lock her in place, but Maya was ready for that in turn. The moment he locked her down; his sword froze in its path, like an unawakened child slamming into a wall.

He dropped the now reflected effect to activate [Dragon’s Breath], creating a gale of cutting winds. As close as they were, her armor lost most of its details as he stripped the outer layers away, exposing the first layer of runes. He furthered the damage by activating [Dragon’s Drought] and adding sand into the air, scouring away even more of her armor. He finished it by activating [Dragonflame], reducing the Republic Ascender to a cloud of wispy smoke that winked at him before dissipating.

A sudden and sharp attack at Matt’s Domain by Maya’s Aspect left her open to Liz’s reprisal, but she still managed to slip out from their range as he protected Aster.

Maya never let off the aggression as she seemed to slide next to Liz, whom she met blow for blow in three rapid exchanges.

Liz used her shield to protect her body from the waves of now teal light that radiated off Maya, even as she tried to trade injury for injury. She went in for a heavy thrust followed by a sweep that Maya dodged, but she took a stab to her side in return. She ignored it in exchange for landing her followup blow that nearly severed the reality warper’s non-dominant arm in a spray of blood that thrilled Matt as his own [Shadowstrike] was about to land and cause even more damage.

Maya responded to the convergence of attacks by severing her own arm and trying to escape. A perfectly timed spray of ice from Aster both dispelled her working and halted her movement before she gathered her momentum. The opening allowed the second human Liz, aided by her large golden wings, to drive a spear into her back before she could flee. Her charge was followed up an instant later by a synchronized shield bash from her second body and the resulting clash caused massive damage to the Republic Ascender.

As the feathered implements slammed into and through Maya, the world exploded as a miniature black hole appeared where she’d been. Space twisted despite Aster’s rapidly deploying Spirit Space which sought to stabilize it, alongside Liz's own efforts. It tried to draw both Liz’s inside, but Matt only needed a thought to overpower the anti-liquid trap she’d created to destroy Liz with his own Domain. He didn’t use his Intent, which would have matched the attack, rather he tapped into his Concept to counter it directly.

It was faster, and for him, easier. Without his Meld, he’d have lost control over his Domain, pushing up against its polar opposite, but he did have it and was easily able to mirror the move Maya liked to counter Aiden’s liquid forms with; a plethora of experience balancing that coin.

The world turned monochrome, but it was a purely visual effect that ended almost as soon as it began without his Domain propping up both sides of the effect.

Maya must not have been too happy about it either as her voice called through the rapidly freezing battlefield. “Come on, man! That was supposed to work longer than that!”

He didn’t ignore the voice that appeared from behind him, but he didn’t pay it any mind either. Aster had activated her own anti-illusion Intent ability and her [AI] passed the knowledge of what was an illusion to him. It didn’t locate the hidden Gladiator for them, but it did eliminate a lot of places she wasn't, which was almost as helpful.

He eventually spotted her both far off and between him and Aster but it was too late. It took him a moment to figure it out but he realized she was using a perspective trap based on his own viewpoint and bond between himself as Aster as leverage to make her newest working. As such, he could only watch as she cupped Aster in her hands to form a bubble that became a snowglobe glimmering with trapped auroras.

His bond didn’t go down without a fight and even as Matt tried to nullify the attack or launch his own in reprisal, the cage in Maya’s hands started to bounce widely enough that it threatened to slip out of her control. While she kept her grip, her continued efforts were punished as Maya’s fingers started to blacken from the cold radiating out of the orb. Sadly, due to Maya’s working being based on his perspective, the pair were too far in the distance for his help to reach their teammate in time.

Liz wasn’t affected by the working but she’d been the farthest away from Aster and even her golden wings weren’t enough to let her cover the reality-warped space in time.

Aster continued to rage against the bubble she found herself within but he knew things weren’t going well when he saw her Spirit Space fold in around her, collapsing from the general battlefield. Worse, the snowglobe only appeared to strengthen as the wintry landscape fully manifested within it. As the trap fully locked into place, Matt felt Aster’s presence fade from the battlefield entirely, taking her buffs and debuffs along for the ride.

Maya sped up even Matt and Liz felt themselves slow, which evened the battle out.

Through his bond, Matt felt as if Aster was a great distance away but she was firmly alive, if annoyed. He knew she was working to claw her way out of Maya’s trap, but through that same connection he knew that would take her quite a bit of time without her own Aspect to resist from the inside.

Matt flashed his Meld, hoping to shatter Maya’s hold, but he felt the cracks on the surface of the snowglobe mirrored within Aster’s own Domain and stopped. The bubble was so carefully intertwined with her spirit that Matt couldn’t attack the former without also targeting the latter.

With her down a Domain stage, he couldn’t risk it.

The move did however reveal a clearly startled Maya who was moving to attack one of the Lizzes from her spear side, hoping to land a surprise blow.

Maya was close enough that Liz was only able to strike with the butt of her spear, but she landed the attack as she dodged the thrust to her heart from a silver flaming blade. She used the butt of her weapon to try and pull the Republic cultivator closer instead of creating distance which Maya had clearly expected and prepared for.

Liz’s wings let loose a small volley of golden feathers as she was caught off tempo that carved their way through the Republic Ascender and out the other side covered in red.

Matt added to the damage with a wave of [Gravitic Bolt]s, hoping to saturate the area and prevent her from escaping invisibly. Both hit Maya and, unlike most of their earlier attacks, there were clear signs that they’d hit the real her and not another illusion.

Namely, that her blood twisted and turned itself back on the Republic Ascender, proof that Liz was controlling the blood and working to worsen her wounds.

One of his [Gravitic Bolt]s even caught a leg and obliterated it, leaving behind only a mist that Liz took control of.

Just as he believed they were about to turn the battle, the badly damaged Maya spit out two words. “Round two!”

While most of the power of the working was centered on her, Matt felt as part of his mana was returned to him, along with a modicum of physical strength and stamina. Liz’s armor, on the other hand, visibly healed from the earlier damage, but the badly damaged Maya was undoubtedly the winner as she looked fresh and chipper, as if she’d just taken a nice bath.

Both Lizzes were already darting forward to skewer her with the help of Matt’s [Mana Beam] when Maya clapped her hands and the world warbled before forming a box.

“Now there, children. It's not fair to increase the numbers mid-fight when I just removed one of you! One at a time!”

Matt felt as Liz's bodies were drawn together through his connection to their rings, even as the walls of his box turned concrete grey and the vacuum of space was replaced by any normal training cell.

He didn’t wait for the law to finish taking hold of reality and bolted forward with [Cracked Air Slide] ushering him along. He slashed out with [Titan’s Wrath] that seemed to not just fill the training room but overflowed it. However, Maya easily dodged the attacks, as if the room was ten times its apparent size.

In return, Matt reached out and grabbed the walls with [Earth Manipulation], deliberately not considering if the spell would work. He was already inside one of Maya workings and knew that sometimes playing along with the delusion could be more beneficial than fighting it.

With a tremendous effort, he yanked the walls closer and turned their square room into a narrow hallway. He darted after her but no matter how fast he ran or how much she stood still, Maya always seemed to be just out of range.

While he chased the realty warper, he did see and pay attention to the flashes that rippled across Maya’s skin every few moments. They were cleared quickly but he recognized Liz’s curses shining through the supposed one versus one and pushed harder to capitalize on any of the gains his wife was making. At worst, he’d annoy Maya and at best he’d help Liz secure the win. If they were lucky, they’d cause enough damage they freed Aster which was just as good, as it would return her buffs and debuffs to the battle.

Thankfully, he wasn’t limited to his melee abilities and had a solution for someone who was constantly a hand span out of reach. A train-sized [Mana Beam] was equally effective in a narrow alley as his sword, if not more so.

Maya managed to dodge the bar of blue death for a commendable amount of time, but Matt’s own constant rearrangement of the terrain eventually cornered her.

He expected her to dig deeper for another law, but he didn’t expect her to shrug and stop after dodging a sweep of the beam hemmed her in. Instead she simply raised her shield, which became so shiny and reflective it almost looked transparent. Inside the shield he could see the reflection of an endless star field and the enormous column of pure power that was the [Mana Beam] barreling down on her.

As it impacted the shield, the attack bounced back, impacting itself as though Maya was unleashing an attack of equal magnitude. The mirror shield didn’t only redirect the attack but fully reflected, further amplified by the reality warper, which let the bolstered beam cut through Matt’s original attack until it reached him.

Rather than dodge, he locked himself in place with his Intent, allowing the reflected attack to part over [Cracked Phantom Armor]. The boosted spell initially did a decent amount of damage, but his armor rapidly adapted to the beam and it did less and less.

While visually in her favor with him paying for both attacks, it was a trade he was more than happy to make. He was only spending mana, whereas every second of the working cost Maya willpower and he saw the consternation rise on her face as the moments ticked by.

Instead of growing angry, she grew even more indifferent as she scoffed, “Alright, it looks like the others are getting close to the end, so how about we finish this up as well? Sorry Matt, I didn’t want to beat you like this, but a girl's gotta do what a girl's gotta do. No more man-urk!”

Matt felt reality lock up around his spirit and Domain, a bone deep feeling of raw revulsion and refusal sprung forth as without conscious thought his entire Aspect flared itself in defiance. It caught him off guard enough that it took his conscious mind a moment to register what his subconscious so violently rejected. She’d thrown herself fully into an attempt to cut off all new sources of mana - absorbing mana stones, [Sacrifice] spells, Domain benefits, and even regular mana generation.

If Matt’s Talent truly was about absorbing mana, which was what most people generally believed, that probably would have spelled a very bad day for him as it forced him to break through an existing Law to continue the fight. However in reality, Maya had tried to cut off Matt’s ongoing mana regeneration and that wasn’t so easy.

Forty three billion mana per second provided by a Talent, backed by his Endless, Dauntless, and newly spreading and tentatively named Control sub-aspects, as well as his entire Aspect’s core function was not something Maya could so casually halt. His power was his alone to arbitrate and he hadn’t given her that right and the backlash was extensive. If she’d actually been fully focused on him with the entire might of her Domain and Laws there might have been a bit of a struggle, but she’d come at his strong point with one of her Laws alone and that was never going to end well for her.

Aster used the opening to billow out of the scratched and fractured snowglobe, carrying the shattered pieces of Maya’s prison with her. In doing so she revealed herself to be in fox form as she bit down on the false glass that tried to fade away into nothingness. She refused to let go even as it turned her teeth into sparkly crystals that threatened to spread to her head. Her determination meant a large portion of the other woman's Domain was prevented from returning and being used to soften the backlash.

While Matt and Maya were both reeling from the direct contest between their Domains, a Liz who burned with silver flames wasted no time to swoop in for the finishing blow. Iridescent blood struck like a thunderbolt while a wall of frost penned Maya in and prevented her from fading away and escaping the spell.

Instead of fighting on, Maya fluctuated her surrender, giving them the win just a few moments after On The Last Line defeated Tick and Tock.

Matt was excited for the next round and moved to join the now extinguished Liz as she moved to assist Aster’s own recovery.

One round down. Two more to go.

He couldn’t wait.
