The cloth came away slightly pink. Ash wrung it into the basin, watched the water darken. She dipped the cloth again.

She’d been at this for some time now. Long enough that the basin had been changed twice. Long enough that the lantern had guttered out and been replaced. Lysanthia lay on the bed, and she was small. That was the wrong word. Lysanthia had never been small, not even stripped of her power, not even kneeling before the Inker with her jaw so tight Ash thought her teeth might crack. But the woman who lay before her now had been burned down to something Ash didn’t know how to reconcile with the Demon Queen.

Most of her skin was raw. Red and blistered across her back, cracked and weeping across her arms. One of her horns was chipped, the tip sheared clean away. Her hair was largely gone now.

The egg sat beside her, cradled in the curve of her body. Gold, with veins of black that drank in the light. Lysanthia’s arms had curled around it in sleep, or unconsciousness. Ash could not tell which. Any attempts to pry the egg away had made the woman groan and pull back hard, and so Ash had not tried again.

Ash knew how to take care of the injured. She had done it dozens if not hundreds of times before. She insisted she do it now -be the only one to do it, in fact.

Ash moved the cloth over Lysanthia's shoulder. There were burns there, the nastiest of which curved all the way toward the collarbone. Ash kept her mind on the task at hand. It wandered anyway.

All of the villagers called Ash dear now. Martha had first said it while handing Ash a cup of water and she had almost dropped it.

For years Ash had lived as a man. Five as the Hero, five more training to become one. There were no female Heroes, and yet that’s the path Ash had been set on. Ash could not twist the expectations of an entire world. All she could do was twist herself to meet them. So she had twisted and twisted and hidden herself.

A Veil, before the one on her arm. Ronan had never known. Elowen had never known. Even she hadn't, and Ash had so badly wanted to tell her. Ten years of brotherhood, and not one of them had ever known or even suspected.

Everybody knew here. There was Tom, who knew and had asked Ash to lift crates simply because she was a woman who was strong, rather than a man who was supposed to be strong. There was Marsh, who talked to her and looked away while doing it. The way men spoke to all women they were slightly nervous around. Everyone in Hamel knew and nobody cared.

She wrung the cloth out again, catching sight of the Veil Spark on her arm. The System had looked into her soul and given her this -more than something to mark her great deeds or her greater sacrifice.

It had given her something to mark who she had always been: one who hid. Perhaps the System had always known her better than she’d wanted to be known.

She wrung the cloth, dipped it and returned to the wound. And then she saw the thing she always did, when she was alone. When there was nothing else to focus on. She saw Orvyn's face. All of her.

The stricken look her friend had worn. Her hand, reaching down to wipe away some of the blood on Ash's cheek. Gentle, as she always was. Then Orvyn had pulled out a blade and stabbed her. Ash hardly remembered the blade anymore. Its edge had dulled. Orvyn's face was as sharp as ever. It always would be.

And then there was what Orvyn had said before all that. She'd said just three words. You will live. Ash had heard them, and she'd been so utterly relieved. Even without the Hero's Requiem, Ash had somehow always known the confrontation would be her end. She had been relieved that it might not be.

Maybe that's why Ash had stopped the act. And she had talked, as she was. Not as the Hero of the world, but as the scared woman beneath. And then Orvyn had stabbed her for it.

And there were more words that came to Ash after those three. Always. Now that makes perfect sense. I could see how a Saint might turn their blade against the Hero.

Those words had been enough to send Ash into a blind rage. She had wanted to kill Lysanthia for it. For her making it all sound so simple. Instead, Ash had sat down with Lysanthia. She hadn't understood what the woman was saying to her then.

Now, with so much time having passed, she understood it. Some small part of it at least. She understood Roles now. A Saint's Role must have been to preserve the divine order. If the Hero surviving disrupted that order, then a Saint would correct it. The Saint's own feelings did not matter.

It was easy and simple enough to understand, and Ash certainly wanted to believe it. But was it the truth? Ash wondered at that. She had wondered about it since she had first heard Lysanthia in the forest, and she wondered about it now. If it really was compulsion, then the murder was an injustice as much unto her as it was onto Ash. But if it had been choice and not compulsion, then everything Ash thought she knew was a lie.

And Orvyn had cried as she'd stabbed Ash with that dagger. Could a Class compel tears? Maybe even Lysanthia didn't know that.

Ash sighed and lifted Lysanthia's arm to reach a burn along her ribs. The Demon Queen did not stir.

Ash had been trying to find an answer for some time. Not to that question perhaps, but to a similar one. Was Ash only a Hero because of a Role? Was everyone just who the Class made them, or was there something inside of them that would have been the same no matter their class?

There were two answers. If it was yes, then Ronan's courage had been his own. Then Elowen’s generosity had been real. Then Orvyn’s gentleness hadn’t been fake. None of it was a puppet show performed for an audience of Gods who would never clap.

If it was no…Ash did not want to believe what that meant. She did not want to think about it either.

She had tried to find the answer by testing herself, day after day. By helping around the village with whatever they needed. Whether that be splitting logs or carrying weights.

The world had been simple before, and after the life she'd lived, Ash thought she had known just how it worked. There were some people who protected, and there were some people who did nothing but destroy. Ash was the first, and Lysanthia was the second. The line between them could not have been more clear.

A phoenix had come down from the sky and burned that line to nothing.

Ash had stood against the massive beast, just like she had before many beasts just like it. But unlike before, some part of her wanted to turn and run. She had even said it. And all the while, a single thought had kept coming to her, over and over again. Whose hand would hold the blade this time?

Ash had saved the world once and survived. It was the surviving afterward that had gotten her killed.

Ash had truly wanted to run. But the Demon Queen had seen a child sitting in the dirt, and she had moved while the Hero had paused. Ash had found her answer, and now she wondered if it had been worth finding at all.

The choice was real, and Lysanthia had chosen right, and Ash had chosen wrong.

Ronan's choice must have been just as real then. If goodness lived beneath a Role, then there hadn't been much of it inside her. How could she call herself a Hero after that?

Ash stared at Lysanthia's face. A face that belonged to just a woman, and not a Demon Queen. Not a woman who had committed one atrocity after another. Orvyn had looked like that too. Just a woman, doing something unforgivable.

That was the issue, wasn't it? Ash had been trying to solve Orvyn like a question that had only a single answer. It could either be her Class, or it could be her. Ash needed it to be a question with only one answer. For then she could grieve, heal, and move on. Otherwise, the wound might never close.

But Lysanthia was right in front of her. The Demon Queen. The worst Demon Queen as far as any history Ash had ever read was concerned. One who had turned cities to ash and burned villages to nothing. She was the same woman who had put herself between a beast of legend and a crying child.

One did not cancel the other. Did not even redeem the other. They were simply irreconcilable truths, resting on either side of the same scale.

Orvyn had loved her. Orvyn had killed her.

They were both true. And they were both just as true as the other. Ash did not have to choose which one was more real. That way only led to madness. Ash had to pick which truth she wanted to care about. Yes, maybe the truth was rarely ever one thing. She had to pick the parts she cared for, and sever them from the rest.

Ash winced. Heat moved through her left arm. She looked down and saw the Spark on her left tremble. Then it cracked and split, extending outwards into a single black line up her arm.

Ash stared. She had been using her mark every second she had, and only now had it actually answered her. Ash closed her eyes and picked up the cloth.

Ash blinked. She opened her eyes and saw light pressing against the shutters. How long had it been? A few hours? The whole night? She hadn't meant to fall asleep. Her back was pressed against the wall closest to the bed. When she rose, it was stiff. That was not a surprise. She looked at Lily. And there she did find a surprise.

The many burns along the woman's arms were lighter now. The burns that had been an angry red were only a tender pink now. Many of the blackened edges were now gone entirely.

Then there was the hair. It had been singed to nothing, but Ash saw dark strands pushing through the scalp now. And then there was the horn. The right one, where the tip had been sheared clean. Ash leaned closer and saw that the break was still there, but the edge had rounded.

The Demon Queen was slowly but surely healing. The Hero had never been more relieved.
