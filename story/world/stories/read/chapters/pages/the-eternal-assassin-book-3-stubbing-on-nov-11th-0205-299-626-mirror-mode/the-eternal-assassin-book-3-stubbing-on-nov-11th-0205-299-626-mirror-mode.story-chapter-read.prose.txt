Asher had gone up against quite a lot of different enemies at this point over the last couple of months he’d been in this new world. Horrific undead that came in all shapes and sizes. Assassins with a wide spread of varying elements. Even demons from another realm that fought and killed for nothing more than their own pleasure.

But there was still something about a giant wolf with his own blood dripping between its teeth chasing him into the astral realm that triggered a powerful, almost primal instinct nestled deep within his core.

With a shout, Asher threw himself to the side at the last second, narrowly avoiding having his head bitten off but still receiving a handful of painful gashes from the wolf’s sharp fur. Managing to at least turn his frantic dive into a roll, he leapt back to his feet, turning and hurling one of his steel daggers at the wolf. To his shock, the wolf flickered back into the material realm to let his dagger pass through it, before returning to the astral and growling as it stalked toward him.

Huh… So that’s what that feels like.

“What the hell are you?” Asher asked, narrowing his eyes as he unsheathed Crescent and prepared himself. Up until now, the only opponents he’d ever faced who were capable of following him into the astral realm were the greater demons, but even that wasn’t quite the same as what the wolf was doing. Greater demons existed in the material realm and astral realm at the same time, they didn’t hop back and forth. In contrast, the wolf moved like he did, capable of picking and choosing which of the two realms it wanted to be in at any given moment. He’d already gotten over his initial, instinctual fear, no doubt thanks to having fought far larger and deadlier-looking opponents. But he was still being cautious.

Asher knew better than anyone just how powerful the ability to shift between the astral and material realm on command was. Not to mention deadly.

As if to prove his point, the wolf lunged forward, shifting back to the material realm as it tried to run through him and attack from behind. Not giving it the chance, Asher shifted back as well, elongating his dagger and moving to strike the beast down. But just before it could be cleaved in two, the wolf paused almost unnaturally fast, crouching as it lowered the front of its body.

The move confused Asher for a moment, right before the incredibly sharp scorpion tail it definitely hadn’t had a moment ago snapped forward and stabbed him in the chest.

Asher gasped as the tail stabbed into him, but he managed to grit his teeth and follow through with his prepared strike, cleaving the end of the tail right off and spilling purple blood all over Richard’s fancy carpet. The wolf howled and leapt back to the edge of the room, snarling as its wounded tail continued spilling blood everywhere. Struggling to grab the tip of the tail with his one hand without dropping his dagger, Asher ripped it out of his chest with a grunt, preemptively triggering Complete Resilience’s purge effect to rid himself of any potential poison.

“You definitely didn’t have a tail a moment ago,” Asher muttered, narrowing his eyes as he waited to see what the monster would do next. He didn’t know if the beast was some sort of failed magical experiment on Richard’s part or some freaky assassination attempt that he’d just happened to stumble into while Richard was out, but either way, he was taking this thing down before it could hurt anyone else. Anything strong enough to put up a decent fight against him wasn’t something he was willing to let run loose through the city. Not to mention after eating his left hand, Asher’s own Ring of False Death was now sitting somewhere within its stomach.

He intended to get that artifact back.

Rather than go on the offensive again, the wolf surprised him a third time by biting into its own tail and ripping off the last few inches of it just as it had his own hand. The monster must have sensed his Sever the Thread skill at work, and Asher clicked his tongue as he realized hanging back and letting the thing bleed to death wasn’t going to be an option. Rather than wait for its next attack, he decided to go on the offensive, creating a tunnel of compressed space as he tried to drive Crescent straight into the wolf’s head.

The wolf vanished at the last second, and Asher cursed as he threw himself to the side without hesitation, dodging the monster’s snapping jaws as it reappeared directly in front of where he’d stood a split second ago. While he was able to see into the material realm from the astral, he couldn’t see into the astral while still in the material. Finally, Asher got an appreciation for just how terrifying fighting himself had to be for people as the wolf winked out of existence yet again as if it had never existed in the first place.

Rather than continue guessing where it was going to appear, Asher followed it this time, shifting over to the astral and watching as it raced toward him. Timing his strike just right, he lunged forward and shifted back into the material, intending to slip behind the beast and strike from behind.

It seemed the wolf had had the same idea, because the two of them popped back into the material realm at the same time, immediately crashing into one another and going down in a tangle of limbs.

Asher shouted as he was stabbed in half a dozen places from the monster’s jagged and spiky fur, but he gave as good as he got, taking advantage of the surprise contact to ram his dagger deep into the monster’s side. The wolf let out a howl that seemed to shake the very foundation of the palace itself, before kicking Asher back and putting some distance between them again.

“Let’s see you bite that one off,” Asher grinned, ignoring the spread of wounds all over his own body as they slowly began healing. This time, the wolf had a deep gash sliced straight into the flesh of its side, and its freaky, purple blood was flowing freely. It didn’t look like he’d managed to strike anything of particular importance, but blood was blood. Assuming the monster functioned like any other living thing, it would soon slow down and die even if Asher didn’t land another blow. Asher didn’t know if the wolf somehow understood that on an instinctual level or if it was simply angry at having been wounded, but it let out one more powerful howl before lowering itself into a crouch as it prepared to lunge at him once more.

Though right before it could get the chance, Richard appeared in the center of the room in a flash of brilliant purple light, his eyes panicked as he took in the state of his once pristine workshop in an instant. The moment his gaze landed on the wolf, Richard shouted.

“Bad! Bad, Mutae! Sit!”

Asher had almost reflexively stabbed the kingdom’s only wizard when Richard appeared directly in the center of his battle without warning, but he nearly dropped his dagger when Richard began barking orders at the giant wolf like it was a normal dog. Almost more shocking was the fact that the wolf listened to him, its deep growl turning more into an angry whine as it plopped its hindquarters down and snarled at Asher.

“Richard, what the hell?!” Asher demanded, keeping his eyes locked on the wolf just in case it changed its mind and decided it wanted to see if his right hand tasted as good as his left. “That thing attacked me out of nowhere when I entered your room after hearing a giant crash and seeing colorful smoke spill out from under your door! I thought it was here to kill you!”

“Damn it, Mutae,” Richard frowned, pinching the bridge of his nose as he pointed a finger at the monster. Even sitting, the wolf’s head was at the same level as their own. “I told you not to break anything while I was gone! No… no treats for a whole day!”

The wolf actually whined at that of all things, as if that truly was a meaningful punishment. Asher spent another few seconds looking back and forth between the grumbling wizard and the sad-looking monster before he shook his head and slammed his dagger back into his bandoleer.

“Richard, you’ve got about five seconds to explain what’s going on before I finish what I started. That thing took something of mine, and I want it back.” Asher added emphasis by waving the stump of his missing left hand in front of the wizard’s face, and Richard winced as he sighed.

“Yes, I see that… I’m sorry, Asher, I probably should have warned you what was going on before now, but you’ve been bouncing all over the place lately and this isn’t exactly something I have much experience with. You remember the egg you brought me back from the wild realm? Well, meet what came out of it.”

“Wait, seriously?” Asher asked, most of his anger vanishing in an instant as he turned to take in Scritchem’s offspring in an entirely new light. The egg he’d brought back had been laid when Scritchems looked like a small, fluffy deer that ate flowers.

It seemed Scritchem’s tendency to change form over time wasn’t just due to the wild realm.

“Seriously,” Richard nodded, already seeming to have forgotten his ‘punishment’ of no treats for a day as he pulled a chunk of meat from his pocket and tossed it at the large wolf. Mutae snatched it out of the air and gulped it down, his now suddenly normal tail wagging happily. “The egg only hatched a few days ago, and I’ve been desperately trying to figure out how to raise a pet. I started by trying to train it to attack anyone who entered my rooms without asking, but after seeing how that turned out when you tried to check in on me… That may have been an oversight on my part.”

“No, you think?” Asher drawled, shaking his head at Richard’s stupidity. “Richard, you live in the royal palace! This isn’t your house! What if a royal guard or any of the countless staff came in here to clean? That thing would have torn them to shreds!”

“In my defense, it was a lot smaller and more of a turtle when I left it a few hours ago,” Richard said weakly. “I wrapped it up in a blanket and told it to stay put.”

“Because young pets are so good at following instructions,” Asher said, finally remembering that he’d stabbed the thing in the side and it was still bleeding freely even while sitting there. Despite the fact that the monster didn’t seem too worried about that fact just yet, Asher cancelled his skill anyway, figuring it could probably heal itself just fine. “You need to figure out how to actually train that thing before it kills someone. And you need to get me back my Ring of False Death.”

“I can do that much,” Richard said, clearing his throat before pointing at the floor. “Mutae, give back Asher’s hand. At least for a moment.”

Whining again, the monster opened its mouth and hacked up a few times before spitting Asher’s severed hand out onto Richard’s carpet. Snatching it off the ground like it was a chew toy, Richard plucked his ring off of his finger and tossed it to him.

“You didn’t have any plans for this, did you?” he asked, waving Asher’s hand at him.

“It’s covered in bile and half digested.”

“Just thought I’d ask,” Richard said defensively, tossing the hand back to Mutae, who snatched it out of the air as his tail wagged happily. “Now, why did you come visit me in the first place?”

“We found another one of the liches, but there’s something weird going on with their minions,” Asher said, trying to ignore how Mutae was now chewing on his severed hand like the world’s freakiest chew toy. “They kidnap men from a nearby village and shove these runestones into them, causing them to grow gigantic muscles, become immune to a lot of elements, and giving them a ridiculous healing factor. I was hoping you might be able to help us figure out a way to remove them without killing the men.”

“That’s a tall ask… but I can see what I can do. I assume you brought back some of the runestones?”

“These were all the ones in a single body,” Asher confirmed, pulling them all out of his rift and dumping them straight onto the carpet. Seeing as the fancy carpet was already covered in blood, bile, and wolf slobber, he didn’t think a bit more of a mess would exactly hurt. “The man in question was taken three years ago. According to Identify and Judgement he was still in there.”

“I wouldn’t get your hopes up. But give me a couple of hours,” Richard said, giving Asher a tentative smile. “And I suppose we can count this as one of the favors you're owed…?”

“Yeah, not a chance,” Asher snorted, pointing at him with the stump of his left hand. “We’ll call this even for your new pet trying to eat me. I’ll come back once my hand’s finished regrowing. For your sake, I hope you’ll have managed to figure out something to help us by the time I do.”
