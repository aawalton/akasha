Chapter II.II (2.2)

The bits of the gunwales flew back together, reassembling itself. Mitsuko noticed that, unless she focused her intent specifically for an outcome, the Mend spell defaulted to whatever made the task the easiest on her blood reserves. So while nearby wooden wreckage replaced itself and sealed back together, pieces of wood left behind at the kraken’s attack instead reformed using the newly unlocked creation aspect of the spell. Likely because they’d sailed too far away at this point for the existing materials to be viable. A change not only in the ship’s location when it broke, but also physical distance between pieces. Still, when she focused, she could still force shards of wood to return to their position on the gunwale, but it felt to be the absolute limit of her powers. And unlike when she normally cast the spell, the effort left her light-headed.

She wished not for the first time that Sterling was here beside her. Alas, she’d fetched the ship’s cat as soon as the danger was cleared and found the feline unremarkably similar to most others of its species. It was an asshole. But just the standard cat sort of asshole. Not a thousand year old know-it-all sage kind of asshole.

“Fascinating,” Holly said beside her. She rapped her knuckles on the newly reformed gunwales. “And you said this all came to you in a dream? A spell this powerful? I’ve heard legends of mages being blessed with powers by the northern aurora in Tross, but this was just a mundane dream you experienced?”

“Yesterday I didn’t have them,” Mitsuko reassured her gnome friend, not for the first time. “I fell on my face when the barrier dropped and saw a vision of how the next week would play out. When I pushed myself back to my feet, I unlocked temporal powers.”

It was a lie. But at least one that edged around the truth. Enough that even if Holly used a truth detection spell, it likely wouldn’t trigger any sort of alarm.

“You’re certain you didn’t pick up an artifact at a shrine in Hon? Maybe the one with all those creepy jingling woodland spirits? Or maybe obtained a curse back in those catacombs we explored in Tross last month?”

Mitsuko spread her arms, gesturing at her lack of artifacts. The only one on her person was the ring she’d had for years.

Then a thought occurred to her, why was she even bothering with all the deception? Holly had been by her side for years. And while there was definitely a chance she’d melt down at the notion of only existing for seven more days, her friend could also prove to be an invaluable asset in tracking down the remaining three sages. It was a gamble of her friend’s usefulness to her objective weighed up against her mental well-being for the next week. Mitsuko closed her eyes and took a deep breath of salty sea air.

“Holly,” she finally said. “Listen to me. If you aren’t already, I want you to activate a truth detection spell right now.”

The gnome blinked. “Sure? I can do that.” She glanced at the others in the ship’s deck. “Do you want me to cast a silence bubble as well?”

Mitsuko nodded. “That might be smart. It’s better that less people know what I’m about to tell you. Regardless of my doubts that anyone else would even believe it.”

A smile touched the edges of Holly’s lips. “Damn, Mitsuko. You know how to tease a girl. One sec and I’ll have it all set up.”

She took out a knife and began to quickly etch a silence ward into a nearby crate. Mitsuko knew immediately when it completed as the entire world’s noise disappeared in an instant. Gone was the steady gust created by the ship’s elementalist. The lap of water as The Selcouth Sable cut through the sea. The gull’s cries as they circled nearby. The steps of sailors and their chatter as they worked. Everything beyond them cut out as Holly activated her spell. All that remained were Holly and Mitsuko.

“Holly. I’m going to be honest with you here. It wasn’t all a dream. I’m stuck in a timeloop, reliving the same week over and over.”

Holly’s brow furrowed and she studied Mitsuko for a long minute. The silence stretched until Mitsuko finally broke it.

“Do you have any questions?”

The words appeared to snap Holly out of her reflections, bringing her focus back to the moment.

“Yeah! What happens at the end of the week? Does it all end in death, flames, and explosions?”

“What? No. Nothing happens. The week just resets and I return to the deck at the moment the barrier appears.”

“So the trick is to just break ourselves out of this? Hm. It’s too bad we don’t have Kizu around to shatter the spatial barrier. But I’m sure there must be another way. Have you spoken to Wan? Please tell me you didn’t keep this a secret from him. He made it to the city safely, right? And how many loops have you experienced before this? Do you continue to age as the loops progress? Retain wounds?”

“It’s…more complicated than that. I’m at the center of the loops. From what I understand, my soul is what triggered it all. And everything outside of the archipelago is frozen in time. So there’s no way to contact anyone. And unfortunately Wan has been taken out of commission.” “Understood.” Holly stretched and looked skyward, at the sun locked in place overhead. “So we’re in a time dilation chamber. Only on a massive scale and you’re the focal point powering it. Do you know what happens if you die? Is everyone else freed? We need to plan accordingly if that’s true. I’m sure you already know this, but people will hunt you down if they uncover that information. They’ll try to kill you.”

“But you won’t?” Mitsuko bit her lip. Of course Holly wouldn’t try to kill her. But still…if that was the first thing that came to mind after Mitsuko’s explanation….

The purple haired gnome rolled her eyes. “Yes, Mitsuko. I’m planning to carve out your lungs and turn them into balloons. That’s why I brought the subject up. Because that way you know ahead of time all my diabolical future intentions. Now how about answering a few of the other questions I asked?”

Mitsuko sighed. “Fine. I revive on death at the start of the loop. Killing me is useless. Unless they manage to puncture my heart or shatter my soul.”

“What if that did happen?”

“Then the entire archipelago swaps its situation with the outside world and everything under the dome is frozen in time for eternity.”

“I see…any leads on how to break free of the timeloops without costing an archipelago’s worth of lives?”

“There are six sages. One for each island. I need to free them. They give me a new temporal spell when I meet with them. And they said once I master all six spells, the timeloops will end.”

“Mauve, Ashen, Vedant, Porcelain, and Amber.” Holly flicked a finger up for each as she listed them. “That’s only five. I’ve looked at a hundred different maps of the archipelago before our expedition. Granted, they were all incomplete, amateur, or completely inaccurate. But I’m fairly certain I would have noticed a sixth island on one of them.”

“It’s apparently hidden.”

“Are you sure you’re not thinking about Amber Island? I’ve heard it’s going to be a bitch to map out since it’s mobile.”

“No. The sixth island is known as Azure.”

“Interesting. And one of these ancient sages told you about it? Do you know where it’s hidden?”

“No. They wouldn’t say.”

“You could ask now though,” a new voice spoke up. “You do have four questions available from your recent levels.”

Mitsuko whirled around, ready to summon a blade. And saw no one. Not a soul nearby, let alone within the radius of Holly’s silence bubble. Then she processed the words spoken. And placed the voice.

“Juniper?”

“Yes, poppet?” A seagull shifted and made eye contact with Mitsuko. While its feathers were a mundane gray and white, the eyes glowed with an unnatural brown hue. Easily overlooked with the daylight streaming in from above.

“So you can defy the boundaries of Holly’s spell?” Mitsuko asked the bird. “Can you allow her to hear you so she can be let in on our conversation?”

“Unfortunately, that’s impossible unless your friend happens to secretly be a master soul mage. You and I are connected via our souls by the creation of the Prismatic Spiral. We’re linked in an entirely unique manner. As are the guardians. Which is why you’ve likely heard them speak even without your ears attached. There is sadly no known means in which to tie your friend into the Prismatic Spiral, thus she’ll never be able to hear us speak.”

Mitsuko scowled. She recognized that information dump for what it was. “You just stole one of my questions,” she accused the gull.

“Poppet, it’s not theft when I gave you something of equal value.”

“If I rob a shop and leave a sack of unwanted grain, it’s still robbery.”

“Mitsuko, are you talking to that bird?” Holly asked.

“Yes. Holly, please meet the Sage of Deterioration. Juniper. Do you have a last name Juniper? Or are you going to take another of my allotted questions because I asked?”

“Fauns have one name only,” Juniper replied. “We decide them ourselves soon after our births. We choose based on what draws us in as individuals. As an infant, I found the juniper bushes called to me, and so my family acknowledged me and dubbed me, Juniper.”

“Is that insightful information costing me a question?” Mitsuko repeated.

“Not this time.” If seagulls could smile, she certainly was in that moment.

“It’s a pleasure to meet you, Juniper!” Holly said. “Please ignore my grump of a pal, Mitsuko. You’ve caught her in a bit of a foul mood. Might have something to do with you imprisoning us and forcing her to confront unbeatable foes. I also doubt she’s particularly fond of the idea of everyone, including myself, being obliterated at the end of every week, but nevermind that! I don’t suppose you can chat with me?”

“She can’t,” Mitsuko replied. “She just confirmed as much.”

Holly tugged a strand of her purple hair as she examined the bird. “What about if I was a mental mage. Could I read Mitsuko’s mind and hear your responses using her as a conduit?”

The seagull blinked. “Yes. I suppose that would be hypothetically possible. Are you such a mage?”

“She’s not,” Mitsuko said. “But that’s good to know.”

One of the sailors came by and attempted to shoo Juniper away, waving their arms. The bird blinked at them, amused.

“Is she not afraid they’ll kill her?” Holly asked Mitsuko.

“No. She can remove her soul from the bird at a moment’s notice. Then she’ll be back again in a new body. It’s only an inconvenience if there are no easy bodies accessible.”

“Enough about me,” Juniper said, flapping her wings for extra umph as she hopped from the gunwales over to the crate beside Mitsuko. “You now have access to my spell. We should waste no time discussing my spell’s functions. Why don’t we begin with you reviewing what you know of it so far.”

The sailor, now seeing Mitsuko’s attention on the bird, slowly backed away, giving up his attempt to ride the ship of the pest. After seeing Mitsuko rebuild the ship before their eyes, most of the crew had been giving her a wide berth.

“Holly, the sage wants me to chat about the spell she specializes in,” Mitsuko said to her friend. “Any complaints?”

“No, no. You’ve told me that cooking the bird will only kill the poor thing it inhabits, so I’m at a loss at how to proceed in my efforts of destruction."

“Don’t be dramatic. You don’t even eat meat,” Mitsuko pointed out.

“If there’s a time to be dramatic, I believe this is it,” Holly replied. “But yes, I wouldn’t harm the bird. Especially not to eat. Though I might make an exception if I could just cook the soul.”

“Gnomes continue the tradition of vegetarianism?” Juniper asked. “That’s lovely to know! I’m glad that the world retains some of the more inspiring bits of culture. Fauns always felt a kinship with the gnome species.”

Mitsuko considered telling the sage that nearly the entire gnome population had been decimated by a genocide two decades ago and only a scattering population remained, but decided against diving into that conversation. Better to focus on the spell.

“Deteriorate is the counter spell to Sterling’s Mend,” Mitsuko said, summarizing her knowledge of the spell. “While Mend restores objects to their previous state of existence, ultimately fixing them, Deteriorate accelerates an object’s state of being. It's destruction in the form of a spell.”

“The future does not only hold ruin,” Juniper said. “Beauty takes place at all stages of existence, not only the past. But, yes, I admit that my spell has far more offensive potential than a spell like Mend or Retrospection.”

“I see. So it looks into the future and rapidly makes that future a reality. But I can stop it at any time, cutting it off when it's at a stage where I want it to be. It doesn’t have to end in destruction.”

“How does the spell know what fate holds in store for its subject?” Holly asked. Despite her clear frustration with the sage, Holly was engrossed in the one side of Mitsuko’s conversation she could hear. “Even us diviners struggle with finding any sort of solid answers when wrestling with future predictions.”

“Fate is not reliable,” Juniper agreed. “My spell simply takes the most basic route forward based on the object’s current trajectory. Looking to the future is too malleable for us to fully comprehend without error.”

“Hm.” Mitsuko repeated the statement to her friend while she considered what was said. “Then I’m assuming that at higher levels I might be able to instead influence that version of Deterioration in different directions. Different paths through the future.” She made certain not to phrase that guess as a question, and as such, Juniper remained mum on the topic.

“Can you show me it?” Holly asked. “I saw you use your other spell earlier to save the ship.”

“Yeah. I can.” Mitsuko picked a ribbon out of Holly’s hair. Then she cast Deterioration on it. Nothing happened.

“It’s unlikely that anything will happen to that bit of cloth in the near future,” Juniper explained. “Do you recall when you first unlocked your Mend spell? You couldn’t turn time back far at first. This is simply the opposite. Sadly, most things remain unchanged in the world for the short term.”

That made sense.

Mitsuko let the ribbon slip from her fingers and fall to the deck.

“Come on,” she said, stepping over it. “Let’s head to the kitchens and grab some grub.”

“But you didn’t show me the spell,” Holly said, crouching down to examine the bit of unchanged cloth.

“I’ll show you once we get there.”

Before leaving, Mitsuko set her hand on the ward of silence Holly had carved into the wooden crate. A quick Mend, and all that remained was a smooth plank. The spell nullified as if never cast.
