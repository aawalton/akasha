Chapter V.XXIII (5.23) - Comet

“Kizu never told me about a haunted mansion!” Aoi cried out.

“Slipped my mind,” Kizu mumbled.

She turned and gave him an accusatorial glare. “Vincent lives alongside a permanently bound spirit? How could that possibly slip your mind when talking to me? I’m a necromancer! It’s a massive part of my entire personality.”

Kizu ignored the glowering princess and gloomily examined the griffins as they walked through the stables. All around them the riders were grooming and taking care of their mounts. Despite the violent nature of the race, all the participants appeared deeply concerned for the griffins’ wellbeing.

Well, until they reached Star’s stall. His rider, Carlton, was passed out in the hay amongst the griffin droppings. He slept in an awkward angle, his hips pointing up towards the roof and forming a triangle out of his body. Star eyed the man with contempt. He looked to be debating whether or not to stomp on his rider’s spine and end the man. He kept raising a paw before slowly drawing it back. Kizu quickly came to the conclusion that this was an even worse nap spot than he’d first assumed. And his first assumption had been… very bad.

“Should we wake him?” Basil asked. “Congratulate him on a victory?”

“I say let him sleep,” Ione said with a dismissive wave of the hand. “All praise should be directed at this beauty.”

Ione approached and raised a hand to touch the griffin’s neck feathers. The beast’s eagle head swiveled to focus on her. Its glare for Carlton shifted to a narrow-eyed suspicion for Ione.

“I don’t recommend you take Star as your prize,” Vincent said. “He truly does not like other people. It would be a dangerous choice and I’d rather not have Gizrim’s secretary hunting me down about an escaped griffin causing havoc on the island.”

Ione slowly lowered her hand, clearly put out.

“Do you have a different recommendation?” Kizu asked on her behalf.

“Yes, I do!” Vincent replied. “Follow me, just around the corner to the next stall.”

Ione looked conflicted about leaving Star, but she was still the first to peak around the corner.

“A baby!”

Kizu screamed internally but he managed to keep a neutral face. With a heavy heart, he also stepped around the corner.

A baby griffin, with big eyes and black feathers gaped up at him. It clacked its beak together. The tail swished back and forth. Not a lion's tail though. In fact, the entire body was different from a lion. Instead, it was more horse-like. Kizu frowned, was this even a griffin?

“A hippogriff,” Ione whispered excitedly.

“Where’s its parents?” Basil asked. “Looks lonely.”

“You just saw one of them,” Vincent said. “Griffins have a habit of eating their young if they get annoyed and Star is far from winning any father of the year awards. After this one’s brother got eaten, we confiscated her from Star’s presence."

“What’s her name?” Aoi asked.

“Comet. We wanted to stick to an off-world theme with Star’s family. But she’s still young enough that you could rename her if you chose to take her.”

“Kizu is definitely choosing her,” Ione said.

“Am I?” Kizu said dryly. “I don’t recall—”

“If you don’t, I’ll tell Anata,” Ione threatened. “We both know how she’ll react to you turning down a baby flying creature.”

Kizu’s eye twitched. “I thought you wanted Star. He’s clearly magical.”

“Again, I would greatly prefer you don’t create issues on the island,” Vincent said.

“You don’t think a baby hippogriff will cause issues?”

“Those are small issues. Not biting heads off, issues.”

“Star is gorgeous,” Ione admitted. “But his offspring is equally interesting. Especially if I can get ahold of Comet’s pedigree and record of Star’s development. Hippogriffs are so rare!”

Vincent clapped his hands and a stable boy dashed holding a mound of bound papers. The boy glanced over at Comet who opened her beak and made a guttural clicking noise. The boy winced and quickly held out a small binder to Vincent. Instead, Ione snatched it up and swiftly flipped through.

For a stable boy, he certainly didn’t look very comfortable around any of the large winged creatures as he edged away from the hippogriff. The boy licked his lips nervously and looked from Ione, to Kizu, to Vincent, to Basil. Basil made a face at the boy, changing his eyes to glow an ominous red, splitting open his cheeks, and lengthening his tongue to lull out of a disjointed jaw. The boy yelped and scurried away. Vincent turned his head at the sharp noise but Basil had already snapped his jaw back into place and now had an innocent look on his face. Aoi elbowed her boyfriend, quietly reprimanded his uncouth behavior while holding back snickers.

Kizu frowned at the exchange. He had his potions on hand. For a moment he was sorely tempted to splash a bit of adhesive to the bottom of Basil’s boots. But before he could decide whether or not to retaliate and claim vengeance for the poor boy, Ione flipped to the next page of the binder and spoke up.

“You’re certain that Star is the father?” she asked as she read, blissfully unaware of Basil’s antics. “It says here there are two other potential fathers. Why are you assuming it's Star? Why not blood test them?”

“Griffin blood responds differently to testing than humans,” Vincent explained. “We narrowed it down to those three males listed in there, but the unique magical attributes in their blood made it particularly difficult to probe further. It is the same for many magical and Awakened creatures. But trust me, Star is definitely the father.”

“Hmm.” Ione nodded along. “I’ve read about that. But how did they end up in a situation where they could breed with a horse to begin with? Did they escape? Surely a breeder wouldn’t just let them all frolic together unsupervised?”

Vincent sighed. “That whole debacle was a mess. An unleashed race where a score of griffins escaped up into the mountains. After funding a nationwide search and rescue campaign, we found them huddled in a valley, half-starved, a week later.”

“Can we back up?” Kizu asked. “This baby is a hippogriff. The father is a griffin. And the mother is a… horse?”

“That’s how hippogriffs are produced,” Ione confirmed. “It rarely happens. Most griffins are far too proud to breed with mundane animals like horses.”

“Yes,” Vincent agreed. “It’s not something you can force. Many breeders have tried and failed.”

“Follow up question, why would you want a hippogriff?” Kizu asked.

“Besides their absolutely gorgeous appearance? I imagine breeders want them because they’re better for riding and faster steeds,” Ione said. “Though the expert is welcome to correct me if I’m wrong.”

Vincent shook his head. “You are correct. I personally don’t want a single hippogriff because there’s nothing to be done with it. It won’t get along with the griffins. And it’s disqualified from racing in a competition designed for griffins. I could still sell it to a collector or menagerie owner for a pretty yennie. But I’d rather she go to a good home with people who genuinely care. I hardly need the funds, after all.”

Kizu massaged his temples. “I’m a teenager. I am the furthest thing from a ‘good home.’ Why are you insistent on trusting me with a baby rare magical creature?”

He stared at Comet. The black hippogriff had taken notice of them and clacked its beak, begging for food. Ione complied, serving it a handful of finger-sized worms that were in a jar nearby. Ione giddily dropped them overhead and Comet snatched them out of the air as they fell. The hippogriff gobbled them up, happy as can be. It clacked its beak, begging for more, and Ione complied.

Vincent stepped forward and clapped a hand on Kizu’s shoulder. Beyond the monocle, his eyes were stern, lacking the usual twinkle there.

“You’re more than just a teenager. You’re Anna’s brother.”
