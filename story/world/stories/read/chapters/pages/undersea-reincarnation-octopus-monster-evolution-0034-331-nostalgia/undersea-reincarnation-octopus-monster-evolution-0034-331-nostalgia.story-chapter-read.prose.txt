The festivities lasted past dawn. It had started with plenty of foods and drinks, then they ran out of food and Henry just started pulling eels, which were quickly seasoned then grilled before they were distributed and then were washed down with even more liquor.

Of course not everyone stayed until dawn. Plenty went down after a couple of hours, especially the younger ones, and while Henry would have been tempted to slink away after a couple of hours himself, he found himself genuinely having a good time socializing, and whenever he got a little tired of it, he’d take a peek through his clones and see what stretch of the ocean they were exploring.

Surprisingly enough, it didn’t take him long to start seeing some familiar fauna. Then some familiar geological formations.

Henry hadn’t expected to see the familiar grounds he’d once hunted in so soon. The recovered shoal of Nautiloids swam around casually, and Henry noted there were plenty of small ones being carefully guarded by the larger individuals. Mostly in D-rank, though a few had already broke through to C-rank, then he looked past them at the cliff side that marked the edge of the Sanctuary—what he’d come to learn were pockets of safety and low-energy zones scattered within the Pillars.

Maybe it was because his clones were much stronger than he’d had been back then and he could just afford to swim in a straight line. Maybe it was the use of Control Water and his larger size that made crossing the distance much faster. But he was here already.

It barely took him a day of travel to reach the Sanctuary in which it had all started. And it might take him a couple more to reach that first reef, but Henry didn’t have to supervise the whole process himself.

Still, Henry wondered if he was close enough for an attempt at divining the whales’ location. With a shrug, he ordered his navigator-Octomind to try and locate whales, just as Velistraine dragged him to talk about some underwater ruins she’d once encountered in her travel.

If it still doesn’t work, it might be worth trying to reach the calving grounds, he thought as he left his Octominds to take care of the rest. That space was significant enough to them that it might help the divination. But just in case, he also allowed the navigator to burn one of Maurice’s Curiosity shards, which should bolster Find the Path even further.

With that done, Henry fully turned his attention back to the party and to apologetic smiles Velistraine’s crew were giving him.

It’d be nice to unwind for once.

***

A few hours after dawn and after the party was officially over, Velistraine announced that they’d set sail in three days. Enough time for everyone to relax a little more, restock and make their last preparations.

For Henry, it meant he had three days to finish up the couple of items he wanted to take care of. His earlier attempt at divination hadn’t worked but all hope wasn’t lost. He was very close to getting a clone to the area he’d arrived in, and he was confident his attempt at the calving ground would fare better. Still, on the way there, he had hoped to hear the familiar clicks, but there was no trace of the dolphins. Not even when he managed to locate their old home.

It wasn’t that he planned on making too big of a stink, but he did want to slap Sundots around a couple of times and thank him for that send-off he’d graced them with. Unfortunately, the cave network was deserted, and Henry assumed one thing or another had forced them to migrate away. In any case, that wasn’t really a big deal. What was a big deal was that one of his Trickster’s Shadows had finally reached the algae forest, and within the hour, it was looking down on a field of golden, swaying algae.

The calving grounds where he’d once met the whales.

Rolling hills of swaying algae stretched ahead of him, and Henry took a minute to just admire it. It was nostalgic, being here once more. There was no trace of the Bahamut whales, as expected, but curiously, just like every other spot he’d crossed with his Shadows, the area was a lot more active than how he’d left it. It was teeming with life.

Either everything came to the Sanctuary to reproduce during the wave, or everything instinctively got out of the Current and came here. Either way… this is likely the Wave’s doing, 100%, Henry thought.

Swimming around, Henry could see massive shoals of sharks roaming the space, casting their shadows down on the seabed. In the tall algae, small serpents peeked their heads, watching for any prey that might get close enough for an ambush and occasionally, they’d dip below the grass and disappear as a shark or another predator would get near for a closer look. Henry even noticed a handful of curious juvenile kraken watching his clones here and there. One of them—an Arcane kraken—had even swum out of the algae forest to approach one of the floating Shadows.

“[Shoo,]” Henry sent it, and it shuddered, colors and limbs rippling with magic. The telepathy scared it off a little, and it slunk away, but it never left. It was still watching him, invisible, though to his mana-seeing eyes, he could see the excess Invisibility produced. It wafted off of the kraken, clearly outlining its silhouette to his eyes.

Was that why his own Invisibility had been so easy to see through? Immediately, set an Octomind on casting the skill with minimal waste.

Henry looked away from the kraken and lowered the Shadow to the seabed. Nestling himself on a rock, he took a moment to really connect with the place before he gave the Octomind the go-ahead once more.

“If this place’s not enough of connection to find them, then we’ll just hope for an eventual lucky encounter,” he told it. Without further ado, he stepped back and let the navigator take control of the Shadow, and Henry watched over its shoulder. For a boost, he channeled a dollop of Thaumaturgy to the Octomind, and as the water around the Shadow began to ripple with magic and mana, Henry held his breath.

He really hoped it’d work.

Henry had barely begun peering back at the curious beings that had been attracted to the new intruder when a gold, cord-like thread surged into existence in his mind’s eye. It stretched infinitely in front of him, and Henry grinned.

He just had to follow it to find the whales. That had been easier than he had expected.

I guess I have a way toward them now. Just need to not lose this instance, he thought, reaching into his Maw for a small, pale pearl. Closing his eyes, he summoned the bead in the beak of the Shadow that held the nexus for Find the Path, then slowly nudged it toward the bead.

The next was a little stubborn at first, but as Arisia had shown him, the small enchanted beads were really good at accepting half-formed skills. The nexus slipped into the bead, and the enchantments of the object captured most of the skill instance. The golden path to the whales disappeared, but one simple activation of Find the Path while focusing on the bead was enough to pull it back up.

Just like the orbs behaved or that anchor Zerathstra had shown him, this bead should know always point him toward the whales—so long as they didn’t oppose it, that was. But just in case, he—or more accurately, his clone—swooped down toward the algae. Henry gently cut a handful off, scooped some sand and rocks, and after encasing everything in a round bony-orb, he cut off the limb and popped it in storage.

I don’t know if those would work as well as being here… but it’s worth it. Though there’s something else I could do, he mused. The issue was that he was leaving Thalis soon, so he wasn’t sure he’d have the range to peer into this area for a while. But his Shadows—thanks to the recent upgrades—would not die off just because he got too far. They’d just be disconnected until he could regain contact. He could spread a few out in the area, hide them well and leave them dormant. As his abilities and Aspect gain further level, sooner or later he’d regain contact with the area. That way, if the whales were to come back this way, he could just pop in and say hi.

Yeah, Henry thought. That should work.

Straining, Henry located a few redundant Shadows along the way and dispelled them before recreating them here. Then he spread them about, located some good hiding spots for all ten of them—one of which he hid in the old Trickster’s home—then he was about to send his awareness back to Thalis when he heard a click through one of then ten he was about to leave.

Henry froze, then smoothly moved his consciousness to the clone in question. It was the closest one to the mana-stealing jellyfish territory, where once him and Maurice had fought a C-rank Telekinetic Eel.

Leaving his Octominds to finish hiding the other clones, Henry looked around through the clone and listened. The area was just as scenic as last time, with lush reefs and a swarm of slowly floating, luminescent jellyfish. But Henry couldn’t focus on that too much, and it took a few more seconds until he heard it again. Though it was not close by, Henry was curious. Was this something new, or was it the dolphins?

Only one way to figure it out, he thought, looking down at the clone’s arm. With a flex, he shaped a couple into stick-like, hollow bones, then he slapped them together a few times, waiting a few seconds before every echoing tap. He heard no clicks in response, but he didn’t lose hope and just waited. Sitting on top of a rock, he didn’t hide himself, though he did create another Shadow and stuffed it in a nearby cave, and nearly five minutes later, he heard the first approaching click. Then another.

They were coming, and as Henry worked on his disguise and altered his mana-signature, he wondered what—or who—he was about to see.
