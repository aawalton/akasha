Valanor and his unlikely companion had been shadowing the strange army for nearly two weeks. They both recognized the Dark affinity Familiars used by the unusual creatures leading it, but were utterly perplexed by the rest. Hidden among the rocks and brush on a nearby peak, the brothers stared down at the group slowly marching through the dense mountain range.

“Have you ever seen anything like it?” Valanor asked.

“Perhaps if you ask enough times, my answer will change,” Savilar snapped back. “No, brother, I have not. Those are demons. Dozens of them, all Twilight rank, and completely smothered by those Dark Familiars. I’ve seen the Dark-born before, but I’ve never seen this.”

Valanor grunted. He knew he’d asked the question before. He might ask it again–his mind was clouded at best, thanks to the continuous exposure to Potentia’s corruption. He just couldn’t make sense of what he was seeing, and it was infuriating.

The ground rumbled with yet another earthquake. Valanor didn’t like that either.

“Do you know where we are?” Savilar asked.

Valanor turned to him, eyebrow raised. “We’ve been lost in these damn mountains for months. I’ve never known where we are.”

The demon smirked, then pointed at the peak the army was working its way up. “That is the western border of Viridus,” he said. “At least it would be, on Nexum. We’ve made it.”

Valanor leaned back, eyes wide, and he nearly stood up. Savilar’s clawed hand fell on his shoulder, making sure their position wasn’t given away.

“We’re here,” he whispered. His hand drifted to his storage rune, though he knew the Church’s relic would do little good at the moment. Then he paused, and turned back to his brother. “Can you feel it?”

There was no need to explain what “it” was. Savilar nodded readily. “The Throne is over there. It’s not much more than a faint instinct for me… Truthfully, it pushes me away more than it draws me. I’m not ready for it.”

“But we’ve found it,” Valanor said. “Maybe I truly can complete my mission.”

The demon eyed him. “Perhaps you truly can tell me your mission. Finally.”

Valanor grinded his teeth. He was still conflicted about the idea. Savilar had fought by his side for months, but he was still a demon. Even before he’d been a demon, he’d been a traitor.

“You were wrong,” he said mechanically.

Savilar rolled his eyes. “I was right,” he said dismissively. “Now, why are you here, little brother? And no more nonsense about hunting gods.”

Valanor glared. “The union is tearing our world apart,” he said. The words felt like they were being pulled from him, demanding to get out. “We need to move the Union forward. That means finding Dusk. The Church believed The Throne holds the answers to finding him.”

Savilar’s cold demon eyes widened in shock. “You were serious?” he demanded, disbelief obvious. “You bloody madman, Valanor! Just what do you think you’ll do even if we do find Dusk?”

Valanor blinked. “We?”

Savilar actually roared. “Yes, ‘we’, you stubborn fool! You think I’ll leave you to die now? Goddess knows you’d deserve it after doubting me this long.”

Valanor let out a long breath, then crawled back until he could stand without being seen. He looked over at his brother, the man transformed into a demon, yet somehow still a man.

“Let’s go, Sav.”

The journey from Bastion to The Throne was easily one of the most unusual experiences of Ethan’s life. After some time for his Spirit to recover and drain ambient corruption, he was able to power up Fable enough to create an enclosed platform large enough for the entire fighting population of the remaining Nator. That just left the several day journey north, riding the remarkably fast Champion rank sea-serpent.

It had been strange, watching the miles disappear as they slithered toward the seat of Dusk’s power. Ethan could feel The Throne like a second heartbeat now, a burning beacon of light leading them directly toward the circle of mountains he’d first appeared in, so long ago.

Fast as they were going, it was still a fair journey, surrounded by hundreds of seven-foot tall Nator. And while they were heading toward an unknown battle, one from which they might not return, anticipation and tension couldn’t hold out against the seemingly-endless hours of travel.

Soon they were bored, and confined, and frustrated. First they played games, then they argued, then they fought. Selina and Vetta worked tirelessly to keep the unruly group focused, with mixed results.

However powerful the group might be, these weren’t truly warriors, and the likelihood of their own deaths did nothing for their patience. After the first day, Ethan had closed himself off in a private room near the front of the platform. From there, he watched the red wasteland rush by through the windows, trying to ready himself mentally for what was to come–while ignoring the queasy feeling that came with riding an enormous, twisting snake.

His duplicates would join him for short times, but they were infected by his dark mood, and wisely gave him space. By the third day, even Selina had had enough of her newly discovered people, and came to join him, leaning against the window with a curious expression.

“Why am I not more confident?” she asked after a time.

Ethan turned to her. “What do you mean?”

Selina gestured around them. “We’ve managed to get a Champion rank demon on our side. We should be unbeatable. We should be grinning, excited to crush the Followers and take Dusk’s power. Why don’t I feel it?”

Ethan nodded. He knew the feeling. “We’ve been at this too long,” he said. “We know that nothing comes easily, and I get the sense that the Emperor has as much riding on this as we do. I can’t imagine him leaving the Earth behind if he had any other option.”

Selina sighed. “I was hoping you’d convince me this would be easy,” she teased.

“Oh, it’s going to be so easy,” Ethan said with a grin. “We’ll just coast in there, take what’s ours, and be done by dinnertime. Hell, dinner will be served on a brand new world, full of nothing but peace and joy.”

Selina smiled back. “I want burgers!”

Ethan smirked. “That is an absurd meal for this level of celebration.”

“I disagree! Not only are burgers delicious, but this is going to be easy, remember? It’s not worth waiting for a table somewhere fancy.”

Ethan chuckled, but his eyes were drawn back to the window. He could feel the elevation rising, and The Throne getting close. Alarmingly close.

“We’re almost there,” he whispered.

That sobered Selina immediately. “I wish we still had Allyara,” she said. “Or even Steelbinder.”

Ethan shrugged. “Steelbinder had nothing left to give. Both Champions gave everything they had to get us this opportunity. Now we just need to make good use of it.”

He closed his eyes, and it seemed like he could still see the blazing light guiding him to their destination. Finally he opened them again.

“Come on,” he said to Selina. “This is a view to remember.” He made a gesture, and Fable knew what he wanted.

Abruptly, the ceiling opened above them, and rails rose up in a circle around the pair. A moment later they were rising upward on a platform of steel.

Ethan couldn’t see much more than mountains in any direction. They’d been moving through the Rockies for some time, and the red peaks were everywhere now. Still, he didn’t need to see very far to know what they were coming up on.

“This is the equivalent of the southern mountains in Viridus,” he said.

“That’s where I grew up,” Selina said, eyes searching.

They kept climbing, the Leviathan demonstrating its nearly limitless energy as it moved higher into the mountains. Then, seemingly all at once, the path up became a path down, and Ethan laid eyes on the sight of their final battle.

The ring of mountains spread out before him, creating something like an enormous red bowl. While there were blotches of yellow and brown where the living forests writhed, it was the glowing blue ring that dominated the sight. The massive rift was right where Ethan remembered, casting its light in every direction.

Memories flashed through Ethan. Climbing that rise, rushing away from demons, confronting king koncrete. It was all done in the pale blue light of that daunting portal.

Yet there was more to see. A shockingly deep trench had been carved around the rift, and the landscape still bore the damage and evidence of the battle between Chosen Champions. Beyond that was an expected sight that still made his eyes narrow in fury.

The Follower army was already here, and waiting.

Groups of a dozen or so Twilights were gathered together, drenched in black and with a general hovering over them ominously. As the Leviathan moved forward, the army became easier to see, and Ethan found himself glancing around furiously, trying to find the Emperor…and Prince Calevaro.

“I don’t see–” Selina began, likely thinking the same as Ethan. But she was interrupted by a powerful earthquake, one so large it shook even the Champion rank serpent.

Then everything became clear all at once. Ethan knew where the Emperor was, and he knew why the Followers had rushed here–and also why The Throne remained empty. Ethan could feel within himself that no one would take Dusk’s power, not yet, not while there were two contenders.

The earthquake wasn’t natural, it was the ground opening up as something rose up from the depths. Something painfully familiar to Ethan.

First he saw legs, hairy legs, and far too many of them, dragging a weighty body up from the sandy ground. Then came an enormous tail, revealed to be a deadly stinger. Before the horrific head had even appeared, Ethan was letting out a sigh, and Selina made a gagging sound. Ethan reached over to pat her back as he shook his head.

“Well. That is, beyond any doubt, the worst thing I have ever seen.”

The Emperor had found his Champion. A scorpiursi.
