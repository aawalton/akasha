An uncomfortable silence settled among the cave.

Ace and his team stood around Rachel’s crudely drawn map, each staring down at it as they lost themselves in thought. Ace had a feeling that Victor would be back, and he didn’t know what he would do if the man returned.

The more powerful Victor became, the more dangerous he was to the others—and, frankly, to Ace. The man was leveling so quickly, from the sound of it, that Ace didn’t even know what humanity Victor had left in him.

That was assuming there was any to begin with, of course.

Olivia shook her head in annoyance. "He's not going to kill us, guys."

"Are you sure about that?" Ace asked, his brow furrowing.

Their resident dance-murderer frowned. Her gaze shifted back to the map, but she didn't answer. Something flickered across her face—doubt, perhaps, or recognition. Olivia had been spending more time alone lately, practicing her bloody ballet in the shadows when she thought no one was watching.

He made a mental note to keep a closer eye on her. The line between ally and enemy seemed thinner with each passing day.

"The Crucible is suicide," Rachel interjected.

"So is sitting here while everything around us gets stronger," Tara shot back.

“Calm down, and let’s focus,” Ace said. “We need to make some decisions. Where we go next could Level us to where we need to be to survive the Crucible."

"Does it really matter where we go?" Marcus laughed sharply, the sound lacking any humor at all. "We're all turning into monsters bit by bit anyway."

No one spoke, but that didn't change anything—even if it was the truth.

The sprawling world before them was rife with options. Ace studied each sector of Floor 1, marveling at the complexity of the massive world the System had created—and all within just one small sector of her domain.

According to her, there were six more to go.

"This doesn't feel like a dungeon Floor," Tara said quietly.

"Truth," Marcus commented.

"You have a point," Ace agreed. "With this many ecosystems? Floor 1 on its own is an entire world."

The thought struck him as odd.

Wrong, somehow.

Just… off.

From the encounters they’d had with the System thus far, however, she had made a few mistakes. She had even made mention of this before, calling this her world instead of her Floor. It had felt like a slip up when she said it, and just like now, it had felt strange to hear her say it out loud.

Important, even—like she had given away something she shouldn't have.

The sergeant shook his head to clear his thoughts. He had to focus.

"We just came from the forest," he said, his finger hovering over the Gloomvale Woods region. "But maybe we take the risk of looping back to where we just were.”

“That’s the opposite direction of where the Crucible Map is taking us,” Marcus pointed out. “What good is it to go back?”

“Besides,” Rachel added. “It’s been suspiciously quiet these last few days. Something isn’t right in there, and I don’t want to figure out what it is.”

“We've survived it once,” Ace pointed out. “Higher-Level monsters mean faster advancement. It doesn't seem like there's much for us in the Highlands."

"No." Tara's response was sharp and immediate. "We're not ready for another Thornhide. We need to build coordination first and establish a rhythm to our fighting style."

"The forest has what we need," Ace countered. "Enhanced shadow connection for my abilities. Defensive EXP Solutes for—"

"It nearly killed us last time," Tara interrupted. "We need to go after manageable targets first. Grind against creatures we can reliably take down. Perfect our tactics before escalating to bigger prey."

She placed her hand on his arm, and Ace was surprised at how naturally the gesture came. More surprising was how he didn't pull away.

"Remember when the Thornhide charged?" she continued, keeping her voice low. "We all scattered. No coordination, no plan. You went left, I went right, Marcus froze completely, and Rachel nearly got trampled. Only Olivia had any sense of positioning."

Ace remembered. The chaos, the screaming, the desperate scramble to avoid being crushed. They'd survived by luck more than skill.

"She's right," Olivia added unexpectedly. All eyes turned to her. She twirled her staff absently, its tip tracing patterns in the dirt. "I've watched you all fight. No grace. No rhythm. No awareness. Like children with knives."

“Damn, Olivia,” Marcus said under his breath. “Don’t sugarcoat it or anything.”

Their Blood Dancer just shrugged.

Marcus raised his newly cleared arms. "In case anyone's forgotten the fine print—every kill comes with side effects. This shit isn't just cosmetic. Before we killed those Forgeborn, it was getting deeper every day, spreading through muscle tissue, reorganizing nerve endings. I could feel it... thinking."

"And that was just what we can see," Marcus continued, voice dropping. "The real changes were happening inside. My dreams weren’t even mine anymore. I saw forests I've never visited, creatures I've never fought." He looked around the group, his expression uncharacteristically serious. "We need to be damn sure whatever we kill next is worth becoming."

Well, now.

That was a somber way to put it.

A heavy silence fell across the cave. Ace studied his squad's faces as he debated on how to reply. Each vampire seemed to be contemplating their own transformations, and no one met his eye.

Sharper canines. Enhanced night vision. The growing hunger for violence that whispered beneath through their unconscious minds. They were slowly becoming what they killed, one deliberate sacrifice at a time—just like the System wanted.

Tara examined her hands. The skin had taken on a faint iridescence after their battle with the Crystal Wolves, catching light in ways that weren't quite human.

Rachel touched her temple, where small, hard bumps had formed just beneath the skin—the beginnings of what, though, he couldn't yet tell.

Olivia alone seemed unbothered, perhaps even pleased with her transformation. Her movements had become increasingly fluid, almost liquid in their grace.

"Rachel," Ace said, ignoring the weight of all the unspoken questions lingering on the air. "Did the System mention where we can find any low-Leveled monsters? Something we can kill en-masse?"

"Well, there's the Corrosive Slugs," Rachel said. "In the forest two miles south. Low threat individually, dangerous in numbers. No idea what the EXP Solute will be though."

"Slugs," Marcus said with a hint of disgust. "Am I going to start oozing, next?"

Tara grimaced. "God, I hope not."

"What do you know about them?" Ace asked, ignoring their commentary.

"Not much,” Rachel admitted. “Just that they secrete acid that can dissolve organic matter. They're not particularly intelligent, so I guess that’s a plus.”

"That sounds too easy," Olivia observed.

"Maybe," Ace replied. “But Tara’s right. If we approach the slugs correctly, we get a chance to establish our fighting dynamic and practice working together.”

"Plus," Marcus added, "if they're acid-based, developing resistance to corrosives could be useful."

“If we’re lucky,” Rachel muttered. “If not, we’ll start oozing goop when we walk.”

In unison, Tara and Marcus scrunched their noses in disdain.

Ace considered the idea. Deliberately choosing their physical evolution path was a strategy they hadn't discussed before, mainly because it carried a lot of unknown variables. Fighting slugs might give them acid resistance, enhanced perception, better battlefield awareness—all attributes that would serve them well against bigger threats later.

Or, like Rachel had said, it might make them leak goop.

At this point, it was about a fifty-fifty chance.

"Let's put it to a vote," Ace suggested. "Corrosive Slugs? Or do we look for something else?"

"Slugs," Tara said without hesitation.

"Slugs," Rachel echoed, "but with extreme caution."

Marcus sighed dramatically. "Fine. Slugs. But if I start leaving slime trails, I'm going to smother all of you in your sleep.”

Tara elbowed him hard in the side, and Marcus huffed as the air was kicked out of him.

Ace’s attention shifted toward the dancer at the edge of their circle. "Olivia?"

She twirled her staff once more, then planted it firmly in the dirt. "I’m in.”

"Then it's decided." He stood.

“So… what’s next?” Marcus asked, still rubbing his side where Tara had elbowed him. “Are we going to run drills or something?”

“Not a bad idea,” Ace admitted. “We’ve all been practicing our Skills. Now, it’s time to put them into action.”

“What do you mean?” Rachel asked.

“I mean we practice in coordination with each other. Before we head out, we need a full day of practice,” Ace explained. “Rachel and Marcus, you need to learn how to control those fields of influence you used against the Carnage Fiend. Figure out how to compliment each other. Tara, maybe you can work on long-range healing. Olivia, you and I should probably practice some CQC drills.”

“Deal,” Olivia said.

“Alright,” Tara added.

Marcus and Rachel glanced at each other.

“Yeah,” their Mind Drinker said with a shrug. “You’re probably right.”

Rachel simply nodded.

Good.

For the first time since arriving in this hellscape, they had a plan—not just for survival, but for growth. For becoming something more than frightened individuals thrown into an impossible situation.

He clapped his hands together, and a plume of rock dust shot from his palms.

It was time to shift gears—and become the hunters they needed to be in order to survive this place.

Together.
