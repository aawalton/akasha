Over the next three months, the Heartcord Clinic and the Saint’s Hands rounded up the children of Miss Umbrage’s clinic, put them on payroll, and had them teach grown gangsters how not to embarrass themselves on the water.

Gael had seen worse hiring practices.

Rebus, Bran, and Nixy became the Saint’s Hands first official local guides, but the arrangement spread quickly through the rest of the children. Some of them knew where the deepest lakes were, so they became more specialized guides. Others knew which stretches of canal were dangerous, which fish migrated during certain weathers, which docks would let the Saint’s Hands anchor without asking questions, and which markets would buy which of their monstrous catches. The younger children mostly stayed behind, helping out at Miss Umbrage’s clinic or taking odd jobs around the Saint’s Hands warehouse, but anyone old enough to properly grip a spear eventually found themselves given one. After all, the prospect of regular food, actual wages, and permission to shout orders at adults did wonders for their motivation to work.

Their greatest contribution, however, was convincing Gael even further that his magnificent three-hundred-thousand-Mark fishing fortress was completely fucking useless for hunting Myrmurs. The ship remained docked outside their warehouse like an iron monument, and it’d be a shame to take it apart even if it was just to get back some of their investment, but it hadn’t sailed out since the first week Gael got it. In its stead, the Saint’s Hands had uglier ships built. Three dozen of them. The local shipwrights had taken Rebus and the other children’s instructions very seriously, producing three dozen fishing barges so wretched-looking Gael could hardly bear to moor them at his dock. Their hulls were pieced together from dark driftwood, old planks, nailed scrap, warped beams, and adhesive tape. No two sides matched. Half the engines coughed, and most railings were clearly repurposed bedframes, but Saintess, did the Myrmurs not love them. They were just about the types of ships a Myrmur would be fooled into believing they were utterly harmless.

To that end, the barges had been sailing out to over two dozen deepwater lakes scattered throughout Wraithpier, with three local children assigned to each vessel as fishing instructors for roughly a dozen Saint’s Hands. While the Saint’s Hands weren’t nearly as strong as Gael and Maeve, or Jin or Vivi, a considerable number of veterans had been with them for over two years at this point, which meant they’d been receiving a steady supply of points from all the Myrmurs they’d been taking out across the cardinal wards—and that meant there were at least three high-rank Wretch-Class or low-rank Blight-Class Saint’s Hands on each barge.

Their Jumping Spider Classes may not be particularly useful on cramped vessels with nowhere to really jump to, but they still had six arms each, and when there were twelve system-enhanced men jumping a single Myrmur, well…even a low-rank Blight-Class Myrmur would find themselves in a little bit of trouble.

The Saint’s Hands caught their first Myrmur without Gael’s help three days after their new fishing operation began, and that was when they started optimizing for efficiency as well. Gael had several dozen gloam lanterns shipped north from Blightmarch and installed two aboard each fishing barge, making it so the Saint’s Hands could cut out any Myrmur Host they reeled up even without him being there. Then he had several hundreds of crates of parasitic flowers shipped north so they could be placed aboard the barges as well, allowing the Saint’s Hands to prevent the Breeder’s bombs from going off should they manage to reel up an artificial Myrmur Host. Since Wraithpier locals recovered abnormally quickly from injuries and ailments that would take any other Bharnish thrice as long to recover from, they soon established a new efficiency pipeline: Myrmur Hosts who were well enough to walk away by themselves were kicked off their barges the moment their Myrmur hearts were removed. No need to tend to them. No need to waste time on people who didn’t need the recovery time. Artificial Myrmur Hosts, however, were all sent straight back to the warehouse to have their bombs physically removed—because that still needed to be done by a professional surgeon—and then interrogated for information once they recovered.

As a result, their warehouse had stopped resembling a warehouse some time ago.

The enormous central floor that’d once served as a comfortable communal living space was now packed with beds. Canvas curtains and wooden dividers separated makeshift wards. Wooden shelves had been bolted onto pillars to hold medicines, surgical supplies, parasitic flower vials, clean cloth, and whatever chemical Gael needed that week. The side platforms had all been enclosed with tarps and turned into treatment rooms, while several smaller sheds had been nailed onto adjoining docks for storage and convalescence. Basically, they’d turn the warehouse into a second Heartcord Clinic, and currently, about fifty recovering artificial Myrmur Hosts occupied the place. Most were sleeping on their beds while junior physician apprentices checked their pulses, but a few were staring blankly at the walls while their minds adjusted to being only human again, and an even smaller few were sitting upright eating Miss Alba’s noodles—which they also had shipped up here since her noodles were medically approved for nutrition, and by ‘medically-approved’, Gael really meant ‘him-approved’.

Fifty artificial Myrmur Hosts out of about five hundred total Myrmur Hosts over the past three months. Gael considered that excellent progress.

… So he and Maeve were currently contributing to said progress by doing absolutely nothing.

It was nearly evening, and they’d once again spent most of the day interrogating the many artificial Myrmur Hosts under their care. Suffice it to say, they were tired. And pretty bored of it as well. So now they lounged side by side in reclining chairs inside their second-floor office, which overlooked the central clinic hall through a broad window. Their feet were propped up, their bodies were sore, and their only saving grace were the strips of dried Myrmur meat they were chewing on.

And they didn’t have to look at their status interfaces at the same time, but they did. Because what else were they going to do until the man they’d just operated on—their latest artificial Myrmur Host—woke up in sound enough mind for an interrogation?
