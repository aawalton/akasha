The Apostle.

A foe Viktor had already considered the single most troublesome among those who came to invade his dungeon today, thanks to the warning of that mysterious woman. Now that he was getting an eyeful of the bastard in the flesh, though, “troublesome” turned out to be an understatement so profound it was almost impressive.

Oh, well. There was no point in wasting time complaining about it. Turning his gaze away from a problem was not going to make it politely excuse itself and leave on its own.

What he needed to do instead was keep thinking, keep turning the puzzle over and over in his mind, picking apart everything he had seen of this dangerous enemy and everything he could infer from it so that when the moment finally came, he would have at hand something resembling an effective response.

“Now, let’s see...”

Whatever power it was the Apostle wielded, it had to be some sort of body manipulation, which extended not only over his own flesh but over the flesh of anyone or anything he had physical contact with.

When he used this power on himself, he could make his body extraordinarily resilient to all damage, which would have been quite enough on its own, before you even factored in the fact that he was also, guess what, a human Reliquary. Furthermore, he also had superhuman strength and unparalleled regeneration. The guy might well stand on equal footing with the raw power Viktor himself could only get through “Rekindled Ember,” except he got to keep it indefinitely instead of having a clock ticking down on him.

And when he used the power on the others, he could stitch a severed limb back to a torso, without bothering to ask if those parts matched, and he could fuse several separate bodies into one. He should be able to heal, too, when he had the stomach for it. After all, the horrific side effects that accompanied his so-called “help” for the Iskora adventurers had far less to do with any limitation of the man’s power and far more to do with a simple absence of care on his part. If he had ever sincerely wanted to fix them, Viktor had little doubt that he could have done exactly that.

From those facts, it followed naturally that the Apostle could enhance the bodies of others, to make them stronger and tougher in the same way he had done to his own. And that meant the abomination he created and brought along might be a great deal more dangerous than a simple sum of three people’s strength added together.

Now, the next terrifying aspect of his power: the ability to absorb biomass directly into his body. He had so far only demonstrated this on that swarm of insects, though it would have been foolish to imagine that flesh and bone and hair would somehow be immune to whatever hunger lived beneath that stretching skin. And Viktor found himself wondering if the guy could eat a tree if he was in the mood.

Such an ability also raised several more questions.

First, what exactly did he get out of all this absorbing? Was he merely using it to kill whatever he touched, or was his body going to be somehow strengthened by what it consumed? Could he pack on denser and more powerful muscle by devouring more flesh? Could he harden his bones by feeding on those of the others? Or could he grow some manner of carapace, a living plate of armor assembled from all the bugs he had just eaten?

And second, how quickly could he absorb something after he made contact with it? Those insects had been consumed in what seemed like an instant, but that was hardly a fair comparison, given how small each of them had been. What would happen, then, if his target was a living human? How much time would that person have to fight back, to break free, to survive before that hungry patch of skin finished whatever it was doing to them?

Well, the safest course of action was obvious enough: keep anything alive as far away from that man as possible.

In that light, the Great Hallway might turn out to be the most promising ground for facing the Apostle. After all, other than Sebekton, there was precious little breathing there. Tomb guards, Templars, Wisps, and both Galatea and Khenemhotep themselves. All of them were as dead as the architecture. Though if bone was indeed on the menu just as chitin was, then even the tomb guards would need to be thrown into the fight with a good deal of caution.

Viktor sighed. He had decided only a short while ago that the Apostle would be allowed to pass through while Xander and the abomination were kept trapped behind. But now that plan would have to change considerably.

Oh, well. That was the nature of planning, wasn’t it? Strategies rarely survived first contact with the enemy, and a wise commander adjusted rather than clung stubbornly to something the battlefield had already outgrown.

So be it, then. He was going to start the thing, and improvise as developments arose.

And so, he closed his eyes once more, sending his vision to the great ramp before the Chamber of the Dead, where the decisive battle was about to begin in earnest.

Once he arrived, he saw the three familiar figures. After the spider-women called their insects back, Valeska-Rowan-Cynthia had been saved from the onslaught, and Xander had slipped back out of whatever wall he had been hiding in, like a rat emerging from behind a cupboard. Then, the three of them pressed on, continuing on their way toward this place. They were going to climb the ramp, where the ambush was set to trigger the moment they hit the midpoint.

And sure enough, the instant they reached it, Templars and tomb guards came pouring out from the entrances above and below, sealing off both ends of the hallway. Above, Galatea rose into view in her towering, armored form, her great warhammer raised and ready. Below, Sebekton took up position at the head of his troops, cutting off any avenue of escape.

“They look... different from what we’ve encountered before,” Xander murmured, his gaze flicking up, then down.

The Apostle strolled up beside him. “I think it’s time for you to do something,” the gray-haired man said with an easy smile.

Xander responded with nothing more than a grunt, but he raised his flaming sword regardless. And then, in the blink of an eye, he was no longer by the Apostle’s side, but hovering right before Galatea instead, the fiery blade already lifted high.

As fast as teleportation, indeed.

Yet, for all that, even with his sword aimed at the construct’s head, the blow itself never fell. It gave Galatea just enough time to spin her armored bulk around and swing her hammer at him first.

I see, Viktor thought.

The bastard needed to revert to his physical state in order to attack, but doing so would leave him vulnerable in that same instant. So he intended to let his opponent’s weapon pass clean through his intangible form first, before he committed to solidifying and striking back himself.

But that turned out to be a grave miscalculation. Because Galatea had infused her warhammer with the power of the Light.

And so the glowing head of the weapon crashed squarely into the bastard’s chest, and Xander screamed in pain as the impact sent his body hurtling backward through the air.

Only after several long seconds of tumbling did he manage to bring himself to a stop, hanging there clutching at his chest, his voice cracking with disbelief.

“What... the hell?”

“Be careful,” the Apostle called out from below, his tone as unbothered as ever. “That shiny toy of hers can still hurt you even when you’re a ghost. So try not to get hit.”

“You... you know?”

“Of course.”

“Why didn’t you warn me?”

The gray-haired man offered nothing more than a casual shrug in response.

“Some lessons are easier to remember if they hurt a little. It’s not like a single hit is enough to kill you anyway, right?”

“Bastard...”

Xander had spent the entire journey looking down on the other Iskora adventurers. He hadn’t given a damn when the gray-haired man carved his little experiments into their flesh and bones. And now he had found out, the hard way, that two monsters cut from similar cloth didn’t necessarily make for friends.

Still, whatever bitterness he might feel toward the Apostle, he had no time to dwell on it. His attention snapped back to Galatea and the troops arrayed at the top of the ramp. He raised his flaming sword once more and swung it in rapid, sweeping arcs, sending crescent after crescent of fire toward their position in a relentless barrage.

The Templars and tomb guards raised their shields to block the attacks, while Galatea simply stood there and let the flames wash over her armored form without so much as flinching. Then she raised her left hand, and at her command, eight chains of light tore free from the walls flanking the ramp, all of them converging on Xander to bind him in place. At the same time, the Templars at the rear drew their great bows and unleashed a volley of arrows. This way, no matter which state the bastard chose to occupy, some part in that combined attack would make him regret it deeply.

But of course, catching the bastard was never going to be quite that simple. Not while he still had those winged boots of his.

Xander blinked himself out of harm’s way before either the chains or the arrows could reach him. So the chains adjusted course and continued their pursuit, while the Templars kept firing, sending volley after volley after him with enthusiasm.

As Viktor watched the exchange unfold, several new details about how the Reliquary actually functioned began to fall into place. It seemed Xander could only move in a straight line, so he needed to bring himself to a full stop before he could shift direction. So no, he couldn’t remain in constant motion, reducing himself to a blur no eye could follow. He could only jump from one point to another. Of course, if he chose not to stop, he could shoot himself out of the tomb entirely. But that possibility only existed so long as the net of light woven through the walls and ceiling and floor wasn’t already in place. And since that net had been set, the bastard was not going to leave the Great Hallway, no matter what happened.

What was the Apostle going to do, though? Would he step forward and join the fray as well, or would he remain where he was, content to watch his “companion” fight alone against the full weight of Galatea’s forces?

And that was when Viktor realized the gray-haired man had disappeared.

Gone.

Down on the ramp, below the dance between chains and arrows and spike-haired uglyface, there was only the fused creature left standing. Its creator had vanished without a trace.

No, not quite without a trace.

Because there, exactly where the Apostle had stood moments before, lay the tattered robe that once clung to his body.

Did the guy just undress? Why?

As Viktor swept his vision back and forth across the ramp, he caught a hint of movement. Something. Low to the ground and fast.

Was that... a blob?

The formless mass surged up the ramp, moving faster than anything with no legs had any right to move. It slipped under the soldiers at the top, all of whom had their attention fixed entirely on Xander. And within seconds, it passed unnoticed through their ranks and disappeared into the entrance of the Chamber of the Dead beyond.

“Damn it,” Viktor cursed. “Celeste, warn Khenemhotep. The Apostle is about to walk straight into his chamber.”
