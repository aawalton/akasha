Wet from the rain and highlighted in the light of the sun that has emerged now the cloud has passed by, the field of destruction looks so much worse now than it did during the night.

I pause just outside the shelter, looking around in dismay. We’ve managed to obliterate a whole section of the forest. Almost all the trees within a range of about three hundred metres are charred, half-destroyed, or mere stumps.

And standing at the edge of it with his arms crossed, is Elarion. He isn’t alone, either – Artio is with him, and is already sniffing at the closest charred wooden stump. As I step towards them, he looks up at me and points a wordless accusation at me over the Bond. Further back, and looking with mixed expressions of curiosity, amazement, perturbation, and upset, are a number of people I recognise as Plant Mages and, curiously, Enchanters.

“I was trying to learn Lightning Shaping,” I tell them defensively as I get closer.

Elarion raises an eyebrow.

“And how much practice did you need when you had learned how to do it?” he asks me pointedly.

“I only managed to do it once before the storm arrived,” I admit.

Elarion’s eyebrow lifts higher and he stares pointedly around him.

“That was Tilathis,” I reply to his wordless question, shamelessly throwing the Tier four under the bus. “And some was Romeo.”

The larnatis huffs in disgust as he comes next to me.

I had to learn too, he protests.

Tilathis just grunts disinterestedly, emerging from the shadows of the shelter and stretching. I see several of the people who have come to visit us step back as his mouth opens cavernously and reveals his curved, sword-like teeth.

He then stands upright and looks at Elarion with cool arrogance.

Do you have anything to say about my teaching methods?

Elarion doesn’t look perturbed on the surface, but the way his expression blanks rather than remaining condemning indicates that he’s rethinking his decision to take the cause of this forest’s destruction to task.

“Merely that I request you choose a more open field next time.”

An open field? For lightning? Do you not know anything? Tilathis demands scornfully. Then he turns to me. I am going to hunt. We can discuss more about this party of humans when I return.

“I’ll discuss it with the head of House Rockfell if they decide to come to the tournament. We can’t move further forwards right now, but it’s good to at least have your tentative agreement.” I expect that the local head of House Rockfell will decide to attend – I hinted that we might be able to smooth the way to them finding a source of ore. Even if I wasn’t certain that Tilathis was the Beast King they’d been referring to, I was certain enough that we’d be able to help in some way.

And if they send more parties between now and then?

That’s a good question.

“If you can, warn them away and indicate that they will need to speak to me to make an agreement. Then drive them out of your territory, preferably without killing anyone. Though, if they try to kill you, then, by all means, respond in kind.”

Tilathis huffs.

You ask for much. Invading my territory is a death sentence of its own. Yet you do not wish me to kill them?

“Because every one you kill puts us in a worse negotiating position. At least if you warn them and try to be non-lethal to begin with, we can point to them being unreasonable or not listening – making it their fault.”

Tilathis rumbles thoughtfully.

Very well. Though they should pay for every time I must exert myself unnecessarily.

“Fair enough,” I shrug. “You can be part of the negotiations.” Actually, that’s probably a good idea in many respects – showing the sheer power of the Tier four they would otherwise have angered. “And if you find yourself struggling in any way, call for help over the Alliance Bond. We’ll support you.”

Very well, Tilathis agrees, then stalks off into the forest, disappearing far too quickly for a white and black nunda of his size.

Romeo murmurs something about finding Bastet and having his own hunt, then flies up into the air, heading in a different direction from Tilathis.

I turn back to Elarion.

“Alright, so apart from complaining about the trees, did you want to talk to me about something?”

“Markus, I take it you are discussing the matter of the Rockfells seeking ore?” Elarion asks, not answering my question directly, a faint furrow between his brows. I nod. “You do not intend to bring us into conflict with their House, do you? They are respected miners and metal-workers who will be very valuable on this continent.”

“I don’t,” I reassure him. “But they’re seeking to mine in Tilathis’ territory which, since he’s in Alliance with me, I’m claiming as part of our land for administration purposes. So, if they attack Tilathis on it, or seek to profit from it before we’ve made an agreement, then they’re attacking us.”

“They will not see it that way,” Elarion warns.

“Then we’ll have to make sure they do during the tournament.”

“Assuming they come,” Elarion points out.

“Do you think they won’t?” I ask, raising an eyebrow.

Elarion is silent for a few moments. Then he sighs.

“If they feel that we are at war with them, they won’t,” he warns. “Otherwise? They most likely will.”

I shrug.

“If it looks like things are deteriorating too much before the date of the tournament, I’ll fly over to their settlement and discuss it with them.”

Elarion shoots me a sharp glance.

“You just want an excuse to explore.”

I grin at him, but don’t answer.

“So, what did you want to speak to me about?”

Elarion sniffs haughtily.

“I did not. I was on my way to the wheat fields with the Enchanters – apparently they have some sort of new array to test – when I saw the destruction that had been wrought. Out of concern that it might be some powerful beast, I decided to investigate before continuing. But it was only you.” He looks down his nose at me, easy since he’s slightly taller than I am. I just smile – his words lack the edge they used to hold so I know he’s not being entirely serious.

Then what he said registers.

“Wait, a new array?” I glance over at the Enchanters, seeing Lizil there along with Alyna and several of the journeymen and apprentices. Hunter is absent, but maybe she’s busy elsewhere. Excitement grows in me – there’s only one array Elarion can be talking about.

“Yes, something that should deal better with the mana infusing our fields. At least, when it’s finalised – apparently, it’s very experimental right now,” Elarion replies, confirming my thoughts.

“Well then, let’s go,” I tell him with enthusiasm, nodding at the party of people who have come with Elarion, and then walking quickly towards where the fields are, around the corner. I hear a sigh of exasperation from behind me, but the other lord quickly catches up and calls for the others to join us.

“I suppose I should be grateful that the wheatfields were left untouched,” he murmurs as he gazes sadly at the trees around.

“They are? I mean, of course they were,” I chuckle nervously. “I did my best to prevent the fires from spreading.”

Elarion gazes at me for a long moment, then shakes his head as if even he doesn’t have words.

We are forced to walk at the pace of the slowest of the group, and it almost makes me fidget with impatience. I turn my head to watch Artio.

The owlbear’s vines are spread out around him, wiggling unnervingly through the dead and wet leaves that cover the forest floor. Every so often, they wrap around one of the damaged or dead trees.

“What are you doing?” I ask him curiously.

Life continues, he replies cryptically. Though the lightning has brought death, it has spread life too. The trees are not gone, only changed.

And then he goes quiet, focusing on his task. Since his words weren’t much use, I hope that his actions will explain more clearly.

Looking closely, I see the damaged trees healing. Not in the way that I heal with Flesh-Shaping – closing up the wounds – but instead by allowing new growth to sprout. Even the smallest stump is covered in green sprouts by the time Artio’s tentacle unwinds.

Closing my eyes, I pull on my magic sight, and see the green tendrils of his magic reach into the wood, feeding it even as he pulls at the same time.

I feel like I’m on the edge of a realisation.

He’s not just taking something, nor is he just giving something–

A sudden, sharp pain sparks through the side of my face and a duller one through my chest. I open my eyes and turn my head to see something blurry and brown far too close to me.

I blink in confusion, and the brown blur resolves itself into bark.

Heat rises to my cheeks and I step away as I realise what happened – so wrapped up in watching Artio, I wasn’t watching where I was going…and walked straight into a tree.

No hope that my gaff went unnoticed, either. I might only be bruised, but Elarion’s look of amusement and the quiet murmur that I hear in the group of people following us are evidence that the damage to my reputation is far more than that to my face.

I clear my throat and continue walking, trying to pull the shreds of my dignity back around me, and refusing to look at the little smile on Elarion’s face. Maybe I’d best keep my eyes on the route ahead for now.

Fortunately, we’re not far from the fields, and my cheeks stop burning soon enough. I’m pleased to see that Elarion was right – the wheat has taken a bit of a beating from the wind and rain, but no damage from lightning.

Elarion clicks his tongue.

“It will be a poor harvest in comparison to the others,” he sighs, then directs the Plant Mages and farmers forward to harvest what they can. “And check it is all edible,” he calls after them, receiving a wave of acknowledgement.

“You think it might not be?” I ask, concerned.

“It depends on whether the mana absorbers were able to endure to this point,” my ally replies grimly. “But they were changed shortly before the storm, so it should be well enough.”

Turning his attention away from the harvest, Elarion focuses on the Enchanters.

“We’ll start a new field,” Elarion decides. “Here.” He creates four ice pillars and uses them to delineate a new square space a good distance away from the two fields of wheat.

“It must be a circle,” Lizil informs him.

“Then make a circle within my ice pillars.”

Lizil nods.

“Yes, my lord. We will need the ground inside the field to be free of any mana you do not wish to be present – and full of mana you do. In short, please could you direct your mages to prepare the ground for planting – including with any enhancement potions that you wish to use on the plants.”

Elarion gazes at her thoughtfully.

“We usually use different enhancement potions at different stages of growth.”

“Then please ensure that they are all present while we set the array. After we have done so, any mana – from a potion or otherwise – that is added into earth will be absorbed by the array.”

Elarion looks concerned.

“Then what about harvesting the crop? Or replanting it? If the array will consume my mages’ mana, that is less helpful than our current system.”

Lizil shrugs.

“The array can be deactivated, however then you would need to ensure that the conditions within it are reset for when it is reactivated. The simpler option would be for your Plant Mages to deposit a little of their mana in the active area of the array to give it a ‘taste’. The array does not manage concentrations of mana, but types.”

Elarion considers that for a moment, then nods.

“Very well. Please inform us when you are ready to activate the array and we will ensure that its interior is as it should be.”

“Yes, my lord,” Lizil agrees. Then, as Elarion strides away towards where one of his farmers is trying to get his attention, she turns to me. “Would you like to see what we have designed?”

Silly question.

“Of course,” I answer, stepping forward eagerly as she pulls a scroll out of her pouch.
