Stromni had been staring at the heavy crossbow for so long that Alric was starting to wonder if it contained ancient dwarven secrets, available only to those willing to risk eye strain.

They were in Stromni’s forge; the heavy crossbow was on the workbench in front of them. The original drawing lay in front of it, and a collection of neater drawings was scattered around. A round bar had been fitted underneath the crossbow, running perpendicular to the front.

“Aye, lad, I understand the lever. What I don’t understand is this hook. How’s the string ta stay straight?” Stromni asked. He used his finger on one hand to point to the drawing, and one on the other hand to the top of the crossbow.

Alric frowned at the drawing; it made more sense when he drew it, but seeing it now was raising more questions. He glanced around the forge and then spotted a small hammer. He picked it up. “Alright, so imagine this is the handle, right?” He explained, putting the handle at the very front of the crossbow. Alric then mimed attaching it to the bar in the stock. “So when you pull—”

“Ya explaining the same thing back ta me, lad. I understand that, how is the string stayin’ flat?” Stromni explained, showing the flat movement on the top of the crossbow.

'Oh! Yes, that part doesn’t need to be solid, uh...' Alric began, scanning the forge for inspiration or, failing that, chain. 'Got any chain?' he asked. The forge, naturally, was full of chain, all of it deeply committed to things like anvils.

Stromni sighed, opened a drawer in the workbench, moved some pieces around, and passed him a length of chain. Alric grinned and wrapped it around the hammer.

Eventually, he had two short lengths of chain dangling down past the hammer's head. He repeated the demonstration. Stromni tilted his head, looking between the chain, the hammer, and the crossbow. He went to another drawer without saying a word and came back with a straight piece of iron.

“Alright, lad, if I hold this where the brace is, now do it,” Stromni said, holding the bar against the hammer and the brace. When Alric attempted the motion again, Stromni moved the bar up and down. “Here’s the issue, lad, ya see?”

Alric opened his mouth, reconsidered, and closed his eyes as if hoping the answer would be written on the inside of his eyelids. He looked back at the drawing. 'Ahh... that bar is meant to be curved. I think.' The drawing, however, showed it as straight.

“Ya think?” Stromni asked, still holding everything, his eyes wide.

“Think like a pitchfork, but with just two forky bits,” Alric said with a smile.

Stromni let out a deep sigh. “How bent?”

Alric’s smile widened. “Enough.”

Stromni’s shoulders drooped. “Ya sure this improves the crossbow?”

Alric nodded and shrugged. “I don’t know the numbers, but it can shoot faster. How much? I don’t know.”

Stromni turned back to the crossbow. “Aye, well, ya half-remembered stuff is all dangerous and useful. This feels like a dwarven thing. I’ll make it, but we're gonna have a lot of back-and-forth.”

Alric grinned and put everything down. He then started heading for the door; Stromni stood staring at the crossbow for a moment longer before following and dusting of his hands.

Alric’s guards quickly formed up around the pair as they headed back to the distillery. Stromni’s forge was pretty close, so it wasn’t an issue to leave for a short while.

“How’d your winter prep go?” Alric asked Stromni as they began walking.

Stromni glanced at him. “Badly, ya kept me ta busy. Ya work me too hard building ya strange things.”

Alric laughed and gestured back the way they'd come. “There is an easy answer, you know. Come stay at headquarters for winter. You won’t have to worry at all.”

Stromni scowled, though it was more of a rental scowl, and Alric had definitely seen better from the dwarf. 'I’m not moving to your headquarters just because you’ve decided I’m a pet that needs feeding.'

'You’d certainly be an ugly pet. We don’t have one, though. That could be you. Wait, no, Hal and Mara have two cats. You’d be at least third in line.' Alric said with a grin.

Stromni’s head tilted further away from him, but the scowl still failed to meet expectations. “So, not even ya favorite pet then.” He said before he glanced back at the guards. Both were carefully looking everywhere but the dwarf. “There will come a time, I reckon, ain’t today, though.”

“Suit yourself, I’ll let Seren know you find snow delicious,” Alric said with a nod, which got him the scowl he wanted; it definitely could have won a prize. Alric chuckled, and even Stromni scoffed.

They reached the distillery; one of the guards ahead opened the door. Stepping inside felt close to stepping into the sauna from the bathhouse. Steam rose from the boilers; the washback was half full of wort. Nearly everyone inside had their sleeves up.

One of Alric’s guards let out a grunt. Alric turned to look at him and found him grinning. He rubbed his hands and fanned himself. “You guys sure like guarding this place, don’t you?”

“It’s warm in here, Mister Alric.” The guard said with a smile. Two other guards sat near the wall, where they could see the doors.

“Don’t let it make you get sleepy, and don’t go much deeper,” Alric said, but they all nodded; they had been told this many times.

Alric couldn’t fault them. The distillery really was one of the warmest places around. The brewery might be warmer, but the constant dampness always annoyed him. Whatever he was wearing tended to stick to his skin, and the garment would always smell of wort after that.

He moved to the first washback. It was now half full of wort. Alric wanted to pitch the yeast, but he had no thermometer. He put a hand against it, partly to feel the temperature, but mostly just to warm his hand. He’d need to wait until closer to day's end to add the yeast.

He looked over at the second washback. It was currently empty. It was due to be filled today. They had scrubbed the inside already.

“Ya sure it ain't clean enough?” Stromni asked from beside him. He was pointing at the washback, but Alric hadn’t noticed him and got a fright.

“Do you want to throw out a batch because lye got in it?” Alric asked.

Stromni folded his arms. “Aye, I get ya point. We need ta swell the staves even so.”

“Well, boiling water helps with all that; once the staves are sealed, we’d need to dry them to clean the sides,” Alric explained.

He was more worried about wort getting trapped between the staves or deep into the wood and it turning bad. The boiling water was also for sterilizing, but he hadn’t thought to try to get lye between the slats when they filled the first washback.

“Tip ready!” a worker called. Alric and Stromni immediately stepped back.

A cloud of steam headed for the ceiling as Alric and Stromni backed further away from the washback. Both watched from where they were; they heard a loud splash in the washback. Looking at the floor below the troughs, they saw water dripping and splashing onto the floor.

“More lips,” Stromni said, pointing at the pool and then upward to the troughs.

“On the list with everything else,” Alric said with a shrug. They both nodded at the same time.

Alric headed back to the first washback and put his hand on it.

“What ya checkin’, lad?” Stromni stood nearby with crossed arms.

“If it’s cool enough yet for my important mystical ingredient,” Alric said, shifting his hand.

“Why don’t ya ‘ave a paddle drip some on ya hand?” Stromni suggested.

That made Alric pause. On one hand, it was a good idea. On the other, he wasn’t sure he wanted his hand to become official distillery equipment. 'That’s a good idea,' he said, with some trepidation.

They had to wait for the other two boilers to be tipped into the second washback. After that, they brought two ladders; one worker carried the long-handled paddles they had gotten made. They looked like giant wooden spoons. The paddle had also been boiled.

Stromni stood at the bottom of Alric's ladder and held it for him as he climbed. Once up, the worker carefully but awkwardly moved the paddle for the hole it went in. Alric had been specific, telling them not to touch any parts that would be dipped into the wort.

After a slight bit more shuffling and repositioning the ladder, they finally got it in. Alric stood at the top of the ladder watching. They had put the lid on the washback; that might have been premature, but Alric was nervous about anything splashing. Eventually, they got it right; they carefully moved the paddle over. Alric held his hand out.

A small amount dripped down on him. It was warm without being hot. He rubbed it between his fingers before tasting it.

The flavor was just strong malt. It hadn’t been toasted much.

“And?” Stromni called from below him.

“It’s fine. Let's pitch.” Alric nodded. The workers' shoulders dropped slightly as they began to manoeuvre it back through the small slot in the cap without touching it. Alric used his item box to bring out the yeast cask.

Since discovering he could summon dry yeast, many of his previous issues had become easier. Yeast could be sent to the brewery or the bakery as needed. This reminded him that the bakery started next week, and they had asked him to look things over before the first day.

He shook his head slightly to clear his thoughts; the worker had started stirring the wort. Alric pulled the bung and poured out the powder. He couldn’t see much of the wort when he looked into the opening. A small patch of light, but he could hear it moving.

How much yeast to add to seven and a halfish thousand liters was another matter entirely. Alric just let it run out of the cask.

“Just keep stirring.” He called out, and the worker nodded.

Better too much than too little.

Once it started to look excessive, Alric replaced the bung and glanced over at the worker. The worker looked to be struggling with moving such a big, awkward tool, and Alric noticed a new issue.

“Swap with him, please! I can’t have you drop sweat in there!” Alric called out to the worker.

The worker frowned but nodded. He positioned the paddle so it was within reach and went down the ladder. After putting the cask back in his item box, Alric then went down. Once he reached Stromni, he said, “We don’t want sweat dripping in there.”

Stromni opened his mouth and then closed it. He nodded.

“We jus need ta keep replacing the stones ya said?” Stromni asked, glancing up at it.

Alric nodded, looking back at the washback. “In eight days. We have wash and then Maggie gets busy.”

Stromni nodded. Alric put his hands on his hips, thinking that when it started properly, the ferment would probably keep itself warm, but it definitely needed a boost at the start.

“So we see ya in eight days?” Stromni asked with a grin.

“No. I’ll be back in the mornings for the heating stones.” Alric said with a mock scowl at Stromni, who chuckled.

“If I want ya back, I just need ta make ya crossbow fork.”
