“Curse this desert!” Harold wheezed. The older man pressed his body tightly against the side of a butte, desperate to take advantage of the small scrap of shade it provided. The rock formation was around 1000 feet high and half that in diameter. The tall, pillar-like stone was one of many in the area, though the next closest was still several thousand feet away.

Samantha did her best to ignore his antics. He’d done nothing but complain since they’d ascended to the second floor and arrived in the Shattered Lands. She’d enjoyed a brief reprieve when she climbed the butte to scan their surroundings earlier, but he picked right back up again as soon as she was within earshot. She suspected he was attempting to annoy her enough so that she’d actually consider his earlier request to carry him around—if only to shut him up.

Harold squinted toward her and let out a dissatisfied huff. “So, are you going to tell me what you really found up there? Or are we going to wait around in this heat until one of us dies from exposure?”

If one of us dies from exposure, it certainly won’t be me, she thought.

Though Samantha found the intense heat to be uncomfortable, her 22 Resilience helped to take the edge off. For a new cultivator like Harold, this environment would probably feel like he was being baked in an oven. Even Silas was faring better than the man despite being covered in thick fur.

“I did tell you, Harold,” she said. “Sand, sand, and more sand. I guess there were a couple birds or something flying off in the distance… but that’s it.”

His eyes widened. “You were being serious?”

“Why wouldn’t I be?”

“Because a sane person would be concerned if that were true!” he said desperately, gesturing vaguely to her. “Not standing around like an idiot with a… creature on her head!”

Currently, her bonded Lunar Wolpertinger was using her shoulders as a perch and was shading them both by extending his wings overhead like an umbrella. Silas’s wings gave her more shade than Harold was getting from the butte, and he was slightly waving them up and down to give them both a bit of a “breeze”—a clever idea Silas had come up with all on his own. Odd as it probably looked, it was unexpectedly effective. She absentmindedly wondered if maybe that was the real reason why the alchemist was being so hostile right now. Jealousy tended to bring out the worst in people.

Harold whistled sharply at her to get her attention. “Hey, kid! Still talking to you here!” He added under his breath, “Gods help me.”

Samantha fortified her waning patience by reminding herself that she was partially responsible for Harold’s ascension. “I get that you’re stressed right now, but I’m going out of my way to try and help you survive, remember? There’s no need to be so—”

“So I’m just supposed to bow and scrape to you if I want to live, is that it?”

She let out a deep sigh. “All I’m saying is that it’s better to get along. If it’s too hard for you to call Silas and I by our actual names and not insult us, fine. Just don’t be surprised when I stop being as nice and accommodating.”

Harold scowled but seemed to think over her words. After a moment he relaxed his posture slightly and nodded. “I’ll… work on it.”

She gave him a small nod. “As for your question earlier, I’m not panicking because I have an idea for how I might scout our surroundings a bit more. I’m reserving my worries for if that also comes back with nothing.” As she spoke, she mentally pulled up Silas’s Profile and navigated to the specific Ability in [Jackalope of All Trades] that could help her.

Offered Bond Abilities:

Bond Ability Points (BP) Remaining: 1

Jackalope of all Trades (Rank 3)*Mongrel Bloodline

(0/3 Bond Ability Points Invested):…

Spectral Projection [1 BP] - You gain the ability to temporarily project your consciousness outside of your body. You can maintain the projection for the cost of 1 qi per minute, and upon canceling the ability or running out of qi your consciousness will be returned to your body. …

After her ascension representative revealed that projection arts could be used to communicate with beings on lower floors in the World Spire, [Spectral Projection] became a must-have Ability. Even if Samantha could never see her parents in person again, at least she might talk with them through her projection. In this situation, however, the Ability would serve a more practical purpose. It would allow her to scout the area without risk of excessive harm or exhaustion to her physical body.

Bond Ability selected! Changes have been made to your Profile.

Her binding thread swelled with potency as she spent her final Bond Ability Point. Qi gathered on Silas’s end of the bond for a heartbeat before surging toward her, striking her dantian, and flowing through her. Her body shivered involuntarily as the knowledge of how to use the Ability embedded itself into her spirit.

“And I suppose I’m just going to be waiting here for your return for an indeterminate amount of time?” Harold questioned. “That doesn’t seem incredibly safe.”

Samantha didn’t trust Harold with full knowledge about what arts and Abilities she possessed, so she kept her response vague. “I’ll be using a scrying art to look around, but I’ll be right here if anything dangerous happens.”

Harold followed her over to a different spot beneath the butte that was semi-shaded. “Scrying typically puts people in a trance-like state. How can I communicate with you if I see something coming that you don’t?”

She set her gear down and settled in the lotus position—legs crossed with each foot placed on top of the opposite thigh. She pointed upwards to Silas, who was still perched on her shoulders. “Get Silas’s attention and he will inform me.”

Trying to center herself before activating the Ability was harder than usual even with [Sage’s Rumination] active. She was reminded of a comparison she’d once heard, which likened achieving inner peace to gardening. People unpracticed in meditation were starting their garden in a wild plot that was rocky, untilled, and filled with weeds. With regular practice they shaped the land into fertile earth that’d be easy to grow things in, but if left untended the plot would start to return to its natural, unmaintained state. [Sage’s Rumination] was equivalent to having a small section of her garden growing things year-round, or maybe having someone help to keep the larger area maintained. Usually that was good enough to let her quickly bring her entire plot to life whenever she wanted, but sometimes a giant meteor (or several) fell from the metaphorical sky and she had to figure out how to deal with them first.

Putting aside the mental and emotional turmoil of ascension and having the truth of the World Spire revealed to her… it was hot. She’d been able to distract herself from thinking about it too much thus far, but completely embodying tranquility required the absence of distractions. She was armored, sweat was dripping in places she didn’t even know she could sweat, and everything felt vaguely damp and sticky. She hated feeling sticky.

Just as she was finally finding tranquility and started to funnel qi into the Ability, Harold piped up with another question. “Is there anything in particular I should do to get his attention? Or…?”

Samantha irritatedly swiped beads of sweat from her face as her inner peace evaporated. “Just… clap or something! He’ll probably see whatever it is that’s coming long before you do anyway.”

“Hmph. I see how it is. Fine, I’ll just sit here quietly then!” he sulked, stomping away to a different nearby scrap of shade.

Samantha began the process of recentering herself again from the beginning.

Activating [Spectral Projection] for the first time was an unusual experience, to say the least. One moment she was in her body, eyes closed, and then the next she was incorporeal and peering down on her physical form from above. Staring at herself for a few seconds, she narrowed her eyes.

Is that really what I look like?

She drifted closer to get a better look.

Closed eyelids tipped with short, dark lashes hid what she knew were earthy brown irises beneath. Sweat dampened strands of ink black, shoulder-length hair clung to the edges of her face and the nape of her neck, and eyebrows more flat that arched caught the occasional droplet of moisture that ran down her forehead before it reached her eyes. Her lightly tanned skin had a mild flush from the desert heat which, when combined with shadows deepened by sitting in the shade, seemed to highlight the sharpness in her cheekbones and jawline. It was only the relative softness in the shape of her nose and lips that kept her overall appearance from being too harsh.

While these individual facial features were recognizable, something about them seemed… off. They didn’t match what Samantha saw anytime she looked into a mirror.

She looked at Silas. His green fur was spotted with brown across his back in a fawn-like pattern. This was a color scheme the bird-like wings he was using to fan her shared, though they lacked the spots. Two hard, circular disks—from which he could grow antlers with his Ability, [Antler Genesis]—rested flat against his head in the space between his amber eyes and tall, upright ears. Talons emerged slightly from mitten-like paws to get purchase on her armor and keep him from slipping off her shoulders. He yawned once, revealing an unusual set of sharp teeth which possessed predator canines in addition to the incisors and grinding molars of hares. There was nothing off about his appearance.

Maybe it’s just humans? she wondered.

She glanced over at Harold. Mundane tan work robes were closed with a hastily tied waist sash under his pot belly. His sleeves were rolled up to combat the heat, which revealed colorful stains on the skin of his forearms and hands from working with alchemical solutions. The sweat dampening his short, gray hair made how it was thin on the top more noticeable, but he had no spots of outright baldness. He was barely taller than her own height of 5’6”. Upon reflection, she recalled him being shorter (or more stooped) as a mortal, but that probably wasn’t a result of viewing him via projection so much as it was one of the benefits of cultivation’s age-reversing or age-slowing effects. He was just standing straighter as a Low Copper than before. Besides his height, the cantankerous, sixty-something year old man looked the same as ever, too.

Looking between herself and her companions a couple more times, the realization hit her. She was viewing herself as an outsider would rather than seeing a reflection. That’s why her appearance seemed weird. Knowing that, she concluded she much preferred her mirror image to reality. Seeing herself this way, minor imperfections that she usually didn’t notice stood out like glowing signage. The way one of her eyebrows had a slightly different angle than the other, or how one corner of her mouth rested a tad higher than the opposite side did, for example.

Shifting her gaze away from her own face, a couple of other things caught her eye. Contrary to when she was in her body and sensed her binding thread’s presence with her spirit, her spectral form perceived it as if it were a physical object. A blue thread about as thick as a string of yarn came out of her body’s torso and went into Silas’s. There was a second thread now as well—one that connected her spectral body to her physical one. It was gossamer thin, but she got the sense that the silk-like line was impossibly strong.

As for her spectral body, it looked almost identical to her physical one. The main distinction was that she lacked any armor as a specter. Instead, she wore a long, flowing set of robes made of a material resembling white fabric—though not one she’d ever seen before. It had no visible threads or seams, but appeared to be tailored perfectly to fit her and moved with her as she would expect cloth to.

Curious about this new form’s capabilities, Samantha flexed her will with the intent to fly upwards. The world blurred around her as she zipped past the top of the 1000 foot high rock butte in a blink. Hurrying to halt her ascent, she came to a stop around a mile above where her body was seated.

That’s a bit too fast.

Even though she knew that her current form wouldn’t be harmed from falling, being this high up with nothing to stand on or grab hold of made her feel queasy. Trying to move at a more measured pace, she descended until she was floating near the ground again.

Better.

Next, she reached her hand out and pushed it against a nearby rock. As if nothing was there at all, her entire arm disappeared into the solid stone. She pulled her arm back and it still looked and worked perfectly fine. Shrugging once to herself, she stuck her head into the rock as well. She expected to lose her vision while her head was inside, but that was only partly true. She could see the various grains and granules that made up the stone within about a foot of her head, but beyond that was pitch blackness. As soon as she removed her head from the rock she regained her full range of sight.

The last things she wanted to check involved her interaction with people. Floating over to Harold, she placed a hand on his shoulder. Unlike the rock earlier, her spectral hand met resistance as it landed on him—though she felt she could easily push through it if she wanted. Her best guess was that she was interacting with his natural qi defenses somehow.

She tried using [Inspect] on Harold while she touched his shoulder. This was the one skill she was almost positive would work in this form, but it didn’t do anything. Getting an idea, she pushed the tip of her pointer finger past the thin barrier of resistance around Harold’s body. She paused to make sure that this was not hurting him or having any other adverse effect, but he didn’t seem to notice her. Again, she tried using [Inspect].

Harold Greene - Low Copper

Highest Attribute: Endurance (7)Lowest Attribute: Resilience (5)

Humans are one of the most adaptable and versatile species. Neutrally attuned beings at a baseline, they are able to convert any type of qi into usable energy. Unlike spirit beasts this stored energy does not coagulate into a core at the time of death, but is released into the surrounding area.

She pulled her hand away from Harold and the small hole she had made in his defensive shell closed up as if she’d never penetrated it.

She looked down at her own hands in awe for a moment, drinking in the gravity of the power she’d just gained. This Ability could change everything for her, and she was just scratching the surface of what it was capable of. “Incredible” as a descriptor didn’t seem to cut it. But, as keen as she was to experiment more with it, it’d have to wait until they were all in a safer situation.

She checked her qi pool—which was at 174.6—and did some mental math. With the Peerless Tempest pattern she’d just learned, and [Sage’s Rumination] doubling her qi regeneration rate, her total qi pool size was 177.6 qi and she regenerated 48 qi an hour. With [Spectral Projection] having an upkeep cost of 1 qi per minute, for every hour she had the art active she would lose a net total of 12 qi. In other words, she could maintain her projection for about 14 and a half hours straight if she was willing to use her entire qi pool and didn’t use any other arts. This would be more than enough time to get a better idea of their surroundings—especially with how fast she could fly around.

Slight movement overhead caused Samantha to glance up. At some point the birds that she’d seen flying in the distance earlier had made their way over to the butte. They now flew in lazy circles a couple miles above her.

Might as well [Inspect] them, too, she figured. Regardless of if they were mundane or spirit beasts, she’d likely glean something about this mysterious floor from the notification.

She headed upwards until reaching about the same altitude as the fliers. It was far from a perfect execution since Samantha had difficulty telling exactly how fast she was going, but once she reached the correct height it was easy enough to home in on one of the three birds (which were vultures of some kind). It took her another minute or two to figure out how to keep pace with them in order to be able to touch one of them, but before she got quite that far in her plan, a new concern arose: a blue thread extended from the vulture’s chest toward a distant dune. Checking the other two birds, it was the same. All three of these vultures were bonded beasts, and they seemed to have thicker binding threads like she and Silas did. They could probably share senses with their bonded humans too.

Afraid of what she might find, she touched a vulture, pushed through the qi defenses she felt around its body, and used [Inspect]. After five points had been spent she cut off the flow of energy and looked at the message.

Desert Vulture (Rank 2 - High) [Bonded]

Highest Attribute: Endurance (16)Lowest Attribute: Strength (4)

Desert Vultures are excellent flyers, often able to reach heights exceeding five miles and able to stay aloft for days at a time. Their eyesight is specialized to detect small creatures and movement even from great distances.

It was a relief to see that the beast’s highest attribute was only 16 and its Rank wasn’t terribly high. That wasn’t to say it wouldn’t be a dangerous opponent in a fight, but there shouldn’t be any insurmountable power differences between them.

<Silas, these birds flying above us are bonded like you. I’m almost certain we’re being watched. Can you keep an eye on them to make sure we aren’t unexpectedly attacked? I’m going to investigate where their binding threads lead.>

<Yet another bird enemy! I will watch them. They should fear me now. I have wings, like them. Once I learn how to fly, none will escape,> he swore.

She smiled to herself at his enthusiasm.

Closing the notification, she belatedly realized that it took more qi than she would’ve expected to get this amount of information from the low-Ranked beast. Even when she was Low Copper and [Inspecting] similarly ranked creatures it had only cost her three qi points—not five. It was possible that using arts through her spectral form cost more qi the further she was from her body, or alternatively, maybe channeling the arts through her spectral form in general made the qi cost less efficient. Regardless, she’d need to be mindful about the increased qi expenditure.

Taking advantage of her increased altitude before setting off, Samantha scanned her surroundings once again. The heat haze coming up from the sands distorted the things behind them, but it seemed like there was some sort of new geographical feature several miles away. The coloring was different from the surrounding sands, and there might’ve even been the barest hints of green dotted around it. Every other direction seemed to lack anything notable—aside from the long line of plateaus, mesas, and buttes that stretched to either side of her as far as she could see. The dune where the binding threads led was equally nondescript. She never would’ve guessed anyone hid there if not for her newest Bond Ability.

Following the long binding threads down to their origin behind the dune, she came across a group of seven cultivators. They were huddled together under the shade of a tan-colored canvas stretched over tent poles. The three cultivators bonded to the vultures sat with their legs crossed and their eyes closed, like they were in the midst of meditation. The other four did a variety of activities, from eating to cleaning armor and weapons to snacking on jerky.

“Any movement from the group yet?” a man cleaning an unfamiliar weapon asked.

“Not yet, Cain. The girl is meditating or something though.”

The man named Cain laughed. “She got a death wish? Who cultivates first thing after ascending before even checking if the area’s safe?” The men around him laughed along with his words. Encouraged by the reaction, he continued, “We’ll be doing them a favor, really. Better to die early and quickly than letting them get torn apart by Scorps or Biters once night falls.”

There goes any hope of these people being friendly, she mentally grumbled.

“You think they have any new arts on ‘em? I’ve been waiting ages to learn my first one! Ooh—or enchanted goods?”

Cain nodded. “Ascendants always do.” He turned generally toward the vulture-bonded men. “Oi! Can you tell what their Stages are?”

“Hm… my bond’s thinking the girl is out of the Copper Tier, but the man isn’t. The strange beast is stronger than any of ours, though.”

Cain spat off to the side. “She’s Bronze? Might need to be careful then. If her defensive arts are too good the casters might have trouble penetrating. Well, yours will, anyway.” He looked down at the weapon he’d been polishing and stroked it affectionately. “Nethershot cuts through anything.”

Behind Cain one person mockingly imitated him stroking Nethershot. This earned him a slap upside the head from the person sitting beside him, along with a stern expression that screamed “knock it off before he sees you”.

Samantha examined the foreign weapon, wondering what it did. The metal contraption looked very similar to a crutch or a cane and was about four feet long in total. At one end was a wooden “Y” shaped handle, and the other end was a long, straight, metal pole. All around the pole were enchantments etched into the silver material, and the entire weapon had been polished to a shine.

“They won’t even see us coming…” Cain said, grinning widely. The man pushed the wood into the crook of his shoulder and held the pole parallel to the ground while looking down its length. “Rest up boys and getch’yer gear in order! We’ll ride once the sun’s at our backs.”

The men clapped and cheered for Cain—who Samantha was assuming was the group’s leader—and went to fetch their own “casters”. The strange weapons weren’t as impressive as Cain’s, but everyone had one. Looking over the group and their equipment more closely as they readied themselves, Samantha noticed that there was a fourth man that had a binding thread—this one leading down beneath the dune. Steeling herself, she followed the blue line into the sands.

Try as she might to remind herself that she couldn’t be physically harmed in this form, being only able to see one foot in front of her face at any given time was nerve-wracking. She moved slowly along the binding thread, trying to mentally prepare herself for the inevitable moment when a spirit beast would appear in front of her.

That was when she ran face first into an enormous set of hairy fangs.

She screamed and scrambled backwards, shooting out of the sands and back into the open air in an instant. Admittedly… not her proudest moment. Gathering her courage, she reentered the sands and located the fearsome beast once more. She had to know what it was.

Seven points of qi later she had her answer.

Alpha Sand Spider (Rank 3 - High) [Bonded]

Highest Attribute: Endurance (20)Lowest Attribute: Resilience (7)

Sand Spiders travel in packs that are led by a singular, strong alpha. Although not venomous, their pincer-like fangs are strong enough to dismember prey. Primarily ambush predators who favor dragging unsuspecting victims into the sand, they can also track prey over long distances and run at high speeds for extended periods of time.

Samantha made a guttural noise that was a cross between “ew”, “ugh”, and gagging. It was such a visceral reaction that she didn’t think she’d ever made such a sound before, and wouldn’t ever be able to recreate it. Mundane spiders didn’t bother her all that much, but something about one that was larger than she was… horrifying? Deeply repulsive and disgusting? An awful realization hit her as she was struggling to find an appropriate word. If this was the alpha… how many more Sand Spiders were around?

She moved through the area with all four of her limbs fully outstretched in the hopes she would feel the qi defenses of nearby spiders even if she couldn’t see them. After stumbling across four more of the beasts over the next half hour she re-emerged from the sands. There were probably more of them around, but it was taking her too long to locate them when none of the others appeared to be bonded. She didn’t know how many hours were left until sundown, but her time would be better spent elsewhere.

The odds of her and Silas beating seven cultivators (at least one of which had a higher Stage than her) and a minimum of eight spirit beasts in a fight were terrible.
