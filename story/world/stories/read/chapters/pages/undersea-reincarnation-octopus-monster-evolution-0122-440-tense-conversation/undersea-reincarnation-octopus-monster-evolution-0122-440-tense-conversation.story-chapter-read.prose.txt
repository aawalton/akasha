The air shimmered and trembled around them as the two glared at each other with barely restrained hostility. Henry’s muscles were coiled and taut, and he could say the same for the Merfolk from the way his neck tendons seemed to ripple. The tension was palpable, and for a brief moment, the Merfolk’s emotions were a riot of impressions as Henry’s claim was slowly digested. At first, it was sheer disbelief, followed by derision that slowly curdled into pure, unadulterated rage at being threatened in his territory. The deep, heavy aura turned murderous, aiming to choke the breath out of Henry’s lungs, but Henry’s own aura stood its ground, letting the weight of the Merfolk’s hostility grind down its edges as it was deflected to the side. Henry was holding up just fine, but there was no denying the experience and weight behind the old Merfolk.

The latter probably explained why a part of the Merfolk was still considering the words, and why the building in which the two were facing each other hadn’t been reduced to silt just yet. But even with that in mind, the murderous intent was still heavy in the air. Without taking a step back or pulling his aura back, the Merfolk narrowed his eyes up at Henry, and with great effort, words managed to slip from between the sharp teeth. He still looked like he’d love nothing more than to tear into Henry, but like a well-trained dog painfully waiting for permission, he didn’t swing.

“Before we address this… insolence, which you will dearly pay for, what is this otherworldly threat you speak of? What proof do you have? You have five seconds.”

Henry’s eyes glowed with a deep, golden yellow as he met the Merfolk’s glare. He was very tempted to let the five seconds go by and call the Merfolk’s bluff. Just like the humanoid, Henry wanted nothing more than to beat the ego-tripping bastard senseless. But when Henry closed his eyes, he could still see the tens of thousands of ships that blotted out the stars, like a swarm of locusts looking for another world to doom. He remembered how damn many of them there had been, and if even a fraction of a percent made it into this world, they could wreak havoc on everything in their path. While this world might not be as populated as Earth, there were still millions of innocents that called this place home. If this abrasive piece of garbage could eliminate a few hundreds or even thousands of the swarm and spare the innocents a painful death, that could make a difference. That was the priority. The Merfolk was strong, and if it wasn’t for Henry’s crushing advantage of having thousands of bodies to throw at him, he wasn’t sure of his chances.

He couldn’t be the one to mess this up. But once the big threat was dealt with, they could settle their grudges. Henry was extending the olive branch, and it was on this supposedly intelligent creature to take it. So long as he could push their enmities aside, Henry would accept the truce. For the common good. If not, though, then Henry would shove the olive branch straight through his beady green eyes.

“Insectile swarm,” Henry managed after a couple seconds. “They look a bit like humanoid mantises, with bladed arms. There are thousands of ships, with even the smallest of them packing a couple dozen high B-ranks and a mid A-rank elite. They’re being led by three queens that are beyond A-rank.”

As he explained, Henry waved his arm and summoned projections of the sights he had seen, and with every item he displayed, the deep, fathomless scorn of the Merfolk was being punctured with doubt. The hostility-laden aura retreated, and Henry pulled his back as well. No matter what happened here, Henry wouldn’t be the one that broke the peace. Though even if Henry’s illusions were lifted straight out of his memory, they were still illusions, which the Merfolk was quick to point out.

“This is still not proof,” the Merfolk said, taking a step back and leveling his spear a little lower, though there was no mistaking the still latent hostility in the air.

A part of Henry egged him on to be as antagonistic as possible, but he refrained and instead, after considering what he was about to do for a moment, he summoned a bone reservoir, full of pressurized air.

The spear immediately touched him between the eyes. Henry didn’t even look at it.

“This contains air, fish-for-brains,” he simply said. The insult slipped out, but he continued as if nothing had happened. “These beings might contaminate the water here, and we don’t need to be taking that risk,” he said, then he constricted the arm surrounding the bone orb, and air exploded outward though with the pressure they were under, it barely filled half the room, and Henry adjusted the air-zone by controlling the water surrounding them.

Naturally, the Depth Lord didn’t take kindly to the insult, but nothing more was said. The spear was pushed slightly forward, but considering Henry’s Constitution, it barely got a drop of blood out.

Then Henry summoned the first corpse of a Void Lord, and immediately, the Merfolk’s hostility dropped by multiple degrees. Henry then summoned a Stalker. Then one by one, a few of the elites he recovered were hovering between them, cocooned in a thick layer of Telekinesis, just in case.

By now, the Merfolk was fully focused on them. His spear was still tightly gripped, but most, if not all of his hostility was gone as he examined the creatures’ carcasses. Some were in better shape than others, and even with Henry’s Telekinesis around to create another defensive layer, there was a telepathic buzz in the air, just from the corpses.

Then, just as suddenly as the first one had appeared, Henry shoved them all back in his Maw. Henry’s interruption wasn’t meant to be aggravating, but it was still a little funny to see the Merfolk twitch as he was shaken awake from his fugue.

Naturally, the hostility ramped back up, but Henry raised an arm to curtail it. “Can’t take the risk of having them out for long. They might act as a divination anchor for the queens.”

The Merfolk eyed him with annoyance and quite a bit of barely-restrained rage, but he looked away after a beat, glancing back at where the corpses had floated. “That’s where you were, then.”

Henry would have raised a brow, but it wasn’t surprising that this guy had tried to find him considering their last encounter. Come to think of it, this one and that other Merfolk lady had displayed both anti-divination skills, anti-teleportation, and gate opening magic. They likely had tried to find him.

“That’s where I’ve been, yes. And now I’m back, to warn everyone and to make sure we’re ready.”

The Merfolk’s eyes flicked back to him, narrowing for a moment. “How did you get through the barrier?”

Henry stared for a moment, then slowly tilted his head to the side. These people really knew their stuff. Too bad they were annoying to deal with. “The System let me through.”

The Merfolk crossed his arms, resting his spear on his shoulder as he continued staring at Henry, then nodded. “You spoke to it. Does it know what’s coming?”

Henry did not like how reasonable the Merfolk was acting, but then again, one did not get to this level and rule over a settlement of powerful beings without a modicum of intelligence. “We suspect it does. It might be why there’s a barrier in the first place.”

At that, the Merfolk narrowed his eyes. “Who’s this ‘we’ you’re speaking of? Who are you working with? In fact, how about you stop drip-feeding me these details and start talking.”

And there it was. Henry was once again tempted to let this situation devolve into violence. But he wouldn’t be the one to jeopardize these… talks. If one could even call them that. And for a moment, he considered what to divulge and what to keep to himself, then he decided that keeping secrets was worthless. If this fish-prick wanted to double-cross them somehow or try to sabotage them, then he’d be signing his death warrant. There was nothing he could do to stop Henry.

So Henry told him everything they had put together with Zerathstra and Velistraine. The questions about the missing ascended, the rainbow turtles and whatever was out there hounding them, everything. The Depth Lord didn’t react to Velistraine’s name, but when Zerathstra was mentioned, Henry saw the Merfolk’s eyes light up with recognition, but he didn’t say anything. But when the orbs were brought up, the bored, nearly contemptuous expression fell away. The Merfolk blinked a few times, brows knitting, then his green eyes widened before they morphed once more into a wince.

“The Founders’ Key. That’s what you’re looking for. And I believe I know where the last one is.”

Taking a deep inhale, the Merfolk turned and with a smooth, slow movement, swam back to his desk and looked up. Up there was a mural. A large one, and from this angle, Henry could see a massive underwater city. But then the Merfolk moved his desk away, and the edges of the mural became visible.

They were pitch black, with thin tendrils sneaking toward the bright city.

“The Doom of Nymara, Pearl of the Deep. Where our capital once stood before the abyss dragged it down. That’s where you’ll find the last one,” the Merfolk said, pain and reverence equal in his tone. Then he glanced back, and his face morphed once more, marred by hostility. “You might be the one best equipped to deal with what’s down there. And if the situation is as dire as it seems, then you better stop wasting my time and yours and get moving. Leave this copy here and keep me abreast of the situation.”

Henry was still parsing everything he was hearing, but he still narrowed his eyes at the jumped-up fish and his orders. Henry was about to give him a piece of his mind when the spear disappeared and the Merfolk sat back down on his chair.

“You’re dismissed,” the Merfolk said, and Henry seriously considered detonating his Avatar right then and there. It’d be so easy, and it’d get his message across so well.

The common good, Henry reminded himself, closing his eyes. The Merfolk was clearly provoking him, and so long as Henry gave him room, the Depth Lord would figure out a way to get under his skin. So Henry shut off his vision and hearing from the clone, lest he hear something else that set him over the edge. The Merfolk hadn’t lost it when Henry had thrown a few barbs. Henry wouldn’t be the one to break the peace. But Henry would get him back for this.

Maybe not today. Maybe not tomorrow. But he would pay him back for the insults.

So long as they all lived.
