First demons, now Singers. This was turning out to be a real shitshow of a day.

"This is bad," Hannah said, wiping raindrops off her face.

Lemira hugged herself, grabbing hold of her own bare arms and shuddering. "Some of these demons are known to me. This is not a threat to be taken lightly, with divided attention."

"That's why they're coming now," Luke said, turning to face the direction of the portal. "To capitalize on the chaos."

"I'm going to deal with the Intruders," Curtis said.

"If you ignore the demons and they are allowed a foothold in this world, all will be lost," Lemira argued.

Milla (Guild): "This is not a drill, people. I have received reports of over a hundred Singers having come through the gate already. They are fortifying their positions as we speak. This is happening across the globe."

Milla (Guild): "This is an invasion."

Milla (Guild): "I need everyone back at headquarters right now. We will need to be careful about this."

Milla (Guild): "Right now, people. I mean it."

Ray grinned. "What if she kicks you out?"

"Then she kicks me out."

"That's the spirit," Ray said. "I'm coming with you."

Lemira stepped up in front of them. "This is precisely the sort of behavior she is attempting to prevent."

Ray gave her a long look, then grinned even wider. "Yep!"

Hannah looked to Luke, who was about to speak without knowing what to even say, when things got even more complicated. A pink portal shimmered into existence right in front of him.

"What the?" Luke said, stepping back with a frown. Like a few other times, he more sensed than saw a thread emerging from the portal, leading off into the weave. Not the weave of Earth's system, this time, but to The Greater System. As if that wasn't enough, another thread ran from the front of it, right into Luke's chest, connecting him with the portal.

"That's... Interesting," Ray said.

"Luke?" Hannah asked.

It wasn't until then that he realized the pink portal in front of him wasn't the only one. Another had opened in front of Hannah.

"Ignore them," Curtis said. "We've got enough to deal with right here."

"You're just jealous you didn't get one," Ray said.

Curtis frowned. "You didn't 'get' one either."

"What does it mean?" Hannah asked.

"Cerise? I have never heard of a cerise gateway," Lemira said, a hand raised to her mouth in astonishment.

"What's Cerise?" Ray asked.

Hannah sighed. "Pink. Dark pink, kind of."

"We have to go in," Luke said.

"Which one, though?" Ray asked.

Luke glanced over at his friend and shook his head. "They won't work for you this time. Just me and Hannah."

"How do you know?" Hannah asked.

He sometimes forgot others didn't see the multitude of weaves that made up the system, and grasped for words to describe it, coming up short. "It's a Lifeweaver thing."

"Fuck that," Ray said, stepping up to Luke's portal. He reached a hand to it, then let out a disgruntled sigh. "Stupid portal. Won't take me."

"You shouldn't go in. Not now. You could be gone for days if we're unlucky. By then, the Singers will have taken over the damn world," Curtis said.

Lemira brought out her now-healed lion and stroked its fur. "Or the demons."

A tugging on Luke's chest confirmed what he'd suspected since the moment he saw the thread connecting him to the portal. "We won't have a choice. They're pulling us in."

"So resist it," Curtis said.

"Luke?" Hannah asked, holding out her hand.

He grabbed it and pulled her into a hug. "I don't know what this is, Hannah, but I'll see you after, okay? Right here."

Hannah pulled away, her lips brushing his, then nodded and stepped back toward the portal, looking as if someone was hauling her in with a rope. "Right here."

With that, she was gone.

The light drizzle increased in intensity, and Luke looked up at the sky, feeling the water against his face. "I'll try to be quick through whatever this is."

"I will return to guild headquarters with the group of humans you healed here," Lemira said before turning and running away, the lion at her side. Mid-stride, her mount appeared, pushing her up into a riding position. With that, she too was gone.

"Just the boys left," Ray said.

Curtis still hadn't turned away from the direction of the portal. Luke grabbed his shoulder. "Be careful with them. The Singers are no joke."

"I will," Curtis said as his monster core-powered electric scooter appeared in his hands. With that, he was off.

Ray sighed and dove into stealth, his voice sounding a little resigned. "I'll make sure he doesn't die or anything, but it's looking pretty damn bleak out here."

Thunder rumbled in the far distance, and Luke couldn't help but smile. "You've got reinforcements coming. Don't forget, we make our own rules now. If we want to enforce them, we'd better have the balls to back it up."

"To balls!" Ray shouted, and Luke sensed him disappear after Curtis at a speed he'd be hard-pressed to match.

Luke stood alone in front of the pink gate, the glow giving the rain an otherworldly feel. Drops of water struck the drifting motes so they gave off brief twinkles. It felt like the last and first time he'd gone through a pink gate was an eternity ago, but the apprehension was still there. No one would blame him for that, Luke figured, considering the repercussions of meeting Relian and everything that happened after. Even now, events unfolded due to The Fallen Shepherd's meddling. If not for that monster of a man, they might never have met the Singers, and that, in turn, could mean the current invasion wouldn't have happened. A lot of ifs and buts, but his gut told him they were living through the effects of a psychopath's decisions.

Smudge was connected to it all, too. Luke hadn't accepted his new friend being gone. The void was not devoid of life. That meant Smudge could still be out there. If only Luke had healed him when he had the chance.

At this point, Luke felt himself stalling rather than entering the portal. The pull was growing stronger by the second, tugging on his Meta-Heart like a hand on a leash. Had the same happened with the first pink portal? Had the party that went through all felt it without knowing it for what it was? An interesting thought, but not one he had time for.

Luke stepped through the portal. Another message from Milla, and one from Lara, of all people, appeared, but he didn't have time to read them. Once through, all messages disappeared, and he could not send new ones. Damn. The pink portal closed behind him. Not a great sign.

Looking around his new surroundings, a few things were readily apparent. First, he was in a hospital. The room he appeared in was a triage room in an ICU, complete with equipment to make a first assessment of a patient's state. The off-white color on the walls, the lowered, tiled ceiling, and the blinking lights on the door confirmed as much, as well as the faint whiff of disinfectant with undertones of unwashed bodies. Secondly, he was no longer on Earth. Yes, the writing was all in English, and the equipment was familiar, but Weaver's Perception told him none of his friends were near. In fact, he couldn't sense them at all. It was as if he'd been cut off. Even when concentrating, there was no reaching beyond this strange dungeon's own weave. No, wait a moment. Something became visible to his senses.

Luke frowned. "Dot?"

He made an attempt to send a message to her, but it didn't work. Still, there was no mistaking it. What he sensed was her echo. It wasn't too far away, either. Somewhere above him and off to Luke's left. His first impulse was to use Weavestep and reach her right away, but he thought better of it. Not knowing what to expect, he couldn't risk hurting himself that much and wasting more than half his mana. Better play it safe. If Dot just stayed put for a little while, reaching her shouldn't prove too much of a challenge. And if Luke knew her, she would be too afraid to move, since this was her first time in a dungeon.

"I'm coming to get you," he whispered as he stepped toward the closed door out of the examination room. Putting his hand on the handle, he stopped. A sound had just reached in from outside. Someone running. A groan. The squeaking of metal on metal.

This was a dungeon after all. Dressing it up as a hospital wouldn't change that. There would be monsters. Best be careful until he knew more of what he was getting himself into. Even so, there was nothing to gain by staying put in that room.

Before opening the door, he checked his interface and saw the familiar dungeon quest message. He did not like what he read.

New Quest: Unite or eliminate. The void approaches.

Reward: The continued existence of your system.

The stakes of this dungeon just increased to encompass the fate of Earth, if the text was to be believed. Setting his jaw, Luke pushed on. This was not the time to doubt himself.

As he pushed the door open just a crack, someone pulled on it with incredible force, dragging the handle out of Luke's hand. Two men appeared. They wore hospital scrubs, both with flecks of blood on them, and they pushed a wheelchair into the room. The one sitting in that chair made Luke's eyes go wide.

"Singer?" he asked, blinking in confusion as the two human men shouted at him to get out of the way. Stepping back, Luke shook his head. "What's going on here?"

The men hauled the bloodied Singer out of the wheelchair and onto the reclined hospital chair, then turned to Luke. "What are you waiting for, Dr. Quinn? Start the primary survey!"

"What?" Luke grunted, eyes flicking from the Singer to the men waving in his face.

"Airways! Check the airways!"

Luke swallowed hard, cheeks flushing. "What?"
