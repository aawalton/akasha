To pull this off, the timing would have to be immaculate; their margin of error less than a second.

Scarlet opened the bunker door just enough that it was no longer latched shut, leaving less than a centimetre of an opening between the frame and the door. Synching fully with Maus, she set the little guy on the floor facing the wall to the left of the door and in a direct interception path.

All decked out in what she was calling her ‘combat attire’, Scarlet moved far enough back that the door wouldn’t collide with her, but central enough on the landing that she would be within striking distance of the bat.

The slow coalescence of mana began to peter off, and Scarlet sent a ‘ready’ signal towards Maus. The bond was already as open as it could get, and while the constant feedback from Maus was slightly disorienting, she was able to compartmentalize the foreign sensations and focus on what was going on outside.

With the door cracked ever-so-slightly open, it was easier to feel the mana fluctuations. She was also getting significant emotional feedback she hadn’t expected. There was aggression there, but also something like determination and… possessiveness? Apparently, her newly enhanced psionics could be hindered by things like physical barriers. Or maybe it was the new density of mana in the air?

She put ‘wondering what changed’ to the back of her mind as she tracked the shadow bat’s retreat.

The moment it accelerated forward, both she and Maus were ready.

There was a flurry of movement, an abrupt acceleration that distorted the mana around the bat in a way she might have found disorienting if she’d been any less focused.

Five metres, four, two, one: A sudden spike of mana, and then instead of the ‘thump’ of the Skill-reinforced bat hitting unmoving metal, the door was thrown open so violently, it chipped the concrete wall as very stoppable force met easily movable object.

The bat slammed through the gap with a violent burst of motion, its form barely resolving before its remaining inertia carried it just inside the bunker.

Disoriented, it fell to the ground in the same moment its skill wore off.

She didn’t even need to give the order as Maus was already moving. He was on the dark, greyish-black creature about the size of a rugby ball before it even hit the floor.

He vanished in a flicker, his tiny form a blur as he activated both Blink and Ravaging Bite directly behind and on top of the bat. He struck like a cobra, his sharp little teeth finding their target before he Blinked away again, reappearing outside in the yard, barely a metre away from the entrance.

Scarlet was moving the instant before he blinked away, making it to the bat the moment he cleared the space.

Like a buried instinct, she flipped her cane over, midair, and pulled back.

When she’d been perhaps six or seven years old, her father, resenting her presence the few days he’d had custody, had sent her to skating lessons. He refused to enroll her in classes for ‘sissy-ass ice-fairy dancing’ and stuck her in ice hockey instead.

The man had attended one game. Her first game. Back then she still didn’t have particularly good control of blocking out the ‘noise’, and the waves of energy that had buffeted her when the teams hit the ice had been so overwhelming she’d barely been able to skate in a straight line.

While she’d still been painfully disoriented, a teammate had passed her the puck. Ignoring her blurred vision and roiling stomach, she’d swung her hockey stick so hard she managed to turn herself all the way around. A full 360. Then she’d lost her balance and faceplanted into the small black disk that had promptly skittered away.

Her father had never attended another of her games, but she hadn’t let that stop her from practicing her swing with a zeal that bordered on obsession.

She felt that same textbook-perfect form translated through muscle memory as she stepped in, pivoted, then drove forward – through the hips, mind the core – in a clean, controlled swing that would have made any Canadian proud.

The impact was clean, solid. Dead on target.

It barely did any damage.

While worrying about timing she’d neglected to factor in her own low Stats. It didn’t matter how vulnerable the creature was to damage if she didn’t have the strength to deal any.

Honestly, it was a measure of just how fragile the bat had to be that her strike had even managed to stun it.

The impact sent it reeling backward, disoriented, out of the bunker and toward Maus. However, Scarlet hadn’t done any meaningful damage that she could see.

Maus blinked back in, intercepting again before the creature could regain its bearings, forcing it into another erratic tumble as it tried to orient itself.

Thinking fast and realizing blunt damage was not going to get her anywhere, Scarlet changed tactics.

The cane twisted in her grip, her thumb finding the hidden catch in the handle. As she pulled, the head of the cane came free in one smooth motion, revealing the narrow blade concealed within.

Precision over force, she reminded herself. She remained steady, even as her body shifted to compensate for the slight drag in her injured leg.

The bat jerked as it was pummelled by Maus once more. Unfortunately, it had wised up to his attacks, and while he’d managed to clip a wing, he hadn’t been able to get a substantial bite on anything more vital.

The bat was recovering. Maus had been forced to blink away as a sudden spike of mana caused distortions in the air around the bat’s body.

It wasn’t out of mana, and while it didn’t have enough power to shield itself, that didn’t necessarily mean it couldn’t use whatever other Skill it had.

Scarlet was approaching as fast as she could. She could feel as the shadow bat became more frantic, its intent coming through to her clearly. It wanted out. It wanted to escape. After almost an hour of harassment, and a near fatal ambush, this sneaky sky-rat wanted to run away.

The going got tough, so the bat got out. Was that it?

Not on Scarlet’s watch, and with her current Perception, that watch extended quite far.

Scarlet stepped forward, her world narrowed, her thoughts and senses dialed all the way in.

Her focus was not on the creature itself, but on the space it occupied, the space it would occupy, and the potential actions it might make, lines of movement that branched out from its current position like a web of possibilities.

Her perception stretched, sharpened, and she felt her mind accelerate to meet it, calculations snapping into place faster and faster.

She added and discarded variables, taking in its emotional fluctuations along with the faintest mana emanations and most probable escape routes.

She wasn’t trying to aim for where it was, but at where it would be.

Her grip tightened on the blade, and as she adjusted her stance she realised she wasn’t going to make it.

Feeling through the bond, she could sense that Maus was low on mana. He’d already done two big Blinks and a smaller one in just the past ten or so seconds.

She needed to stall the bat for a moment. To keep it off balance enough for her or Maus to get to it.

She thought about throwing her blade, but giving up her weapon like that seemed beyond foolish right then. Who’s to say without it the bat wouldn’t just try to attack her like it had attacked Maus, only this time she’d be weaponless.

Barely three seconds had passed from when she’d pulled out the blade, to watching the bat begin to take flight once more.

That’s when it happened.

A strange, somewhat familiar pressure began to build around her and inside her.

Following her instincts, instead of pulling the emotions inwards to repress them like she used to when the world became too loud, Scarlet chose a single feeling, and using it as a focus, she ‘pushed’.

The bat’s panic compounded, causing some sort of overload, or disruption. She wasn’t quite sure. What she was sure of was that it was doing something to the bat that the creature couldn’t handle.

The effect was subtle, however it was enough to make the creature hesitate.

During its moment of confusion, Scarlet had made it within range.

The blade flashed. The slice was precise, cleanly cutting through the space she’d precalculated in her mind. Unlike when she’d swung on it, the resistance she felt was brief, almost negligible, as the strike landed exactly where it needed to, bisecting the bat with little fanfare.

It hit the ground in two uneven halves, each side still twitching as it landed before both pieces stilled.

Scarlet didn’t move right away.

She watched it for a moment, her breathing heavy but steady. After a few seconds she realised she was still holding the shaft of the cane in her off hand. Chiding herself for the useless thought, she used it to nudge the top half of the bat.

“Quite dead, my dear,” she told Maus. At least this time none of the gore was on her nice, clean clothing.

She let out a slow breath, some of the tension draining from her shoulders.

Her gaze shifted briefly toward Maus, who had reappeared at her feet in a blur of motion. His small, blood-soaked form scrambled up her clean pants and jacket, and settled onto her shoulder.

“Oh, for–”

She stared down at the trail of crimson that led in a straight line from the toe of her boot to her shoulder, and simply sat down. Right there in the grass. In front of the bodies of her defeated foes.

The laugh hit her before she could stop it.

It wasn’t graceful or controlled. It was a laugh of complete catharsis as she sat on the grass under the early night sky.

Her hands came up to cover her face as she hunched over slightly; knees raised, feet planted, and shaking with laughter as the adrenaline bled off in the only way it could.

“Of course,” she managed, watching from the corner of her eye as Maus began to groom himself.

Scarlet dragged in a breath, then another, forcing herself to rein it in as she pulled herself together. In the corner of her eye the notification indicator was glowing brightly. Looked like the System had something to say.
