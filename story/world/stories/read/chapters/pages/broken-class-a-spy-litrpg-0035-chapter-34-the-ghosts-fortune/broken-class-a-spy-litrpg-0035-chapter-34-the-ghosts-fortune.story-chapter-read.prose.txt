Arthur remained a ghost at the edge of civilization, his vigil at the walls of Copperpine a lesson in patience. For days, he studied the city's strict routine. The wilderness outside was too dangerous for anyone to travel alone, a reality that had created a powerful, unwritten rule: people didn't travel solo. To arrive at the gates alone would be to immediately mark oneself as suspicious. Entry was a privilege reserved for organized groups: large caravans hauling goods or registered hunting parties returning from the wilds. Even the hunters were subject to a formal check-in, pausing to pay a tithe from their kills before being granted passage. As a lone outsider, Arthur knew that simply walking up to the gate was impossible.

After concluding that he had learned all he could from a distance, he realized he needed a different angle. If he wanted to find a weakness in the city's formidable defenses, he would have to shift his focus from the place itself to the people who moved in and out of it. The next morning, he spotted his target: a new party of five emerging from the gates, their gear practical and worn, their movements lacking the aristocratic flair of the noble teams. These were hunters, not scions. Professionals who killed for coin, not glory. Arthur melted into the trees, a shadow falling in step with them as they ventured into the forest.

He trailed them from a distance, a silent observer in the canopy. They were good. They communicated with a fluid series of hand signs, their words sparse and reserved for true emergencies. They moved like a unit that had bled together, their trust forged in shared danger, not sworn pacts.

For the first half of the day, the forest was quiet. The hunters moved with a quiet focus, their eyes scanning the undergrowth, their senses sharp. Nothing. Arthur watched, cataloging their formation, their individual tells, the subtle ways they covered for one another.

Around noon, they found their prey. A lone Grubtusk Devourer, grazing placidly in a small clearing. The team fanned out without a word. The fight was not the clean, brutal art of Yudursh, nor the overwhelming force of Rowler's squad. It was a grind. A methodical, attritional battle of endurance. They were efficient, taking few risks, chipping away at the beast’s defenses until it finally collapsed with a shuddering groan. There were no serious injuries, only the exhaustion that came from a hard-won fight.

What came next, however, held Arthur’s attention more than the battle itself. They began to harvest the beast with the reverence of priests at an altar. Hides were carefully skinned, meat was carved and wrapped, and tusks were cleaned. They worked with a quiet intensity, their movements economical and practiced. He looked at the ring on his finger. Drogan had called it a legendary artifact before, but the remnant's boasting was easy to dismiss as arrogance. Seeing them painstakingly pack away monster parts into simple sacks drove the point home with the force of a physical blow. He suddenly understood just how rare and valuable his ring truly was.

Then came the moment of discovery. One of the hunters, a burly man with a thick beard, let out a whoop of pure joy, holding up a small, brown, pulsing lump. A monster core.

The mood of the party erupted.

“A core! A whole one!”

“Thank the stars! We’re set for the month!”

“No more watered-down ale for us, boys!”

They clapped each other on the back, their faces beaming with a relief so profound it was almost painful to watch. They treated the E-grade core like a priceless jewel, a ticket to a month of security and comfort.

Arthur watched from the trees, a slow realization dawning on him. He thought of the dozens of cores he’d consumed, the small stash of them still sitting in his ring, and the menagerie of live, sleeping monsters he had collected. He had been sitting on a treasure hoard without ever understanding its true value. To these men, one core was a month’s wages. To him, it was a snack.

A new plan began to form, cold and brutally efficient. He had no idea if he could get back out into the forest once he was inside Copperpine’s walls, which meant this might be his last chance to gather resources freely. Getting into the city would have to wait. His new priority was clear: amass power in the form of a massive collection of monsters.

The next few days blurred into a relentless cycle of nocturnal predation. While the forest slept, Arthur hunted. He moved silently through the night, his Mana Sight turning the darkness into a map of glowing, vulnerable targets. He didn’t kill. He captured sleeping Grubtusks, slumbering Stonejaw Hounds, and dozing Ironhorn Goats. One by one, they vanished into the stasis of his ring. His collection swelled into the high hundreds, quickly approaching a thousand.

Occasionally, to keep his skills sharp and his lead on the leaderboard unassailable, he would hunt. The kills were quick, silent, and ruthlessly efficient. His movements grew sharper, and his name remained a fixed, untouchable specter at the top of the Great Hunt rankings. He was a ghost, a legend, and a very, very wealthy man who didn't even know it yet.

He was mid-hunt when the chime echoed through his mind.

[System Alert]

The Great Hunt has concluded. All kill counts are now final.

Congratulations, Participant Arthur. You are the victor. Please select your reward.
