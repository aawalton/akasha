The Vistula looked like a shimmering vein of silver running through it all. My goodness, it was making me feel poetic. I could see why they built this inn here, and with this view, the name The Golden Wheel felt appropriate.

“It’s beautiful,” Kasia whispered, leaning her chin on her hand.

After some time the mead and our food arrived. The beaver tails were carefully placed on bright white plates. It kind of looked like a semi-translucent slab of fat. Pouring the sauce over it probably would have made it look a bit more appetizing, but not by much.

Where is the meat? I wondered. Am I just eating wiggly fat?

It did taste expensive thanks to the mixture of rare spices. Rich people liked to eat strange foods and pretend it tasted better than it did. To me it just tasted like I was eating buttery wiggles, and not in a good way. I wondered if it was too late to ask if they also served meat pierogi.

I looked at Kasia, who was already on her second bite. Her eyes were closed. She seemed to be genuinely enjoying it. It was probably the first time in her life tasting saffron.

I poked at the beaver tail and it jiggled slightly. This sucked. I was getting hungry, but I didn’t feel like eating more of this… stuff. I glanced at the well-dressed man, but he wasn’t paying us any attention. He had resumed his conversation with the barman and was spinning his little cup again.

I took a sip of the honey mead. It was quite thick and heavily spiced, but in a good way. It prickled my throat. At least that tasted pleasant.

The more I thought about it the less I wanted to take another bite of beaver tail. Kasia had already finished half of hers, so she clearly liked it. I pushed my plate toward her. “Here. Have mine as well.”

Kasia pulled my plate closer. “If you don’t want it…”

I smacked my mouth, the insides still covered in an oily film. I took another sip of mead, hoping it would reduce the beaver tail coating. The feel and taste were not great.

“I don’t think they’ll care,” she replied, cutting off another piece of beaver tail.

I looked back out of the window. This place was pretty nice and peaceful. Too bad the food sucked. This could have been an amazing experience. I hoped in the future we could have more moments like these, sitting together, enjoying the view without anyone trying to kill us. Only next time with some good food.

I decided I needed something that wasn’t beaver tail and was willing to risk offending the chef since I didn’t want to leave this inn hungry. I waved over the barman.

“Yes. That sounds lovely, thank you.”
