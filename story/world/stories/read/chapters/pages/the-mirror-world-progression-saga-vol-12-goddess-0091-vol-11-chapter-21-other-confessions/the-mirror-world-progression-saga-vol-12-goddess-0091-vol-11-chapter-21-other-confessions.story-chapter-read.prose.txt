The feasting, merriment, and general celebration of the delightful wedding went on through the day and into the night. Redberry dallied here and there. The newlyweds retired early in the afternoon but returned later in the night to enjoy the more chaotic environment developed by then. People got drunk, of course. Servitors were great for carting people off when they passed out.

I know this all too well.

She avoided Zadkiel. After that one regrettable indiscretion, she had every intention from then on of keeping it as one. It wasn’t that it wasn’t enjoyable — it had been exciting and scintillating — it was that she just couldn’t see anything lasting or serious happening with the great demonkin himbo. He just wasn’t her type.

Zadkiel, predictably, remained a bit obsessed, despite her specifically telling him not to expect anything more out of their little liaison. He tried to keep it cool, but she was far too keyed up to the signs and observant of such things. Who could hide such matters from the Goddess of Fertility, after all?

Being aloof and dismissive of his little attempts to stir her interest again worked well enough. He’d found other distractions.

He doesn’t need me, whatever his loins tell him. Anything further clouding his mind is just self-deception. We’d never connect correctly as permanent lovers. He should just take the ‘win.’ Very few males can claim this victory.

Late in the night, she did a little dancing, unable to resist watching all the youthful merriment — it was mostly the young and the immortal still carrying on by then. During an up-and-down tempo dance she favored, where partners constantly switched up and matched with other people, sometimes whirling, sometimes getting slow and potentially intimate, she suddenly found herself being spun around by Zadkiel.

What a surprise.

As she was spun into his arms, and facing outward, she muttered, “Watching me, are you, Zadkiel? You don’t dance much, yet here you are when I am.”

“Who wouldn’t want to be?” he replied in her ear.

Redberry rolled her eyes as she was spun back out, one hand still connected. “A non-stalker, for one.”

Zadkiel smirked and raised a brow. “If a stalker acquires their quarry, what are they?”

“Temporarily lucky. But some quarry avoid what is believed to be a fatal strike…” She began a fluid, playful, but energetic dance, not quite touching her large partner, but moving around him, suiting the song as he did his own due diligence competently, as well. “The heart can be an elusive target, Demon King.”

Zadkiel winced and shook his head as they continued the dance. “I have to re-earn that title, actually.”

Redberry scoffed derisively. She affected a mildly mocking tone as she asked, “Why, because of your contract? Because it wounds your pride? Please. You’re simply under an Empress. Do you think kings don’t bow?”

Zadkiel suddenly formed a typical devil’s grin. “I certainly don’t mind that dynamic in the bedroom.”

Rolling her eyes again, Redberry took the available opportunity in the music’s beat structure to switch partners to a lovely elven lad. Lovely enough to flirt with, actually, but he tightened up and almost tripped once he realized she was indicating interest. Blushing profusely, he recovered to finish up his required moves and then desperately switched partners.

He is too adorable. I feel a bit bad. Perhaps I could set him up with a younger, sweeter dryad, that’ll be less intimidating-

Her thoughts were interrupted as she realized Zadkiel had cut in to be her partner again.

“Seriously?” she asked in exasperation, as she took his hands, danced, and gave him a withering glare at the same time. “You are not supposed to do that in this dance, you oaf. Don’t do it again.”

Frowning for some reason, Zadkiel shrugged. “I need to talk to you, Red. Alone.”

Redberry made a disbelieving face at him, getting only a sober look back, and it made her burst out in laughter. “That’s a clumsy attempt to repeat your success, Zadkiel! How stupid do you think I am?”

The God of War actually growled as he grimaced at her, eyes shining. “I’m not making a pass, I swear.”

The audacity to say that, when she could smell and feel his lust and desire for her, was just too much. Red disconnected their hands and delivered a little push into his torso — not that it moved the giant in her current form, but it emphasized a point. “You’ve ruined my taste for dancing. Congratulations.” She turned and stalked off, seeking a table with drinks.

Idiot! I never should’ve taken him into my bed. He’s addicted already. I knew this would happen; even gods aren’t immune to my charms. Now what?

She got a cup of wine and began sipping it in earnest, rolling her neck and chiding herself for her stupid impulses and the trouble they always got her into.

“Red, it isn’t about what you think,” came his voice again, behind her. “Let me-”

“Shut up, Zadkiel,” Redberry said with a sigh, shaking her head. “Just shut up and go away, before you make me truly angry.”

Instead of obeying, he whispered in a strangely different, lighter voice, “I’ve been lying and pretending to everyone about everything, to an ultimate degree, including who I am, and I want to tell you the truth. Please.”

Caught with the wine cup at her lips, Redberry slowly turned to stare back at him incredulously. The words, the tone, all of it were way off from usual for him.

Zadkiel looked very uncomfortable. Despite his size, from his voice to his demeanor, he seemed smaller. Certainly, he was unspeakably different. She sensed that his admittance was truthful. It piqued sensitivity within her Domain, Freedom, for some mysterious reason. That made her very curious indeed.

Moreover, he seemed more vulnerable in this moment than she’d ever seen him, even wounded on battlefields, even naked and bound on her bed. This was something far more than physical exposure.

Zadkiel seemed to see something in her expression that made him sigh defeatedly, and he looked away. His voice was more normal, though cracking somewhat, as he continued, “You can have someone else with us if you want. Preferably, only your most trusted. Preferably, Vinewicker alone.”

Redberry, shocked by the whole matter in general, struggled for words. “Y-... You truly just aim to talk?” She almost winced at how stupid it sounded in her ears.

Zadkiel shook his head as a hand came up to rub his chin, his expression thoughtful.

He has thoughts? Nonsexual, non-battle-related thoughts?!

“Not just talk, per se,” Zadkiel replied. “It’s more like a revelation to reveal. But you have my word, this is not about making a pass. It’s about…” He seemed to fight and struggle for words, swallowing hard. “I want you to know who I really am. Who I thought I merely was, and left behind… but certain people in my life revealed it was a hollow within me. I believe it has been robbing me of something, leaving me intrinsically unfulfilled. One of those people was Samantha, one Azure, and the other… is you.”

Finally, Zadkiel sighed and met her eyes, his now miserable. “I realize this incredibly unappealing, inane chatter has probably bored and disturbed you, but I hope you’ll humor me all the same.”

Redberry’s hand with the wine had dropped down, as she’d listened with rapt attention. She shook her head. “It’s just the opposite, Zadkiel.” For perhaps the first time, she found herself speaking gently to him. “I can see this is authentic. You already sound… different.” She took his hand. “Come, then. Let’s hear it. Privately.”

Zadkiel’s eyes were wide in near shock as she led him away, putting the wine cup down along the way. She mentally pinged Jeeves to direct her to a private meeting chamber available, which he did by sending a Resemblant to lead them.

Before long, they were sitting down in a small tea room on a couch, the door locked with guarantees of no interruptions. Zadkiel sat down rather uncomfortably, his hands on his knees, looking down. Again, she was struck by the sense of his genuine vulnerability. No veneers, here, just a blend of shame, reservation, and a growing undercurrent of contrasting excitement.

Redberry sat, her legs crossed, peering over at him and raising an eyebrow. “Well? Out with it, Zadkiel. You’ve intrigued me, so don’t leave me waiting. I’m burning to know the intrigue at this point.”

Zadkiel glanced at her and nodded, taking a deep breath as he leaned back, hands folded in his lap. “My origin is a lie. A complete fabrication crafted to appeal to the iconic machismo of the Naugite power structure and those who hold the power within it.” Ironically, Zadkiel’s new manner of speaking seemed to be getting more natural for him than she’d seen before, supporting his words quite tangibly. “Rather than being a man’s man rising to the top of this ‘pyramid of prowess,’ I started just the bloody opposite, Red. I was nothing. Less than, in the eyes of my society.”

There was a long pause as Zadkiel looked off, and he was clearly lost in bitter memories. His expression was sour. “It’s… best to just sh-...” He swallowed and squeezed his eyes shut, briefly. “It’s best to show you at this point. I… have had this prepared for a long time. I had it ready, even that time… the night we were together. But that happened, and I lost the nerve, felt the fear all over again, and didn’t want to lose what I’d gained. Now it's clear I’d only attracted your temporary fancy, and I’ll likely obtain nothing more of your interest romantically, either way. I want to do this, now” — he finally met her eyes — “simply because I want you to know. To understand why I am the way I am.”

Finally, he rose, turned toward her, and began to shapeshift — began to shrink.
