Chapter 59: Impaired

Jessica wasn’t sure how much time had passed in that dim, dank room, exhaling thick clouds of smoke. When she wasn’t taking a puff, she held on to the bottom of the hookah for dear life. It felt like she was spinning in one direction, and the room going another. She was painfully aware of every part of her body, every cramp and kink, but they were far away, and more curiosity than concern.

Things had been fun, listening to Dexi and her brother Sohx catch up and talk about family. Fun, and a little horrifying. Since she was an only child, Jessica couldn’t quite understand what it was like to have over a dozen siblings at one point, their continued laughter at the ones who didn’t make it did strike her as a bit dark.

But who cared? The spinning was fun, and for the first time in years, she wasn’t about to crack from that inexorable sense of pressure that followed her. For the first time since she was a preteen, Jessica wasn’t the youngest and weakest Vanderborn, trying to carry the weight of her family’s expectations and disappointment on her back.

She was just some lump in a kobold den of vice, puffing away while her new friend cackled every so often. Her throat and eyes burned, and she badly wanted a drink of something cold, but that could wait until later, when moving sounded appealing again.

“Lookit her, she’s about to pass the fuck out,” Sohx’s lower, darker voice rasped. “Let’s rob her!”

Jessica murmured a little, but didn’t look up.

“Oh, don’t be that way. She’s not too bad. A bit stuffy at times, but she’s a good egg. We should probably get moving before that big guy has to carry her and the box. I sure as hell ain’t doing it.”

A second later, they got up and helped Jessica to her feet. It was a struggle at first, and she giggled relentlessly as they maneuvered her through the smoking building. Before they could get her to the door, Jessica pulled away and went to the bar.

“Water,” she said, throat turning into the Taklan desert in a matter of seconds.

The kobold working the bar looked at her companions, then filled a mug of lukewarm water for her. She drained it and asked for another, which he filled. It helped clear her head a little, but she still wasn’t thinking of much.

“Ahh, I forgot mammals have a tougher time with the stuff. Hells, it’s going to be a pain to get her back up to the trains. C’mon Jess, let’s go.”

She did as she was told, and the moment they were out of the unmarked building, Raeleq let out a distressed sound.

“What did you do to her?”

“What? Nothing,” said Dexi from a mile away.

“Yeah, she did this to herself. We just kinda didn’t stop her.”

She looked up to see Raeleq drawing himself up to his full height, genuine anger in her eyes. “What is wrong with you? You know we have stuff we need to get done. We can’t afford to have her like…like this!”

Dexi had the good grace to look at least a little ashamed. It was kind of funny, since Jessica had never seen it before, but the kobold shrank and looked down. “Yeah, not a great call. But the good news is I got a lot of leaf for the road, so we won’t be slowed down anymore.”

“I’m okay,” Jessica finally said, though it was like through a mouth of cotton. “I’ll be fine. Just a bit foggy. But people smoke here all the time, right?”

Silence.

“Well, kobolds do,” said Dexi. “Humans, sometimes, but it’s not as socially accepted, if you take my meaning. You’ll be fine, really! You just might get a few dirty looks. But honestly, they’ll just ignore us even harder.”

Raeleq sighed and lugged the Box over his shoulder once more. “No more detours, no more problems. Understood?”

Dexi nodded and took Jessica’s hand. “Yeah yeah yeah, I got it. Let’s get going now, okay? C’mon Jess. Bye loser.”

“Bye worm-breath,” Sohx returned with a wave. “I’ll miss you like I miss a bad hangover. After a few years, I’ll tell our moms, okay?”

Once Dexi guided them out of the maze of small, squat buildings in the kobold section of town, Jessica found her head getting more clear. It was still warm out here, but not stifling and stuffy. The relatively cool breeze helped her focus, though everything still swam and wiggled.

She normally took pride in her body. It was hard work to not only keep up with studies, but also stay fit and, more importantly, graceful.

At the moment, she wasn’t graceful at all. Climbing the stairs felt like learning to walk all over again, and she had to cling to the railing to deal with the vertigo. What the hell was that? She’d never had vertigo in her life, and actually liked high places, most of the time. Now, when Jessica’s eyes followed the stairs all the way to the top, it made her feel like she was falling up.

Raeleq steadied her with a hand on her shoulder. She looked up, expecting to see more of that disappointment, but there was only concern. “Do you need to take a minute or two to sit down?” he asked.

“No,” she lied, “I can handle this.”

It was clear he didn’t believe her, but he said nothing. Dexi, however, never missed an opportunity to yammer. “Honestly, you took to it like a beetle to shit. Give it a few more times and it’ll get easier.”

“Why would she want to?” Raeleq scoffed, but dropped it.

They took their time climbing, with occasional breaks. Bedrock Spire was stupidly tall, Jessica decided, and the lack of lifts or ways to quickly traverse levels put it at one of the worst cities she’d ever been to. At least in other places they had public transit, or were laid out in a way that made sense from a community standpoint, and not just a way to maximize resource extraction.

Just as Dexi’Lynn promised, the smell kept people apart. At no point during their long, tedious climb did they have to worry about others getting too close or stopping them. The worst that happened was some wrinkled noses and muttered insults. A paranoid part of her screamed that it would be what got people to turn them in.

No sooner did she have the thought then a siren blared, harsh and inescapable.

Jessica whirled around and nearly fell again, but braced herself. Raeleq dropped the Levi-Box with an alarming thud and clamped his hands over his ears, while Dexi’Lynn squeaked and dropped. It went on for ten seconds, like the growing yowl of a cat being tortured, only to fade and rise again.

Then it cut out, and a voice played, feminine and exact, “Attention Bedrock Spire. Remain calm, but there are intruders here, fugitives wanted by the Emperor. Guards will be conducting sweeps, so please remain calm and remain indoors until their job is done. And remember, if you see something, say something. Be safe, and praise Emperor Turhan!”

And just like that, terror pierced through Jessica’s intoxication and cut her down to her soul. They’d made it, what, nearly two hours before they were detected? Stupid stupid stupid.

“Maybe it’s another group of fugitives?” Dexi’Lynn tried weakly.

Raeleq picked up the box, his own fear worn openly on his face. “We need to move, now.”

“Yes,” said Jessica, forcing her brain to work. “Back to the trains. We’ll find some place to take shelter there, like we agreed. We have to trust the others to do the same.”

They moved faster then. She thought that there would be more suspicion, but no one paid them any attention. Every person they came across, be they human, molekin, kobold, or even other, everyone got the hell out of the way and towards the nearest building. She overheard one person crossing their path mutter, “That’s fine, I didn’t want to meet my quota today anyway. Fucking rebels.”

Jessica couldn’t help but laugh, and Dexi’Lynn joined her. It forced her to take a moment and look up. The vertigo came back, but she had a better impression of how far up they were now. It was only another three levels or so to the trains. They wouldn’t be running, obviously, but they didn’t know the city enough to meet their friends anywhere else.

Those stairs were harder than the rest, and her lungs burned by the end. That was to say nothing of poor Raeleq, who she’d never seen look this drained before, not even after the fight with the Spider Queen. He gently eased the Levi-Box to the ground and collapsed beside it.

The train level was the same odd mix of chaos and quiet terror as the other levels. The shops and kiosks were still manned, but some had put up signs stating they’d be back later and hid. The guards were nowhere to be seen yet, but it seemed no one wanted to be out and about when they did make an appearance.

“Now what?” Dexi’Lynn asked. “There’s a pub a bit down the ways. We could sit in there for the next hour, and pray that the guards find some other poor bastards to drag in.” Her tongue darted out, wetting her snout hopefully.

“No,” Raeleq said through heaving breaths. “No more smoking, or drinking, or anything that might make things worse! Now is not the time.” He wasn’t angry, he was pleading.

Jessica’s thoughts went to their friends. Did they do something to tip the authorities off? Did Ambrose mouth off to the wrong person? Did Raeva react negatively to guards doing a sweep? Luthor was good at keeping people out of trouble, but he couldn’t stop people from being themselves.

The fear nibbled at the back of her mind, seeking to sink its teeth in deep. She breathed, but it was labored. All that previous nervousness, the anxiety that suffocated her everyday, came back with a vengeance. One quick look at the wild wizard showed he shared her terror, and neither of them knew what to do.

Anyone else in the group, they’d be more decisive, be able to make the call for everyone. Anyone but her and Raeleq.

“Look, you two are starting to freak out, and that’s not the time for it, right?” Dexi’Lynn tried with uncharacteristic gentleness. “For what it’s worth, I’ve been through sweeps like this before. They tend to ignore us If we act like we don’t care, like we’re equal parts mad about the inconvenience and scared of the guards. We’ll be invisible. C’mon, let’s get to a bar. I’ll only have one drink,” she added before Raeleq could protest.

Jessica stood up, ignoring the trembling in her knees. It was absurd, but she helped Raeleq to his feet as well. Or at least held his hand while he pushed himself off the ground. He flashed her a grateful half-smile, and they looked in the direction Dexi’Lynn had pointed.

“What if they’re already caught?” Raeleq asked, voice way smaller than it should be from a man of his size.

“Then they’ll get us before too long,” said Dexi with a shrug. “If that’s going to happen, I’d rather have a drink in hand when it happens.”

It was good logic, even if Jessica knew she would only get water. And lots of it. “Okay,” she said, “let’s -- “

An explosion from somewhere above them killed all hope of that.

“Ambrose…” Jessica groaned.

“Or Raeva,” he groaned.

They didn’t have to confer. Raeleq grabbed the box and slung it over his shoulder once more. Rather than take shelter, they followed the next stairs up, towards the sound of fighting.
