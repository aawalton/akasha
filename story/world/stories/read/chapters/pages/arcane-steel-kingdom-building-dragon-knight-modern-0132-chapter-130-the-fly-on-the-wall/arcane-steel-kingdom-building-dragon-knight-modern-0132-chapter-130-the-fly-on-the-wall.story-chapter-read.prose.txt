After sundown, the Cinderfall war council convened.

They met in a hastily erected stone redoubt, an ugly, brutalist structure built specifically to house the King. It sat miles behind their frontline, a stark, grey block amidst a sprawling sea of crimson canvas.

The building bristled with magical wards and barriers that lit up the night sky, a glowing, neon sign pointing directly to the most important people on the battlefield. Further out from the King’s redoubt were the ornate, sprawling tents of the high nobility, each one demarcated by its own localized, shimmering energy shield. Beyond them sat the tents of the lesser nobles and generals. Finally, miles away in the mud and the dark, was the ragtag, sprawling encampment of the regular army.

The class system was mapped out in the dirt, visible from orbit.

My army functioned differently. Every living member of the Aegis Legion was a highly trained officer or specialist. The higher-ups did not receive exclusive, palatial lodgings. General Goliath slept in the same reinforced bunkers as his junior lieutenants. The only "luxury" a senior officer received was a private room instead of a bunk bed, and an attached kitchenette to brew their own coffee.

In my Legion, equality was a logistical necessity. The Warlords regularly ate in the same mess halls as the cadets. They ate the exact same MREs. The only exception was Commodore Theramore, who routinely caused a ruckus in the naval galleys demanding extra portions of protein and whatever passed for dessert.

My attention drifted back to the live feed illuminating the CIC.

The Cinderfall nobles had finally settled into their heavy, ornate chairs. The heavy oak doors swung shut as King Theron entered the room, his face a mask of weary, suppressed rage.

This was their most highly classified, top-secret strategy meeting. Only the highest-ranking generals, the most powerful nobles, and the King himself were permitted inside.

And the fly on the wall.

The fly was an advanced, miniaturized surveillance drone, currently clinging to the ceiling molding directly above the King's head, feeding crisp, high-resolution audio and video straight to my flagship.

The room was also bugged to hell and back. Patricia's Shadow Order had been busy. The ornate chairs, the heavy chandelier, even some of the jewelry the nobles were wearing contained active listening devices. I had a front-row seat to the enemy’s war council.

The meeting began with an emotional, highly dramatic account of the afternoon's parley. The speaker was none other than Sir Dumaric Farthallow.

His version of events was creative.

Listening to him, you would think he had single-handedly cowed the entire Aegis Legion. In his telling, the "elven representative" had practically groveled in the ash, begging for forgiveness in the face of Dumaric’s overwhelming valor and the sheer majesty of Cinderfall.

According to Dumaric, he had bravely refused our terms. He claimed he had orchestrated the duel to force us into a corner, giving Cinderfall the opportunity to set "reasonable and favorable" terms for the war.

I watched the grizzled generals and proud lords nodding along with this entirely fabricated narrative, murmuring their approval. I was dying from second-hand embarrassment.

"With this opportunity I have created," Dumaric concluded, puffing out his chest and sweeping his gaze dramatically around the room, finally stopping at the King, "we can force them to fight in a head-on charge. A war of honor and valor, not one of shadows and deceit. We can forbid the use of their cowardly, long-range weapons and their invisible sky-demons. We will rob them of their greatest advantages and ensure a glorious victory for Cinderfall!"

The nobles erupted in unanimous agreement, thumping the table.

I sat back in my chair on the CIC of The Aegis, utterly dumbfounded.

Did they actually think winning a single duel meant I would agree to ground my air force and silence my artillery?

By their logic, I thought, rubbing my temples, what if our representative wins? Can I just demand that no phoenixes or magical beasts are allowed to participate in the war? That's asking a nation to cripple itself. It's humiliating. Am I the one lacking common sense here, or are these people completely delusional?

[Master,] Tes’s voice chimed in my mind, a calm stream of logic cutting through the absurdity. [Your strategic measures are highly unorthodox according to this world's standards. Warfare here relies heavily on precedent and agreed-upon rules of engagement. Because your methods are unprecedented, they fall into a gray area that the enemy believes can be regulated through formal dueling contracts.]

My attention snapped back to the feed. Another noble had stood up, shifting the discussion to my supposed military strength and who might represent me in the duel.

The debate quickly turned heated. The consensus in the room was that the absolute highest-ranking combatants in my army were Tier 8.

A lord with a scarred face stood up, recounting the recent massacre in Oakhaven. He detailed how his retreating armada had been annihilated by the "Lord of house Wight" and a massive, starlit dragon. He confirmed that the mana signatures radiating from the man and the beast were unmistakably Tier 8.

As another general pointed out, leaders of an army did not participate in parley duels. The duels were to the death. Having King Theron or myself enter the ring would mean a swift end to the war if we fell. A representative had to be chosen.

Cinderfall's intelligence concluded that I had no other Tier 8 combatants besides myself and my dragon.

It was a wildly inaccurate assumption. They didn't know about Bob. They didn't know about Aquarius. Their information sources were severely lacking.

It was true that my organic army was light on Tier 8 heavy hitters. That was by design. I made up for it with naval tonnage and air superiority. Who needs to spend decades training a Tier 8 mage when you can build a Sovereign-class supercarrier in a few months?

King Theron slammed his gauntleted hand onto the armrest of his throne. The loud crack silenced the murmuring lords.

"That is absurd," the King growled, his voice heavy with disbelief and a deep, festering anger. "Even Kaelen Wight took over a century to reach Tier 8, and he was considered a once-in-a-generation genius, breaking all known records. Are you telling me his whelp achieved the same in a fourth of the time?"

Finding out I matched him in raw power had struck a very raw nerve.

The murmurs resumed, lower this time. I noted with grim satisfaction that any mention of Ela had been carefully scrubbed from the official reports presented to the King. The soldiers in Oakhaven had been too terrified to mention the little girl.

Theron raised a hand, silencing the room once more.

"Let this be a lesson," the King stated, his amber eyes hard and unforgiving. "Next time an enemy shows up at our doorstep, we end them immediately. If we had acted decisively right when he first appeared at Oakhaven, we wouldn't be fighting this war in our own lands."

He took a slow, heavy breath. "Now, regarding the candidate to represent us in tomorrow's duel."

The room held its breath.

"It shall be Sir Xeroth," the King announced. "Knight of the First Flame."

He stood up, ending the discussion, and strode from the room. The remaining nobles marveled in hushed, awed tones, clearly agreeing that this was the absolute best choice.

I leaned forward. Tes, what the hell is the First Flame, and what is its knight?

[Master,] Tes began, her data retrieval instantaneous. [In Aethelgardian mythology, the First Fire was a localized cataclysm—a massive lightning strike from the heavens that created an ever-burning, unquenchable flame, powered by the planet's own ambient mana network.]

[This flame is considered a sacred, priceless artifact by the dwarves, and by Cinderfall. The dwarves originally possessed it, until the First King of Cinderfall claimed it through conquest. He utilized the immense, concentrated thermal energy of the First Flame to incubate and hatch his mount—the Mother Phoenix.]

[Consequently, for any phoenix to be born with the genetic and magical potential to reach Tier 8, its egg must be incubated in the direct warmth of that original fire. The knights who successfully bond with these rare, Tier 8 phoenixes are inducted into the Order of the First Flame. They are the absolute elite of the Cinderfall military. They spend their days guarding the sacred fire in the capital. They answer only to King Theron and the Mother Phoenix.]

"Tes," I asked, my mind running tactical simulations. "What is the probability that Bob and Aquarius can beat a Knight of the First Flame in a duel to the death?"

[In a one-on-one engagement, General Goliath and Aquarius possess a significant elemental advantage. Water counters Fire. Furthermore, a single Dragon Knight is fundamentally superior to a Phoenix Knight in raw destructive output. The Hegemony's doctrine relies on numerical superiority and attrition. While a Phoenix Knight's regenerative capability is formidable, it typically requires a coordinated strike team of several Phoenix Knights to overwhelm a single Dragon Knight in the field.]

I keyed the command console. "Ping the Warlords. We need an immediate briefing."

The faces of my commanders lit up on the floating holographic screens around the CIC. Valen, Theramore, Ivela, Mirelle, Patricia, and Bob all appeared, their expressions shifting to crisp professionalism.

"Briefings first," I said, leaning over the console. "Valen, Crawler construction status."

Valen nodded smoothly. "The primary chassis of Crawler One is at 11.5% completion, Lord Commander. Crawler Two is holding steady at 9%. The elven engineering squads are working continuous shifts, and the automated builder units are running on a rotating charge cycle."

"Mirelle, supply lines," I prompted.

"Stable, my Lord," Mirelle reported, adjusting her spectacles. "The transport relays from the Obsidian Fang are maintaining schedule. Commodore Theramore's escorts have ensured no losses to our material shipments across the Maelstrom."

"I haven't even seen a Cinderfall ship in a week," Theramore grunted from his screen, looking vaguely disappointed. "The sea is quiet. Too quiet."

"Keep your eyes open, Commodore," I replied. "Ivela, aerial superiority?"

"Total dominance, Lord Commander," Captain Ivela stated, her eyes fierce. "The enemy has grounded their remaining griffins. We own the sky over the valley. The C-130s are loitering on station, ready for fire missions."

"Patricia," I finally turned to the feed from the intelligence director. "Shadow Order activity?"

"The inland purge continues," Patricia reported, her voice calm and lethal. "Cinderfall command structures in the southern territories are collapsing. Morpheus Black’s mages are capitalizing on the confusion. The Hegemony is bleeding from within."

I nodded, satisfied. The machine was running perfectly. Now, I needed to throw a wrench into the enemy's gears.

I shared the audio and video clips I had captured from the Cinderfall war council. I watched my commanders' reactions. Valen had to press a hand over his mouth to hide a smirk during Dumaric’s heavily embellished tale of the parley. Theramore scoffed loudly.

"They intend to send a Tier 8 combatant," I said, bringing the briefing to the crux of the matter. "Sir Xeroth, Knight of the First Flame."

I turned my attention to the screen showing the General of the Steel Tide. Bob looked tired, but his eyes were sharp.

"Bob," I said, my voice dropping the formal, command cadence. I spoke to him as a friend. "I need you to step into the ring tomorrow. I need you to be my champion."

Bob didn't hesitate. He didn't ask about the opponent or the odds.

He simply slammed his massive, gauntleted fist against his chest plate. The sound echoed clearly through the audio feed.

"My loyalty lies with you, my Lord," Bob rumbled, his voice a bedrock of absolute conviction. "I am your blade. It would be my greatest honor to avenge my fallen brothers, the Dragon Knights of Aerthos."
