Descending the strata of the sect was far easier than ascending, and with Lilian by their side, Aodhán and Crixis soon made their way toward the core sect.

Aodhán had picked Crixis up after Lilian—or Elder Vaust, as was her proper title—had insisted on accompanying him to see the triplets. As they flew through the skies of the sect, Crixis frowned thoughtfully.

“So let me get this straight,” he said slowly. “Our cores are basically transmutation engines?”

“Yes,” Aodhán nodded.

He had explained everything he’d gleaned from the texts to them, and both Lilian and Crixis seemed just as fascinated by the revelation as he had been.

Not shocked, though.

Everyone in Gordon already understood the dangers of absorbing unaligned cores. It was common knowledge. But there was a vast difference between knowing something was dangerous and understanding why it was dangerous.

“It really does put things into perspective,” Elder Vaust murmured. “We’ve always known impurities formed within the core from absorbing unaligned energies, but viewing the process as transmutation…” She shook her head slowly. “I wonder how many people had to die before someone realized it.”

Aodhán grimaced.

His mind flashed briefly to Councilman Balor and the countless others who had lost their lives to the Limit back in Lutia.

That number was impossible to measure.

Still, amidst all that darkness, there had been one massive silver lining—something he had only realized after leaving the library.

His oath chains had not reacted at all when he considered teaching the people of Lutia about the core being a transmutation engine.

Not even slightly.

And he had quickly realized why.

The information did not explicitly reveal the cause of the Limit or how to prevent it. But if he could somehow teach people to view the core differently…

If they could reach the realization themselves…

Aodhán let out a soft breath and glanced downward as they breached the barrier separating the special sect from the core sect. The energy density dropped sharply, and while both he and Elder Lilian grimaced instinctively, Crixis released a relieved sigh.

“By the storm,” he muttered, “this feels amazing.”

Aodhán looked at him with amusement. “If you love the core sect so much, I can have you transferred down here.”

“Don’t even joke about that,” Crixis snorted. “The air is just better here. Less like I’m breathing liquid.”

Aodhán chuckled. “You need to build spiritual stamina.”

“I am building it,” Crixis sighed dramatically. “Unfortunately, not all of us possess terrifyingly powerful spirits and bottomless willpower to help stave off the discomfort.”

Aodhán shook his head in amusement and looked down at the sprawling city beneath them.

The core sect was far smaller than the outer sect, yet leagues larger than the special sect. Several towering structures stretched across the landscape beneath them, their surfaces covered in shimmering runes that gleamed silver beneath the moonlight. The voices of hundreds of cultivators drifted through the air, and from somewhere below, Aodhán heard the raucous laughter of a group deep into their drinks.

A faintly nostalgic smile tugged at Aodhán’s lips as he remembered his first days in the outer sect and the drunken friends he had made. Just what was Rhetta up to these days?

His thoughts were interrupted as Crixis muttered, “Well, it’s certainly a lot noisier than the special sect.”

“As it should be,” Lilian nodded. “There are thousands of disciples down there. Many of them arrogant.”

Aodhán smiled.

Of course they were arrogant.

The core sect was the highest level an advanced-class cultivator could reach unless they were double inheritors or transmigrants, who, well, were a special case. It was home to geniuses and champion candidates like Cyrus and Lillith Bloodmoon—cultivators capable of rivaling the champions of Ragnarok within the same tier.

If Artemis or Geneva had been storm cultivators, this was where they would have belonged.

Powerful enough to dominate nearly anyone within their class. Strong enough to battle seven or eight tiers above their own.

Yet unable to cross the overwhelming divide between classes.

Chuckling faintly, he glanced at Lilian. “Where do we go from here?”

She clasped her hands sagely. “The residential district would be the best place to start. At least one of the triplets should be home.”

Aodhán nodded, and a few minutes later, they descended toward the entrance to the residential district. Unfortunately, despite keeping Varéc tucked away in his spirit to avoid being recognized, their arrival did not go unnoticed for long.

If the war had done one thing, it was make every Special known throughout the sect. Everyone had watched it from their homes.

Everyone recognized him.

And the moment a few disciples caught sight of his telltale midnight skin and frost-white hair, excitement rippled through the surrounding area in an instant.

Heads turned. Eyes widened. Whispers rose.

Then became noise.

Only a handful had noticed him so far, but judging by the rapidly growing commotion, Aodhán knew it was only a matter of time before the entire sect learned of his presence.

He turned to Lilian. “Is there any way to avoid this?”

“Not really,” Lilian smirked. “But if we move quickly enough, we might postpone the inevitable.”

“Then let’s postpone it.”

Aodhán teleported toward the wide-eyed guard at the entrance, a tier 50 cultivator about a dozen years older than himself and bowed politely.

“Good evening, sir.”

The man blanched.

“G-Good evening, Crown of Ruining Storms,” he stammered, bowing hastily in return. “Who... who are you here to see?”

“The Vaust triplets,” Lilian answered, gliding smoothly to stand in front of Aodhán. She rested a finger lightly on the counter and lowered her voice. “Do they live together or separately?”

The guard swallowed. “Together, Elder. Can I know why you’ve come to see them?” His gaze darted hastily to Aodhán before snapping back. “I need it to keep a proper record.”

“I need to ask them a few questions,” Aodhán answered. “If you could point us to their residence quickly, I would be most grateful.”

“Of course, Special Disciple Aodhán.”

The guard immediately pulled out a small registry book, flipping through the pages with frantic speed while trying—and failing—not to stare at Aodhán.

While that happened, those who had spotted Aodhán began gathering, both their courage and their numbers as they approached him.

Aodhán grimaced, his eyes narrowing as he mentally urged the guard to hurry.

This was the exact reason he had avoided visiting the other strata of the sect until now. Every time he visited the elder sect, none of the elders behaved like this. They simply smiled, nodded, or waved whenever they saw him.

Why couldn’t the core sect do the same?

“Because they believe that somehow, if they try hard enough, they can become you,” Lilian replied, reading his frown as easily as an open book.

When he glanced at her, she smiled faintly. “The elders feel the same way too, trust me. We’re just better at hiding it.”

Aodhán regarded her for a moment before letting out a resigned sigh.

Had he come here alone, he would have simply activated Stealth and avoided this entire situation altogether.

But things were different now.

He was no longer just Aodhán Brystion the transmigrant. He was the Crown of Ruining Storms. And that name came with responsibilities exactly like this.

Even without looking, he could sense the absurd number of cores rushing toward the entrance as cultivators called their friends over to see him.

If he wanted to postpone this, he only had seconds left to do so.

He turned back to the guard and was just about to hurry him when the man suddenly brightened.

“I found it!” He looked between Aodhán and Lilian, clearly unsure whom to address. In the end, he settled on Aodhán. “House 107. I can take you there if you’d like.”

“That won’t be necessary.”

Aodhán summoned Varéc from his spirit immediately and smiled as the Fury burst forth with a thunderous roar, his midnight scales gleaming beneath the moonlight.

He had grown tremendously since Aodhán arrived at the sect, standing nearly twenty-three feet tall at full height with a wingspan stretching over sixty feet.

His roar shook the earth and sent chills racing down the spines of everyone present.

Aodhán smiled.

Varéc had always been intimidating, but as he landed before him—massive horns arching backward and black fangs gleaming in the moonlight—Aodhán couldn’t have been prouder of the Fury he had become.

An aura of storm and time radiated from him, instinctively stirring fear in the hearts of those nearby, and when Aodhán glanced around, he realized more than half the crowd that had gathered to see him had abruptly vanished.

Even Lilian seemed to freeze for a brief moment.

The only person who didn't seem particularly intimidated was Crixis, though even he flinched subtly when Varéc turned and grinned at him, rows of dark fangs catching the moonlight.

Aodhán’s smile widened into a grin as he calmly walked forward and climbed onto his familiar. After settling into place, he looked over at Crixis.

“Would you like to ride with me?”

“Absolutely not,” Crixis replied with a tight smile. “You go on ahead. Elder Vaust and I will meet you there.”

The mention of her name seemed to shake Lilian free of her momentary fear, and she immediately straightened. “Ri-right. We’ll come join you.”

“Okay,” Aodhán replied, amusement plain on his face as he rubbed Varéc’s back and whispered, “Let’s go, buddy.”

Varéc crouched low, fangs bared in a predatory grin as his massive wings unfurled with a sound like cracking thunder.

Dust exploded outward. Wind surged in every direction, and the ground fractured beneath his claws as he launched into the air, the force of the takeoff sending roaring gusts tearing through the entire area.

Several nearby disciples stumbled backward, eyes wide as saucers, and Aodhán barely suppressed a chuckle at the sight.

All of this was completely unnecessary, but as long as it kept the overly enthusiastic cultivators at a distance, he was more than happy to indulge Varéc’s dramatics.

Inhaling deeply, Aodhán let himself relax as Varéc soared into the clouds before descending once more to glide above the core sect like a cloud of roiling storms...

Or perhaps, he smiled faintly, a cloud of ruining storms.

Grinning to himself, Aodhán looked down at the sect below, his eyes scanning the neat rows of houses for number 107.

Unlike the special sect, there were no sprawling mansions here, but the homes were still beautiful—far superior to the communal housing offered in the outer sect.

Several cultivators stood in the streets, eyes wide as Varéc glided overhead, but Aodhán barely spared them a glance.

He spotted House 107 a few seconds later, and his eyebrows rose slightly when he saw the triplets already waiting outside, their expressions apprehensive as they stared at the Fury descending toward them.

Even from above, Aodhán could feel their tension. Could sense the fear in the way they held themselves.

The sight sent a chill down his spine, and he realized that, consciously or not, he had built a reputation founded on power... and fear.

He wasn't sure how he felt about that.

As usual, the inheritor stood at the forefront, her core blazing with greater intensity than those of her siblings.

At tier twenty-six, she was the only one among them to have stepped into the advanced class. And from the instability in her aura, Aodhán suspected the advancement had happened recently.

A week ago, perhaps.

Varéc descended slowly, his massive wings kicking up dust and whipping the air into a frenzy as he settled onto the ground.

For a brief moment, the only sound was the faint crackle of lightning dancing across his scales. Then Aodhán slid from Varéc's back, his boots touching the ground as his robes rippled around him.

The girls bowed immediately, and for half a second, Aodhán found himself at a loss for words.

His eyes widened slightly as he realized these girls were the first set of people to ever bow to him. Aside grom Crixis, of course.

He truly had built up a reputation.

Not too long ago, they had been taking the initiation trial together, and now they were bowing to him.

His throat tightened, but he pushed through it and cleared his throat.

“Rise.”

Lilian and Crixis arrived at that moment and moved to stand beside him as the girls straightened.

“Good evening, Crown of Ruining Storms,” the inheritor said, her silver gaze fixed intently on him. “We cannot express how honored we are that you would land at our doorstep. How may we be of service?”

Aodhán regarded her quietly for a moment before speaking. “What are your names?”

The question seemed to catch her off guard, and she exchanged glances with her sisters before answering.

“I...” She straightened slightly. “I'm Lysera Axilas Vaust.”

“I'm Lorielle,” the one on the right said.

“And I'm Lunareth,” the one on the left added.

Aodhán smiled faintly. “It's nice to meet all of you. I bear no ill will toward you. I'm simply here to ask a question.”

The girls visibly relaxed at that, and Lysera allowed herself a small smile. “What is your question, Crown of Ruining Storms?”

“I recently read a book on transmutation written by two of your ancestors—a duchess and her sister, Selena and Lorene Vaust.” Aodhán pulled out his notepad and held it toward her.

Lysera accepted it carefully, and he continued.

“The book discussed something called pure—or primal—energy, which transmuters can apparently access during transmutation. But in the dedication, the author implied that her son, your great-grandfather, Benevolence Axilas Vaust, cultivated a path capable of doing something similar.”

Lysera glanced up at him sharply while the other two exchanged quick looks. Eventually, she spoke.

“My great-grandfather is in seclusion. He has been for the last seven years.”

“I heard,” Aodhán replied, briefly glancing at Lilian before continuing. “But I also heard your inherited path resembles his.”

Lysera lowered her gaze back to the notebook. “I still don’t understand what exactly you want to know.”

Aodhán took a step forward. “I want to be able to generate pure energy. I need you to teach me how.”

Lysera's expression shifted, and a faint smirk tugged at her lips. “Do you even know what you’re asking for?”

Aodhán frowned. “Yes. I think. I’ve researched it extensively.”

Lysera looked up then, her silver irises bright beneath the moonlight. She seemed to mull over his words for a moment before slowly nodding.

“Pure energy is not a secret among purification and alchemical circles, though there are certain requirements attached to generating it.” She pressed her lips together.

“I can teach you,” she said. “But you’ll have to do something for me in return.”

Aodhán nodded easily, having expected as much. Knowledge like this never came freely.

“What do you want?”

Lysera regarded him for a long moment, her breathing deepening as though she were gathering the courage for what she was about to say.

Then she spoke. “I want you to make me your second underling.”
