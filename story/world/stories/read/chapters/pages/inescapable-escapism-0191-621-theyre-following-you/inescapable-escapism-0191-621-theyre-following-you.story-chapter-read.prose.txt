My gaze flitted back and forth between the two screens as I waited for something to happen. It felt like I had been sitting there, staring at them, for hours, and a slight ache had begun to build in my lower back from how long I’d been hunched over, but I couldn’t do anything to help it. I was too scared to move, too scared to do anything that might distract me from my objective. It was too risky.

“Anything yet?”

The question made me jump. I wasn’t expecting to hear my agent’s voice coming through the speakers, and I had to fight to stop my eyes from darting towards the girl seated towards the bottom of one of the screens.

I assumed that was where she was, anyway. She’d disappeared into the cafe some time ago, emerging a few minutes later with a cappuccino and a newspaper before taking a seat at one of the small tables under the awning that jutted out from the building.

She probably hadn’t moved from there; it was too good a location, but I didn’t want to check. I couldn’t. I was too busy watching the CCTV stream of people wandering down the street in front of her, and if I allowed myself to get distracted for even a second, I could miss the target. They could strike before I even noticed them, and my agent, the person I’m meant to be protecting, would be hurt.

They could be killed, and it would all be my fault.

“No, not yet,” I said into the microphone on my headset. “Sorry.”

A grimace danced across my face as I heard the tutor standing behind me shuffle ever so slightly. The temptation to look back at her to see her reaction pulled at me. I longed to see whether she appeared annoyed or disapproving of my slip-up, but I forced myself to continue staring at the screens.

I couldn’t let it happen again, though. I’d been warned to limit how often I was using the comms, to only speak when it was necessary. Otherwise, it could be picked up by the target or anyone they were working with, and they could trace my agent’s location. My apology wasn’t necessary, though. I didn’t need to apologise to her, but I had. It was just a simulation, so it was probably fine, but I was worried that mistake was enough for them to fail me.

Was it even possible for me to fail the training exercise, I wondered. I wasn’t sure. I guessed that if I missed the target completely, and they got to my agent before I could warn them, they’d have to fail me, but I wouldn’t let that happen. I’d already decided that.

Taking a deep breath, I tried to force my attention back to the screens in front of me. I was still far too aware of the tutor standing behind me, but I couldn’t let that distract me. I needed to focus.

My gaze roamed the busy street, darting between the people streaming along it. The security camera footage was a little blurry, but it was nowhere near as bad as I’d expected. I thought it would be grainy, like in every spy movie, but it was clear enough for me to see most of the people’s faces, and I was fairly confident that the man we were expecting had yet to arrive. I assumed it was only a matter of time, though.

They appeared happy, though. The people walking down the street moved at a leisurely pace, a smile was on their lips, and so many were holding brightly coloured flowers. It seemed like they’d just come from a festival of some kind, or maybe they were moving towards it.

The cobbled streets were packed, so that would have made sense, but I didn’t know for sure. Normally, I would have received a briefing. The celebration would have been mentioned in the file I should have been given, but the training exercise was meant to simulate an emergency, so I had received neither. I didn’t even know which city or country I was looking at.

It appeared European, though, I thought as I tried to think of any sign that could give it away. When I looked before, at the beginning of the simulation, I could just about make out some words on the glass behind my target. They weren’t written in English. Of that much, I was sure, but I didn’t know what language they were.

Perhaps Italian. I didn’t know much of that language, and I’d never been there, but I was pretty sure I’d seen pictures or something of Rome or maybe Venice at some point. I couldn’t quite remember which one, but it must have been featured in a movie.

The street did look vaguely Italian, I decided. The only building I could see was a long terrace made of a light-coloured stone, with a white awning attached to the front, shielding the clusters of tables below. That in itself gave nothing away, but the windows above the cafe had balconies. Each one was small, barely big enough to hold a table, but that confirmed my agent definitely was not in the UK anymore. The buildings there rarely had balconies, and even if they did, no one there would want to sit out on a balcony. They must have been somewhere much warmer.

My hand twitched as the urge to reach out, to cycle through the security cameras until I found something to confirm where I was looking at, gripped me, but I forced myself to remain still and for my gaze to return to the people once more.

Tension grew even stronger within me as I glanced from face to face, trying to pick out the person I was shown a picture of before, but it was hard. There was very little about my target that made them stand out. Too many people on the street in front of me had dark hair, and the camera feed wasn’t good enough for me to spot the man’s distinctive green eyes, even though they’d been burned into my brain.

They were too bright. The colour was a little too vivid, and I assumed he must have been wearing coloured contact lenses or something. No one’s eyes were that shade naturally. Other than that, his face was utterly forgettable and unremarkable. His nose was a little crooked, I remembered, but it wasn’t extreme enough to be noticeable from most angles.

He did have a tattoo, though. That I remembered as I scanned the crowd, trying to catch sight of their forearms, but the people were too tightly packed. Frustration and anxiety peaked within me as I realised it was hard to get more than just a glimpse of a bare arm.

The clothing. That had to be what would give them away, I decided. Cara had shown us a picture of the target, claiming it had been taken at a nearby airport earlier that day, so I hoped that meant the man hadn’t had a chance to move. Therefore, I just needed to look out for anyone wearing a blue and white striped button-down and light coloured jeans.

Surely, that would help. Most of the people I could see were wearing dresses or shorts, which further cemented that the place I was looking at was somewhere warm. I could only see a couple of people in jeans, so if the target was still wearing them, he’d stand out.

Restlessness seemed to thrum beneath my skin. I had no sense of time. It was impossible to know whether I’d been staring at the screens for an hour or six, but we must be nearing the end of the class. I knew the simulation was meant to be long. It was designed to test my sustained attention, something I needed to improve, but I doubted Cara would keep us there for much longer. The class was our last of the day. It was already late, and if the target didn’t show up soon, we’d miss dinner.

As if sensing my thought, something on the screen changed. My heart leapt into my throat, immediately starting to race, as a ripple appeared to go through the crowd. Some ducked, most people froze, and a few spun around to stare at something off-screen, but I couldn’t see what it was.

There was no sound. The agent hadn’t reactivated her microphone, so I couldn’t hear anything through the speakers, but it was clear something bad had happened from the sheer terror on every person’s face, and I needed to know what.

I leaned even closer to the screens as a jolt shook the camera, and the people on the street below began to run.

“Update!” a voice blared from the speakers, the tone sharper than before.

Smoke began to appear at the edge of the screen as people fled, but my agent had not. She was on her feet, the newspaper lying on her table forgotten. Her eyes appeared fixed on the far side of the screen, seemingly staring in the direction of whatever had happened, but I knew she must have been scanning the crowd just as I was.

I opened my mouth, intending to tell the agent that I’d not spotted anyone and had no idea what was happening, but something caught my eye. The words died on my lips as I stared at the figure pushing through the people on the street, moving towards the source of the chaos.

They may have just been trying to help out, a voice in the back of my head reasoned as I stared. Perhaps they were worried, scared for those caught in the disarray, and wanted to assist them however they could.

But surely they’d be moving quicker if that was the case. There were too many people on the street. It seemed hard to push through the bodies streaming along the street as they tried to get away from the smoke that was now billowing, but they didn’t appear to be trying that hard, and they didn’t look scared enough. Their pace was too slow, too steady, and there was no fear on their face.

Someone bumped into the man, careless in their haste to escape, and the sleeve of his striped shirt was caught on theirs. Their forearm was revealed, and my eyes found the swirling mass of black ink. The man muttered something at them, their expression furious, before looking forward again. I saw their head turn to the side ever so slightly, and they glanced directly at my agent.

“He’s there!” I cried before catching myself. That wasn’t what I’d been trained to say. “Target spotted. Behind you. Location… five o’clock.”

A muttered swear came over the speaker, but the agent didn’t look back towards the man.

“How far?” they asked.

Panic gripped me as my gaze flicked back and forth between the man and the agent.

“Uhh… twenty feet. Maybe a little less!”

It was hard to tell. My time at the Academy had taught me I was terrible at estimating distances, and the angle of the camera made it even harder to tell. I was worried that I was way off, that I’d given the agent a false sense of security, made them think they had more time than they did, but another swear blared from the speaker.

“Evacuating now,” they said as I saw them shake their head slightly, as if shaking off their shock before raising their voice. “Oh god! They need help!”

The agent dashed forward before I had time to react, sprinting towards the smoke, and I quickly reached for the mouse. I clicked one screen, keeping the view of the target on it whilst switching through the cameras on the other.

“They’re following you!” I told the girl, my voice far too loud as fear pulsed in my heart.

I cycled through the security cameras, trying to find an exit route for the agent, but the computer was slow to respond. My breath hitched as the camera glitched, the image flickering before freezing. Anxiety tightened around my throat, and I clicked again, hoping that would fix it, but both screens just turned black.

Fear exploded within me as I clicked again, unsure what else to do, but nothing happened. The feed did not reappear. My hands were trembling. I was shaking and terrified that I’d left my agent alone to deal with the man chasing her, and I looked back at Cara helplessly. There had to be something she could do, some way she could get them back.

Confusion stilled my thoughts as my eyes found the tutor’s face. She didn’t look scared at all. Instead, she was smiling, and that felt wrong. It was wrong.

“Congratulations,” she told me. “So, what gave them away?”

I glanced back at the screens before me, my eyebrows pulling together as applause sounded around the room. It was just a training exercise, I realised. None of it was. I’d known that before. That was what Cara had told me, but I’d gotten too sucked in. I’d forgotten that my agent was not truly in danger, that I was not actually the person calling the shots. I was just sitting in a classroom and watching a recording of something.

“Umm… the way the man was acting,” I said, turning towards the tutor again.

That was the best I could do, and I knew it wasn’t enough. It was too vague, but adrenaline was still pulsing through me, and my head had yet to stop spinning. My heart was pounding far too fast, and I couldn’t gather my thoughts well enough to explain myself properly.

“Okay, good. Tell me more,” Cara prompted.

I paused, forcing myself not to respond straight away and take a deep breath instead. I needed a moment, needed to pull myself together so I could actually answer her.

“They didn’t seem shocked by whatever happened,” I started.

“By the explosions?”

“Yeah,” I said. I had no idea there had been any explosions, but it made sense. “They weren’t scared either. They just kind of… didn’t react.”

“Good. What else?”

“Mmm, I think I saw them look at the agent,” I said.

“You did,” Cara confirmed.

“Yeah, and they matched the description, and the picture too. I think they’d changed their jeans, but the shirt was the same.”

The tutor nodded.

“That’s right. Well done,” Cara said, sending a burst of pride through my chest. “This was a fairly straightforward example. The target was an amateur, and he was working alone, but you spotted them quickly, and the agent had enough time to get away safely.”

“Great!”

I couldn’t help but grin as triumph surged through my body. I’d done it. Because of me, the agent hadn’t been caught, and I knew it was just a simulation, but I’d never felt so proud of myself. It was addictive, though. I wanted to chase the feeling. It felt like I’d gotten an unexpectedly good grade or something, but much, much better. It was so much more intense, and I needed to experience it again.

Hopefully, next time, the simulation will be longer, I thought. I wanted to follow the agent through the entire city, to help them get to a safe house or an extraction location. That would be good. Surely, if I managed to do it successfully again, I’d feel even more proud of myself than I already did.

“Okay, we’re running out of time today, but on Wednesday, we’ll go through the footage properly and pick out anything Grace might have missed,” Cara announced to the class. “But for now, all you need to know is that the agent successfully escaped, and the target was later apprehended and charged with four counts of terrorism. They’re now serving a life sentence at an undisclosed location.”

Shock crashed into me, and my mouth dropped open. I gaped at Cara for a few seconds before finally recovering enough to speak.

“Wait, it was real?”

A small smile pulled at Cara’s lips.

“Yes, most of it was,” she said. “Some bits were sped up or edited, but most of what you saw was taken from a real mission.”

“What do you mean most?” one of the other trainees asked.

“Well, we added in a little more smoke to make the situation more tense and to see how you reacted under pressure, and we manipulated a couple of moments,” she explained. “And if you’d have failed to spot the target, the ending of the clip would have been entirely fictional.”

She grinned at me, but I couldn’t return it. I was too surprised.

“What?”

“We recreated the set in one of our studios, just in case the analyst misses the target or doesn’t spot them in time,” she said. “We wanted to make it so the exercise didn’t just end there, but instead so we could show what would happen in that case.”

A shiver of fear slipped down my spine.

“What would have happened?” I couldn’t help but ask.

“The agent would have died.” Cara shrugged. “We’ll watch that clip during the next class when we analyse your performance, but for now, you should all get to dinner. Go on!”

Immediately, the sound of chairs scraping against the floor filled the room as the rest of the trainees stood, and I followed them half a second later. Thoughts were still racing around my head, and I felt wired. A combination of the excitement of what I’d just done, the triumph, and fear was making me light-headed.

“Good job, Grace,” one of the other trainees told me.

“Thanks,” I replied with a shy smile, waiting for them to leave the classroom ahead of me.

It had been scary. It was intimidating to sit in front of the computer in front of the others, and my knees had been shaking as I walked towards it. I knew they’d all be watching me, that they might spot the target before I could, and I was worried that would distract me. The thought of so many eyes on me, watching my every move, could have made it impossible to focus on the thing I was actually meant to be doing.

But it didn’t. I still managed to spot the target, and I’d actually enjoyed it. Part of me had been dreading the additional observation class ever since I’d returned to the Academy and realised it had been scheduled. I didn’t know what to expect from it, but it was cool to actually put some of the things we’d been learning into practice, and I’d even done well. Cara had said that I had, and I wasn’t used to teachers praising me, but I wanted it to happen again.

Another emotion simmered beneath my rush of pride and happiness, though. Jealousy. It felt wrong, but part of me was actually jealous of the agent I’d been working with. I wished that I were the one sitting outside a cafe in some foreign country rather than cooped up in some windowless room, forced to watch the action take place through security cameras. It had still been fun, but I wanted more. I longed to actually experience it myself.

It wasn’t too late, though, I realised. I still had a chance to do that. I hadn’t chosen my speciality just yet. I was being trained to become more than just an analyst, so I could still become a field agent if I wanted.

My steps slowed as I toyed with that thought. Perhaps I should speak to Rodgers, I mused. Or maybe Ms Brice would be better, if she were still at the Academy. I hadn’t seen her in a while, though, and Rodgers had said something about how busy she was, so perhaps it would be best to just speak to him. I could ask to switch my classes around a bit, to do more preparation to go into field work, but still keep my options open if I changed my mind.

Was that even possible? I wasn’t sure. I was already being trained in both specialisms, so surely it wouldn’t be too much of a hassle to shift the balance a little and put more emphasis on the classes that are more relevant to field work.

It didn’t sound like it would be too hard, but hesitation pulled at me. I knew nothing about how the scheduling at the Academy worked, and I didn’t want to bother anyone. The thought of becoming a burden to anyone there dulled the happiness that was still burning within me, and I recoiled away from the thought.

Perhaps I’d stick with my current timetable for a little while longer, I decided. I’d give it a few more weeks, and then, if I still wanted to change it, I’d speak to Rodgers then. More people would probably be out of the induction wing by then, so the classes might have changed around by then, and it would be easier for them to fit me into the right ones.

Or maybe it would be even harder.

Taking a deep breath, I pushed that thought away. It would be fine, I told myself as I reached the canteen. Everything would be fine, and there was no need to worry about it. I’d figure it out.
