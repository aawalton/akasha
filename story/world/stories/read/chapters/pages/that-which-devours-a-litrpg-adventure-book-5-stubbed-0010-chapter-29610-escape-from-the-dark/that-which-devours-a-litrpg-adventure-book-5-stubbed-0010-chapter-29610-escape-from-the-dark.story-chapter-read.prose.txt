The cliffside loomed in front of us, and we all waited as Grizzle shifted from his massive form to his smaller, two-legged form. Inside my head, I called it the humanoid form, though with his massive tail, it wasn’t all that humanoid.

“We need to hurry,” said Shy, her voice shaking.

Shy didn’t have a smaller form, but she was so small already that she could fit up the narrow pathway. Grizzle went off first, just as we planned, with her first behind him.

Again, I waited, taking a deep breath.

No frost yet, but there was a ton less light in the sky.

Neither one of them moved as fast as me, and we had a long way to go. The forest hadn’t been a problem, everything had stayed away. Up top, that’d be less true, especially since there were more rank three beasts up there.

I crouched down, then leaped up, landing about half-way up. Then I leapt once more, to reach the top. I didn’t use my wings for the moment; my hunger increased too quickly when I did, and I didn’t want to leave Grizzle and Shy too far behind.

Already, we had a wonky plan that hopefully would work to bring her directly to Lakeside Landing.

The top of the cliff side still had light where I stood, but there were pools of darkness caused by a mess of shadows from the tall stacks of rocks. A splattering of red dots covered my radar, but they weren’t clustered up. They were more spread out across the way. If nothing moved too much, plotting a path wouldn’t be too bad.

Grizzle and Shy climbed the path, and I jumped once more. This time, I landed on the top of a stack of rocks.

This let me see farther into the mess of rocks we needed to pass through on our way to the portal.

The T-Rex from before wasn’t anywhere nearby, and I wondered what’d happened. They hadn’t attacked, but that didn’t mean Grizzle and Shy wouldn’t be tasty targets for them.

I just needed to get us all to the portal and home. From there, operation track-down-Strange would start.

Hopefully.

As long as what I saw in my rank up hadn’t actually happened.

I didn’t think it had, but something about it lingered.

Finally, both of the Spinos made it to the top of the cliff path. The cold increased just the tiniest bit, as the sky darkened even more.

They passed under me as I leaped to the next tower of rock, going beyond them. They would hurry in my wake, I knew. I kept our path away from any of the red dots on my map, moving as quickly as I could to outrun the cold.

Maybe once they were through the portal I’d test myself, but not before then.

The deeper we traveled into the shadows, the colder it became. Shy slowed down first, the distance between her and Grizzle slowly increasing. He noticed and reduced his pace, but glanced up in my direction with a head shake.

We were at least halfway, if not more, but we weren’t there yet.

Only a few of the red dots were moving. Others had vanished, which was worse, since I couldn’t keep track of where they were without my radar.

The cold didn’t touch me at all. Grizzle stuck close to Shy, and the next slow down didn’t come for a while. But then she stumbled. A rock went rolling across the area, making a ton of noise.

Both of them froze, while I searched my radar and our surroundings.

Nothing stirred.

But by this point, the sky had shifted. The darkness had deepened, and now tiny bright lights gathered in the distance, like pinprints of blue.

Those worried me more than any creature in the dark.

Still, I leaped on, and waited for them to follow. There wasn’t anything else to do.

A red dot lingered near the portal, but I hoped that by the time we got there, they might be asleep.

Another hovered nearby, thankfully far enough away that just using the portal shouldn’t trigger it, unless it was actively hunting.

I sighed but kept going, needing to take to the ground as the rocky piles ended. Finally, we reached the area near the red dot further from the portal, and I recognized something about it.

The T-Rex.

It didn’t move from its spot.

Grizzle didn’t even react to it at all, which worried me.

Shy started shivering, which made everything harder. The portal wasn’t much further, just up around the bend.

We all paused. I shoved a hunk of meat in my mouth, as did Grizzle. He forced Shy to eat one as well, which helped a little. Her muscles had all tightened up, though, and I knew she wouldn’t last much longer.

It was time. It had to be.

I pulled out my spear, and readied myself.

Grizzle grew. Not into his massive form, but his humanoid form grew taller, and wider. Then, he picked Shy up. I couldn’t think of the strength he needed for that, and I was glad he had it.

Then, he took off running as fast as he could around the bend, heading straight for the portal.

I pulled on the shadows surrounding me and vanished, then followed.

The red dot near the portal opening waited in the nearby shadows.

It had to be a Rock Breaker.

It waited until the Grizzle and Shy were more than halfway across the clearing before it struck.

Long, thin tentacles shot out at Grizzle. He didn’t react, just kept running, picking up speed just a little.

Shy whimpered.

I leaped.

My spear sliced through the limbs before they touched the couple. The Rock Breaker screamed, and the sound echoed around the entire area.

More tentacles shot out, but within the darkness I moved. Cut and slice, cut and slice, I systematically removed limbs as they approached.

The Rock Breaker finally launched itself out of its hiding place between the two boulders, heading to intercept Grizzle. It probably couldn’t see me, even though I’d been hurting it.

[Dim, Rock Breaker, Level 225, Tighten, Predator, Sour Candy.]

They were almost there.

That’s when it appeared.

The T-Rex.

Out of the shadows where it had inched closer as we distracted the creature, it lunged at the Rock Breaker.

Grizzle dodged around the T-Rex, and dove to the portal. A notification dinged, but I ignored it.

I circled around the backside of the Rock Breaker as the T-Rex missed.

It leaped, hitting the T-Rex in the head, and wrapped its long limbs around its jaw, closing the huge, toothy mouth.

The T-Rex couldn’t reach the Rock Breaker to scratch it off.

I slid across the rocky surface and sliced upward, aiming to free the T-Rex. I cut through four loops of the Rock Breaker’s tentacle limbs, but not all of them.

The T-Rex didn’t move, but breathed hard out of its snout, trying to open its mouth.

I sliced up again when Dim didn’t get off.

The T-Rex then jerked back and flung his head up. The Rock Breaker went flying off and slammed into a boulder.

It struggled to its feet, but a greenish tint covered it.

I dashed in its direction, and pierced the side of it with the massive black-tipped head of my spear, before the rest of my weight hit.

The T-Rex glanced at the portal once, hesitated, then barreled back into the fight with a huff.

He snagged a few limbs that were slapping all over, and yanked the Rock Breaker forward. My spear dug even deeper.

Finally, it slumped to the ground.

[You have survived battle against a level 225, Rock Breaker, and gained bonus experience.]

[You have leveled.]

[You have leveled.]

I yanked my spear away and let my stealth vanish.

The T-Rex stepped back from me.

“Thank you for the assist,” I said honestly. Though, I stepped closer to the Rock Breaker and used my knife to find its core, claiming my part of the kill. Then, I tossed it in my mouth. I almost flinched at the sour candy flavor, and crunched down instead of savoring it.

[You have devoured a level 225, Rock Breaker, and gained extra experience.]

Oh, that was good. I really didn’t want tentacles or the Tighten skill, whatever that was. It didn’t really fit what I wanted for my path.

The T-Rex eyed me, breathing heavy. “You leave?”

“Yes, I’m going to head to my territory,” I said with a smile. Hopefully, Grizzle and Shy had made it safe and sound to Lakeside Landing. “How is your quest going?”

The T-Rex huffed and pawed at the ground. “I can leave now…”

“That’s good!” A blazing white streak crossed the sky, making me freeze. “What the heck?”

“The frozen water,” said the T-Rex, again pawing the ground. They glanced between me, and the portal.

“Would you like to go with me?” I asked, picking up on the tension.

“Can I?” They leaned closer.

An idea came to me, as a second streak covered the sky.

“Sure, I think. I have an idea…”
