The room turned to Gio at his odd suggestion.

“Seen you die?” Chandrika repeated. “Like with divination?”

“I already agree that he is likely some sort of diviner,” Arseya grumbled. “And a good one, to boot. He seems to have a great deal of information, regardless if it’s all reliable or not.”

“But…” Gio said. “What if… he isn’t a diviner? He said that he has never grieved me. Miss- uh, Grand-”

“You need not use my title, dear.” The old sage smiled.  “Arseya would suffice, but I would be delighted if you wanted to call me grandmother as my babies do.”

Gio blinked at the overt familiarity, blushing slightly.

“Okay, then… grandmother…” Gio awkwardly said.  “We both watched me die a few times a moment ago… would you say that you grieve those who you see die in the Weave?”

She paused in contemplation. “No, definitely not. Foresight is often fickle and unreliable, and I’ve seen myself and my loved ones perish in vision more times than I could recount.”

“What are you suggesting?” Saint Baphelus asked Gio.

“I… I don’t know.” Gio answered. “He seems to have access to information… and it could be from foresight or mind reading, but something about that doesn’t make sense to me. He speaks with a sense of certainty that almost feels like it comes from experience.”

“Giorgio.” Headmaster Vespertine sighed. “You are trying to be indirect, but we all know what you’re suggesting. It is so impossibly fantastical as to be the stuff of fae fables and bedtime stories.”

“It’s the imagination of youth, methinks.” Saint Baphelus chuckled. “He’s smart enough to know that it’s impossible.”

“I know! I know. It’s just that I have already seen some things that I would have thought were impossible up until recently. I didn’t want to discredit the possibility.” Gio harumphed.

“I think I missed something,” Chandrika noted. “What are you talking about?”

“Giorgio here is suggesting that our villain is a time traveller.” Headmaster Vespertine slyly smiled.

Chandrika stared blankly at Gio.

“Look!” Gio said. “I know that it sounds crazy! I know that there has never been a publicly recorded case of time magic being used… but I’m just saying that we’re dealing with a potential unknown here, and that maybe we should-”

“Gio.” Saint Baphelus gently said, placing his arm on Gio’s shoulder. “I think that you’re under a lot of pressure, and have experienced more wild sights in a few short months than most students your age would see in years. Nobody is making fun of you, but you need to understand that Chronomancy has been proven to be impossible by countless mages over the aeons.”

“But… What about the time magic dungeon? The Glass Clocktower?” Gio asked. “Headmaster Vespertine, the school teaches the history of the ‘megadungeons’, I thought that there was proof that the city of Luma once existed?”

Saint Baphelus conjured a melon-sized chunk of aquamarine glass in a display case, the glass clearly eroded by the elements and worn down to be smooth to the touch.

“This is a system-verified fragment of the famed ‘Glass Clocktower’ of Luma.” Baphelus casually said. “You are correct that there was once a place where the spirits of time congregated to interact with one another. We theorize that it was akin to a beacon of sorts- a device not only for keeping time but also for finding time… but ultimately, the entirety of Luma was swallowed by the Bleached Desert, and time itself was not kind to the corpse of that civilization. Between the elements, raiders, the whims of the spirits, and whatever other forces of entropy you could imagine, nothing has been left of Luma save a few pieces of chipped glass. This lore was already ancient history before my childhood… and I don’t believe anyone needs a reminder of how old and dusty I am. This chunk of glass is completely inert- I keep it around as a nifty piece for my curio cabinet.”

Gio marveled at the artifact in front of him, useless or not.

“I have another little piece of horrific history that I would make you look at if I thought you were going to try and mess around with time magic,” the lich gravely added. “I won’t spoil anyone’s appetite by actually opening the door, but there exists within my domain a private lab that was once used by a scholar who claimed to have cracked the secret after years of research. He was going to shunt himself forward in time by using what he claimed was ‘an ingenious array of effects’ that would force time mana into his internal mana reservoirs. Several others and I forbade him from doing the experiment, but to save you the suspense, he did it anyway. The resulting effect is that every year on the anniversary of his attempt, like clockwork, another tiny shredded piece of his body appears in the chamber that he used to test his array. Even still, we autopsied his experiment and found that he did not succeed at aspecting even one singular mote of time magic. Without going into an academic fugue state to relay the reality of results, the abbreviated explanation is that he cast more space magic than anything else, and I have the whole thing preserved in a more modern stasis array as an exhibit to remind those who are more inclined to listen to pride over reason. By now, his chest cavity is almost completely reassembled if you decide that you want to see it.”

Gio cocked his head to the side, taking the gruesome mental image in stride while he contemplated.

His mind journeyed far, remembering the barest taste of chaos from when he and Rio lost control. He remembered the feeling of tangible possibility.

Magic can already do the impossible.

“How did this ‘scholar’ not realize that his experiment wouldn’t work? If it’s as clear-cut and simple as you say, why did he still try?” Gio pressed.

Headmaster Vespertine grasped Gio firmly by the hand. She intensely locked eyes with him, and Gio’s breath caught as the full weight of her focus bore down on him.

“Giorgio, I plead with you to listen to me at this moment with your full focus.” She urged.  “I see that look in your eye, and I have seen that same dangerous gleam lead to the ruin of several promising students and academic minds. To add to what my husband said, Time travel may not be possible, but it is very possible to fool yourself into thinking that it is. Magic, like electricity, often takes the path of least resistance: the man who tore himself apart by accident is just one example of magic ‘taking the path of least resistance’. I’ve seen a mage accidentally create a subdimension where reality would shift to give her the illusion of rewriting history instead of her intended result. I have seen a young man who broke himself with mental magic until his system told him that he was a “True Chronomancer”, when in reality he inspected as a [Self-Deluded Mindbreaker] who erased the memories of those around him to give the false impression that he had traveled back. In both cases, those mages had to be put down because of the harm that they were causing others. As one with access to Chaos itself, I need you to understand that if you go looking for time magic, you will instead likely find a way to break reality open at the seams… before you manage to rewind even a single second.”

Gio felt uncomfortable. The Headmaster’s gaze reminded him of the look on his friend’s faces after he regained control. It was like the headmaster was trying to prevent him from falling off some invisible cliff.

But… If I were to ask chaos for the power to rewind time… would it work? What would chaos ask in return for giving me that power?

Gio lightly slapped himself on the cheek.

“I understand,” Gio said, catching himself before he could venture down that path any further.

“I hope that you do.” Eudoria nodded.

She looked over to the projected memory, watching as it repeated once more. Saint Baphelus frowned as the man spoke about him.

“I wonder if this man is the same as those mages… a sorry delusional soul that imagines himself a time traveller?” the Headmaster wondered aloud.

“In my experience, a mad mage is worse than any beast or monster. Especially as if he is as old as I suspect he is.” Saint Baphelus agreed.

“It seems that we are at an impasse.” Queen Aayana noted. “With no concrete knowledge of our foe, it becomes difficult for us to move against them. Perhaps… without knowing our enemy’s true intentions, we should play into their hands?”

Eudoria raised an eyebrow, turning to the Queen with a smile. “Now there’s a trick. Foil the fortune-teller by doing exactly what they want you to do.”

“Precisely,” Aayana answered. “We shall pretend that this meeting went exactly as they thought it would. We posture to the world as a whole that the Ringed City and Ujjbala Sūrya have a strained, tenuous relationship.  We will outwardly suspect each other of foul play while we privately communicate, ever watchful of parties that attempt to capitalize on this ‘break in trust’. They’ll have to slip up eventually.”

“Whip-smart, my daughter is.” Arseya proudly exclaimed, before turning her face back to Chandrika with a gentle frown. “So… that unfortunately means that my little spider should come back to Bangla, as we would not leave her here in such uncertain times.”

Chandrika frowned.

“If we are meant to do what we would have done if this meeting turned sour… then I should escape from you, and claim protected status as a student of the Crystal Ring Academy.” Chandrika firmly replied.

Chandrika’s family turned to her.

“Rika…” Her mother calmly said. “Are you certain? If word gets out, and word always gets out… There will be a scandal. What will Jai Matra think?”

“Jai knows me, and he will understand… though I would be very grateful if certain salacious rumours about my time at the CRA could be curbed,” Chandrika answered, looking pointedly at her aunt.

“You out me as a spy in front of foreign political elements… You tell me that I am not your aunt… You call me - what was it you said, a ‘tool’? - and then you have the nerve to ask me for a favor?” Madame Mata hummed.

“Aunt Mata… I’m sorry for what I said.” Chandrika frowned. “You know that you’re my favorite Aunt. Please? I don’t want Jai to have to read those nasty stories, no matter how obviously fake they are.”

Chandrika’s aunt tapped her chin for a moment. “Well, I’ll see what I can do.”

Chandrika smiled, sprinting up to the severe woman and wrapping her in a hug.

“On one condition.” Mata interrupted.

Chandrika reeled back in surprise.

“I will be the one to apply the alta on your hands and feet on the day of your wedding,” Mada demanded.

“Aunt Mata…” Chandrika gasped, sounding more choked up than Gio had ever heard her. She replied in a soft voice. “I would be honored if you would help me prepare for my wedding.”

Ah, so that wasn’t a real demand.

“Your Aunt Aishwarya is going to be so jealous. I can’t wait to rub it in!” Mata preened.

Chandrika and her aunt embraced even more tightly before Aayana came to squeeze her daughter’s hand.

“We will… do our best to assure the young sultan.” The Queen added. “Just… be safe.”

“Thank you.” Chandrika earnestly replied. “I will.

“This will, perhaps, be for the best.” Arseya hummed. “Leaving her in the obscuring mist of chaos might in fact be the best option when our opponent is such an unknown.”

Aayana nodded. “I was thinking the same. Even if not a sage, this man and whoever he’s working with clearly have some way to gain information beyond the realm of common information gathering. Between the formidable staff of the Crystal Ring Academy and Giorgio’s unique constitution… I strangely believe that this dungeon of terrors may be the safest place for her… provided no more dragons are running about.”

“Well…” Headmaster Vespertine coughed. “No, a subject for another time.”

The Queen raised her eyebrows at that.

Chandrika, now looking much more relaxed than she had previously, made the rounds and embraced many members of the Queen’s ‘war-party’, including many who had not spoken up during the odd meeting. Strangely, Gio noted that she bowed to the Grand Weaver’s mechanical constructs, greeting them with a measure of reverence.

“There is… one last thing to deal with before we depart.” Arseya interrupted.

Eudoria turned to her with a start. “Oh?”

“I am not in the habit of leaving debts unpaid.” The sly old weaver smiled. “And I do not abide by the laws of the spirits of this land, nor the social mores of its nobility. I have a …gift of sorts… that I prepared in the event that Giorgio was, in fact, not a weapon made specifically to thwart me.”

Gio baulked. “A Gift? For what?”

Gio didn’t mean to sound so rude, but his surprise made him temporarily forget his manners. Thankfully, the weaver did not seem to mind his abrupt response.

“For saving my granddaughter’s life on several occasions, of course,” Arseya replied.

“I appreciate the thought, but I didn’t save Chandrika to be rewarded.” Gio disagreed. “I would do it again, and I don’t know if I can accept a gift for it.”

Gio was acutely aware that gifts from powerful individuals rarely came without strings attached, in the case of the Grand Weaver of Bangla, perhaps more literally than most.

“While that may be true, I don’t really care.” Arseya quipped. “I can do what I want - and I want to reward you. What you do with the gift is up to you… But know that if you were a member of my country, I’d have thrown money and honors on top of you to an extent that you’d be drowning. We witches know that a debt unpaid is a debt multiplied.  I pay my debts; life debts even more so. Consider this an investment into your growth, for I think that we might be seeing more of each other in the future as your friendship with our youngest sage grows.”

Gio didn’t know how to respond.

Arseya withdrew a neat rectangular package bound in golden thread and colorful silk. She gingerly placed it within Gio’s hands.

As soon as she brought it out, Baphelus sharply inhaled. He strode forward to take Gio’s side before he had a chance to open the package. He placed a hand over Gio’s. “Arseya, are you certain?” the lich asked.

Gio looked up at his mentor with a half-frown.

“Don’t give me that look. I’m not trying to prevent you from being rewarded… but that book is not something so easily given.” Baphelus told Gio. “This will come at a cost to her.”

“Just because you people are so uptight with your gifts does not mean I need to be,” the weaver said, waving him off. “It’s not like I give one of these out every day.”

“Thanks… but what is it?” Gio asked. “You said it was a book?”

“Why don’t you open it and find out?” Arseya eagerly prodded.

Gio pulled on the golden string, and it smoothly unwound, revealing a nondescript brown book.

The Grand Weaver of Bangla waved a hand in front of Gio, and a golden thread wrapped around his wrist before returning to do the same to hers. She spoke in a clear, powerful voice.

“I shall pay the price for this bequeathment. This power is mine to do with as I see fit, and I will give it to Giorgio in thanks for the service he has done to my family and my country.”

The book shifted in his hands, as images of keys and locks swam across the surface of the hide bindings, like tattoos given life. The binding stitches changed color from brilliant gold to silver, and a metal clasp grew from nothing, securing itself to the side of the book as the book itself spat out an ornate yet sturdy-looking metal key.

On the cover, the book read “[Lockwork and Locksmithery: Beginner]”

Gio gasped. “This is… a skill book!”

He nearly dropped it in surprise.

Arseya turned, reading the cover. “Good, it’s the one I thought it would be. Tough to tell which skill it will turn out to be once the spirits get a hold of it.”

“Arsey- no… grandmother,” Gio said. “Thank you… But can I really accept this? I don’t even know if you can buy skillbooks anywhere.”

“You cannot.” Baphelus harrumphed. “They aren’t easy to come by… unless you can make them, in which case they just take a portion of your essence, isn’t that right, Arseya?”

The weaver waved him off. “You’re clearly more worried about it than I am. Essence recovers. Now, to explain the choice in subject matter.”

“Locksmithing?” Gio replied. An odd gift, but not necessarily a useless one. He would have had to work for a very long time to manually acquire a locksmithing skill without aid.

“Yes, but you should look beyond just mundane locksmithing,” Arseya noted.

“Like… automata, like yours? Mechanisms and clockwork?”

“Perhaps… but then you would have consumed a skill rooted in opening and closing to create a skill about movement. What could a locksmithing skill become if it maintained its focus on opening?” Arseya asked.

“I don’t understand,” Gio said. “I understand that skills can evolve, but how could a locksmithing skill become anything other than a skill for unlocking things or making locks?”

“What a good question. Let me give one back to you: how does a weaving skill become one that lets a helpless girl seal away would-be gods and foretell the future? What hidden powers do we possess, and how might we unlock our own potential? I gave you that book because I noticed a pattern. You’re a tenacious young man, and even before I saw your future, I could tell that possibilities unfold around you like blossoming petals from a massive flower… A skill for locks and things might help you lock away the paths that do not serve you, as well as open doors previously unavailable to you.”

Gio nodded, digesting her words.

The Grand Weaver of Bangla produced another item. This time, it was a woven rug.

“But… I know youth, and I cannot help but wonder if a lockpicking skill might not provide you with enough intrigue to keep your focus… so I shall also provide you with a challenge. Hang that on any wall, and it shall conjure a door. Beyond that door lies a piece of my handiwork that I designed just for you…. But only if you can figure out the lock.”
