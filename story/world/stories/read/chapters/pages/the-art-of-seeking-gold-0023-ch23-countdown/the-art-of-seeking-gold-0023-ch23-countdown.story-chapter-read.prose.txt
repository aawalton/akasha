Tuesday. Four days before entering the Eastern Wing.

Amy tried putting energy through the spirit stone again. Nothing. She turned it over in her hand as though the other side might be more cooperative.

"It doesn't work."

[You have been practicing for fifteen minutes.]

"A very long fifteen minutes." She slid down in her chair. The corner she'd chosen in the library was empty, so at least nobody was watching her fail. "I've been concentrating this whole time."

[You counted the ceiling tiles twice.]

"My eyes needed a break."

[Naturally.]

Amy sat up with a sigh. "I'd have an easier time if someone told me what I'm actually meant to do. For some reason there are no books on how to use this shit, and Lirienne just said ‘channel your energy through it’ like that’s some kind of helpful instruction."

[Noble kids learn to do this by themselves at the age of three, you know.]

Amy ignored the comment and instead took a deep breath, closing her eyes again, focusing on the sensation she’d experienced during the last class. She found the warm path easily enough. It had felt just like this in class, gold and clear. But whenever she tried to guide the energy toward her hand, she lost track of it.

A few more attempts got her nowhere. Amy leaned back and let her arm drop. "I don't understand what I'm doing wrong."

[Have some patience. You barely tried. What happened to the Amy Stake who spent the whole night trying to get her ability to do anything? Where did my high-effort loser go?]

"..."

I wish this damn book could feel pain.

[C’mon, try again. You just need more time.]

"I don’t have time… Alright, maybe I do have a little time. But still, I’m basically useless in a fight. I need to get stronger—fast."

[Rome wasn’t built in a day.]

"But I don’t have to build Rome. I just need to... I don’t know, build a small defensive hut. Enough to not be completely helpless…” She let out a long sigh. “If only there was an easier way…”

As she uttered these words, an idea suddenly struck her.

Wait. Maybe...

Without wasting a single second, she closed her eyes and reached out with her ability. She felt the pressure behind her eyes and concentrated on a question: what do I need to do to make this stone work?

She saw herself sitting there, breathing more slowly. Then she saw where to send the energy: out of her core, through her arm, into her palm. Only after it gathered there did it enter the stone.

Amy winced at the strain, then copied what she'd seen. Slower breaths. Energy into her hand first. Something warmed against her fingers, and she opened her eyes. The crystal was glowing gold.

"Ha! I did it!" she exclaimed, low enough not to disturb the distant students. "I did it! I can’t believe I just did that—that was so easy."

[That’s cheating…]

"No, it isn’t. If it was, then why is it possible?"

[That logic makes no sense.]

"Ughhh. It’s not cheating. It’s... creative problem-solving. Yup, that’s what it is." She held the stone up, grinning. "Look at it. I did that. And now I know what I'm supposed to be feeling, so next time I might not need the instructions."

[I’m surprised your ability works for this, too. Makes me wonder what other applications it has.]

"I’m amazing, I know~," Amy replied, letting the glow fade as she released her focus.

[Still, you won’t be able to use that in a fight. Your core will get tired twice as fast. However, I’ll admit it’s a very interesting approach. You’re very good at being lazy.]

"Thank you!" she gathered her things, feeling oddly accomplished. Somehow, she had a feeling she wouldn’t feel as good if she’d accomplished it the standard way.

Yup. Cheati—Errr… Creative problem-solving is the best!

Amy was still playing with the stone when she left the library. The shadows across the path had grown long. She concentrated, got a little light out of it, and smiled again.

"Think how much time I could save with this. I wouldn't have to keep guessing what I'm doing wrong."

[You could also learn to copy something without understanding it.]

"So? At least I'd have done it once. Then I'd know what to practice instead of sitting there getting nowhere." She looked down at the stone. "I can work out the rest afterward."

[...I hate the fact that you’re right… The worst thing is that I can’t nerf it since the line between your own power and the reader’s given ability isn’t well defined.]

Amy smiled at those words. She hadn’t thought about the fact that her ability could have been nerfed, but it sure was reassuring hearing it wouldn’t.

Back in her room, she continued using the new trick, training with the stone and trying to strengthen the link. It barely took a few hours for the results to appear.

[Ability Mastery has increased to level 5. Magic has also gone from C (F) to C (E). Congratulations—And before you ask, C corresponds to your magic power and E to your proficiency.]

It was night, and her head throbbed with pain, a little blood coming from her nose. Yet she felt everything but tired—at least, not mentally.

"Easy peasy~"

Wednesday. Three days before entering the Eastern Wing.

Hardly anyone was in the Hall of Elements. Libris had said most students trained in the afternoon, which was why Amy had dragged herself here so early. She wanted privacy. It was not because she didn't want anyone watching her try to exercise.

"Mornings are evil," she said again, reaching over her head to stretch.

[You seem quite certain of that. Are they doing this to you deliberately?]

"Look at me. How could they not be?"

She found a mat by the spirit archway and put Libris where it wouldn't get stepped on. Then she started the warm-up Drayke had taught them.

Ten minutes later, sweat was running down her forehead and she couldn't catch her breath.

"How is this... a warm-up?" she asked between jumping jacks. "I don’t think I can…" she trailed off as she suddenly collapsed onto the mat, sprawled out like a starfish. "I'm dying."

[You look fine to me.]

"You… don't even have eyes, smartass…" she grumbled, chest heaving.

[I don't need eyes to know you're being dramatic.]

Amy reluctantly pushed herself back up. She pulled out her focus stone, concentrating on channeling her energy through it.

Although she hadn’t found much information to help with the stone yesterday, she had discovered that training her magic after a long session of physical effort was a very efficient way to empower her core.

"See? Progress..." She grinned as the crystal glowed brighter than yesterday, responding more readily to her intentions.

[Indeed. At this rate, you'll master basic energy channeling by approximately the year 12057.]

"Annoying piece of paper..."

Once she could breathe properly again, Amy returned to the exercises. She spent the next hour switching between those and the stone. By the end, standing felt like more work than she could reasonably be asked to do, but she'd kept the crystal lit for almost five minutes. She hadn't managed that before.

She was getting ready to leave when Drayke came in carrying training equipment. His mechanical arm made a quiet whirring sound under the load.

"Miss Stake. Didn't expect to find you here this early."

"Neither did I, Professor." Amy tried to stand a little straighter.

His ears twitched. "Well, you got here. Keep doing that and it becomes a habit." He noticed the light still coming from her stone. "Good to see you putting the work in."

Was that praise? "Thank you, sir."

He looked at how she was standing. "You need considerably more of it on the physical side, though."

And there it was. Amy resisted the urge to sigh. "I'm working on it, Professor."

"Work harder," he said simply, but without malice. "In combat, a strong ability means nothing if your body fails before you can use it. This applies double for you, since your ability is directly correlated to your stamina." His words uncomfortably echoed what Libris had been telling her.

"Yes, sir."

As she left the Hall, she checked her status window, curious if her efforts were making any difference:

STR: F / END: E+ / DEX: E / AGI: F / MAG: C (E+)

"E+ Hehehe~" she said, feeling a small surge of accomplishment.

[You are enjoying this more than you should...]

"Honestly, I’m starting to get why people go to the gym. It’s a pain in the ass at first, but the accomplishment it gives…" She shook her head with a smile, letting the words die.

[...Who are you, and what have you done with Amy?]

Thursday. Two days before entering the Eastern Wing.

Amy took her seat and tried not to yawn. Vanheim's lecture had been added to their schedule with very little warning, and apparently nobody was allowed to miss it.

"It had better be worth this," she muttered. Other students were still coming in. Some seemed interested; others looked about as pleased as she felt.

[Presumably there is a reason they called it a special lecture.]

The talking stopped when Vanheim entered. He took a moment to look around the hall.

"Good afternoon. Ordinarily, you wouldn't study today's subject until your third year. After recent events, we have decided that is too long to wait."

He wrote nightmares on the board.

Everyone started talking again.

[Good timing.]

Amy sighed, suddenly feeling nervous as she was reminded of what was coming. This class obviously wasn’t a coincidence; it was foreshadowing, and barely disguised at that.

"It’s really coming, isn’t it…" Amy muttered.

[It is. Sorry, Amy. If it were in my power, you’d be rotting in bed for as long as you wanted, but–]

"It’s alright, Libris. I think I have already accepted my situation."

Vanheim let them talk for a moment, then waited until he had their attention again. "After Building B, the faculty agreed that you all need to know the basics. We do not usually share this information so widely. Although this is merely an introduction compared to what you’ll study in the future, I heartily recommend that you pay close attention; some of what we’ll cover today might very well save your life."

Nobody spoke after that. Building B was still recent enough for them to take him seriously.

"A Nightmare reacts to what happens within it," Vanheim said. "A threat may make it adapt. Usually, it wants more room in our world, or it needs energy to accomplish something. You may not discover what it wants before you have to deal with it. That does not mean it wants nothing."

He added three labels to his drawing. "Tether, Core, Catalyst. The Tether keeps the Nightmare attached to our reality. Its internal structure depends on the Core, and it grows using what the Catalyst provides. You need to be able to tell them apart.

"Usually, disrupting the Core or Catalyst gives you a way to escape. I would advise looking for the Catalyst first. Severing it is generally less dangerous than interfering with the Core, though you should expect danger either way."

Amy copied the names. Which would they find in the Eastern Wing? And would she recognize it when they did?

"You will have to reconsider things you normally take for granted. How long something takes, how far away it is, even what causes what. A wall may not stop your hand. You may reach across what looks like a room. Do not rely on appearances."

He brought his pointer down on the board with a crack, startling several students.

"This is where training alone can fail you. If your usual response achieves nothing, change it. Don't keep trying because it worked outside.

"Look for things that repeat. There is a reason they repeat, and finding it can tell you something about that Nightmare's rules. You will need those rules to survive, and you may need them to get out."

It was getting difficult for Amy to believe this wasn't a lecture specifically for the group going into the Eastern Wing.

A second-year raised his hand. "Does someone have to create a Nightmare, Professor?"

"Usually, yes. Most of the ones we have recorded were made through rituals. But there are places where reality is less stable. Nightmares can form there without anyone creating them, particularly under certain celestial alignments."

Like the Vernal Alignment Zayd mentioned, Amy thought.

"A natural Nightmare often has less of that directed hostility. Its behavior can be harder to account for, though. We have seen its rules change without understanding why."

Vanheim continued for another hour. He used records of earlier Nightmares to explain how people had detected them and what had helped them defend themselves. Amy wrote down enough to fill several pages.

"Before you leave," he said, interrupting the students already packing, "remember the signs. Space that appears distorted. Emotions with no apparent cause. Symbols you keep encountering. Any of those can mean a Nightmare is forming near you. Notice them while you still have time to act."

Amy read over what she'd written as people left. The lecture hadn't made her want to go into the Eastern Wing, but knowing what to look for helped. It had to help.

She still hadn't put the notebook away when the hall was nearly empty. Looking down, she noticed her fingers trembling against the pages.

A thought she’d tried to ignore—and that had been hiding in the back of her mind—emerged for the first time, whispering in her ear. Maybe the Nightmare could affect her too, in addition to the protagonist’s group.

She had already faced worse, she reminded herself. Even if this was mentally worse—even if the Nightmare in the Eastern Wing contained things she had hoped would stay buried forever—

It wouldn’t matter.

Because she knew the rules now: knew what to look for, knew her limits, knew her strengths.

It should be easier. There would be fights, sure. But there should be time to react. It wouldn't be like the impostors, where one mistake could kill somebody before she could do anything.

"Nobody's dying this time," Amy whispered. "It's mostly in your head. Illusions, things that scare you... I can deal with that. I've dealt with worse."

She managed a small grin.

Then she thought about what she'd just said, and it went away.

Things that scared her. There was one question she hadn't asked Libris, mostly because she didn't want it answered.

She tried to leave it that way. Looked back at her notes, as though there might be something there she needed to read again.

But eventually she heard herself asking.

"Will I see him?" Her throat felt tight. "My father. In there."

Libris didn't answer at once.

[...Yes.]

Amy stared at the floor beneath her desk. She'd known it was possible. That was why she'd asked. Hearing Libris say it was another matter.

It offered no further explanation, and she couldn't think of anything she wanted to ask.

Eventually, she shut her notebook. She held it against her chest to hide the shaking in her hands and got up.

"I see."

She walked out of the hall. Those were the last words she said that day.
