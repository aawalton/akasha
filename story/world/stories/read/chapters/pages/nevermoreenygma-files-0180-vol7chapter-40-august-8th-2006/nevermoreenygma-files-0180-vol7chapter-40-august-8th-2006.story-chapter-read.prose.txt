Chapter 40

August 8th, 2006

Another year passed, and summer had returned to Japan. Fortunately, the air conditioner had been repaired a few months earlier.

It felt good.

Considering how much work they’d had over the previous months, maybe it was time for a break.

Miho wanted to head north, somewhere cooler, but the recent streak of jobs had postponed that plan. Maybe if they worked enough during the summer, they might even afford a long vacation and trade winter for the warm of the south.

Though that depended on whether as much work arrived as they expected—and work always spiked during the Obon festival season and stretched well into late October.

Mai was sitting on the couch surrounded by books, scattered notes, case folders, and small artifacts they had recovered a few days earlier from a job. Miho had asked her to study them and see whether she could figure out what kind of curse they carried based on the effects they’d had on their victims.

Her silver hair was a complete mess, and her moth-like antennae moved slowly.

Lately, when she was helping Miho more actively, her hair sometimes changed color whenever she pushed her abilities hard—shifting into that silver shade she had when she first appeared in that alley. It drew attention, but she liked the color. But the tanned skin or bioluminescent marks she saw the first time had never reappeared.

She stretched her legs and yawned. She was wearing very light clothing, which was a relief.Her legs were tired. They had trained for hours under a sun hot enough to turn concrete into an improvised oven, and then they’d had a meeting in the afternoon.

The beer can on the coffee table was half-empty, sitting next to the car keys—Mai’s car keys. Miho had definitely rubbed some habits off on her. Green tea in the mornings, a beer or two to end the day after dinner. Honestly it wasn’t that bad after a workday, as long as she didn't have to drive.

Since Miho was officially blind, she couldn’t drive, but she’d bought a car nevertheless—and Mai drove it. It didn’t bother her. Driving felt good.

A noise distracted her. Her eyes fixed on Miho, who had just stepped out of the studio, turning a pen between her fingers with a strangely serious expression. She still wore her shirt, but she had pulled it out of her pants changing them for shorts, giving her an effortlessly disheveled look.

“We’ve been called in for something important,” Miho finally said, with an oddly playful enthusiasm. “the eight there’s going to be a meeting between the vampire conglomerate and the Japanese government.”

“Uh?”

“At the hotel behind the Metropolitan Government Building...”

Mai raised an eyebrow. “That already sounds like a pain in the ass. Are you invited to the meeting?”

“Yeah. But also it’s about something else.” Miho set the pen down on the table and leaned forward. “It’s not just any meeting. It’s an attempt to start putting pieces together. To begin normalizing the existence of the hidden world in the eyes of humans.”

“That’s… good?”

“Yes… but it’s still secret. It’s not like they’re going to reveal anything publicly. It’s more of a commercial agreement meant to build ties with pharmaceutical companies.”

“The vampires have big corporations, right?”

“Yup.”

“It always starts with money.”

Miho tilted her head. “Yup.”

Mai took a sip of her cold beer. “Don’t tell me we’re part of the welcoming committee or something like that. It doesn't sound like the kind of thing you would do. Is it requested by the Section?

Miho shook her head. “Not exactly, is just an extra task. Our job is to observe. Make sure nothing gets out of control.” She crossed her arms. “Because some people are not happy about this initiative.”

Mai sighed and set the can back on the table. “Let me guess. There are groups that think revealing our existence is suicide.”

“Exactly. And not only them. There are circles that want everything to stay the way it is. Some because they fear what they don’t understand. Others pigs because they’ve been profiting off exploiting feys and humans with special abilities for decades.” Miho went to the kitchen, grabbed a beer can, and opened it. She held it without drinking. “You know as well as I do that technology is advancing too fast. It’s only a matter of time before people start noticing the anomalies. In recent years, increasingly strange things have been happening.”

Mai rested her elbow on the back of the couch and glanced at her sideways. “Sounds like this event is more of a symbol than anything.”

Miho nodded slowly and flopped onto the couch across from Mai. “Yeah. A message that coexistence is possible. And it’s no coincidence that one of the companies involved is ZAIEN.”

Mai rummaged through her mental encyclopedia of organizations linked to the feys. It had grown considerably over the past two years.

“That… was the multinational that helped stop the experimentation on feys in the United States?”

“Uh-huh.” Miho raised the can to her lips and took a small sip while lifting her shirt to scratch her belly button. “For years now, the major powers have stopped seeing feys only as threats or resources. But there are always loose ends. Groups that don’t want to lose their grip on what they consider weapons. Japan government is no saint in that regard. One reason for having so many feys in the country is to ensure that some are willing to be part of a secret national defense network.”

Mai narrowed her eyes. “So you think someone’s going to try to sabotage the meeting?”

“I think it’s a very real possibility.” Miho set the can down on the table with a soft clink. “And we’re going to be there to make sure it doesn’t happen. In case someone summons something fun.”

“For future reference, that doesn’t sound fun.”

“It probably won’t happen, but… just in case.”

A long silence settled between them. Outside, the cries of insects drifted in through the window.

Mai exhaled through her nose and returned to the books. “We won’t be the only ones, I’m guessing?”

Miho smiled, leaning back with a playful air. “Nah, there’ll be plenty of people. We’re just extra hands.”

Mai stretched. “Well, I hope nothing goes wrong.”

“I know.”

“Why are you so excited about it?”

“The pay is good!” Miho removed her glasses and raised her eyebrows mischievously as she took another sip.

“The money.”

“The money,” Miho agreed with a little foam mustache.

Mai stuck her tongue out at her.

“I can see that.”

August 8th

The sunset painted the hotel windows gold as Mai and Miho watched from a distance,feeling the unpleasant sensation of their clothes sticking to their bodies due to the humidity. They’d spent days monitoring the area along with a team of undercover officers, but the surveillance had been little more than a formality. No threats, no suspicious movements, nothing alarming.

The most exciting thing they had encountered was a lost foreign kid the day before, whom they helped reunite with his mother in the chaos of Shinjuku traffic.

But that evening, the atmosphere felt different.

There were more plainclothes officers scattered around, and even a few feys called in by the Special Security Office of the Inoue Section. Mai thought she recognized a hobbit trying to pass as a child in the nearby park. Let’s just say his skater disguise wasn’t exactly convincing.

There were about five members of the Wishper team that Miho had mentioned before, with others stationed on the upper floors. Some people treated Wishper them like global vigilantes, but their success in liberating feys made them indispensable here.

The first to arrive were the representatives of the Japanese government. Black cars, tinted windows, men in suits with earpieces and expressions trained to ignore the sweat running down their temples. They entered the hotel without incident, crossing the lobby with the efficiency of people who had rehearsed every step.

Ten minutes later, the air changed.

From the corner, Mai and Miho saw a group of dark vehicles approaching. Not gaudy limousines or flashy transports—just sleek, discreet cars built for subtlety. But subtlety was a joke when everyone knew who was inside.

The doors opened, and the vampires stepped out. A dozen in total, moving with the fluidity of beings who knew every eye was on them. They slipped forward quickly, like shadows too polished to belong to the real world.

“There they are,” Miho murmured, arms crossed, a half-smile on her lips. “The crème de la crème.”

Mai watched them closely. At the front were two figures who immediately stood out.

The first was a woman with ash-blonde hair and ice-blue eyes. Her face was youthful, but her gaze was serious. Mai recognized the posture of someone used to the world bending around her whims.

“Ella Rozenkreutz,” Miho supplied, as if reading her mind. “Don’t let that face fool you. They say she could rip your heart out and still keep her schedule for the day.”

“Seriously? Even though she looks so stiff when she moves?”

Miho examined her more carefully. “Hmm… a bit, yeah. I think her aura is a little different from what she's trying to project.”

Mai shifted her gaze to the other leader.

A man of medium height, brown hair, and a softly rounded face that gave him a boyish air. A nickname floated into her mind before Miho even spoke.

“Franz Rackhenhell,” Miho confirmed. “They call him ‘The Boy’ because he always looks fifteen. But trust me—innocent is the last thing he is. He’s been running the family business for decades. Rumors says he is the soft approach of his family.”

“R&R are the ones who developed the vaccine for the sun problem?”

“Yes, apparently the government is interested in the vitamin research that R&R has been doing for a long time. Today, all vampires who have secretly integrated into the civilized world can walk in daylight thanks to that.”

Mai leaned back against the wall, watching as the vampires ascended the hotel steps. “So how well do they get along?”

Miho snickered. “Oh, that’s the fun part. Rumor is they’re in love, but their families already picked different suitors for them. Ah, the classic drama of vampiric nobility.”

Mai narrowed her eyes. “Let me guess. Internal problems.”

Miho turned to her with a mischievous smile. “Like any family… A friend in foreign intelligence told me something’s cooking in the shadows. No one knows what exactly, but between forced alliances and internal betrayals, this whole thing is basically a bomb waiting for someone to light the fuse.”

Mai clicked her tongue. “Perfect. Just what we wanted for a calm little surveillance shift. I should have brought the gun.”

“You can only take it out with authorization from the department, remember. Just because you have a gun now doesn't mean you have to carry it all the time, right?”

“Yeah… I know. More since what happened with that case...”

Miho sighed, watching the vampires disappear inside the hotel. “Well, now the interesting part begins. Let’s see if someone in that meeting decides to stab someone else before they even serve the appetizers.”

“Don't talk about food. We’re simmering in our own sweat out here. I want icecream.”

“Quit complaining. Look at that kitsune in the corner wearing a beanie to hide his ears.”

Mai pitied the fox-boy, and pressed her antennae closer to her temples, and checked the time.

Not even thirty minutes had passed, and she already felt it was going to be a long night.

Mai was leaning against a police car, watching the hotel doors with the calm patience she’d cultivated over the last year. He had changed her way of thinking since then, and now it seemed like a distant part of her past, even though only a year had passed.

Everything had been calm since the vampires went inside.

The summer heat clung to the skin like a damp blanket when the quiet shattered all at once.

All was too calm.

But just as she was thinking how strange it felt, she sensed the shift in the air. The temperature dropped abruptly, like a northern wind had slipped into the street. Mai knew far too well what could cause that kind of change.

A spirit in closed space can, but on a outside that could be…

An invocation? She thought.

Miho, who had been right beside her a moment ago, had gone rigid—like someone had driven a knife between her neck vertebrae. She lifted her gaze to the sky with a look Mai had never seen on her before.

“It can’t be… it’s huge... It's a... Shambler.”

Mai looked up, but all she saw was the darkening sky. She squinted and she thought she could make out a distant point, barely visible. It was a dark shadow, almost undetectable, but one thing was certain. It was growing in size.

And suddenly, Miho snapped her eyes toward the building where the meeting was happening and bolted toward the entrance.

“Don’t follow me! Get everyone away from here! ” she ordered over her shoulder, her voice so sharp it cracked like a gunshot.

But Mai was already moving behind her. Some of the other fey nearby had turned as well, sensing the same thing she did.

Miho shot across the pavement like a furious whirlwind, shoving aside anyone in her way. The hotel security guards reacted instantly, blocking her path, but Miho didn’t hesitate for even a heartbeat.

“What are you doing?!” asked one of the guards as he held her by the arm.

“Can't you feel it?! We have to get everyone out of there, or at least let me go up so I can try to stop before it reach the ground!”

“What are you talking about?!”

“That thing in the sky is coming this way!”

“What thing?”

“Let me through, damn it!” she roared, trying to shove past them. Then, with effort, she reached for her jacket and threw some wheat seeds. “Kon!”

At that moment, two of the guards were knocked away by an invisible force. Miho had summoned one of her invisible fox spirits. Miho entered the hotel followed by the spirit.

The police officers stationed farther away froze, unsure of how to react.

Mai didn’t freeze.

She launched herself toward the other guards with the reflexes of a predator. In under a second, one was on the ground, disarmed and swearing. The other didn’t fare any better. “Sorry...”

Miho spared her only a quick sideways glance before storming into the hotel. “Notify the top floor immediately! I'm on my way there, evacuate everyone from the surrounding area!”

“Miho, wait!” Mai yelled, but she was already on the stairs, taking them two at a time in a blur.

Mai picked up one of the communicators and tried to speak, but only static came through, while she felt the cold intensify and heard a pitiful moan behind her.

Mai turned her head—and froze.

Near the elevator, a guard lay motionless on the floor. A thin stream of dark blood trickled from his nose and ears… and the blood was floating, weightless, lifting itself off the skin like it was rising toward something unseen. The ones near the entrance showed the same impossible phenomenon. All the guards who had arrested Miho were in the same condition, and the blood disappeared towards the entrance and then going outside, drawn toward the sky. While a gusty wind had risen in the surrounding area

It was like they were suffering some kind of internal attack—one powerful enough to alter gravity itself.

Something was happening inside the hotel. Something bad. The temperature felt even colder now and lights flickered.

Miho has disapeared upstairs.

Mai slammed the elevator button. No response. No flickering light. No mechanical hum.

“Fantastic,” she hissed.

There was only one option left.

She sprinted up the stairs, forcing her legs to move faster. She could hear Miho above her, her footsteps barely audible—too light, too swift. She wasn’t just running. She was using something. Maybe a wind spirit or just the shikigami fox.

Mai gulped a breath, ignoring the burn in her muscles.

If Miho was running like that—if her instincts had triggered such a reaction—then whatever was happening above them was serious.

And in the past year, Mai had seen all kinds of madness in the cases Miho solved. She herself had had to shoot someone to save Miho.

But this time… this time was different.

And she wasn’t about to be left behind only to figure it out too late.

Miho had said Shambler. She didn't think she had read that name in any of Miho's books. Unless it was in the ones she hadn't touched yet.

As she ran, she saw several guards on the stair landings in the same state—blood floating weightlessly from their faces, bodies slack. She wanted to stop, to help them, but whatever was happening was coming from above. Miho had sensed something, no doubt, and she was heading straight for it.

Screams and heavy impacts echoed down the emergency staircase by the time Mai reached the 30th floor. Her lungs were a whirlwind inside her chest, but her body moved purely on instinct.

Up ahead, she spotted Miho. She was locked in combat with two guards whose glassy eyes and levitating blood marked them as already lost. Miho’s posture was taut, her eyes blazing with a fury Mai had rarely seen.

“I told you not to follow me! There are infiltrators in the hotel! This is a curse!” Miho shouted, sweeping one of the guards off his feet with a sharp twist. “We need to evacuate everyone right now!”

Mai frowned and run towards Miho. Infiltrators? What the hell was she talking about? Wasn’t this because of the thing she sensed up above?

As far as they knew, the meeting was supposed to be completely sealed, protected by tight security protocol. But something in Mai’s gut told her this wasn’t Miho being paranoid. Miho had been unsettled the previous day when they helped that child in the park, muttering about a bad feeling she couldn’t explain. Had she seen something Mai hadn’t?

Miho had said curse.

Mai’s pupils widened.

One of the guards slammed into the wall with a dull crack, and Miho turned her head toward Mai, her face tight with urgency.

“Too late… Mai, get out of here!!”

But then it happened.

The disaster erupted like an invisible bomb.

The floor shuddered violently as if something colossal had struck somewhere inside the building and then exploded. An explosion ripped through the structure and Mai was thrown backward, smashing into a support pillar as the windows obliterated outward. Shards of glass rained onto the street below—where the crowds had already begun to flee in terror—not just from the glass but from chunks of concrete now falling like sudden, lethal hail.

Above them, a dry, revolting sound filled the air—meat tearing, bones snapping under impossible pressure, something inhuman forcing its way through the flesh of the world.

Dazed, her antennas vibrating with the aftershock, Mai couldn’t hear through her ears—only a shrill, relentless ringing. Echoes crashed into her from every direction, disorienting her sense of balance.

Pain stabbed deep into her right shoulder. A thin iron rod—no thicker than a spike—was lodged there.

She tasted blood. Smelled iron.

The guards already lying shattered on the ground began to convulse unnaturally. Their bodies arched with violent spasms and, before Mai’s stunned eyes, their pupils dilated until they swallowed every trace of color.

They weren’t breathing.

They weren’t alive.

Mai couldn’t hear anything except a deafening whine inside her skull. Pain lanced through her like barbed wire, each heartbeat pounding agony through her ribs. The iron spike that had been buried in her shoulder now lay on the floor, slick with her blood, but the wound it left behind throbbed like an open furnace. Everything was dark with smoke, the air thick enough to burn her throat with every breath.

She stumbled through the debris, her body a limp marionette responding only halfway to her commands. Around her, flames licked at what remained of the floor, the once-structured environment reduced to a twisted mess of concrete and steel. The explosion had surely caused some parts of the wiring to catch fire. The ground beneath her feet groaned and tilted, threatening to give way at any moment. Mai braced herself against a shattered wall, struggling to stay upright.

She didn’t understand what had happened. An attack—someone had attacked. But who? And why?

The Shambler. A curse.

She inhaled shakily and looked toward a massive breach in the wall, revealing the city beyond. Through the smoke, Tokyo still moved—bright buildings, traffic flowing, life continuing as if nothing had happened. Only the area around the hotel had descended into chaos, but from the angle Mai couldn’t fully see it.

Still, she knew this wouldn’t stay hidden. What had happened here would erupt into something massive.

And then she saw her.

Miho.

Miho lay only a few meters away, her body crushed beneath the ruins—everything from the waist down buried under tons of concrete now stained with still-warm blood. Her eyes were open, but unseeing. Her photochromic glasses—the ones Mai always teased her about, saying they made her look like a strict teacher—lay beside her, perfectly intact.

A sob caught in Mai’s throat.

“M-Miho…”

She fell to her knees beside her, her shaking fingers lifting the glasses as though they were the most fragile thing in the world. Hot tears slid down her face.

No.

No. No. No. No. No. No.

That couldn’t be real.

No.

She couldn’t be dead. Not like that.

Not without warning.

Not without shouting, or cursing, or making some stupid joke about how this would ruin her day.

Mai screamed, but she couldn’t hear her own voice. Her ears were still ringing from the explosion.

The shock made her forget her wound entirely.

She threw herself at the rubble with bare hands, scraping her fingers against concrete and twisted metal. She pulled at stones heavier than her entire body, pushed at beams that didn’t move an inch, as if sheer desperation could bend the laws of physics. She didn’t even feel the pain in her shoulder.

“Miho! Please—hold on!” Her voice fractured with every syllable, a howl closer to a plea than a command.

Dust mixed with her tears, blurring her sight. She called her name again and again, as if repeating it could anchor Miho to the world of the living.

But Miho’s lips remained still, her eyes open—clouded, unfocused, staring into a horizon Mai couldn’t reach. Half of her body was destroyed entirely, the other skewered by steel and glass.

Mai’s throat burned, a scream splintered inside her like broken glass. She knew it was useless—that even if she managed to move every piece of debris, what waited underneath would not be the person she had known. And still she kept trying, with nails and blood, refusing to accept what reality was placing in front of her.

It wasn’t possible. Her friend. Her sister. Her boss.

That boss who was always a little affectionate and playful, who wore band T-shirts with holes in them when she was relaxed or fell asleep scratching her belly button.

That sister with whom she shared beers on summer nights and who loved street food.

That friend who left ashtrays everywhere with cigarette butts and who could just as easily wear priestess clothes as a bikini in the afternoon to sunbathe in the backyard.

That person who always ruffled her hair and smiled behind their glasses. That person who gave her a place to call home.

That person would never come back.

That could not be.

A hand settled on her shoulder. Mai jolted and turned sharply, her vision drowning in tears.

It was Kii.

The police officer—her friend, the one who had stood by them so many times in the past years whenever a case involved the department. His face was streaked with dust, a cut bleeding down one side of his forehead, but his eyes were steady, resolute. There was no time to mourn. His eyes widened when he saw Miho, and then Mai’s face—caked in dust, blood, and tears.

“We need to get out of here… Come on. Mai-san, come on!” he said. His voice reached her faint and hoarse, as though he had swallowed words he wasn’t ready to say.

Mai wanted to scream—tell him no, that she couldn’t leave Miho here, couldn’t go on without her. But the world lurched around her, the floor cracked a little more, and Kii grabbed her just before she collapsed.

There was no choice.

The groan of collapsing concrete was the only warning they heard before the crash hurled them both to the ground. Mai felt the vibration run from her skull to the tips of her toes as a cloud of dust swallowed them whole. A massive weight struck the floor just meters away, chunks of cement and twisted steel rolling to their feet. Kii coughed, swatting at the dust, and pulled Mai to her feet. She barely reacted, still clutching Miho’s glasses in her left hand.

They staggered upright, and as the dust thinned, the sight in front of them froze them in place.

The entire upper floor had collapsed, as if an invisible beast had torn it loose and hurled it down. The elegant meeting room was gone—reduced to fire and wreckage. Somewhere beneath those twisted beams and collapsed walls, Ella Rozenkreutz and her entourage had vanished. There was no trace of the Wishper team, nor of the government officials.

Mai felt her stomach twist. She tightened her grip on Miho’s glasses.

This was a disaster.

A nightmare she wanted to wake from.

Kii wrapped an arm around her shoulders to steady her and urged her forward. “We need to get out before the rest comes down,” he said urgently.

Mai nodded weakly. They began descending what was left of the stairs when a faint, strangled sound froze them both. A muffled groan—barely audible over the creaking concrete.

They exchanged a look and move toward the sound. Mai stumbled against something soft. When she looked down, she saw an arm protruding from the rubble. Kii dropped to his knees and began pulling away chunks of what had once been a wall.

Beneath the wreckage they found Franz Rackhenhell—The Boy. He was covered in blood, a metal rod piercing through his side. His pale skin had turned a ghostly shade of grey, and his red eyes stared blankly into nothing.

“Ella…” he whispered with trembling lips. “Ella… Ella…”

Mai swallowed and crouched beside him. “W-we’re getting you out of here.”

The young looking vampire blinked as if struggling to understand. His lips quivered, but the only thing that escaped him was that same name. Mai felt a shiver crawl down her spine.

Franz wasn’t here—not fully. His mind was trapped somewhere just before the explosion, repeating a single thought.

Kii didn’t wait any longer. “Come on—we’ll pull him out together. Help me!”

Mai nodded, swallowed her tears, and yanked the metal rod free while Kii held the vampire steady so he wouldn’t move. Between the two of them, they managed to drag him out from under the rubble. The boy was lighter than he looked, but every step they took was a battle against dust and the shifting floor. Every crack beneath their feet sent a shiver through them. Franz could barely stand, his legs buckled like jelly. Mai held him tightly, and he clung to her without realizing it, with a desperate, trembling grip.

As they descended, Mai felt something inside her fracture.

She had lost Miho.

Now the horror of fire, rumble, smoke and collapse felt like a prelude—an omen of something far worse. And though she didn’t yet know what that was, she sensed it like a shadow lurking at the edge of her vision.

The air was thick with dust and ash by the time they finally made it out of the building. Mai could barely stay on her feet, Franz Rackhenhell leaning heavily against her, whispering Ella’s name over and over. Kii walked beside them, gasping for breath, his uniform coated in dust, his expression shattered—like a man who no longer believed what his eyes had seen. Adrenaline had pushed them this far, but now, standing at the threshold of safety, Mai felt her strength drain away.

Sirens split the night air. Red and blue lights flashed in every direction, reflecting off the shards of glass scattered across the street. Police officers, paramedics, and firefighters ran everywhere—calling evacuations, establishing safety perimeters. Some stared at the wreckage in shock, others wore the hardened expressions of those long acquainted with catastrophe.

Mai could barely process any of it. Everything felt distant, hollow, distorted by her dazed mind. She sank onto the sidewalk beside Franz, who kept murmuring, eyes glassy. Kii knelt next to them, holding Franz shoulder, saying something she couldn’t quite hear.

The glasses were still in her hand, but their owner was buried somewhere above. Her eyelids grew heavy, and before she could resist, darkness swallowed her whole.

***

When she awoke, the white ceiling lights blinded her momentarily. She tried to sit up, but a sharp pain in her shoulder forced her back down. Her heart pounded in her ears as she looked around.

She was in a clinic—not an ordinary hospital, but one of the emergency facilities reserved for the hidden world. She knew it by the scent: a faint trace of medicinal herbs mixed with the usual disinfectants. Her clothes were clean, though she felt the tightness of bandages wrapped around her torso and arm.

Time passed strangely in that place. She couldn’t remember clearly how she had gotten there, nor how many days had gone by. Flickering images surfaced now and then: Franz being rolled away on a stretcher, Kii arguing with someone, the sound of a television somewhere mentioning the attack. But it was all veiled in an impenetrable fog, as if her mind refused to rebuild the sequence of events.

The attack made headlines around the world. The explosion, the partial collapse of several floors, the list of victims and missing persons. But the true cause never reached the surface. Officially, it was the work of a terrorist group known as Octogonus, the same one behind other past attacks. Reports spoke of structural failures, a detonation in one wing of the hotel, a supposed government meeting disrupted by chaos.

There were no mentions of vampires, or feys, or secret negotiations between the two worlds. The truth had been buried beneath tons of rubble and governmental cover-ups. Conspiracy forums quickly filled with speculation: anonymous witnesses claiming strange movements before the attack, rumors that police had cordoned off the area even before the explosion, security camera footage that mysteriously vanished or was manipulated.

But in the official narrative, Mai did not exist. Neither did Kii nor any of the officers who knew the truth. And of course, there was no mention of a psychic detective and medium named Miho Sagara.

It was as if she had never been there at all. As if her story—her life, her shared days and nights with Mai—had been reduced to ashes, just like the ruined building left behind.
