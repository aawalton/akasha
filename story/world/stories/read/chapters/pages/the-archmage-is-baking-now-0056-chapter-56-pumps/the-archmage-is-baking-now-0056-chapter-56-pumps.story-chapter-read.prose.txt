The first pump was a surprise to Jane. While she had envisioned a hand pump in someone’s yard or home, this was a fairly large machine running by itself. She groaned inwardly upon seeing it. A hand pump would have been easy enough to examine using her magical senses. This was a big thing with moving parts, roiling with the magic of a slow-burning fire as it tried to perform its intended function.

Except it was failing. The machine was running, but Jane knew from Dave that it hadn’t pumped up any water in days.

“They’re keeping it running because it’s a hell of a thing to get it restarted once it’s cold,” Dave explained. “It’s coal-driven. Has to be loaded every few days or it conks out.”

Bella raised an eyebrow. “Every few days? I thought coal would burn faster than that.”

“Not in this.” Jane put her hand near the machine and felt a mishmash of magical signatures from several small circles, each propelling a different spell. “It’s magic. Enchanted, more or less. Like a cold box, only much more complex. How did this come to be here?”

Dave shrugged. “We hired it out. The town isn’t hurting for money. They come by every six months and re-up the magic, somehow. It takes them almost a week.”

Jane pushed back the thought of how much shorter a time it would take her to do the same thing. She wanted to avoid developing any feelings of unearned superiority.

Instead, she let hope push through to the forefront of her emotions. Perhaps whoever was doing this work had made an error somewhere in their calculations. If so, it was entirely possible that one of the magic circles had worn down or malfunctioned.

Maybe the problem is something that simple, she thought. Something I can identify. Something I can fix.

Jane let her magic surge forward into one of the circles, checking every element of its design. It wasn’t simple work, but by her standards, a circle like this one was as easy to read as a book.

A distressingly well-written book.

She analyzed every component of the machine’s magical structure and found it to be sound. Maybe a bit inefficient here and there, but not flawed in any significant way.

“Anything?” Bella asked, after a few moments of silence. “Anything at all?”

Jane shook her head. “Not yet. But there’s a lot of work just on this one pump. Give me five minutes.”

Bella and Dave chatted as Jane dove deep into the pump’s workings. Unfortunately, even an extensive search showed nothing clearly wrong. If there had been a serious problem, she would have seen it in some aspect of the machine’s energy flow.

There was no flaw in this water pump. And yet there it sat: running smoothly, and not yielding any water.

“It’s not this one,” Jane admitted. “This pump is fine. Time to check the water below the ground.”

She took a deep breath and said her next words silently, in the privacy of her own mind.

I’m going to hate this.

Looking at things close up with magic was easy enough, but it got harder and harder the further those things were from the observer. Though Jane had quite a bit of skill, even she couldn’t look through tons of rock and dirt without difficulty. Or, more importantly, without some kind of boost.

She took a few minutes to scratch out a rudimentary amplification circle in the ground. It wasn’t as precise as she might have liked, but it would work for her needs. Once it was in place, she stood in the center of the circle, charged it with magic, and sent her senses diving down into the earth*.*

She shot past what felt like miles of earthy magical signals before finally reaching her goal: a wet patch of soil at the end of a long, metal pipe. It was damp, but nothing like she would have expected to see from a water layer this far down. Where the pumps she had examined in Glenfall were nice and water-rich, this one felt like it was drawing from a sputtering, struggling resource.

Next, she looked around for any possible causes of that problem, but found none. By this time, her head was starting to hurt from the magic drain. She withdrew before she could empty herself so fully that it would hurt her recovery times.

“That’s a bust. I can see a problem, but not a cause.” Jane scuffed up her circle with her boot and walked over to her companions. “Next pump, please.”

Getting to the next pump was easy. Better still, this one wasn’t magically powered, so Jane could examine its workings without expending any of her own magic. By the time she had finished with the pump itself and had sketched another amplification circle, she had recovered just enough magic to dive down again.

At the bottom, she found a scene much the same as the last pump.

“This is terrible,” she announced, giving up any effort to shield Dave from the reality of the situation. “This work. It’s miserable. I knew it would be, but wow.”

“What’s so bad about it?” Dave asked, wide-eyed. “Didn’t you blow up a dragon?”

“I convinced it to follow its dreams. Kind of.” Jane sighed. “That was a different kind of bad. This is like picking grains of rice up off the ground. It’s tiring and frustrating. And I’m going to have to do it so many times before we’ve seen all the pumps.”

Bella looped her arm through Jane’s. “Just think ahead to lunch. If nothing else, this town has fed us really well. Just work towards that.”

Jane took her friend’s advice to heart as they headed to the next pump, and the next, and the next. There was little variance between them, and each took about the same amount of time to examine. Once they had covered all the pumps within the town limits, it really was time for a meal.

“Where’s good, Dave?” Bella asked. “For a meal, I mean.”

“There’s really just the inn,” Dave said. “Most people make their own food for midday, or eat at the jobsite. But the kind of food they make at the mines isn’t really suitable for guests. Just a lot of potatoes and meat. Hardly any seasoning. They eat it for energy, not taste.”

Bella frowned. “Hmm. I guess the inn would be all right. I was hoping for some variety, though.”

“You’ll have it.”

The group turned to track the new voice. Hattie was standing behind them, holding a basket.

“Dave, find us some place to sit,” the old woman ordered. “I have plenty for everyone.”

The nearest benches were not far away, and at this time of day, they were completely unoccupied. Jane got the sense that the village was fairly quiet throughout the day, with most of the residents spilled out to the mines. She wondered what evenings were like in Trailor. She had yet to experience one, since the night before had been monopolized by settling in.

Once they were seated, Hattie removed her basket’s cloth cover and handed each of them a small lump of soft, brown bread, still steaming with heat from the oven.

“This is a food from my hometown. It’s bread baked around meat.”

Bella bit into her roll with no hesitation. Jane’s mouth watered as the uncovered meat filled the air with a heavily spiced aroma.

“It’s supposed to be a traveling food,” Hattie explained. “Folks used to take them on trips and eat nothing but them for days.”

“Isn’t that unsanitary?” Jane asked. “They’d go bad, right?”

“Not with the right spices,” Bella replied around another mouthful. “And sealed inside the bread. They’d stay good for a while.”

“That’s about the size of it. Not everyone likes these, but I think you will.” Hattie grabbed her own bun and took a bite. “Now eat up, before they get cold.”

The team ripped into the lunch. There were two buns for everyone. Jane found it to be a surprisingly heavy, filling type of food. After her second bun, her mouth was so full of the spiced flavor and her stomach was so packed with condensed nutrition that she doubted she could have eaten another.

Hattie had stopped at one roll. “Good to see young people with healthy appetites,” she said with a smile. “I told myself I’d make these last night, when you two ate almost that entire bowl of noodles. Now, how are things going?”

It would have been possible to soften the story of their morning, but Jane decided that if anyone could take the truth, it was probably this old woman.

“Not great, Hattie. I can see that there is less water below the ground than there should be. Pushing the draw-pipes deeper might solve the problem for a while, but…”

“But if the water level keeps dropping, and it will, then that’s no solution.” Hattie nodded. “We’ve already tried that. It worked for a bit. You don’t know why there’s less water down there?”

“I don’t. But I will. Or at least, I hope I will. A place like this doesn’t get much rain, but that means the water below the ground should be more stable than other places. I can’t imagine you are overdrawing it, either. You don’t take more water than you used to?”

“Not much, anyway. The town has always been about the same size, and the mine has always had about the same demands.”

“Then there’s something else, somewhere. And I’m going to find it.” Jane stood up. “There’s no time like the present. Dave, you said there were pumps outside town, for livestock? I need to see them.”

Dave also stood, stuffing his last bite of roll into his mouth. “We should be able to see all of them before dark, if I keep the carriage moving fast.”

“Then let’s do it.”

Jane was turning to go when a thought hit her. There was something about the makeup of her lunch she hadn’t quite understood.

She turned back to the old woman. “Hattie, was that a special kind of bread wrapped around the meat? It seemed interesting.”

“There’s a bit of a trick to making it, if that’s what you mean. Why do you ask?”

Bella grinned. “Jane runs a bakery back in Glenfall. She’s always looking for new recipes to try.”

“Is that so?” Hattie’s eyes sparkled as she looked at Jane, as if seeing her in a new light. “Would you like to learn the recipe for those buns?”

“I would,” Jane confessed. “Only if it’s not too much trouble.”

“No trouble at all. Come by this evening, and we’ll make some together. That’ll provide your dinner, if you don’t mind eating these twice in one day. The fillings have some varieties, too. I’ll show you.”

Jane was surprised at how much lighter she felt at the prospect of such an evening.

Just get through this afternoon, she told herself. A few more hours, and you get to bake again.

She thanked Hattie warmly, then set off with Dave and Bella back to the inn where they had left Dave’s carriage.

The first pump they visited was near the mines, only a short carriage ride outside of town. Jane sighed with relief when she saw this one was truly a hand pump, situated above a long, deep trough.

“The rancher comes out here once every few days and tops it off,” Dave told her. “I heard him complaining at the inn about how long it takes now.”

“Still, he shouldn’t be able to do it at all. The water level is so low, it would take hours.” Jane touched the pump and found nothing unusual in its workings beyond the normal magically-dead signature of most conventional machinery. “I’m going in.”

She drew her circle and dove down, almost surprised when she hit the bottom. The water was significantly nearer to the surface here, if only by a matter of inches.

“Huh. That’s good.”

“Is it?” Bella looked closely at the pump. “It has the same problem, right?”

“Yes, but less. Which is a difference. Not enough to matter for this pump, but if the town’s pump was here, it would still be drawing good water. I’m guessing he’s dropped his pipe deeper, too. But if we find different levels at more pumps, we might be able to track the source of the issue.”

The rest of the afternoon was spent chasing that goal. They visited all the agricultural pumps in the area, and Jane took careful notes about the water levels, how long it took to pull water manually out of each pump, and how the surrounding earth felt to her magic. At the end of the day, she had a jumble of data that she had yet to decode, but it felt like progress.

“All right,” she said, scuffing away the day’s last magic circle. “Dave, you can take us back to town now. To Hattie’s, I guess? No, to the inn. I want to freshen up.”

“That’s it?” Dave looked doubtfully at the hand pump Jane had just finished analyzing. “Doesn’t seem like we got much.”

Jane lifted up her notebook. “Oh, we did. Trust me. I’ll do some studying with these notes. They should tell us something. And then tomorrow, we’ll look at the mines. Deal?”

“Deal,” Dave said excitedly, opening the door to his carriage. “Now enjoy your ride back. I’ll try to keep it smooth.”
