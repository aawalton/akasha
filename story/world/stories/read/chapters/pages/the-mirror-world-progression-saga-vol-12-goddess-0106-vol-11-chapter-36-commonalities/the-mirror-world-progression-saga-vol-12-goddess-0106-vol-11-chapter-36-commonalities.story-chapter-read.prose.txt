Samantha and a collective of Servitors pushed up on the colossal demon's foot as its tremendous pressure bore down. The entire construct buckled and bent itself, digging into the ground, effectively slowly burying the whole standing circle. It felt as if the whole region was giving way on some level.

Pillars cracked and had to be repaired, and the more flexible supports bent more and more. Sounds of strain came from every direction.

Ozra suddenly thrust a finger up. “I’ve got an idea!” A giant feather appeared, and it gently brushed over an open spot underneath the foot of the demon.

It might’ve twitched.

“Torture, is it?” the demon asked, mildly amused. “Nice try, but it won’t work on our kind.”

Ozra blew air out of her mouth grandly and threw up her hands, banishing the feather. “Welp, I’m out of ideas.”

A central pillar collapsed on itself, and the whole construct buckled yet again.

“Redouble your efforts, all!” Samantha called, as she suited her words and drove upward with the raw strength of her deific power. She grew out additional arms via [Conjure], becoming a veritable spider of limbs, to cover more surface area and gain a better grip.

A great shout came from below, and a whoosh of air and power collapsed downward, as she realized that it was Glae’s final word.

It meant something she couldn’t quite grasp.

She turned her head to see the hammer raised for an instant, crackling and resonating with intense white energy, leaking out ghostly trails so prolifically that it made a fog… and then it came down onto the tablet.

An explosion went off as the tablet was shattered, and likely atomized, as it blew everyone and everything away, including Glae himself. His protective cage vanished instantly as he went flying, his body smoking, and the hammer itself was knocked from his hands.

The resonance of the explosion was something that shook Samantha to the core, mind, body, and soul. She was like nothing more than water turned into ripples, and the rest of reality with her, made to warp and warble in some uncanny fashion. If it went on, all would simply melt and pool into an aggregate slurry.

Fortunately, the ripple calmed and cooled, leaving only echoes. But definitively, the world had been changed, and that included everything in it. Some things had been dissolved, had been melted and carried away, like vanished layers, like a desaturated stain. And she knew what the final meaning Glae had ended the ritual on.

Harmony.

You’ve become aware of a new World Spell:

“Harmony of Commonality” — All other World Spells not beneficial and usable by commoners are dispelled and banned from being cast. Any individual without a spellcasting class has two free levels of innate Magic Resistance. This is an irremovable, global effect.

The quest “Demonic Waves, Ritual Matters” is complete. 9(7) exp gained in [Goddess], 18(9) gained in [Magineer], 7(5) gained in [Brute], 5(4) gained in [Bard], 4(3) gained in [Saboteur]. Over 75% reward grade achieved. [Matter, As Spirit] improved to +1.

Samantha breathed an unusual, soul-deep sigh of relief, which felt aligned with Calrenazzod itself.

All around, most people were on their butts, looking stunned. Glae was apparently incapacitated, lying out on the ground, his whole body smoking like the evaporation of many ghosts. Fortunately or unfortunately, the body underneath wasn’t actually going anywhere, though.

The demon above them seemed to have paused. Even the rampaging giant eyes had stopped, pupils all pointed the same direction, one with a minotaur literally still grappling with it, who was also looking over at the completed ritual center in shock and awe.

“Well then,” the demon began, and lifted its foot off of the construct — this act, suddenly uncompressing various pillars and supports, caused many of them to fall apart or topple. “The ritual seems to be complete. We failed. That concludes our contract.” Slowly, the eyes began lifting by their end tail viscera, almost comically, while undulating and jerking, as if they desired to get away. The minotaur grappling with one of them let go a little too late, crashing to the ground with a thud.  “It seems there are no stipulations about apocalyptic revenge visited like a scourge upon offenders. Likely an oversight.” With a shrug, the demon began slowly floating back over to the portal. “Regardless, we’ll take our leave now.”

Samantha couldn’t help but sneer sourly as she gave a sarcastic thumbs-up. “Sure, whatever.” She shrugged. “Guess I’ll call off the dogs, then.”

One by one, all of the Servitors and such attached to the colossal demon relented their assault and flew away. Well, almost all.

“Deathsting!” Azure called admonishingly, hands on her hips. “Let go! Now! Let the demon go home!”

Deathsting growled as he stopped chomping on a toe to glare over at Azure. “This scum should be destroyed, utterly and completely, not released! You are all fools!”

“Deathstiiiing!” Azure repeated in a higher-pitched, even more warning tone.

With a final snarl, Deathsting finally disengaged, though not before stabbing the foot one last time with his tail. “Do not return, demon, or you’ll face my wrath again! Double my wrath!”

As the first of its eyes shlorped itself back into a socket, the demon replied in amusement, “We won’t if we can avoid it, loyal steed.”

Deathsting whirled around, incensed. “Wha-?! What did you just call me?!”

Before Deathsting could do more, Azure had him grabbed around the neck in a bear-like hug. “Just let it go, just let it go…”

As Samantha studied the regenerating wounds of the nearly indestructible demon, she suddenly ‘tsked’ loudly with her tongue. “Dart, get the fuck off of him already!”

The dragon had lingered on the same giant hand he’d started on, still biting and clawing, attached much like a ‘playful’ housecat on a ‘beloved’ human. He only growled in reply, apparently regressed to baser animalistic instincts. Despite multiple calls, he didn’t relent, and Samantha began flying over, wondering if she’d need to de-summon him.

The demon reached its portal and turned its six eyes on the dragon still attached to its hand. “Silly creature, you wouldn’t want to come with us. It would end your fragile identity.” With that, the demon made a sign with one hand, and a dark blade fell, severing its own arm at the elbow. “One we find fetching enough to preserve.”

Dart only snapped out of it enough to beat his wings and cushion the fall, landing while still biting the now slowly dissolving hand and tossing it around.

The demon finally parted through the portal without another word, and the vertical slice in reality closed behind it.

“You are so fucking dumb, Dart,” Samantha cried in long-suffering, shaking her head.

Various people flew over to Glae’s vicinity, who was just stirring from his incapacitated state, still lying on the ground. Samantha floated over, too, and dropped her light sheath effect — ironically, its defensive benefits hadn’t mattered terribly much, but the speed and offense bonus definitely had. She opened her real eyes, as the spirit world was something of an oversaturated eyesore that she’d gotten tired of right then.

Glae groaned as he sat up, holding his head. He was still enshrouded by a ghostly aura bleeding upward continuously. He looked up at the sky solemnly. “It’s done.” He squeezed his eyes shut. “For you, Klymene; for you, it is done.”

Samantha nodded to herself and glanced over at the half-destroyed ritual area. Now it looks like a right Stonehenge. “Sorry about the mess.”

Glae lowered his head and only opened his eyes slightly. “It doesn’t matter.” His jaw clenched with a sour expression. “We did what we needed to succeed. At all costs, it had to.”

Samantha studied him, wondering what he was thinking. Was he mad he’d legitimately needed her help for such an insane assault? That was probably it. “It was great work. I notice you didn’t bother mentioning the other particulars of that spellcraft.”

He sighed wearily, as if an old teacher with a problematic pupil. “Why would I? You might protest this or that irrelevant World Spell effect that your covetousness enchants you with.”

Samantha frowned and narrowed her eyes as she considered how true it might be.

Jeeves was hotter on that pulse than she was. He reported mentally, “Very little detriment has been detected so far, Your Majesty. Itha Ranon bears no ill effects, likely not reliant on such a spell, exclusive or no. There was some debate about that, though the consensus was that it wasn’t.”

Samantha replied, “And the ‘My Future Now’ spell is an aid to commoners, so that remains intact…”

“Quite right, yes. The only other flagrant sign of change is the total disappearance of the fog around the Primal Mystic’s territory. The Otherlands appear no longer hidden. We monitor our borders constantly, so this is physically confirmed and even recorded in a mirror.”

“Oh! Interesting. We knew regional World Spells existed just from that island curse we lifted. So another one is gone, then, even if technically protective. I guess an annoying fog wasn’t beneficial enough to the common man. It was too exclusive.”

“It should also be noted that the Primal Mystic seems to have anticipated and prepared for this, with the contact, the engagement in the war, the secured colony, and even connections of trade opening up to their lands.”

Samantha found herself grinning wryly. “Only to be expected, with the woman behind it all, yes. Anyway, thanks again, Jeeves.”

Glae was now peering at her with a frown, an expression she was sure was paranoia, and wishing he knew what she was smiling about. But he said nothing.

Guess we’re similar in at least one way.

“Alegon Glaelychus!” cried the Antimage woman in the mask, still floating and connecting six others to her weird staff. She was now shaking visibly with strain and panting between words. In a near panic, she continued, “We still hold back a spell… and it has terrible conditional backlash!” She groaned. “We are but minutes from defensive collapse!”
