One of the most sickening, wrenching sounds James had ever heard filled the air as he used Roscuro to execute the white-gloved man.

It wasn’t the pain of dying that elicited that. It was the involuntary reaction of a person having his soul consumed. Roscuro’s power, which James hoped would get around the rules of the melee—resurrecting the fallen—had successfully consumed the first Holy King’s soul.

Delicious, came Roscuro’s telepathic voice. The Soul Eater’s tone was even deeper than usual. I’ve never eaten a Ruler’s soul before. I can feel the power flow through me. Marvelous. Master, I have a new Skill!

“Great, Roscuro,” James said quietly. “If it’s useful later, feel free to use it. For now, we move on to the next one.”

As you wish, master. James could see the Soul Eater’s face in his mind, smiling.

The man lying on the grass had not stirred, but now that James was close to him, he could tell that despite his grievous wounds, this Holy King still clung to life. The System was a marvelous thing. It had granted so many abilities James would once have considered unnatural.

He stepped over to the wounded man, turned Roscuro point downward, and stabbed this Holy King right through the heart as well.

Despite the man having been unconscious, another, matching, soul-wrenching scream rang out through the air.

James could feel the attention of others on him and his allies, but he didn’t care for the moment. Luna and the Panther Queen were hardly injured at all from their confrontations with these two. No one in their right mind would pick a fight with the three of them when they were all gathered together.

“So, that was what you wanted to do,” the Panther Queen said. Her tone was non-judgmental, but James had the idea that she was a bit shocked.

“These are enemies of our country,” he said firmly. “They must be ended completely.”

“Yes, Your Majesty.” She bowed her head.

Luna and the Panther Queen quickly departed again. There were more Holy Kings to be hunted.

James sat back and activated Invisibility again.

All right, Roscuro, what’s the new Skill? he asked. Since we’re here alone…

Black Veil, the Soul Eater replied. Creates a dark curtain that blocks all sensory perception between the two sides. I think that with some practice, I will learn to turn the block one-sided. Could become very useful.

James nodded. That sounds pretty good.

There was no purpose to testing it just then—creating a black wall around himself would effectively make him more visible, since he was already using Invisibility—so there wasn’t much he could do unless he wanted to abandon his position.

He wasn’t waiting for long.

Within five minutes, Count Aleph returned with an unconscious man tucked under his arm. James recognized it was one of the Holy Kings he hadn’t spoken with.

“Fisher Emperor?” the Count called uncertainly.

I guess the smell of blood faded quickly.

He deactivated Invisibility.

“Ah, there you are,” Aleph said. “I suppose I am the first one back. I don’t see any Kings here.” He puffed out his chest.

“You’re not,” James said. “But pretty close. You’ll understand why soon. Give him to me.”

“He’s only mesmerized,” the Count warned. “In another minute, he’ll wake up, fighting fit. You’ll need to do whatever you’re doing quickly.”

“Not to worry,” James replied, smiling thinly. “I wasn’t planning on keeping him around long.”

He extended his hand, and Count Aleph handed the Holy King over with a trace of reluctance. The body twitched slightly, and James didn’t hesitate. Roscuro transformed into a dagger in an instant, and James plunged the blade into the figure’s chest.

There was another horrendous shriek as the soul inside the body shattered and was consumed. Then the body turned to nothing.

The Vampire Count froze, staring fixedly at the place where the Holy King had just been.

“I didn’t think,” he began, then made himself stop.

“Is something wrong, Count Aleph?” James asked pleasantly, trying to keep a straight face.

“You know damned well what’s wrong!” the Vampire exclaimed. “You never told me that this was what you wanted my help for. Murdering them! This is an environment set up to make death nearly impossible. Like the World Leaders’ Summit. But I sensed—there is nothing left of that man. Nothing for the System to put back together!”

Please be right, James thought. I still don’t know for certain if this will work the way I’ve been hoping and planning.

“It’s a place set up to undo physical damage,” he replied as evenly as he could. “If the System wanted to make souls invulnerable to being destroyed, that’s undoubtedly something they could have done. Since they didn’t do that, we must assume they did not intend to do.”

“What the System wanted is not my point, Fisher Emperor.” The Vampire spoke through teeth that ground loudly together. “It’s the nature of your plan, which you had not revealed to me—had probably not revealed to Cara, or that pleasant young lady would not have roped me into it either. Because you knew—”

“What, are you saying you wouldn’t have agreed to help if you had known I intended to permanently destroy this threat to myself and my family?”

“I… you have made what I bargained for almost worthless by executing your plan!”

James successfully kept himself from smiling.

I know. I don’t care.

“You will still have my protection,” James said. “Is that worthless?”

“By murdering a Holy King and joining a conspiracy that I imagine encompasses slaughtering all of them, I have made their entire Kingdom and all its sympathizers around the world into enemies! Hundreds of millions of humans, on the low end.”

“That does sound challenging,” James said. “But you will be able to call on me for assistance—”

“Twice!” Count Aleph growled. “That would have been a lot more valuable if I was not incurring the ire of a large share of the world in exchange.”

“You knew you’d be pissing off the Holy Kingdom. You just didn’t know the extent.”

“It is a declaration of war! Against a population of fanatics!”

“Then we’d better win,” James replied. “So, get back out there, bring me another Holy King. If we’re lucky and good, we’ll kill off all of the leaders, and they’ll be ill equipped to organize a response. The survivors, without a Ruler to defend them, will get ripped to shreds by their neighbors. With successful completion of my plan, you’ll not only be perfectly fine, you’ll get the full benefit of our bargain. My assistance in two future fights.”

“And if we fail? What will your protection be worth against the relentless onslaught of—”

“If we fail, you can always swear your allegiance to me and become one of my subordinate Rulers,” James said. “Then you would live within my territory, and we would provide for each other’s defense at all times. In the event that the Holy Kings retaliate, you would be right there alongside me.”

“You intended this,” the Count realized aloud.

James didn’t say anything, but he finally allowed the smile he’d hidden to leak out.

“Better get going,” he said. “Even if you deny participating in this, lots of people and monsters have already seen what we just did. You’re better off just committing now that you’re in it.”

“Son of a…” Count Aleph levitated into the air and flew off, still cursing under his breath.

He’ll come around. I haven’t really left him much of a choice.

Aleph’s territory in Eastern Europe was too close to the Holy Land to be defensible without a dense network of allies. The Vampire Count probably already had enough trouble with his human neighbors who weren’t super-religious.

James hadn’t exactly intended to put Aleph and others who agreed to help him in the position of needing to bind themselves to him for their own security. It was just something he’d been aware was a strong likelihood. A likelihood that might give his family one or two more strong defenders close by.

But the top priority was still clear.

The Holy Kings must be destroyed, no matter what.

As he thought that, a pair of shapes blocked some of James’s light overhead. He looked up and saw the two Taiwanese warriors. Each man had a figure over his shoulders.

It’s really cool that both Zhang Chāo and Han Jianguo made it here, he thought. I already knew Han Jianguo was strong, too, but it’s still surreal to see someone from another Ruler’s entourage in this place. He isn’t a subordinate Ruler. Just strong. James waited a moment, looked down at his arm to make sure he hadn’t reactivated Invisibility and forgotten to deactivate it again. But he was entirely visible. Are they coming down?

—

“Are you certain you want to do this?” Han Jianguo asked for what felt to Zhang Chāo like the tenth time, though it was probably more like the third.

“I thought you trusted my judgment, my friend.”

“You’re walking, eyes wide open, into his trap. I just want to be sure that you have actually definitively decided to do it. For yourself and our entire nation.”

“That’s why I trust you,” Zhang Chāo said with a reluctant smile. “When I die, and you become the new Ruler, Jianguo, you can break the pacts that I make guilt-free. Especially if you objected to them at the time. But at this moment, I don’t see a better path forward. James has placed his allies into the situation of choosing him or his enemies, and I would prefer to be his ally rather than take the other side. This is just him… living up to the other part of his reputation.”

Han Jianguo snorted but nodded.

The pair had seen and overheard the entire conversation between James and Count Aleph. The dialogue seemed to reveal a side of the Fisher Emperor that had been somewhat hidden at the World Leaders Summit—a more pragmatic aspect, reminiscent of how Americans had conducted diplomacy in the Far East for generations before he came along.

That was why they hesitated. This was the last moment when they could choose not to deliver the Holy Kings into James’s hands—not to participate in this unambiguous act of war.

But I already chose to tie my fortunes to his, Zhang Chāo thought. He will either be a key ally or my undoing. I have to trust my judgment of his character, and of the global circumstances. And above all, I already gave him my word. Promises must be kept.

Zhang Chāo began floating down toward the Fisher Emperor, and after a moment, Han Jianguo descended alongside him.

As they landed, James smiled.

“For a moment, I thought you weren’t coming.” He bared his teeth, and his smile took on a slightly wolfish quality.

Zhang Chāo forced himself to smile back. “This seals the permanent friendship between our two peoples, I hope. A shared struggle, from which neither of us can back down.”

James locked eyes with him, and his face became serious. “You can count on me. The two of us will defend each other—and each other’s peoples—until the maggots devour our flesh and the worms turn our bones to soil.”

Zhang Chāo felt a weight to the words that was more than rhetorical, and he wondered if James was using a Skill—his Ruler’s blessing, perhaps.

There was only one way to know, he sensed.

The Heavenly King dropped the Holy King from his shoulder, to James’s side. Then he took James’s hand and shook, maintaining eye contact.

The weight of an intense, intangible force fell upon Zhang Chāo, and he felt just a little more powerful, more solid, more real.

So I was right. Those words were his Blessing. Which should mean that neither of us can break the words we just spoke without taking a significant hit to our power. We’re actually in a permanent alliance. Easily worth fighting the so-called Kingdom of Heaven.

“May the sun never set on the Fisher Empire,” Zhang Chāo said.

“May the Heavenly King reunite all of China under his wise and steady rule,” James returned, smiling.
