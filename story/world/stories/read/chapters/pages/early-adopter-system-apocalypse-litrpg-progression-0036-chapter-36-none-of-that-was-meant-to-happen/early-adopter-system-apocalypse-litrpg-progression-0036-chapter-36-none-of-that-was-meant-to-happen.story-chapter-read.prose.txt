Scarlet woke with the distinct impression that something had been done to her. She wasn’t sure how she knew, but she did know that outside of those awful, glowing, scribbles on the floor, someone had done something to her.

She kept her eyes closed as she assessed.

Her body felt off. Everything ached, yes. But that was expected. Nothing felt newly broken, which was good. Actually, nothing was broken, which was less good. Certainly, less reassuring. Why was nothing broken. What happened? Just how long had passed and what had they done to her.

Her eyes opened and Scarlet sat upright fast enough that the room tilted unpleasantly for a second before stabilizing.

The Historian, who had apparently resumed pacing glanced over at the movement and, without ceremony, tossed something at her. Scarlet caught the small crystal vial from reflex.

“Drink that please. It’ll cure up the rest of it.” The Historian gestured at, well, all of her.

Scarlet looked down at the vial, then back at him.

“No.”

Tareth made a small noise that Scarlet could easily interpret as offence. Unlike the Historian’s carefully guarded feelings the other guy was a wide-open book.

“What do you mean no? I said it would help.”

“I mean no. I mean I do not trust you. How am I to know this isn’t poison,” Scarlet knew just how absurd that was. If the Historian wanted to smite her, he could do it with a particularly aggressive yawn, and they both knew it. Still. The example may have been murky, but the point was clear. She did not trust him, and she would not blindly do what he requested once again.

“Seriously, this again,” this time the Historian was frustrated. He’d gone along with her little act and even agreed to her terms despite the ways they might inconvenience him. Now she was refusing to participate because of what?

“That was not a strength test, and if it was, it’s the poorest one I’ve seen used.” Of course, she was keeping that she knew they did something to her close to the vest for now. She’d see if they would confess that on their own. Instead, she decided to call out the incident as gross incompetence. “I will not work with someone who would so immediately break trust so newly established. Or did you think I wouldn’t know?”

“How dare you speak to the Historian like that,” Tareth began. His master raised a hand and cut him off.

He stared back at Scarlet for half a second, then his ears twitched with irritation. It was a fair point. He had lied. Her not immediately trusting him and drinking the mysterious liquid was probably reasonable. However, the WIL test was one he needed her not to be aware was happening for him to get the data he needed. At least, that’s how it was supposed to work.

Of course, he might not have told her anyway, but it had nothing to do with malice or plans against her. He simply had never before seen a need to explain himself the subjects of a test. They just needed to follow orders. How was he supposed to know that she’d puff up like a spiky little blowfish over a tiny miscommunication. It wasn’t as though he’d meant for her to pass out. And he was sure now that everything would be fine going forward.

“Oh,” he said. “You have yet to see the results and you’re already objecting to my methodology?”

Perhaps they wanted to trap her in debt the whole time. The Historian himself had said that she was free to quit but that she’d owe him for costs. That was more than reasonable considering both were working towards the same outcome. What if he never wanted her to make it through this ‘training’ in the first place?

Well, if that was their plan, they’d be sorely disappointed. She might not be the first one to speak at a party, but she was always the last one to quit once she’d commit.
