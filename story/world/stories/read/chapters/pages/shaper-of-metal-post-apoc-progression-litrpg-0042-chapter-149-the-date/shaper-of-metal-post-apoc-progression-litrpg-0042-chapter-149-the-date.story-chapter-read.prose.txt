Chapter 149: The Date

A smiling Neex raised her brows and asked, “Do you know why I chose white?”

Jack paused in consideration, eyeing the dress and meeting her eyes, deeply mining his brain for the answer.

Think, Jack, think! Don’t be distracted by the sexy octobabe!

Obviously, the color meant something. Abstract? White to represent the first real date, something like that? Maybe. Probably not.

Hmm. First date, first meeting? Something to do with our first meet- ah! That’s it! Hahaha, I got it! Great job, brain!

Grinning, Jack grinned and nodded cockily as he declared, “Easy! It’s an homage to what you wore when we met: a white t-shirt.”

Neex’s eyes widened, and she gasped in surprised pleasure, her hands clutching together at her heart. “Ohhh, you got it right! Bo was so wrong!”

“Damn right, she is!”

“And I am so touched!” With a widening smile, Neex took what was almost a running start to glomp him, then seemed to catch herself and approach him in a more controlled fashion, flinging her arms out for a hug. “Careful not to mess up my dress or my hair! We have to be careful about that!”

Jack leaned down to hug her gingerly. “I wouldn’t dream of not being careful about it!”

After they lingered a bit enjoying one another’s closeness, Neex abruptly declared they needed to take pictures and video to remember the occasion. Jack endured it and helped out with holding ‘the phone of Ira’ thanks to his metal control. Fortunately, the phone had a great smart agent to take good pictures, so it didn’t take long. Then again, Neex wasn’t picky to begin with.

Finally, they took their seats, as pre-arranged drinks floated over from an alcove on a tin tray. Jack had infused black tea, while Neex had a custom cocktail of orange juice, lime, mint, ginger, and he forgot what else.

After Jack took a sip, he asked, “What are we up to on filled-out notebooks?”

“Twelve!” Neex reported. “It’s very frustrating not to be able to experiment in my lab with facilitating mechanisms for you all. I’ll be extra ready when I’m finally able to use my gifts, in any case.”

“Good. I look forward to it.”

The appetizers came, as a variety of six different ‘rolls,’ two of each, in neo-Vietnamese, -Cantonese, -Chinese, or -Hehadnoclue-ese styles. Whether they were authentic was anyone’s guess in New Babylon. There was a classic fried egg roll, which Jack knew Neex had never had, as well as crab rolls (which he also had no intention of eating personally), ‘lemongrass chicken rolls,’ spring rolls, and so on. It had three dipping sauces.

Jack was quickly impressed with the peanut sauce and did not stray from it, though he ate some without sauce at all, too. They both generated sounds of appreciation for the chicken rolls, which were just ridiculously good. Neex was impressed by everything but was thankfully especially drawn to the crab rolls and more than happy to eat both.

They only intermittently chatted until the main course came in, which was identical and discussed beforehand: two plates, one of roast quail and pickled vegetable medleys with rice patties, and the other noodles with charbroiled pork and a slightly different medley including onions, and drenched in a specialty house sauce with an unpronounceable name. They quickly dug in with tongs, and sounds and comments of appreciation soon commenced.

Jack spoke in between eating. “So, obviously, we need to discuss things related to expectations when we arrive in your society. Assuming everything works out on that front.”

Neex nodded reluctantly, as her totality of body language indicated one thing: reluctance. This was uncomfortable territory. “Yes. It is time, isn’t it? Well, first of all, I can say that our, ah, coupling — ahem — could be a very good thing! Yes.” She drummed her fingers nervously on the table as her eyes shifted away, her pupils erratic in shape.

“Mm-hmm,” Jack said supportively, and otherwise just waited. He took a sip of his drink.

“Yes, so… in my culture… usually… the mother, in particular, approves of potential suitors beforehand… only interest is expressed between potential m-mating pairs…” Neex slowly began turning paler, gradually matching her dress. Her voice picked up speed, though. “Often, the mother, or mothers, in coordination, will float suitors around the daughter, and she’ll communicate in the fashion she prefers — if shy or coy, which is common, that’s just to her mother, possibly leaving the suitor guessing and drawing more effort, often to give thoughtful gifts to show determination, especially if the daughter is literally indecisive, and” — she took a breath — “well. It can be very straightforward, despite how it sounds!”

Jack nodded slowly, eyes flickering around as he took a small bite of food and swallowed. “I’m sure.”

With a sigh, Neex continued, “If romantic relations occur between two, it’s simply proof of a match, and arrangements are made. Declarations, potentially. They’d only keep secrets if they are unsure and are experimenting with romance.”

“But this would be with a suitor already approved by the mother.”

“Yes.”

“What about the father?”

Neex shrugged, and Jack detected a note of petulance in her countenance. “A father will never approve of any suitor without active persuasion, so they can hardly decide things rationally, can they?”

Jack burst out laughing, perhaps with a bitter note or two, considering this was probably exactly what he’d be facing. “Ha… You do have a point, there, I suppose. And, ah, what we’re talking about here is” — Jack rubbed the back of his neck — “marriage, essentially?” His voice’s sudden squeaky noise made him wince.

Neex had actually been turning almost blue before that question. Face frozen, eyes wide, she quickly went fully opaque white like her dress. “No,” she chirped quickly. After a deeper breath, she continued, “Just promise declarations and a precious stone exchange. P-potential permanent lifebonding — marriage — is much later, as a deepening dedication and commitment to family, and the stone is fitted to an anklet, often with others representing the blessings of the closest friends and family. Before the anklet, mating pairs wear a gold cord or ribbon tied somewhere, while in public, to signify they’re taken, the details depending on the knotting.

“Skyhumans do not seem to have an equivalent, but the closest is the engagement ring. Skyhuman engagement periods seem more final and shorter in duration, however. Some declared pairs never bother marrying at all.”

“I see.” Jack navigated the discomfort with another bit of food intake and a sip. Finally, he replied, “It does sound fascinating, Neex.”

She smiled in some combination of relief and nervous energy. “I’m glad you think so, Jack.” With effort, she went back to eating some, and color oozed back into her skin. After this pause, she offered, “It is normal to be nervous about permanent ideas so soon, Jack. Do not feel pressure. We are making a journey experimentally together. Time will breed adaptation and answers along the way.”

Jack nodded vaguely. An experimental journey with an alien princess. No big deal, right? “Right. And now I need advice on how to ‘adapt’ to your parents, if the situation we anticipate arrives.”

Neex waved her hands dismissively. “I’ll handle it! But my mother will certainly speak with you. Don’t worry, Jack — she will love you! Just be yourself.”

Jack continued nodding, though he was very doubtful about it being that simple. Even Neex probably didn’t fully understand the sorts of things a mother would discuss with an ‘alien boyfriend out of nowhere.’ Jack could only imagine. “I appreciate the confidence, but can you give me some things that would help get on her good side? And things that will do the opposite, so I can avoid them? Taboos, faux pas, and such.”

Neex frowned even as she nodded reluctantly. “I suppose that’s wise. Be charming, but not too charming. A bit of… how to put it… aristocratic-style flattery? Maybe that would help, but it’s probably impossible not to seem rustic to our elites. I know that it would be seen through, so to speak, but I’m afraid that’s j- I see you smiling, Jack. What is that smile, exactly?”

Indeed, Jack had grinned at ‘rustic.’ “I guess I’m curious if that’s how you see me, as well. Rustic.”

Neex’s pupils did a swirl. “Would that be a bad thing?! It’s not a bad thing!”

Jack chuckled as he shook his head. “No, I suppose not.” Jack dialed up a thick hick accent, heavily exaggerated, and continued, “Well, here’s to hopin’ this ole rustic charm works on Momma! Like they say where I’m from, if Momma ain’t happy, ain’t nobody happy!”

Neex couldn’t help giggling guiltily at this. She averted her eyes and went back to eating. “That is clearly amplified for humorous effect, but I actually like that accent. I hope I can freely move among your kin on Eden, one day, basking in that charm. In any case, even if your attempts are regarded as clumsy by my mother, I feel she would find them endearing. You win either way, you see?”

“Mmn.” Jack ate a little more along with her. “I feel I should ask how both your parents regard the old order? The warrioristic society of the past. That might tell me how they’ll regard their daughter being the bondmate to a Skyhuman Allotted guy.”

“My father is wishy-washy on it but leans to disapproving, as he did to such ideas as the one which led me here, and certainly not just regarding me — in this, you can multiply his disapproval by a thousand, and perhaps understandably, as I almost died at least three times on this enterprise. I imagine that this will not improve his disposition. My mother… I simply don’t know. She is enigmatic about things she considers irrelevant. What we are bringing to her doorstep is no longer that, so I suppose we’ll just have to see. I am hopeful that a brazenly ambitious Deucalian such as her sees the potential of this. Of us, both personally and otherwise…”

Neex was now moving her food around with her tongs and staring at it. “I hope she will be proud of what I’ve accomplished, despite the tragedy that surrounds it. I failed in so many ways, but I succeeded in you, especially.” She smiled his way briefly.

Jack smiled, too. “I’m sure your father will be, too, in time.”

Neex sighed and shook her head. “I am not sure at all, and I know he will be very upset with me initially. I do hope that too, but will not expect it anytime soon. If I win over my mother, however, things will be tolerable. It’s a perhaps cynical outlook, but those two feuding is both nothing new and would take the heat off of me. While I don’t particularly deserve the cool weather, I need it politically.”

“It wasn’t your fault what happened to your friends, Neex. You know that.”

“Yes… personally, I know. We were prepared to die, and most of us did. What ensued was what we all hoped for, despite this, and I will not surrender the pride of that. For them, I will keep it in my heart principally and not dishonor their sacrifice. However, I must also face the ire of their families, who will not understand, and lay much blame at my feet. It will likely be a weight and a stain on me forever, and I will have to endure it and defend what we did despite it.”

She sighed and sat back, looking at Jack with a helpless expression, shaking her head. “So, you see, it is impossible. It is right, and it is wrong. It will not be spoken of in public, but I will face the fullness of their spite in private, alone, and their grudge will follow me in relative silence the rest of my days, because I will never, ever admit publicly that what we did was not right. That’s the honor owed to my colleagues, owed to our collective act together, in what I hope is seen as a heroic sacrifice one day.

“Regardless, I will not betray their pride and rightness in doing this, not even for the peace of their families — which is horrible, I hope you can see. What we did was not technically a crime in our laws, just deeply taboo, which controls most things. If I have my way, they must never receive official, public acknowledgement of wrongdoing from my superiors. I simply can’t allow it.”

Jack nodded even as he grimaced. He certainly had no solution he could see to such a thing, especially with his lack of knowledge about her culture and society. Perhaps the future held an answer somewhere. He hoped so. “So, that’s why you froze up real bad at the meeting, when Solvent was talking about the dead. You seem more comfortable discussing it now.”

Neex gave a subtle acknowledgement. “Shameful tragedy is not the sort of thing to shine a light on publicly. But you are my bondmate, and we are increasingly very close, so you should know, regardless of the discomfort and difficulty explaining.”

“I appreciate that trust, Neex. I will keep it to myself, naturally.”

With a sudden smile, Neex nodded and leaned back over to the table, picking food up with her tongs. “Let’s finish so we can have dessert, Boyfriend!”

Jack picked up what remained of the food he hadn’t devoured and did a ‘salute’ with it. “You ask for it, you get it, Girlfriend.”

After they both wiped out all other food, the dessert came on floating plates with spoons atop them: a kind of moist, drenched cake with a caramel sauce on top. It was light, divine, and the sort of dessert that left you considering seconds when it was gone.

Despite this, Neex dropped her spoon and leaned forward with a chin on her hand, gazing over at Jack and sighing. “Is it possible to go somewhere with a view outside?”

Jack considered it. “Probably? You don’t exactly look full ‘octo’ right now. I’ve seen people with blue skin before. Granted, you wear it better than any of them.”

Neex smiled in pleasure at the offhand compliment. “Whatever could be arranged. I know it’s short notice.”

Jack pinged Mini for authorized options, and he got a nice priority allowance of a special balcony of the tower, shrouded only by a hologram, just about undetectable from the other side, and pretty much fully open-aired. It even faced grid-north, which tended to mean less wind.

They exited the private room and took an elevator to a high level, high-clearance observation zone not far from Central territory. A short walk hand-in-hand through the depth of the tower’s outer wall, and they were there, on a small, quaint balcony with ornate steel railing and a smattering of very cushy seating. Despite the mitigation of the facing, it was still a bit windy, but the fresh air felt great. It was chilly, but Neex was unsurprisingly not at all bothered by that. They were also blessed by the moon being visible at a significant size, though not full.

They looked over the side down at Origin and New Babylon below, lit up in a dark, winter sky. Other platforms were evident directly forward in the distance, mostly by their various glowing or flashing lights.

“I love looking out on this,” Jack said. “Always have.”

“I can understand,” Neex replied, smiling. “It really is nice. I still find it bewildering that I could be so high up, so free and exposed to the entire planet like this.”

Jack nodded, wondering what it must be like, after spending most of one’s life surrounded. Embraced by walls or water. “It must’ve been scary initially.”

“Oh, definitely. Exhilarating, all the same.”

Jack suddenly laughed as he looked at her, as he noticed her updo hair was stubbornly, impossibly not budging in the wind, and showing strong signs of being a tentacled fraud. When Neex looked at him quizzically, he just made a finger motion at her hair.

“Oh,” she said, laughing. “There should be more chaos, hmm? Actually, I was practicing this earlier! Watch.” She got a sudden concentrated look on her face, and the hair blew out long, as though caught in the wind.

Jack marveled. He knew the tentacles were thicker within the mass, and mostly just showing the surface, but at a glance, it looked just like real hair blowing, complete with some gaps and very thin trailing strands. “Wow,” he exclaimed, “it’s definitely supernatural what you can do with those.”

Neex giggled. “Hehe, yes, isn’t it true?! I am very good at shapeshifting. The others never suspected I was an alien, as Ira!”

Jack nodded along, not bothering to point out they would never consider that possible until Memoria told them it was. Otherwise, just about everyone was suspicious in one way or another about her. “They never suspected a damn thing, did they? Haha! So gullible.”

Neex grinned impishly and curled her arm around his, laying her head on his upper arm. She sighed contentedly. “Does this night really have to ever end, Jack?”

“It’ll go on as long as you like, I’d say.”

“Mmnph. This is what is called a ‘white little lie’ in your culture, as it cannot be true.”

“You’re already wearing white, so it can’t be such a lie.”

“What?! That is silly, and you are making it up.”

“I’ll tell you what I’m not making up.”

“What’s that?”

“You’re more beautiful to me with every passing hour I’m with you.”

He felt Neex’s breath catch as she tensed briefly, then shifted to peer up at him, her pupils doing the ‘almost circle’ thing again. “Do you really feel that?”

Jack gave a subtle shrug as he leaned down, slightly closer. “You’d know if I was lying, wouldn’t you?”

Neex’s eyes widened as he drew closer, but she quite perceptibly did not pull back. Her eyes might’ve flashed to his lips. “The bond, it-it’s not foolproof…”

Jack nodded slightly, indulgently. “I have a question.”

The eyes of the blue beauty squinted slightly. She leaned closer. “Yes?”

“Just how close do you think a new boyfriend and girlfriend should get?”

Neex swallowed visibly, eyes darting. “V-very,” she said breathlessly. “Very close, I think. If they’re both comfortable with it, of course.” She pursed her lips — briefly — and leaned closer still.

Jack answered once more by getting closer, to where their lips were almost touching. With a little smile, he whispered, “Is this close enough?”

“Not quite,” she whispered back, the heat of her breath on his face.

A moment later, he kissed her.

She was very awkward for a split second, as if unsure how to kiss back and perhaps having pre-planned ‘tactics’ going into it, but the mental part of things quickly eroded as her body melted into his, the emotional connection overwhelming the anxiety, and she realized all she had to do was enjoy it for what it was. The bond helped, because they both knew how the other felt about what was happening, erasing doubt in a sea of inner warmth.

That enjoyment went on for quite a while in the windy night, with the moon’s light shining down, and the bond within them sparking something far newer and brighter in their lives.
