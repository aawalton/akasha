Moonlight

In the howling winds that shifted stalks against each other, a new, foreign noise was introduced into the mix.

Ting, ting, ting

Resounding, almost echoing. It was sharp and piercing, whereas the wind was rumbling. It cut through the space in chiming waves and, with it, the scent of blood. Sweat, sickly, maddening blood capsizing with so much promise. The twitch of a root raised loose dirt in a tide. The movement was slow, but once it started, it never stopped. The creature grew more alive. Awareness screamed at the edges of its consciousness but failed to get through the invisible barrier as it flailed in wild motions -- utterly helpless against its frenzy.

Then, all at once, the creature stopped its movements. Still. For one second. Then two. It put a tentative hand forward.

TING TING TING

The noise rang in the air once more, and with it, the scent of blood. With the same soft footfalls that disturbed no ground, the thing padded forward, flat on hands and feet. The movement was tilted and strained, looking like a limping dog as one arm and one leg on opposite sides extended two feet more than its pair. Sometimes, it crawled at a languid pace – one hand, one foot, opposite hand, opposite foot – other times, it favored something else – elongated hand, elongated foot, followed by several short hops of the shorter limbs as it gyrated and lowered its body to increase purchase. But no matter how it moved, the frustration of the creature only grew as it vibrated and bristled uncomfortably.

It never lost track of its goal. The blood. The noise. The creature found it. A canteen, the noise, and the smell came from its same internal source as it hung low on a corn stalk close to the ground.

The canteen. It needed the canteen, now. Why didn't matter. It needed it, and every moment it was away from the source was torture. It increased its pace, feet sliding across upturned dirt. With shaking hands, it bent its neck down and reached out towards its prize. Then, the ground reached up to engulf her head in darkness.

Blinded. Dirt, tearing its way through soft eyes as they darted in every direction to no avail. The creature vibrated, unable to scream out in pain as it reached to clear its face, but something was covering it. Nylon. What did that word mean?

It was on the ground. When did it get there? Something was constricting around its face, smothering it. A sharp pain in its legs. It tried to roll onto its back, but whatever was on top was too heavy. Off. Off. It needed to get it off. It was uncomfortable. It hurt. It wanted to breathe in deeper, to get more air into its lungs. But it didn't have lungs. Why didn't she have lungs?

It remembered. She remembered lungs. Air. How her lungs filled with it as she screamed for help and a voice. Calm. Sharp. She remembered what it said.

"Hold still. This part always hurts."

More and more panic as it flailed, flinging its arms back, hoping to catch the attacker. Another sharp pain at the shoulder, then it flared as a punctured hole turned into a tear that ripped the arm off.

The flailing tripled in effort, every piece of the creature that could move did, trying to buck the attacker off with kicking feet and a punching remaining arm.

And one by one, they were each torn off.

It couldn't move.

Then it was dragged away, still blinded, helpless to do anything.

The creature felt everything for an excruciatingly long minute, unable to see or know what was happening, and then something sharp pierced its back.

And then she was being peeled apart. Again.

Dorian

The trap worked well.

I got the idea thinking back to one of those books detailing different animals and their unique hunting strategies. In this case, trapdoor spiders and antlions. They were ambush predators that hid in the ground and waited for their prey to walk over them before striking.

During the brief encounter I had before, it was apparent that the creature was drawn to Nate through blood. It followed us perfectly, even with no line of sight on me or the other man. It was also relatively easy to subdue the previous monster once I was on top of it. The creature was plant-based, after all, and not of stocky trees, but rather from corn stalks – most likely.

So I came up with my plan. I dug a small hole and covered myself with the sleeping bag and a thin layer of dirt. I used the multi-tool to cut my palm and bleed into the canteen, then bandaged it up and hoped that the closed wound underneath the dirt would go unnoticed for long enough. In truth, the hardest part was waiting. Occasionally, I'd reach an arm up from the dirt to shake the corn stalk the canteen was attached to. I had put some of the silverware inside to cause a little more noise, hoping that if the smell of blood didn't travel, the foreign sound would.

In total, I waited for about thirty minutes before the monster arrived. It had been a struggle, but one much easier than last time, with the help of Peeler to dismember the grotesque plant creature.

I debated for a little bit on what to do with the body. The real question is how long the creature could survive without its limbs, or if it even needed anything besides the head. Hopping off the creature's back, I went over to the canteen and refastened the lid just in case any other monster got a bright idea. Hmm, actually, that was a good point. I could figure out exactly what I wanted to do later. Making sure I was safe was the most important thing right now.

So I dragged the creature as quietly as possible closer to the Greenhouse before reproducing Peeler. My greatest weakness was knowledge, so it was time to level the playing field. Raising the crowbar above my head, I slammed it into the creature's back along its spine. The odd disturbance in space appeared, and instead of pulling the weapon out, I rotated it, prying the space apart – and suddenly--.

"It's our only shot. We need a bit more Presence before we can descend. Come on."

God, the place was creepy. Endless fields of corn. Three whole Restrictions, too. That was by no means a small amount, but luckily, her teacher had drilled into her head just how important picking Restrictions were. Boons were nice. They were unique, and some had the potential to completely change how someone interacted with Instances. But Restrictions were how Instances made you lose.

Thomas hadn't been that lucky. It was his first excursion into the Dream, and he hadn't gained any Restrictions for completing his tutorial. Usually, they'd never be in a situation like this, but they just had to get caught in the reshuffle and get shunted up to the higher layers.

Thomas got all three [See No Evil, Line of Sight, and Watches Back]. It was a fierce combo. He wouldn't be able to see the enemy—if the enemy saw him, he'd be frozen, and if he looked at an enemy for more than a second, the enemy would know where he was.

The two of them heard a scream in the cornfields. They were able to push off the monster that was attacking the man and make a run for it. The man was too injured, though, and ended up falling to the ground. He told the duo to run, and they listened. They didn't know where they were running, so they followed the direction of his finger.

They found the Greenhouse shortly after. They ran straight into the spike traps, piercing their feet. The elderly lady inside was panicked when they burst in. Seemingly scared. She understood the situation quickly, though, and used some of her supplies to bandage their feet and gave them a place to rest. There were four cots. Two had items by them, but the older lady told them to just take the cots and rest up anyway. She made them tea, and they slept. She felt waves of exhaustion fall over her heavier than usual.

The woman woke up strapped to an operating table. She didn't wear clothes, but that was the least of her worries as the majority of her skin had already been peeled back. A man with brown hair and tired eyes spared her a glance before sighing.

"I hate when they wake up." He approached with a scalpel, syringe, and a bucket of writhing greenery. "Hold still. This part always hurts."

Then just darkness. Pain and a struggling torso with all its limbs removed. It couldn't see, and only the barest hints of consciousness remained, but they were there. Watching everything.

I blinked, processing the information as I sat in the field next to the monster. It didn't feel right to call it … her that anymore. I lightly tapped my skin, making sure that it was still there. That it hadn't been peeled back.

I knew her. In some strange way I knew that woman now. Her name was Kailey, a wet nurse, but people called her Kay. Her world was ruled by warlords, and each warlord forced their subjects to become Visitors. They had to pay a tithe of Presence every month, else they'd be dragged into an Instance and drained of Presence until they were just husks. She couldn't run either; she technically had the option, but she had a kid in her world -- two actually.

One by blood: that she switched with one of the warlords' newborn babies, and the other by love: the child that she had switched her own out for. Her plan had been to make the change and then abandon the other child and try to survive in the Dream, but things didn't work out like that. Love and kindness were such damning things.

It was nice to know that my suspicions weren't wrong about Nate and Maggie.

Cold comfort, at least.

What the hell is wrong with me.

I put my head into my hands and pulled my knees up to my chest.

I really hated people.

I should kill her, right? That would be the least cruel thing. Gods, I had dismembered her without feeling a thing a few minutes ago. The situation hadn't changed, just the information I had. Nothing I could've done about it. And I probably would've done it anyway if I had known. It didn't matter.

It didn't matter.

I hated this place.

I hated these people.

I hated this fucking Dream.

I hated this Instance.

I hated whichever fucked up Instance Holder decided to make this place.

Finally, I understood the second win condition. 'Helping the Scientist'. Gods, what a bastard.

How long had she been trapped here? In this place.

The only lose condition was to be killed by one of the monste– people turned plant creatures.

This woman hadn't been expelled from the Instance, so she hadn't met the win or lose condition. It also meant she wasn't technically dead. Whatever that scientist did to her somehow kept her alive, if only technically.

Nate's words resounded in my head, 'Don't kill it!'

I let out a sharp, howling laugh. Oh, that bastard.

Yeah. Yeah, that was right. That's why he didn't want me to kill the creature when we first met. Because it would free them, and they'd be able to re-enter the Instance again or just walk away.

What deal had they made with the "scientist" or even the Instance Holder of this place, I wondered. I looked down at the twisted woman. I felt my lips quirk up into a grim, plastered smile as I shook my head.

"Sorry ma'am. I'll set you free soon, but I'm going to need your help first."
