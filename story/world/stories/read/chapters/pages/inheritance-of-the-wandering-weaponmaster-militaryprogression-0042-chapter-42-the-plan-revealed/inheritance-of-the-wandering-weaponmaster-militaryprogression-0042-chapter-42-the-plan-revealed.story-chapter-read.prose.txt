As the group of people turned and walked away, Ezra struggled to understand the anger that the Madam held back because of his helping her. A plan that he almost ruined by saving her life? It wasn’t that he didn’t understand the words, but he could still feel the adrenaline pumping in his chest, his hands shaking ever so slightly as he tried to recover from the high of battle. He knew how lucky he’d been in being able to take out the two thugs before their leader had intervened. Nicholas was obviously much more experienced as an Adept than Ezra himself, and only one of them was using a weapon they were used to wielding. His mind struggled to parse the words.

The Madam’s statement that, apparently, The Dragon’s Toenail being burned to the ground and everybody being left homeless was according to plan didn’t make sense. And how did him fighting to protect his home and his family nearly ruin anything?

Uncaring to his confusion, the sound of two dozen pairs of feet walking echoed through the streets as they strode further and further north. Finally, Ezra couldn’t help but ask, “What is this new plan? It’d better be something good to warrant all of you just standing aside while they burned the place to the ground.”

“Ezra,” the Madam’s voice cut through his own biting words. “I said that I was grateful for you helping to keep me safe. That gratitude does not mean that I was helpless without you, nor that, had you not appeared, everything would have been destroyed. Perhaps I would have been taken as a prisoner and would have suffered as a result, but I have made it through forty-four years myself. I do not need you to question my abilities or my intelligence. I have maintained my position as the owner of my shop alone, without any hidden backers, extensive bodyguard networks, or anything else in that same vein. And I have done so exclusively through relying upon my own intelligence and planning. Do not insult me.”

“I wasn’t insulting you,” Ezra replied. “It just doesn’t make any sense what you’re doing.”

“And I have not confided in you what was happening in the slums for the past two months, nor have I seen fit to share with you what my plans were to maintain abreast of the shifting tides while also keeping my people safe here.” Her voice was even, but firm.

“I went to join the army because I didn’t have any other choice,” Ezra protested.

“And I have not faulted you for that,” the Madam replied. “I am merely telling you that things have happened in the past months that you are unaware of, things that you may have known if you were in frequent contact with anyone from the slums. I have been aware of many changes here, and I have prepared myself and my people accordingly. Now, if you would like to request information instead of demanding it, I am sure that there are several people here who are willing to apprise you of what the plan has been for the past several weeks.”

Ezra took a deep breath before nodding. “I’m sorry. I wasn’t trying to insult you. What’s happening in the slums? Knifefingers has never been bold enough to try stupid shit like this before. What happened to make him willing to piss off every other little faction in the south?”

“He’s always been hungry to prove himself,” Liam said. “Everybody knows that the Blades are petty and will come after anybody who crosses them.”

“Yeah,” Ezra nodded, “but that just meant that they held onto their couple of blocks and everybody else left them in peace. What changed?”

“You met one of the big changes,” Liam grimaced.

“Nicholas?” Ezra intoned it as a question, though he knew the answer.

“Nicholas,” Liam nodded, and several of the other employees of the Inn nodded in agreement. “He’s the ratkin’s newest muscle, and anyone who’s stood up  to him gets beaten down or even killed pretty quick. And there’s him and some new Priest? I guess one of the clerics of Jibahia decided to come and support his claim to control here?”

“An actual Priest? Not a Deacon or Acolyte?” Ezra confirmed.

“That’s what the word on the street is,” Liam shrugged.

“It isn’t confirmed if the one they’re calling the Priest is a Priest in the truest sense,” the Madam spoke, the mutters of conversation dying as she spoke, “however, it is confirmed that they are at least a Deacon. Second circle divine magic has been witnessed, with a blessing of leadership being bestowed on Knifefingers in broad daylight.”

“How did he afford to get consistent support from someone like that,” Ezra wondered, “as well as an Adept as strong as Nicholas? If the priest is actually a Priest, and can cast spells of the third circle, Nicholas might even be a Pseudo-Knight.”

“It doesn’t really matter to us if that is the case, does it?” the Madam asked. “Either way, it is a force that we cannot confront directly. With the influence of two powerhouses like that, the Blades’ expansion has become more violent, especially with so many of those who might have stood against them being pulled away as levies and soldiers. By now, they’ve largely taken control of the south, with the last holdouts at most days away from succumbing to the pressure. If we were to stay in the southern Quarter, it would only be as servants or slaves to the Blades under Knifefingers.”

“So you organized an opportunity to relocate farther north,” Ezra understood. “Do you already have a location set aside for The New Dragon’s Toenail?”

“Now you understand,” the Madam nodded. “We were waiting for the Blades to come and follow through on their threats. Once they did, we could begin our relocation in earnest. Bit by bit, over the past weeks, we’ve taken the most valuable items out to our new location, but couldn’t relocate ourselves before then without triggering any suspicions.”

It all came together for Ezra at that point, and he understood how his actions could have been harmful to the plan.

“And you expected that holding the ground like you had would have resulted just in the shop being burned down, with Knifefingers waiting for you to come crawling back, begging?” he clarified. “If I’d managed to kick them all out, then the Blades would have come around with a vengeance, trying to beat you down for standing up to them.”

“Exactly. Instead, he seemed to have wanted to take a prisoner or even to teach me a physical lesson. For that, I am grateful for your presence. However, I wish I could have had some more time to explain exactly what we were doing before it was too late,” as she said that, the Madam’s face became concerned and she worried at her bottom lip. “My information was that they would only appear a day or two later.”

“Well, I’m glad I was here to help,” Ezra nodded. “You’ll have to tell me what else I can do for you in the next day or so. I have another full day of leave to help you with relocation, preparing anything that you need, and so on.”

“Well,” the Madam said with her usual faint smile, “you already requested washing and a bath, at a reasonable rate. I suppose that rate can be waived for your participation in my protection. However, the food will remain at its usual price.”

“I would expect nothing less,” Ezra laughed as he walked forward.

They walked a few steps before the Madam looked up at him and said, “You seem to have done well for yourself there. When you have an opportunity and no longer bear the scent of weeks of marching, I would like to hear of your experiences.”

Ezra smiled and nodded in response. The group of people walking north went quiet as he and the Madam finished their conversation, though Ezra couldn’t help but notice that Matilda had been virtually silent the entire time. Without knowing what was wrong, he walked up beside her and nudged her with his hip.

“What’s wrong?” he asked, keeping an eye on her face. The freckles dusted across her nose seemed to darken as she glared up at him.

“Two months and you said nothing?” she said. “You had two months to send any information our way, to drop by, to in any way, shape, or form let us know that you were alive, and you said nothing. Then, finally, you drop in and help, but also nearly make everything go tits up.”

“That’s no language for a lady,” Ezra teased as he smiled down at his old friend.

She didn’t rise to the bait, instead just looking up at him, the faint hint of tears in the corner of her eyes.

“Why the hell didn’t you say anything?” she asked, her voice soft. “I know you were where you’ve always wanted to be, but do the rest of us really mean that little to you?”

Ezra’s stomach dropped as his mouth went dry at that. “I’m sorry. I was just busy, and then most of the time I wasn’t even in the city, you know. We only had about two weeks of training before we were gone, and then—”

“And you didn’t think to send a single note for those two weeks,” Tilly shrugged. “You had plenty of time, and you just didn’t think about it. You didn’t care to think about it.”

“Well, that’s not fair,” Ezra snapped back. “I was training literally for fourteen hours a day every day to make sure that I didn’t get killed by a horde of goblinoids. I just didn’t have any time, I was focused on making sure I could suvive!”

“Cut the bullshit,” Tilly said, her crying face becoming angry. “It takes two minutes to write a letter, to do something to send our way, and then it would be plenty easy to send a letter somehow, I’m sure. You just didn’t think about it. So why do we not occupy your thoughts at all? You just like fighting that much?”

Ezra clenched his jaw and continued walking in silence for ten, twenty steps. The quiet conversations that had existed among the other employees of The Dragon’s Toenail had died, and Ezra felt a flush of shame across the back of his neck and cheeks.

“I worried about you every day,” Tilly said, and Ezra’s shame went from having this conversation being overheard to instead his own lack of thought. He remembered being abandoned, worst of all by his mother, but plenty of other times by friends.

“I thought about you,” Ezra said, though the excuse sounded weak to his own ears. “I’ll be better.”

“I hope so,” Tilly said, and they walked in silence for the next couple of kilometers until they arrived at the new location. A few times Liam seemed to want to approach to ask some question, but decided against it. Instead, he allowed Ezra and Tilly to walk together, and Ezra wanted nothing more than to be rescued from this situation. Eventually, nearly half an hour later, conversations had broken out again around them, but Ezra and Tilly remained in silence themselves.

Once, he’d tried to gain a little separation from the pissed off girl, but she’d shadowed him as he went, and the others retained a separation of a few meters between themselves and him, so he was left in the uncomfortable silence. The few times he tried to break the silence she hardly engaged with him, and he was left in a frustrated silence. Finally, the madam’s steps slowed and the refugees arrived outside of regular-looking building.

“Welcome home,” the Madam said, her tone warm. “Now, let’s get you taken care of, shall we?”
