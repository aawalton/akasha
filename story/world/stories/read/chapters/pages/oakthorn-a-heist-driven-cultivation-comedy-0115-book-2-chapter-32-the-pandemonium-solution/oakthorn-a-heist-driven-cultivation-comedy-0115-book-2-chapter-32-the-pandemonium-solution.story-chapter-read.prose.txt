The stone beneath their feet split with a sound like the world ending.

Nora felt it before she heard it—a primordial shudder rippling through the ancient crypt, as if the very foundations were rejecting them. The floor cracked in jagged lightning patterns, emerald light seeping through the fissures like poisoned blood.

“Move!” she barked, yanking a still-dazed Evie into her arms. The siren was barely conscious, her eyes fluttering open with unfocused clarity. “Everybody move now!”

No one needed to be told twice. As chunks of ceiling plummeted around them, Nora’s mind calculated trajectories with paladin precision, even as her heart hammered with very mortal fear. They were trapped in a death box designed by ancient powers beyond comprehension, and their only exit had just been sealed by a possessed gold-ranker who’d kidnapped their ward.

And people thought being a Fateweaver was predictable work.

“Left passage!” Elena shouted, dodging a collapsing pillar with the grace only a master thief could manage. Her whip-dagger sliced through a falling beam that would have crushed Rayka, buying precious seconds.

Nora hefted Evie more securely against her chest and lunged forward. The siren was light—all gangly limbs and flowing hair—but even a feather becomes a burden when navigating a collapsing maze of death. A massive chunk of masonry crashed where they’d stood half a heartbeat earlier, sending shrapnel skittering across the stone.

“Nora!” Cade’s voice cut through the chaos.

He stood at the entrance to a narrow corridor, destruction magic blazing around his hands as he held back a cascade of debris. Silver-gold flames pulsed from his palms in concentrated waves, vaporizing smaller chunks and redirecting larger ones. Sweat poured down his face, his stance unwavering despite the strain of keeping a literal cave-in at bay.

“This way!” he bellowed.

She pivoted mid-stride, changing course without hesitation. Trust was a luxury in her former life as a paladin; in this crew, it was the only currency that mattered. If Cade said “jump,” you were already in the air before asking questions.

The corridor was barely wide enough for Gavin’s broad shoulders. As they squeezed through, runes hidden for millennia blazed to life along the walls—ancient symbols pulsing with malevolent purpose. Nora recognized sigils for ‘purge’ and ‘cleanse’ among the arcane script.

This wasn’t just a collapse—it was a defense system.

“The crypt’s trying to expel us,” she called to Cade’s back as they sprinted down the increasingly narrow passage. “Like a body purging poison from its veins.”

“Thanks for the fascinating insight,” Cade shouted back, his voice strained as he used another burst of destruction magic to clear falling debris from their path. The silver-gold flames carved through stone like butter, leaving molten edges where they struck. “I was worried this was just a regular old death trap.”

A blade of pure energy sliced horizontally across the passage ahead, nearly decapitating Gav as he ducked with reflexes only partly human. The lycanthrope’s eyes flashed amber, his muscles tensing with partial transformation.

“That wasn’t there before,” he growled, the words slightly distorted by elongating canines.

“None of this was,” Nora replied grimly.

The passage suddenly opened into a circular chamber they hadn’t encountered before. Massive metal scythes whirled in complex, overlapping patterns that left no safe path across. The edges gleamed with enchantments that hummed with deadly promise.

Bunny darted ahead, the little dragonling’s agility a white blur as he navigated the first few blades. His tail narrowly avoided amputation as he leaped and rolled, chittering urgently for them to follow.

“Show-off,” Elena muttered, but her face betrayed terror beneath the bravado.

Cade cursed colorfully, destruction magic flaring as he targeted the mechanisms. The silver-gold flames struck one of the axles, causing the attached blade to falter in its rotation. Not enough to stop it completely, but creating a momentary disruption in the pattern.

“We can’t go back,” Rayka said quietly, glancing over her shoulder at the collapsing corridor behind them. “And we can’t stop here.”

Nora made a split-second assessment, balancing Evie’s dead weight against the patterns of the blades. “I’ll go first with Evie. Cade, you’re after me. Elena, stay with Rayka. Gav—”

“I’ll bring up the rear,” the lycanthrope nodded, eyes tracking the blade patterns with predatory focus.

No time for debate. Nora counted three spinning cycles, identifying the overlapping moments when passage was possible. Then she moved.

The first gap appeared precisely where she calculated—a momentary opening between two crisscrossing blades. She lunged through it, Evie’s unconscious form curled protectively against her chest. Cold steel whispered past her spine, close enough to trim her hair.

Two more steps. A duck. A pivot that would have made Jer proud.

The thought of their acrobat sent pain lancing through her chest sharper than any blade. Focus. Survive now, mourn later.

She reached the halfway point, where three blades converged in a complex pattern. The timing required split-second precision. Nora breathed out, centering herself in the way she’d been taught as a novice paladin.

I have walked through the darkness. I have walked through the darkness. I have—

“Nora, shadow above!” Cade’s warning cut through her concentration.

She spotted it instantly—a fourth blade descending from the ceiling, altering the pattern. Improvising, she dropped to one knee and twisted sideways, the blade passing within a hair’s breadth of Evie’s face. The maneuver left her unbalanced, teetering at the edge of another blade’s path.

Cade’s hand materialized, grabbing her arm and yanking her forward with desperate strength. They tumbled together through the final gauntlet of blades, rolling into the relative safety of the far doorway.

“Next time,” Cade panted, helping her situate Evie against the wall, “let’s rob a nice, sensible dragon hoard instead of cursed crypts.”

“Noted,” Nora replied, already turning back to guide the others. “Elena, follow my path! Third blade moves faster than it looks!”

One by one, they navigated the deadly gauntlet. Elena moved with a thief’s grace, making the deadly obstacle course look like a mild inconvenience. Rayka was less fluid but no less precise, her movements careful and deliberate. Bunny zipped back and forth, seemingly taunting the blades that couldn’t touch him.

Gavin was last, his partial transformation giving him enhanced reflexes but bulkier form. As he neared the final sequence, the entire chamber shuddered ominously. Cracks spread across the ceiling, altering the trajectory of the hanging blades.

“Gav, wait!” Nora shouted, but it was too late.

The lycanthrope was mid-leap when a section of ceiling collapsed. He twisted in mid-air, narrowly avoiding being crushed but landing directly in the path of a horizontal blade. For a heart-stopping moment, Nora was certain she was about to watch him bisected.

Then his body contorted unnaturally, spine arching backward at an impossible angle as the blade passed cleanly over his chest. He completed the movement with a backward handspring that brought him safely to their side, breathing hard.

“Show-off,” Elena echoed her his earlier taunt, but relief made her voice unsteady.

“Let’s not do that again,” Gavin muttered, his eyes fading from amber back to normal.

They had no time to celebrate. The doorway they’d reached led to another ascending passage—one they hadn’t seen before. It seemed to have materialized from nowhere, steps spiraling upward into darkness.

“Up and out,” Cade ordered, taking point. “Stay tight, stay alert.”

The ascent became a blur of desperate scrambling. Nora’s arms burned with the effort of carrying Evie while climbing, jumping, and dodging. The stairway itself seemed alive, steps occasionally disappearing beneath their feet or shifting positions when no one was looking directly at them.

Cade’s destruction magic proved invaluable, clearing paths through sudden debris falls and holding back cascading sections of ceiling. Each display of power left him visibly drained, but he pushed forward with grim determination, one hand constantly wreathed in protective flames.

“Duck!” he shouted suddenly, dropping flat as a massive stone block swung across the stairway like a pendulum.

Nora threw herself sideways, nearly losing her grip on Evie as the stone missed them by inches. Elena wasn’t as lucky—she’d been a half-step too slow.

Without conscious thought, Nora’s greatsword left its sheath. In one fluid movement that belied the weapon’s massive size, she cleaved the block in two, splitting it cleanly down the middle. The halves fell harmlessly to either side of a wide-eyed Elena.

“By the four hells,” the thief breathed, staring at the shattered remnants of what would have been her death. “Remind me to buy you a drink if we survive this.”

“Make it the whole bottle,” Nora replied, already resheathing her sword and adjusting Evie in her arms.

Their path took them through the empty tunnel that had previously led to the watery chamber—Jer’s final resting place. Only now it wasn’t empty. Whispers filled the air, countless voices speaking words just beyond comprehension. Ghostly hands reached from the walls, grasping with spectral fingers at anything within reach.

“What fresh horror is this?” Rayka whispered, pressing herself against the center of the tunnel to avoid the reaching appendages.

Cade’s destruction magic flared again, burning away the nearest ghostly hands. “The Hall of Whispers,” he muttered, recognition dawning in his eyes. “I’ve read about places like this. The dead try to reclaim life through touch. Talk about desperate.”

Elena froze.

“What is it?” Nora inquired, sword hand already twitching toward her grip.

“You don’t…” she swallowed hard. Elena’s red-rimmed eyes met hers. “You don’t think he’s one of them?”

Nora cursed internally. She hadn’t even considered where these restless souls came from.

“If he is, he wouldn’t want to be the reason for your death, El,” Cade answered, putting a tentative hand on the redhead’s shoulder. Nora noted that she didn’t pull away from his touch as he led her way from a particularly earnest arm reaching for her face.

Nora was about to add to the sentiment, but a particularly large section of the ceiling gave way directly above them. With a shout of effort, Cade channeled a massive surge of destruction magic upward, the silver-gold flames expanding into a dome-like shield. The debris struck the barrier and vaporized on contact, raining harmless ash around them.

The exertion left him staggering, blood trickling from his nose. Nora caught his arm before he could fall, steadying him with her free hand.

“I’m fine,” he insisted, though his complexion suggested otherwise. “Just need a moment.”

“We don’t have moments,” she replied, nodding toward the end of the whispering tunnel. “Look.”

Where there had previously been a solid wall, a new staircase now spiraled upward—steep, narrow, and ominously dark.

“Another ascent,” Cade observed, wiping blood from his face. “How thoughtful of our hosts.”

“How far up do we need to go?” Gavin asked, eyeing the stairs with understandable suspicion.

Nora shook her head. “We’re at the very bottom of this place. Who knows how many levels we’ll need to climb to reach the surface?”

“Only one way to find out,” Cade said, straightening his shoulders with visible effort. “Unless anyone fancies staying here with our friendly ghost hands?”

No one did.

They began the ascent, the narrow staircase barely wide enough for single-file travel. Cade took point again, one hand trailing the wall for balance, the other still flickering with subdued flames. Nora followed with Evie, then Rayka, Elena, and finally Gavin bringing up the rear, his enhanced senses alert for pursuit.

The staircase seemed endless, winding upward in a tight spiral that left them dizzy and disoriented. Unlike the previous passages, this one was eerily quiet, the only sounds their labored breathing and footsteps echoing against ancient stone.

After what felt like hours but was likely only minutes, Cade called a halt. They’d reached a small landing—little more than a widening in the staircase, but enough for them to huddle together and catch their breath.

Nora gently set Evie down, massaging her cramping arms. The siren’s breathing had steadied, but she remained unconscious, her features pale in the flickering light of Cade’s magic.

“She needs more than we can give her right now,” Nora said softly, brushing a strand of hair from Evie’s face. “Whatever she saw in that vision—it was too much.”

“We’ll get her out of here,” Cade promised, though exhaustion lined his face and dulled his usually sharp eyes. “We’ll get everyone out.”

The words hung in the air, heavy with the absence of those already lost. Jer, sacrificed to the crypt’s hunger. Orro, lost in the chaos of their escape. And Chubbs, stolen by something ancient and terrible wearing Bernard’s skin.

The silence stretched, filled with unspoken grief and recrimination. Finally, Cade broke it, his voice low and heavy with regret.

“I should have told her,” he said, staring at his hands. “Chubbs. I should have told her who she really was. What she really was.”

Nora watched him carefully, noting the genuine anguish etched into his features. “Would it have changed anything?”

“Maybe she’d have been prepared,” Cade replied, looking up with haunted eyes. “Maybe she wouldn’t have looked at me with that... betrayal. Right before Bernard took her.”

“You were protecting her,” Rayka offered gently. “She’s just a child.”

“A child goddess,” Elena corrected, her voice brittle with grief and anger. “Let’s not pretend we didn’t all know exactly what we were doing when we took this job from Destruction.”

Cade’s jaw tightened. “Yeah, well, things change.”

“Do they?” Gavin asked quietly. “Was delivering Creation to Destruction ever the plan you intended to follow through with?”

The question hung in the air, weighted with implications. Nora watched Cade carefully, waiting for his answer though she suspected she already knew it.

“No,” he admitted finally. “Not since... I don’t know when exactly. Maybe not since the first time she called me ‘Cadey’ and asked for a bedtime story.” He shook his head, a mirthless smile twisting his lips. “So much for not getting attached.”

“She’s family,” Nora said simply. “It happens.”

“Some family we are,” Elena muttered, tears glistening in her eyes that she refused to let fall. “Jer’s dead because of this cursed place, Orro’s gone, and now Chubbs—”

“We’re getting her back,” Cade interrupted, his voice hardening with resolve. “All of this—the lies, the mission for Destruction, the deal with the Clover Clan—none of it matters now. What matters is that Chubbs is out there with that... thing wearing Bernard’s skin, and I’m going to get her back. I’m going to make this right.”

“And when we do?” Rayka asked quietly. “What then? Destruction will still want her sister delivered.”

“Then Destruction can take it up with me personally,” Cade replied, a dangerous edge to his voice. “Chubbs stays with us. She’s one of us now.”

“So we get Chubbs back,” Cade said, his voice steadying with purpose. “And we figure out the rest later.”

“Simple as that?” Elena asked, skepticism and grief warring in her expression.

“No,” he admitted. “Not simple at all. But we do it anyway.”

Nora knelt beside Evie, gently checking for fever or other signs of distress. The siren’s breathing had steadied somewhat, but she remained unresponsive, lost in whatever vision had overwhelmed her consciousness.

“We need her back too,” Nora murmured, smoothing a strand of pale hair from Evie’s forehead. “Whatever she saw... it might help us understand what we’re facing.”

As if responding to her voice, Evie stirred slightly, her eyelids fluttering. Encouraged, Nora squeezed her hand. “Evie? Can you hear me?”

The siren’s eyes opened partway, unfocused and distant. She whispered something too faint to hear clearly before drifting back into unconsciousness.

“What did she say?” Rayka asked, leaning closer.

Nora reached to her hip, withdrawing a slim journal bound in steel-reinforced leather. The tooled cover bore the distinctive eye-and-thread symbol of the Fateweavers.

“You’re seriously taking notes now?” Elena asked incredulously.

“Every word matters with prophecy,” Nora replied, already flipping through pages filled with precise, angular script. “Especially Evie’s.”

She found a blank page and withdrew a thin charcoal pencil from the journal’s spine. As she began to write, Cade leaned over her shoulder, eyes narrowing as he studied the strange symbols flowing from her hand.

“That’s not Common,” he observed, brow furrowing in concentration. “Some kind of... cipher?”

“Fateweaver shorthand,” Nora confirmed without looking up. “Designed to capture prophecy verbatim without losing nuance.”

Cade’s head tilted, eyes tracking the symbols with remarkable intensity. “So that mark there—that’s for ‘beneath,’ right? And the loop represents... chaos?”

Nora paused, glancing up in surprise. “How did you—”

“Pattern recognition,” he shrugged, already focusing on the next line. “The symbols follow logical groupings based on similar phonic structures, and there’s a consistent stroke pattern for conceptual versus concrete words. That curving line means ‘order’ or maybe ‘structure,’ and the—”

“Could you not?” Nora interrupted, shifting to block his view. “These records are meant to be private.”

“Sorry,” he replied, not sounding sorry at all. “Force of habit. Thief, remember?”

Despite herself, Nora’s lips quirked upward. Of course Cade would start deciphering Fateweaver code mid-crisis. The man couldn’t help himself when faced with a puzzle.

She finished recording Evie’s words: “When chaos sleeps within the flame…

And order breaks its chains…”

The phrase struck something in Nora’s memory, a teaching from her Fateweaver days about the fundamental duality of existence. Chaos and order, eternally balanced, eternally at war. The dichotomy was central to their understanding of fate itself.

Another tremor shook the staircase, sending dust filtering down from above. A reminder that their reprieve was temporary at best.

“We should keep moving,” Gavin urged, glancing nervously up the spiraling stairs. “This whole place feels like it could come down any moment.”

Nora gathered Evie back into her arms, her muscles protesting the renewed burden. Before she could stand, Cade stopped her with a gentle hand on her shoulder.

“I’ll take her,” he offered. “You’ve carried her long enough.”

Nora hesitated, her instinctive protectiveness warring with practical necessity. Finally, she nodded, allowing Cade to lift Evie’s slight form. The tenderness with which he handled the siren belied his usual brash demeanor.

“I think...” Nora began slowly, her mind still turning over Evie’s cryptic words. “I think she’s telling us how to fight back.”

“With what, philosophy?” Elena scoffed.

“No,” Nora replied, her hand moving to the small pouch at her belt—the one she’d kept sealed and warded since leaving the Fateweavers. With deliberate movements, she unfastened a hidden compartment and withdrew a small wooden cube, its surface inscribed with intricate patterns that seemed to shift under direct observation.

“What is that?” Gavin asked, leaning closer.

“The Pandemonium Box,” Nora answered, holding it gingerly. “It’s something I kind of… stole from the Lifekeeper vaults. Or it stole me?”

“I knew we were a bad influence on you, paladin! Welcome to the life of crime, Nor!” Elena teased.

Cade’s interest sharpened visibly, despite the exhaustion evident in his posture. “What does it do?”

“I’m not sure. I think it releases chaos in some form,” Nora explained, turning the cube in her palm. “I just have to solve this riddle here to use it. ‘Born in fire but fearing wind, I am as strong as a dragon’s breath yet as delicate as a fairy’s wing.’”

“And you’re just mentioning this now?” Elena demanded.

“It’s dangerous,” Nora replied simply. “I have no idea if there’s any way to control what comes out once it does. We could be collateral just as easily as Bernard and his freshly reforged crew.”

“But it could help us against Bernard? Against whatever ancient power is controlling him?” Cade pressed, shifting Evie’s weight in his arms to examine the cube more closely.

“Possibly,” Nora admitted. She wished she could give him more, but she wouldn’t be the reason for false hope.

The Dreadcaller looked up from the cube to find Cade watching her with a familiar gleam in his eye—the look he got when the impossible suddenly became merely improbable.

“Nora,” he said, a slow grin spreading across his grimy face, “have I ever told you how absolutely terrifying you are?”

“Not nearly often enough,” she replied with a small smile.

“So that’s the plan?” Rayka asked skeptically. “We use this chaos box to somehow rescue Chubbs from a being powerful enough to collapse an entire ancient structure with his mind?”

“It’s a start,” Cade replied, his energy returning with the prospect of action. “We need to find where Bernard took her first. If I had to guess, he’s performing some kind of ritual, and rituals need specific conditions.” He looked at Nora. “Any ideas where an ancient evil might set up shop for a goddess-killing ceremony?”

Nora considered the question, her Fateweaver training offering potential insights. “Ritual magic requires power sources. They need access to ley lines…” she saw Gav and Elena’s blank stares. “They’re convergence points of the various dominions,” she explained with a wave of her gauntleted hand. “They’re places where the veil between realms is thin. Given that we’re in the Blightmarshes...”

Her eyes went wide and Cade started to smile. It was a dark, mirthless visage.

“What are the odds the old Progenitors set up shop here because it was at one of these convergence points? Evil Bernard said something about finishing what he started,” Cade said with a shake of his head. He cursed.

Then, with a raised finger as if he just thought of something, he pulled out the old Lifekeeper ward book from his pack and started to sift through the ancient pages. Nora noted with a small jolt of appreciation just how many pages were bookmarked in the old tome. Maybe she wasn’t the only closet-scholar in their strange group.

“What? What is it?” Rayka demanded impatiently.

“I think,” Cade said under his breath, “that I have a way to make Evil Bernard have a really, really, bad day.”
