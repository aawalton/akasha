Silently, I climbed the steps of my destination, a strange calm having rolled over me. I was fully prepared, now, and whole once again. Core and Middle were with me in their well-worn spots within my mind, still, silent, and ready for what was to come.

That wasn’t totally true, I suppose. There was something all three partitions of my mind were currently feeling. An emotion that rose through our readiness.

A kind of anticipatory hunger.

We were very ready to test out our new Capstone Skill against the Savoy forces. It promised to be a very…pointed lesson for them.

I ignored the dark chuckles that thought spawned from Core and Middle, as I crested the battlement I was climbing…

And beheld the river.

Across the wide breadth of the Fiume d’Oro that bearded the city of Rhoscara, the river was now…full.

Very full.

For nearly as far as I could see, in both directions, the river was now occupied completely by the invading force of the combined Savoy/Velancia forces. General Raccant hadn’t been exaggerating when he’d reported that the invasion fleet was well over two hundred ships.

If anything, he might have been under-reporting. I didn’t blame him, though. It had probably been hard to spot all of the smaller ships trailing in the shadows of the bigger ones. There were a lot of those. Mentally, I ratcheted up our estimations of forty-two thousand by at least two thousand more. But like Ely had said…

What did it matter, how many there were? What was the difference between forty-two and forty-four thousand invaders?

We still had to resist them either way.

But I would admit.

Seeing that many ships, of all shapes and sizes, gathered in one place, at one time…

It was a bit intimidating. I’d grant Anguis that much.

When the four hours had finally passed, a large portion of the invading fleet had continued sailing onwards. Not because they were trying to continue on down the trunk of the Fiume d’Oro, and possibly reach the eastern coasts.

No, it was because the river bent slightly within the next few miles. It wasn’t much, but it was an ever-so-slight method of trapping the city. Plus, I had no doubts the invasion fleet was going to be dropping their payloads of soldiers off. It was something all of our planning sessions had pointed to. It just made good sense for them to try to encircle us on land. They would try to hit us not only from the north, from the banks of the river, but the east and the west and the south as well. It wasn't like the Principality forces had no way of surviving out in the corruption of the Skyfall, after all. They had those strange, crystalline Sorcerers to protect contingents of soldiers.

But we wouldn’t be making it easy for them. I had been there for the meetings, where plans were created on how the city would fight back against the enemy. Hell, if things got bad enough, I would even be out there dealing with them. The nature of the APD’s made members of the expedition very flexible when it came to battle directly outside the wards. I’d even look forward to it.

But for now…the battle had yet to begin. Irritatingly enough, the Dwarves had one of their little rituals to adhere to. It would have been perfectly sensible to simply begin the slaughter we all knew would happen on this day.

That’s not what was happening right now, though.

Instead, the leaders of both forces would be meeting in order to formally begin hostilities. Not even to try and negotiate an end to them, or try to avoid the battle. No, according to both ancient Dwarven tradition and current Velancian law, if you wanted to engage in open warfare with another faction of Dwarves, the occasion had to be noted down in separate documents for each side, signed by the opponents, and then returned. It apparently wasn’t an officially recognized battle if that didn’t happen.

The first time I’d heard about this little peculiarity of her people from Elysael and questioned the necessity of it, for once, the normally well-spoken royal had given me a flat, non-understanding look. She had then explained to me, slowly, as if I were a dim-witted child, that the battle wouldn’t count if it wasn’t signed and agreed upon.

Fucking Dwarves, man. I still didn’t understand them.

But as part of this little ‘initiation of hostilities’, I had been told in no uncertain terms that I needed to be present as one of the primary commanders of the defense. This extended to the rest of my leaders within the expedition…including Bleddyn.

I had barely seen hide nor hair of my old friend and fellow former slave since we had set foot within Rhoscara. He’d shown up to a couple of the planning meetings for the defense, enough for him to know what he and his Unchained were meant to do. But that was what he was really focused on.

The Unchained.

For God’s sake, I hadn’t even met anyone else in his organization since we’d been in the city! But, apparently, most of them were holed up here, somewhere within it. They were even supposed to be contributing to the defense in some way.

How, I didn’t know. That was apparently something kept secret between the Unchained and the Rangers.

But Bleddyn had been making himself scarce ever since we’d arrived. It was…unlike the man.

He looked good, though, standing up on the same battlements I had climbed with his arms crossed over his chest and a massive frown on his face. Firmer, somehow, as if a weight had been lifted off his shoulders. He even gave me a slight smile as I came to stand with him, followed by the rest of the expedition’s leadership. Somehow it felt right to have Bleddyn on my left and Sylvia on my right. My lover did her best to smile at me as I arrived, but it faltered almost immediately. Not because she was unsettled, no. Sylvia was made of sterner stuff than that.

She could sense something was up with me.

I just gave her a small smile as the Sculpted woman eyed me with a mix of suspicion and slight concern.

As my Captains spread out across the battlements to their own ends, I nudged Bleddyn in the ribs to get his attention. As they’d been instructed, my fellow leaders among the expedition were mingling with all of the other Dwarven leaders of the Rhoscaran defense. We didn’t have real-time communication, outside of messengers, so it was best if they stayed abreast of the situation from speaking to our nominal peers. There was at least a chance that anything we learned from them could help us in the battle. “So,” I asked quietly, nodding down below us. “How long has that been going on?”

Bleddyn scoffed, his arms tensing as his gaze remained focused below us. “Not long,” He muttered to me. “They’re just gettin' started.”

Below us on the outer docks of Rhoscara, floating over the water but still within the reach of the wards, was the apparent meeting of the Dwarven leaders. This, then, had to be where they were to sign what I understood to be called ‘Declarations of Bloodshed’. A wooden table had been set up, long and large enough to fit the two unrolled scrolls lying upon it. I was on a wall that hugged the outside of the exterior docks to the right, allowing me to see the scene in its entirety, so even from this distance, I could tell that the scrolls were dense with Dwarven legalese.

On one end of the long table, the one closest to the city, I could see Elysael. She looked to be slowly reading the scroll that had presumably been prepared by the Savoy for Rhoscara to peruse. The Prince of Ravens wasn’t alone, of course. She was surrounded on all sides by members of the Corvid knights. The red-plated, avian-themed Knightly Dwarves were a literal wall of steel, guarding their Prince with their very bodies. The only part of her that wasn’t protected by them was the front, from where she stood before the table. One of the Corvids even had a shield held protectively over her head, shading her.

But even with the covering, I could make out Ely’s facial expression, as she read the Declaration.

She didn’t look impressed. If anything, Elysael looked more repulsed than I had ever seen her. A visible grimace of distaste was visible on her royal features, and her eyes were narrowed, fixed upon the parchment with unerring displeasure.

But on the other side of the table…

I was initially confused when I set my sights on the Savoy delegation.

Confused and enraged.

This. Wasn’t. Possible.

He was dead. I know he was. I had burned his death into the very foundations of my mind. I often dreamed of the moment I had killed him. I relived that moment over and over and over in those dreams, repeating the moment I crashed into his feculent body, sending us crashing through the window and into the open air. It brought me a form of comfort I knew was twisted, but nonetheless still consoled me as if it were a childhood blanket, warm and safe.

The gleam of the forest fires set in the distance, growing towards the town.

The rush of the wind as we plunged toward the ground.

And the very moment my crudely forged dagger had slipped into his heart.

He was dead.

So why the fuck was Magnus of Savoy standing on the other side of the table?!

I didn’t realize how heavily I was breathing, or how hard I was gripping the stone crenelations of the battlement, before I heard a sharp splintering noise echo out over the waters. Slowly, barely aware of what I was doing, I looked down to see that I’d broken off a large hunk of stone from the wall with my bare hands. In my rage, my sheer, mind-numbing, all-consuming fury, I hadn’t been paying attention to my own strength.

When I looked up, it was to see that my inadvertent noise had drawn the attention of everyone. Every person on both the section of wall I stood upon, the other walls surrounding the docks, and the actual people down upon them…were now looking up at me.

Including him.

A small, knowing smile of recognition formed upon those oh-so-painfully familiar, thin lips when equally familiar golden eyes lit upon me. I hated the spark of delight I could see in his gaze as he stared up at me.

Almost involuntarily, I reached for my new Skill.

I could do it. I was sure I could. At this distance, my new Capstone Skill could reach the delegation. It was powerful, too, I had no doubt about that. The bare few moments of testing I’d been allowed before leaving for this meeting had shown me that. The potential of the Skill was strong.

Strong enough that I might just be able to kill him where he stood.

The only thing that stopped me…was a wide, strong palm clamping down on my left shoulder.

Hard. Hard enough to grind my bones together, in fact.

I turned wide, murderous, nearly unseeing eyes upon the offender…on to find Bleddyn looking back at me. The depths of understanding I could see in his stormy blue eyes startled me out of my rage, if only for a moment.

That was all he needed.

“It’s not him,” Bleddyn said heavily. At my slow blink of non-understanding, a mirthless smile crossed his lips. “I reacted nearly the same way…but it’s not him. Look closer.”

Breathing heavily, I turned my gaze back down to the docks and did as my friend asked.

Slowly, the details of the person standing across from Elysael filtered their way through the haze of my fury, and I realized…

Bleddyn was right. That wasn’t Magnus.

They were too old, for one. Magnus had been a young Dwarf at the end of the day. I had never asked his exact age, and I didn’t care, but I’m sure he had been younger than I had been at the time. This Dwarf looked like he was in his fifties or so, at least physically. Not that physical appearance meant much for real, highly Leveled Classers. He could have been fifty or five-hundred, and I wouldn't have been able to tell. There were slight wrinkles on his slightly more tanned skin, in contrast to Magnus’s pale, untouched noble shade. His hair was cut shorter, as well. Not by much, but enough to see that maybe Magnus had been trying to imitate someone else’s style. Someone with the same shade of ebony black locks, but shot through with a few streaks of grey.

And the eyes…they might be the same shade of nearly pure, pale gold, but there was more animation to this person. Magnus had always looked dead to the world when you looked in his eyes. Like there was nothing more inside of him than an obsession with pain and his own hedonistic delights.

But this Dwarf…he was interested.

In me specifically, it seemed. Even now, he was still looking up at me instead of the Declaration held in his hands, crafted by Rhoscara.

I let out a shuddering breath as I realized who this was, who this had to be. The resemblance left only one person.

This…must be Anguis Savoy himself.

The Serpent of Velancia.

Magnus’s Father.
