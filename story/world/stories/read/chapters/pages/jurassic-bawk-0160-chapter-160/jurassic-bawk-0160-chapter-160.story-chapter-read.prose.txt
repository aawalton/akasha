The signs led Charlemagne through winding corridors stuffed with all manner of creatures.  A procession of grey-scaled, six-legged beings passed him going the other direction, each about twice the rooster’s size.  The most interesting thing about them was that they had no heads.  A ring of constantly waving feelers grew from the front of the torso.  One paused as Charlemagne approached, its scales shifting color to bright red, then it hurried after the others.  Not long thereafter, the temperature within the hallway plummeted as something that appeared to be a giant ball of snow rolled by.  Out of reflex, Charlemagne’s mana flared up to protect him from the biting cold.

“Oi, watch it!” came a high-pitched voice from out of the ball.  “I’ve turned down my aura as much as I can, no need to blast me with heat!”

Before the rooster could react, however, the other being was long gone, taking its aura with it.  The creatures around Charlemagne, however, suddenly gave the rooster a wide berth.  He ignored them as he continued to follow the signs left by the Squiggles.

After a few minutes of walking, Charlemagne was led into a large, open room.  The grey walls, floor, and ceiling all seemed to be made of a squishy substance that gave easily, even under the rooster’s relatively slight weight.  A heavyset, two-legged creature with black fur and long, multi-segmented arms stood in the middle of the floor with an impatient look on its face.

“You’re late,” the creature announced in a voice that seemed to combine low bass tones and piercingly high frequencies into a strangely beautiful harmony that was decidedly masculine.

“Bawk,” the rooster answered plainly.

“Yes, I imagine it was rather crowded in the hallways.  I guess it couldn’t be helped.  Well, anyway, I am Assessment Officer Blandin Voan.  I am legally required to tell you that I have been forbidden from killing you during this Combat Assessment.  I see that you have arrived with an Item Set that includes a customizable appearance, so let’s get you properly decked out before we begin.”

Charlemagne processed what the Assessment Officer had said and was about to ask for clarification as to the final point when his Fop’s Raiment began to transform.  His vest changed in both color and size, appearing more like one of the uniforms that the humans who had originally hunted him down had worn.  His top hat had changed as well; its shining black color had given way to a deep maroon.  The hat also appeared to have lost some of its structural integrity, as it flopped over on itself and protruded far enough out that he could see the edge in his peripheral vision.

“There you go, I was worried that you were too good to get your claws dirty, looking like that.  But you look like a proper System soldier now,” Assessment Officer Voan continued with a curt nod.  “This assessment will help us find out if you bleed like one.”

“Bawk?” the rooster asked.

“Well now, aren’t you a cocky one?  No, it wouldn’t do just to jump right into it, although the System knows that’s exactly what I like best.  But I can’t afford any more incid…deviations from my orders.  So first things first, I’ll need to take a peek at your Status.  Don’t worry, it doesn’t show me everything…just what I need to know to properly assess you.”

One of Voan’s overly long arms whipped around and tapped the creature’s temple.  A white metal device appeared, extending out from the side of Voan’s face.  A transparent green screen extruded from the device and snapped into place in front of the Officer’s right eye.

“All right, let’s just get this calibrated,” the Officer muttered as he tapped at the side of their head.  “And…there.  I hate these things, but they’re better than relying on the Identify line of Skills.  Now, hold still and don’t fight the effect.  This might feel a bit…strange.  Don’t move a muscle.”

Charlemagne felt as if thousands of people were staring directly at him.  However, far from feeling uncomfortable from the attention, the young rooster felt only pride in how far he had progressed in such a short amount of time.  The results of his hard work were surely self-evident.

“What…what is going on here?  Like, this makes no sense,” Assessment Officer Voan said after a moment.  “No, seriously.  Is this thing broken?  Hold on…”

The black-furred creature pulled off the white and green device, smacked it a few times, and then put it back on their face.

“Nope…either this thing’s busted or you are,” the creature announced.

“Bawk?” Charlemagne asked.

“Well, for starters, your Attributes appear to be way higher than the baseline should be for your body type and level.  Your level is in the low nineties, right?”

“Bawk,” the rooster confirmed.

“And you’re not from a high-gravity world?”

“Bawk?”

“High gravity means that everything weighs more than it should.  Like here on the System’s Favor, gravity is about 26 meters per second squared.  Is that higher or lower than you’re used to?”

“Bawk…”

Assessment Officer Voan sighed.

“All right, let’s try it this way.  Do you feel heavier here than you did on your planet?”

“Bawk, bawk bawk!”

“How was I supposed to know you gained most of your levels in the Systemic Games?  That’s not really anyone’s fault, right?  Hmmm…I see you have wings.  How easily could you fly without using mana or a Skill on your planet when you were level 1?”

“Bawk,” the rooster responded, not wanting to admit the whole truth.

“All right so you could sort of fly.  That means your world didn’t have super strong gravity.  So yeah, you shouldn’t have anywhere near the number of Attributes that I’m showing here.  Unless…do you have a lot of Achievements?”

“Bawk bawk!” Charlemagne answered.

“That’s…wow.  Okay, I guess it’s possible that this thing is working right.  Now, let’s take a look at your combat Skills.  Wait, what is this?  An Advanced Core Skill, high-level too, a Physique Skill, an Advanced Martial Arts Skill, an enrage-type Skill, something to resist pain, and a couple defensive Skills.  Oh, and some sort of acceleration Skill.  Is that correct?”

Charlemagne nodded proudly.

“This is by far the weirdest collection of Skills I have ever seen.  How do you have so many Advanced Skills at a high level, but the gaps in your basics are so big you could drive a starship through them!  Where’s your vision enhancement Skill?  Reflexes?  Danger-sense?  Memory enhancement?  And why don’t you have a self-healing Skill?  How do you keep yourself from bleeding out during combat, and how am I going to beat you half to death if you can’t recover from it in a reasonable time frame?  What about attacking at range?  What about resistance to various forms of incapacitation?  As it stands, if we sent you out into battle now, you’d get your brain melted six ways to Sunday!”

“Bawk!” the rooster rebutted.

“You use mana for ALL that?  That’s not even possible!”

“Bawk,” Charlemagne insisted.

Voan made a complicated sound that the Squiggles didn’t bother translating.

“Prove it,” he finally said with a harsh laugh.

Charlemagne looked down in pride as Assessment Officer Voan slowly pushed himself up off the floor, his weirdly-jointed arms snapping back into place with nauseating cracks as the joints mended themselves.

“I call shenanigans.  There is simply no way that you could be that fast without having a dedicated speed build.  But you hit like a truck, too.  And you can create mana shields faster than most mages I know.  And your Martial Arts Skill allows you to fight in three dimensions without getting disoriented.  Do you have a mana-based attack too?  I couldn’t tell if you had one fused into your Ignition Core Skill, but I bet you do, since the Skill is at level 25.  Which is equally ridiculous, by the way!”

“Bawk,” the rooster confirmed.

“Well, don’t show it off in here: this place was built to be durable, but not to that extent,” the Assessment Officer looked at his fur as he said this, leading Charlemagne to believe that it was not the room he was worried about.

“Anyways, I think we’re going to have to schedule you for an advanced assessment.  Don’t worry; you’ll get a few days to settle in before that happens.  It’s just that we are lacking the proper facilities to appropriately test your limits.  I don’t think that I’ve seen anyone below level 100 that was too powerful for this chamber, so you should feel proud of yourself.”

The rooster puffed himself up at the praise.

“That being said, there’s a lot that you could work on in the next few days.  Why don’t you purchase some of those Skills that I mentioned from the System Shop?  None of them are expensive, at least the common versions aren’t, and you probably received a bunch of Shop Points as an enlistment bonus, right?”

Charlemagne asked the Squiggles to tell him how many Shop Points he had.

“Bawk,” he informed Officer Voan.

“You have over 100,000 Shop Points?  Congratulations, you’re now my best friend!”

“Bawk!”

“Hey, that is no way to talk to an Officer!  But, I suppose I had that coming,” Voan said as he stretched his body and checked it for any lingering damage.  “But seriously, you might as well spend some of your points now.  There are plenty of things you could get that would make you even stronger than you are now.  Body-tempering treasures.  Fragments of Concepts that can strengthen your Ignition Core Skill and help it evolve down the road.  Skill Books.  Elixirs to raise your Attributes.  All sorts of things.  Remember, you can’t spend your Shop Points when you’re dead!”

Charlemagne was about to start shopping right then and there when a thought stopped him.

“Bawk?” he asked.

“Yeah, there’s lots of stuff for creating safe places for noncombatants in the Store, I think.  It’s not like we see a lot of civilians out here on the System’s Favor, so I’m not sure what exactly is in there.  But you can find almost anything in the Store, so long as you’re willing to pay.  But hey, you may get a good deal out here since the demand for that type of stuff is pretty low.”

“Bawk,” the rooster inquired.

“You know, supply and demand?  Buy low, sell high?  Basic economics?  Did you not have currency in your world?”

The rooster shook his head.

“The wonders never cease,” Voan muttered.  “Well, since we’ve still got plenty of time, let me explain how currency works.”

“Bawk!” Charlemagne protested.

“Yes, but do you understand the difference between elastic and inelastic demand?  Marginal utility?  Opportunity cost?  Comparative versus absolute advantage?  Did you even know that you can create things and sell them for profit?”

The rooster said nothing.

“Yeah, I thought not.  I’ll have you know that my first career was as a professor.  I tutored the rising stars of Klastinon nobility, or at least those rich enough to afford my prices.  But then the anti-System forces invaded my reality…they killed off all the noble scions, along with the entire ruling caste of my people, and then just disappeared.  The devastation that followed was horrifying.  Decades of civil wars, famines, plagues, monster attacks, Dungsplosions…”

“Bawk?” the rooster interrupted.

“You know, when a Dungeon explodes from having too much mana packed into it.  It’s a well-known phenomenon…but where was I?  Ah yes, it was horrible.  We lost entire generations: the best youth that our intensive breeding programs could produce.  All because those reckless fools thought that we would be better off without the System!  There needs to be something that maintains order, doesn’t there?  You can’t just lop the head off of the body and expect nothing bad to happen!”

Charlemagne, having never heard of a particular headless chicken, nodded in agreement.

The black-furred officer took a deep breath to calm himself before continuing.

“Well, anyway, I was a tutor in mathematics primarily, although I also taught economics, history, and a few other subjects as needed.  If you’d like, I can give you an introductory lesson on System economics right now.  We’ve got the time, and you’ve got the Shop Points.  What do you say?  One hundred Points for a lesson?”
