It shouldn’t have been a big deal. It wasn't. It was just a sand castle. I didn’t care about that. But this wasn’t about that.

I would have to live with this kid for the foreseeable future. If I let him walk over me now, I’d have trouble every time I went out. So I used the only language bullies knew— force.

I pushed him.

He barely took a step back, and then grinned at me. He pushed me, and I flew on my ass. This kid had monster strength. He clearly had the System unlocked and it had given him plenty of physical Attributes. All the tools I was working with still needed a lot of work.

He took a step forward to better loom over me. “My sand.”

I would not let him win. So I decided to act. Decisively, savagely. Screw fair.

“Here’s your sand!”

I grabbed a fistful of sand, and when I got up, I threw it at his eyes. He gasped and I pushed him down. I sat on top of him and started punching him. I couldn’t hit him hard, hell, I wasn’t even two. But I was fierce, and he hadn’t expected it. I kept hammering him, because if I did this harshly enough, I only needed to do it once.

Predictably, the adults had other ideas.

Someone grabbed me by the neck of my shirt and pulled me away. Then they immediately picked up the ogre of a child and cradled them and looked down at me coldly.

“So that is how Jonaka grasps onto the number two spot. Cheap tricks.”

“Verana,” my mother said with a flat tone as she appeared behind me. “What a lovely day. Take back what you said now.”

“Excuse me?”

“Jonaka is not number two,” mother said fiercely as she stepped beside me. “Nobody catches as many fish as he does.”

“And nobody catches bigger fish than Marek,” the woman said.

The mothers around the scene whispered with each other. It seemed like it wasn’t quite clear who was number one.

Oh… So that’s how it is. No wonder father had a rivalry.

The big boy was glaring at me with a murderous gaze. I decided to stare him down flatly as our mothers snapped at each other. He didn’t like that.

“Your boy is frail. Must take after his parents,” Verana said.

My mother growled. I hadn’t heard that before. “He kicked your boy’s ass!”

The other mothers laughed, but the wife of Marek snarled. The ogre-kid was opening and closing his fists as he cried openly, but still managed to glare at me with pure wrath. What a psycho.

“He cheated. Sand in the eyes. I demand they wrestle again.”

“My boy isn’t even two!” mother said. “Are you an idiot?”

“I demand a wrestle!”

“This was no wrestle,” mother said. “Your boy came to bully mine. And it was his ass on the ground, not my boy’s.”

“It doesn’t count,” Verana hissed between her teeth.

“It counts where it matters,” mother said. “He was bigger and stronger and came to pick on someone weaker, and got what he needed. Some humility.”

“Hmph,” Verana said. “Vorok will learn nothing but pride in the Moon Festival when he wins the generation’s fishing competition. Surely your boy will participate now that he has joined them in play?”

Mother scoffed. Apparently that was the tradition. I had heard of the Moon Festival. Father had fussed about it. It happened every five years. The boys would show off their Skills and compare with boys from the other villages. Nobody expected me to participate or perform because I was the runt of the litter.

Until now. Verana had made my participation a public affair. Ugly trick, if you asked me.

“Of course he will participate,” mother said loudly.

“Good,” Verana said. “Then we will see in nine months that my boy is better.”

I put on my most innocent face. “Mama. If he is better, why is he the one crying?”

I absolutely loved the look of my mother trying not to burst out laughing. Some of the mothers in the audience couldn’t contain it.

Verana spat on the ground and turned to storm off the beach. “Come Vorok. We will work on your Wrestling Skill when father comes home.”

My mother shook her head. “Already awakened his system.”

Then she squeezed my hand. “You’re not the only prodigy of your generation it seems.”

I looked at Verana walking her son back to their hut. I did not like bullies. I had just been minding my business and he just had to come pick on me. That boy was a brute. But clearly stupid strong. And if he had already awoken his System, it meant he was going to become even stronger and more dangerous.

I had won with quick wits and decisiveness. I might not be able to reproduce my results. This kid was crazy strong, and in the Moon Festival we would be competing on his terms. Swimming, wrestling, spearhandling— fishing. I would need to learn to use my magic. I now had the basics. They were not enough against Vorok, for he was strong and dangerous. But it was time to become stronger and more dangerous myself. I grabbed my mother’s hand and squeezed it.

“I will beat him,” I said.

“Good,” my mother said.

“That’s my boy!” Father bellowed and drank down a swig of beer. “Everyone saw that. They saw that my son is not a pushover. I’m so proud of you!”

“Thank you, papa,” I said and preened. I was rather proud of myself. But this had now created a dark cloud in my otherwise idyllic life.

“He will come again, papa?” I asked. “To fight me?”

His expression sobered up and he looked at me. Then he nodded. “His father is the same, son. Marek and I have been rivals ever since childhood. The only reason he doesn’t bully me like that is because he can’t.”

“So I have to be so strong Vorok can’t bully me?” I asked.

“Yes son,” father said. “That is how the world works.”

Mother had cooked a special dinner tonight in honor of my victory. A sweet tuber stew with snake eggs and nuts. She gave the bowl to me, with an adult spoon.

“Big man like you, I figured you can eat like one,” mother said with a smile.

“Yes!” I said and grinned.

“Eat,” father said. “You’ll grow strong.”

Physical size wouldn’t hurt, I mused as I chewed. But I doubted I’d get as large as Vorok. He was freakishly large and older than me to boot.

“Now that you have your System unlocked,” father said and put down his mug. “I think we should talk about what Skills to take. We should design a build for you. I think you should prioritize Wrestling.”

“Considering the circumstances, I agree,” mother said.

Crap. Yeah of course this would happen. Now I needed to make a choice. Sure, I had one Skill Slot open. I could take up Wrestling. That would give me immediate tools against that ogre of a boy. But I didn’t want Wrestling. I wanted to use mana and learn magic.

And I didn’t want to lie to my parents. I still remembered who I had been. I remembered those wasted years. Watching reruns of shows I barely laughed at anymore. Being lazy instead of taking care of myself. I remembered my resolve. I wanted to master magic and I wouldn’t break that promise to myself. Breaking promises to myself had been a big part of why I had failed at my last life.

I also didn’t want to keep secrets from my parents. I loved them. They deserved as much of the truth as I could give them.

“Mama, papa,” I said slowly. “What is “World First Achievement?”

Mother’s face went slack and father’s mug clattered on the floor. They were silent for a while.

“World First Achievement…” father muttered. “My boy… It really is like the fables.”

“I… maybe I should call for Elder Piraka,” mother said.

“No,” father said firmly. “We are not doing anything hasty. Sit down.”

“How did you get it, little gem?” mother asked in half a whisper.

“Did I do something bad?” I asked.

“What? No. No you splendid boy,” father said. “What you have done is… rare. But good rare.”

“Okay,” I said and nodded. “So I’m not in trouble?”

“No,” father said. “Be proud. Right, Ramona?”

Mother was usually the one leading these conversations. Now she was slumped over the table, just looking at it with a blank expression. “World’s First Achievement…” she muttered.

Father smiled and grabbed her gently by the neck and started massaging her. “She will snap out of it.”

“How?” mother asked me.

I thought for a moment before answering. “Between napping when I was smaller, I liked to fall into my mind.”

“Huh?” father said. “What does that mean?”

“Just… thinking deeply. Not even thinking, papa. Just existing with an active mind. I liked this feeling. Breathing and feeling happy. It made my mind sharper.”

“Sure did, my boy.”

“So I got my first Skill. Meditation.”

“Meditation…” father mused. “I have heard of this skill. I heard the main continent has Warrior Monks with such a skill. They live in caves and sit and think but can fight like wild hogs.”

Mother gave him a weak smile. “Look who has the fables now.”

“So what does it do?” father asked. “What Attributes does it give?”

It gives me Perception and Focus.”

“Oh,” mother said.

“Oh,” father said.

“That explains so much.”

“Perception is a good Attribute,” mother said. “It can be used for many things.”

“Like magic!” I suggested.

“Let’s put a hold on that,” father said. “What happened when you learned Meditation?”

“Then the System said I was the first person to unlock it so young and it gave me the Achievement.”

“A World’s First…” mother muttered. Then she smiled and reached to kiss me over the head. “Sorry about that, little gem. I am very proud of you. What kind of reward did you get?”

I recited the system description.

I told them about Mana Sight. I told them about the mana particles and learning to control them. I told them that’s why I wanted to learn from Piraka.

The silence that stretched over the dining table had some real weight to it. Finally my father broke it. He got up and poured himself beer.

Mother was back to her old tunes, after having recovered from shock. She was actually taking it really well in her stride. She smiled at me. “I knew it.”

“That there was something about you. I’ve known it for a while. I knew you were special, but to think…” she shook her head. “A World’s First…”

Father shook his head. “He would be trampled even by the weaker kids. Vorok would eat him for breakfast.”

“Papa,” I said firmly. “I won’t give up magic.”

My father gave me a weighty look. There were a lot of things in that gaze. Disappointment, trepidation, confusion. But also pride. Pride of a father pleased with his son. That made me happy. His gaze softened and gave way to that pride.
