My heart thumped as I looked up at Duncan, far too aware that our arms were still touching. I hadn’t moved away after nudging him with my shoulder, and I could feel the heat of his body on my bare skin. It was strangely distracting, and I needed to focus so I could answer his question.

He’d asked me when I was free next, and I wasn’t sure what to say. My head felt like it was swimming, and the relief that welled up in my heart wasn’t helping me think either. I’d been so worried before I’d gotten to his house about how awkward things might get between us. I was worried that after the date, we’d both be so uncomfortable with what happened that we couldn’t even look at each other, and we’d never talk again.

But that wasn’t happening. Duncan wanted to see me again. He sounded hopeful, like he wanted me to say some time soon, and I was tempted to, but a soft voice in the back of my head warned me to answer carefully. I couldn’t seem too eager because that would be bad.

Happy busy.

That was the advice I’d been given by countless influencers online. I could barely recall actually paying attention to any of them, but their voices echoed in my head as I tried to come up with a response to Duncan.

When flirting with a guy, if he asks when you’re free, they said to try to seem happy and busy. They said the guy would be most intrigued if I made it seem like I had a lot going on, a bustling social life, but that I loved it, and that would make the guy want to be part of it too. They’d be thrilled that I was willing to make time with them. Apparently, knowing that others wanted me, that they wanted my attention but I was giving it to the guy, would make them want me more.

I wasn’t sure how much I believed that. Even when I was watching it, I remember being kind of unsure. It seemed fake, and that was my problem. It was lying. Even though I didn’t want to tell Duncan the full truth, I didn’t want him to know that I was a loser without many friends, I also didn’t want to lie to him that much.

“I can probably do whenever, really,” I said. “I don’t think I’ve got too much planned for the rest of the holiday.”

“No?” Duncan replied quickly.

To my relief, he didn’t appear disgusted by my lack of social activities, like the influencers had implied. If anything, he seemed kind of relieved, and that was strangely reassuring to me. It seemed almost like he’d been worried about what I was going to say again, and I couldn’t help but wonder whether he’d been concerned about what would happen after our date too. Maybe he’d been just as scared that I wouldn’t want to see him again too.

That seemed unlikely, but it made me feel a bit better, and more words spilled out of my mouth before I could stop them.

“I don’t think so. I mean, Phoebe is back next week, so we’re going to do something then, and I think my mom’s going to take me shopping for a new uniform and some school stuff at some point soon, but that’s pretty much it…” I explained.

A hint of anxiety washed over me as I trailed off. Despite his unchanged expression, I was still briefly worried that I’d said too much and made myself sound boring. Maybe I should have pretended I had something more exciting coming up too.

I did kind of have some plans, after all. There were a few things I wanted to do before we went back to school. However, I couldn’t exactly say I was planning on finishing the rest of the books Dad had gotten me, as well as spending a fair bit of time at the Academy, before our teachers drained all of the energy out of me once more and made it harder for me to be able to go back.

“Okay, awesome!” Duncan said, his tone almost relieved. “How about— Oh, wait…”

Duncan’s eyebrows furrowed as he fell silent. It appeared as though he was deep in thought, but I had no clue what he was thinking about. I was torn between not wanting to interrupt his thinking and being painfully curious about what was on his mind.

I knew it would have been better not to speak; people didn’t like to be interrupted. Mom had told me that many times, but I couldn’t help it. I’d opened my mouth before I even realised it.

“What?”

“I just remembered that my parents are coming back soon, and we’ll be going out for dinner that day,” he told me. “I think they said either sometime this week or next week, but I can’t remember which.”

“Ah,” I replied, relieved that was all it was.

“Yeah,” Duncan said as he reached for his phone and began scrolling through his texts. “Damn. I don’t think it’s here. Dad must have told me when he called the other day.”

He looked up at me with an apologetic expression, and I hurried to reassure him.

“Oh, that’s…” I started to say, but I wasn’t sure how to finish the sentence.

I wanted to tell him it was fine, but, despite the way he was grimacing at me, he hadn’t really apologised or anything, so it felt weird to do that.

“Can I text you tomorrow once I’ve spoken to them again and found out when they’ll be back?” he asked quickly. “Then we can plan the tournament?”

“Yeah, sure. That’s fine.”

Duncan smiled at me, and I returned it before a fanfare blared from the speakers, capturing my attention. I was a little relieved to have an excuse to look away from Duncan. Although the alcohol was definitely making me less nervous than I’d usually be in that situation, I was still kind of anxious, and that made me want to run away or change the subject. I would have leapt at any excuse to make things less awkward.

“Oh damn, look at that,” Duncan said, leaning forward slightly.

His eyes were fixed on the graph that appeared on the screen, showing our progress up until that point in the game. His character had taken an early lead. He’d gotten the first star, and I’d caught up, as had the other NPCs, but he’d pulled ahead again.

A heavy sigh slipped out of my mouth, and I shook my head, my expression turning mournful as I stared at the picture of his character on the map. It was just above mine. Only one star separated us, but I could have pulled even if I’d just gotten a better final roll.

Duncan glanced back at me, snickering softly.

“Oh, shut up,” I told him playfully. “I could still win.”

“We’ll see about that,” he shot back as he pushed a button on his controller to move the game on.

An announcement appeared on the screen, informing us that it was time for the bonus stars. My chest tightened in excitement and anticipation, and I couldn’t help but lean forward as well, unable to look away from the television.

There would be three bonus stars. That’s how many were always handed out at the end of the games, and I just needed one to pull even and two to win. If Duncan and I both had the same number of stars at the end, it would come down to who had the most coins, though. I couldn’t see how many we both had, but I was pretty sure Duncan would come out on top. He’d won the last mini game, so that would help, but there was a chance I could win, and that was better.

“First up, we have the slowpoke star, for the character who rolled the lowest dice total throughout the game,” a character in the game announced.

Another snicker came from Duncan, and I narrowed my eyes, glaring at the little mushroom-hatted character. That was just rude. It felt like even the game was mocking me for how horribly my last roll had gone, and I wasn’t sure I even wanted that star.

No. I still did. I wanted to win the game, but I wouldn’t have minded too much if someone else were given the slowpoke bonus instead of me. It would have been hilarious if Duncan had gotten it, but he did not.

I watched as the spotlight roamed back and forth on the screen, shining on each of our characters for just a moment before coming to a stop on mine.

“Yeah, that sounds about right,” Duncan chortled.

My face became a mask of mock outrage as I turned towards him.

“What’s that meant to mean?” I demanded.

Duncan held up one of his hands in surrender, the other still holding his controller.

“Nothing, nothing,” he replied, his tone overly pacifying. “I’m just saying, you didn’t exactly go far this run.”

I narrowed my eyes at him and pressed my lips together, trying to hide the smile that threatened to break free, but it felt impossible. I had to look away, turning back to face the screen just to stop myself from laughing.

“Well, if it gets me the win, I’ll take it,” I decided.

Duncan laughed again.

“We’ll see, we’ll see,” he said. “I could still take this. Come on, mini game star!”

That time, I couldn’t stop a smile from appearing on my lips, and a giggle escaped as words flashed on the screen before us.

“Next, we have the shopping star, for the player who bought the most items!”

Tentative hope began to flicker in my heart. Maybe there was still a chance for me. I didn’t pay much attention to the shops during that game, but I’d definitely bought a few items, and I couldn’t remember if Duncan had stopped at any. He must have done. He’d used an item to get to the star at one point, but…

My thoughts came to a halt as the spotlight stopped on his character, and my mouth dropped open.

“What?” I said as Duncan threw his hands up in celebration.

“Yes!”

“How? That is so unfair!”

Duncan grinned at me.

“Nope! I knew all those dice blocks were worth it,” he cried. “And you said they were a waste!”

I had, but that wasn’t even in the last game. Duncan always stockpiled dice blocks, but he rarely used them, and I’d told him it wasn’t worth his money. He’d missed out on getting a star in one of the earlier games because he’d blown all his coins on them, and that was the only reason I’d won. I’d teased him for that, but he’d stubbornly claimed it was a worthwhile risk. I guess he was right.

“They are!” I argued before falling silent as the final bonus star appeared on the screen.

“And finally, we have the eventful star. This is for the player who landed on the most event spaces throughout the game. Which one will it be?” the announcer said as the spotlight began to circle the on-screen stage once more.

“Event spaces?” Duncan repeated, glancing at me. “Is that just like… the question mark spaces, or do the red spots count too? I feel like they’re kind of event-y, right?”

“I don’t know. I feel like those are just regular spaces, no?” I asked. “Like, they wouldn’t count green spots either, would they?”

“I guess not…”

Duncan was definitely going to win the game. I knew that even before the spotlight came to a stop. I’d only stepped on one spot that might have counted as an event space in the entire game, and that was a gold one. As far as I could remember, Duncan hadn’t gotten any, but that didn’t matter. He’d gotten one of the bonus stars, and the two NPCs were too far behind to catch up even if they both got the bonus star.

“Birdo!”

“Urgh!” Duncan and I exclaimed at the same time before laughing as we glanced at each other.

“Of course, Birdo got it,” Duncan said, shaking his head in feigned disgust.

“I know.”

We watched in silence as the bright pink bird celebrated on the screen before Duncan turned to me, his expression morphing into one of delight.

“Wait a minute,” he said.

“What?” I asked slowly.

I knew what he was going to say, but it was more fun to pretend I didn’t.

“You know what this means, right?”

Groaning loudly, I threw myself back on the sofa again and covered my face with my hands, as if I couldn’t bear to watch Duncan be crowned victorious.

“Yes,” I grumbled.

My body felt on high alert as I sat there with my eyes covered. I was half-expecting Duncan to put his arm around me again. I hoped that he would.

“I win!” Duncan cried.

I groaned dramatically again as Duncan cheered, but I couldn’t keep up the facade of being upset at the results. It made me feel guilty for not congratulating him, and I didn’t want him to feel bad for beating me. After all, he deserved it. He’d played better than me.

Heaving another slightly less heavy sigh, I sat up again and watched as Duncan raised his hands triumphantly, savouring the applause coming from the speakers. There was something kind of cute about his celebration. It made me wish I could reach out and…

My thoughts came to a halt. I knew what I wanted to do, but I couldn’t admit it to myself. Even just thinking that I wanted to grab his shirt and pull him towards me, to press my lips to his, felt too scary, but that urge simmered in the pit of my stomach.

“Well done,” I told Duncan with only a hint of reluctance in my voice. “You beat me. Good game, I guess.”

He laughed, causing happiness to burn in my chest, and I couldn’t help but smile.

“Thank you, thank you. Good game too,” he repeated. “Man, look how good I look up there.”

Duncan beamed proudly at the screen as his character took to the podium, standing in the centre. One of the NPCs stepped up next to them, having snuck into second position at some point in the game. At least I was still on the podium, though. I was third, but that was better than not being up there at all.

“Yeah, you do look pretty good,” I admitted with a sigh.

Out of the corner of my eye, I saw Duncan turn towards me, a cheeky smile on his lips.

“You think so?” he asked, raising one of his arms and flexing. “Why, thank you. I have been working out more.”

Immediately, my eyes went to his arm. My thoughts abandoned me, and I couldn’t help but watch as he showed off his muscles. After a few seconds, my brain seemed to catch up with what was happening, and I tore my eyes away from his bicep as my cheeks began to heat up.

“I meant your character!” I explained quickly, gesturing towards the screen. “Mario looks good!”

My face was burning, and I really hoped that Duncan wouldn’t notice, but I could feel his eyes on me even as I stared at the television. I longed to glance at him again, to check whether he was still looking at me, but I couldn’t bring myself to meet his gaze.

“Huh,” Duncan said softly. “I didn’t know that was your type.”

That made me look at him.

“It’s not!” I cried in horror.

But Duncan didn’t look away from the television. Instead, he just cocked his head to the side as he continued to examine the character for a few more seconds.

“I mean, I do kind of get it?” he said thoughtfully, but I could hear a teasing note to his voice. “I’m sure loads of people are into him, but damn. That is disappointing.”

I wanted the floor to open up and swallow me whole. Even though I knew Duncan was just messing, I was embarrassed. Even just talking about stuff like that with Duncan made my heart pound, and it didn’t help that Mario wasn’t my type at all. He was a cartoon character, and I knew some people were into that, but even if I was, he wouldn’t be the kind of character I dreamt of. He was too…

Something. I wasn’t sure what he was, but I didn’t think about him like that.

“He’s not my type!” I insisted before realising what Duncan had said. “Wait… why is it disappointing?”

I saw Duncan’s eyes flit towards me before returning to the screen again, and his throat bobbed.

“Well, I look nothing like Mario,” he told me with a disappointed shake of his head.

My heart seemed to leap into my throat, and I felt my eyes widen. I was at a loss for words, unsure of what to say to him, but I was pretty sure I knew what he was getting at. Part of me was worried, though. I was scared I was misinterpreting his words, that I was utterly wrong about what he meant, and I didn’t want to say anything too bold, just in case.

“And?” I asked, hoping that was a safe enough thing to say.

It didn’t give away what I was thinking, and I hoped it would prompt Duncan to explain it more. Hopefully, he’d be clearer about it. He’d be explicit about how he felt, and then I’d have no reason to be anxious. I’d be able to just believe him.

Duncan glanced at me again, but that time, he didn’t immediately look away. He held my gaze.

“And… I was kind of hoping I was your type.”

His voice was soft and strangely hoarse, and it pushed all thoughts out of my head. I’d been hoping he’d say something like that, but it still wasn’t clear enough. I needed to say something, to ask him something else, so that he’d just come out and tell me if he was into me, but I couldn’t even form thoughts. I had no idea what question would make him admit that.

“Oh yeah? Why’s that?”

The words just slipped from my mouth without my consent. It felt like someone else had said them, and I was nothing more than a puppet. I was a doll, forced to say what they wanted me to say, but that was good. I was glad about it because I knew there was no way I’d be able to think of anything that might work as well as that had to.

Cheering continued to echo from the speakers as Duncan’s gaze darted back and forth, his eyes searching mine, but it felt distant and far away. The only sound I could hear was the rushing thump of my own heart pounding in my ears as I waited for Duncan to speak.

“Because…” he started, taking a deep breath before continuing. “I’m—”

A loud buzz came from the table, and we both jumped back. My pulse became even faster as my head snapped around, and my eyes found my phone.

“Fuck,” I muttered as I reached out for it.

Disappointment crashed into me, and I glanced up at Duncan.

“Is that your dad?” he asked, running a hand through his hair.

“Yeah.”

“He’s outside?”

The urge to lie surged within me. I wanted to ignore the message or tell my dad there was a change of plans. I didn’t know what excuse I’d use, but I longed to say something that meant I could stay at Duncan’s, and he could tell me whatever it was he was about to say.

He was going to confess that he had feelings for me. I was almost sure of it, but the moment had been ruined. He was so close. He was about to say the words before the text had interrupted us, and I wished there was a way I could go back in time and turn my phone off because he wasn’t even meeting my eye anymore.

“Yeah,” I said again, trying to keep the disappointment out of my voice.

“Oh…” Duncan replied. “I’ll, umm, walk you down?”

His tone was so uncertain that it sounded like a question, and I hesitated before saying, “Sure.”

I glanced at my phone again, unlocking it quickly and typing a reply to my dad before grabbing my bag from the floor as Duncan rose from the sofa. I quickly joined him, my thoughts still racing as I tried desperately to think of a way to fix things and get the moment back.

But I’d stood too quickly, and I wasn’t sure if it was just the alcohol or what, but dizziness slammed into me. Panic flared in my heart as the fear that I was about to be dragged into another world pulled at me, but I couldn’t feel any of them looming over me. I was pretty sure it was just normal dizziness. That happened sometimes when I got up too suddenly, even if I hadn’t been drinking, but it normally wasn’t too bad.

Duncan caught my arm as I swayed, helping to steady me as the room continued to spin.

“Are you okay?” he asked, sounding concerned.

“Yeah, fine,” I said, blinking quickly to clear my vision before looking down. “Thanks.”

“Any time.”

He didn’t let go of my arm, though. I couldn’t help but glance at him again, aware of how close we were standing. I wanted to say something more, to take another step towards him and close the distance between us, but I couldn’t. I was too flustered, and I chickened out.

Dropping my gaze again, I turned away and started to cross his room. Footsteps followed me as I walked slowly along the landing towards the stairs, my mind racing as I tried desperately to work out what to do.

We were running out of time. I was about to leave Duncan’s house, and I was worried that if I did before he told me how he felt, he never would. He might overthink things, realise that he didn’t actually have feelings for me, or maybe he’d just chicken out like I did, and I really didn’t want that to happen, but I didn’t know how to fix it. I was too much of a wimp to make the first move, but I had to do something.

I reached the bottom of the stairs and glanced towards the door, seeing the glow of headlights through the frosted glass on either side. Biting my lip, I moved towards the storage bench, taking a seat as I pulled my shoes towards me and began to slip them on.

Duncan still didn’t speak. I could see him standing in front of me out of the corner of my eye, but he remained silent, and that made me even more anxious. He was normally more talkative than I was, and it felt weird when he wasn’t speaking. It made me want to talk, even if I didn’t know what to say.

“Thank you for today,” I blurted out, grimacing at how loud my voice was before continuing. “I had a really nice time.”
