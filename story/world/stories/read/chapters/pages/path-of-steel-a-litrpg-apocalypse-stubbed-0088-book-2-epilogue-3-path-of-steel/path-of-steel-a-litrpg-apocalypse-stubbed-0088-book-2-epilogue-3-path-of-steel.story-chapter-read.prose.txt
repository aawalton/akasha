Tables filled the grass in front of Satan Gym. Hundreds of climbers and monsters sat there, drinking and eating to their heart’s content as imps carrying large disks streamed out of the gym.

“To Satan!” someone cried out.

“To Gary!”

“To Feng Shen!”

“To us!”

People laughed and cheered merrily. They drank with gusto, as if alcohol was exactly what they needed after a hard war. The puppeteer had spread her power too thin on the chimeras, leaving her defenseless without her immortality. The moment she’d been defeated, thanks to a well-timed combined attack by Satan Jr. and Feng Shen, the horrors had collapsed, and the opposing climbers had been banished back to the second floor with a “poof.” They couldn’t climb again for ten days.

Of the enemy forces, Moe, Alpha, and the new healer brother had survived, along with Liu Dong, the swordsman sector boss. The majority of their climbers had survived, too, including Chloe. The Slime King’s fate was unknown.

Of the allies, many had fallen, though none that Gary recognized. Torga’s sacrifice was his only personal casualty, but it didn’t feel like one. It felt like victory. A life well spent.

“To Torga Ba,” he said, raising a cup. Everyone on his long table followed. “To Torga Ba!”

Asim was there, along with Cog, Alicia, Delilah, Marla Jackson, and other drafters from the Earth Alliance. Bob the cultist with two of his friends was also here—apparently, they’d been part of the rogue climbers in their army the whole time. Gary’s mercenaries had their own table, where they drank with the silent Jester of Hearts. Gary occasionally visited their table. He had no idea what the future held for them, but for now, he didn’t want to investigate anything. He just wanted to celebrate.

Feng Shen had also made it through the final battle, as had Lin Xin, Aurelius Pratius Lutius, and most of the Heaven Reaching clansmen. Rogue climbers made up the vast majority of the casualties in either army.

“I’m looking forward to climbing,” Alicia said, her cheeks red. “The Cove Floor, the Crystal Palace, the Ruins, the Fortress… I’ve been hearing stories about them since I was a kid. I’ll finally get to see them all!”

“Don’t forget about the twenty-fifth. If you’re lucky, you might see the stars,” Cog said. Alicia gasped.

“The stars…” she muttered.

“Hey, spoilers!” Asim said, to everyone’s laughter.

“You were really brave out there, Gary,” Delilah said. “I admire you. Really. A toast?”

“Ah, fuck it,” he replied, laughing as he cheered with her. She scooted closer.

“Is it true?” she asked. “Did you really—”

“Gary Steel,” a rough voice came over them. Gary raised his head.

“Satan,” he said.

“Step into my office for a second,” Satan Jr. said. He was back in his tuxedo, muscles bulging everywhere, wearing sunglasses and an earpiece.

“Why?” Gary asked.

“Just come. Bring your friend too.”

“Which one?”

“The alchemist.”

Satan walked off. Gary motioned to Asim, and they followed him through the banquet tables, into the gym, into his office in the basement. “Shut the door,” Satan said. Asim looked at Gary, who nodded, then closed the door.

“Finally,” Satan Jr. said, relaxing into his chair. He took off his glasses and earpiece. “Can I offer you anything? Shakes, drugs, prostitutes?”

“We’re fine, thank you,” Gary said.

“What about coffee?”

“I could use some coffee,” Gary admitted.

“Tea for me, black,” Asim said.

“We don’t do tea. Too peaceful.”

“Coffee, then.”

Satan slicked his hair back, then paced to the side of the room and worked the deluxe espresso machine. “I hope this quest was fun for you guys,” he said.

“I wouldn’t call it fun, but it was certainly inspirational,” Gary replied.

The devil returned with two small cups holding a brown-red liquid.

“That’s not coffee,” Asim said.

“It’s strong coffee.”

Gary took a whiff and felt his heartbeat spiking. He gently placed the cup down. Asim took a sip, then jolted his head up.

“Why did you call us here?” Gary asked.

Satan took the third cup to his desk, where he downed half in one gulp. His throat steamed. “I heard what you did towards the end,” he said. “You sacrificed a man to get a name. A name that certain people had gone to great lengths to hide.”

“Do you know him? Esquiardo?” Asim asked, speaking faster than usual.

“I’ve heard of him. He’s powerful, even amongst toppers, and I hear he’s a real piece of work,” Satan said with a chuckle.

“Are you going to go after him?” Gary asked.

“Me? No. What he tried to do to me was a hostile business takeover. It’s you guys who almost died.”

“So why are we here?” Asim asked.

“I don’t care about Esquiardo. I care about you two. You showed some real balls back there. I like people with initiative, and I absolutely love rebellious climbers. How would you like to work with Hell?”

“So, it does exist,” Asim said.

“Of course not. It’s just a figure of speech.”

“I don’t understand why everyone’s got to pretend that Hell doesn’t exist when it clearly does. It was right there in the imp’s description,” Gary said.

Satan squinted. “No, it wasn’t.”

“Anyway, we don’t want to work for anyone, let alone Hell,” Gary said.

“I didn’t say work for Hell. I said, work with. The agents of Hell are a loose organization of freelancers who only assist our interests when there’s mutual benefits.”

They glanced at each other. “Really?” Asim said.

“Think of it as a group of like-minded individuals. People who don’t appreciate the present status quo and might want to do something about it. Not a secret organization, exactly, but very exclusive. They throw the wildest orgies.”

“You want to invite us to orgies?” Asim asked.

“Your friend would like it,” Satan said, barking a laugh. “Missions are on a voluntary basis. If you join, you can choose to do nothing all the way to the top, and you get access to safehouses, contacts, opportunities. No strings attached.”

“What do you get out of this?” Gary asked.

“Not many people are willing to work with us. We get access to a group of such climbers, and we get to make them talk to each other, so they aren’t peer-pressured by everyone else to hate us. It’s tough being the devil.”

“So we join your freelancing agent orgy group and get access to safehouses but no responsibilities? I don’t believe that,” Asim said.

“Trust me, it works out for everyone. Nobody will make you do stuff you don’t want, but the missions we offer tend to be win-win situations. People generally accept. The only downside is that you become associated with Hell, which would make most climbers frown at you if they found out. Certain righteous organizations will know and blacklist you. They consider us evil, when we’re really just opportunists.”

Gary and Asim glanced at each other. “I have a question,” Gary said. “Why the hell do you think we’d be willing to help you with anything?”

“Sounds not worth the hassle,” Gary said.

“Yeah,” Asim said.

Satan grinned as he tapped a finger on his desk. “We have a very specific set of interests. Upsetting the tower’s balance is one of them. Working from the shadows is another, and eliminating certain high-tier people is a third. Above all, we’re very good at protecting climbers that the higher-ups want dead. I believe a cooperation would be very mutually beneficial.”

“We don’t need your protection,” Asim said.

“Especially not if it means owing you,” Gary said. “We can handle ourselves.”

“I’m sure you can,” Satan said. “So, what do you think?”

“Honestly? You’re offering us access to an underground society,” Gary said. “My liaison just confirmed everything you said. The benefits are good, and there aren’t many strings attached, just a severe lack of standing with most organizations if they find out. It’s a dirty road to power. And most of the missions will be illegal, which is great. I have no respect for the tower’s laws.”

Satan cringed. “Don’t say that stuff out loud. The void has ears,” he said.

“The void?”

“The space beside space. The Watcher’s Realm. Anyway, glad we’re on the same page. What about you, Asim?”

The Egyptian shrugged. “It goes without saying. The tower has only tried to fuck us over so far, so we have to fuck it back. The dark side is our side.”

Satan stretched his red, muscular hand. They shook it in turn. A screen chimed in front of Gary.

You have received an executive invitation to join the Chaos Symposium. Do you accept? Y / N

Gary raised a brow. “Chaos Symposium?”

“We needed a catchy, non-commital name,” Satan replied.

The two climbers glanced at each other, then accepted.

Congratulations! You’re now a part of the Chaos Symposium.

While the banquet still went on, Gary walked alone into the forest behind the gym and found a big flat rock to sit on. He sighed, relaxing his mind.

“Hey! What are we doing?” Goldie asked.

“I’m thinking, Goldie.”

“Thinking about what?”

“Everything that has happened. The System, the cheating, Torga, now this Chaos Symposium. I felt so lost when I first arrived at the tower, but I think I’m getting the hang of it now. I’m getting a direction. Unfortunately, it’s a dark one.”

“The Chaos Symposium sounds fishy,” Goldie replied.

“It is. Joining might have given me a whole new set of troubles to worry about, but I can’t deal with toppers alone, Goldie. Not yet. I need someone to have my back, even if it’s the devil, and I need power. As much and as fast as I can get it.”

“What if they make you kill babies?”

“They won’t make me do anything. Well, they’ll certainly offer all sorts of unsavoury stuff, but this is the road I must travel. If they’re evil, I’ll just have to ruin them, too.”

“And the list of enemies grows.”

“Tentative enemies. They’re allies for now. I need them, Goldie. I need the power. The Network destroyed Earth, and if I must become a devil to destroy them back, then so be it.”

Rage was bubbling up inside Gary. He was hungry. The tentacles yearned to lash out at everything surrounding him, but he kept them at bay.

“Your eyes are weird again,” Goldie said.

“They aren’t weird.”

“Are you sure? They’re black, with red irises, and they scare me.”

“These are my real eyes, Goldie. I’m not a human, but a monster. I’ll do what I must for vengeance, like Torga, like all of us.”

“So you will kill babies.”

“I won’t lose myself. I am not evil, and I never will be, but I won’t bow my head either. I won’t close my eyes. I have seen their filth, and no matter how low I must sink, even if I must sacrifice myself, I will not let it go unpunished. My path is forming, Goldie. I can see it now. I’m out for vengeance. For justice. My justice.”

“You can’t judge people.”

“Someone must do it,” His eyes darkened further. Tentacles licked out, draining the air of aura, feeding it to his warped starvein diagram and to his void lightning core. “The tower is corrupt, Goldie. It’s rotten from the top. Justice won’t do its job, so the only choice is steel. That’s my place in the world. That’s my mission. That’s me.”

Suddenly, Gary felt his mind rising higher. He saw his life from above as if he was someone else. He saw the aimlessness of his time on Earth. The drafting’s horror and grief. The unfairness. His own stubbornness on the Floor of Trials, Kyle’s teachings and subsequent fall. He saw himself becoming aware of the tower’s corruption, from the Sui family to this Esquiardo person. He’d always chased vengeance, but he hadn’t known how. It was finally becoming clear.

Something was taking form. A path, based on his emotions and goals, with his skills and knowledge as the core. A path which descended deep into the pits of hell, where he’d find the power to strike the heavens. Ruin, Lightning, Nemesis, the spear, the resolve to sacrifice his life for the cause. This path wasn’t just his—it was something greater than Gary, far greater, something that had always been there and which he’d been following blindly. Now, he saw it. It was one path out of millions. His feet finally stepped down, and all the other paths disappeared. The bloody gray of this path consumed him. He let it. They were one.

In the distance, far down the path, some ancient power beckoned.

Gary snapped his eyes open, realizing that his aura had changed. His vibrations, which had been messing with each other before, had now fused. They were different parts of a symphony, working harmonically to produce a single, wrathful melody. For the first time, his soul pulsed with it. It vibrated. Gary’s entire being was aligned, body, mind, and soul.

He was home.

He knew his eyes were black again. He’d assumed this was a transformation brought upon by the tentacles, but now he realized they had nothing to do with it. It was his own transformation. These were his eyes, his real eyes, which had finally opened. And they would never change again.

A screen arrived before him, in the same bloody gray as the path in his vision. It was unlike any he’d ever seen. Ancient. Dark.

Fitting.
