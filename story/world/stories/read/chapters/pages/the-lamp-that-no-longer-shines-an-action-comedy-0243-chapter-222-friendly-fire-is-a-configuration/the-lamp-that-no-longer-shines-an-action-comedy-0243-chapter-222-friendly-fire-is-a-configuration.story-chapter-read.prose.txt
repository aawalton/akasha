[Time]: Autumn Semester, Day 83 — 1:00 PM[Location]: West Campus · Yggdrasil Academy · Octagon Island Simulation Facility

Booking a sparring partner in autumn was, Hathaway discovered over two evenings and a considerable amount of her own dignity, a seller's market in which a second-year did not qualify as a buyer. The year ended on Irene Habsburg's Super Prize Cup, founded the season she took her seat and never once permitted to be a quiet evening, with a purse that moved a mid-sized hive city's currency, and every witch on the continent with a rating worth defending had shut herself inside her own training ground to grind rotations for it.

Whale event. Hathaway's inner producer watched the directory return its ninth consecutive unavailable. Limited banner, whole server logged in, and I need a raid boss for one afternoon.

Mihaye Grün took all five of them asking, and the Greed Umbrella signing off on it, because they were grinding for the same Cup and one Saturday afternoon of her came out of somebody else's preparation budget. She was a former Mandate of the Fire Dragon, and as a proxy for the Head of Evocation she was the closest thing available without committing a felony.

Katalu's morale is dreadful, Mihaye had appended to the confirmation. I don't run her as a combat asset anyway. But if you'd like to know how it feels to share a field with a fully buffed veteran who wants you dead, I can absolutely arrange the pressure.

The construct was a one-to-one replica of the exam arena, and standing at the staging window looking down into it did nothing pleasant to anybody's afternoon. Eight-Part Island, all eight wedges of it, tens of miles to a side.

Students picked their own deployment coordinates and the bell put them there. If a second-year's anti-divination was clean enough to blind the Head of Evocation for five straight minutes, Clarissa Deiner let her sit out the whole exam and signed the passing grade. Last year's one survivor had done exactly that.

Clarissa's entire loadout was displacement artifacts, because her innate firepower was already the ceiling of the continent and packing damage would have been a category error. She needed to find you, which reduced the entire examination to a two-item menu.

"We are not hiding," Surtrina had said, and nobody bothered to disagree.

"Then we fight," Victoria said. Her gloved finger moved across the holographic wedges and stopped.

"Two conditions. Hathaway's boundary has to be readable by eye, by any of us, at speed, under fire." The finger moved again. "And when the line breaks, every one of us clears that boundary at maximum velocity without negotiating a single obstacle."

Jungle, swamp, ruins, peaks and lake came off the board in under a minute. Nobody proposed the Permafrost Tundra twice with a Balor Witch in the room. The Crimson Desert ran open sun from one horizon to the other and put down no shadow anywhere on it, and Spectra let that sit until somebody else said it out loud. Which left one wedge where nobody present gained anything and nobody lost anything either.

The Badlands. Wind-scoured down to bare plate rock. Flat, fast, distance reduced to an honest number.

We just deleted cover from our own build, Hathaway's design brain observed, and paid for a ruler.

They locked the coordinates and gathered on the steel of the staging floor with the array humming under their boots, and Hathaway opened with the least sophisticated plan of her academic career and no shame about it whatsoever.

"Brainless dragon transformation. All five of us hold the complete [Assume the Dragon] matrix, it goes up the instant our boots touch rock." [Star Orbit] turned its three rings around her wrist in a slow idle. "Nothing deletes you in one spell inside that chassis. The window is the entire point."

She turned her hand over, and the innermost ring swung round and stopped.

"All three slots are preloaded with the original. She walks into range, I dump the magazine. I refuse to believe she eats three."

"Agreed on the transformation." Spectra's voice took a degree out of the room on its way past. "I layer [Greater Ghost Life Form] and [Apotheosis: Ghost] and hold her inside the boundary. When you fire, I fold." She considered the middle distance with her usual warmth. "I do not expect to need long."

Victoria reached into her jacket and produced a heavy scroll bound in gold wire, and the ambient mana in the room turned toward it the way smoke turns toward a vent.

"I hold a Tier-9 record of [Time Stop]. I intend to tear it on the opening frame and establish the perimeter while the clock is not running."

Yenna's golden ears rotated sideways. She hesitated, and then she drew two silver-capped scrolls out of her sleeve.

"But I intended to deploy [Time Anchor] and [Space Anchor] immediately."

Surtrina rolled a jagged runestone across her knuckles, and the thing bled a purple light that made the steel underfoot look unwell.

"[Banshee's Wail]." Her tail cut the air behind her. "It clips her once, she is finished, we all go home. I am not seeing what a perimeter contributes."

Hathaway looked around the circle at four of the finest second-years in the Inner Sea.

Her developer brain put both hands flat on the desk and stared at the manifest. Five flawless win conditions, and not one of them checks whether the port is already in use.

Yenna sighed, kept both anchors in her hand, and said nothing at all.

Surtrina flipped the runestone up, caught it, and showed everybody her teeth.

"Whatever. We deploy, we fight, we sort the order out once something is on fire."

The bell went, and the floor let go.

Hathaway hit the rock and came up a Force Dragon, whole chassis, matte-black scale and obsidian horns. Off on the flank, magma and gold scale roared up as Surtrina and Yenna finished their own shifts, and Yenna tore both scrolls before her claws had finished settling. The air went rigid. Something enormous and unfriendly settled over the wedge and locked its coordinates into a grid with no give anywhere in it. [Time Anchor]. [Space Anchor].

A handful of microseconds behind her, too late to call back, a thread of mana came off the iridescent chassis and took the gold-wired scroll apart down the middle.

The wind kept blowing. A skein of grit went on drifting across the plate at exactly the speed it had been drifting before, and a Tier-9 record of [Time Stop] came down as two halves of very expensive paper.

Port conflict. Her developer brain watched the two halves of the scroll settle on the rock. The grid cost us a Time Stop and Spectra's fold, and cost the boss every time and space spell she owns. Good trade. Better one if anybody had announced it.

A line of dust came off the eastern edge of the wedge with Mihaye at the front of it, coming fast. [Greater Ghost Life Form] and [Apotheosis: Ghost] were up over Spectra's chassis before the dust had covered half the distance, and Spectra went out across the open plate to intercept.

Ballistics only, then. Come on. Hathaway brought [Star Orbit] up and fired the first slot while Mihaye was still a long way out and Spectra a long way short of her. The outer ring ran the gap, and [Ovelia's Evil Lantern Cat Sunflare Burst] closed a hundred and fifteen meters wide with Mihaye at the center of it, a sphere of gold with an edge you could have measured against a rule.

The gold went out on empty rock. Beside her, Victoria had already swung thirty degrees off the crater and put a breath into what looked like open ground. Illusion. Hathaway wrenched her neck around, found the real Mihaye sprinting the flank, and fired the second slot.

Mihaye reached back and tapped a brass cylinder strapped to her lower spine, a [Jetpack V-19]. A storm ring detonated out of its vents and threw every living thing within a hundred meters off its feet, and she rode her own knockback out along a flat, ugly, entirely unmagical parabola while the second sun closed on a hundred and fifteen meters of unoccupied rock.

A consumable. Something behind Hathaway's eyes went white. Three generations of my house died in the margins of that spell and she stepped off it with a gadget you can buy in a mall. Who approved that item.

She tracked the arc and fired the third and final slot into the place where Mihaye was going to land. Spectra was closing on the same spot from the far side, still outside the radius.

Mihaye landed, raised one hand, and flicked a [Fortune Theft] hex into Hathaway's targeting matrix with no apparent interest in the outcome. Something microscopic went wrong in a draconic forelimb that had never mis-executed anything in its short existence, the rings kicked, the payload went off axis, and the boundary closed directly above Spectra.

Both of her buffs were up, and the spell treated the pair of them as fuel. Every enchantment and artifact on her came apart at once, and the shell melted off her and left a shivering translucent puddle of ectoplasm on the smooth new floor of the bowl: Ghost Slime, the last survival state available to her race. Mihaye put an [Annihilation] through the middle of it without turning her head.

"Spectra!" Surtrina put a dragon breath down the flank and fired [Banshee's Wail] off the runestone in the same motion.

Mihaye turned the breath aside with the back of one hand, and it came around ninety degrees onto Hathaway, who put her own breath up into the path of it. On the death-hex Mihaye spent a [Wish]. Reality stuttered, the hex left the runestone at an angle Surtrina had not chosen, and it clipped the trailing edge of an iridescent wing. Victoria went down.

The stone fired a second time into the body, and a third time into the rock where the body had been, discharging the last of its charges with the mechanical thoroughness of an appliance whose button has stuck.

Surtrina held perfectly still over what she had just done to a teammate. Then her wings came down and she charged.

"Right," Mihaye said pleasantly, and tapped her wand against her thigh. [Swiftness].

Yenna had been holding since the anchors went down, waiting for one thing worth spending on, and from the first cast she knew three more were coming. [Greater Swiftness]. [Mythic Swiftness]. [Legendary Swiftness]. She brought her [Eradication] down on the same beat as the fourth.

Two of the four came off, and with the other two still running Mihaye had both back up before Yenna finished the follow-through.

The casting cap of the physical universe stopped applying. Tier five through eight, thirty a second, each one landing before the last had finished, and the plate went white and stayed white. She is putting out her best work at the rate of her worst, Hathaway's developer brain reported from underneath something heavy.

Three chassis went to full defense, and everything the three of them owned went into holding it there. Break line of sight. She got the wings open. Space is anchored. Nobody anchored the sky.

She launched. Yenna and Surtrina came off the rock half a beat behind her, three sets of enormous wings hauling for altitude through a wall of noise, and for one entire second they were clear of it and climbing and out.

Mihaye watched them go. She let them get all the way up, all the way clear, long enough for every one of them to know she had made it. Then she spoke a short line of very dense syllables: [Declaration: Face the Enemy].

A golden ring came down over the whole wedge, and Hathaway's wings locked at the top of a beat. Her momentum stopped being hers. Several tons of Force Dragon was rotated in the air by something that regarded the chassis as a piece on a board, set down on its feet facing forward, and held there, and when she tried to turn her head the head did not turn. Yenna and Surtrina were planted beside her in the identical orientation, staring down the same roaring cylinder of artillery.

The carpet came back down, and they drowned in it.

[Time]: Autumn Semester, Day 83 — 2:45 PM[Location]: Octagon Island Simulation Facility · Respawn Chamber

They sat in a loose circle on the cold floor with nobody looking at anybody. Surtrina had her tail wrapped around her ankles, the flame at the tip of it down to a pilot light, Yenna's ears were pinned flat, and Spectra was against the bulkhead, or most of her was.

Hathaway pulled her knees up, put her chin on her arms, and kept every part of her mouth shut, which cost more than the entire afternoon that had preceded it. Because she had spent a professional lifetime in rooms where one person made the call and eight others executed it without argument and the boss died on schedule, and she knew exactly what that position was named, and which disposition in this circle fit it. Four independent threads, and not one of them would authorize the sentence.

Victoria stood by the wall with her cuffs straight and her hair unmoved, and spoke in a voice with nothing arranged about it.

"I tore a Tier-9 scroll because I decided to," she said. "Nobody told me not to. Nobody could have."

She looked around the circle and made every one of them meet it.

"One of us calls. The rest execute. It does not matter who, as long as it is not five."

The silence went all the way down, because the answer to who was now lying in the middle of the floor where everybody could see it, and there were only two ways to reach for it. You said your own name, which was a claim. You said somebody else's, which was a concession. All five were used to being the one giving the orders, and four of them would have to say somebody else's name, out loud, in a room with the lights on. Nobody reached.

Twenty meters away Mihaye Grün leaned on the archway with a betting slip open on her terminal, listening to five prodigies fail to say a name, making no effort whatsoever with her face.

She coaches the other five. Hathaway did not look over. And she has never once had to sit through this.

For the remaining three hours Mihaye drilled them, merciless and extremely good.

She took Hathaway's targeting matrix apart, showed her twice how anyone competent would corrupt it, and made her fire through the corruption until she stopped flinching. She taught Spectra to hold a target from a distance. She spent forty minutes on the angle at which a dragon breath can be turned, with Surtrina eating her own fire until she stopped presenting the line. She gave Yenna a second answer for after the first one had been paid for, and showed Victoria how fast a wing has to fold to get under a stray hex. Every minute of it went to one witch at a time.

She upgraded the weapons. Hathaway watched Yenna hold back a scroll she would have spent that morning. On a friendly-fire problem.

When the booked time ran out Mihaye checked her terminal, gave them a wave that was almost affectionate, and went.

They packed their gear in silence, went out through the main doors together, said the ordinary things at the intersection, and went five different ways into the long gold light of a Saturday evening.

Hathaway got back to Room 302, dropped her coat where it landed, and opened her terminal. [Unpaid QA Testers] sat at the top of the list, as quiet as the old channel had gone, and under it the roster still held five names.

All five still logged in. Something in her chest quietly stood down. Everybody broke, and the whole raid is still standing at the entrance. She grinned at the ceiling. A wipe disbands. This is a lobby.

Below her a door opened and closed, and a familiar pair of boots came up the stairs at an unhurried pace and stopped outside hers.

Hathaway got up to open it, and thought that whoever finally said the name, she was going to enjoy watching the other four take it.
