Despite having to rely on his own mana pool, Vin’s personal mana supply was large enough at this point that he was able to leapfrog them a mile or two at a time with Redirect Gravity, carrying them across the fragment as the day passed. Shia and Scule each continued taking new samples with each stop they made, and Vin received a welcome surprise after a few hours.

Vigor increased to 29!

“Just got an attribute increase,” he informed his friends, getting a thumbs up from Scule and a squeak of congratulations from Reginald. “Vigor this time around. The only other time it happened it was dexterity, from eating one of the monsters from the Sacred Forest.”

“On the one hand, that’s good,” Shia said, brushing off her knees as she got up from the ground. “On the other hand, that means we should really pick up the pace and get out of here before we start seeing the first signs of monster-meat poisoning. Though we probably have until Scule and I get attribute increases before we really have to start worrying.”

With that in mind, the four of them picked up the pace, and Vin allowed himself to grow dangerously low on mana as he pushed his gravity magic a bit harder. Scule ended up announcing his increase in vigor not half an hour later, and Shia her own a bit past that. Thankfully, before he could start feeling so much as a headache, much less a burning desire to kill and eat all of his friends, they reached the edge of the fragment.

“No island, we’re coming in hot!” Vin warned them, getting shouts of affirmation as Shia and Scule prepared for anything. The four of them shot through the fragment barrier and drifted to the ground, weapons at the ready and eyes scanning their surroundings for any threats.

Third ring fragment discovered! 1,500 exp gained.

They’d spent most of the day working their way through the tainted fragment, which meant the sun was starting to lower itself in the sky as late afternoon came to an end. That said, Vin didn’t think they were going to have any sort of problem when it came to a lack of light.

Not with the distant, colossal lighthouse bathing the entire fragment in a wash of warm light.

“What on earth,” Vin muttered. He squinted when the powerful light swept over them, illuminating what had to be a good quarter of a mile of land before it continued on, slowly traveling around the entire fragment. The lighthouse was a massive structure, practically the size of a skyscraper seeing as they could easily make it out from a full six miles away.

“Is that a building?!” Scule asked, looking just as shocked as Vin felt as he stared at the giant structure. “How can something that tall even be built? Wouldn’t it just fall over?”

“It’s bigger than any tree I’ve ever seen,” Shia added, frowning as she watched the light continue to swivel across the fragment. There were actually four openings in the top of the lighthouse, meaning four separate rays were sweeping the land all at the same time. “Way bigger than the Tree of Ancients. What is it doing?”

“On my world, lighthouses were used to help keep ships from running ashore,” Vin explained. “But they’re not normally so ridiculously large. And seeing as this one is in the middle of dry land… I don’t have the faintest idea.”

Reginald squeaked out a suggestion, and Scule translated for Shia as Vin nodded.

“Reginald wonders if it might be a fortress of some sort, similar to the Citadel. It would make sense I guess. If the land isn’t suited to build out, just build up instead.”

While the giant building had been enough to draw their eyes upon landing, Vin had to admit the rest of the fragment didn’t look like it would be all that great to live on. The ground was rocky and largely barren, with little more than wilted grass and the occasional sad clump of flowers or thistles desperately struggling to hold onto life. There were craters here and there that reminded him of the surface underneath the floating library, albeit far fewer in number.

“I think Reginald might be onto something,” Vin said, kicking at a loose rock. “This land looks like it’s been through hell and back. Maybe everyone took refuge within the lighthouse when the monster waves got worse. We might very well have another incident of people not even realizing the world they’ve come to know is completely gone.”

“Been a while since the last one,” Shia said, still staring at the huge building as if the fact that it was taller than her people’s Tree of Ancients was a personal attack on her specifically. “What was it? The valley in the mist?”

“Yep, that was the one,” Scule nodded. “Wonder whatever happened to Normun anyway. Guy became an Explorer and just vanished, right? Sure hope he’s not… well… you know. Dead.”

“Thank you, Scule, I think we’re all hoping that,” Vin drawled. Shaking his head, he began walking toward the only thing on the horizon. He also found himself occasionally wondering what happened to the man they’d helped lead away from a village of people who refused to access the System. Even if Normun hadn’t ended up following in Vin’s footsteps and taking the Explorer class, he would have liked to have known where he headed off to after leaving Terra. As much as Vin hated to admit it, most likely, Normun had died shortly after going off on his own. Trying to navigate Edregon alone and at a low level was a death sentence. He could only hope the newly minted Explorer had managed to end up in capable hands.

As the four of them made their way across the dying landscape, it wasn’t long before they encountered their first monster. While Vin was busy ignoring some renewed bickering between Scule and Shia, this time surrounding whether or not the Citadel or the Sacred Forest was the better place to live, he noticed a slight trembling in the ground under his feet. Despite standing on his shoulder, Scule seemed to notice it too, and the petian immediately stopped arguing as he pulled out his blowgun and looked down.

“Vin, you feel that?”

“Yeah. Something’s coming,” he confirmed, not wasting any time as he cast Tremorsight. His mana transformed into vibrations that shot out through his immediate surroundings, and Vin pointed a finger as he located the cause of the foreign tremors. “Underground beast, two o’clock! Shia, get ready!”

“Right!” Shia said. Leaping off Blossom, she turned the cat back into staff form and leveled the weapon at the spot he had indicated. The vegetation on this fragment wasn’t very prominent, which meant the Druid would be largely relying on her staff to hold her own. Barely a few seconds after he’d called it out, the ground erupted and dirt was sent flying everywhere as a half-dozen mole-like monsters erupted out of the ground and into the air. That was startling enough on its own, but it was nothing compared to when they extended their arms and began flying, shifting directions in the sky as they divebombed them.

New monster discovered! 200 exp gained.

“Didn’t see that coming!” Scule laughed. He easily shot down the first monster with a well-placed dart that punched through the monster’s hard skin and deposited its poisonous payload into its flesh. The monster’s limbs went slack and it slammed into the ground, its flesh already turning a sickly green as it died.

“No kidding,” Vin muttered, taking down two more with carefully aimed Stone Shots. “At least they’re just regular monsters.”

“Yep, piece of cake!” Shia grinned, using Rapid Growth to cause sharpened branches to explode out of her staff and stab through two of the monsters trying to fly around her and attack from behind. Scule managed to take the last one down just as easily with another dart, and just like that, the fight was over.

“Not exactly the deadliest of monsters,” Shia said, nudging one with her foot as she crouched down to examine its leathery membrane. “The wings remind me of the avisul a bit.”

“I think it may be more like we’re a hell of a lot deadlier than we used to be,” Vin said. Shaking his head, he imagined how such an encounter would have gone for them back when they were only level 10. “I think it’s far too soon for us to grow lax. Let's all try to stay on guard as we keep moving.”

With that, they left the monsters where they killed them, carrying on toward the towering lighthouse in the center of the fragment. The team was forced to stop and deal with a few more ambushes, all curiously coming from monsters that seemed subterranean. After the flying moles, they encountered a large snake that burrowed through the dirt as though it were water, and a swarm of insects that looked like large beetles with fangs. That last one was the deadliest, as if it weren’t for Vin’s fire magic, they would have been in for a rough time.

Scule technically had the reagents required to create a poisonous smog if it came to that, but Vin preferred not fighting in a deadly cloud of poison whenever possible.

“Alright, what the hells are the lights even doing?” Scule asked as they jogged the last half-mile up to the base of the lighthouse. “They don’t stop when they pass over us, so it’s not like they’re looking for survivors. And nobody is coming out to help when they sweep over the monsters, so they’re not looking for a fight. They seem pretty pointless if you ask me.”

“Maybe it really is functioning similarly to how they did back on my world,” Vin shrugged. “Alerting people of the presence of… something.”

“A few more minutes and we can ask them ourselves,” Shia said, pointing to the base of the lighthouse as it grew closer. “You know, assuming they’re willing to talk to us. Let’s hope this isn’t another Citadel scenario.”

“Hey, the guards still talk to you guys! They just won’t let you in,” Scule argued weakly, coughing as he turned away from Shia’s eyeroll. “Anyway, it’s not like they’ll be able to keep us out. With my skills and Vin’s magic, I doubt there’s a lock on all of Edregon we can’t get past.”

Unfortunately, the lighthouse proved to be one step ahead of them, quickly revealing that it didn’t need a locked door to keep them out.

The architects evidently decided to get around such a weakness by not including any doors at all.

“Stupid, anti-Rogue building,” Scule grumbled, motioning for Vin to lean closer so he could kick the lighthouse. “There aren’t any doors! How are we supposed to get inside if there aren’t any doors?!”

“Maybe it’s designed with flying crafts in mind,” Shia muttered, tapping her chin as she peered up. Now that they were at the base of the building, the colossal tower leered over them, blocking out a massive portion of the sky when they looked up. “I’ve never seen any myself, but my master told me about how one kingdom far away on our world had been experimenting with ships that could sail atop of clouds. And Vin, you said your world had flying ships as well, didn’t you?”

“Yeah, we had airplanes and things like that. Maybe that is what’s going on here,” Vin muttered, deciding there was only one way to find out. “Well, if there are no doors down here… guess we check up there.”

With that, he cast Redirect Gravity, deciding to angle them sideways so they could jog up the side of the building rather than just fall into the sky. While they ran, he opened his core, steadying his breathing as he borrowed mana from the world around him to keep his mana pool stable. The bright light shining off into the distance was warm and welcoming, while the stone wall that the building was comprised of was hard and unyielding. Scule whistled while they jogged up into the sky, the petian peering down at the ground below as they traveled higher and higher.

“It’s crazy that this doesn’t freak me out as much as it used to. Guess you really can get used to anything if you do it enough.”

“Speak for yourself. We’ve already established petians fall slower than other races, you’ll probably be fine if Vin stopped his spell,” Shia pointed out. “On the other hand, I would plummet to my death.”

“I’m not going to let either of you fall,” Vin said, rolling his eyes before spotting something a good two-thirds of the way up the tower. “Wait, what’s that? Is that a window?”

Sure enough, the blemish in the otherwise flat stone wall ended up being a ring of windows spanning the width of the entire tower. The windows themselves were about six feet tall, leading Vin to believe they’d happened upon some sort of observation deck facing out from within the lighthouse. As they jogged, he spotted a face peering up into the sky, the person’s gaze in the wrong direction to notice their approach. Vin was more than a little surprised at the discovery of a new race of people, but he put that on hold for the moment, slowing to a walk as he approached the edge of the window. Clearing his throat, he bent down and knocked on the bottom of the glass.

“Hey!” Vin called, causing the pale-skinned being with large, feathery wings growing out of its back to leap back in fear, staring with wide eyes at the four of them standing on the outside of the tower.

“Any chance you could let us come in?”
