The world pulsed.

Volcanic glass beneath the two men’s feet lit up in blinding orange radiance for a single heartbeat. I blinked away the spots that had momentarily seared their way across my vision, expecting to find the Jorge and the stranger locked in a furious melee, but to my surprise, Jorge came striding my way with even steps.

Markas hung suspended in the air, arms stretched out to either side and held by blinding chains of orange and gold that dripped molten mana to the ground. Not a single part of him moved, save his blazing serpentine eyes. They tracked Jorge’s movements and eschewed a hatred more powerful than a hundred suns—or so it seemed—and I could only hope that this man would die quickly down here in the dark.

“Easy does it, lad,” Jorge said with his usual gruff charm as he levered me up. “You’ve got perhaps ten minutes before everything goes to shit. I can’t contain him for long.”

He gestured vaguely at the most powerful man I had ever met, restrained in the air and completely helpless so far as I could tell. Well, second most powerful, I suppose.

“Why don’t you just kill him now?” I asked, voice still husky with pain.

Jorge shook his head. “Doesn’t work like that, sadly. Listen, lad, this’ll get messy. There’s no guarantee I make it out of here after this, so you don’t waste time with silly questions now. Get to Vera and the others, alright?”

My eyes glazed over slightly as my knees gave out. He caught me. A couple of light slap in the face brought me blinking back to the present, and I was met with Jorge’s concerned gaze.

“Hey, with me,” the old man said as he checked me over, sucking in a breath as he saw the gaping gash in my forearm, leaking life-blood even now.

I’d lost too much. I knew that now. My vision was blurring just from standing in place, and I felt feverish and cold despite the neutral temperature of the Vault. Jorge unstoppered a flask he had retrieved from somewhere and pressed it to my lips with urgency. The syrupy liquid slid down my throat and warmed my stomach from within, spreading like fire through my system and going some way to rejuvenating me.

He then uncorked a bottle of something that smelled suspiciously like hard spirits and handed it over. I regarded it with scepticism, but Jorge just thrust it harder into my hands. “Trust me, you’ll be wanting this,” he said.

I shrugged and upended the bottle, chugging as much as I could before handing it back. He cracked a grin and gave me a nod as I coughed and spluttered, trying in vain to hold down my gorge at the scalding bitterness across my tongue and throat.

Then his smile faded as he took a hold of my wrist. “You ready, lad?”

I grimaced and set my teeth, and that was enough of an answer for him. I nearly passed out again as he pressed his hands together, manipulating the torn flesh into place and resetting the bone. I couldn’t see what he was doing—I’d deliberately looked away—but my mind couldn’t help but dwell on it. I knew my wrist hadn’t been severed, and from Varice’s words, I suspected the bone was still mostly intact, but the manipulations Jorge was doing now implied that something had separated, at least. I felt my gorge rise, but the pain soured away the sickness quickly.

My legs had turned to jelly and Jorge’s grip was all that kept me standing. A whimper fell from my lips alongside a string of drool, but the torture ended soon. Jorge hastily applied a bandage then passed me another bottle.

It took another few breaths for the sobbing to stop, but once I’d composed myself, I took another drink. Thankfully, this one was simply a small potion bottle. Down the hatch it went, and after another few breaths, I started to feel the knitting of tissue below the bandage.

Unfortunately, it turned out that this—not the physical manipulation from moments ago—was what the alcohol had been meant to help with. It hurt. It hurt so fucking much. Instead of coming on all at once, the sensation crept up over my wrist like a rising tide of stinging insects, biting and burrowing in a frenzy. That thought only made things worse so I sought another analogy. Like the dawn, perhaps? Creeping fingers above the horizon…

I choked out a strangled laugh at the absurdity of negotiating with my own gods-damned mind. Still, the pain quickly reached its peak, then began to recede.

“Fuck me, that hurt,” I gasped out once it was done.

Jorge gave me a hearty slap on the back in response. “Good to have you back, Lamb.”

I managed a weak grin, though the trauma was still bubbling along beneath the surface. Adrenaline had a way of making major events seem minor when compared to the future though, and my body was a veritable soup of adrenaline currently. I managed to push away the terror and pain and focus on what came next.

“What’s the situation?” I asked.

“A mite complex, I’d say,” Jorge replied, and I smiled at the likely understatement. “Vera and Nathlan are holding the north tower, pretending to have a fight with the rebels that are assaulting it. Nathlan took down some of the wards, the canny little bastard, so they’re just biding their time before Vera strikes at the duke.”

“What’s the duke doing?” I asked.

“Oh, right! He’s fighting the Sultanate. The Sultan and his forces have lain siege to the castle, and the duke is busy trying to push them back. Fandar and his group are at the north tower, as I said, and Jacyntha and Sadrianna are fighting with the duke currently,” Jorge explained. “…Unless it’s all gone to shit since I left, mind you,” he added with a chuckle.

I absorbed it all as best I could, trying to think through the best course of action. “Shall I get Vera down here to support you? I’m not sure there’s much I can do myself, to be honest.”

“Just get yourself out of here. Markas is my problem to deal with. You let Vera know that it’s him I’m down here with and she’ll understand. Her focus is the duke, and your focus needs to be supporting her and the rest of the group, aye?”

He held my eye even after I nodded, clearly expecting something more. “You keep them safe, lad. You keep them alive. Understand?”

“Aye, Jorge. I’ll see it through,” I said with a confidence I didn’t feel, my legs still wobbly and hands still shaking.

“Right, off you go. Not much time now.” He dismissed me with a warm smile and a hearty backslap.

There was so much I wanted to say, given this could be the last time I’d ever see him. But there was no time, so I started stumbling out of the small room that housed the Hasta’s cultural heritage. Smooth cut obsidian passed by on either side as strength returned to my steps, and soon I was running once more. Rooms passed in a blur as I retraced my steps, thoughts a blur.

The fight with Varice, the revelation about Vanguard of the Lost and what it could give me, the stranger’s intervention and now Jorge’s potentially final words. It had all happened so fast.

Reaching the entrance to the library, I saw the door—the black door that even the duke had only been able to open a sliver of the way—splintered a cracked. Shards of obsidian lay on the floor inside the library, and a jagged hole was blasted through its centre. The level of power that this spoke to was frightening but, I remembered Jorge’s words about haste.

I took a final look back at the library; a crooked forest of smooth black walls rising a dozen feet into the air and a warm orange light glowing in the distance from where I knew my friend would be soon facing the man that had wrought such destruction on the Vault that I had previously assumed to be nigh-on indestructible.

I ducked through the jagged entrance and sprinted off through the Ashkanian ruin. There was barely any sound as my booted feet pounded along smooth slabs. I felt like a ghost slipping through a titanic tomb beneath the earth.

My wrist was no longer throbbing. I twirled it as I ran, feeling the tendons move smoothly and the muscle respond to my commands with ease. Any other niggles and pains seemed to have been sorted by whatever Jorge had fed me, and I focused on putting as much distance between myself and the coming calamity that a fight between two 4th tiers was sure to be.

End of the Hunt I kept active to shield me from the dread aura of the Ashkanian Vault. It had been disabled earlier, but it could return at any moment, and it’s not like I needed any help fearing for my life currently. I let the familiar pressure of my pathbound skill reassure me as I reached the entrance and began the climb up the scaffold that formed the ligneous exoskeleton around the Vault’s exterior.

A part of me wanted to turn back, to face Markas alongside my mentor. Running from a foe was difficult to square with my pathbound skill’s intent, after all. But still, the gulf of power between me and the two old monsters below was so astronomical that even my perpetually defiant skill seemed to acknowledge the sense in retreat.

I spent every breath for the next five minutes just cimbing. It took me back to how I had begun things in Tsanderos—running for my life. Stride The Edge guided me over difficult terrain as surely as Cloven-Hooved ever had, and my enhanced attributes helped propel me to new speeds as I danced across crooked wooden beams, vaulted over stairs four at a time, and scampered up scaffolding in places where the switch-backing path seemed slower than climbing directly.

I knew the moment Markas and Jorge clashed for the first time.

It felt as if the vault itself—the great pyramid deep within the earth that I was still scurrying up—shook to its foundations. But it wasn’t the obsidian blocks that moved. Instead, the air itself shivered. The first blow shook the void and made me dizzy from the impossibility of it.

Each step became confused, as if I was moving underwater in thick currents—the space before me seeming to twist and writhe. My feet moved in unpredictable ways, landing an inch to the left or a hair to the right from where I intended. I looked up in despair, knowing I was still a good thousand feet from the entrance tunnel above the top of the pyramid, and knowing equally that I would be unable to reach it at my current pace. I felt as if I was moving through molasses.

I focused on my goal.

Nathlan, fighting alone in a sea of blood. Vera, fighting the demon within that craved vengeance, only held back by her desire to protect her friends. Sadrianna and Jacyntha, risking death beneath a foreign sky with only the trust that we would come for them soon to see them through the chaos. And beneath all of those noble reasons, a far more selfish goal raised its head—survive, and recover my memories.

I poured mana from my core into End of the Hunt and winced in pain as I felt my soul strain against both the pressure of the battle far below, and the recent injury caused by Markas’s soul attack. Nonetheless, I grit my teeth and pushed on, flaring my aura around me like a cloak.

I pushed it further, first a cocoon and then a bubble enveloping me and the surroundings a few feet in each direction. Within, I was dominant. I was the retaliation that haunted the hunter’s steps. I was untamed, wild, and utterly without peer. With the full force of my soul, I proclaimed my pre-eminence in that small domain of space and felt the weight of the peak existences below fall away.

My feet landed where they should, the air no longer wavering. I pushed on, my full speed unlocked once more, and I sprinted the final distance towards the top of the pyramid. When I reached the tunnel, I risked a final look back and saw nothing but hazy darkness in the cavern below. The near-black pyramid hung silently in the void, swamped by depthless darkness all around. A sonic boom cracked the still air, causing a plume of dust to shake itself free from the ceiling, and that was enough to convince me to run for my life.

Tunnels passed by in a flash, and soon enough I began to relax my aura skill, allowing mana to flow once more into my core from where it was nearly spent. I would need it soon enough for my own battles, no doubt. A crossroads passed me by, and then a couple of tunnels to my right, spaced apart by a mile or so each, then I was nearing the end of the tunnel, the regular wooden support beams and mage-lights giving way to unworked stone and wall-mounted sconces filled with mundane torches.

By this point, I could no longer feel the punishing auras of the 4th tiers and so took a few moments to catch my breath and drink some water from a waterskin in my storage ring. I ran on, slipping through the corridors of Castle Ryonic and seeing not a soul as I did so. Far too soon, I came to the heavy iron-banded door that separated the inner courtyard from the keep itself, and I took a moment to think through my next move. I didn’t feel ready, didn’t know what I would face or how to counter it when I did.

I took a few moments to review the notifications I’d received from killing Estan.

Attributes allocated, I took out my armour and began to dress, slipping out of the simple tunic and cloak that had made up my disguise. Soon the humble God-Touched was gone. In his place stood a 2nd tier warrior, blood of the mountains flowing through his veins and conviction strong is his heart. It was a little ostentatious and arrogant to think of myself in that manner perhaps, but I was about to risk my life in battle—after what I’d just been through, I needed the confidence.

I still felt adrift; cast about in waters I didn’t yet understand. The battle below was a reminder that I was far out of my depth, and only moments before that I’d been utterly helpless at the hands of somebody still only in the 2nd tier. She had been about to cut my fucking hand off, and she wasn’t even a combat classer. When I stepped through this door, I’d be facing others with power like her, possibly even beyond her.

My breath had been even despite the earlier run, but it began to speed up once again as I remembered the pain and terror. My wrist throbbed, but when I looked at it, the skin was even and clean, though it was absolutely covered in blood. I tried to calm myself, to reassure myself. I’d been winning—I’d escaped her clutches and had been dancing around avoiding Varice’s attacks until the end. Until I’d unlocked the memory of my past life.

There was that ache again, right in the centre of my soul. As if something was missing, and I only knew it by its absence. But something was missing, and I wasn’t crazy. I’d remembered once, and I could do again.

None of this was helping. I needed grounding, needed certainty. Who am I? What am I even fucking doing here? I heard muffled shouts beyond the door, the chaos of battle roaring its challenge, and leaned my head against the ancient wood. I was here to save my friends. They were out there, and they were risking life and limb right fucking now. I might not know exactly who I was, might be cast adrift on tides of confusion as my mind swirled and skipped across an ocean of hidden memories, but I knew I wasn’t the type to let my friends face danger alone.

I pulled the armoured vest over my chest, letting it sit flush against my skin. “I am Lamb of the mountains,” I said quietly, willing certainty into my voice. It was rough and gravelly, tortured from too much screaming, and there was slight warble to betray the uncertainty I felt. But it was my own voice, and it was under my control.

“Wielder of Resolution,” I spoke slowly as I strapped each buckle into place. “Surefoot, World-Walker, Titan-Forged and Unbroken.”

The reinforced plates of steel and strips of the Corrinian Rhai exoskeleton sew within the vest gave it a reassuring weight, and I rolled my shoulders to settle it in place before moving onto my heavy boots with their reinforced shin-guards.

“I have endured the predations of the Crimson Lions, and beaten them and the Wielders of Azlan both,” I said, strapping the silver-steel gauntlet to my right wrist, covering the scar left over from its near-severing only ten minutes prior. The tanned flesh was stained red from the blood that I’d lost, as if I was clothed in it from forearm to shoulder on my right side.

“I am the pack’s last hunt. I’ve ended bandits and mercenaries, skeletons and wild beasts beyond counting,” I growled. The shield of the Corrinian Rhai carapace sheathed my left arm from elbow to fist, two small spikes emerging over my knuckles like punch daggers.

“I have treated with the Subakir, have explored the Lost Grove and plumbed the depths of the Hollow Mountain,” I called into the corridor, daring anyone to disagree as I strapped my hatchet to one hip and my fang dagger to the other.

I took a final breath, pulling Resolution free and expanding it to its full war-form with a gentle caress of my mana. It responded with fervour, the red-lacquered haft gleaming in the firelight from the torch behind me, the white wolf-leather grip soft and supple in my hand.

“I am Lamb of the Mountains, and I am lost no longer,” I whispered, before kicking open the door.
