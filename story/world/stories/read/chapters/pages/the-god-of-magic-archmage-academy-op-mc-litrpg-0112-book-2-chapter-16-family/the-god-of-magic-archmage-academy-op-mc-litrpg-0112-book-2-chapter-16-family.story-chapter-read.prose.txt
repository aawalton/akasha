Solomon

The members of Meteor Haven met in front of Lord Thessus’ estate. It was still dark, and only the mana-streetlights illuminated the area around them. Luckily, the rain had stopped, and the mist had faded. Now it was just a cold, dark night. At least, it would be until they began their work.

Agatha and Theodora looked fine. Simeon wore a frustrated look and was the most bruised up of them all. Bartholomew was grinning from ear to ear.

“What’s got you so happy?” Simeon grunted.

“I haven’t had that much fun fighting since Solomon hunted me down.” Bart cackled.

Thea shivered. “It still bothers me that you laugh every time you talk about it. He killed your entire cult, and you just…joined him? If…” She hesitated.

Bart turned to look at her. “If what?”

Thea’s voice fell. “If someone killed all of us, would you join them too? Would you laugh about it afterwards?”

Her question surprised Solomon. Bartholomew, of all people, would rarely take such a question seriously. He frowned. It was easy to forget how young she was sometimes.

Bart walked over and placed a hand on her shoulder. “If someone ever kills you, little Thea, it’s only because I’m already dead.” He chuckled.

Solomon’s eyes widened.

Simeon looked between Bart and Thea. “Uh, are we sure he wasn’t replaced? Maybe we should go back and check for a body.”

Bart turned to look at him. “I heard ye lost your fight to a little beasty. Pick out a finger so I can use its bone as a toothpick later.”

Simeon took a step back to hide behind Agatha. “Never mind. It’s him.”

“What are your orders?” Agatha asked. Solomon’s family turned to look at him.

He returned their gazes before looking up at the estate that dominated the landscape of the city. Lord Thessus and his forces were inside. “We’re going to burn it to the ground.”

“What?” Theodora demanded. “But this is our only lead! If we do that, then...”

“I know. But look at what we did tonight. Look at what we fought for?” He’d known they would have to look the other way at drug peddling and shady deals. But dreadbeasts slaves? No, he never would’ve set forth this plan if he’d known about that ahead of time. But he’d convinced himself it was for the greater good when faced with the wicked truth of the expansion Thessus had been talking about. Hindsight was always clearer, even without an Asteroid Eye to guide him. No, tonight he’d acted horribly, and fought against good people who simply wanted freedom for themselves or others.

He of all people should understand what the dreadbeasts had lost all that time ago.

Meteor Haven had to atone for their actions tonight, even if it meant a setback.

“We’ll find another way. One that doesn’t compromise our destination,” Solomon said. “Do any of you disagree?”

Theodora went quiet. She stared at the ground. Simeon looked at her, and then at Agatha and Bart.

Agatha eyed the estate while she thought.

“I have a question,” Thea said.

They all turned to look at her. “Yes?” Solomon asked.

She grinned. “Who gets to kill Thessus?”

Solomon chuckled. “Kill all those complicit. The house servants are innocent; allow them to leave. Use your best judgment otherwise. The guards and everyone who helped make and distribute the drugs and counterfeit goods, and especially those who knew about the slaves, die tonight. Simeon, follow the underground routes to destroy the storage. Theodora, you memorized all the safe houses distributed throughout the city. Deal with them and those inside. The underground operations must stop completely. Leave nothing behind, so that no one can easily fill the shoes Thessus leaves empty.”

“What about Lady Orianne?” Simeon asked. “I don’t think she actually knew about any of this stuff. What do we do with her?”

Solomon nodded. “Spare her with the servants and her maidens. Now go. We need to finish.”

They nodded. Agatha drew her sword and cut through the gate.

The guards hurried forward. “Lady Agatha, what’s going on—”

Blood splattered the wall of the estate with her slash.

Theodora and Simeon disappeared, splitting up to enter the estate at different points.

Bartholomew smiled. “Ah, this is it. Moments like this are why following you around never gets old.” He hefted his axe on his shoulder and leapt. One moment he stood in the street outside the estate; the next he plowed through a second-story window covered in flames.

Solomon held up a hand and flexed it. It had been a long time since he’d used this power, but he needed to make sure no one escaped.

Luckily, this Fate Line was hidden even deeper than the others. He’d lost his powers before; he would never be caught unprepared again.

“Comet’s Eye Cosmos,” he said with a grin. An eye made of floating rock appeared in his palm. He held the palm toward the estate. It wasn’t as powerful as the Asteroid Eye, and it needed to be aimed directly to show him what it saw, but it would do.

He would oversee the destruction. Tonight was a setback, but he would find the trail again.

He squinted. A group of men escaped through the rear doors of the estate. He sensed Thea pursuing them, and he willed the power of the comet to bar her path with flames. His way of claiming these men as his own prey. Thea hesitated only a moment before turning back to continue her hunt inside the estate.

He’d just answered her question from earlier. He would be the one to kill Thessus.

It took only a moment to catch up; he jumped from rooftop to rooftop until he descended, landing in the city streets to cut off a retreating Lord Thessus.

The man actually looked relieved to see him. “Solomon! Oh, thank System! The estate is under attack.”

Solomon nodded. “I noticed.” He turned and scanned the group to confirm something. “Where is Lady Orianne?”

Thessus swallowed. The faces of his guards fell with shame. “There was no time…we had to leave her. Her guards will get her out.”

“I could go—” Solomon offered.

“No!” Thessus said quickly, with widened eyes. He cleared his throat. “Stay and protect me. We need to leave the city immediately. At least until we find out who’s responsible for this.”

Solomon clenched a fist. He couldn’t believe what he was hearing. “What kind of coward would abandon his child? His flesh and blood just to live a few moments longer?” he demanded.

“Excuse me?” Thessus asked as if Solomon was the one out of line.

Solomon took three steps and plunged his hand into the coward’s chest. He pressed the Comet’s Eye right against Thessus’ still-beating heart. A pulse of mana shot outward. The guards were disintegrated immediately. Thessus’ eyes bulged, and he opened his mouth to scream, but no sound escaped him.

Solomon wrenched the man’s soul from his body, embedding it within the Comet’s Eye. He lifted his hand toward the stars above and released it. “Soul Soar Suffer Cosmos.”

A chunk of the Comet’s Eye shot into the sky above, piercing the heavens and soaring to the heights of stars. It carried Thessus’ soul within. He thought for a moment of an appropriate sentence. The man had abandoned his daughter to save himself. He should have ample time to reflect on that. Not to mention how many lives the drugs he peddled affected and stole. The man had even hoped to embed himself in the heart of the slave trade. Yes, Solomon had an appropriate number in mind. “Judgement - Four Hundred Years.”

Plenty of time to suffer and reflect.

Thessus’ soul would linger among the stars for four hundred years in constant agony. Along with all the others that Solomon had been forced to condemn.

Small fingers wrapped around the pointer finger of Solomon’s hand. The one without the Comet’s Eye in it. “Daddy,” a young girl said.

Solomon closed his eyes and inhaled deeply.

She was back.

She always visited him when he used the Comet’s Eye. Never the Asteroid Eye. Only the comet’s. It was the reason he’d stopped using this version of the spell.

“Yes?” he asked the ghost who took the form of his daughter.

“Was that him? Have you punished the bad man yet? So I can rest?” she asked him.

This isn’t her. She’s not real.

“Not yet, sweetie. Soon.”

“Good.” She nodded and turned to watch the destruction unfolding at the estate. Shattered glass and screams filled the night. “That’s evil blood, right?” She pointed to the crimson splatter on the windows from the floor Bart had leapt to.

He pushed her from his mind and focused on his goal.

Solomon would have the head of the man responsible for his family’s death.

The man who had taken his powers.

The man who had destroyed the Church of Gom.

He would end the comet’s curse and be free of it all. He was grateful to the man he’d met tonight.

The journey mattered, and he would walk this path until he plunged a fist into the heart of the man who had taken his family, his magic, and his church.

Solomon would walk this path until he condemned that man’s soul to an eternity amongst the stars.

Sen

“I should kill you,” Sen spat.

He’d released his Asteroid Eye spell as soon as he’d seen that the Khans and members of Meteor Haven had stopped fighting. Now he and Micah sat outside the silver gateway Sen had created in an alleyway for the dreadbeasts to escape. All of them were gone now. They’d finished teleporting during his fight against Solomon. Luckily, his short time in the temporal world hadn’t affected this gateway at all. Now that their use for it was finished, he intended to send Micah through and close it.

Micah swallowed. His eyes didn’t meet Sen’s.

“Do you know how many lives you endangered, you coward?” Sen asked him. “Can you think of nothing but yourself?”

“I—” Micah started to say.

“You what?”

“We can’t…leave the city yet,” Micah said.

[I think we wants to die. Maybe we should indulge him?]

Sen was almost inclined to agree. Micah was the worst type of person. The only reason he was still alive was because Drussan had asked Sen to spare the boy before the uprising. But the dreadbeast might change his mind now that they knew Micah was the one who led Meteor Haven to the east gate. He was only a few years older than most of Sen’s classmates had been at the academy. Age was no excuse for someone so craven as this.

If the next words out of Micah’s mouth annoyed Sen as much as the last words, the boy’s fate was truly up in the air. “And why can’t we leave?” Sen demanded.

“They have Slari…they took her to the estate!”

Sen blinked.

Tutor?

[That was the name of the other dreadbeast Solomon said they had captured.]

Oh. Right. That was one Drussan had recognized. Apparently, she’d been serving as rear guard. “Tell me what you know,” Sen said.

Micah stuttered during his story. A lot. But he eventually got the details out. Drussan showed up towards the end and exchanged a nod with Sen.

“Everything okay?” Sen asked, eyeing the dreadbeast’s fist.

They were covered in blood.

Drussan nodded. “It’s not all mine.” He grinned.

When Drussan said he’d remained prepared to fight all these years, he hadn’t been lying. He’d taken on a high-level lord-class mage and came back in one piece. Sen now understood why Drussan had been chosen to protect a descendant of Garlon. He only wished he could’ve seen him in action.

“Micah was telling me about Slari. Why don’t the two of you go meet the others? I’ll find her and catch up,” Sen said.

Drussan shook his head. “No. I told you all. I can’t leave until every dreadbeasts is free of the city. We’ll go together.”

Sen sensed two people approaching the alleyway and looked up. “Someone’s coming!”

Drussan and they exchanged a nod before hurrying towards the end of the alleyway. If they had a fight on their hands, it was best they take it into the streets instead of being cornered.

But as they turned the corner, they were met with a surprise.

Theodora stood next to a dreadbeast woman.

“Meteor Haven?” Sen said.

“Slari?” Drussan asked.

Theodora scratched the back of her head. “Solomon asked me to return her to you. He said it was part of making things right.”

Sen nodded. “Take her back, Drussan. I’m going to visit this Thessus myself.”

Theodora winched. “I don’t mean to take the wind out of your sails, but…we kind of dealt with that already. Estates burning down as we speak.”

Sen shook his head. “What? You did?”

Drussan raised a brow. “Did I miss something? You’ve turned on your employers?” He looked at Sen. “How badly did you beat them?”

Theodora scowled. “Hey, hey! Nobody got beat. Well, maybe Simeon a little bit. But that was funny, so it doesn’t count. The boss just decided that he wanted to do things the right way. Whatever that means. Anyway, I’ve got to go. Good luck with your, uh, freedom fighting or whatever!”

She disappeared in an instant.

Slari immediately dropped to her knees. “Please forgive me, Khan. I failed in my duty as rearguard. I do not deserve the honor bestowed upon me and ask that you revoke my status and banish me.”

Drussan sighed. “I refuse. Stand up, Slari.”

She looked up at him in surprise. “But Khan, I—”

“I only heard the tail end of the story, but from what I heard, you sensed a dreadbeast and turned back to help him. That’s not a failure on your part, Slari. You couldn’t have stopped that guild on your own. They were strong enough to go hoof to hoof with the other Khans and I. You served the horde well. Rise and feel honor,” Drussan said, placing a hand on her shoulder.

Slari looked up, tears in her eyes. “Thank you, Khan Drussan.” She noticed something behind them, and her eyes widened. “Mi?!”

Sen turned to see Micah peeking around the corner. He hid when she noticed him.

Slari hurried past them and grabbed Micah by both shoulders. “Mi! You’re alright!”

Micah blinked in surprise. “You’re…you’re not mad?” he asked.

Slari shook her head. “I’m glad you’re okay. I was worried about you.”

“You were?” Mi asked.

Sen exchanged a look with Drussan. “What? You’re happy to see him?” He asked in disbelief.

Slari smiled. “It’s true he told them how to find the others, but only after they threatened to kill me if he didn’t. He saved my life. I would never risk the horde to save myself, but Mi isn’t a warrior. I can’t fault him for a situation he was only in because I was too weak to protect him.”

Sen shook his head. That was absolute nonsense. Micah had sold out slaves on the verge of escaping. Slaves he and his father profited from. But to be fair, Slari didn’t know that. He expected Drussan to scold her, but to his surprise, his dreadbeast friend was focused on him.

Drussan chuckled. “Dreadbeasts value the tribe above all else. Warriors fight so that civilians escape and the tribe endures. When a warrior cannot protect those untrained to fight, they do not blame the untrained. Micah is one of us now. But he’s no warrior. We do not blame civilians for surviving at all costs. That is their duty. It’s why our warriors fight.”

Slari turned to look at him. “Now? What do you mean one of us now?” she asked.

Micah looked concerned for different reasons. “Wait, are you saying this is permanent?” he asked, looking at his dreadbeast hands.

Drussan sighed. “Only Sen can turn you back, and he—”

“My condition for allowing you to live was this. If you’d like to die as a human, feel free. But if you and your father are going to live, well, get used to your new forms.” He’d spared the boy for Drussan’s sake, but Micah was still a slaver, and Sen would see him punished for that, regardless.

Slari looked utterly confused at it all but wisely realized now wasn’t the time.

Drussan approached and put his arms around Slari and Micah. “Come, let us join the others. Malachi is still recovering; we will collect him later. For now, we need to escape Vitree.” He escorted them to the portal and nudged them each forward. Micah hesitated, but Slari offered him encouragement, and they entered the portal together.

Drussan turned to face Sen. “My friend, tonight was only possible because of you. Garlon declared you an honored one in his time with his dying breath. When I return to my people, I will ensure you are declared one in this time as well. Will you come with us? I am certain Garvhadda will wish to meet you. There will be celebrations and feasts. You should be there for that!”

“I can’t. I’m sorry.” Sen frowned. “I must wait for my friends to return. Besides, my work isn’t done. There’s still something I must do to ensure all dreadbeasts find freedom tonight.”

Drussan’s face hardened. “The spell. I see. Well, once your friends have gathered, feel free to seek us out. You will always have a home among us. Please know that.” He turned, but hesitated a moment. “Would you like me to stay? Until your work is done?”

Sen shook his head. “No, your people need your guidance. Go on ahead, Drussan.”

“Thank you, my friend. For everything.” With that, Drussan turned and walked through the gateway. When he was through, Sen willed it to shut.

He allowed the notification he’d been delaying to appear.
