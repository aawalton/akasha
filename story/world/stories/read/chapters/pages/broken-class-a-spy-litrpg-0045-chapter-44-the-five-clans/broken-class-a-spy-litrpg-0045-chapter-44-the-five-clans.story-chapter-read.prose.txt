With the foundation of his new identity set, Arthur’s focus shifted from the man to the world he had to navigate. He looked at Ysara, his expression unreadable, waiting. Understanding the silent command, she took a breath to steady herself. "The kingdom is ruled by the Monarchy, but its power is not absolute. True influence is divided among five great noble factions, or Clans, each tied to a fundamental element."

"At the top is my father, the King," she said. "He is the only D-grade entity on this planet. He holds the ultimate political authority, but he himself answers to a C-grade faction from off-world, a group known as the Radiant Court. Our entire world is, in effect, their vassal state."

Arthur’s mind latched onto the detail. A king who isn't even a king. Just a warden for a larger prison. The thought sharpened his next question. "And what is the price for the Radiant Court's oversight?"

"People," Ysara said, her voice dropping. "Our most powerful. It is the tithe. Anyone who breaks through to D-grade is required to leave this world and serve the Radiant Court. In exchange for this tribute, they offer us protection and maintain a semblance of order."

"If they need powerful people, why not empower the slaves?" Arthur asked, the question sharp and logical. "Why not unlock the System for everyone? More people with power would mean more potential D-grades."

Ysara looked uncomfortable, reciting an argument she had likely heard her entire life. "It was tried, centuries ago. But giving everyone access to power led to chaos. Constant rebellions, infighting, criminal factions... the kingdom nearly tore itself apart. The nobility decided that limiting access to the System was the only way to maintain law and order."

Drogan’s voice exploded in Arthur's mind. 'What a weak-ass excuse! The King is too feeble to control his own subjects, so he puts collars on them and calls it order! Pathetic!'

For once, Arthur found himself in complete agreement with the remnant. It was a flimsy justification for hoarding power. If they truly wanted order, they could have found a way without resorting to mass enslavement.

"You said your father is the only D-grade, but that true power lies with the Clans," Arthur said, steering the conversation back. "Tell me more about them. How does your father fit into this structure?"

"He is from the Water Clans," Ysara explained, her voice taking on a more formal tone. "The Water Clans master the manipulation of water, ice, and even blood. Their power controls trade on the rivers and can be devastating in battle." She paused, a flicker of pain in her eyes. "Garran Drax was a member of the Water Clans. His death has... significantly weakened the next generation of their house."

The mention of ice sent a jolt of recognition through Arthur. Rowler Drelmor, he thought, the memory of the ice mage's defeat still fresh. He used ice magic. He must have been from the Water Clan as well. A grim smile touched his thoughts, and by his hand, the future of a major clan had been decapitated before they even knew he was an enemy.

"The Water Clans are traditionally allied with the Nature Clans," Ysara continued, her gaze distant. "My mother was from a Nature house. They command the forces of growth and vitality, healing allies, and influencing the beasts of the wild."

The distinction caught Arthur's attention. Beasts, not monsters? he thought, his mind flashing to the chimera that killed his parents and the creatures now sleeping in his ring. He projected the question to the remnant in his head. Drogan, what's the difference?

Drogan snorted, a sound like grinding stones in his mind. 'Finally, a question that isn't completely stupid. Beasts adapt to the world's mana. They're part of the natural order. You can tame them, train them, ride them into a pointless battle. Monsters are walking disasters. The mana makes them chaotic, aggressive, and fundamentally broken. You can't control a mistake of nature, boy. You can only kill it.'

While Arthur processed the internal lecture, Ysara continued her overview of the other factions. "The Fire Clans command military might through destructive magic and are allied with the Air Clans, who control travel and aerial warfare. When they join forces, their magic is said to be unstoppable. They are usually opposed to the Water and Nature alliance."

"And the fifth?" Arthur asked, his voice a quiet prompt.

"The Earth Clans. They control the mines and the mountains. Their power is in defense and the unyielding strength of the land. They are neutral, preferring to trade with all sides and commit to none."

Arthur ignored Drogan's continued grumbling, his focus entirely on the princess. "What about powers outside these elements?"

"They exist, but they are rare," Ysara explained. "A mage with the ability to create portals, for example, would be an invaluable asset. The Clans recruit them aggressively. Anyone with a rare or powerful skill doesn't stay independent for long."

She looked up at him, her expression a mix of weary resignation and grief. "There are too many important figures to name. Powerful dukes, cunning matriarchs, ambitious generals. To list them all now would be useless. It would be better if I explain who they are as you encounter them."

Arthur gave a slow, deliberate nod.

"And my role in all this?" he asked, gesturing to the face he now wore. "Kaelin's role."

"You are one of my sworn bodyguards," she confirmed. "As such, you have quarters next to mine in the palace. You accompany me whenever I leave my chambers. Your presence will be expected. As long as you remain quiet and follow my lead, no one should question you."

He wanted to press for more, to learn about the palace layout, the guard rotations, and the King's temperament, but he saw the raw grief in her eyes. The sight of Kaelin's face had clearly unsettled her, and he sensed that pushing for more information now would be fruitless. Her cooperation was a fragile thing, and he could afford to be patient.

He straightened up, the decision made. The first phase of his infiltration was complete. It was time to move on to the next.

"That will be enough for now," he said, his voice the quiet, final tone of Kaelin. "It's time we leave. We have a mayor to meet."
