Fourth ring fragment discovered! 2,000 exp gained.

While entering the minotaurs’ dungeon from the Underside prevented Vin from getting his experience for discovering the dungeon correctly, being reunited with Drintus and getting to follow the mighty warrior back to his home town was a more than worthy exchange in his opinion.

The dungeon itself was surprisingly simple, taking the usual form of a cave. There were minotaur warriors stationed within who were busy using it as combat practice, each of whom paused and rapped their knuckles against their dense armor when they saw Drintus walking past. Some of them didn’t even wait for a break in the fighting before doing so, ignoring the monsters that tried to bite or claw into them as they focused on the tall warrior.

Just as Vin had thought, it was quickly made clear that Drintus was an exception among his people. While minotaurs were definitely larger than humans, with their entire race ranging from six to seven feet tall, none of them came even close to rivaling Drintus’ massive frame. His height combined with the respect everyone showed him as they exited the dungeon made Vin feel almost like they were walking in a tiny parade.

“This is Harmstead!” Drintus smiled, gesturing to the town they suddenly found themselves in. “My people have lived in these mountains for many centuries, and we will live here for many more!”

There was a chorus of agreements shouted out as dozens of minotaurs greeted Drintus with that same rapping against the chest plate, but Vin’s eyes were drawn to the town around them.

For the most part, the town was the same as any other. Other than the fact that everything was just a bit bigger to account for the minotaurs’ large size. Though most noticeably was the distinct change in elevation throughout town. Harmstead had been built on the literal side of a large mountain, and Vin stared in awe as he looked up at the town sprawled above them, and then down at the town below.

“No wonder his calves are the size of your head,” Alka snorted, stopping beside him as she followed his gaze down to the other half of town. “Getting around has got to be exhausting.”

From their vantage point, it was easy to see all the different minotaurs going about their days. Crafters toiled away in their shops, and laborers lugged crates around. Warriors sparred off in designated sections away from others, while young children sat and watched, pointing and cheering whenever one fighter managed to land a blow on another. There were even a handful of humans and elves directing a few minotaurs off in some slanted fields, carefully planting seeds and monitoring crops grown on the harsh incline.

“Why did your people choose to found a town on the side of a mountain?” Vin asked, peering at one of the nearby houses. One side of the house had to be drastically longer than the other in order to keep the whole thing level. “I feel like there could have been easier places to build. Like on the top.”

“It is much too cold up there for the average person,” Drintus said, heading uphill as he led them into town. “While I and the other Vanguard members would often run up there to train, most would not risk it. There are some rather nasty monsters that spawn up in the colder air. Good for training, but not for the common folk!”

Vin sent a silent thank you to the Gods for making the Explorer class give him so much extra endurance. Even with his high attributes, he could feel the strain in his legs after only a couple of minutes of following Drintus deeper and higher into town. It wasn’t as if he had any tools for measurement, but he would bet the entire contents of Scule’s cape that the side of the mountain the town was built on was resting at roughly a thirty or forty degree angle. No matter how long they walked, he couldn’t shake the feeling that if he tripped or leaned back, he’d roll all the way down to the bottom of the mountain.

The minotaurs were clearly more used to the sensation than he was, and had even found ways to take advantage of that incline.

Vin smiled as he watched what looked like a trolley of sorts go sliding past, utilizing the power of gravity to follow a set track and bring a few dozen elderly and young minotaurs down to the lower portion of town. Once everyone had gotten off, a powerful team of minotaurs with muscles almost as impressive as Drintus’ own got to work pushing a series of pulleys, dragging the trolley from where it had stopped all the way back to the top, where those who had gotten on at the bottom disembarked and the cycle began anew.

Despite the late hour and the lack of sun, it seemed Harmstead remained lively late into the night. The large portion of the mountain was lit by a series of massive braziers scattered all throughout town that functioned as roaring bonfires, casting warm light everywhere.

“The lights will be extinguished within the hour,” Drintus said, stopping outside of a small building that was built into the side of the mountain. “This is the Vanguard’s headquarters. It is empty these days, so you are welcome to rest here for now.”

“Thanks, Drintus, we appreciate it,” Vin said, following the minotaur into the empty building. Sure enough, it was much bigger on the inside, having clearly been dug into the mountain itself. Past the main room and giant dining table were a series of individual doors that presumably led to private rooms. “Is there any reason your people haven’t appointed more people to the Vanguard? You sound sort of important.”

“We do not go looking for people to join the Vanguard,” Drintus said. A sad smile stretched across his face while he took in the large, empty room that had once been filled with his friends. “Those who are worthy of joining rise up and come to us. Heroes in deadly battle. Saviors in terrible accidents. It usually only happens once every few years, so this is nothing to be worried about. More will come, and when they do, I will train them as my mentors trained me.”

“Damn it,” Alka muttered as the minotaur bid them goodnight and walked into the first of the many rooms, for once not having to duck his head in order to clear the doorway. “I’m not really one to fangirl, but I think Drintus might be the coolest person I’ve ever met. You’re lucky I respect you so much, otherwise I’d be giving some serious thought to leaving Terra and moving into here.”

“Are you kidding me? I’m arguably one of the most important people in all of Terra and the thought crossed my mind for a second there,” Vin said as he shook his head. “But on a more serious note, as amazing as Drintus and his people are, we can’t stay too long. Unfortunately, this wasn’t one of the two fragments we still needed for the third ring. According to my Mental Map, we need to head south-east to double back and hit the next one. We’ve still got some time before the festival, but not a ton. I think we can afford to spend one day in Harmstead before we have to leave.”

“Man, keeping to a schedule is a real pain,” Alka grunted. “Still, unlike you, I don’t need to sleep. I think I’ll go hike up to the top of the mountain and take a look at these scary monsters Drintus was going on about. Though before that…”

Alka tapped at the side of her head, and Vin watched as a thin pool of slime streamed out from the crack between her helmet and head. Epli formed a small version of Alka in her hands, peering curiously at her with her own green eyes.

“Alka?”

“I’m about to go somewhere super cold, so stay here with Vin for now,” Alka instructed. “I know that’s pretty much the one thing you don’t like.”

“Yes. Bad,” Epli nodded. Turning toward Vin, it turned into a small version of him instead. “Wait.”

“Hey, Epli. Long time no see,” Vin smiled. “Sorry I know you’re nocturnal and normally spend the nights chatting with Alka, but I was planning to catch some sleep.”

“Okay,” Epli shrugged with his own small shoulders, giving him a warm smile. “Watch.”

“Epli finds it interesting how you guys need to sleep,” Alka translated for the schlime. “It likes to watch you sometimes. I try to encourage it to watch Scule the most. Twice now Epli’s almost given him a heart attack when he wakes up in the middle of the night to find a larger version of himself staring down at him!”

“So that’s what that was…” Vin muttered. “I thought he was just having nightmares he didn’t want to talk about.”

“He probably does now!” Alka chuckled. “Anyway, I’m off. I’ll be back at some point!”

Bidding her good hunting, Vin turned and picked one of the many rooms at random. It was an interesting experience opening a door designed for someone who was eight feet tall without including the horns on their head. The room itself was simple enough, with a massive bed and some basic furniture. Setting Epli down on the bedside table, Vin looked at it.

“So… I’m just going to go to bed. I guess you’re welcome to watch me sleep if you want.”

“Thanks,” Epli replied, smiling at him with his own face. Vin shook his head at the tiny, slimy version of himself watched him go about his routine. He summoned his shimmerwing mattress and tossed it on top of the giant one, before creating himself a cloud pillow and wrapping the merider-silk blanket around himself. After a moment, he reached out and created a tiny cloud for Epli to sit on. The schlime looked curiously at it, losing Vin’s form as it turned back into a misshapen blob. After slinking its form onto the cloud, it turned back to Vin, taking his shape once again.

“That is always so weird to see,” Vin muttered. Nodding goodnight to the tiny him, he cast Dream Walk and focused on the Dream Mark located three fragments away.

Opening his eyes, Vin looked around, surprised to find himself in Lumel’s childhood room in her father’s palace rather than in the Underside. If he hadn’t gone through part of Lumel’s dream journey to rescue her, he never would have recognized the room at all. Lumel herself was sitting on her bed, a pensive frown on her face as her thoughts plagued her.

“Feeling nostalgic?” Vin asked, causing her to jump before she spotted him and relaxed.

“I wasn’t sure if you were going to visit tonight,” she admitted, giving him a sorrowful smile as she looked around the old room. “To think I used to like pink so much…”

“I’m still curious how your people manage to do stuff like construct buildings and weave fabrics underwater,” Vin admitted. Sitting beside her on the bed, he pointed at her pillow. “Like that. Why isn’t your pillow floating away? It looks like it’s too light to rest at the bottom of the lake. Shouldn’t it rise up?”

“Different densities in the water and magic,” Lumel shrugged. “I don’t know all the details, but my mother explained it to me a while ago. There are rituals underneath the entire capital that alter the physics of everything. Without them, life would be a lot more difficult.” With that, she shifted over, placing herself onto his lap as she sighed and laid her head on his shoulder. “I don’t know what to do, Vin. The meriders think the Airwalker is going to show up any day now, and I don’t even know what I’m going to say. I mean, I was banished. What right do I have to ask him how things are going, let alone seeing if he would take a message for me to my mother?”

“The worst he can do is say no,” Vin said. Wrapping his arms around her, he gave her a gentle squeeze. “Guess what. Alka and I are currently in a town built onto the side of a steep mountain and filled with minotaurs. I’m going to see if they have any mages tomorrow. We ran into Drintus.”

“Drintus?” Lumel repeated, recoiling in surprise. “Wow, small world! I haven’t seen him in ages. How is he?”

“He’s good! Seems a little lonely, but just as loud and strong as ever,” Vin chuckled. “But you should know, it turns out he has a method of bypassing the Underside barriers as well. You might be out of a job before long.”

“I’d hope that there are at least a few other reasons you might be keeping me around,” Lumel giggled. Smiling, she sat up and wrapped her arms around him, giving him a tender kiss. Vin was slightly surprised by how hard she pulled him against her, but he wasn’t about to complain. When they finally came up for air, or water, in this case, he smiled back at her.

“Maybe one or two reasons. So, what’s the plan? Want to cram in a little magic practice?”

“That’s basically all I’ve been doing while waiting for the Airwalker,” Lumel admitted, flushing slightly as she gave him a coy look. “I’ve really missed you, and we are in my old bedroom. I can think of at least one other way to spend the time.”

“All those weeks spent comatose in order to gain dream magic were one hundred percent worth it,” Vin said, getting another giggle out of his lifemate before she pushed him down onto her old bed. The last thing Vin saw before the lights in the room winked out like magic were Lumel’s robes vanishing in a flash of purple as the pulmon took his hands in her own and gave him a soft, loving kiss.
