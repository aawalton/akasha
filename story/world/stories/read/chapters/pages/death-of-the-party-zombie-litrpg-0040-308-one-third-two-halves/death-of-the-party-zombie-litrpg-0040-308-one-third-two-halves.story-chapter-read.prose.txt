Sally turned the dagger around in her hands as her crimson eyes scoured the quiet woods.

Eventually, she deflated and glared at the cat. “You know I don’t like sitting idle, Arch.”

“You’re already operating at a disadvantage by fighting in the dark,” he replied. “Plus, you’re clearly fatigued.”

“When has a negative condition ever stopped me from trying my best?” It was more of a rhetorical question, and the zombie looked back up at the spaceship rather than wait for an answer.

Edward had been gone almost ten minutes and had looked like he’d survive half of that being possessed. At this point, she was just waiting for Jackie to confirm the demon had returned to the Omen after dying. While mapping out the interior of a Radoch vessel seemed like a good plan at the start, it wasn’t entirely realistic. Not unless she had a full party of Outsiders - maybe two.

“Do you know anything about our shadowy friend?” she asked, eyes still skyward.

“From my limited understanding… they are not unlike an Adventurer, but are clearly not human.” Archie followed her gaze.

“Perhaps they’re from one of the fantasy worlds, then.”

The cat hummed, but didn’t confirm or deny the idea.

Sally’s stomach rumbled, filling the area with the sound of her hunger. “Boy, Bully better hurry up. I could eat a-”

Before she could think of something large and edible, a shockwave blasted down from the spaceship. White light bloomed, showering the area with near daylight as several linked explosions erupted in tandem near the back end of the vessel.

“Holy!” Sally covered her eyes as gusts of wind billowed around her, shaking the trees. An earth-shattering growl followed, almost deafening her as the ship tilted in the air as if in slow motion.

Archie hopped down from his perch and approached the zombie. “We need to get out of here, unless you wish to become a pancake.”

Her stomach complained again before she gave the cat a nod. “I don’t think I’m outrunning that, but I have one last potion of random teleportation.”

The air continued to vibrate as the alien craft fell to the ground. Sally pulled out a small bottle and popped the cork. “Here’s to hoping RNG doesn’t just move me twenty feet away.”

She downed the liquid, and a flash of blue snapped her away from the woods.

Jackie turned in her chair to see a figure walk into the room.

“That was certainly a novel experience.” Edward rubbed his head as he slumped down in a chair.

“You look rougher than normal, demon-boy.”

He split his fingers so that he could look at the gangster, his blue eyes slightly dull. “Feels like a bad hangover. I don’t even think it’s resurrection sickness; that… entity just wore away at my being.”

Jackie drummed her fingers on the arm of her chair. “Ya know, I thought up a buncha good jokes while you were possessed, but blowin’ up the bug’s vessel was pretty cool.”

“Glad to have entertained you.” Edward leaned back in his seat and closed his eyes. “I’m not sure it really helps our cause, but I think I can tick off my evil deed for the day.”

“Dropping several miles of bullshit onto the Outsiders does sound like a you thing to do.”

The demon grinned, exposing sharp teeth. “Ah, but the lack of the pair being here with me surely means my act of betrayal failed.”

Jackie rolled her eyes and turned back to the monitor. “Sally teleported to the other side of the continent. The walking tin-can somehow just… survived having the ship land on him.”

“What of the Radochs?”

“Too early to tell.” She scratched at her loose purple hair now that it was out of its ponytail. “Some further from the impact have paused in place, probably waiting for orders. A few groups tried running from the collision point, and didn’t make it. Estimated casualties… several hundred?”

Not a bad final blaze of glory, Edward thought. Once this excursion was over and done with, he’d probably have the second highest kill count of the five. He had no doubt that Sally could overtake him. It was almost a given that she would be the last one left on the planet.

The demon shuffled uncomfortably in his seat. Why did the part of his back that had sprouted wings still itch after he had resurrected?

“A valid question.” Archie sat with his tail curled around him. “Your guess is as good as mine.”

Sally rolled her eyes and surveyed the surroundings. The potion had taken her way out of the splash zone of the dropped spaceship, although the result of the collision was clearly visible from her position. She was now by the coast, a stone’s throw from a beach. The sound of the crashing waves were somewhat calming, but that didn’t help quell her questions.

With the cat being unhelpful, she sent a message up to the Omen.

//Sally: My STAR is being screwy. Who returned?//Jackie: Just Edward.//Jackie: No sign of his dark passenger.

She gave a confused nod to the information and closed the window. It wasn’t surprising that Bully survived the crash, given he wasn’t too close and also in a reinforced bunker. Humphrey was still out there, somehow.

“What now, then?” she asked Archie. “The updated Map says there aren’t any Radoch groups close by. We’re kind of in the middle of the remaining two spaceships, just… away from the meat of the mainland.” Her stomach growled again.

“My suggestion is to sleep.”

The glare given in response told him what she thought of that idea.

“Personal Bubble,” the cat continued. “I know you don’t like it, but we can’t progress the main quest until morning, and fighting through the night will just get you killed.”

Sally ground her sharp teeth together before throwing her arms up in resignation. “Fine. Tomorrow we’ll go and get your magical whatever and then ditch this planet. We aren’t saving it, right?”

“Chuck’s priority is the world where Theo is.”

She gave him a nod and glared up at the dark sky. It was unlikely she could kill everything on this planet, but if she could bring down those other two vessels, it might strand the remaining Radochs here. Detonating the planet might also be a neat idea… but it had taken a lot out of her and the Outsiders to get this far. It was time to be realistic.

With a sigh, she gestured for him to put up the Personal Bubble.

While not as well-versed as in his spellcasting days, the cat had learned a couple of useful abilities for this kind of situation. As his influence was currently tied to the power generated by the Omen, the usage of these few abilities was rare and limited. But there was no better use-case than this for the Bubble.

As if pumping himself up like a balloon, Archie took a single deep breath. After a second of focus, a magical sphere of energy formed around the pair, blotting out the surroundings as if they were just ghostly suggestions. A small oil lamp hung from the top of the dome, illuminating a basic bed.

“Yurting just isn’t as fun without fangs,” Sally complained. With a grumble, she plopped herself down on the moderately comfortable single bed. “Do you know how he is doing?”

Archie trotted over to a small cat bed at the edge of the spherical space. “No complications, as far as I’m aware.”

The zombie lay down and tried to fluff up the pillow. “That just sounds like complications are yet to happen, then.”

He circled his bed several times before settling down. “You two being chaos magnets doesn’t have to be a constant truth.”

Sally smiled. “We know you’re wrong there. So, this power you’re trying to find… it’s something like our Aspect powers, isn’t it?”

The oil lamp dimmed, and the small chamber fell into silence for a few moments before Archie finally spoke.

“Similar, perhaps. While the powers you and Theo received from Thrimble were both thematically relevant to yourself and the situation there, an Aspect isn’t a given position necessarily. Theo being the Aspect of Death doesn’t mean he is the entire System’s version of Death.”

The zombie hummed in thought, closing her eyes as she got comfortable. “So we just happened to get lucky that Thrimble had a Life and Death demi-god, and Theo and I are aesthetically those two powers?”

“Luck isn’t quite the word. More like… chaos magnetism.” Archie closed his eyes. “You could think of it as an advanced, unique Class given to a select few.”

“Momma’s got moderator privileges,” came the murmured response.

No reply was given to that comment, and the dome fell into silence. By the coast, the bubble of invisible and untraceable energy shielded the pair as they got much-needed sleep.

Theo hummed to himself as he strolled through the early morning sunlight. He had said his goodbyes to the adventuring party that had hosted him while they were still bleary-eyed and half asleep. He had woken up early, and for good reason.

Today, he would find the heroes of this world and invite them to join Sanctuary.

How could they refuse? With any luck, he’d be back home before dinner. Surely Sally must have finished her mission by now and would be back around the same time. Realistically, he knew neither of those things were true.

There was something odd about this world that he couldn’t quite grasp yet. While Chuck wasn’t usually the sort to withhold information, there must be more to this quest than just adding a couple of powerful characters to the roster.

Plus, Sally would have her hands full for a while. Due to time dilation she might not even be home for days after chewing through her world. Either way, headquarters hadn’t been keeping him up to date with how the Outsiders were getting on. It was a pretty stacked party, though - how bad could it be?

Following instructions, he had been traveling east toward the main city. Apparently the heroes lived somewhere in the woods to the south of it, but often traveled here and there to perform magic shows. A novel concept, and something that intrigued Theo.

He paused and moved to the side of the road, giving a carriage a nod as it passed him.

It was interesting to be a visitor to a world that had been saved previously. Being an observer rather than a participant. He tilted his head and left the trail to enter the woods. If only every System was lucky enough to have Adventurer’s strong enough to fix errors and defeat those who sought to ruin the experience for everyone else.

Then again, perhaps it was partly luck. Nobody really chose to be transported to a System world, nor could they decide which one they landed on. Fewer seconds of hesitation and he might not have been taken alongside Sally. The thought was chilling.

He pushed through a set of bushes before circling the treeline. Yes, there it was. Almost like a cartoon creature being pulled along by the smell of a freshly baked pie, Theo could almost feel the thread of powerful individuals.

Hopefully, they didn’t try to fight him.

While his death wouldn’t be permanent, it meant a major setback in the recruitment plan. With a lump of excitement and apprehension, he pushed through the last of a group of trees, emerging into a clearing.

A small cottage sat up against a hill across from him. Two figures sat in a swinging chair out front, although they both paused as soon as he arrived. A man in a bright purple suit with messy brown hair, and a blonde elf in a softer blue suit.

The heroes of this world.

Grinning, the light hitting his fangs, Theo stepped forward to greet them.
