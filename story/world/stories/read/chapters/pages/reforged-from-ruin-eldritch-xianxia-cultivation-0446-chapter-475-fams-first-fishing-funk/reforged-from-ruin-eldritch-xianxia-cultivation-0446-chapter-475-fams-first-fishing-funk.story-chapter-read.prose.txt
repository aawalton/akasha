If you ever think of starting a clan, don’t.

It’s very lucrative. It can help secure your holdings and interests for a decent couple of centuries. It even, if you’re smart about it, can help you find some peace and quiet for a time.

But at the end of the day, it is just not worth the gods damned drama.

–Feng Jahao, a lesser noble of the Feng clan, speaking drunkenly to his fifth wife after the death of one of her sons at a half-brother’s hands. Whether or not he was aware of his audience is unclear.

_____________________________________________________________

The sun comes up like any other day. The writhing snakes of bright glowing flame circle across the world over and over again, in a cycle on and on, reaching back to the frozen north. A bundle of divine, god-like wyrms coil back together, rising up into the sky from the heat of their coupling.

Raika enjoys fishing.

It’s really not that hard, once you get a few pieces together. She made the rod out of a tree, which took some whittling, but it was old and dead and fallen, and not that heavy anyways. The fishing line she actually bartered for, paid for by helping with a garden and fixing a twisted ankle. Figuring out the mechanics of it is… mostly just practice. And it’s easy to practice. You put some meat on a little hook, throw it out into the water, and wait.

She’s got her own spot. Takes like, two hours to get to, which is annoying as hell, but that’s the thing about mortality and trapping yourself in it. If you’re going to commit, you have to commit, or else what’s the point?

If it can be done as a mortal, she is doing it as a mortal.

So, two hours and a long fucking walk after, she’s sitting on a log, overlooking the lake, with her replacement walking staff stabbed into the dirt, its line going far out into the water.

“And you do this every. Day.”

She laughs softly. “Absolutely not. That walk, at my speed? Terrifically annoying. Maybe twice a week, when I’m not busy with other stuff.”

“And how long do we have to stay?”

“As long as we feel like. You can head out whenever. I’m not stopping you.”

Geng He pretty much growls at that. But he also doesn’t leave.

His own fishing rod is much more boring than hers, mostly because he made it in like six minutes, and only barely skirting past her restriction on not allowing their cultivation. It’s an ugly thing, barely able to hold itself together, but that doesn’t make it any less technically useful for catching fish.

Rai Jin laughs at his grumbling, which really doesn’t help with all of Geng He’s simmering fury.

“It’s not so bad. A good moment to meditate. And the lake is full of things to watch. So much going around, eating and dying and living. Fish reproduce so weird.”

“It’s all relative, kiddo. They probably think we reproduce way more weirdly. If anything, they’re pretty goddamn neat about it.”

“Why are we talking about fishfucking?” Chen Tai asks, chewing on her bait.

“Why not?”

She pauses at that. Takes a minute to think about it. Works it around in her mind.

Good. She’s learning. Good lessons, all around. It’s always nice, seeing someone you’ve tried to teach take a lesson to heart.

“I don’t know why not,” she eventually says, swallowing whole the bait she’s been gnawing on.

“Do you not want to be talking about it?” Raika asks her.

“I don’t think I care. I just also don’t think I’m interested.”

“Would you like to talk about something you are interested in instead?”

“Yes.”

“And what would that be?”

She pauses at that. Takes a minute to think about it. Works it around in her mind.

“This cannot be how you intend to keep us while we’re here,” Geng He groans.

“Boy, it is not all surgeries and war all the damn time.”

“That’s not–”

He does stop himself before he goes all the way out into lying.

“I just… with all that’s going on, is this really the best thing you can have us doing, master?”

“Maybe not. But that’s what makes it the best thing.”

All three of her students currently present turn to look at her with that.

“It’s…” she sighs. “It’s hard to explain. I’ve gotten a bit of a feel for it, I guess. Optimizing just… isn’t optimal. Not really. It relies on the idea that we as people can be replaced by tools of ourselves. And that is, in fact, doable. Definitely a real and useful thing, especially if its use is breaking shit. But being a person is harder. It requires more. It’s more complicated than that.”

Something tugs at her fishing line. Not strong enough that she really has to bother with it, but enough to know she’s got a catch. Her rod stays planted in the dirt at the edge of the water, but she wraps a hand around the line and gives it a yank.

A fish comes flying out of the water, unfortunately snagged by her hook, and is pulled almost perfectly into her hand.

From there, it’s a relatively shorter jump over to Chen Tai’s sharp teeth, ready and waiting for a fresh catch.

“Next one you get, you’ll have to snag on your own,” she tells her disciple.

“Can I do it without the stick?”

“If you want,” Raika shrugs. “Just no cultivation.”

“Well… I am cultivation.”

“Wiser and more incorrect than you think, kid.”

“I’m older than you!”

“Only technically.”

“How old are you, master?” Geng He asks, interrupting to get the attention back on himself. Or on his desire to get stuff out of her. It’s kind of hard to tell with him.

“Pretty sure I’m around 37,” she tells him. “Give or take a bit. I think that if you stretch or squeeze space hard enough, it makes time sort of wobbly, and if you count every version of me separately then it’s a lot older, but that’s more-or-less right.”

Again, everyone turns to stare at her.

“What?”

“Well…” Jin says, slowly. “I always just sort of… assumed you were older? Just a bit. I thought you were, like, forty.”

“Forty? For hells sakes, she’s one of the strongest people in the world! I thought you were some ancient monster! An old master from another era! You mean to tell me you’re not even a decade older than me?”

Raika laughs. “You never asked, kid.”

Geng He throws down his fishing rod, obliterating it into shrapnel from sheer force. “This– you–”

He doesn’t flare his cultivation. He doesn’t even start cycling outside of his body. She doesn’t stop him.

“How did you get so strong?” he asks at last.

“Luck.”

She’s not sure what he expected her to say, but it wasn’t that. He’s just quiet for a moment, his eyes sort of wide, his breathing elevated. Jin and Chen Tai are both looking at him carefully, one of them ready to step in and defend her, the other simply with interest.

“That can’t be it.”

“Geng He–”

“That can’t be it. I don’t accept it. I–”

“Geng He.”

He hears what she has put into her voice well enough to shut the fuck up.

“I have suffered. I have persevered where others could not. I have been imaginative and bold and brave and self-sacrificing and more. And none of those things would have meant fuck all if I wasn’t lucky. There are only two things in this world that I know for sure decide these things. People, and consequences. People, mad and wonderful and horrible, and consequences for shit beyond our comprehension or our control. I got lucky. An inch to the metaphysical left and I’d have died instead.”

“And you’re pretty lucky yourself, junior brother,” Rai Jin chimes in.

Geng He whirls, his teeth clenched and his fists curled at his sides. “You don’t get to call me that. I will not have a child talk to me like I’m–”

Raika’s hand lightly pats him on the shoulder, resting there.

Geng He stops himself. Takes a long, slow breath. Exhales.

“I… apologise. I didn’t mean to…”

“Jin?”

Jin sighs, but he sets down his fishing rod, standing up to offer a polite bow. “I also apologize, fellow disciple. I caused offense I did not intend.”

Geng He takes a moment to process that, but eventually, he nods.

“I am also somewhat… oversensitive on the subject of seniority. I did not mean to react so strongly.”

Chen Tai, off to one side, is staring with rapt attention and visibly taking mental notes on the interaction.

“Good. Now that that’s settled, Geng He, you’ve lost your fishing rod. Would you perhaps like to go do something else for a bit?”

He has to take a moment, breathing in and out slowly, but eventually, he does manage to turn to her and offer a bow. Then, without another word to anyone, he starts walking off the way they came, back towards their house.

“I really didn’t mean to insult him, master,” Jin says, though she notices that he doesn’t say it particularly remorsefully.

“I know you didn’t, but you also don’t know him very well. It’s often better not to speak as an expert on someone if you’ve barely met them, nevermind gotten to know them. Not that that’s all your fault, the boy couldn’t share something if his life depended on it. Seriously, I’ve seen his life depend on it like four times and had to bail him out of at least three of those.”

Jin shakes his head, turning back to his fishing rod. Raika, however, starts wrapping her line around hers, pulling it up out of the ground to use as a walking stick on the way back.

“Master?”

“We might not be using Qi, but that was enough killing Intent I just showed to keep the fish away for a good while. You’re free to stay, if you want to wait for them. I have no more plans for today.”

Rai Jin hesitates, then nods. “I think I will stay. The lake is a beautiful view, and I’d like to meditate on my fellow disciple’s plight a bit.”

She nods. “Chen Tai? Do you mind coming with me instead of staying here?”

The beast-woman seems to think on the subject for a moment. Only after she’s certain does she give a sharp nod, standing up and leaving her fishing rod entirely abandoned in the dirt.

“I’ll see you at home later, Jin. I’ll be cooking dinner tonight. Try not to be late.”

He nods, bowing to her politely before he settles into a little crook of the tree he’s sitting against, staring out over the water.

She and Chen Tai walk for a while, taking a slower trek through the woods than Geng He did. It might still take her a good two hours to make it back to her cabin the normal way, but adding another few minutes is no big stress, and the view is beautiful.

The sky is clear and bright blue. The clouds drifting by are soft and while, their shadows dancing over the treetops, the wind that moves them moving the grass below them in tune. Raika knows that, a few minutes in one direction, she’ll find the edge of the farm lands and be able to see the people she can hear tilling the fields and finishing out the previous harvest.

Chen Tai walks alongside her, perfectly content to say nothing at all.

“So, what did you do back there?” Raika asks.

The beast-woman blinks, turning to look at her. “...Nothing? I sat and watched. And learned. And ate the fish. Do those count as nothing also?”

“They do not. But did you want to do something?”

Chen Tai’s head tilts back and forth as she mulls it over. “Not really. Geng He is strong, but he is also volatile. Not safe to share a cave with, and he is too hungry to share a true meal with. But brother Jin is too soft. He should have better stood his ground, even if his Apex was going to resolve it. That’s you.”

“I am aware, Chen Tai, but thank you for clarifying. Your language skills continue to improve.”

She smiles at that. “Thank you master! I practice all the time. Sometimes I even think in words now! It’s very strange. Much more specific but also sometimes so limited. And if I can’t find the right word then I just get stuck.”

“That happens to everybody, don’t worry too much about it.”

“Do you agree with me, master?”

Raika turns to look at her then, an eyebrow raised.

“On what part?”

“About my fellow disciples. The other parts of your pack.”

She tilts her head back and forth, imitating her student a bit. Funny how easy it is to pick up mannerisms when you’re not paying attention.

“I do agree somewhat. I do think that I stepped in a bit too quickly, but I don’t want anyone to cause any fights with each other that don’t have to happen. There’ll be time over the next few months to resolve things. I don’t love that Jin stayed back there, though. He’s a good kid, but I worry that he’s too isolated. Especially for his age, but in general, too. He’s too used to his own company, and that of those only he can see. I’d like him to confront more social situations directly.”

“Then you agree with me about fellow disciple Geng He?”

“Oh yes. Entirely.”

They go for a while in silence after that. It’s almost an hour before Chen Tai breaks it.

“Why?”

“Why what?”

“If I’m right, why have him in our pack?”

Raika sighs. “If I was going to pick only people without problems to try and make my disciples, I’d have very few options indeed. He’s my responsibility. I am part of what made him, makes him, the way that he is. He’s known power almost exclusively through pain and subjugation his whole life. I added to that, and I feel that I owe it to him to be better. And if I can help him be better, all the better for it.”

“That was a lot of ‘better’, master.”

“Sometimes language repeats itself a lot.”

“This is true, master.”

Another half hour or so in silence. By this point, the sun is high in the sky, making the shadows of the trees dance.

“Is that why I’m here, master? So you can help make me better?”

Raika stops. Chen Tai almost steps into her, it’s so abrupt, but steps around her instead, coming to face her directly.

Raika meets her eyes, her own warm and calm. “What do you think?”

“Yes.”

“Why do you think that?”

She pauses at that. Takes a minute to think about it. Works it around in her mind.

“Because you owe me?”

“That’s part of it. Why else?”

“...because I can tell that there are things missing.”

Raika nods. “And why else do you think I’d want to help you be better?”

A longer silence. Several minutes this time. Raika doesn’t interrupt, letting her newest protege think through the implications, the connections, the all-new pathways her mind can take.

“Because you want to make lots of things better,” her student eventually says.

Raika snorts. “Yeah. Wiser and more incorrect than you know, kid. Now come on. If you promise to keep your Qi close to your body, you can walk around more relaxed for a bit.”

Chen Tai lights up, smiling and giggling a bit. By the time Raika’s taken her next step forward, a long, tall, hard-to-glimpse thing has started following her, slithering and climbing through the hidden parts of the trees around her as they walk home.
