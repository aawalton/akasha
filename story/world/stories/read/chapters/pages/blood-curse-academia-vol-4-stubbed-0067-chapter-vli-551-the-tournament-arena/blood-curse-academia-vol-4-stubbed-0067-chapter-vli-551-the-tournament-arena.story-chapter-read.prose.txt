Chapter V.LI (5.51) - The Tournament Arena

“Excellent! I see you’ve chosen us seats ideal for spectating. Well done, son.”

Kizu sat in the tournament stands next to his father. He hadn’t given much thought to where they’d plopped down, but if his father wanted to read into it, Kizu wouldn’t stop him.

Today’s morning matches were the first batch of fights to kick-off the tournament as a whole. Neither he nor any of his closest friends were competing, so Kizu reluctantly took on the responsibility of attending alongside his father. He figured this time would hopefully fulfill his familial quota for the tournament while also helping him get a better grasp on the nuances of the arena. After all, one person he did know was meant to be battling in the third match of the day. His little brother, Finn.

“Last year, Finn achieved an adequate end result of 388,” his father said. “Not a number that stands out as particularly strong, I know. However, we must also take into account that he was the fourth highest first-year. Still, not top of the class, but adequate. His other class rankings throughout this last year have been steadily advancing, albeit not nearly as quickly as your own. I hope we can expect to see a performance that exceeds the expectations Finn set last year.”

“He does spend a lot of time studying,” Kizu replied noncommittally.

Having opted not to subject his niece, girlfriend, or other friends to his father’s presence, Kizu sat alone with the man.

In many ways, Kaga Kubou looked a lot like Kizu. He saw that same jaw and ears whenever he looked in the mirror. Obviously, his father’s hair was a stark black and eyes dark whereas Kizu’s was green and with golden eyes. However, even despite that, nobody would ever mistake the two of them for anything other than father and son.

While the first contenders stepped onto the field, his father listed and ranted about Finn’s academic and social failures. He explained in-depth how he had hoped his younger son might be able to take control of the accounting side of things at their family business while Kizu moved to a figurehead role. A position Kizu had no intention of ever taking but decided to not fight at the moment. Apparently, Kizu’s recent leap in numerology ranking had convinced his father that Finn was not even worth placing into accounting. He was asking Kizu’s opinion on where to shuffle his brother in the company. Kizu, having absolutely no idea how their family’s silk trading company functioned, simply tried to answer as vaguely as possible.

Arclight completed her announcement and stepped aside to allow the first two fighters to face one another. They bowed.

In a blink, the entire stadium’s layout shifted into something altogether new. Gone were the wooden planks Kizu had seen the mages placing down over the last few weeks as they built the arena. In their place was a field of yellow daisies.

Nobody had mentioned this aspect of the tournament fights to him. Kizu found himself on the edge of his seat, straining to examine the new stadium with his spellsense.

It appeared to mostly be nothing more than an illusion; however, when he focused on a creekbed that ran through the field’s center something felt a bit off. It took him half a minute to place it. The creek was connected to the academy outfits.

By the time he’d come to the conclusion, the two students, both of whom Kizu had never spoken to, were well into their battle. One having summoned a wobbly red monster that bobbed through the air with bat-like wings, while the other attempted to zap his way to victory with elemental magic.

Kizu watched as the spark of lightning dispersed into the creek. That wasn’t normal for an illusion. It immediately reacted to the electricity, as if real. Even more interesting, the opponent’s foot was in the creekbed when the jolt touched it. The electrical current in the water seemed to knock the summoner over.

“Is it… are the outfits enchanted in a way to respond to the illusionary threats as if they are real?” he muttered to himself.

“What was that?” his father asked. Kizu had mistakenly interrupted the man’s rambling about a trade deal with the summoner's family that had fallen through last year.

“Oh, nothing. I was just examining the illusions,” Kizu answered honestly.

His father nodded and leaned forward. “Good. You’re studying the terrain and examining your options. Plan for the future, but don’t become overly reliant on that plan. My mentor used to say, ‘Rain ruins routes.’ You never know what might be around the corner, so plan, but keep yourself flexible and adaptable for both tragedy and opportunity.”

Kizu blinked at the genuine advice from his father. Naturally, the man had to ruin it by continuing on.

“Finn is one who can’t ever change his plans. He is stubborn, which can be a good trait to have in an attack dog, not a leader. Unfortunately, Finn hasn’t excelled in places where his stubbornness would be a boon. He under performs, especially when placed beside your own achievements. He dims while you shine.”

“Finn is plenty strong on his own,” Kizu said in defense of his surly brother.

“Hm. I suppose we will see if there’s any weight behind your belief in him soon enough.”

The match ended with the summoner’s red, bat-winged creature unhinging its jaw and clamping down on the other student’s head. There was a sickening crunch as the skull cracked under the creature’s jaw strength and the victim fell over.

The stage flickered back into its original wooden platform.

Polite applause sounded from the stadium stands. Kizu added his own noise in with the others.

“Not the strongest competition,” Kizu’s father commented. “I would not hire either combatants as protectors for my shipments.”

Kizu held back from commenting that neither of these students had any interest in guarding crates of silk. He deemed it best to not even engage. That conversation would doubtlessly only end with him more irritated than he already was.

The second match had a familiar face. One of Kizu’s dormmates. He recognized the student as the boy he’d punched in the face on his very first day of school. He currently faced down a fourth-year girl.

“No one note-worthy,” his father said after examining them. “Both from uninteresting families with little to no political power. Their current rankings also don’t hint at any potential for social excellence beyond the academy.”

Despite his father’s dismissal of them, Kizu watched with interest. The girl had a belt with four different wands holstered into it. Arclight was verbally dismissive of wands, so he was surprised to see one of his peers utilizing one.

The terrain shifted as the match commenced. The wood was replaced by black grass speckled with tombstones. Kizu recalled his own experience fighting in a cemetery, back in the village in the Hon mountains. The hoards of zombies chasing after him and Aoi hadn’t been a particularly enjoyable encounter.

Nothing so dramatic occurred for the two combatants today. They used the tombstones as cover as they blasted one another with spells, but otherwise the scenery was barely more than flavoring.

Kizu’s dormmate turned out to be an earth elementalist mage. Unfortunately, while it acted identically in most ways, the ground under his feet wasn’t true earth, so he stuck the edges of the stadium, sending out bounders from out of bounds in the direction of his opponent.

The girl replied in turn with a rotating selection of elemental spells from her wands. Ice spikes, wind blades, and… Kizu blinked, the last one was spatial magic. Not anything particularly impressive, it was a simple form of telekinesis that he had recently been studying. He found the spell extremely easy after training with barriers under Wakino’s tutelage. Still, he’d never actually seen another student use spatial magic in combat before. She used that wand defensively, rerouting the projectiles her opponent sent her way. She kept that one up in her left hand while her right hand used either ice or wind offensively.

One of the bounders blasted apart into chunks as it slammed into a small mausoleum at the center of the stadium. The blast took out a portion of the wall, revealing a coffin in the middle of the room.

That physical contact convinced Kizu of something he’d been suspecting since seeing the electricity’s interaction with water.

“It’s more than an illusion. There are physical changes to the stadium,” he said to himself. While he wasn’t quite certain how they’d done it, that was invaluable information for his upcoming fight tomorrow. It also raised the question of whether or not someone like an earth elemental mage could, in fact, interact with the stone and dirt nearby. The opponent currently fighting clearly assumed not, as he stayed on the arena’s edge. But Kizu hadn’t seen him actually try to manipulate the arena floor. The electricity hit the water, so could a water mage have manipulated that stream? All those thoughts went through his mind as he watched the two of them exchange blows on the stadium below.

“Hm?” His father had gone silent this match, a pocketbook in his left hand seemingly absorbing all his attention. He didn’t even look up from it as he raised an eyebrow at Kizu’s commentary. His right hand was a blur as he dotted down notes.

Kizu glanced at it. The page was filled with flawless looping calligraphy and sums that numbered in the tens of thousands. He spotted a few family names on it that he recognized. One was struck through the middle, removed from the list. By the time Kizu figured out what his father was doing, the match concluded.

The girl with the wand had beaten up Kizu’s jerk of a dormmate, slamming his own stones into his face and beating down his rocky armor until there was an opening for an airslash that sliced open the lower half of his skull.

Arclight stepped forward and announced the match’s victor to the cheers of the crowd. Unlike Kizu’s father, the rest of the spectators roared louder than the first match, having clearly been far more satisfied by the skill on display in this competition.

“You’re categorizing all the students,” Kizu realized. “You’re finding out the strength of your peers and rivals’ children.”

“Correct. Astute observation.” His father turned the book, offering Kizu a better look at the notes within. “Keep your mind sharp and watch your colleagues. Take into account that any of your peers today might be rivals tomorrow, regardless of your current friendship. Friends are a means to an end. They will let you down in the end.”

“Like with Jeri?” Kizu asked flatly.

“Jeri?” His father frowned and leaned back in his seat. “Do you mean the gnome? Interesting. You’ve been in contact with him? He used to be one of the most well connected gnomes in Ilosin-Don. However, when it fell he had next to nothing. I offer him no ill will, but I also see nothing to be gained by offering him anything else.”

Kizu wanted to snap back. To argue with the callousness of his father’s world view. Arclight’s booming voice from the stage interrupted him.

She announced the next contestants.

It was time for Finn’s first battle.
