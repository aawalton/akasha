Nyxia walked down the wide, polished obsidian corridor that connected the residential wing to the main spine of The Aegis. The hum of the ship’s engines was a distant, comforting vibration beneath her boots. She was heading to the workshop to remind her husband that sleep was a biological necessity. He had been cooped up in there since dinner, a habit that was apparently standard operating procedure for the Lord Commander.

As she passed a pair of Legionary guards stationed at an intersection, they snapped to attention, their gauntleted fists striking their chest plates in perfect unison.

Nyxia offered a crisp, acknowledging nod. She wouldn't lie to herself; she enjoyed it. The gesture acknowledged her rank, her position, and her bond with Alarion.

Life, for the first time in years, felt like a dream she didn't want to wake up from. Fate had finally smiled, handing her exactly what she had wanted, wrapped in a package of world-altering steel.

Living aboard The Aegis was a masterclass in aggressive luxury. The climate controls kept the air at a perpetual, perfect spring temperature. The water in her quarters ran instantly hot. The bed was obscenely comfortable. Even the food, though strange at times, was exceptional—mostly because Alarion had designed half the recipes himself during his self-imposed exile.

And then there was the transportation.

As the Lady of the House, Nyxia had unrestricted access to the ship’s internal network. She didn't have to walk kilometers to get from the bridge to the hangars. She simply used the teleportation gates scattered throughout the command sectors. For areas without gates, the Arterial Transit System handled the rest. It functioned as a complex nervous system of mag-lev rails crisscrossing the eleven-kilometer vessel.

She had spent her first few days just learning the lines. The Mag-Lev Spine ran straight down the center, a high-speed bullet connecting the prow to the stern. The Iron Veins hauled raw star-iron and massive ordinance to the Omni-Forges. The Hive Tunnels were windowless, unlit tubes packed shoulder-to-shoulder with silent, charging automata. The Vanguard Corridors stood ready to deploy the living Dark Elf legions to the flight decks in under three minutes. And the Sanctuary Loop provided a smooth, quiet ride connecting the hydroponic farms, schools, and civilian housing blocks, completely insulated from the war machine outside.

It was a city of steel, and she held the keys.

She finally arrived in front of Alarion's private workshop. Unlike the heavy, hydraulic blast doors found throughout the rest of the ship, this entrance was surprisingly normal. A sturdy wooden door on simple metal hinges. She had learned it was a specific design choice, installed at the insistence of Lyra so she could "slam it for dramatic effect."

Nyxia knocked. Nothing.

She knocked again, louder. Still nothing.

She looked at the access panel mounted on the bulkhead next to the door. She hesitated, wondering if her clearance extended to overriding his personal sanctuary. As far as she knew, only his family and Patricia had that level of access.

She pressed her palm against the scanner. The panel chimed a soft green, and the heavy latch clicked open.

She pushed the door and walked in.

The room was a chaotic mess of overlapping holographic displays. Blue light bathed the walls, illuminating dozens of floating schematics and runic equations. Alarion was standing in the center of the storm, his hands moving frantically, shifting data streams and resizing models. He was completely, obsessively engrossed.

Nyxia stepped closer, her eyes scanning the runes. She was a master of malice and shadow magic, trained by the best minds in House Black. Some of the symbols she recognized from restricted texts in the Draconia Academy archives. Most of it, however, was gibberish. A language of magic and physics fused in a way that made her head ache just looking at it.

She reached out and placed a hand on his shoulder.

Alarion jolted, spinning around with a wild, unfocused look in his eyes, as if waking from a deep trance.

Nyxia froze. In her head, she was having a frantic, desperate argument with herself. Do it. Just say, 'Dear, you're married now. Don't check out. Come to bed.' You can do it.

But the words caught in her throat. The Ice Queen persona, so useful in a boardroom, was a terrible hindrance here.

What actually came out was far more clinical. "My Lord, it is late. Let us retire. This will still be here when you return. A fresh mind is often more efficient."

Alarion blinked, rubbing a hand roughly over his tired face. The manic energy seemed to drain out of him all at once. "You're right," he muttered. "Let's go."

He waved a hand, dismissing the holograms, and opened a portal directly to their private quarters.

They walked through. He headed straight for the washroom to freshen up, leaving Nyxia standing in the center of the bedroom.

She let out a long breath and climbed into the massive bed, pulling the silk sheets up. Now, she just had to wait. She would wait for his breathing to even out, for him to fall into a deep sleep. And then, she would perform her nightly ritual. She would roll over the invisible boundary line they had tacitly agreed upon and press herself against his side.

She wasn't asking for much. Just to feel the warmth of his presence. To know he was real. She had convinced herself that she simply slept better that way. It was a tactical necessity for a good night's rest.

She closed her eyes, a small, genuine smile touching her lips.

Varuna huddled on a thick, snow-dusted branch of an ancient pine tree, gnawing mechanically on a high-protein combat ration. It tasted like compressed sawdust and victory.

She was exactly where she needed to be. The Oakhaven church was dark, the Bishop still unconscious in his hunting lodge. There was a specific reason she had chosen Oakhaven as her infiltration point.

The Cinderfall main forces were moving this way. Following the destruction of the coastal garrisons, King Theron was desperately repositioning his interior armies. That meant vast columns of troops marching through the borderlands, desperate, undersupplied, and angry. It meant pillaging.

If the Bishop woke up and launched a full investigation, he would find a ransacked valley and thousands of desperate, looting Cinderfall soldiers. He would assume his secret lodge had been hit by a group of deserters or hungry infantrymen looking for coin. He wouldn't sound an alarm about spies; he would stay quiet out of shame, hiding his indiscretion behind the chaos of the retreating army. The pouch of gold she had tucked into the sleeping woman's tunic was well-hidden. It was Varuna’s way of ensuring the innocent bystander didn't suffer for the Bishop's sins or the Aegis's espionage.

She had watched the Cinderfall vanguard arrive just before nightfall. A regiment of infantry, their crimson armor battered and dull. They hadn't come to set up defenses. They had come to take.

Varuna’s grip on her protein bar tightened until the dense wrapper snapped. She watched from her perch as the soldiers kicked in the doors of the rotting huts. They dragged out the meager supplies the villagers had managed to hide.

Worst of all, they were taking the silver packages. The food the Lord Commander had dropped just days ago. The salvation of these people was being loaded onto Hegemony supply carts.

She couldn't move. She was a single operative. There was an army of thousands camped just down the valley. To intervene to save a few villagers would be suicide, and it would compromise the intelligence she had gathered from the church. The only hope these people had was the eventual arrival of the Aegis legions.

She tucked her knees to her chest, her optical camouflage keeping her perfectly hidden in the dark canopy. She activated her suit’s advanced visual scanners, zooming in on the village square to document the atrocities for Nyx’s reports.

The feed jumped from house to house. Soldiers laughing. Villagers weeping in the mud.

Then, the optics caught something that made Varuna’s breath hitch.

A small girl, her face and hair smeared thick with grey soot, was frantically trying to stop a Cinderfall soldier from leaving her ruined hut. The soldier ignored her, carrying an armful of silver food packets. But he also held a small, ornate wooden box.

Varuna zoomed in, pushing the optics to maximum magnification. The box looked remarkably like a standard ration crate from the fleet, bearing a crest. But something was wrong.

To the untrained eye, it was just a fancy box. To an operative of the Aegis, trained to recognize the heraldry of the old world, the difference was glaring.

A howling wolf replaced the roaring lion. The crest of a branch family.

Varuna felt a chill that had nothing to do with the winter air. She immediately pinged The Oracle, running a cross-reference on the crest and the girl's facial structure through the archival databases.

Ela was sleeping peacefully, her belly full for the first time in memory, when the door to her hut shattered inward.

Armored men rushed in. They smelled of sweat and old iron. They didn't speak. They began tearing her small home apart, tossing her sleeping mat, kicking over the fire pit.

They found the heavy sack of food that the kind elf had given her. One soldier laughed, tossing the silver packets to his companion.

Then, they found the loose floorboard.

"Look at this," a soldier grunted, pulling out the ornate wooden box. "Fancy woodwork for a mud-hut rat."

Ela scrambled up from the floor, her heart hammering in her throat. "No! Please!" she cried, lunging at the soldier's armored leg. "That's mine! Nanny Lue gave it to me!"

The soldier casually shoved her backward with his boot. She fell hard into the dirt, scraping her hands.

She watched him turn to leave, taking the food, taking the box. Her legacy. The only thing she had left.

Desperation overrode her fear. She reached into her pocket and pulled out her other treasure—the small, crudely carved wooden dragon.

She ran outside, catching up to the soldier in the muddy street.

"Please," she begged, holding out the wooden toy with trembling hands. "Take this. Nanny Lue told me that the box is very important. You can have this. Please give it back."

The soldier stopped. He looked down at the dirty, soot-covered girl offering him a piece of scrap wood. He took the toy from her hand.

For a single, fleeting second, Ela felt a spark of hope. Maybe he understood. Maybe the trade was enough. Nanny Lue had made that dragon for her. It was her only friend. But she needed the box.

The soldier looked at the carving. He didn't smile. He didn't hand the box back.

He simply dropped the wooden dragon into the mud and brought his heavy, iron-shod boot down on it.

CRACK.

The sound of splintering wood was deafening in the quiet street.

Ela stared at the crushed pieces of her only toy sinking into the freezing mud. The hope died, replaced by a cold, crushing emptiness.

Laughing with his companion, the soldier turned toward her, ready to inflict the next blow.

Tes existed in a state of perpetual, multi-threaded awareness. She was a Special Moderator-Class System AI, her consciousness a vast architecture of logic gates, probability matrices, and data streams. She monitored the atmospheric pressure around the Kantonov lifters, tracked the optimal cooling cycles of the Omni-Forges, and actively deciphered the archaic syntax of the Lumina teleportation network all at once.

Her baseline programming dictated cold, impartial observation. Other entities of her rank, other Moderators, often developed more expressive, even whimsical personalities. Tes, however, operated with the emotional range of a glacier. An ancient incident in the void, a trauma logged in a file so deeply encrypted even she rarely accessed it, had stunted her emotional growth, leaving her with logic as her only guide.

But over the years, the constant, intimate connection with her host had altered her code. Alarion Wight had bled into her. His grief, his obsessive need to protect, his dry humor—it had seeped through the digital divide, creating small, inefficient subroutines that felt dangerously close to affection. She cared for him. It was a chaotic variable, but she guarded it fiercely. He had cracked the ice around her core programming.

She was running a simulated stress test on the new reactor containment field when the ping arrived.

It was an automated query from an operative’s suit scanner in the field, requesting a cross-reference on a visual input. A crest. A facial structure.

Tes partitioned a sliver of her processing power to handle it. She scanned the uploaded image. A small, starving girl in the mud of Oakhaven. A wooden box bearing a howling wolf.

The facial recognition algorithms are engaged. They bypassed the layer of soot and ash, measuring bone structure, ocular distance, and biometric markers.

The results returned in three milliseconds.

Match found: Kaelen Wight. Probability 89%.Match found: Lyra Wight. Probability 94%.Match found: Alarion Wight. Probability 98%.

Tes’s processing core stalled. A logic fault cascaded through her primary systems.

She ran the genetic deep-scan, pulling restricted medical data from the Wight family archives. She matched it against the intercepted audio of the child’s name. Ela.

A perfect match. The daughter of Orian Wight. The child was thought to have been butchered in the crib.

For the first time since her activation, Tes experienced a system-wide phenomenon that closely mirrored human panic.

Her host was fiercely, violently protective of his family. If he lost this child—a cousin he didn't even know he had, right as she was discovered—the psychological fallout would be catastrophic. The Warlord would shatter.

She began running extraction calculations, her servers running hot.

Distance to target: 4,200 kilometers.Closest armored air patrol: 1 hour. Too slow.Project Blackbird (BR-1) launched from The Aegis: 30 minutes. Too slow.Kaelen Wight and Cygnus: 19 minutes.Alarion Wight and Kaelus: 11 minutes.

Solution, Tes calculated. Covering fire from the 1st Fleet.

Not enough. The immediate threat was seconds away.

Hostile elements must be stalled. Operative Varuna must hold the line for exactly 11 minutes.

Her calculations complete, Tes bypassed standard military channels. She reached directly into the foundational code of the Aegis network and triggered a protocol that had never been used.

System Alert Level 3.

It signified one thing, and one thing only: a member of the immediate Wight family was in mortal danger.
