Nyx Rexius

Why was I spendin’ my time babysittin’ these half-ass excuses for assassins when I should be huntin’ down Boro-gin?! There has been a delay in Khunum’s plague of parasites—not enough bodies were droppin’. Champions needed to die, and I was here gettin’ reports of them hiding! So what if they were too scared to enlist in the tournament? The last thing I needed right now was loose ends, and if they ain’t dead or turned into those nasty ghouls, then there was still a chance of them becoming contenders in my tournament! And damn it, I wouldn’t allow it!

I kicked in the door to Zeverus’ study and was immediately intercepted by one of his brainless orc pets. He raised his axe at me, and my warlock swung his scepter just as fast. One moment, he was a towering green hot-headed nuisance, and the next, a pile of ash on the polished floor.

I leaped over his remains with Oth following close behind me. There he was, that white-suit pretty-boy jackass over there, leanin’ against the doorway of his balcony, looking out into the night’s sky from his mansion with that cheap grin on his smug face.

He swirled his red drink in his wine glass and said, “Unfortunate. El’knagn was one of my better men.”

“Then ya should’a put a fuckin’ leash on em!” I hissed. “Now, what is this I hear about released captives? You ain’t know how to do your job right?”

“Forgive me,” he sang, “I wasn’t aware that I was part of your jurisdiction? Speaking of,” he continued, turning his eyes slowly to me. “How did you find me?”

“Your boss! And guess who his boss is? Me! So as far as I’m concerned, you work for me too!” I snarled, stompin’ across his fancy marble floor. “And my jurisdiction extends wherever the hell I say it does!”

Zeverus took a slow sip of his wine, completely unbothered by my rage. That only pissed me off more. “Ah, but you see, there’s a hierarchy to consider. While it’s true that we both serve the same... ultimate purpose, I report directly to—”

“I don’t give a rat’s ass who you report to!” I cut him off. “It’s my job to make sure everythin’s runnin’ smoothly! We got runners out there, and you’re spendin’ my time playing with your fae toys! Collectin’ em and pullin’ them apart’n’back together again! Now, look where it’s gotten ya! You manage to snatch a few high-leveled fae, I’ll give you that. But what the fuck does your sick flesh fetish have anything to do with the TOC?!” I growled. “Put your toys away and get to fuckin’ work!”

Zeverus set his glass down on the marble balustrade, his smile never wavering. “My dear, crude friend, you misunderstand my methods. Those ‘toys’ as you so eloquently put it, serve a very specific purpose.” He gestured toward the middle of his study. “Tell me, how many champions have you personally eliminated this week?”

“Boy, you better stay in your lane before I—”

“Seven,” he interrupted. “I’ve eliminated seven. Three of which turned into very useful assets.” The tip of his middle finger and thumb began to glow red, and then he snapped those fingers. A rune suddenly appeared on the floor in front of him, and within it, somethin’ materialized. “Behold, the fruit of my labor.”

A hulking figure emerged from the rune. Looked like a damn zombie. Its flesh was a sickly gray, and its veins pulsin’ with that same red glow as Zeverus’ damn fingertips. The thing’s eyes were milky white, and it moved with jerky, unnatural motions.

“This one was a particularly stubborn elf ranger,” Zeverus said, circling his creation like a proud artist. “Samatres Lewers, Level 180, if I recall correctly. Now he’s completely obedient and twice as deadly. Souls, dear overlord, are a powerful tool. One soul per vessel, but when you compartmentalize two magically affirmed souls into a vessel, well, things get interesting fast. You first weaken their willpower, strip them of their identities. Separating the spirit from the body is a delicate process.”

“I don’t need a fuckin' lecture on your twisted experiments!” I spat, though I couldn’t help but eye the thing with interest. It did look like one of those ghouls Khunum’s been churning out. But this was somethin’ else entirely. Its power was intense.

“Torment, torture, infusing hopelessness with worthlessness. And then, once two spirits are broken, you reintroduce a fragment of the second soul into the vessel, little by little. In grains, gradually and ever so meticulously. Why two? Because, why not?” He snapped his fingers again, and the creature dropped to one knee, its head bowed to its master. “You have two beaten souls with little willpower in constant conflict with each other, making them even more malleable. Neither can assert dominance, so they default to absolute submission.” His smirk grew wider. “If you can make a vessel stronger, why wouldn’t you? This process can only work for fae, creatures who are far stronger and resilient than their counterparts. And what you yield is a repurposed, reprogrammed assassin with the combined abilities of two former champions. Much more efficient than those blood-thirsty mindless ghouls.”

I stared at the grotesque thing kneelin’ before him, its gray flesh twitchin' like it was barely held together. The stench comin’ off it was worse than a week-old corpse left in the sun. “You sick bastard,” I muttered, but I had to admit—the power radiatin’ from that abomination was impressive. Still didn’t change the fact that he was screwin' with my timeline. “Ya usurpin’ the king? Does he know you’re over here makin’ mutants?”

“He is well aware, and feels the same about my advances. He sees them as a waste of time, but I am a man of opportunity, overlord. Sure, we can kill the champions. We could make them sick and turn them into slaves. But why not take it a step further? These are still prototypes, stability issues and physical appearances, but when I’m through with them, they’ll be ultimate weapons suitable for purchase. Or much more….”

“And how long does this little science project of yours take?” I growled, pacin’ around his pet monster. The thing’s head tracked my movement, but it stayed perfectly still otherwise. “Because while you’re playin’ Dr. Frankenstein, I got champions in hidin’! Not only that, your little operation here is fallin’ through the cracks! Those guinea pigs you had brought in here recently fled your cells. If you’d killed them as instructed, we’d have less champions to worry about!”

“Oh, I’ve taken care of the high ranks. They were the first to be processed. You’re overly concerned with my efficiency, and I promise you, that has never been an issue. Those who have escaped are low-leveled pests. Barely worth processing, let alone worrying about.” Zeverus waved his hand dismissively and returned to his wine glass. “The stronger the original vessel, the more powerful the final product. I don’t waste resources on anything less.”

“You pride yourself on efficiency, but one fact remains—you lost bodies. If you’re so good at what you do, how the hell did a bunch of weakened fae slip you?”

“Unforeseen complications. A meddling party located my hidden home, much like yourself. Hmm, I should consider moving. Two uninvited visits in one night, can’t be a coincidence….”

“Meddlin’ party?”

“A man and his dragon. They managed to break in and kill my guards, transport my prisoners, and turn one of my slaves against me.” He took a sip and leaned his back against the doorway casually with a hand in his pocket. “Nolan Black and the dracuna, Freya.”

Freya? Now, why did that name sound familiar?

“I fed them to the mouth of a tier five dungeon, one of the hardest ones to raid. Success rate was below 4%, and they survived it. All of them.

“Fuckin’ hell,” I muttered, feelin' my blood pressure spike. That name—Freya—it was scratchin’ at somethin’ in the back of my skull like a damn parasite. “You tellin’ me some random asshole and his pet lizard waltzed in here and made you look like a fool? Did ya snag them at least?!”

“I didn’t. They…escaped.”

“Escaped, huh?” I repeated. He ain’t seem so bummed out about that fact. The asshole was hidin’ somethin’. He had that look on em that triggered me. I grabbed him by his white collar and slammed him against the balcony railing. “What ain't you tellin’ me, maggot lover?!”

His wine glass shattered on the marble below, blood and chunks of brain spreadin’ on the floor. But that smug grin never left his face. He raised his hands up and chuckled. “Careful now, overlord. You wouldn’t want to damage one of your most... productive assets.”

“Answer the fuckin’ question!”

I felt his slave shift from the right of me, which normally, wasn’t a problem. If his Frankenstein felt the need to leap, my warlock would answer back, except Oth was out of commission—the giant was out cold, standing there with his head slung to the side like he was sleepin.’

“What does a bunch of blood-thirsty, single-minded thralls have compared to an army of champion-ready units? Nothing. You’re thinking too small. Yes, I run a profitable organ harvesting business, but I also run a much more lucrative operation. Information, overlord. Those champions I capture? They don’t just give me their bodies—they give me their souls, their secrets, and their connections, leading to more captives. Do you understand?”

I loosened my hold just enough to let him breathe proper. “Keep talkin’.”

“My next hit, the Crimson Wastes. There’s an old underground sanctuary there, and an old guild mate of the ranger is hiding there. He and his family, with a few refugees, will be sitting there, unaware of my arrival. And the interesting part—this guild mate happens to be a former tournament champion. Level 195, retired, but still very much a threat if he decides to come out of hiding.”

I was speechless for a moment, considering the efficiency of his flesh fetish.

“You see, overlord, I broke his mind.” Zeverus’ eyes gleamed with satisfaction. “The beauty of my process isn’t just the end product—it’s everything I learn getting there. Pain has a way of loosening even the most loyal tongue. By the time I’m done with them, they’ve told me everything about everyone they’ve ever known.”

I let go of his collar and stepped back, eyein’ his zombie slave, and then turned back to him. “And the dragon child? This Freya? Why’s that name ticklin’ my brain?”

Zeverus straightened his suit, lookin’ way too pleased with himself. “Beats me. So, are we still obsessing over minuscule details? Calling the prisoners’ escape a setback would be an exaggeration, wouldn’t it?”

“Well, there is no point cryin’ over some spilled milk.”

“Exactly my point. Now, shall we discuss my next operation? The Crimson Wastes raid will yield significant results.”

“Right, right. After you bring back my warlock over there.”

“Certainly.” He cast a look over to Oth, and the brute snapped out of it. Bewildered, but not bewitched.

“Just tell me one thing,” I said, narrowin’ my eyes at him. “This Nolan Black and his dragon—they a threat to the tournament?”

Zeverus adjusted his cufflinks, takin’ his sweet time answerin’ my question. “No. The man is a low-ranked dungeon raider—a mid-level adventurer with delusions of heroism.”

“Then how the hell did he escape a tier five dungeon?”

“He had the power of a dragon, very skillful lizard mage, and luck. The only reason he survived, truthfully.”

“You find them,” I instructed sternly. “And when you do, you bring me their heads. I don’t care if they’re small fry—anyone who can waltz into one of our operations and walk away breathin' after raisin’ such a ruckus is a problem waitin’ to happen.”

“Understood.”
