Dawn broke over the jagged peaks, painting the mountains in hues of deep gold and amber. The late summer sun glinted off sheets of ice and snow that clung stubbornly to the rocky outcrops, while the valleys below showed the first hints of autumn's approach—scattered splashes of russet and gold among the evergreens.

After their dramatic escape through the mountain's heart, days of constant vigilance, and swift travel, several days had passed. With each kilometer, the pull of Ziemrot's earth magic had faded, giving Aira confidence that Khanorrh's reach couldn't extend this far. Now, she stood at the edge of their makeshift viewing point, surveying the vast expanse before them.

Al perched on Aira's shoulder, his tiny nose twitching as he sniffed the thin mountain air. Behind them, Lila and Alliot finished dismantling the minimal camp they'd established not for rest, but for orientation and planning.

"Bones and bark, it's still bitter cold up here," Lila muttered, adjusting her cloak against the mountain chill. "How much farther do we have to go?"

Alliot glanced up at the brightening sky, calculating. "Hard to say. We left Ziemrot without proper bearings. The mountains distort distances."

Aira closed her eyes, extending her senses outward. The Splinter of the Worldroot pulsed faintly in her pocket, still resonating with the earth element they were leaving behind. "I can't sense Lamcada yet," she admitted. "We're navigating blind until we get closer."

As they descended a particularly steep section of the trail from their vantage point, Aira broke the contemplative silence. "Something's been bothering me since we left."

"Just one thing?" Lila quipped, carefully navigating around a patch of loose scree.

Aira smiled. "Khanorrh's way of speaking—did you notice how similar it was to your expressions? All those references to roots and mountains..."

Lila stopped so abruptly that Alliot nearly collided with her. "By the twisted branches, you're not suggesting I talk like those stone-stubborn enlightened, are you?" Her face contorted with incredulity. "That's torch-blind ridiculous!"

"But where did you pick up those phrases?" Aira pressed gently. "They're not common in Mountain View, are they?"

The question hung in the air as Lila's indignation slowly gave way to thoughtfulness. "My grandfather," she finally admitted, her voice softening. "He was a ranger before me—traveled farther than anyone else in our village. All those sayings... they were his first."

"Your grandfather's bag," Alliot spoke up. "You took it from your house before you left Mountain View."

Lila's eyes widened. "Root me sideways, you're right! With all these adventures, I haven't even properly looked through it yet."

***

As evening approached, they established a temporary outpost for a brief meditation session in the shelter of a massive boulder that blocked the worst of the mountain wind. While Aira practiced manipulating small stones with her newly enhanced earth affinity, Lila finally emptied the contents of her grandfather's weathered leather satchel onto the ground between them.

"Let's see what the old fox was hiding," she murmured, fingers reverently touching each item as she laid them out.

A brass compass with a cracked face. A bundle of dried herbs tied with twine. A small knife with intricate carvings on the bone handle. Several folded papers, yellowed with age. And at the bottom, wrapped carefully in oilcloth, a leather-bound book.

Lila unwrapped it slowly as if handling something sacred. The binding was worn smooth at the edges, and the pages were wavy from humidity and age. A faint smell of pine resin and smoke rose from the book as she carefully opened the cover. Her fingers traced the first page with reverence before her expression shifted to puzzlement.

"Cold as the cliffs, I can't read this," she said, turning the book toward Aira. "Now I remember why I put it aside so many seasons ago. It was useless!"

The pages were filled with tight, precise handwriting in a script neither Lila nor Aira recognized—angular symbols arranged in neat rows, occasionally interrupted by rough sketches of landscapes, plants, and what appeared to be maps. Even the archives of the ancient facility didn't have documents with a similar script.

Some pages had dried flowers or leaves pressed between them, preserved despite the years. In the margins, small drawings that might have been runes or symbols were scattered like afterthoughts, their meaning as obscure as the text itself.

Alliot, who had been keeping watch nearby, approached and peered over Lila's shoulder. His eyes widened, and he carefully took the book from her hands.

"This is one of the Old Tongues," he said, his voice hushed with surprise. "The language of the humans who lived near Ziemrot before the Dawn of Enlightenment."

"You can read it?" Aira asked.

Alliot nodded slowly. "Some. In Wentouk, we preserve knowledge of all the ancient languages. But this dialect... It feels strange."

"So my grandfather..." Lila began, her voice trailing off as implications settled over her like falling snow.

"Had connections to Ziemrot," Alliot finished. "Or at least to its human neighbors."

As they repacked the satchel, Alliot hesitantly reached for the diary. "I can try to translate more of this once we've settled in Lamcada," he offered. "It might give us insights into your grandfather's connections."

Lila nodded, her expression thoughtful as she secured the bag. "Assuming we can even find Lamcada. But I'll keep it for now. How far are we?"

Aira closed her eyes, concentrating. The Splinter of the Worldroot pulsed faintly in her pocket but beyond it... nothing. There was no connection through the Rune. No energy signatures that would hint at a large congregation of enlightened people were visible through her Energy Manipulation skill.

"I should be able to sense their Source from about 150 kilometers," Aira said, opening her eyes with a frown. "But there's nothing yet. We're still out of range."

"Then we keep moving south," Alliot said. "And hope we don't stray too far east or west."

"If only I could boost my range," Aira murmured, more to herself than the others. "With my Energy Manipulation at level ten, I could probably sense it from twice the distance. But I'd need three thousand skill points for that upgrade!"

"And there's no convenient dungeon full of monsters to slay in our world for quick XP," Lila quipped, then paused at Aira's surprised expression. "What? I've been paying attention to how your System works."

Aira couldn't help but smile. "You're right. No monster hunting here. Just the long way—finding the city, connecting with the Source, completing the quest." She sighed. "One step at a time."

***

Their journey southward proved more challenging than any of them had anticipated. The mountains surrounding Ziemrot formed a natural barrier of serrated peaks and treacherous passes. Each day brought new obstacles: narrow ledges where a single misstep meant certain death; unexpected snowfields that had to be carefully traversed despite the late summer season; valleys so deep that descending and climbing back up added days to their journey.

As the first week passed, the landscape gradually began to change. The barren, rocky terrain gave way to alpine meadows dotted with hardy wildflowers. The air grew warmer as they lost elevation, though nights remained bitterly cold.

"At least the view is spectacular," Lila remarked one evening as they paused on a rocky outcrop. The setting sun painted the mountain range in hues of orange and gold, shadows stretching like dark fingers across the valleys below.

"Spectacular, but not easy," Alliot replied, surveying the terrain ahead. "We need to keep our wits about us. However, once we fully descend, it should be easier to navigate. Lamcada is supposedly directly south of here."

Aira nodded, her eyes scanning the horizon. "We should stay vigilant. I can't sense any pursuers from Ziemrot, but there's a chance Khanorrh might have ways to mask his people."

Melting snow fed roaring mountain streams that carved their way through the landscape—their glacial waters so cold they burned to touch, their currents strong enough to sweep away boulders. The constant thunder of water against stone filled their ears as they detoured for miles in search of crossings, the spray beading on their skin and clothes like tiny diamonds that evaporated in the strengthening sun.

"We could backtrack to find a narrower section," Alliot suggested, studying the threatening current.

Lila shook her head. "Backtracking means losing a day, maybe two." She pointed to a massive fallen pine that stretched nearly across the water. "Nature's bridge, right there."

The log crossing proved treacherous. Halfway across, the bark beneath Aira's foot peeled away, sending her scrambling for balance as the roaring water beckoned below. Only Lila's quick reflexes—grabbing Aira's cloak just as she tipped sideways—prevented disaster.

"Careful," Lila warned, steadying her friend. "This river's got grit in its teeth."

At night, while they didn't need to rest, they would often pause to reorient themselves by the stars, ensuring they maintained their southward heading. These moments of stillness provided opportunities for conversation and planning, strengthening the bonds between them despite the challenges they faced.

During one of their evening pauses, as Alliot consulted the stars to confirm their route, Aira turned to Lila with curiosity in her eyes.

"I've been thinking about what happened back in Ziemrot," she said quietly. "How I could suddenly hear what Khanorrh was saying to you, even though he wasn't addressing me directly."

Lila nodded, her brow furrowing. "Cold as the cliffs, that was strange. I didn't do anything different—at least, not that I noticed."

"I think it might be connected to this." Aira gestured between them. "The 'build a bond' aspect of my Two is a Crowd skill. It's like we've established some kind of deeper connection that's letting us share more than just basic party chat."

"Like what?" Lila leaned forward, intrigued. "You mean you're learning my grandfather's catchphrases?" She smirked.

"More than that," Aira said, her expression thoughtful. "I think we might be able to share aspects of our skills. Limited access to each other's abilities."

Lila's eyes widened. "You mean I could use your Energy Manipulation?"

"Not fully," Aira clarified. "But maybe elements of it, filtered through your own abilities and traits. And vice versa. Though your level would be a limiting factor."

"Sharp as thorns, let's test it," Lila suggested, her eyes brightening with excitement. "What if I tried to sense energy flows? That's your specialty."

They sat facing each other, knees almost touching. Aira guided Lila through a focusing exercise, then gently extended her awareness through their bond, allowing a trickle of her Energy Manipulation skill to flow toward Lila.

For a moment, nothing happened. Then Lila gasped, her eyes flying open.

"I can see it," she whispered, wonder in her voice. "Not like you described before—not the full range or detail. But there's... something. Like shadows and light playing around you. Around Al too."

"And I can sense some of your Peacemaker ability," Aira said with surprise. "Not enough to actually calm someone, but I can... feel the potential of it. Like knowing where to push to diffuse tension."

"By the twisted branches," Lila murmured. "So this bond—it's not just communication. It's actually changing how our skills work? Making them... different?"

"Not changing them exactly," Aira said. "More like... blending aspects. Creating new potential." She looked thoughtful. "I think there's much more to explore here. And I can't help wondering..."

"What?"

"If I can bond with Alliot too," Aira said quietly. "And what kind of shared abilities that might create."

Lila's expression turned serious. "Just be careful who you bond with, Aira. Watch the shadows. The closer we get to the realization of Ainorrh's plan, whatever it is, the more I realize we need to be selective about who we trust completely."

Aira nodded, glancing toward where Alliot stood watching the horizon. "Agreed. Though I wonder if that's why the System gave me this skill in the first place—to help us navigate these complex relationships."

"And maybe to find others like us," Lila added. "Other folks who aren't lost in the lichen and could help us understand what's really happening in this world."

"All this reminded me of something," Aira muttered, still talking to Lila but glancing at Alliot.

"Alliot, if I had similar powers for the Air affinity," she continued, addressing all three people now, "would I be able to overwhelm your defenses?"

The enlightened defender was visibly uncomfortable when he heard Aira's question. His shoulders stiffened, and his eyes darted briefly to the horizon as if checking whether anyone might be listening.
