James blinked and turned his gaze to the timer.

[00:00:12]

Well, how time flies.

He cracked his neck, stretched his arms and legs once, blinked, and then opened his eyes somewhere else.

As his eyes shifted from side to side, he saw it wasn’t just any somewhere else. He was in a large arena, one with tile on the ground and a crowd in the stands. It was a lot of the same individuals from the waiting room, those who weren’t combatants, plus people from the Summit who had not been participants in the competition.

James tried to move but found that his body seemed to be fixed in place. Since there was no one pounding him with fists, he supposed that this was the System’s doing rather than someone hitting him with a Skill.

That’s interesting. I guess we’re not allowed to start yet?

He could see his opponent, though. Across from James stood a dark-skinned black man wearing what looked like a sort of full-body hoodie in beige, with the hood a darker shade than the rest. He wasn’t quite in the center of James’s field of view, but partly in peripheral vision, so James wasn’t entirely certain of the details of the clothing.

Well, hopefully this guy can give me a warmup…

The man across from him wasn’t someone who James had ever heard of. He had seen the name on the bracket: Elijah Goodfellow.

If he had been someone really dangerous, it was likely he would have stuck out in either the World Leaders’ Summit or the melee. But no.

That was why James felt fairly confident going into this fight.

[Combatants, before you begin the first round, here are the rules: you must fight within the space provided. You lose if you fall on the ground outside of the tiled fighting area and are unable to get back up for one Earth minute, if you verbally state that you give up, or, of course, if you die. Destroying the fighting stage simply means that you must fight in the crater where it formerly existed. Begin!]

The control that kept his body still suddenly released, and James slipped smoothly into a fighting stance.

“Fisher Emperor,” said the man across from him, nodding in his direction. He spoke in an African accent that James found it difficult to specifically identify.

“Goodfellow,” James replied, nodding back.

He finally got a decent look at the other man. He didn’t particularly like what he saw. The clothing that had seemed like some sort of full-body hooded outfit was actually a giant lion monster’s skin that had been morphed by the System into a head to toe armor with lion claw blades extending from the fingers of the man’s hands like knives.

The big cat gear gave James an unpleasant feeling, reminding him of the Panther Queen. This was probably roughly what sort of gear she would have become if she had been Looted or Pillaged after being killed, rather than her body being destroyed completely.

Get a grip. Don’t get annoyed by that when this guy probably doesn’t even know who she was. You already killed the one responsible. Even if that fight felt more like an execution than proper revenge.

With this quick look at the man’s features, James also saw that the African was extremely well muscled, had a jawline that would be the envy of even post-System humans, and possessed a steely look to his eyes that probably should have made James nervous. He still didn’t assess the enemy as a major threat—based on aura alone, he was not in the same league as James—but Goodfellow would at least be physically tough.

“May the stronger of us win,” said Goodfellow.

“I will,” James replied.

Goodfellow lunged as James was closing his mouth, and a long spear materialized in the African’s hands. In a single, smooth motion, he thrust at James’s chest. The Fisher Emperor sidestepped effortlessly and then moved back to his original position as Goodfellow pulled his hand back.

Unfortunately for you, you’re not quicker than me, James thought.

“Arrogant,” Goodfellow said, his voice almost a hiss.

“It’s not arrogance if you’re right.”

Goodfellow thrust the spear forward in a flurry of fast-moving strikes that seemed to come from multiple places at once, the speed blurring the image of the tip even for James’s senses.

It still wasn’t fast enough, though.

James used Roscuro’s shield form to fend off the strikes that came closest to hitting him and evaded the others.

“Is that the best you can do?” James asked. Goodfellow wasn’t actually unimpressive, but taunting his opponents in combat was both a habit and a consistent source of strategic advantage for James.

“I am the greatest hunter of an entire nation,” Goodfellow said as he drew his spear back to a ready position. “Don’t assume—”

“Nothing personal, but I’m going to end this quickly,” James said, interrupting. “It’s just that your armor reminds me of someone. I don’t like looking at it.”

Goodfellow snarled, and a huge surge of mana flared up around his body, green and silver colors mixed together.

He charged that quickly!

James charged some mana of his own, though his had become naturally invisible some time ago. He had three different attack plans running through his mind, all fairly standard, but each potent against most of the opponents he had ever fought.

Then Goodfellow clapped his hands, and the mana instantly dissipated. The ground burst all around James’s feet in a whirl of green leaves as plants shot out of the ground, growing so quickly that it was recognizable as an attack, and a difficult to avoid one. At the same moment, he felt the air all around his upper body whip into a frenzy. A cut opened up on James’s left cheek.

He knew in a fraction of a second what was happening.

A two-pronged attack from above and below. Goodfellow was trying to crush him between these strange, spear-like plants and a buzzsaw of wind blades that were much like James’s own Air Strike.

Warp Step.

James took a single step forward and appeared beside Goodfellow, evading the long, sharp, bladed grasses that had exploded out of the ground by his feet and the blades of wind that were cutting through the space where his head had been.

Goodfellow’s eyes widened, and his body jerked back, but he wasn’t quick enough. James threw a heavy punch, operating on instinct now that his plans had, for the moment, been disrupted. Part of his mind focused on recalibrating even as his arm whizzed through the air.

Meteor Strike.

A flaming fist struck Goodfellow right in the face, snapping the hunter’s head back and sending him staggering away from James. But the flames, James saw, dissipated in an instant. They hadn’t burned Goodfellow’s skin or scorched the lion skin armor at all.

It looked like they got absorbed by the lion’s fur… So, fire attacks are probably completely out. Lightning? No, no magic. Minimal magic, in case that thing just absorbs mana. I could inadvertently make him stronger.

Goodfellow opened his mouth to say something at the same time that he swung his spear in front of himself and tried to steady his body. It was obvious to James that the warrior was about to make a play for time and perhaps try to get some distance.

At least the physical force of that punch threw him off-balance, James thought. Which makes my path clear. I told you I was going to end this quickly. I didn’t say it would be gentle or neat.

The Fisher Emperor used earth magic to make himself a stone dagger from the arena floor tiles, and he hurled himself after Goodfellow. The spearman tried to stagger backward more quickly, but he began to lose his balance and was forced to take a step forward so as not to fall.

James was just getting closer with each passing moment.

Predator’s Venomous Armaments.

Venom secreted onto the earthen blade.

Goodfellow sprouted wings from his back and took off.

Warp Step.

James took a step and reappeared just below Goodfellow in the air. He stabbed upward with the dagger.

Goodfellow slashed downward with his spear and tried to take on a defensive stance—his neck, where James was aiming, suddenly sprouted a coating of scales. Then James hit him with the move that changed this from a simple bum rush into a slightly strategic engagement.

Soul-Freezing Gaze.

James made direct eye contact, and Goodfellow’s whole body shuddered and froze.

The Fisher Emperor shoved the spear out of the way and stabbed his dagger through the space uncovered by the lion armor, directly into his opponent’s throat, easily breaching the half-formed scale armor that Goodfellow had been growing.

Both men struck the ground.

Goodfellow coughed and writhed as he bled, his body still mostly paralyzed but incapable of holding completely still while he began to bleed out. The spear slipped out of his fingers as blood rolled down his shoulders, soaking the lion skin and the flesh underneath.

I didn’t come into this ring to kill you, but you’re not truly a fit opponent for me, James thought. I haven’t had to pull out a single one of my more interesting tricks, and bothering to use venom on the blade at all was completely unnecessary. But the fight isn’t over unless you give up, die, or get stuck outside the ring.

Goodfellow tried to sit up, spat some blood to the side of his mouth, and seemed to be self-healing, either deliberately or with a passive Skill. Either way, James saw the opening in his neck beginning to close.

So I’ll have to do this in a more violent way.

James threw a full force punch directly at Goodfellow’s face. Then one at his throat. Another into the face, shattering the cheekbone. He grabbed Goodfellow by the neck of the lion armor and slammed him head first into the ground.

He did this five times, ten times, twenty times, the other man feebly twitching and attempting to resist at first.

Finally, after some unknown number of blows, James felt Goodfellow’s body slacken with unconsciousness.

That took much longer than it should have, but it was hard to be sure how much strength to use. Too much, and I would just kill him, a helpless opponent. Too little, and we get what actually happened. A fight that drags on for a bit too long.

James rose and dragged Goodfellow’s body with him over to the side of the ring, then dumped it unceremoniously over the edge.

The Fisher Emperor rose and acknowledged the crowd for the first time since he had begun fighting. He forced his lips into a smile and formed the ‘V for victory’ sign with his fingers.

A moment passed in which one almost could have heard a pin drop.

Then the crowd burst into loud cheering and applause.

James’s smile turned a little more genuine.

At least they can appreciate this. I can’t say it did much for me, personally, but if seeing a one-sided fight is good for this crowd…

The Fisher Emperor pumped his fist once and then looked to the left.

For the first time, he realized that in addition to the ring he had been fighting in, the arena the crowd was observing had three more rings with combatants still fighting.

There’s probably some kind of invisible barrier preventing us from interacting with them or something, but it’s kind of surprising that there would be others going on at the same time. Maybe there are too many fighters in this competition otherwise. We’d be going for days.

In mid-turn, James found himself suddenly back in the waiting area.

I guess the System decided that Goodfellow had officially lost, and there was no point in wasting more of my time standing there looking at the crowd.

The Fisher Emperor shook his head and looked around himself.

The room was almost completely empty now, with only a few other participants there who had completed their initial battles more quickly than James had. But there were some giant magical screens, basically flat screen televisions floating in the air with no apparent power source, which displayed the matches still ongoing.

James moved toward the nearest ones. It was time to learn a bit more about his future opponents.
