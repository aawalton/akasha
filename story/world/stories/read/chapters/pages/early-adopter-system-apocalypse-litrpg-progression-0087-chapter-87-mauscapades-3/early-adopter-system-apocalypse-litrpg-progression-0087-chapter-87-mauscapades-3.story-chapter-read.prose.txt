The Administrator looked down at herself, arms extended, before looking at the doors with longing.

She took a breath and shook out her limbs before speaking.

“It occurs to me I’m not appropriately garbed. I’d rather not have to murder all the guards when my conspicuous attire invariably gets us dragged in for questioning.”

Maus wasn’t sure why he’d be dragged in with her, or why they’d question her at all over clothing. He’d have to ask Boss when he got back. She’d know for sure; she always looked amazing. Perhaps there was more to this propriety thing? Maus guessed.

“I see,” Maus said.

He did not.

Still, he pulled out a number of the garments the guards and staff had been wearing, everything dropping to the stone floor in a pile.

Gelken looked down at the worn clothing, shuddered nearly imperceptibly, then sighed and pulled something on over her clothes. Then they were off once more.

The moment they left the prison, Gelken began to move with all the forward momentum of a geriatric slug.

“Excuse me,” Maus called. “Why are you creeping?”

She kept her eyes forward as she responded. “So that I do not run into the, what did you call it? Glowing death wall?”

Maus tilted his head, about to ask if the vulpian couldn’t see it when it was clearly right there.

Then he remembered a conversation he’d once had with Mother.

‘Buddy, you’re always free to ask me anything. But you should think about questions before you share them,’ she’d said.

‘What do you mean?’ he’d asked.

‘Sometimes what we don’t know can reveal just as much or more as what we do. Never allow your ignorance to become someone else’s information.’

So Maus kept his questions to himself, focused his Perception on monitoring the prison and allowed the Administrator to continue creeping towards what to him was a clearly visible wall of shifting energy.

Gelken must have had some way to sense it, because she stopped just when they’d made it within a meter of the field.

“Well?” she asked, turning to look at Maus.

Maus nodded and concentrated on pushing his energy outwards, on retracing the steps he’d taken with Boss back at the library.

“Don’t fight, okay? And push your mana in,” Maus instructed.

“Push my mana into what?” Gelken asked. Then the vulpian gasped at a sensation she’d felt only once before. It had come from a creature in a Tier 2 Portal World her team at the time had carried her through. They hadn’t all made it out alive.

She tried very hard not to think about what she’d felt back then. Yet now, emanating from this tiny mouse, was the same sensation.

“You can use spiritual energy?” Gelken asked.

“Yep,” Maus said, unsure why she was reacting this way. He’d have to ask the Boss about that later too.

For now, Maus coated them both, just like he’d done when he entered. It took a lot of his attention, and a bit of the Administrator’s mana to help bolster and attune things, but he was doing it.

“Okay,” Maus said, the vest translating the strain in his mental voice. “Let’s go.”

In one quick step, both Maus and the Vulpian had crossed through the murder wall. The moment they were past a notification sprung up.

[Would you like to begin the next Trial?]

[Yes | No]

Oh, Maus thought, dropping the weave that had been covering them.

If he’d known that getting past the murder barrier was enough to accomplish the mission, he could’ve just murdered everyone and smashed his way out.

Welp, better safe than silly, he figured.

[Yes]

Maus waited for the System to transport him elsewhere, only nothing was happening. That’s when the new notification popped up.

[Trial Objective: Negotiate prisoner transfer to a FelisCanis Faction Representative and give them leverage over the alliance of Factions governing VixiaorFacilitate Administrator negotiation with the last Vulpian Independents’ Representative, granting them the key to future sovereignty]

Oh.

Oh, Boss had told him about this one.

This was a tricky little trap. A false dichotomy. That’s what she’d called it.

Maus didn’t have to choose. He could do whatever he wanted. The System wouldn’t catch him in its tricky little trap. Nope.

Maus was the smartest and the best mouse. The Boss said so, and the Boss didn’t lie.

Well, the Boss didn’t lie to him.

Maus looked over at the still somewhat alarmed prisoner and came to a decision. Why only complete one objective when he could do them both? And he could do it without being super mean to this Administrator person.

“Excuse me?” Maus called.

“We made it,” the Administrator responded. Apparently Gelken had been lost in her own head when Maus called out.

“Do you know a Faction called FelisCanis and one called Vulpian Independents?”

“The Independents are not a faction,” the immediate rebuttal and tensing of Gelken’s expression told Maus all he needed to know.

Now Maus felt kind of bad. He’d just decided what to do without asking Gelken her opinion at all.

“Do you want me to take you to them?” he asked.

“Certainly not,” Gelken said.

Maus’s whiskers twitched, and he gave them a quick once over as he pondered, before settling back onto all fours. He’d asked, Gelken had answered, Maus had given it consideration. Then, with his diligence done, Maus informed the vulpian of his decision.

“I’m gonna need you to meet 'em.”

The Administrator stiffened, their mana churning.

Maus caught the change and immediately went on alert.

Gelken froze.

Could she fight the mouse; escape? Yes. Would she come out cleanly? Unlikely. And would she be able to hide from the Factions, the Independents, and the terrifying mouse that could use spiritual energy? Beyond unlikely.

Truth was, Gelken wasn’t optimised for combat, she was optimised for administration, and Maus had been in too many battles to miss the difference.

“Well, you are aware of my wishes,” Gelken said slowly. “Should you insist upon attempting to bring me before either the Factions or the Matriarch, I am informing you that I will resist wholeheartedly.”

“Matriarch?” Maus asked, entirely unperturbed by the threat. That was not reassuring to the recently freed vulpian.

“Yes, the Matriarch. She’s the current representative of the Vulpian Independents,” Gelken said with obvious unease.

Maus wondered if this was the same Matriarch the Historian had spoken of. Perhaps Boss would know.

“What if…” Maus began slowly, his voice a faint projection in the bright early-morning air around the prison. “What if I brought them to you instead?”

The Vulpian blinked once, then again, and turned to face the mouse.

For Maus's part, he really didn’t want to fight. The Boss always said the best fights were the ones you won before they started.

So how could he win this one before it started?

“What do you mean?” Gelken asked.

Maus pressed his paws against the fuzzy side of the vulpian’s face.

“You have a Portal World, right? That’s what you’re administering?”

“Administrating, but yes.” The vulpian nodded.

“Well, how about we go there first, I can tell you my plan on the way.”

It wasn’t really a concession, but it was more than nothing, Gelken thought as she took off at a sprint.

When it became clear to Maus that she was speeding up as they approached the settlement, not slowing down, Maus spoke up.

“Why are you still running?” he asked.

The question brought the Administrator up short.

“What do you mean? We’re escaping, aren’t we?”

"We're about to enter the gates." Maus countered. There was a pause before Gelken responded.

"I'm very fast," she said. Perhaps she was fast enough not to be caught, but she definitely wasn't fast enough to go unnoticed. Boss always said the element of surprise was a critical resource in any conflict, and that an advantage should never be given up casually, no matter how certain a potential victory.

“You're not very good at escaping,” Maus said. “People don’t usually run places unless they’re running from something or to something. Either way, if you want lots, and bunches of people to look at you, that’s a good way to do it,” he announced.

“What do you suggest, then?”

“Well, we just walk in. If you hide you can hide how strong you are, you're a regular person, aren't you?”

Ah, changing her clothes made sense to Maus now. She’d look like just one of the other vulpians walking around, if in slightly ill-fitting clothes. But she wasn’t the only one with that problem either. Perhaps this Administrator wasn’t terrible at escaping, just really bad.

For her part, Gelken just looked at the mouse from the corner of her eye.

“You are suggesting we just ‘walk in’?” the Administrator was incredulous.

“Why not?” Maus asked.

“We’re playing pretend.” Maus nodded to himself. “I hide, and you pretend to be a normal person. Lots of the best disguises are hidden in plain sight," Maus said confidently.

“I’m well aware,” she snapped. “The issue, if you’ll remember, is that I’m a wanted prisoner. The settlement guards will know my name, my face. I doubt there’s anyone in a 10-kilometre radius who hasn’t been told to keep an eye out for me. Virulent mange! They even have my mana signature, and every entrance has been warded so I can’t even use my illusions.”

“Oh,” was Maus’s only response. That would be a problem. Maus liked solving interesting problems.

He thought about the pattern he’d woven to get them through the death wall, thought about everything he and Boss had been working on together. Thought of the way he’d used Gelken’s mana before. “I think I can hide us both,” he said.
