As Kain stepped beneath the towering canopy of the Riftwood forest, the trees themselves seemed to greet Kain with an intriguing stillness. Jagged shafts of dim light slipped through the dense web of branches above him, creating strange shadow patterns.

The air felt heavy and damp, carrying a unique scent of what seemed like decay mixed with something strange and unfamiliar. He had only been inside for a few minutes, but the weight of the dungeon sat heavily on his shoulders.

RIFTWOOD DUNGEON

[F-RANK]

Welcome to the Riftwood Forest, a dungeon where only the strongest survive.

This biome is a crucible of evolution, where all creatures compete ruthlessly for dominance and resources. Within these warped woods, predators stalk their prey, and survival hinges on cunning, strength, and adaptability.

Dungeon Rules:

Kain exhaled, his grip tightening on the jagged pole he’d managed to salvage. His lips curled in a wry smile.

An F-Rank dungeon. Yesterday, I was hunting S-Rank monsters with an elite team. Today, I'm scraping by with a stick against F-Rank creatures. The System's idea of humor, perhaps.

Kain's muscles tensed up; he let his second nature kick in, and instinct took over. He was watching his foot placement, silently weaving his way through the thick shrubbery. Hyperaware of the faint vibrations and rustles he felt through his hunter's senses, he sent them out consistently to maintain awareness.

The Riftwood was alive with sound—chittering, clicking, and growls in the distance. The ecosystem was chaotic but eerily familiar. He’d grown up in a world dominated by insects, where the few remaining mammals were desperate scavengers. But this? This was something else. There were mammals here… thriving.

A branch snapped nearby—amateur mistake, sloppy footwork. Kain froze. Another person? Or something worse?

The air shifted with a heavy musk that carried notes of decay and something else—a chemical signature his enhanced senses recognized immediately. Bear. But not like any bear from the old world.

Kain eased himself behind a twisted trunk as a massive shape lumbered into view. The creature moved with the confidence of an apex predator, its shoulders standing higher than a man's head. But what truly captured Kain's attention was its hide—not fur, but a series of overlapping plates that resembled bark, complete with swirling patterns that matched the surrounding trees.

[RIFTBEAR - LEVEL 9]

Massive and territorial, the Riftbear is a predator adapted to the chaotic ecosystem of the Riftwood. Its bark-like armor provides exceptional defense, while its powerful limbs can crush foes with ease. Riftbears are known to protect high-value resources, particularly Riftfruit Trees, which grant significant energy to those brave enough to claim them.

Ahead, the huge Riftbear lumbered further into view, its organic, almost bark-like armor camouflaging impressively with the thick roots at its feet. It moved with a careful slowness, the slowness of a predator who has nothing to fear. It clawed thoroughly at the base of a large tree. At the foot of the tree lay several large, orange fruits, their vibrant skins releasing a strange light.

The bear leaned down and began to eat, chewing noisily without a care in the world.

Kain crouched, keeping his presence concealed as he observed the beast. “Riftbear,” he muttered to himself. “Massive, armored, and stubborn as hell. Not much different from the ones in the colony books.” He studied its movements, his mind instinctively cataloging its habits and weaknesses.

I could try to bait it into charging me, he thought, his gaze flicking to the jagged pole in his hand. If Heart of the Storm activates again, it’ll be over in an instant. But his hands trembled slightly as he remembered the surge of power—the pain and chaos it brought. He still didn’t know how to control it, or if it would even activate under his terms. Raw power, yes, but directionless—as likely to kill him as his enemy.

"Too risky," he muttered, mentally cycling through alternatives. "Not dying twice in one day."

Kain leaned back, letting out a quiet sigh as the bear continued to gorge itself. Just as he began planning another approach, the forest erupted into chaos.

The bear raised its head suddenly, nostrils flaring. Had it caught his scent? Kain tensed, ready to activate [Lightning Dash]—his one reliable escape option.

But the bear wasn't looking at him. Its massive head swiveled toward a dense thicket, muscles bunching beneath its armored hide.

Something was coming.

The chittering hit him first—a sound Kain knew from nightmares, from countless battles beyond colony walls. High-pitched, rapid communications that operated at frequencies human ears barely registered, but hunter training had taught him to recognize.

Ant communications.

The brush exploded as four massive insects burst into the clearing. Each the size of a large dog, their glossy black carapaces caught the filtered sunlight like polished obsidian. Mandibles clicked and scissored, dripping with viscous fluid that sizzled against the forest floor.

[RIFTANT SCOUTS - LEVEL 3-4]

Small but deadly, Rift Ant scouts operate in coordinated packs, overwhelming prey with precision and acid-coated mandibles. Their rigid social structure makes them both highly efficient and vulnerable to disruption if the pack leader is eliminated. These ants typically travel in groups of 3-6, gathering resources and information for their colony. Larger swarms or leaders are likely nearby if scouts are spotted.

The bear roared, rising to its hind legs in a display that would have sent any natural predator retreating. But these weren't natural predators. The System had changed them, optimized them.

Two ants circled left, two circled right—perfect flanking maneuver. The bear swiped massive claws at the nearest ant, but it darted backward with uncanny precision, almost as if...

They're anticipating its moves, Kain realized, professional appreciation mixing with dread. Analyzing attack patterns in real-time. Fuck, they shouldn't be this advanced yet.

The coordinated assault that followed confirmed his fears. Two ants latched onto the bear's hind legs, mandibles piercing through the armored hide with targeted precision. The bear bellowed in pain, trying to dislodge them, but the remaining ants used this distraction to scramble up its back.

Kain watched with clinical detachment as they targeted joint connections and sensory organs—exactly where the armor was thinnest. Acid hissed and bubbled, melting through protective layers. The bear's roars transformed from rage to agony, then to gurgling desperation as mandibles found its throat.

The massive creature crashed to the ground, its struggles weakening as the ants methodically dismantled it. Within minutes, the forest fell silent again except for the efficient dismemberment of their prey.

Ants. Kain's jaw tightened until pain shot through his temples. Always the fucking ants.

In his time, humans and insects were locked in a desperate struggle for survival, specifically the ant colonies. The ants had become the dominant species, their rapid evolution making them nearly impossible to defeat.

Colonies of humans had persisted, but only by fortifying themselves against an endless tide of chittering mandibles and acidic venom. Every battle against the swarm had been a desperate gamble to hold onto dwindling scraps of land.

But now, in this earlier era, the balance was different. Mammals still thrived—powerful and wild. The insects, though growing stronger, had not yet cemented their dominion. It was a fleeting window of opportunity, one Kain realized he might be able to exploit.

“These ants,” Kain whispered, watching the ants dismantle the bear with merciless efficiency.

They’re the future. I gotta take them out now, or they'll conquer this dungeon like they conquered my world.

He clenched his fists angrily, feeling the Dao respond to his emotions.

The Riftwood forest clearly isn't just a dungeon; the System is smarter than that. There's never anything without purpose. It's a goddamn testing ground. I'll sharpen my skills and refine my Dao here, not because the System wants me to, but because I want to. This will be a glimpse into how humanity might survive or fail in the face of the System's merciless evolutionary brutality.

Kain’s thoughts turned to his time in the colonies. As an S-Rank Hunter, he fought to protect his people, never questioning the origins of the colonies or the trials that must have forged them.

The elders had spoken only vaguely of the early days, of battles fought and alliances made; all Kain’s knowledge of the early days came from reading in his spare time and classes. But standing here, watching the Rift Ants claim their territory, a cold realization crept over him: this might be where it all began.

If this era was the foundation of the colonies, then his presence here mattered. He wasn’t just fighting to survive; survival was irrelevant without understanding, and this time around, it needed to be different.

In his time, insects outpaced humanity because they adapted faster and evolved ruthlessly. If he could learn their weaknesses now, if he could grow stronger before their dominance was complete, he might carve out an advantage humanity had long since lost.

“This isn’t just about me anymore,” Kain murmured, his voice low but resolute. “This power, this Dao—it’s not just a second chance. It’s a tool. And if I use it right, maybe we don’t have to lose.”

The weight of that thought settled over him like a cloud. He didn’t know how the colonies would eventually form or what trials the System would impose to decide who rose and who fell. But he knew one thing: strength would be the deciding factor. And strength was something he could build, here and now.

But something was different this time. The ants were splitting up, breaking into smaller groups rather than dragging the entire carcass back to their colony. Two scouts headed deeper into the forest with smaller portions, while four veered eastward with the bulk of the kill.

Intelligence. Strategy. Resource allocation. Kain's mind raced with the possibilities of the scouts' movements, cataloging each deviation from expected behavior. They're not just scavenging—they're establishing their own supply lines back to the colony.

A cold realization settled over him. In his timeline, the colony records dated ant dominance as beginning decades after the Integration. But the behavior he was witnessing suggested an accelerated timeline. Something had changed.

Or maybe the records were wrong. Maybe it started earlier than we knew.

The weight of this knowledge pressed against him like a physical force. If the ants had already organized this efficiently, humanity's window for establishing a foothold would be narrowing by the hour.

Kain forced himself to breathe evenly, calling on meditation techniques that had sustained him through countless hunts. Panic wouldn't help. Emotion clouded judgment. Another basic hunter principle.

The smaller group first, he decided, watching the pair of ants disappear into the underbrush with their prizes. Follow, observe, and understand their patterns. Then eliminate.

He moved through the shadows with practiced silence to follow the smaller group while old hunting memories instinctively guided his movements. The ants moved with precision and methodical exactness. Kain used the dense undergrowth to stay downwind while concealing his position.

The deeper they moved through the woods the darker the forest became while he observed their eastern path through the dimming transition to an ethereal twilight. A metallic aroma filled the thickening air which caused Kain’s senses to become hyperaware. As he emerged from the thick underbrush into a clearing his pulse quickened upon seeing the ants halt at an ancient tree's base.

The massive tree stood above its surrounding forest, its twisted trunk showing signs of age and strength. The tree's bark had a gentle shimmer, displaying gold and bronze that appeared to flow like molten metal under the twilight light.

The tree's massive, exposed roots extended outward to form a complex network while glowing amber sap coated their surfaces. The sap throbbed gently like a living organism while individual droplets moved slowly before sinking to create tiny sparkling pools on the forest floor.

A warm, golden light bathed the clearing, giving off an inviting yet foreboding atmosphere. The surrounding air buzzed with energy around the tree. Dim lights hovered gently near the tree, resembling fireflies trapped in continuous movement. Their illumination formed unusual and moving patterns on the tree roots.

As the ants came closer, Kain crouched and made himself as small as possible, his eyes tightening into slits. The ants transported their bloody burden to the tree's roots and eagerly thrust their mandibles into the thick glowing sap while clicking. The sap energized the creatures, which caused their muscles to move while faint crimson veins appeared on their shiny black shells.

The tree was beautiful, and yet Kain couldn’t shake the strange sense of danger that emanated from its core. He tightened his grip on the makeshift weapon in his hand.

Whatever this tree was, it wasn’t just a part of the Riftwood—it was the heart of something far greater.

The familiar blue rectangle flickered into Kain’s vision:

SAP VEIN

[F-RANK POWER NEXUS]

The Sap Vein is a concentrated source of the Riftwood’s essence. Consuming its sap offers immense power at the cost of intense physical and mental strain. Predators are drawn to its energy, and few survive its trials.

Kain’s eyes narrowed as he studied the ants. Their movements seemed sharper, more energized with every drop they consumed. He clenched his fists, lightning crackling faintly along his arms.

If they’re feeding on this, then they’re stronger than I thought. I can’t let them get back to the colony with this boost. I wanna see what this Dao can do.

Kain weighed his options with cold precision. The ants were distracted, their systems processing the influx of energy from the Sap Vein. Their enhanced state made them more dangerous, but their focus was divided. A tactical opportunity, if he could exploit it.

He activated [Lightning Dash], feeling the energy surge through his pathways as his body transformed momentarily into pure electrical current. The world blurred around him as he crossed the clearing in less than a heartbeat, materializing directly behind the ants.

The shockwave of his arrival scattered leaves and sent vibrations through the ground, announcing his presence with deliberate intent. No point in stealth now—he wanted their full attention.

The ants spun toward him, mandibles clicking in what he recognized as alarm patterns. Their black eyes reflected his image back at him—a lean figure wreathed in electricity, dancing across his skin, holding a makeshift pole posing as a weapon. Yet, with the contradictory stance of someone who had stood before these monsters many times before.

Kain's lips tugged at the corners into a predatory smile, the first genuine expression since his monumental resurrection. This—this was familiar territory. Hunter versus prey, though both roles could reverse in an instant.

"Always wanted to be here," he said softly, the words meant more for himself than the creatures before him. "At the beginning. When your kind were still vulnerable."

The Lightning Dao surged in response to his intent, crackling visibly along the metal pole in his hand. He shifted into a stance drilled into him through decades of training, muscle memory asserting itself despite his new body's limitations.

"Let's dance."
