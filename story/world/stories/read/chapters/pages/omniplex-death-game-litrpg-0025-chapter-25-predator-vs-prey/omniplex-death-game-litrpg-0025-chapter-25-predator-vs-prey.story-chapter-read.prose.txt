A familiar laugh echoed across the room. A tall figure, holding a blood-dripping butcher knife, stood before him, shrouded in shadow. He couldn’t say a thing, unsure if he even had a mouth. It felt as though he had no body to move in either.

A strange feeling whelmed within him seeing the figure.

The figure spoke.

“Remember, son, a human head is only valuable when it’s attached to a neck. Without that, it’s just a misshapen ball, not even fit for kicking.”

He finally felt the ability to move. He felt something in his hands, heavy and spherical. A guttural scream erupted from him after a single second of observation.

A decapitated head.

The scene went dark.

Ciro drew a sharp breath, his body snapped upwards. A cold sweat ran down his entire body. A throbbing pain stretched across his ribs. He winced, placing his hand where it hurt. He felt the tight fabric of bandages wrapped around his entire midriff. His eyelids felt like led, but he kept them open, his surroundings were completely unfamiliar.

He drew more breaths, slow and rugged. The air was thick with the scent of decay and damp wood. There was little to no lighting. Everywhere Ciro turned, it felt as though something could pop out.

Where am I?

His body felt foreign, stiff, and unresponsive. He shifted slightly, feeling the rough fabric scratching against his back. It seemed to be a makeshift bed, layered with cloth and other random items, soft enough for someone to sleep on.

Ciro didn’t even think about who could have placed him here. The only thing running through his mind was Carina’s smirk as she dropped him off the cliff. He didn’t understand. What did he do to her? He didn’t deserve it.

It mattered not. She failed and Ciro lived.

I’ll get my revenge, Ciro swore to himself. He shouldn’t have been alive. The last thing he remembered was the wind screaming past his ears and the jagged rocks greeting him as he hit the ground.

There was a faint rustling sound to his side. He wasn’t alone.

“Oh, he’s awake—”

Ciro wasn’t taking any chances. He ignored the pain that erupted within, his rage and inadequacy fueled his every movement. Ciro shot up from the makeshift bed, grabbing the voice by the throat. He had both on his neck. Ciro gritted his teeth, squeezing as hard as she could.

It was just a young man. His face would’ve been pale, if not for Ciro robbing him of all air. Ciro lifted the man off the ground. “Who are you!?”

The man could barely speak. “we—we saved you,” he gargled.

Ciro’s eyes widened. His grip on the young man slowly faded. What am I doing? He thought to himself. Just as was about to release his hands from his throat, he felt something charge at him. His body tumbled back, falling into the array of cloth and palettes that made the bed.

“He’s crazy…crazy—I’m telling you,” the man croaked.

Hitting the floor harshly, all the injuries he sustained screamed for mercy. Screaming for him to just stop moving. He stumbled onto the floor. He turned towards them, his body crouched low, as if the stance could protect him.

“I’m sorry…” whispered Ciro, his voice hoarse.

He peered at the two men in front of him. Am I more sick than I realize? Why am I seeing two of the same person?

He blinked furiously, waiting for the figures to form into one. He realized he was staring at two identical men. They both wore blonde hair that seemed to be the brightest thing in the room. They both had long faces that made Ciro think of a donkey.

They looked at each other.

“What do we do Birk?”

“I don’t know Ivo, what should we do?”

“I already asked that, Birk?”

“What did you ask, Ivo?”

Ciro’s headache grew, their voices high pitched and loud. They were twins. That much was obvious. They seem to share the same brain cell as well as DNA, Ciro grumbled. The pain flared in his body once more. The twins continued to argue, completely disregarding him. Ciro crawled back into what was left of the makeshift bed after he fell on it.

He laid on his back, watching the twins argue. Ivo and Birk huh, Ciro noted down their names.

They seemed to talk for an hour, it felt, before they turned back to Ciro. “The Boss will decide!” They both said in unison.

“You two are creepy.” Ciro hissed.

They ignored him, completely retreating to the corner of the room, where Ciro could still hear what they were saying.

“Do you think he’s crazy, Birk?”

“He must be Ivo. He jumped off a cliff.”

“But we jumped off a cliff once, Birk.”

“Yes, I remember. But there was water there, Ivo.”

“I miss water Birk.”

“Me too Ivo.”

They hugged each other. Wow, real heartwarming and such a riveting conversation. I hope this so-called boss at least has a little more intelligence going for him. Ciro shook his head in disbelief.

They sat for what felt like hours, waiting. Ciro wished it were silent. The twins continued to chatter. All of their conversations drivel.

“So how did you save me?” Ciro’s voice called out to them.

The twins looked at each other before stepping closer to him.

“We saw your body, didn’t we, Ivo?”

“Yeah, we sure did. The Boss told us to go loot your body—”

The other twin cut the other off. “But you were alive,” he said, pushing his brother away.

“Then the boss told us to take you with us.” They both said. “Stop copying me!” They began wrestling each other, disregarding Ciro right next to them, who was writhing in pain.

“Please do this somewhere else,” Ciro bemoaned. He shook his head, realizing he was talking to two dimwits. “So who’s your boss?” he asked, trying to get them to focus.

The twins were just about to respond when a booming voice echoed from outside the room. “I’m not their boss, dammit!”

Slowly turning his head towards the door where the voice was coming from, Ciro bit back a groan as his neck flared with pain. The dim flicker of orange light illuminated the room from the corner of his periphery. The cramped room came into focus. A heavy door with no visible handle creaked open.

The twins quickly shot towards each side of the door. They stood stiffly with their hands on the forehead in a salute.

The door opened, and a figure emerged. He was huge. Muscles seemed to shoot out from his body, even his neck had thick striations of muscles lined all across. His body was too large for the door. He entered sideways. Stepping forwards towards Ciro, he punched both of the twins on the top of their heads.

“Don’t call me your boss,” he enunciated each word with pure venom.

“Yes, boss,” they replied in response. They realized what they said a second later before dashing out of the room. Perhaps fearful they’d be struck again.

He shifted towards Ciro. “How are you doing?” The man asked, a smile on his face.

Ciro inspected him more closely. He seemed maybe a few years older than him, pale as snow with a snake tattoo on his neck that shimmered under the orange dome lighting. His head was fairly angular, even with a completely bald scalp. His blue eyes pierced through Ciro, as if assessing his worth.

“I feel like hell,” Ciro answered honestly.

The man let out a laugh, bellowing and unrestrained. “You did drop down a cliff, it was crazy.” He peered over at Ciro. “The name’s Lukasz Mierzynski, you can call me Luka.” He offered out a hand.

Lifting himself up. He took his hand. “I appreciate it. I’m Ciro…Ciro Adams.”

Ciro felt the calluses on Lukasz’s hand. They were rough and deep. A testament to how much he trained his body.

“Well Ciro, you’ve been knocked out for a whole day,” Lukasz stated bluntly.

Ciro’s eyes widened in surprise. “A whole day!”

“Yes, you’ve missed the start of the first event.”

Ciro swore under his breath. “What do we have to do this time?”

“Look at the screen in front of you.”

Having been so focused on his surroundings and the constant pain in his body, he never thought of the system or the messages.

Predator’s Lottery

For Every Predator There Lies Prey. That Is The Universal Law.

Your Role Is: Prey

Survive Against The Predators or Risk Losing Everything

Time Remaining: 6:37:16

Ciro raised an eyebrow. “What does this mean?”

“We were just as confused as you, y’know. Then everything made sense once some alien came chasing after us, trying to kill us. They’re far too strong. We have to band together to survive.” Lukasz shivered in memory.

“What are you talking about, Lukasz? So an alien was after you.”

“I said call me Luka. And yes, the alien was given the role of a predator. As such, they need to hunt after us prey.” He pointed between him and Ciro.

Ciro raised an eyebrow. “How do you know I’m a prey?”

Luka raised two fingers. “Prey have the ability to find other prey. Look towards the top right.”

Ciro did as instructed and he noticed what seemed to be a map of some kind, more akin to a sat nav. A green arrow lay in the middle, which he presumed represented him. Based on that assumption, the green dot next to him was Luka. The two dots slightly further away were the twins.

“Now all you see is green right now, but just you wait. Once you see orange, you run.” Luka grabbed onto his shoulder.

“Predator?” Ciro asked.

“Predator.”

Ciro clutched his head. He had just survived a dangerous event. Last time he was the hunter, but this time the tables turned. “I need some booze.”

Luka saw Ciro’s distress. “Oi Ivo, bring me that Juja Juice we found!” he called out to one of the twins.

Ivo stumbled into the room holding a familiar bottle. He handed it to Luka, who handed it to Ciro.

Ciro looked towards the bottle, seeing the familiar shade of orange and green. “This is called Juja juice?” he asked, before taking a swig.

“It’s a mighty fine liquor. It puts the stuff on Earth to shame, doesn’t it?” Luka smiled. “Although I do miss a sip of moonshine.”

“Hey wait, you just drank the whole thing!” Luka’s eyes widened in surprise, snatching the bottle away from Ciro.

Ciro felt the pain running through his body go numb. The tide finally stopped. He could take a comforting breath. “Bring me some more!” he slurred his words.

Ivo whispered into Lukasz’s ear. Ciro heard a few words. “He’s a little scary.”

Ciro stood up, staggering slightly. “Are we safe here?” Ciro asked, looking around the room.

Putting his eye closer to the bottle, Luka replied, “no, not at all.”

“We’ve been moving and dragging you along every hour. The twins did a good job redoing your bedding.”

Looking towards Ivo, Ciro managed a smile. “Thank you and sorry for choking you.”

“That was kind of my brother,” Ivo responded, scratching his cheek awkwardly.

Ciro reminded himself to apologize to the other twin. What was his name again? He wondered. The alien booze eroded his mind enough for him to draw a blank.

He shook away the brain fog. “So, what do we do now?”

Lukasz smiled, throwing the empty bottle towards Ciro, who fumbled before catching it. He pulled something out of his pocket that glinted under the dim light.

“First, we need to get you into fighting shape. This knife was in your pocket, by the way.”

He offered Ciro a familiar-looking knife. It was stained with blood and still held a sharp edge.

It wasn’t his knife and would never be his. He took it regardless.
