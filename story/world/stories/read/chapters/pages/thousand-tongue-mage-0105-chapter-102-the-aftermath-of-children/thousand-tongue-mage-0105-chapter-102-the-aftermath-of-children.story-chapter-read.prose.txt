No matter how Zora looked at it, it really seemed like Decima was about to die.

He listened as she dragged herself up the steps towards the throne, leaving a bloody smear across the smooth stone. For her part, the Divine Empress still sat slumped and indifferent above the Magicicada Witch. With her chin propped on one hand and her gaze glazed and hollow—and Zora could just tell it was blank—it was like she wasn’t even surprised he had defeated Decima so readily.

The lords of Her Four Households sat just as still, and so were the Spore Knights still frozen to the floor around the hall. Zora gripped his staff tighter—and that was when Decima laughed a dying laugh.

“... What a wicked adult you are, Thousand Tongue,” she rasped, dragging another armful of chitin and cloth up another step. “You… are unfair with your words.”

Zora tilted his head. “Enlighten me.”

“The ‘good’ princess you spoke of watched her mother and sister die right in front of her, while she alone hid under a carriage with her nails in her mouth, too fearful to take a courageous stand,” she croaked. “She carries the nobleness you admire only because cowardice bled out of her hands first.”

Kita’s pulse spiked behind him.

The ‘good’ soldiers you spoke of have spent years oppressing their own people in the millions, and they only fight now because they either wish to atone for the sins they’ve committed or leap onto the winning horse.

Decima coughed, choking on the fluid in her throat, but her voice—somehow—grew steadier.

“You speak of ‘good’ soldiers, but these men… these dutiful sons of the empire?” she continued. “Though they knew they were wrong, they, too, enforced a Witch’s laws for a decade. They oppressed and broke millions of families with the same hands they now lift in apology. Their march today is not born of righteousness, but of guilt sharpened into desperation.”

Several soldiers stiffened, and her three small forehead-ocelli blinked slowly as she turned toward Baya.

“And the ‘good’ lord you speak of…” A soft, serpentlike smile curved her lips. “He sent tens of thousands of children into the Forward Armies like lambs sent to feed the northern flame. Children stripped of emotion. Children hollowed into tools. Children who killed because they no longer knew how not to. Just ask your Worm Mage over there what it was like to fight in one of those Forward Armies.

Baya’s breath faltered for the first time Zora had ever heard, and Enki didn’t flinch, but the air around him grew ever so slightly colder that Zora could hear the ice forming.

Decima’s voice softened at last.

“You call some of them ‘good’ adults,” she rasped, “but some adults are ‘good’ only because they have gathered so much ‘bad’. All adults are mosaics built of their failures. They are stitched-together regrets wearing… the costumes of resolve.”

She crawled another arm’s length, breath rattling.

“They are… accumulations. Stacks of consequences. Piles upon piles of what they once did and what they once refused to do. Adult morality is but old fear repainted, so you wish to know what I think adults are, Thousand Tongue?” she whispered. “They are the shadows children leave behind, and if adulthood is reached by moving past one’s past… then adults, too, can be broken by returning them to the past.”

She reached the first step of the throne. Her limbs shuddered. Her spine arched like a dying insect beneath the Empress’s feet.

“I am a Magicicada Witch,” she said, chuckling through a wet choke. “And they call me ‘Reverberator’ because I make adults… remember. I make their old selves ring inside them until they split.” Her head tilted toward the lords of Her Four Households, silent on their thrones. “That was how I broke Her Four Households. I showed them the echoes of the night the previous Empress died at my hand. I reminded them of their failure to protect her, and they shattered, oh so cleanly. It was… beautiful.”

Then she angled her face up toward the Divine Empress herself, eyes burning with heat.

“And that was how I charmed the current Empress. A girl who inherited a crown still dripping with her mother’s death. I only had to touch the bruise she carried and whisper in her ear… and she folded so easily, too.”

Zora clenched his jaw as she turned her head back to him.

“Yet for months now… my thoughts have been consumed by you, Thousand Tongue,” she whispered. “Such a strange man you are. Such a dangerous man. A man speaking with the wisdom of a thousand tongues, a boy guiding adults as if they were lambs stumbling toward fire, and a mage who kills our kind with nothing but a breath and a story… did I not say, when we first met, that I would make history repeat itself for you? That I would destroy you for the sake of the Swarm, even if I must do it with but a heartbeat left in my body?”

With that, her head tipped towards the nobles slumped in their thrones and the Spore Knights frozen in cracked armor.

“Kill yourselves.”

A synchronized shiver rippled through the hall, followed by the scrape of knives drawn across throats. The nobles of Her Four Households jerked upright and slit themselves open with small blades in their palms as though pulled by a puppeteer’s string. The Spore Knights, too, managed to drag their half-frozen throats across their sawtooth greatswords with awful devotion.

And beyond the Divine Hall—past the great walls and into the folds of the distant city—he heard dozens and hundreds more bodies collapsing in streets, rooftops, and smaller temples all around. Suicide, all of them, blooming across the Divine Capital like sickness carried on wind.

… What?

Decima’s bitter laugh clawed its way out of her chest.

“Of the three sisters,” she crooned, “little Nona is strongest in battle—a child of instinct, drunk on the thrill of winning the moment, but Morta and I are older. We are wiser. We know that wars are not won by strength, and a single mage cannot save humanity, just as a single witch cannot deliver victory to the Swarm. You see, the only real way a single, lowly witch such as I can help the Swarm win the war is—”

The Divine Empress moved.

One moment she was seated, head lolled slightly to the side. The next, she leaned forward, hooked one elegant hand around Decima’s throat, and lifted the dying witch as effortlessly as one might lift a scarf from a hook.

Then her jaw split. It unhinged with a wet crack of flesh and bone, stretching wider, then wider still until her mouth was a cavern rimmed with mandibles she shouldn’t possess—and she bit down on Decima’s head, crushing through skull and chitin in a single, ghastly crunch.

Zora froze. The entire hall froze with him. Even Enki’s cold stare faltered for half a beat as the Divine Empress swallowed Decima’s head.

This is…

The Divine Empress rose from her throne and wiped a streak of blood from her mouth, but the voice that rolled out, cool and queenly, was laced with a resonance no human tongue could produce.

“... Troublesome,” she murmured. “I did not wish to do this.”

Chitin plates broke through her skin as she spoke. They crawled across her arms, her ribs, her throat, as though they’d always been living underneath, waiting only for permission to surface. Her imperial silks tore apart in quiet strips as her body reshaped itself. Joints clicked into new angles, and her stature lengthened with a regal, monstrous grace.

“When Decima approached me a year ago with her silly little plan to break the empire, I had no intention of participating. I had no desire for her theatrics,” the Divine Empress continued. “Yet she had served me dutifully. Ten long years of weakening the empire from within. Ten years of removing its… teeth. I thought it only fair, as a queen, that I must at least honor the labor of my most loyal subject.”

As her human face split cleanly down the middle, she lifted one hand—now plated, claw-tipped—and swept it casually at the open-throated corpses strewn below her dais.

“Wake up,” she whispered. “I shall bury the Attini Empire alongside Decima.”

And one by one, the dead began to twitch.

Heads lolled on half-severed throats, jaws slack, eyes unfocused. Spore Knights in heavy armor jerked up as though hoisted by invisible strings. Limbs twitched before locking into place, then twitched again, stumbling them upright in stuttering, puppet-like steps. Blood dripped in thin lines down their armor. Some breathed gurgling gasps through open wounds, but others made no sound at all, moving in a silence that was somehow even worse than if they’d screamed all the way.

Shuddering breaths spread through the soldiers behind Zora as the dead continued their resurrection, and even he found himself struggling to draw a breath.

That… aura. From the Divine Empress. It was too vast to belong to a human, too cold to belong to a witch, and too old to belong to anything that’d walked the world in a single lifetime.

… She’s not the Empress.

She hasn’t been for at least a year, since I started making new waves in the south.

Every man, woman, and child on the Brightburrow Continent knew the name of the queen of ants who ruled the Swarm in the south.

One of the six.

Regalia.

The Greater Insect God of the Attini Empire Front.
