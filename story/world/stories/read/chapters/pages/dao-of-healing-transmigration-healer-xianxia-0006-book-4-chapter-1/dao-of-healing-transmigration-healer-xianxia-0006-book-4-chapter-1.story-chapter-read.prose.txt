The Foundation Building Realm was not just about gathering more energy and refining it, but turning that energy into something wholly connected to myself and my path. Beyond that, I noticed that during this particular breakthrough my dao fragment seemed to be resonating in my spiritual sea.

At the end of every cycle my qi would crash into itself in the dantian.

Xiao Cui stared at the board in front of her. This game was the most confusing, complex, and yet utterly genius strategy game she had ever played. Granted, it was one of only two strategy games that she had played in her life, the other one being Go, which she had only played twice, but she suspected this strange game would still beat every other strategy game in the categories mentioned.

It was sort of like chess, at least if chess was how she had heard it described, except that there were a lot more pieces. Seventy-two, to be exact. Each piece represented one of the animals that adorned the Black River Gate. However, they weren’t all active at once. No, there were four ‘seasons’ which the board rotated through, each with its own set of pieces. What made the game even more confusing was that the position of the pieces was fixed, even when the ‘season’ changed, but what each piece could do varied wildly.

It meant that one could be in a strong position, close to capturing an opponent’s piece, but then the change of season could suddenly turn the tables and see one losing multiple pieces.

The only silver lining to being suddenly thrust into this awful game was that each of her opponents had never played the game before, either. All of them were figuring it out on the fly. Cultivation was restricted here, too, which meant that only one’s intellect counted. That levelled the playing field, given that most of Xiao Cui’s opponents were in the Foundation Building Realm, while she was still a Mid-stage Qi Gathering cultivator, but it didn’t completely even it.

Even with their cultivation restrained, the benefits of a longer life and other natural abilities still put Xiao Cui at a disadvantage. She had been granted a single practice game against a puppet controlled by the tortoise-wearing old man, in which he had utterly thrashed her in under twenty moves.

After that she was pitted against her first real opponent, the man she was playing at that moment. He too seemed to struggle with the game, but was still beating Xiao Cui. He had taken one of her pieces, while she had yet to take one of his.

To make matters worse, it wasn’t simply a matter of winning or losing this game that she had to contend with. If losing the game simply meant returning to their island without having gained any of the rewards on offer in the Black River Gate, Xiao Cui would not have felt so much pressure to perform. The issue lay in the second phase of this first ‘game.’

In the second phase, the second participants from each group would fight a duel against the other. Xiao Cui’s teammate in this instance was Pei Kexin, while the other man’s teammate was a woman whose face was covered with a veil and wore a dark purple robe. Thankfully the cultivation realms of each duelist had been restricted to Peak Body Tempering Realm for the duel, and no spiritual techniques could be utilised. It was raw hand-to-hand combat. If that rule wasn’t in effect, Pei Kexin would have been sentenced to death the moment she stepped into the arena. The problem lay in the fact that Xiao Cui’s performance in the complex strategy game would directly impact the duel that followed.

For every one of her pieces that her opponent captured, the veiled woman would be granted one unique spiritual ability to use in the duel. The ability granted would depend on which of Xiao Cui’s pieces was captured. The same went for her—if she captured one of her opponent’s pieces, Pei Kexin would gain one extra spiritual ability to use against the veiled cultivation she had to face off against.

“Your sect elders were fools for allowing such an unripe plum to enter such a deadly secret realm. While your life might not be at risk, playing this intriguing game against this lord, your companion’s fate was sealed the moment she stepped onto this platform.” It was just her luck to get matched against an arrogant bastard that loved the sound of his own voice.

Xiao Cui’s opponent reached for one of the smaller pieces on the current board—a bat—and moved it forwards two spaces. The board was a nine-by-nine square, with eighteen pieces in each ‘season.’ The bat was only able to move forwards, but it could move from one to three squares at a time. It could capture pieces on any of the eight squares that surrounded it.

She instinctively reached for her koi. It could move only in L-shapes, similar to a knight from the original game of chess. The bat had been placed such that her koi could capture it. Letting an opportunity to give Pei Kexin an advantage slip through her fingers was unacceptable. Right before her fingers touched the stone koi, she saw the corner of her opponent’s lips curl slightly upwards. She froze.

Even that slip of emotion could have been a bluff. A higher realm cultivator, even when restricted, had unbelievably fine control of their body’s reactions. However, his arrogant personality made her believe it might have been a real smirk. Which meant that he wanted her to take the bat piece. It was a trap.

Her gaze scanned the board, trying to figure out what his game was. How would taking the bat play into his hand? The answer wasn’t clear.

There were no time limits for individual turns. The season could change at any moment, however, which would mean a complete shift in the board’s layout. The seasons were in a constant cycle, the order in which they transitioned fixed. That had been something she learned while playing the puppet in the practice game. She could spend a while trying to figure out what her opponent’s strategy was.

There was a time limit for the overall game, though, which meant that she couldn’t delay forever. After all, if the game ended in the same situation as it was at that moment, Pei Kexin would have no extra techniques while her foe would have one, as well as the benefit of far more combat experience.

For another minute she stared at the board, trying to keep her face neutral but knowing she was failing to completely school her expression. “Did you give up already? There’s no harm in admitting defeat to a superior opponent.” She had to pinch her leg to stop herself from yelling at the man.

Taking the bat wouldn’t expose her koi to any of his other pieces. It wouldn’t open a path for any of his pieces to take her more ‘valuable’ pieces, such as the eel. There was nothing she saw that would make his smirk make sense. Yet she knew that a mistake here would have cascading consequences. If she made the wrong play, she was dooming Pei Kexin. At a time like that, only one thought was in her mind. What would Master Zhao do?
