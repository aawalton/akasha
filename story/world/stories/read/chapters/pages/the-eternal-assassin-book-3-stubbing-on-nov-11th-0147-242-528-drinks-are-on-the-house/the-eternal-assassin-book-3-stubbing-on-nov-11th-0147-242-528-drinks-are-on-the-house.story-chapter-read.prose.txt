Thanks in no small part to his Amulet of Improved Healing, Eternal Regeneration had Asher patched up and ready to go in barely an hour. Despite how much damage Rubble had inflicted upon him, none of it had been worse than broken bones or damaged organs. Regenerating entire limbs or completely missing organs still took him quite a while, and death of course was a whole thing. But anything else?

Asher could just slap some dirt on it and keep stabbing.

Rubble quickly proved himself to be the most impressive of Dormaul’s senior assassins, as Asher blitzed through the rest of the locations Chit had given him one after another with little effort. He faced off against three more senior assassins, not a single one of whom put up anything that could even graciously be considered much of a fight.

First was Venom, a poison-focused senior assassin with both the Snake and Spider elements. Unfortunately for him, Asher was incredibly resistant to poison these days, and Complete Resilience’s purge effect made the senior assassin about as threatening as a rat snake. He actually had far more fun fighting against a regular assassin who was part of the same cell as Venom instead of the senior assassin he thought he’d be focused on. Said assassin had the Shovel element of all things and nothing more, and Asher got to experience what it was like crossing blades with a shovel master.

Novel, but not all that dangerous.

After Venom, Asher encountered his third senior assassin of the day; a woman named Coal. She had both the Rock and Fire elements, and actually reminded him of his time fighting against Slag, another senior assassin with the Iron and Fire elements. Though rather than shooting balls of white-hot fire at him, Coal focused more on enhancing her body, similar to Rubble. Her fighting style relied upon transforming herself into a body of pure, solid rock, and setting herself on fire. Unfortunately for her, solid rock was far easier to deal with than a living mound of rubble, and Asher ignored the flames long enough to drive a pickaxe through her chest, killing her near instantly.

The last senior assassin he faced made what Asher would consider a rather wise choice. Taking one look at Asher’s sudden appearance within her cell, she instantly turned and fled without exchanging so much as a single word. Asher never actually got her name or exactly what elements she had, but seeing as she melted herself into some sort of shining liquid and flowed down a drain in her office, he had a feeling she at least had a metal element of some kind. Rather than attempt to chase her into the sewers, or wherever that drain was connected to, Asher decided to cut his losses with her and head straight onto the big fish. He’d been saving the building on Dormaul’s map that Chit had marked with a finger for last, and he didn’t want to give the senior assassin who had just escaped a chance to reconvene and let Six know he was coming for them.

As he made his way into the deepest, darkest portion of Dormaul’s fourth level, equidistant from the entrance to the city and the portion facing out over the large cliff face, he took a look at his many improvements for taking down three of Dormaul’s senior assassins.

Astral Dip - Lvl 35

Spatial Lock - Lvl 30

Complete Resilience - Lvl 36

Hilariously, despite the main point of him doing all this being to try and get Judgement up from level 19 to level 20, the skill refused to make that one last jump. He could practically feel it teetering on the edge, and he was convinced that if could just witness one more truly horrific aura, that would be the push he needed in order to enter the Stage of Bleed.

With that in mind, Asher grew more and more excited as he neared the last location on his map. Chit’s previous marks had been good, so he had no reason to suspect the man had lied about the location of the Finger. Asher had a broad smile on his face as he reached the last building, and he kept to the shadows as he took in Six’s main base of operations.

Assuming Chit’s intel was good, Six apparently hid themselves in the upper floors of a bustling bar of all things. The ground floor was filled with seedy looking characters coming and going, waving to the bouncer and laughing at one another as they slammed back drinks and swapped tales of the different tourists they’d fleeced that day. There appeared to be a second and third floor to the building, presumably where Guild business was handled and where Six lived, but Asher couldn’t see any sign of stairs through the windows. Deciding he’d spent enough time creeping about in the nearby alleyway, he readied himself and started walking.

The bouncer took one look at him and must have decided he fit among the bar’s clientele, as she merely nodded and went back to glaring at passersby, as if threatening them to try something while she was on watch.

Walking into the bar, Asher ignored the sudden wave of noise and the smell of booze as he searched for his target. Going up was still a distinct weakness of his, so if he couldn’t find any sort of ladder or stairwell, he was going to have to get creative to get to his prey. He hadn’t noticed any windows on the upper levels of the bar, else he would have simply used Distortion to grab their frames and hoist himself up.

You know, a ladder probably wouldn’t be a bad idea to keep in my Personal Rift, he thought as he wandered about the room, acting like he was looking for a friend. It would be a bit silly, but definitely practical.

After doing a quick loop of the ground floor, Secret Seeker began tickling the back of his mind, drawing his attention to a hidden room. He quickly spotted the signs of a fake wall, and he slipped into the astral to bypass it. At this point, he didn’t really care about a few random bar-goers seeing him vanish right before their eyes. According to all his friends, Astral Dip was so sudden and jarring, it probably made the poor drunkards just question their own sanity, or decide they’d had enough to drink for the night.

Finding himself in a small, dark room with a ladder, Asher grinned as he began climbing. He quickly made it up to the second floor, going into the astral in order to poke his head through the trapdoor at the top of the ladder.

The second floor was largely empty, filled with little more than maps plastered all over the different walls with red x’s drawn here and there, presumably to indicate hits that the Assassin’s Guild had recently made. There were sketches of faces tacked randomly to the walls, with heavy, red lines scribbled through many of them, as if someone had had a really bad day and wanted to take their frustrations out on the smiling faces.

Silently, Asher slipped through the trapdoor, stepping into the room and confirming that it was empty. He didn’t leave the astral realm just yet, however, remembering how Talon had fooled him and attacked him from behind when he’d assumed the same about her own apartment.

Using Spatial Lock to make a small bubble around him, he returned to the material realm, cautiously looking around to ensure he was still alone.

Sure enough, nothing jumped out at him. No surprise traps. No hidden assassins. Nothing. Six didn’t even have the decency to reveal themselves and allow Asher to level up his Judgment skill.

“Bastard!” Asher muttered, dropping his Spatial Lock and glaring at the empty room. “I come all this way, and you can’t even show yourself? Where do you get off?!”

Lashing out at a nearby table, Asher’s attempt at kicking it merely resulted in him stubbing his own toe, which only made him even more annoyed with the current situation. Hissing, he swapped one of his daggers with his pickaxe from his rift, smashing it into the table and grinning at its splintered remains. Destroying Six’s property should have made him feel better, but if anything, Asher was just more annoyed at the fact that the Finger’s furniture wasn’t stronger. He’d wanted to destroy something, to let his anger out in full, and the pathetic table had gone down in a single hit.

Something’s wrong… a small voice called out from the back of his mind, but Asher didn’t pay the warning any heed. He’d come here to kill Six, and that was exactly what he was going to do.

“Six!” he shouted, hurling his pickaxe up at the ceiling between him and the third floor to get the Finger’s attention. After all, if the assassin wasn’t down here, that could only mean that he had to be up there. “Get down here you good-for-nothing Finger! I’ve killed two of your kind before, and you’re next!”

Breathing hard, Asher waited for some sort of response. But as the seconds dragged on and Six didn’t bother to respond to his challenge, Asher screamed again, kicking at the shattered remains of the table with his throbbing foot.

“If you won’t come here, then I’m coming up there!” Asher shouted, stomping around the perimeter of the room as he hunted for another concealed ladder. Even as he searched, that small, needling voice in the back of his mind kept insisting something wasn’t right, but he was far too angry to care. As soon as he’d ripped Six’s head from their body, then he could waste the time listening to his conscience or whatever was trying to slow him down.

Rip Six’s head from their body? What the hell?

Hearing his own thoughts repeated by that needling voice almost gave him pause, but Six chose that moment to introduce themselves.

Of course, they still refused to show their face.

“The Reaper, I presume?” a man’s voice rang out from all around the room. “My, what an honor! To think you’d come sweep through my city not just once, but twice in such a short time! Truly, Dormaul must be special to have garnered such interest.”

“Show yourself!” Asher shouted, his anger bubbling anew at the fact that Six didn’t even have the decency to show his face. He’d come all this way, and was so close to leveling up Judgement, only for the Finger to not even let Asher lay eyes on him. It made Asher angry like never before, and he screamed as he stabbed one of his steel daggers into the wall. Yet when his blade scraped off, revealing that the walls of this room on the second floor were actually far stronger than they first appeared, his anger merely doubled again.

“Why would I come down there when you’re in such a foul mood?” Six’s voice laughed, the laughter echoing in Asher’s ears and making him see literal red as a blood vessel popped in his eye. “Here I am trying to run both a successful bar and the entire underside of Dormaul. I don’t exactly have the time or the patience to deal with a toddler throwing a tantrum in my workroom, now do I? You understand, don’t you?”

“I’ve killed things more dangerous than you could possibly imagine!” Asher roared, his heart pounding in his chest and ears as he tried to figure out where the ladder up to the next level was. “Monsters from the abyss! Millenia-old liches! All of the Head’s Knuckles at the same time! You are nothing!”

Six’s voice actually paused for a moment, as if the man was taken aback by the unexpected declaration, and that tiny, needling voice in Asher’s head leapt at the opportunity, all but screaming in the back of his mind.

Something is wrong! Purge!

Asher was angry at Six for not revealing his location. Angry at himself for being unable to find the ladder to the next floor. Angry at the loud pounding in his ears as his heart seemed to smash itself against his chest over and over again, each time harder than the last. And angry at that tiny voice in his head for not shutting up and letting him focus. Not willing to deal with it any longer, Asher screamed one last time.

Shut up! You want me to waste the energy right before a big fight? Fine! Here you go!

Triggering Complete Resilience’s second-tier effect, Asher snarled as he wasted his energy and removed any magical effects on his person.

Snapping out of the forced rage Six had put him under right before his heart could burst.
