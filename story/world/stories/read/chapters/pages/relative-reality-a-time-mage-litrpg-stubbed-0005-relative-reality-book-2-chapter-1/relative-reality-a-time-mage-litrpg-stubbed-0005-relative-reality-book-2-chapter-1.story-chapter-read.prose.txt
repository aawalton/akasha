Rowan ran across the sand, dodging past roots as they lashed out at him. A blade of wind with a lash of fire within it formed beside him, cutting across his path. He barely dodged it, Twisting Mist bending the blade around his body just enough to allow his continued momentum. He was unfortunately brought to a halt as he felt Elise moving up in front of him, dagger held at the ready.

Using his fifth step to activate Flickerstep, Rowan stopped, turned to the right, and began running once more. The alteration of course put his team off for a moment, but not for long enough.

Rowan’s goal was to reach the other end of the room, of which he was only halfway through. Melody’s Song of Peace was reaching into his mind and forcing it to submit, while Helja simultaneously tried to knock him out with Gasping Breath, pulling the air from his body. He had avoided the dangerous combination so far by amping up Relativity, the increase to his own time stream reducing the strength of both effects. Unfortunately, he was taking too long to get to the other side and the song was growing stronger.

The sun within Rowan’s soul that was a visible demonstration of his mana level was dimming, near to the point of losing its light entirely. He could feel the mana headache coming along. So Rowan decided to try a little technique he’d been working on that he hadn’t told them about yet.

Rowan’s storage power had a strange quirk to it. Storing something used a large amount of mana to do, depending on its size, but the odd part of it was that bringing that object back out would flood him with that mana again. As such, Rowan had decided to use one of the spaces in his storage as a kind of… jump start. Focusing himself, Rowan pulled a massive crate out of his storage, dumping it to his left just as Elise made to intercept him. She smacked face first into it just as Rowan felt a massive surge of mana, the sun within flaring back to life.

Pushing Relativity to its limit, Rowan blasted across the sandy arena, giving Melody a shove on the way past to disrupt her song. She landed with a thud in the sand just as Rowan hit the other side of the arena, slapping his hand against it just as he dropped Relativity to move back to a normal time stream.

“Ha!” he shouted.

“Ow,” Melody groaned from the floor.

Long months had passed since the ruptures in reality had been closed, and Rowan and his team’s six month term in classes at Birkston Adventurer Academy was coming to an end. They had been furiously training in that time, now compelled by more than the drive to become an adventurer. Dawn had given them some cryptic warnings about dangerous times coming in the near future and, despite the city of Lyel settling down significantly since Dawn’s taking over governance, they were taking those warnings seriously.

No more opportunities to gain further Stones of Power had arisen yet, despite that, a number of Rowan’s team had risen to Tier three. Helja, Arne and Elise were amongst them, with himself and Melody not far behind. Those opportunities would start popping up like wildfire soon enough though, the day that they entered into monitored field training. Then they would be clearing emergences day after day, selecting their preferred Stones of Power from amongst the loot. Rowan hadn’t been picky up until now, but with only four of his possible ten abilities remaining, he was beginning to think a little harder about exactly what types of powers he wanted to awaken.

With those opportunities soon to arrive, the instructors had been drilling into them how to pick stones to fill out their powers. Unfortunately being picky with powers meant one needed to acquire higher rarity stones. Higher rarity meant more specificity, where lower could provide a wide range of powers.

Instructor Emir had been working with Rowan to help him decide what types of abilities he wanted to get, which basically came down to what approach he wanted to take in the future. If he wished to lean more into the defensive side that he was already heavily invested into, well that was simple enough. To become more of an all rounder than he was though, that was a little harder. He already had three primarily defensive powers, with two utility and the last being the ability that gave him Fracture.

Rowan wanted to go with some truly offensive powers, something that hit hard and fast like Elise or Helja had. Perhaps another bit of utility, though it would need to be pretty specific for him to not accidently get another defensive power knowing his luck.

Before any of that though, they had a tournament to win.

Today’s training was a part of that, and this was the first time that Rowan had ever gotten past his entire team to reach the other side of the arena. The team met at the giant crate that Rowan had dumped in front of Elise.

“That was new,” Helja said.

Rowan patted the giant thing, looking up in admiration. It was nearly taller than Arne.

“Yep, it came to me recently. I’ve been overcharging myself with mana like that due to my work at the docks for months now, and I only just realised that I could use that same effect in combat.”

“So that’s how you had the mana to go so fast at the end there. I swore you were running low and thought you had just tricked us.”

“Nope, it basically refilled me. I could do that seven times every fight if I had more of them, and we can even continue to use the storage space as a storage space, we’ll just have to put everything into the crates. Mass doesn’t seem to matter so much as the area of the object though, so it won’t restore any more or less than an empty one.”

Helja frowned.

“That is if we were okay with you throwing all of our stuff around in the middle of a fight.”

“Broken thing better than broken person,” Arne said.

Helja held a hand up, cutting off the conversation.

“We will have to talk about it in a strategy meeting another time then. For now, congratulations on finally beating us Rowan. Getting by four people, three of whom are a higher tier than you, is no small accomplishment. Your progress has been excellent, and I think we stand a good chance of winning the tournament.”

There was a fire in Helja’s eyes, more than just the actual fire that was within them because of her familiar. Neither she nor Rowan had seen her parents for months now, and focusing on training to win the tournament had been her distraction from that. She put every thought and moment into winning. That concerned Rowan at times, but it was better than falling into a depression and doing nothing at all.

After hearing about William and Esmerelda’s disappearance, Arne had put a hold on his relationship proposal to Helja. Eskel and Heled, Arne’s parents, were not so sure in the match now that the Dawn family was on such shaky grounds. As such, Rowan had been spared the task of being a giver of the heart such that Arne had asked of him. For now.

“Joy and Success,” Fracture said in his mind.

“Indeed.”

Rowan felt the warmth of happiness, both from himself and Fracture. Not only at finally succeeding at beating his team, but at the fact that Fracture knew how to use the word ‘and’ now.

“So now that I’ve managed this, what’s next?” Rowan asked.

Giving the massive crate one more look, Helja meandered over to their piled things and fetched a pad of paper out of it. She flicked through to the most recent page and crossed something off the list, then read the next item out.

“Now we will do some mental strain training with Melody’s help.”

“Which entails?”

“Well, Melody will play her Song of Peace with all of her afterimages, and we simply test how long it takes for all of us to give up, then do it again and do better.”

Rowan gave a long, suffering sigh.

There was no point in resistance, was there? Fighting wasn’t necessary, all he had to do was give up. The song told him so, with its calming rhythms and melodic notes, and it knew better than he did.

No, the point of this exercise was to resist the song, not to give in to it. Rowan needed to remember that.

Melody and her afterimages continued to play, worming her way into their brains to push them to submit. Submit to peace, do nothing and fight no-one. She hadn’t even reached the full power of her slow growing songs and Rowan was just about ready to drop to the sand and curl up. He ever so slowly turned his head to check on the others, but they were doing a lot better than him. That wasn’t because his will was weaker than theirs, but mostly due to the fact that being a higher tier meant that they felt less of an effect.

Rowan finally gave up, dropping to his knees and closing his eyes. Melody’s song immediately stopped affecting him and Rowan got to his feet, suddenly awake and alert, and walked over to her.

“How did I do?”

Melody continued to play on her violin.

“Okay I guess, though not as good as them obviously. You can do better.”

Rowan said nothing more, sitting down to wait for the others to finish.

“Fracture + Help?”

“Help with what?”

“Mind.”

“Help how?”

“Absorbed. Rowan + Fracture. Strong.”

“Sure, we can try it next time.”

Rowan wasn’t entirely sure what Fracture meant, but he had learnt to trust his familiar months ago. While it had taken Fracture a while to get used to its new purpose, Rowan thought that it had figured it out pretty well now.

Out of the other three, Helja was the first to break. Arne and Elise both had innate abilities that strengthened their minds, and remained standing for much, much longer than Helja. Out of the two, Elise went down next having remained standing for nearly ten minutes. Melody’s songs reached their full potential, unable to scale in power any more than they had. Despite that, Arne still remained standing.

The song itself was a battle of will, Melody’s against the affected targets. Melody needed to pump mana into the song, while the affected people simply needed to use the strength of their mind to resist as long as they could. Arne remained standing long enough that Rowan began to wonder if Melody would run out of mana before he dropped. It came close, but after nearly twice as long as Elise had been standing for had passed, Arne dropped to the ground.

Melody let go of her violin and it shattered into golden light, before she too dropped to the sand.

“Damn Arne, you are hard to take down.”

Arne grunted, getting to his feet.

Giving them no more than a minute, Helja called for them to begin again. Her role as the leader of their group had solidified over the past months to the point that when she did call, the rest of them followed without pause. It was a level of trust that they had built up strong so that when it came down to it, they could follow her orders in battle with complete unison. That, more than anything else, was the most important part of their training.

Standing in a line facing Melody once more, Rowan centred himself and prepared. Calming, steady notes rang out and Rowan fought back.
