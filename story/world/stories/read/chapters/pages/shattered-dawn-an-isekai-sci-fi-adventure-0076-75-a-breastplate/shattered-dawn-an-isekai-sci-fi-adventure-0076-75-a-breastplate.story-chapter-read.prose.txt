Elion felt Prator yelling behind him, but couldn’t make out his words. The man’s feet pounded on the ground in pursuit, shaking the floorboards. Elion jumped for the open window. His shoulder caught one of the curtains, pulling it through with him. He fell, tumbling toward the ground.

> << Manifest Armaments >>

Ducking his head inside his arms, Elion prepared to hit the ground as his armor formed around him. His earplugs disappeared.

He struck loose, sandy soil, and tumbled forward, catching himself against a pile of rocks. Prator cursed repeatedly from the window, shouting for guards.

Elion sprinted away from the building, breathing heavily, covering his ears. He hadn’t been sure, but he’d theorized that if Prator issued a command, but he couldn’t hear it, then he didn’t have to obey it.

So far, it seemed to be working.

He should have realized that the earplugs would vanish when he activated his armor; that had been a potentially catastrophic mistake. He disarmed, wanting to save his ascended state for a more urgent situation.

His earplugs came back as his armor faded, and he laughed to himself as he ran into the darkness of the partially ruined buildings. When he thought he’d reached a safe distance, he stopped, catching his breath.

He pulled the earplugs out and listened for signs of pursuit.

Then he began working his way toward the Acid Pits. He still felt a growing tension, a lingering disobedience to Prator that felt like an abandonment of Liora. Elion hadn’t received a direct order from Prator, but he knew he was currently acting against Prator’s wishes, and apparently that still counted.

Elion pushed the urge aside. *So maybe not as flawless a plan as I hoped.*

Picking his way through the ruins, he wondered if he should go to the refinery first, or aim straight for the warlock encampment.

If Venya hadn’t come for Keyla yet, then it would be easier to rescue her from Boff’s, Elion reasoned. Besides, Keyla had the ability to glitch out the machine that captured his blade. Given time, Elion felt confident he could figure out a way to recover his knife, but he didn’t want to count on that.

So he angled toward Boff’s. As he neared, he saw lights shining from the windows. Boff was taking the production requirements seriously, it looked like he had owned working shifts into the night.

Elion scanned the area, looking for signs of Venya and her warlocks. They were not here; come and gone already, or still on their way?

Elion crept noiselessly around the building, aiming for the back window of the sleeping room. He knew he was visible to anyone down in the dark pit, moving along the ridgeline. Peering in the window, he made out sleeping forms in the bunks. He couldn’t see if Keyla’s bed was occupied.

Carefully lifting the latch on the back door of the building, Elion crept inside. He avoided creaky places in the floor as he slipped inside.

Alven sat in the corner by the door to the sleeping quarters. Elion froze, but Alven didn’t react. As his pounding heart calmed, Elion caught the sound of Alven snoring softly.

*Is she in the bunk room, or does Boff have her working somewhere?* Elion wondered. *Even if she is here, she wouldn’t be able to come with me unless I found her bondhold first.*

He heard someone speaking, coming down the hall. He slipped through a side door and entered the refinery.

The huge structure of the refinery consisted of a few rooms. This room, the packaging and shipment room, was lined with shelves, crates, and pallets, some filled with Thrandicite and ready to be shipped out, others empty and waiting for their goods.

Elion ducked behind a row of crates and chewed on his thumb, trying to decide on his next move.

*If I find Keyla’s bondhold and take it from Boff, does that free her from his orders immediately, or does she have to know about it?*

Elion didn’t know. *I need to find her first. She could be in trouble; Venya’s coming for her.*

The voices of workers in the refinery reached him. A man and a woman, who he didn’t recognize, walked past his hiding place. *Maybe there are other, non-owned people working here?*

Someone tapped Elion on the shoulder. He nearly jumped out of his skin, spinning, eyes wide. He was shocked to see Yaret standing behind him.

Yaret took a half step back, startled by Elion’s reaction. “What are you doing here?” he asked.

“I’m looking for Keyla,” Elion said, glancing around to see if anyone else had spotted him.

“Oh,” Yaret shrugged. “Okay. I can take you to her.”

Elion followed Yaret out of the building, toward the stairs.

“Don’t we need protective suits?” Elion asked.

“Unless you’re planning on staying down in the pit all night, you’ll be fine,” Yaret said.

They slipped along the catwalk, heading for the steps.

“Where did you go?” Yaret asked as they moved. “You just disappeared. I was worried you died in that accident!”

“No,” Elion said. “Boff sent me to go work somewhere else. Prator’s digging a big hole, so I was there.”

“Strange,” Yaret said. “Boff’s been desperate for labor. He has everyone working double shifts, trying to pump up production.”

“There’s fighting outside the walls,” Elion said. “I think that they’re worried about producing and selling as much as they can to the warlocks right now.”

“Ah,” Yaret said. “How do you know that?”

“It’s a long story,” Elion said.

As they reached the stairs leading down into the acid pit, the warlock camp came into view. Dark robed figures ran between tents, and arachnatronics powered up. They began riding the spider-mechs toward the refinery.

“We may not have much time,” Elion hissed. “Hurry!”

They jogged down the switchbacks of the staircase.

“What is Keyla doing down here at night?” Elion asked.

“She’s working in the machine shop, trying to fix up the pumps for Boff. Did you know she was an Ascendant?” Yaret stopped, turning to look at Elion. “Of course you did,” he said, then turned and continued down the stairs. “Anyways Boff has her sleeping in the shop, and people bring her food. She got two old pumps running for him yesterday and he doesn’t want anything slowing her down.”

They finally reached the bottom of the stairs. The corroded lower steps creaked as Elion dropped down onto the dirt path. Toxic fumes stung his eyes and mouth, and he coughed.

Yaret lead them to a warehouse nearby, built partially into the side of the cliff.

Inside a faint light shone. Elion’s heart raced, as he scanned for any sign of warlocks. Had Venya recognized him in Prator’s mansion? Was this a trap?

Elion pulled the door open and stepped inside. A whoosh of air blew out of the opening; fresh, filtered air that soothed the stinging of his face. Yaret stumbled in behind him.

Parts of acid pumps strewn around the room reminded Elion of Gorman’s garage. The workshop extended back into the cliff, the back half of the ceiling made up of the natural rock.

He found Keyla sprawled on the floor beside one of Boff’s damaged pumps.

“Keyla!” Elion shouted, imagining the worst as he raced to her side.

She stirred, stretched, and rubbed her eyes. “I’m working on it, I’m—Elion?”

Elion grabbed her by the shoulders, pulling her to her feet. “We have to hurry. We don’t have a lot of time.”

Yaret stumbled into the room. “Someone’s coming!” he said.

Emotion gripped him, and he knew the Bondhold was working on him, calling him back to Prator.

“What—How?”

“I found your mother,” Elion said. “But we have to hurry. Others are coming.”

“You found her? Where is she? Is she okay?”

“She’s… as good as can be expected,” Elion said. “Come on, we have to go.” He grabbed Keyla’s arm.

“Wait,” she said, pulling away from him. “How are you here? How did you escape the bond?”

Elion showed her his earplugs. “I managed to get away before I could receive any new orders,” he said. A pang inside him tugged on him, directing his thoughts back to Prator. *The bond is driving me to go back to him and find out what he wants me to do.*

“That's… brilliant,” Keyla said.

“So come on, we need to get out of here. Venya is coming!”

Elion grabbed her arm again, but she twisted away from him.

“I have something for you first,” she said. “I found a plate I thought would work and I started piecing it together. It’s not done, but…”

Keyla pulled away and disappeared behind the machine she’d been working on. Elion followed her. She held up a curved piece of metal, straps dangling from it.

“Armor,” she said, blushing furiously. “I thought that…”

Elion took the breastplate from her, mouth agape.

“Power up,” she said, and he did. Then he looped the straps over his head. The metal plate hung over his chest, protecting his ribcage and stomach. It hung slightly crooked on him. Another plate shielded his back.

Keyla helped him adjust the straps so that the plates hung straight, holding tightly against his body. “Now power down,” Keyla said.

Elion did. The breastplate disappeared, replaced by his hoodie. The weight of the thing vanished.

Keyla clapped her hands, beaming with delight.

“You made that?” Elion asked, looking around, astonished. “In a cave? With a bunch of scraps?”

“I did! I was very careful to make sure I followed orders, but with all this stuff around it was easy to steal a few moments to craft something for you.”

“You’re like Tony Stark over here,” Elion said. “I can’t wait for the full suit.”

“Who’s Tony Stark?”

“Nevermind,” Elion said. He grabbed her, pulling her into a hug. She melted into him, returning the squeeze.

“Hey!” Yaret called. “They’re coming down the path!”

“We don’t have a weapon,” Elion said. “We have to run!”

“I can’t go,” Keyla said. “Boff ordered me to stay here and fix his machines. I can’t leave the building.”

Elion clenched and unclenched his fists, looking for a solution.

“Come on,” he groaned. “We need to get away from here!”

Keyla just gazed up at him, defeated.

“Look, your mom is here, in New Kairn Tol. Isn’t rescuing her more important than the armband? Leave it behind, and go be with her!”

“That’s not how it works, Elion,” she protested. “If I disobey, the bond will tear my memories of her away from me. I… won’t know her when I see her.”

Elion sighed. “What did Boff tell you, exactly?”

“He said ‘Don’t leave this building. Fix these pumps.’” Keyla offered with a shrug. “So I can’t leave.”

“Elion!” Yaret called again from the door.

“What if I kidnap you,” Elion asked. He lurched forward, grabbing Keyla by the wrists and twisted, trying to pull her into a fireman’s carry across his shoulders.

He was heavier than her, and stronger too. She came up off her feet, but twisted in the air, pulling him off balance.

Her knee caught him in the chin, and he tasted blood in his mouth as he staggered. She slipped out of his grasp and he dropped to one knee.

“Keyla, I’m trying to help you,” he said.

She raised her fists. “I can’t let you. I can’t leave the building.”

Elion reached for her again. “Don’t make me Ascend,” he said, grabbing at empty air as she ducked out of his grasp.

Elion grunted. “Stop—”

Activating his skill, along with a flash of light and the weaving of golden threads through the air. Keyla stiffened, then relaxed, drooping over his shoulder. Her breathing slowed, stopping, and her pounding hart stilled.

He’d stabilized her, locking her in the temporal freeze of his ability.
