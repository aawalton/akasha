The bite of the northern wind felt dull against Nick’s skin as he walked back to the Elpen estate, not even needing to conjure warm air, his body sturdy enough to ignore the biting chill that forced the passing townsfolk to bundle deep into their furs.

Having left the new Lady of House Ultimer in her manse, with strict orders to the guards to obey her from now on, lest they wish another visit to be even bloodier, he felt he’d done as much as he could to guarantee the fragile truce.

They gave oaths, of course, but I know better than anyone how flimsy they can be when spoken by those with shadowed hearts. The threat of ruin will protect her far better than any word.

He found Gaelen and Rhea in the Lord’s solar on the second floor of the manor, the room that had once been Nestor’s and was now once again in the rightful hands. The room’s original tapestries had burned during the siege, leaving a scent of charred wool, but a roaring fire in the hearth fought back the winter draft, and new decorations had been brought out of storage, giving the place a different feel from the last time he’d seen it.

Gaelen stood over a long table strewn with vellum maps and logistical reports, his left arm finally free of its binding linens. Rhea sat in a high-backed chair nearby, grinding dried herbs in a stone mortar.

Nick closed the door behind him and walked to the table. "The Ultimer succession is resolved.”

Gaelen looked up, his amber eyes tired but sharp. "The cousins?”

"Banished from Toneburg. I gave them until nightfall to pack their bags," Nick said, pouring himself a cup of water from a silver pitcher. "Ephor kept a bastard granddaughter locked in a hidden cell beneath the drawing room. Bessalea. She is young and starved, with no allies in the city, but she has her grandfather’s spine. I stripped the cousins of their claims and gave her the seat. It should hold for now, and she’s weak enough to know better than to cause trouble.”

Rhea paused her grinding, exchanging a glance with her brother.

“Yes, I can see how making them entirely reliant on us would be useful,” Gaelen mused, tracing a finger along the edge of a map. "The merchant barons will try to bleed her dry, and the Duke will see it as a chance to install his own steward to control her, rather than fight the elevation of a bastard. She will need House Elpen’s backing simply to survive the winter.”

“An ally bound by necessity is a pretty trustworthy one, even if she’s technically of equal rank,” Nick agreed. "I took a portion of the treasury to pay the Black Hounds and more to replenish your coffers, then sealed the vault with a spiritual lock keyed to the girl’s blood. No one will touch the Ultimer wealth without her consent, which should give her enough leverage to establish herself in her own home.”

Gaelen let out a slow, careful breath as the tension eased from his shoulders. “As much as I would have liked to eradicate that accursed house, we needed a bloodless victory. Messages arrived from the deep roads an hour ago.”

Nick set his cup down, his attention sharpening. News of the war was hard to come by, especially since the Royal Army had descended into the underworld.

"The Crown has taken the First City," Gaelen continued, his voice grim. "The war in the dark is pausing, as the dark dwarves have called for a peace summit. The Royal Army will march back to the surface once the treaty has been ratified by binding magic.”

Rhea resumed her grinding, the scrape of stone on stone loud in the quiet solar. “I have heard the Duke is already planning a Grand Parade through Toneburg to celebrate the victory. He’s probably hoping that plying the victors with glory will make them forget he didn’t join them.”

Nick snorted, but refocused as he considered the political implications, the pieces shifting across the board. The Royal Army’s return meant the King’s justice would follow close behind. It meant the High Nobles of the North—the men and women who had lent their forces to the war while Toneburg remained locked down—would soon return.

"Varg’s pardon should be our priority," Nick said.

"It remains a stay of execution until ratified," Gaelen agreed. "Duke Mordhau will undoubtedly try to paint the Black Hounds as treasonous looters the moment the army arrives. I have heard tell that he wants the mercenaries hanged and their gold returned to his coffers, but we need Varg’s aid until we can reestablish ourselves.”

"I might need to leave soon, then,” Nick said, looking toward the frosted window. His father was with the army, and as a Prestige commander responsible for leading his own force, Eugene Crowley had the authority to ratify the pardon and force the Duke’s hand. "If I can secure his seal before the army reaches the city gates, Mordhau will lose any leverage.”

He wasn’t exactly looking forward to it. He’d already spent enough time playing politics in the north, but he had to see it through before returning to Floria to prepare for his journey to the Capital, where he’d face the King and His Court.

The door to the solar creaked open.

An old steward stood at the threshold. His livery was brushed clean and embroidered with the Elpen gear, yet his face was ashen. He looked at Gaelen, his hands trembling slightly as they clasped before his waist.

"My Lord," he whispered, his voice cracking. "The Matriarch is calling for you. Her breathing has grown shallow.”

The sound of the mortar and pestle stopped.

Rhea stood, her chair scraping harshly across the floorboards. Gaelen closed his eyes for a moment, the mantle of lordship slipping to reveal the raw grief of a grandson, before he forced his expression back into rigid composure.

Nick pursed his lips and bowed his head, intending to give the family their privacy. "I will wait in the study.”

"No," Gaelen said softly, opening his eyes. He looked at Nick with pain and a silent plea not to refuse. "You broke the ritual that would have consumed her soul. Come with us. She would want you there.”

Nick offered a slow nod, falling into step behind the siblings as they followed the old steward down the corridor.

Rosmaria’s bedchamber was thick with camphor smoke, burning sage, and the unmistakable tang of death. The room was dim, the curtains drawn tight against the winter sun. In the corner, her Artifice exoskeleton stood empty and silent, like the discarded carapace of an ancient beetle.

The Matriarch lay in a vast four-poster bed, looking impossibly small beneath thick woolen blankets. Her skin was translucent, the blue veins on her temples stark against the bone. Every breath was a labored, rattling effort, but her amber eyes, tracking them as they entered, burned with the same fierce, unyielding intelligence that had carried her through her revenge, unclouded by her looming end.

Gaelen and Rhea moved to the bedside, while Nick stayed near the door, a silent observer in the shadows.

“Your tears are a waste on me, girl," Rosmaria rasped, her gaze fixed on Rhea’s shining eyes. Her voice was barely a whisper, forcing them to lean in to hear her. "You have work to do.”

Rhea swallowed a sob and gripped the edge of the mattress. "I know, Grandmother.”

“Go back to Floria," Rosmaria commanded, her words punctuated by a wet cough. "Learn from the old dragon all you can. Master the craft. The Elpen foundries will forge the steel, but your alchemy will give it life. Do not linger in this freezing tomb now that you have the freedom to live. Grow.”

Rhea nodded frantically, her tears spilling onto her cheeks despite the scolding.

Rosmaria turned her head slightly, her tired eyes locking onto Gaelen. "The jackals will be coming home. Be ready and sharpen your knives, for while they will smile and congratulate you, they will plot.”

Gaelen inclined his head. "I will.”

“They will offer condolences for our fate," Rosmaria sneered, the expression pulling the thin skin of her face taut. "And they will try to slip a knife between your ribs to take the Forge. Use the Duke against them. Mordhau is a greedy swine, but he is predictable. Feed his ambition, let him fight the other lords for the scraps, and keep the mercenaries close. Varg is a killer, but he honors coin.”

"I will restore our House to its glory, Grandmother," Gaelen swore, his voice steady despite the raw emotion choking him.

Rosmaria slowly drew her right hand from beneath the blankets. Her knuckles were swollen, the joints stiffened by age and trauma. On her index finger rested the ancestral signet of House Elpen—a thick band of dark gold set with a polished ruby, engraved with a gear encased in a blooming rose.

With trembling fingers, she slid the ring past her knuckle and pressed the cold metal into Gaelen’s palm.

"The estate is but mere stone," Rosmaria breathed, her gaze boring into his. "The Forge is but a tool. The blood is the House. Protect the blood, Gaelen.”

Gaelen curled his fist around the ring. He sank to his knees beside the bed, bowing his head. "By the first spark and the final ember, I swear it.”

The Matriarch let out a long, shuddering sigh, her head sinking into the pillows. The tension that had kept her spine rigid for a decade finally began to unravel. She shifted her amber eyes past her grandchildren and found Nick standing in the dim light by the door.

"Crowley," she murmured.

Nick stepped forward, stopping at the foot of the bed. “Lady Rosmaria.”

"You walk a strange path, just like your ancestors,” she said, her voice dropping, the edges of her awareness beginning to fray. “I cannot say I will ever understand you, but your actions are enough. Ten years... ten years I lived in the dirt. You gave me my home again.”

"You earned it, Lady Rosmaria," Nick replied quietly. "I merely helped along.”

A faint, grim smile touched her pale lips. “Yes, quite strange, but loyal and good. See that my grandchildren do not fall to the jackals, will you? That is my last request.”

"They won’t," Nick promised.

Rosmaria closed her eyes. Her chest rose, a slow, arduous pull of air.

Seeing her condition, Rhea moved with sudden urgency. She reached into the folds of her tunic and drew out a hollow, multifaceted crystal the size of a plum. It was a piece of great purity, humming with latent capacity, yet it possessed no power of its own.

She leaned over the bed, holding the crystal an inch from her grandmother’s lips.

Nick had been told this was the old way. A rite born of the northern enchanters' ancient traditions. They did not bury their dead with prayers to distant gods.

"Flesh to dust," Rhea whispered, reciting the ancient vow through her tears. "Mind to memory. Spark to the eternal engine.”

Rosmaria Elpen exhaled her last breath.

It was a quiet thing that carried the fading embers of her life, drifting over the facets of the crystal in gentle whirls. The glass flared, illuminating the dim chamber in brilliant blue light.

The Matriarch’s chest did not rise again. The fierce, unyielding presence that had defined her vanished, leaving only a frail, empty shell lying against the pillows.

Gaelen pressed his forehead to the mattress, his shoulders shaking with silent grief. Rhea clutched the glowing blue crystal to her chest, weeping openly.

Nick’s attention, however, was elsewhere.

When a mortal died, their soul typically frayed, dissolving into the ambient ether like a drop of ink in a rushing river. But a Prestige soul was a crystallized concept, carrying ontological weight.

Rosmaria’s soul drifted softly into the immaterial plane, growing and growing.

Nick felt the shockwave ripple through the ether and knew she was satisfied. She had achieved her purpose, after all. She had reclaimed her home, slain the usurper, and secured her bloodline.

Her soul held no lingering regrets, no desperate clinging to the physical world. It was pure completion made manifest.

A wave of golden and deep blue light, visible only to Nick’s esoteric sight, washed outward from the bedchamber.

It rolled through the manor’s stone walls. He watched as the wave struck the lingering, dark stains left by the siege. He had felt the psychic residue of the Cult’s aborted rituals, the bitter echoes of the mercenaries who had died screaming in the hallways, and the cold rot of Ephor’s demonic magic seeping into the floorboards.

Nick had planned to spend days weaving purifying fire to scour the estate clean.

As it turned out, he did not have to.

The sheer magnitude of Rosmaria’s fulfilled passing was a cleansing flood. The golden light washed over the dark stains, dissolving the lingering ghosts and the abyssal rot as effortlessly as water washed away mud. Any psychic echoes of murder and despair were drowned out by the truth of the Matriarch’s victory.

It expanded downward, rolling through the subterranean layers, washing over the Elpen Forge, and resetting the ancestral home's spiritual baseline.

Only then did the light fade, merging seamlessly into the World's deep currents.
