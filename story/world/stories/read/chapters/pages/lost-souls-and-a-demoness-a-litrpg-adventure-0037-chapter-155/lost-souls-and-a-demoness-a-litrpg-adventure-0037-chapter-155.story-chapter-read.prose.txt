Naomi's world was awash with a sea of frigid pain. Her thoughts were slow and labored, like her mind was mired in a flood of molasses. Everything was hazy and dark, her awareness reduced to the creeping chill that was slowly spreading through her body. Its inexorable advance heralded an end to her suffering, and she was tempted to succumb to its embrace. To let go, to drift away from this realm of pain. But no, she refused to give up hope. One last strand of warmth remained, permeating her body and spirit. She clung to it with all her willpower, praying it could beat back the darkness and gloom. All too soon, though, it began to fade as well. The cold darkness returned, leaving her frightened and alone.

Is this… it?

The end?

No. It couldn't be. She wouldn't let it be. Naomi was not about to abandon her friends. So, she resisted. What little focus remained in her mind poured into her spirit, flaring her aura. Naomi tried to use it to beat back the darkness and banish the deathly chill hanging over her like a shroud. It was the only tool she had in this strange, mental space, but by God she was not going to give in.

As her awareness increased, the pain returned. It was too much, and it only kept growing. She fought and fought and fought, clinging to the last vestiges of life with astounding strength. Just as she reached her limit, the point where she was sure she would slip away, a beautiful golden light descended upon her.

Naomi gasped, and then immediately coughed, blood spraying from her mouth. She coughed again, rolling onto her side and trying to clear her throat of the disgusting sensation. An immense, vibrant energy flowed through her body, as wholly unfamiliar as it was welcome. Everywhere it touched, the pain vanished. She felt the hole in her side rapidly seal, her damaged organs shifting as they repaired and regenerated. The process felt like it took an eternity, although Naomi later learned that the entire transformation was nearly instantaneous. She opened her eyes to see Luis, Jade, and Siora kneeling around her, their eyes filled with tears.

"I… I made it?"

Naomi knew it was a stupid question, but the sheer shock at surviving what she'd assumed to be a lethal wound forced the words from her mouth.

"¡Guau!" Luis exclaimed, gently releasing his hold on her. "I can hardly believe it."

"You did." Jade said with a sad smile. Naomi could see the simmering fury burning behind those bright green eyes. But there was something else too, overwhelming it. A sense of loss.

Naomi's eyes tracked down to the ornate, empty vial clutched in Jade's fist. It took a few moments for her to process the sight, her head still felt a little funny, but then her eyes went wide. She looked at Jade, horrified.

"No… you didn't…"

"It was the only way."Sorrow and regret welled up in Naomi, completely at odds with the blazing warmth lingering in her body. She felt alert, energized, and ready to go. Not at all what one would expect after having part of their abdomen explode. Jade must've picked up on her feelings, because she lay a gentle hand on Naomi's shoulder.

"Don't worry. It was his choice, too." She gave her shoulder a squeeze and stood up, glancing back down the alley. "Now, it's time to find out what the hell is going on here."Naomi made to follow suit, only to realize that she was lying in a disconcertingly large pool of quickly drying blood. Copious spatters and streaks of it marked a path from the courtyard to their current shelter. Was all of that… hers? A shiver ran down her spine at the thought.

"Thanks, Siora." She murmured to the elf, who was still kneeling. She hesitantly met Naomi's eyes. "For helping save me."

She gave a weak smile and nodded once. The elf looked exhausted. Naomi helped her to her feet, trying not to think about the amount of blood covering her armor and clothes. Jade gestured for them to follow, and it was back to business. They weren't done here yet.

The sounds of battle had ceased. Smoke rose in thick plumes from burning vehicles and piles of debris. Rescue teams triaged the wounded and searched for survivors, working around tense soldiers still wary of lingering threats. Jade led the way to an officer who seemed to be in charge, then waited for him to finish issuing orders to a handful of other uniformed men and women. The conversation that followed was short and to the point, as none of them had the time or inclination for pleasantries.

It didn't take long to get the full picture. One flash of their Wayfarer marks was all it took for their group to be brought up to speed. Director Holston had been attacked in her office and sounded the alarm, bringing the base to full alert. Less than a minute later, a portal of crimson energy manifested in the motor pool and immediately began spitting out red-skinned, horned demons, as well as dozens of shadow monsters. They emerged almost on top of the security forces responding to the alarm, and the situation rapidly devolved from there. Dozens of men and women had been killed or seriously injured fighting the supernaturally strong monsters, but many of the invaders had fallen to massed fire as well. Jade could see more than a few of the corpses from the office-building lobby serving as a temporary command center for the crisis, and her stomach churned at the sight.

Life in the Labyrinth was brutal and vicious, and it had hardened all of them in various ways over the past few months. For the majority of that time, though, they battled monsters and demons, only fighting humans on rare occaision. None of them were prepared for the sights and smells leftover from the demon incursion, and it took an act of concious will for Jade to maintain focus on the situation. Somehow, seeing the carnage and destruction here on Earth made it feel more… real.

A few minutes later, a junior officer was showing them the target of the demon's raid: A series of reinforced bunkers that had been torn apart. Jade could actually see large claw marks gouged into the metal doors, and more than one had been ripped completely off its hinges. She peered inside what was left of one of them, a pit forming in her stomach. She'd been too preoccupied to think about what had happened until just now, but an ominous feeling was growing in the back of her mind, and she wasn't the only one. Jade couldn't see any markings on the sealed crates and smaller containers strewn around the wrecked bunker, but the plethora of armed guards monitoring their investigation was the only clue she needed.

"What's in here?" She asked the officer guiding them. "What did they take?"

"That's classified information." He replied stiffly. "If you're finished searching for any traps they might have left behind, then it's time to go."

"What's. In. Here?" Jade repeated the question, weaving power into the words. Allure wasn't quite as potent in her human form, but she'd raised its level more than enough for it to be an effective tool in any circumstance. The man in front of her, an unenhanced human, never had a chance of resisting it. Luis shot her a look, but she ignored him. Morality be damned, this was too important.

"Our shipment of heavy ordnance." The officer replied, his tone much friendlier now. "Plus, a small number of nuclear weapons modified for tactical deployment in the Labyrinth."Somewhere behind her, Naomi hissed.

"Why the hell were these being stored somewhere like this?" Jade demanded, although she strongly suspected demonic charm magic was involved. "Actually, never mind, it doesn't matter, and I don't care. Tara?"

She glanced back at the tek'kalla, but she was already focusing on a spell. The exotic-looking woman was drawing a lot of strange looks from the surrounding soldiers, but no one moved to interrupt her spellwork.

"Working on it. There are still traces of the portal signature still here." She muttered distractedly, weaving her wand in a series of complex twists. She tossed a handful of silvery powder into the air and it flashed, forming into patterns. "I should be able to track them.""We can't let them get away with anything they took." Luis said, a grim expression on his face. "Not if you're right about what they're up to."

Her theory had never been fully confirmed, but with this attack, the evidence was overwhelming. The demons were going to use the explosives they stole to trigger the ritual that would free their home world from the prison of the Labyrinth, unleashing a horde of bloodthirsty monsters into the cosmos. And not just anywhere, either. Right on Earth's front door. Again, Jade's anger and grief threatened to boil over. Why did this have to happen now? She hadn't even started processing the emotional depth charges that were Naomi's brush with death and the loss of Siora's elixir. The broken corpses of humans and demons littering the area weren’t helping, either.

Later. She told herself, willing her hands to stop shaking. I'll deal with it later.

Perhaps not the healthiest coping mechanism, but this was do or die. From the looks of it, no other group of soldiers or Wayfarers had emerged from the brief but brutal conflict in as good condition as them. They had to deal with this, and fast.

"What are you talking about?" The officer asked, still under Jade's charm effect. "Is there a danger we should know about?"

Jade was trying to think of how to explain the situation, and if it was even a good idea to discuss it in the open, when Tara snapped her fingers.

"Got it! I knew they couldn't go far, not on such short notice."

"Where are they?" Fenrin asked.

"Their exit portal took them back the way we came from, right by the Labyrinth entrance we arrived through."

"We don't have much time!" Jade realized. "If they get to the node--"

"We'll never find them in time." Tara finished her sentence.

"What's the fastest way to the Labyrinth entrance?" Jade asked the charmed officer. Nearby, some of the soldiers standing by on security were starting to shoot them suspicious looks. They had to get out of here quickly, before anyone delayed them with pointless questions.

"Well, usually--""Never mind." Jade cut him off and pointed to a section of the courtyard where the parked vehicles looked mostly intact. "Are any of those still working?"

"Ahh… I think so." The young man said, sounding more than a little confused. "Why do you ask?"

The familiar sound of blaring alarms receded behind them, quickly drowned out by the Humvee's roaring engine. Jade clung to a ceiling handle, not trusting her seatbelt to keep her in place as Luis made a series of aggressive turns through rubble-strewn streets.

"Did we have to steal it?" Naomi asked from the backseat, where she was helping make sure the other passengers were properly buckled in. "They aren't going to be happy with us when we get back!"

"We didn't have time to explain!" Jade said, raising her voice to be heard over the vehicle's squealing tires. "Not much of a choice!"

"Think of it this way." Luis grunted, wrestling with the steering wheel as he wove through a particularly tricky stretch of road. "If we survive long enough to come back, they'll probably fall over themselves to thank us for saving them."

"I admire your optimism." Naomi sighed. "As someone who's practically already died once today, I'd rather not repeat the experience."

"None of this will matter if we don't catch them before they get to the node." Fenrin commented, his presence a beacon of calm amidst the day's disastrous events. "Luis, how far is it back to the Labyrinth?"

"Nearly there!"

"When we're done saving the cosmos, we have to come back here." Tara said. She was craning her neck to look out a window, still taking in the many unfamiliar sights and sounds of an alien world. "I barely got to see anything this time!"

"Focus, Tara." Jade admonished. She appreciated that the tek'kalla was trying to keep things light after the carnage they'd just survived, but they could run into dangerous enemies at any moment. "We need to be ready for a tough fight. We don't know how many of them got away."

From the quick descriptions provided by the L-ReF soldiers, only a few of the demon invaders had survived to return through the portal they'd created in the middle of the compound. That didn't mean that there hadn't been more waiting on the other side, though. Tara assured them that the first portal had to have been opened from somewhere on Earth, not from the Labyrinth directly, so that probably put a very low limit on the number they could soon be encountering, but Jade wasn't going to count on that. They'd nearly lost Naomi today, and she wasn't planning on letting that happen to anyone else. If she had to turn into a murderous monster to stop her friends from getting hurt, she had the resolve to be that person. The pain of losing Alan's cure still stung like a needle twisting in her heart, but she roughly steered her thoughts away from it. There would be time to grieve later. First, they had some enormous bombs to recover.

Shouts arose from somewhere ahead. Jade looked up, seeing the shimmering portal in the middle of the road. This section of street had been meticulously cleared of debris when they'd arrived merely two hours earlier, so Jade knew that the several bodies lying in bloody heaps near the aperture were a recent development. Two men with rifles moved to block their path, raising their weapons and shouting something unintelligible over the sound of their fast approach.

"Luis…?" Jade asked. The man wasn't slowing down, nor was he moving to avoid the two men.

"They'll get out of the way."

"And what if they shoot?!" Naomi asked, prompting alarmed looks from the rest of the passengers in the rear of the vehicle.

"Ahh… We duck?" Luis suggested.

"Actually, I just found out that we're pretty bullet-proof now." Jade commented.

"Those giant bruises I just finished healing suggest otherwise." Naomi glared at her.

"Maybe 'bullet resistant' is a better term." She conceded.

"Hang on!"

There was no more time to talk. One of the men dove to the side as they approached, easily getting out of their way. The other soldier jumped through the portal, and Jade belatedly realized that they didn't know exactly what was on the other side of it. The L-ReF camp, yes, and the dirt road that extended from that side of the aperture. But if the demons had just broken through, who knew what debris might be littered around, not to mention people. Fortunately, Luis recognized the danger too. He slammed on the brakes, slowing their breakneck pace to something a little more reasonable just before they reached the aperture. And then they were through, returning to the Labyrinth once again.

Only, this time, every second counted.
