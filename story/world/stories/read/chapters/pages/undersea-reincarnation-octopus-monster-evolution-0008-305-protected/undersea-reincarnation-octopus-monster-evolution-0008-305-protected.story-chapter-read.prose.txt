Henry quickly found out that getting the orbs to reveal the location of the remaining set was hard. Very hard. By now, he was now on his fifth try, and no matter how hard he pushed or how much conceptual energies he added to the skill, he still failed failed. It wasn’t that the magic was fizzling out or anything of the sorts. No, as soon as the skill tried to latch to one of the spheres with the intent of finding the rest of the set, there would be a pulse of something, and his skill would break down. Dispelled. As if the sphere didn’t want to be used in such a manner. Interestingly, this rejection did not occur when he just used Find the Path without a target. Then the orb just nudged the results, and the golden ray would materialize and point up at the floating planet above.

Zerathstra and Velistraine had been closely watching as he made his attempts, and after the last try, the ape hummed. “Anti-divination for that specific scenario, I’m guessing. But maybe there are other angles we could use?”

“Try to find out where they’d been created,” Velistraine said, eyes intent.

Henry nodded and relayed the instruction to his navigator, only for the protective measures of the set to snuff the attempt out again.

“How about who made them?” asked Zerathstra.

“Rejected,” said Henry a minute later.

Velistraine groaned, then paused, eyes vacantly looking to the side as she thought of another angle. “Try… try to find any books or experts that can tell us more about them?”

Henry grimaced. He didn’t think that one would work, but it was worth a shot. Unfortunately, he was proven right. This time, though, the magic completely fizzled out by itself, as he’d expected it would. Henry was pretty sure that was just because the request was not actionable for the magic. It was too… indirect, for a lack of a better word. Find the Path wanted simple, clear requests, and this one might be too complicated for the skill to figure out.

Velistraine clicked her tongue in annoyance, glaring at the three orbs as if they’d personally insulted her. “Then why do they point up at Seavaria?”

The ape hopped up to his feet and stretched, and Henry followed suit as Zerathstra answered. “It must be the default function,” he mumbled. “Whatever that is… But maybe it would change if we find more of them? We know where the fourth is, at least,” he added, giving the captain a knowing look, and Velistraine’s smile immediately wilted while a pleading, pained groan escaped from her throat.

“I know you’re probably right… but maybe there’s another way? Maybe we can ask around? If we have three right here, then they might be more common than we think.”

Henry didn’t comment on that as it was none of his business, but one way or another, Velistraine would need to deal with whatever’s going on with her and this Attal. Or she could not and Henry could try and go get it himself. One thing was for sure, retrieving an orb whose location they knew was likely much easier than stumbling on the others. So a solution would be needed there.

Still, she was right about one thing. What were the odds that three orbs were this close together?

Henry glanced up at the captain as she slowly picked herself off the floor. “Where did you find yours, actually?” Henry asked.

The captain paused mid-stretch, then picked up the orb before she made it disappear back in her ring. “We found both mine and Attal’s in a buried vault. In the Reach. His was gold. The bastard.”

Zerathstra gave her an arched look when he heard that. “And you let him have it? Did you lose a bet?”

She crossed her arms. “I don’t want to talk about it,” she said, lips drawn in a line.

Henry continued listening to the two ribbing each other as they went up the stairs, but one part of him couldn’t help but think of the one being who could give them answers.

The System would know, I bet, Henry thought. But I got a feeling we’ll get nothing there, even if there was a way to summon it.

He paused. Was there a way to summon it? Henry had never seen it outside of the occasional evolution. He looked up at the two A-ranks ahead of him. “Could we ask the System about this?”

The arguing immediately died out, as if Henry had just doused them with a bucket of cold water. Still, Velistraine gave him a grin and shrugged, while Zerathstra answered. Though his voice was pretty neutral, Henry suddenly found himself tempted to take a step back.

The ape rarely let his heavy presence slip out of his control, and even though it wasn’t pushing at Henry right this second, it somehow felt like it was about to be. Though fortunately, the ape quickly regained control over his aura and answered.

“It knows what they’re for. Most likely. And I’d say it’s listening to us right now, so if it wanted to clarify things, it could. But I doubt it,” he added, with some heat in his voice as he spread his arms, looking above and around for a couple of seconds. “System? We’re waiting. Any help?”

Nothing answered him, aside from the whistling winds and the gentle sound of the waves below.

Zerathstra snorted, then gave Henry a tired smile. “See? Things would be so much easier if it answered some questions. But it won’t,” he said, looking up at the sky, his sharp teeth bared. “It only shows up to push us forward. To ascend. Without ever telling us why or what happened to all those who did before us.” He looked back at Henry and shrugged, a small smile on his lips. “Anyway. We’ll figure it out. We always do.”

Henry nodded and stayed quiet as the three continued climbing up the stairs in silence before Velistraine asked the ape for some spellscrolls, at which point the ribbing resumed, and the tension from mention the System faded away. Still, the change was noticeable.

Not everyone was a fan of the creature. Or intelligence? Whatever the System was, it clearly had rubbed some people the wrong way. Henry was curious to find out what was actually happening with it, and as he looked down at the coppery-orb still in his hand, he wondered whether the System was malicious or not.

Henry had a feeling these metallic orbs might be part of the answer.

A few minutes prior…

“Fish and fruits! Come get your fish and fruits. And the fruits don’t smell like fish,” Maurice added, glaring at the neighboring stall. In response, the older man harrumphed, crossing his arms, but Maurice could still see the half-smile across the man’s lips from his angle. After all, Maurice wasn’t really behind the stall. He was standing right next to the cuts of tuna, invisible, while behind his stall was a red-headed teen with multicolored eyes, smiling at the staring shoppers. Some of them would slow and frown at the youth on occasion, not recognizing him. But most weren’t fooled, of course, their sharp gaze flicking to his real form. Still, he was progressing well enough on his Invisibility because so far, for every two individuals that saw through his subterfuge, three didn’t.

2 out of 5. Not bad, he decided. But that didn’t fix his problem. Because so far, not many passerby were willing to approach. And whenever they looked toward him and took in his produce, they turned their nose before moving on to another shop, and Maurice shot the old man next to him a glare.

Maybe Bran was right, Maurice thought, clacking a pincer before he turned his gaze back to the fish. But if I hide the fish right now, I’ll never hear the end of it. That smug bastard…

Frustrated, Maurice rubbed his pincers together, torn between spite and reason as he hesitantly peered down at the fish. A fly landed on one of the cuts of tuna, but when he tried shooing it away with a claw, it ignored him.

Oh. Right. It couldn’t see him. With a flicker of movement, he gripped it with a pincer, studying the creature while he came to a decision.

Dammit. Yeah… Bran’s right, Maurice was forced to admit. The old merchant was definitely going to hit him with a “I told you so”. And Maurice hated that. Annoyed, the crab crunched the fly between his mandibles, shuddered, then spat it out. Ugh, gross, he mumbled as wiped away the bits. He turned and scuttled toward the fruits to store them away, but before he could, someone spoke behind him.

“Young man? Are those Sparkfin tunas?”

Maurice turned, then tilted his eye-stalks back to take in one of the largest humans he’d seen so far. The man was wearing a set of brown pants cut at the knees, and an open red and black vest that revealed a pristine yellow and blue shirt. For a quick second, he glanced toward Maurice’s real body before he resumed examining the cuts of tuna, and behind him two young people were still staring at his illusion. A girl and a boy. Teenagers. Probably the same age as Sera. And behind them, a small group of people were glancing around, wearing similar black and red vests.

This was interesting. New people. Maurice wondered if Henry had seen them yet. Unfortunately, they were skewing his success rate with Invisibility as most of them seemed to be aware of his true form.

Letting out a sigh, Maurice deflated. They still needed to work on the skill a lot more. With a click of his claw, he dismissed his invisibility and his illusion, making the two teenagers jump back with start and using Summon Illusion, once more, he spoke to the large man, who was bewilderedly staring at him. “Hi! Yes, this is Sparkfin Tuna. But it isn’t all that we have available. We also carry Otodus meat, crab meats, and kraken… um. Meat. Yes. Kraken meat only. Nothing else.”

While thinking of the valuable products he had in storage, Maurice almost slipped up and mentioned his kraken roes, but those weren’t up for sale. They couldn’t pay him enough for that. As for the Ambercrisp apples, he had none. He would have been tempted to display a couple just to pull in more shoppers, but Henry was hoarding those.

Maurice rubbed his pincers as he waited for the nicely dressed large man to recover. “So? What would you like?”

The man blinked, then burst out in laughter. “You’re the one running the shop? Ray! Axel! Where’s the captain? Come check out this little fellow!” Turning to Maurice, the man grinned. “You know, I could tell the kid had been an illusion, but I couldn’t tell you were the one who made it! Tell you what, friend. Give me a two barrels of each! Let’s start there.”

Maurice rubbed his claws together in anticipation. Finally, he was getting his first client! Then, realizing he was about to conduct his first transaction, he remembered something.

Wait… how much should I make him pay?

Horrified, Maurice realized he hadn’t thought of much he should be asking for his goods, and a few paces away, a snort told him Bran had realized the same thing. He must have known all along.

A wave of embarrassment hit him. Maurice might never recover from this!
