Derrida didn’t hand him the sphere. She placed it on the ground, winked, then disappeared. She didn’t use a portal, either, she simply blinked out of existence.

Suddenly the tunnel was deathly quiet. The stone walls, the low ceiling, it all felt much more sinister than it had when he’d first arrived. The weight of the odd gravity pulled him down harder than it had before—though that seemed only to be in his mind, he couldn’t really be sure.

This place was alive, after all.

I’m locked in a Void Dungeon, far, far away from my universe, without Roln, without the ability to contact anyone or gain help from the other Xaviers, and I’m about to install a “rudimentary System” created by one of the most powerful beings in the multiverse specifically for me. Yeah, this isn’t where I thought my day would be going at all…

He hadn’t actually tried contacting anyone using his inter-universal Communication Stones, nor had he tried creating a portal or even teleporting, but he knew instinctually based on how easily those walls blocked even the power of his Immortal Soul lens that all those things would be impossible.

Xavier bent down to pick up the sphere, hating the strength of the gravity here—bending down actually made his lower back sore, something he hadn’t had to worry about since soon after the damned System had integrated him—and reached out to it. He stopped his fingers a little short and thought for a moment if this was really what he should be doing.

If I told anyone this story, would they think I was a fool for picking up this thing? Or would they think I was a fool for not picking up this thing?

He sighed, clasped his fingers around the sphere, then stood back up with a groan. The tunnel, he noticed, didn’t stretch both ways. When the portal had disappeared, it revealed a solid wall behind it. This was the start of the dungeon, he supposed, but if it was the start shouldn’t there be some sort of door or entryway to come through—or rather, leave through? How would one escape this dungeon if they so wished?

Maybe this isn’t a dungeon that can be escaped. Not by someone at my level of power, anyway…

Xavier sat down with his back against the wall, facing the long tunnel, briefly watching the torches’ flames flickering, then staring through the gloom farther ahead. He’d thought something would happen the instant he picked up the sphere, but nothing had. Perhaps he needed to activate it first.

He was about to infuse it with a bit of Celestial Energy when he hesitated, remembering Derrida’s warning when he’d arrived—she’d specifically told him not to manifest any spells. And why would that be? Because the use of energy in this place would attract something? He had to assume that would be the case. As powerful as he was at concealing his energy, he clearly wasn’t as good at it as a Wanderer was.

As Xavier looked at the sphere then back down the tunnel, he felt genuine fear. But fear wasn’t the only thing he felt. He felt exhilarated, too. He didn’t know if a small burst of Celestial Energy would attract anything down here, but he had to assume it would. He looked down at his hands. He didn’t have any bone weapons currently created and creating one would require the use of far more energy than what he imagined would be required to activate the item the ancient elven Wanderer had given him.

He could fight with his bare hands easily enough if he did attract something. He was a dragonkin, after all. Melee fighting was one of his specialties—though he supposed he had a lot of specialties.

He’d certainly come a long way from bashing goblin skulls in with his staff… but right now, he didn’t feel as though he had. Not after what had happened with Derrida. The sheer power she’d displayed simply by showing up at his home unannounced. Right now, he didn’t know what to expect from this place at all. His time dilation field was no longer around him—that had been cancelled out when he’d moved through the portal. And something told him he would have to face whatever beasts the dungeon threw at him without it. Because even though he couldn’t sense it, he knew that he was engulfed by the ancient Wanderer’s own time dilation field.

The entire dungeon would be engulfed by it. That way, no time would pass back home while he was gone—not even for the others in his empire.

Xavier released a breath. There was no point just standing there. He’d made his choice.

With the strongest concealment he could manage enveloping him, he sent the smallest amount of Celestial Energy into the sphere Derrida had given him, hoping that was how he needed to activate it—Derrida hadn’t exactly given him instructions.

The instant a drop of Celestial Energy touched the device it rose from his hand and hovered in the air before him. The electricity within the orb intensified, though the crackling made no noise.

A voice emanated from the sphere, sounding loud in the seemingly empty tunnel.

“Rudimentary System activated. Subject identified as one Xavier Collins from universe designated U98452 Version XX248. Permission is required to move forward. Does subject wish to activate Personal System Integration?”

Permission, Xavier thought. Well, that’s rather refreshing.

He paused, straining his hearing down the tunnel. He couldn’t sense anything—not with his spiritual sense or his natural senses—but that didn’t mean he wasn’t alone. He no longer trusted his own senses after Derrida’s appearance.

“Yes,” Xavier said.

He probably should have asked how long the Personal System Integration would take, but that would have just been another form of procrastination.

The orb rose higher until it was aligned with his head. The electricity crackling within it surged, forming more and more bolts until the inside of the sphere looked pure white. The brightness wasn’t enough for someone like Xavier to need to shield his eyes, but even so he narrowed them by reflex.

When the sphere became so bright it was like a thousand bolts of lightning had struck in front of him all at once, it shot forward and slammed straight into his head. Only it didn’t slam. It phased through his skull. A massive pain stabbed his mind. Alarm bells echoed inside his head, the security measures he’d set up to activate whenever he sensed someone—or something—trying to manipulate his mind.

Xavier silenced the intruder alert and breathed through the pain. It sucked, but he’d experienced far worse.

His eyes were still open, and he turned to face the long, narrow tunnel that terminated in faraway darkness. If this little light show had attracted something, the last thing he wanted was to be caught unawares.

Personal System Integration initiated.

The words didn’t merely flash into his vision, they sounded inside his head, speaking with the same voice as the sphere—a voice that was eerily similar to Derrida’s.

Commandeering local resources to expedite integration.

Local resources?

Xavier wasn’t confused by what the notification meant for long. The rudimentary System, or whatever he was supposed to call it, pulled energy straight from his cores. Xavier hadn’t been warned it would have that kind of functionality, and he didn’t much like being surprised by it. An influx of energy poured through his channels straight up toward his brain, pooling there and being sucked away by the sphere—a sphere which must have shrunk small enough to lodge itself somewhere inside his head.

He wondered if it was still out of phase with reality, or if he now had a foreign, physical object inside his brain somewhere.

I guess that’s the last of my worries.

As though to confirm that thought, a sound came from the faraway darkness of the tunnel. A high-pitched, predatory screech. Whatever it came from sounded… hungry.

His concealment mustn’t have done a good job of hiding that energy usage.

Xavier wondered how long it had been since someone had ventured into this Void Dungeon. He didn’t know much about naturally occurring dungeons—the dungeons back on Earth and on other worlds that he’d been on had all been created by the System or by other Denizens, not to mention the dungeons he’d created himself.

But he’d heard a thing or two about Dungeon Cores in his research and from Roln. Left alone, without another traveller venturing or stumbling into them, they could evolve in peace, consuming ambient energy from their surroundings. Though they tended to attract beings toward them to hasten this process.

They craved energy, and out here in the void, where there was none, this one would be starved.

Personal System Integration has reached 1%.

At the notification, more energy surged from his cores and up through his channels. More energy than he liked, especially since he couldn’t sense any ambient raw Celestial Energy in the area, meaning…

There’s a chance I might run out in here. What exactly have I gotten myself into this time?

Another screech sounded. This one even louder than the first, and closer. But it was distinctly different. Deeper.

Looks like there’s at least two of whatever this is… Ah, screw it. They already know I’m here.

Xavier manifested a spell pattern in an instant. Bones in both his arms grew, pushing outward through his palms, forming together to create the shaft of a bow. He grew sinew for the bowstring, connecting it to the bow in a flash, then grew an arrow and nocked it.

The whole thing took a fraction of a second.

There were a whole host of other spells he could manifest, but for the first time since his early forays into the Tower of Champions he was actually worried about his energy usage.

He pushed his spiritual sense, strengthened by his Immortal Soul lens, down the dark tunnel. Actually, he’d been pushing it down the tunnel this entire time. Now, he pushed all of his focus onto it, yet he still couldn’t sense anything.

Then, he saw something through the faraway darkness. It bounded toward him, kicking up what looked like a black cloud in its wake as it made its way to the farthest spot the light from the torches could reach.

I knew that darkness wasn’t natural…

Just like with Derrida, his spiritual sense told him nothing was there.

The Void Dungeon-created beast looked something like a mix between a lion and a goddamned octopus. It had six legs, and while they were covered in thick fur, they didn’t appear to have any bones. So perhaps bound hadn’t been the right word for how this thing moved—it propelled itself forward in a way he simply didn’t have a word for. Its head was lion-like in that it had a great big mane, but he supposed that was where the similarities disappeared, as its mouth was circular. It opened that mouth wide, showing rows and rows of teeth.

It looked like something from a nightmare.

Personal System Integration has reached 2%.

Guess this damned System isn’t ready to clue me in on what this is. But I don’t need to know what it is to kill it.

Before the Void Dungeon beast could reach him—the instant the damned thing became visible—Xavier loosed the arrow he had nocked. Another one appeared in its place.

Nock, draw, loose. Nock, draw, loose. And repeat.

He had ten of them flying through the air before the first made contact.

Thunk.

The first arrow hit, followed by several more thunks.

Not one of the arrows slowed the beast down. As the last arrow hit, a second lion-octopus-nightmare beast appeared.

Personal System Integration has reached 3%.

Xavier should have been afraid—and, admittedly, fear was pulsing through him, making his heart beat rapidly and his breathing shallow—but even so he had a great big smile on his face.

This was something new. A challenge.

All he had to do was make sure he came out of it alive.
