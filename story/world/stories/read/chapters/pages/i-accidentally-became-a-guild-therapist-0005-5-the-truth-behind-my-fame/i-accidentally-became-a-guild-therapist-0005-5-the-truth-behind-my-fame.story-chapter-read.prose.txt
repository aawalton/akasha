ARC I: THE ACADEMIC EXILE

SYNOPSIS: Livia is reeling from the discovery of her own virtual cult. Guild Master Miles Vexley pushes her further, revealing the terrifying extent of her real-world viral fame—complete with ASMR, memes, and a playable class petition. Confronting the absurdity of being an international celebrity while stuck in a fantasy game, Livia demands answers, forcing Miles to reveal the guild’s true motive: they need her, and they will protect their therapist at all costs.

Livia Marcelline Quinn, the freshly minted Mental Supporter Lv. 2, was still recovering from the guild’s unhinged shrine.

She had been sitting on a crate for twenty minutes trying to process it. The body pillows. The statue. The crate she had closed with both hands. The bikini pillow Bron had held up with the energy of someone presenting a trophy.

Her Therapist’s Log bore the scars: Shrine: Existential horror. Issue: I am a meme. Approach: Scream internally.

The Bloodbath & Beyond base camp sprawled around her in its usual state of aggressive chaos—glowing runes on every surface, weapons abandoned at ergonomically puzzling angles, and a slime near the water trough doing what could only be described as a breakdance routine. Nobody was watching. Nobody thought this was unusual.

I am their therapist now. Livia stared at the slime. I flunked psych, and here I am, babysitting sword-swinging maniacs and a slime with rhythm.

[Achievement Unlocked: Self-Aware Doormat. +10 Irony Points (Useless)]

“Wow, system, tell me how you really feel,” Livia muttered, swatting the air. Her progress bar read Mental Supporter Lv. 2: 200/2000 EXP. She was 1,800 EXP away from the next level and approximately one more shrine discovery away from a complete psychological collapse.

Who makes a waifu shrine for an NPC? she thought, her face still burning. Who has the time? Who sits down and thinks, yes, today I will carve a statue of the background support character?

Miles Vexley, the Psychoblade Commander, sauntered over with the timing of someone who had been watching her spiral from a comfortable distance and had decided she’d spiraled enough.

“Yo, Buffer,” he drawled. “Still brooding over the shrine? It’s art. Embrace it.”

Livia shot him a deadpan glare. “Art? It’s a felony in at least three dimensions. And stop calling me Buffer.” She clutched her notepad. “I’m here to fix your issues, not star in your fan club.”

Miles chuckled, producing a glowing orb from somewhere inside his armor. “Then you’re gonna love this.” He tossed it to her.

She fumbled the catch. The orb activated anyway.

A holographic screen bloomed in the air between them, casting blue light across her gray robe.

The header read:

r/LiviaWaifuHub — Members: 75,491 — ↑ 25,244 since yesterday

Since yesterday. The number had gone up by twenty-five thousand. Since yesterday. Livia had been asleep for part of yesterday.

She scrolled.

The top post was a video clip, three minutes and forty seconds, titled:

“[FULL CLIP] Immortal Tank Has Existential Breakdown, Gets Fixed by Random NPC — 2.4M views”

The top comment read: “I came for the dragon raid content and I have been sobbing for eleven minutes. I don’t even play this game.” 94k upvotes.

The second post was a meme. It was a photo of her from the clip—her expression during Bron’s breakdown, the one where she’d gone very still and very careful—with the caption:

“me when someone starts talking about their problems” / “me 0.3 seconds later because I literally cannot help it”

It had been reposted 340,000 times.

That is my face, Livia thought. That is my face, on 340,000 posts, captioning a joke about compulsive empathy.

It is not inaccurate. That makes it worse.

She scrolled further.

There was the wiki.

Miles had mentioned it in the basement—a twelve-thousand-word analysis of her therapeutic methodology—but she hadn’t actually looked at it until now. It was extensive. Absurdly extensive. A full page: Biography. Class Overview. Notable Sessions, with timestamps. A section titled Theories (Unconfirmed) that ranged from mildly insulting to deeply unhinged.

And then, at the bottom, in smaller text: Page History. 247 edits.

She scrolled to the first one, mostly out of morbid curiosity about who had started all this.

Edit #1 — [ACCOUNT NAME UNAVAILABLE] — “initial page creation”

The timestamp was two days before Bron’s breakdown.

Two days before the video that had made her go viral in the first place. Two days before anyone on this server had a reason to know her name.

Livia stared at the date for a long moment.

That has to be wrong. A formatting error. A bug. Timestamps glitch. This is a video game wiki, not a legal document.

She refreshed the page. The date didn’t change.

[System Note: Page metadata anomaly logged. Classification: Unresolved. Filed under: Things We Are Not Discussing Today.]

I am not discussing that today, Livia agreed, and closed the tab before she could think about it any harder.

She kept scrolling anyway, because stopping felt worse than not knowing.

There was merch. An actual merchandise store, licensed, with a banner reading OFFICIAL LIVIA COLLECTION. A white mug with the words “Breathe in for four” in serif font. A gray hoodie styled after her robe, sold out in all sizes. A plushie—small, soft, accurate, wearing a tiny version of her actual robe—for $24.99, currently backordered six weeks.

And pinned at the top of the subreddit, in bold:

[📌 MEGATHREAD] PETITION: ADD LIVIA AS A PLAYABLE CLASS — Current Signatures: 10,847 — Goal: 15,000

Top Comment: “Her entire kit would just be: listen, reflect, suggest breathing. And it would be the most overpowered support class in the game.” — 👍 45k

Livia closed the hologram.

She sat with it for a moment.

I failed practicum, she thought. I got a C-minus on my thesis. I have never in my life been described as overpowered at anything.

And I have eight million people playing my voice to fall asleep.

“This is worse than the shrine,” she said, to no one in particular. “I’m a meme across universes. There is a hoodie. It sold out. I don’t—” she stopped. “Why did it sell out? Who buys a gray robe hoodie?”

Miles looked at her with the expression of a man who owned one but was not going to say so.

Bron stomped over before she could continue her spiral, holding something aloft with both hands like a religious artifact.

It was the plushie.

It was her plushie. Small, soft, unnervingly accurate, wearing a tiny stitched version of her actual robe. It looked at her with its little embroidered eyes and Livia felt something in her brain quietly disconnect.

“Buffer! Snagged this from a loot crate! Limited edition!” Bron cradled it with a gentleness that did not match a man who could tank a dragon bare-handed. Then, unexpectedly, his enthusiasm dimmed into something quieter. He turned the plushie over in his huge hands. “We had someone, kind of. For the feelings stuff. Before you.” A pause. “Wasn’t like this, though.”

Livia went very still.

“Bron—” Miles started.

“I’m just saying,” Bron said, oblivious to whatever had crossed Miles’s face, “this is better.” He hugged the plushie to his chest, the moment already passing for him, already forgotten. “Anyway. Can I get a body pillow too? Matching set.”

“No,” Miles said, before Livia could even process the question. His tone had gone flat in a way she recognized—the tone of a man closing a door quickly, before anyone could see what was on the other side of it.

Livia opened her notepad. She did not write about the plushie.

Bron mentioned someone. ‘Before you.’ Miles closed it fast. Filed. Ask later. Not today.

Phina arrived, Glimmer trailing at her ankles, and surveyed the holographic display with the expression of someone viewing an exhibition she had personally curated. “Your fame is a celestial hymn!” she declared. “The outer realm’s mortals chant your name, Empath Eternal.” She turned to Livia with serene approval. “We have fortified the shrine with a firewall. Seventeen layers. No unauthorized access.”

“Firewall?” Livia’s voice came out slightly higher than intended. “You’re guarding it? You fortified the abomination?”

Nyx materialized beside a crate—his flashlight casting its steady pink beam across the floor—and nodded without meeting her eyes. “Had to,” he mumbled. “Fans kept hacking the server to get into the shrine room. They wanted…” he paused, clearly pained by what he was about to say, “your voice lines. The ones from Bron’s session.”

A beat of silence.

“They were going to extract my voice from an in-game recording and put it in an ASMR video.”

“They already did,” Nyx said. “Eight million views.”

Right.

Alaric staggered into view, holy aura flickering, flask at half capacity. He pointed at Livia with the conviction of a man who had just had a very good idea. “Buffer! I’ve got 500 gold on you hitting 100k subscribers by next week. Don’t tank my odds.” He hiccuped. “Also there’s a community betting pool. Current options: one, you stay NPC. Two, you go rogue and become a villain arc. Three—” he checked a crumpled paper, —“you end up dating the slime.”

Glimmer jiggled.

Phina placed a hand over her heart.

Livia’s notepad nearly snapped in half. “I am not dating a slime! I’m not dating anyone! I’m code! I have zero romance stats! My entire stat sheet is emotional!”

[System Note: Correction — you have +1 Charisma from day 3. This is technically a romance-eligible value. The system does not make the rules. The system merely reports them.]

Livia stared at the notification.

+1 Charisma.

She was going to get a villain arc.

She turned to Miles. “Why is this happening? I wanted to sit on a bench and not exist. That was the plan. Bench. Invisibility. Survival.”

Miles was quiet for a moment. Not the theatrical quiet he used when he was about to say something cutting—a different kind. He looked at the holographic screen, still faintly glowing between them, and something moved behind his eyes that wasn’t his usual performance.

“You’re good at this,” he said. Not “not bad, rookie.” Not sarcastic. Just… stated. “You listen. Actually listen. People feel it.”

He crossed his arms, and Livia recognized the posture—she’d seen it on enough patients to know it: the arrangement of the body into a shape that says I am not vulnerable precisely because vulnerability is close.

“This guild doesn’t—” he started, then stopped. Tried again. “When something’s ours, we protect it. Fans, hackers, devs, doesn’t matter. Nobody gets to decide what happens to you except us.” A pause. “And you. Obviously.”

The addition at the end was quick. Almost like an afterthought. Livia’s Therapist’s Insight—still new, still raw—caught the shape of it anyway:

[Therapist’s Insight — Patient: Miles Vexley]

[Surface: Possessive loyalty. Guild protects its own.]

[Underneath: Terror of losing something he decided to keep.]

[Note: He added “and you” because he remembered, mid-sentence, that people are not property. Progress: marginal. Direction: correct.]

Livia looked at him for a moment too long.

He looked back.

His smirk returned, deliberately, like a curtain being drawn.

“Don’t make it weird, Buffer.”

She made a note: Miles. Still deflecting. Still using the nickname as a reset button. Underneath it… something. File for later.

Out loud she said: “Fantastic. My personal security detail is an immortal tank with a plushie addiction, an archmage who firewall-protected a shrine, an assassin who needs a cartoon flashlight to function, a paladin currently running a betting pool on my love life, and you.”

Miles raised an eyebrow. “And me.”

Livia’s pen was already moving before she caught herself.

And me. Same two words. Same margin.

She flipped back a page without quite meaning to, and there it was—yesterday’s entry, same handwriting, same corner: And him.

He’s done this before, she thought. Not the words. The shape of it. Say the true thing, then hand you a joke to hide behind so nobody has to look at it too long.

She didn’t write it again.

She circled the first one, twice, and closed the notepad.

The guild erupted back into its natural state: Bron negotiating with the plushie, Phina composing an ode to the shrine’s new firewall, Nyx retreating to his crate with his flashlight and his feelings, Alaric updating the betting odds on a scrap of paper.

Livia sat with the holographic screen still faintly glowing beside her. She scrolled back to the top comment on the viral clip, the one with 94k upvotes.

“I came for the dragon raid content and I have been sobbing for eleven minutes. I don’t even play this game.”

Further down, buried in the thread, someone had replied:

“Livia feels like one of us. Not code. Not an NPC. Like someone who also had no idea what they were doing and showed up anyway.”

Livia read it twice.

She was an NPC. A glitch. A support unit with zero combat stats, an itchy robe, and a notepad she’d gotten from a Comic Sans scroll.

She had no idea what she was doing.

She had shown up anyway.

One of them.

Her throat was doing something she wasn’t going to examine right now. She closed the hologram, tucked her notepad under her arm, and looked out at the camp—the chaos, the NPCs on their loops, the players who never stopped moving, the guild that had decided, for reasons she still didn’t fully understand, that she was worth keeping.

The quiet NPC days are officially history.

She was not sure, anymore, that she wanted them back.
