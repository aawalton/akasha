Chapter 2

“YAHHHHHAAHHHH!” Marcus screamed for help, then held up his hand as if he wasn’t a mage who had flown out here easily. As if it wouldn’t take more than a modicum of his power to focus his will into intent and then use that intent to force the world to make him fly once more.

Ava watched, as Marcus lost focus, which itself wasn’t bad. It was what came after the losing focus that annoyed Ava, as she watched Marcus begin to fall. Looking at his suddenly descending body, a body fully capable of free and independent flight plummet mercilessly to the ground, Ava could only glare. Glare and count.

In her mind, Ava was already calculating intersecting speeds, trajectories, and above all she knew that if he didn’t recover on his own, then she would do the worst thing possible for Marcus’s growth. By helping him now, she would all but force Marcus to be dependent on her for today and going forward. Knowing that if he doubted himself, he wouldn’t be afraid to fall, for he would know someone would always be there to catch him. That is why she stared, annoyedly at Marcus.

As he fell, Ava tapped her finger, one because it had a slightly calming effect on her, two because it signified that she was impatient with the fact that Marcus was still falling. Then there was the third and most important part, which was that she was using her finger to keep count. Each tap of her finger over her crossed arms helped Ava keep the time and pacing needed for when she would finally need to act.

Ava was fast at flying, easily the fastest in the class. No one knew how fast she was, because there were very few times she was able to go all out; her flying to save Marcus would be sanctioned and a legal use of her powers, but it would also do the one thing she wanted to avoid. Namely it would let the others know exactly how fast she could be. That is why she just stared down at her friend who still had a look of hopeful helplessness filling his face as he looked up and dreamed that he would be saved.

“Should we get him?” Phoebe asked, having turned around and looking down at the clearly panicking Marcus. Ava for her part just felt offended. First, she knew how much Marcus secretly pined for Phoebe, and how much he favored her opinion over the others in their class. First, she was a Kirkland, meaning she was one of the three great lineages of the city. Second, she was relatively attractive and always gave Marcus a hard time. Though Ava was certain she had caught Phoebe giving Marcus lustful stares when he wasn’t looking, meaning that there was a chance. A slim chance, but a chance nonetheless that if Marcus didn’t totally botch this moment, he might have a shot of both dating the girl of his dreams, while improving his own family’s social standings within the city. That is of course, assuming he didn’t commit suicide by incompetency along the way to class. That is why Ava decided to nip this in the bud and try to help Marcus regain some of his image that might be lost, spiraling off somewhere as he falls and cries for help. Holding out her hand in a halting gesture, Ava spoke.

“No, he is just being dramatic,” Ava said, projecting her voice so that it would hopefully reach Marcus, even as he continued to fall.

“You sure? I don’t think he’s faking it,” Phoebe asked, a bit of tension filling her, as she began to clench and unclench her fists, a sign she was getting anxious. Realizing that even spectators were getting anxious on behalf of Marcus, Ava decided that she needed to go to the next stage of her plan.

“One second, I’m going to have to go motivate him,” Ava declared, dropping her hand and immediately turning her body to dive forward; she then pulled herself downward with the same kinetic energy that had been letting her hover in place a moment ago.

Instantly, Ava felt the thrill and rush of air flow around her face, as she seamlessly tucked her arms into her sides, her torso and legs straight as she let her body flow faster through the air. Normally she would kill for such a chance, to be able to fly at her fastest speeds, while aided by the pull of gravity, and part of her truly loved this sensation. Unfortunately, the joy she was feeling was mostly abated by the mounting fear rising in her gut, as she barreled faster towards her falling master, and friend.

The warmest looks she ever received came from the freefalling Marcus, who actually looked relieved while staring up, arms and legs flailing in the air as he saw that Ava was coming. That look of relief alone caused a number of emotions to rise and do wild things to her already dopamine-filled brain, for she knew that he knew he was safe, that Ava was here and that she would not let him crash. While it was a flattering thought, it was the last thing that she wanted right now, as it would only cause him to doubt himself more, something he always struggled with, especially as he always compared himself to her.

“Help me,” Marcus cried out, excited tears forming on his face and flying up, splashing Ava as she drew closer. His hand extended as he waited for the inevitable safety that was bound to come. Seeing him, Ava realized she needed to get mean to make Marcus focus.

“Save yourself!” Ava shouted back, still barreling downward at an intercepting path, but finally close enough that she could let up on her speed and mostly rely on gravity to help her keep with Marcus’s decent speed.

“What?” Marcus asked, the look of hope he had gone in a second, as he could only stare back at Ava in a look that was equal parts confusion and betrayal.

“I said!” Ava shouted, pulling up right next to Marcus as he fell, her face now parallel to Marcus so they could see eye to eye, “save yourself!”

“But-but—” Marcus stammered out the chill of the wind swooping up around him as he continued to plummet.

“Look, no one’s going to save you, but yourself!” Ava declared, her voice going colder than the biting winds that were tearing at Marcus’s face as he continued to fall, and have his own spit, saliva, and tears run off in freezing waves across his face. With that revelation, Marcus began to panic and did the worst thing possible, he turned his head to look down and see just how close all of the buildings and walkways were below. The fact that he could easily make out individual cars and people showed just how close he was, which only further panicked him. Seeing his reaction, Ava shook her head and then proceeded to get nasty. Nasty enough so that Marcus would listen.

“Look at me,” Ava shouted, but Marcus was too lost as he couldn’t look away from the ground that seemed like it was getting inevitably close. That is when Ava did something she thought she would never do to anyone, let alone someone who had authority over not just her, but her family as well. Still, desperate times called for desperate measures, which is why she reached out and slapped Marcus across the face.

An audible sound and instantaneous jolt of pain ran through Ava’s hand letting her know that it was a solid strike. Marcus who had been lost in his screams, suddenly stopped and stared at Ava, at which point he began babbling.

“You just hit me—” Marcus began, but was cut off by a now visibly annoyed Ava, as it was clearly getting too close to where even Ava felt comfortable with her ability to save Marcus. That’s when Ava reached in deep to her very core, and shouted her next response, cutting off Marcus’ building tirade immediately.

“Shut up!” Ava began, then seeing that Marcus who was falling to his death, had just been slapped, and then had been yelled at by the person he thought was his friend and attendant just stared blankly for a moment. That was when Ava continued her motivational speech, “shut up, and turn on your stupid mage brain. No one is going to save you, which is why you need to get your head out of your ass, and wrap yourself in your stupid air current and stop looking stupid in front of your girl.”

“My girl?” Marcus asked, then he seemed to pause as he looked up and saw the free floating dot overhead that was clearly Phoebe who was now standing, well floating freely overhead, but she was no longer alone. Looking up, Marcus seemed to pause, then Ava could see the moment his mage brain actually kicked on. There was a faint glow just behind his eyes, the sign that Marcus was accessing his core. Then she saw the moment energy began pooling out around his frame, wrapping him in a protective coating. Seeing the movement, Ava began slowing her descent to keep up with Marcus’s gradually slowing pace. The entire time, Marcus’s hate filled eyes were focused solely on Ava, which was good as they were getting increasingly closer to the ground.

“You’d let me die, wouldn’t you?” Marcus asked, anger filling his voice, but anger was good. Anger caused Marcus to focus his mind on two points, flying and now proving Ava wrong. Seeing the sudden surge of anger, Marcus did the impossible, or what should have been impossible moments ago, as he first fought back against the full weight of gravity and his inertia, until he first reached equilibrium, and then finally began to lift off. As he moved and slowed his descent, Ava easily kept up, using her mind to instantly shift its weight, trajectory, and amount of force applied to keep up with Marcus’s manipulation of the wind. Comparatively speaking, Ava felt this was much easier for a Telekinetic-II to fly than for an air elementalist, but she wouldn’t dare make that statement now. Instead, she doubled down on the hate and anger that Marcus was showing, as it seemed to be working in keeping him focused.

“Damn straight I would, I’d let you hit the ground, then dab my bread in your blood for seasoning,” Ava declared.

“What, eww—” Marcus declared, hearing the words and then dismissing them as he shook his head and used his rage at being left to die, to fuel his mad descent upwards. As he rose, Ava kept pace with him.

“Get away from me! Traitor!” Marcus cried out, waving his arm at Ava, who easily dodged the clumsy swipe, but did keep her more than arm’s length distance after the attempted attack.

“Never, you are going to have to fly faster than me, if you want to get rid of me,” Ava declared, making sure to keep Marcus motivated so he didn’t lose focus on his flight, or his anger that was helping him maintain his flight path and surprisingly good speed. Well good for Marcus, who had always been a bit weaker at Air manipulation, compared to Lightning or Fire, the two main attack elements, and the elements his Aurillius family was best known for.

“Oh yeah!” Marcus shouted back, as he began to truly open up his mana circuits, causing more and more air to not only push him up, but circle back around and almost push Ava down and away, before being cycled back into him, to continue to propel him forward in a mage’s wind tunnel. While the wind did surprise her, Ava was able to adjust and used the resistance caused by the circulating wind tunnel air as extra training for her own flight practice.

“Yeah, and thanks for the gentle breeze by the way, feels nice,” Ava added, her taunting words continuing to have their intended effect by forcing Marcus to push himself that much farther and that much faster. Within a minute they had covered the entire distance that Marcus had fallen. Then by the second minute, Ava could see the telltale signs of mana fatigue accumulating in Marcus. Having been around him so long, she was well aware of the signs of exhaustion, for in her eyes, mana fatigue stood out in everything.

Mana fatigue, or corruption as mages called it was a natural byproduct of excessive mana use. It was the reason that purgers, like Ava, had a job and structured spot in society. From what she could tell, all mana corruption looked the same to all purgers. It was also the fact that if you could see corruption building for a certain type of mana that meant you could absorb that corruption. This was why mage families focused on getting capable purgers on their payroll, as they needed purgers to be attuned to the wastes created by their elements to be useful. This is why purgers like Ava were so highly coveted in the mage society, as she was one of the few purgers who could see and interact with the corruption associated with every element, meaning she was valuable to any mage, regardless of their elemental affinities.

When Ava first realized she was an all source purger, she was elated. It wasn’t until later in life that she realized just how close to amazing her life could have been. For here she was, in a world of magic and mystery, but her power amounted to the equivalent of being the world’s best janitor. In a way it was great, as it meant job security, as most mages had more money than sense and would spend handedly on keeping a purger like her on their payroll for the prestige of such a hire alone. But there was a deeper reason why Ava loved being a purger, a reason that she didn’t realize until much later in life, when she began testing her purging skills on Marcus and other Aurillius mages, and guests. Namely, the fact that each time Ava drew corruption from other mages, she gained power. Not much power, and nothing that would show up on any scans, but she could feel it, the power flowing directly through her own body and enhancing her muscles and strengthening her bones. That was why Ava never turned down a request from a mage to be purged. Not only did it ultimately look good on the Aurillius family for being magnanimous with their resources, but it also helped Ava get stronger.

There was a certain level of pain that came from purging, but that was a minor fleeting sensation, compared to the long-term effects of that fire burning away the impurities of her body, and filling her with strength. That is why Ava felt a bit of drool forming in her mouth as she saw the delicious corruption building within Marcus as he pushed himself harder and faster than he ever had before.

“You’re going to have to go faster than that, if you want to ditch me,” Ava pressed, her words pushing Marcus to continue to use his rage to fuel him, burning his reserve more, while causing his corruption levels to skyrocket. Most mages would stop when they felt this level of burn, but then again, most mages didn’t have an Ava nearby flying neck and neck with them, as they continued forward.

“Oh yeah!” Marcus wheezed out, gritting his teeth as he began going into his final sprint towards the floating island, and momentarily pulled away from Ava. That is, he pulled away, until Ava easily pulled even with Marcus, who saw the challenge and used it as further motivation to continue to push himself further, doing the magical equivalent of sprinting, as he took one final breath in, and held as he began pushing his mind and body beyond their normal limits. This caused his energy output to be at its maximum surge level, something that was important for all mages to work on, as it would ultimately help them cast larger and more powerful spells later in life. But right now, all it did was cause him pain. It caused him pain, while he pushed his body to produce and use mana from the air and environment around him at an exceptional clip. Looking at him, Ava still didn’t know how this was all possible, all she knew was that mages could absorb mana from the air and world around them, but breathing was easier, but not always needed, maybe.

There were different arguments for why breathing was in fact needed, and that by sprinting all a mage did was burn away all the remaining mana within their body, thereby expanding the channels from the heat and pressure, while also filling the channels with corruption that also expanded the channels. Honestly, the whole thing seemed more like guess work than true science to Ava, which was why she was happy with her lot in life, being a purger. Someone who could gain money and security by merely attending to the whims of the truly rich and shameless of society.

“You’re going to have to breathe,” Ava said, realizing that they were getting close, but it was also clear from the sputtering nature and nearly full corruption flames that were covering Marcus that he was pushing himself too far. This time if he fell, Ava would have to grab him.

“Screw you!” Marcus hissed through clenched teeth as he somehow reached even further down, while gaining a breath that his body instantly began acting upon and converting for both oxygen and mana particles. That’s when he found yet another gear and proceeded to push himself forward, driven by rage, anger, and defiance. Seeing that stubborn look on Marcus’s face, Ava could only feel a bit of pride at seeing him go. That is why, she pulled up next to him, and then when it was clear he would make it to the floating island where their class was held, and the designated landing area, Ava let up.

Pulling up, just ever so slightly, she made it so Marcus was the first to land, letting him have a small victory. A victory that Marcus took advantage of by crashing into the ground, as it didn’t count as being first, unless you actually landed. Unfortunately, his approach was too fast, as he crashed, knees and hands landing at once. Then his momentum carried him forward, as his protective wind coating dissolved, letting him get scuffed up a bit for his efforts. But he did the most important part, he landed, and then defiantly turned around to point a celebratory finger at Ava, who only glided to a stop, before maintaining her usual hovering distance, twelve inches above the ground.

“Eat it—” Marcus began, but then panted, before taking two full complete breaths, at which point he was able to finally end his comment, “loser.”

Ava for her part just rolled her eyes, then taking the challenge for the invite that it was, she floated down and did just that. Reaching out her hand, she grabbed Marcus’s outstretched finger, and then began eating the corruption that was all but radiating from Marcus in delicious patterns.

The moment her hand made contact, and the purging process began, Ava felt the all too familiar feelings. There was the initial gritty feeling, as if she was touching salt, followed by that salt catching fire and burning her fingers. Then finally the sensation of that fire going into Ava’s fingers, where it worked its way up her arms, into her chest and to the area where she swore her magical core would be, if she had been born a mage. From there, it circled around once, then twice, then finally a third time. With each cycle, the intense burning flames dispersed into her body. The first cycle went to her muscles, which would use the heat to relax. Then the second cycle the heat went into her bones, causing them to feel stronger and denser. Then finally the third cycle hit, and that’s when the real magic happened, for despite it being there, despite her clearly feeling something solid, that final burst of energy just disappeared like ash on the wind.

Finally done, Ava looked out of the corner of her eye to see both Phoebe and Mr. Haylick, the classroom instructor for this period, coming down to land near Marcus. A finally recovering Marcus, who was still panting, still tired, but finally able to stand up as the exhaustion that had filled him was now gone, replaced by whatever mages felt after their core had been burned away to nothing, and then had the subsequent corruption that would normally burn them from the inside out, making future casting all but impossible with too much build up, gone.

“Well then, now that you all have finally made it here,” Mr. Haylick began, “the easy part of our class is over. Now is when the true hard part can finally begin.”

As he spoke Ava’s lips brightened in a widening smile. A smile that only seemed to grow in intensity and luster, the more she saw the look of frustration fill Marcus’s face, as he realized the pain was just beginning.
