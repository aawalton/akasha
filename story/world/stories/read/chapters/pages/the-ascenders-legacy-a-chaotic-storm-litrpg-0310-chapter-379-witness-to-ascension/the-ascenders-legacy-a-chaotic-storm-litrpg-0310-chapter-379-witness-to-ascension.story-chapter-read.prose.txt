The Elder Sect was packed with Mythics when Aodhán and his friends finally arrived, every one of them wearing eager expressions.

Thousands had already gathered. Yet even as they watched, more continued to pour in, filling the earth and skies until even the latter began to feel crowded.

"Ugh, the energy density here is something else," Darien grimaced as Aodhán joined them, his nose creased in discomfort.

Aodhán instinctively flared his aura, exercising his authority over storm to wrap them all in a protective bubble. Everyone let out sighs of relief almost immediately, including Aodhán himself, but the essence density still wasn't easy to endure.

And with the number of Mythics still pouring in, it would only continue to rise.

Sighing, Aodhán turned to take in his surroundings. They stood within a vast plain of verdant grass, seemingly created for this exact purpose, if the overwhelming density of runes covering both earth and sky was anything to go by.

"Have you all been to one of these before?" Aodhán asked.

Everyone shook their heads except Nymera and Ashscar, who nodded heartily.

"It was only a few weeks after Ashscar joined the Sect," Nymera said with a smile. "One of the High Elders was ascending."

"High Elder MountainFist," Ashscar chuckled in remembrance. "He was High Elder Zarxan's predecessor."

"Indeed," Nymera said, shaking her head with a nostalgic smile. "And his ascension was glorious. That was where I gained and perfected my path."

"In the same instant?" Seamus asked.

"Not really," Nymera chuckled. "But I laid the groundwork for it that day. I can say with certainty that I wouldn't be where I am today if I hadn't witnessed it."

"Me too," Ashscar agreed. "Though I had more or less laid the groundwork for my path long before that."

Aodhán nodded, drawing conclusions from their words. It seemed the books had been right after all.

Ascensions truly bathed the skies in insights and mysteries.

He had no idea who this Edrick Ghaltris was, except that he must be related to Elder Ghaltris. Perhaps his father. Perhaps his grandfather.

It seemed the Ghaltrises were no small family within the Sect. Perhaps he truly was making a mistake by not considering Serapha Ghaltris as an underling.

"Transmigrants," a voice called out, and they all turned to see Elder Vasmos floating toward them, a wide smile on her face.

She had been promoted after the war due to her immense contributions and was no longer the administrative head of the Special Sect. It had been a while since Aodhán had seen her.

Their smiles widened, and they all bowed respectfully.

"Good to see you again, Elder Vasmos," Sophran greeted. "It has been a while."

"It certainly has," she chuckled. "I was present for your coronations, and I must say, I could not have been prouder."

Nymera laughed softly. "Don't you think it's a little pompous?"

"Or lazy?" Seamus whispered, glancing around carefully. "It's almost as though the First Seat barely put any thought into my name at all. It's literally the name of my path."

Aodhán shook his head in amusement while Elder Vasmos laughed outright.

"I assure you," she said once she finally got her laughter under control, "the First Seat put a great deal of thought into those titles. Such decrees do not come lightly." She glanced at Nymera, still smiling. "They might have been a bit pompous, but they were certainly not lazy."

Ashscar nodded seriously. "They are clothes we have to grow into."

Elder Vasmos glanced at him, then nodded slowly, as though she had never considered it that way.

"That is a very apt description."

She looked behind them then, seemingly expecting to see more people. When she didn't, she raised a curious brow.

"Did you all forget to bring your underlings?"

Her words caused Aodhán to pause, and he inwardly cursed himself for being so selfish.

In his haste to get here, he had completely forgotten that Crixis would benefit tremendously from an opportunity like this—perhaps even more than he would.

Not bringing him was a disservice both to himself and to the boy he had vowed to make stronger.

Without hesitation, he messaged Crixis just as the others did the same. Nymera and Ashscar had both taken on new underlings, choosing from those who had approached them to learn their paths, and Darien had also accepted Su Tianan, Mirelle's former underling, as his second.

After that, Elder Vasmos led them through the crowd of Mythics toward the center of the plain where the ascension would take place.

The energy there was thicker—denser—and Aodhán realized they would be standing only meters away from a core going supernova.

"You'll have a great view from here," Elder Vasmos smiled. "And don't worry. You'll be standing behind the First Seat and the High Elders, so you have nothing to fear."

"Thank you," Aodhán said with visible relief.

Elder Vasmos simply nodded. "Your underlings will be led to you as soon as they arrive."

She wished them the best of luck in what she called insight farming, and a few minutes later, she departed.

They settled in to wait, sitting atop floating constructs as Ashscar shared his own experiences with insight farming.

Aodhán listened for a while, but his mind soon drifted, and before long he found himself staring at his status screen.

More times than he could count, he had been tempted to use the newest addition to his mansion—his Time Aspect Room—but Aodhán had resisted the urge.

Unlike water, which he possessed a basic understanding of, time was not something he could afford to toy with, especially after what had happened to Seamus.

Besides, the last time he had used the aspect, he had drained his core dry for the sake of a single second. He needed more training before he could attempt working with time. But Seamus, who should have been his instructor, was, quite frankly, not walking the path of time itself but an aberration of it—a cheat.

"By the storm, it's true!" a familiar voice exclaimed.

Aodhán turned to see Crixis and the other underlings hurrying over, excitement plain on their faces.

He grinned as Crixis reached him. "You look excited."

"I thought you could benefit a lot from it." He glanced at the other underlings. "You could sit with me or—"

Aodhán smiled again.

In the past five months, Crixis had grown tremendously. His abilities now embodied the concept of inevitability completely, and for the past three to four months, Aodhán had focused on helping him maximize his output.

His skills no longer had any problem striking their target. Now they simply had to strike to kill. And hopefully, he would gain insights about that from this.

But they weren’t alone this time. A seventh figure stood among them, a storm-wrought mane blazing with radiance.

Tier 199.

Only a single step away from eternality. From divinity.

Aodhán couldn’t even begin to fathom the amount of power the man possessed. He couldn’t sense his core, yet words failed to describe the immense pressure bearing down on him.

On all of them.

Touched the marked circle at the center of the plain.

The earth groaned and compressed beneath him, unable to bear his immense weight.

Runes flashed red-hot as they were pushed to their very limits.

Insight.

Not as words or memories. Not even as explanation.

Defense.

A shield so absolute that ruin itself would break upon it.

Then he ascended one final tier.

And the world failed to hold him.
