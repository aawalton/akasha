Once he accepted the ring expansion prompt, Kairon’s attention would need to be laser focused on the ritual and Hush.

So he took a few precious seconds to go over his plans for after all of that. For the sigil.

Each ring came with a pacted demon for his city. Thus far, that had given him Vorathrex and Sythara. They were invaluable allies.

For the second ring, he’d had a choice between a void demon, a brood demon, and a mixed brood and void demon—which seemed to stem from the 50 percent split on void and brood.

If he chose three different rings today, he could expect at least one demon from each, and perhaps some mixed to follow.

That was good.

But Kairon wasn’t ready to settle for good. He needed to exploit The Plane for all it was worth. To milk these demonic powers for as much as he could.

Flipping his claw upside down, he reviewed the page he’d saved from the demonic regulatory compendium. He was pretty sure he’d found a bit of a loophole where his sigil was concerned.

Kairon wanted to employ it on the aspected power readout. Turn 33% of one aspect into 133%. A number that if it worked how he theorized could only be matched by a very specific handful of individuals.

Glaring at the compendium, he knew exactly who those ought to be.

And when he considered footnote 2A on the page before him…

‘Pact draft pools for ring expansion rituals are a dynamic and growing selection. Criteria are discarded for expanded selection in descending order’

If this was going to work how he hoped, it relied heavily on the dynamic aspect of the draft pool.

Satisfied, he nodded to himself.

“Alright. I’m ready.” Facing his allies, Kairon slid the parchment into his cape and chose to begin the ritual to create the third ring of his city.

As soon as he selected it, there was a soft, gentle tremor. It felt like something was shyly reaching up from beneath the ground, feeling at the city foundation. Seemingly finding what it was searching for, the trembling stopped.

CREAK.

The entire city shuddered as what felt like thunder rumbled beneath it. Glowing, violent lines of demonic text took hold, stretching out in an enormous circle beyond the second ring of buildings. Sulfur and iron scented steam hissed, and the power of the plane took hold.

Just like that, the enormous stash of wealth that Kairon had accumulated—the results of weeks of scheming, plotting, planning, and blood—began to be carried out by the claws of the brood.

Meanwhile, Hush pointed forwards. “Remember… Feel the plane… Feel the air… Feel the emotion…”

To his utter delight, despite having the sigil floating in his head, Kairon still felt that connection. It did feel weighted and a bit more dull, but it was there, pulsing in his mindspace. The only theory as to why it worked now and not before that he could deduce was that he established a connection with the source power and his domain.

Or maybe something within himself was changing. Becoming more… demonic.

Putting all of that out of his mind for now, the city lord slowly breathed in, then out, placing the entirety of his focus back on the ritual itself.

Breathe in, then out. “I’m trying. I’ve done this before. It’s just… I’ve added a bit of weight this time around.”

Something Kairon had noticed in these last weeks without the sigil in his mind was that the larger the ritual, the more powerful the connection seemed to become. And of all the rituals he’d done so far, a ten thousand gold third ring expansion was on an entirely different level.

But this time, he wasn’t going to absorb some dark star and let it draw him into another flesh eating bargain. This time, he had his own wager in the game.

Over the last several days, as Kairon spent time with Hush, he started experimenting with source energy. A trick he’d found was that the golden power could be used for more than a simple shield against the whispers and emotions of the plane.

Calling back to his training over the last week, he pulled upon the power of source. He focused it intently in one place.

His eyes.

Kairon’s vision shifted. From color, to nothing, to gold. Everything took on a tinted, glowing hue.

“Okay. I’m doing it, Hush. I’m doing it!” In their practice, he hadn’t yet tried this on a ritual. Instead, he’d stuck to just watching as Hush called upon minor callings from the plane. Wrath upon a pebble or hunger upon a beetle.

Small things.

This would serve as the first large application of his theory in real observation. In retrospect, Kairon did contemplate for a moment if he should have tried this on a smaller ritual first.

But then again, what fun was that?

With his source powered vision, it was like the world opened up before him. He could see what he previously could not.

The cacophony of pure emotion called upon by the ritual before him reeked of so many feelings. It wasn’t of any singular one—hunger, rage, violence, or any other—but rather, of all of them. Every single one, mixed into some kind of articulately crafted concoction of demonic power.

It was just like the callings, only much, much larger in scale.

I… I get it now.

At its core of power, the plane wasn’t doing magic—at least not in the typical understanding of such. It wasn’t using some kind of runic language of power. The symbols themselves had no magic to them. They were a language. A language that when viewed like this looked less and less like words and more like… a recipe or a mixture.

“I see it, Hush! I see it! I wasn’t sure just watching you, but now seeing this, I finally see it! It’s all just a fancy mixture! A potion! A recipe…” Kairon’s mind reeled. It was all so obvious, yet… He’d just always assumed the symbols themselves were the key. That they had some hidden meaning. But the more time he’d spent with Hush, watching as he’d done his callings, the more he found that not to be true.

When you really, really felt for it, even the callings weren’t pure. Wrath didn’t just call upon rage. It called upon all the aspects. Each had their own role.

The more he watched the powers and emotions before him, in a way that only his glowing golden eyes could visualize, the more sure of that theory he became.

Rituals did the same thing on a much bigger playing board.

“The possibilities if I can learn to harness this…”

What Kairon was seeing was a lot. It was like trying to go from letters to entire books, skipping the things that made them like words, sentences, paragraphs, and chapters. But the theory was there. It was right there.

All he needed to do was increase his comprehension, and turn theory into practice.

“Bark! Get me bark!”

With the brood busy, it fell to Hush and a few of the void imps to serve as his assistants. They hurriedly ran off, before making it back moments later with several of the flattened bark pieces they kept handy.

“Master…” One of the void imps was tugging at his cape. “Tower shaking…”

“Yes, yes. That’s part of the process.” Kairon spoke the words absently, doing his best to maintain focus.

“But. Master. Look!”

Whatever the little void imp was trying to point out, it could wait a few minutes. He needed to write this down while it was fresh.

Part in haze, and part in focus, Kairon began scrawling notes and symbols. “That part of the ritual is what I’d estimate maybe two thirds forge, with the rest split unevenly with brood, void, and pact. More brood… some void… and just a dab of pact. And there. Right there. Yes, yes I see…”

Time was slipping away, and Kairon meant to make use of every moment. He wasn’t spending body parts for this ritual. He was just spending seconds.

Scribbling as fast as he could, he notated every change he noticed. When stone shifted, or steam erupted, or symbols were written, glowed or erased. When he felt a presence, and when he didn’t.

Cause and effect. Variable and outcome.

He tried to take everything to those two components, and then feel at what the plane was using.

A tiny giggle escaped his lips. “Thank you, my demonic friend. You’ve unknowingly given me a front row seat to the secrets of your most fascinating source of power.”

Before Kairon knew it, it came to an end. Half a dozen pieces of bark littered the ground around him, each scrawled with notes. Each showing piece after piece of observation.

It made it quite clear that all of the ingredients of the plane’s recipes for rituals and its power came down to the variable aspects at play. Based on what he’d just witnessed, Kairon deduced that it was something like this:

Forge for creation. Pact for agreement and information. Void for unraveling the realm. Wrath for power or destruction. And brood for consumption and fuel.

Which left Universal. Once again, it seemed to be something The Plane entirely ignored—or maybe, couldn’t use…

He bent down, stacking the notated bark sheets neatly next to him. Straightening, he noticed that his little show had gathered quite the amount of attention.

Sythara, Bean, and Vorathrex were all staring at him.

“So… are you gonna tell us what that was about?” Bean smirked, putting his claws on his hips. “You looked pretty excited, master!”

“Yes,” commented Vorathrex. His expression wasn’t one of worry, but contemplation. “Fledgling, I find myself curious about what. Would you share?”

Sythara glanced at Vorathrex in surprise. “You? Curious?” Shaking her head, she went on. “Mmm… I think the child was trying to decipher the ritual.”

“Something like that.” Kairon waved dismissively. While he wanted to encourage Vorathrex’s growth in that train of thought, now wasn’t the time. “We’ll speak of it later.”

Vorathrex didn’t look satisfied, but Sythara again spoke up first. “Whatever it was, it’d better not disrupt our plans, child. The tier two brand has made progress. There is much I do not understand. Like I’m just grasping the surface of it all. But I think we might just scrape out a communion from this gambit of yours.” Sythara flicked her tentacles at him.

“Not to worry—this will not impede your work. As for the communion, that is more than adequate! Completion itself is all we need. Just get the thing finished, even if barely. From there, my alteration can handle the comprehension gap.”

“Master! Tower!” said Shiny. He and several of the brood imps had joined them mid-conversation. They too had become quite insistent about the tower, which really was odd.

“Yes, yes. I understand. It was shaking.” The city lord patted Shiny on the head.

“No it… Wait.” A new thought seemed to distract Shiny from the tower. “Time to choose? Time to choose… New ring?”

“Indeed, it is.”

“Choose brood! Must grow, master.”

“I know, I know. But we discussed this. I already told you that we would not be making another brood ring. Not yet. Which is exactly why I promised the tier two brand Sythara’s working on will be a brood one. Remember?”

Kairon could tell from the furrow of the imp’s brow that he did indeed recall their chat. It was just last night, after all. But he seemed quite… distressed. Kneeling down, Kairon put his lips to Shiny’s ear. “Something just occurred to me. If I were running, oh I don’t know, some kind of… sustenance based operation. I might worry less about growing my base of employees—you have plenty after all with more to follow each day—and more about growing my market. And I have to tell you, Shiny, customers will always pay more than an employee for your goods.”

Shiny thought about that. “Brood is one, master. So all brood employee… Of mine?”

“I mean they’re really my employees, but as middle management, they’re sort of your employees too.”

He could tell by the glint in the little imp’s pupils he was recalling some kind of memory. Likely something that went on in the brood rookery Kairon wasn’t privy to. Something rather… broodful.

“Which mean…” Shiny spun around, staring at the freshly carved ring. “Oh… Yes… Yes I see. Master, I understand. Make new. More more. Lots more. Not brood! But pact! Forge! Void! Oh! Even wrath! Charge them most! Hah! Haha!” Snickering to himself, Shiny shook his head. “Yes… Price increase. I think we do that. Oh… And master? New imp mean… New wants. New desires… New products.”

Patting the little guy on the head, Kairon smiled. “Remind me to have a sit down with you soon. I think there might be a partnership we can form.”

Shiny’s eyes widened. “Oh? Just let know! Anytime!”

“But for now, it’s time for me to see to one last little detail.” Kairon turned his attention to the waiting third ring choice. He’d mostly made his decision on the matter already.

The first district would be of Forge. Since the first day, problem after problem could have been solved with tools. His warriors had been fighting with claw, ichor, and salvage. His city had been operating on only what The Plane allowed. No safety grating, no true furniture, no carts for transportation. All problems that a forge district could fix.

Like the ichor, it was a force multiplier. Unlike the ichor, it was also a city multiplier. Every facet could be touched by it now that his city was on its feet.

Besides, he was terribly interested in what could be done with those gemstones in the crater and whatever else was beneath his city. Something a forge district would be best for.

All in all, forge was going to be the most influential of the new districts in his city.

Next, Kairon decided the second district would be Pact. From the knowledge he’d gained on that first day, Pact seemed to be a combination of diplomacy and intelligence gathering—which included demonic bargains and pacts, of course. Contracts and the like.

Kairon had resisted the urge to sink his claws into his namesake of contracts long enough. Yet, he also wanted a Pact district because as more and more pieces joined the board of this world, the city desperately needed an intelligence network. With more information, he could do far, far more to influence this world. Besides, with Boney on his first diplomatic mission, having Pact’s support for agreements could be invaluable.

Which left this third choice. After careful contemplation, he opted for Wrath. Wrath only had three building slots in the tiny first ring, and far more battle was on the horizon in their future. Facing it with just three Wrath building slots was an enormous limitation. Something this would fix.

And judging by the size of the rings this ritual had finished carving into the city, they were going to have a lot of new building area. Perhaps double per ring. Meaning 12 or so building slots each!

In conclusion, he hoped this combination for his third ring would give his city the tools it needed to succeed in the upcoming challenges in this realm. Many of which were undoubtedly going to require more blood to be spilled.

Looking inwards, Kairon confirmed the golden sigil was floating aimlessly. For what came next, he was going to weaponize it.

Satisfied, he grabbed his staff and strode out of the tower and into the citadel district.

“Huh. Did these stairs get longer?” he mused aloud. Not caring at the moment, he kept his attention ahead, walking forward as brisk as he could manage.

Behind him, the imps and demons were quite confused.

“Where going?” asked Shiny.

“Master!?” called Bean.

“Child?”

Not answering their questions, he kept walking. Past the citadel district, the wrath, and out onto the freshly branded scripture of the ritual.

Then, he let his frozen foot make direct contact with the ritual scripture itself. Best to be quick about this.

“Here goes.”

Kairon made his selections on the floating prompt awaiting him.
