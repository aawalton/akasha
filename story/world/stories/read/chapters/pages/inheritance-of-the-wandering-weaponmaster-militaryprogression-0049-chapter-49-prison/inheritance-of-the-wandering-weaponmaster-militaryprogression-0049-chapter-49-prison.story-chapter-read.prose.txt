The first night wasn’t anything special, when Ezra stayed awake just long enough to refill his inner world back mostly to its peak. In truth, that took longer than usual, considering how much of his aura reserves he’d spent on forcing the guards away and pushing his speed to the limit to get to the army headquarters. In addition to that, it would appear that having spent as much of his aura as he had had somehow deepened his reserves. Or, rather, it wasn’t that, but something about his inner world had changed. He wasn’t sure what that specifically was, and didn’t care to ask Jormun specifically when he was as exhausted as he was.

With a stove right next to him, Ezra passed the night in relative comfort, definitely more comfortable than many he’d spent on the streets, but not in a real bed comfortable. Then, when he woke in the morning and didn’t want to try to continue to sleep, he looked at his inner world.

As near as I can see it, you’ve got two options right now.

“Only two?” Ezra asked the bearded ghost as he interrupted the young Adept’s self-inspection.

There’s small things you can change with either plan, but yes. You can focus on deepening your aura reserves, or you can focus on broadening your experience with aura.

“I was actually gonna ask you about that last night,” Ezra said. “I’m not positive, but I’m pretty sure that my inner world... got bigger or something after the two fights that I’ve had since ascending to Adept.”

Yes, it should have. I’m a bit surprised that you noticed, but it’s not at all a surprise that that growth occurred. Anyway. There’s two primary ways to expand your inner world, especially once you’ve ascended to Adept. The first is just to fill your inner world all the way with aura, and then continue to meditate. Pulling more aura into your inner world when it’s full will be uncomfortable, but it does expand as you do so. The second option is to use the aura explosively, ideally almost all the way to empty yourself while straining your control. That toughens the inner world’s foundation, though it doesn’t help with the actual progress towards the next tier.

“So what’s the benefit to toughening my inner world’s foundation?” Ezra asked. “It sounds like it’s just slowing down my progress.”

Come on, kid, I know you’re not some construction person, but you gotta think about why the foundation is important. That’s why you’ve been focused on strengthening your legs and your core as much as you have on your arms.

Jormun’s voice betrayed that he wasn’t going to just give Ezra the answer.

“The ascension to Adept,” Ezra said, picking up a smaller piece of coal and feeding it into the stove as he thought out loud, “I only succeeded because I did something I shouldn’t have, and what caused that struggling was that I wasn’t actually as close to Adept as I thought. This foundation that you’re talking about, is that one of the things that I was lacking in my breakthrough?”

Exactly. Jormun’s face cracked in a smile as he answered. The strengthening of your inner world’s concept and foundation are particularly key as you progress towards Sainthood. That foundation is always important. If your inner world is vast but shallow, complicated uses of aura will be impossible, and if you then manage to ascend to Pseudo-Knight, your shroud will be painfully weak and limited. Then there’s visualization of your inner world’s concept, which is particularly key in ascending to being a Master. Of course, that’s a long ways from here, but Knighthood as well requires good visualization of your inner world as you largely solidify your inner world’s aura affinity permanently. Again, that’s not something that you need to concern yourself with now, but recognize that simply making your inner world larger and being able to hold more aura is far from the only thing you need to do to continue on this path.

“And my foundations had better be even better than yours,” Ezra said with a grin, “if I want to end up as a Weapon Saint instead of stuck as a Weapon Master.”

Exactly.

“Then I’ll focus on the foundations,” Ezra stated, squaring his shoulders. “It’d be pretty stupid if I became mediocre while trying to run to the next tier instead of actually  getting better. I’ll be working on my foundation for now, strengthening what it’s capable of. I’d imagine that it’s more than just using my aura, though. You said something about complicated uses of aura?”

You’d remember correctly. In this case, you should spend the aura to stretch what you’re capable of doing, like that eavesdropping thing you did to the noble asshat. Making a simple aura-infused attack is hardly broadening your horizons. I would encourage you to experiment as best as you can, but if you’d like examples of creative uses that I’ve seen from attributes of aura that are very different from your own, I can give them to you. After all, in training, there’s always people who are different from yourself that you can see, and it wouldn’t be at all impeding your own development for me to describe it. I would recommend not attempting to take advice from anybody else who has a wind, storm, lightning, or water affinity, though.

“I kinda understand the wind and storm,” Ezra said, “but though I might become able to use lightning, I’m not able to now. Plus, water affinity is way different than what I have. So why?”

I know you’re hoping to get lightning affinity at some point, that’s why I’m saying not to engage with anyone that can use it. The inner mind, your own perceptions, are just as important as anything else with developing your abilities. If you talk to somebody who has a lightning affinity, and they start to tell you about what they can and can’t do with it, that might limit your own ability to develop into somebody who has a lightning affinity.

Jormun paused, looking at Ezra, as he slowly nodded and accepted that answer.

“So if I met somebody who said that with lightning aura, they could never make the lightning go more than three meters away from themselves, or make the lightning come from the sky instead of from their body, then that might make it so I believe that it’s impossible to do that?”

Exactly.

“And why not ask somebody who has a water affinity? That seems pretty different to me.”

In many ways, water and wind affinities parallel each other, Jormun explained. Water is frequently a more solid, slower counterpart to the faster, less substantial wind affinity in practice. Once you have more experience with developing your own skills, looking for those parallels will be very beneficial, but when you’re first starting out and testing your own limits, it’s better to look at things as different as stone or corruption or poison. Things so fundamentally different that it helps you to stretch your own understanding of what you might be capable of.

Ezra grunted his acknowledgment, then a thought occurred to him.

“In the dream you showed me, your attacks didn’t seem to be particularly creative. It was all just basically making your strikes longer, sharper, and so on.”

I’ll be the first to admit that one of my weaknesses has always been my flexibility in thought. I wouldn’t be surprised if a large portion of my inability to truly become a Weapon Saint was due to my rigidity.

Ezra felt his eyebrows raise, and then nodded slowly.

“I suppose even the late great Weapon Master has things he struggled with.”

Jormun didn’t say anything in response, so Ezra turned back to his experimentation. After all, he had plenty of time to continue to build on his foundations.

***

“Hey, Ezra! The hell’re you doing down there?”

The voice from above startled Ezra, and he stopped drawing on his internal world’s aura. He stepped to the side and looked up at the trapdoor, which was wide open. Sam stared at him, and Ezra shrugged sheepishly.

“Just testing things out with my aura. You know, keeping fit and stuff?”

“You’re rattling the shutters. Quit doing whatever you’re doing,” Sam replied with a laugh. “Though, I didn’t really believe that you were a Knight. How the hell’d you end up in here?”

“I’m no Knight,” Ezra laughed, taking the opportunity to take a seat. “Just barely an Adept. And I was just trying to do something like what I’ve done before, to make the wind come talk to me.”

“The wind talks to you?” Sam asked, his face disbelieving. “You’ll have to tell me more about that some time.”

“Sure.”

***

“I’m not going to jump over the wall,” Ezra said.

“You didn’t yesterday or the day before,” Vash shrugged. “If you were going to make trouble, doing it when you’re halfway through your sentence is a stupid idea.”

Without saying anything, the Adept nodded, and pulled on his aura. In the early morning like today, he could be a bit riskier with how much of his aura he drew on without drawing too much attention. As such, with his aura running down his legs and swirling around them in miniature tempests, Ezra tried something he’d failed to do the past three days he’d tried it.

Instead of just jumping forward, like he’d first done with the wind affinity aura, he ran forward, the ten or so meters he had in front of him disappearing in a flash. With a growl of effort and no small amount of fear, he tried to do the second part he’d been training for, the part he’d always failed on.

The wind around his feet rushed down, no longer pushing him forward, but instead compelling him to turn a hard right. Like every time before, the movement was unnatural, and Ezra knew he was about to smash into the wall again—

Then he turned hard. It wasn’t a good turn, and he only made it a handful of steps forward before he fell into a tumble into the overgrown yard. He slipped and rolled to a stop, still smacking into the wall, but instead of the pure frustration that’d greeted him before, he saw progress. He could see a way to keep going! The issue was that he wasn’t leaning hard enough. If he was, then when his feet hit the ground, he’d be able to go straight ahead. But his ankles needed strengthening as well, and not just with aura…

There was plenty to learn and practice, and Ezra still had another three days of uninterrupted time in the jail to continue to do so.

***

Sweat dripped down Ezra’s forehead as he, for the first time in a while, did as many pushups as he could without drawing on his inner world at all. Instead, with his arms shaking and his breath quivering, he managed to push himself into an upright position before collapsing.

“Shut the fuck up,” Ezra couldn’t summon up any real bite to the words, and he just lay on the stone floor for a few seconds before taking a deep breath and starting to pull the aura of stone into himself and transforming it to a pure, affinity-less aura to spread through his body. It didn’t take long to gain some measure of control over himself again, and, as he allowed the cold stone to soothe his overworked body, he drew on the wind in his inner world to bring him the sounds of outside.

He couldn’t hear anything clearly, but there were the sounds of people going home for the day filling the air, and Ezra couldn’t help but smile. Today was the last day, and after tonight, or so he’d been told, he would be released. To go back to his friends! There was plenty that he was going to show them, and he was pretty sure he could help Boyd to feel aura now. Surely, as an Adept he could.

Even so, Ezra spent the night meditating, strengthening his inner world and allowing it, finally, to grow broader without immediately dedicating his reserves to another experiment, another test of his abilities. And then, sleep found him, and he dreamed of the wide open sky spreading above him and no guards standing nearby.
