Searing Sands Corespace, Earth Core #17, Fourth Floor

The outer edge of the corespace was uncharacteristically crowded. Everyone had relocated, from the lowly guards and assistant ritualists to the commander himself. Elyx's directions received little pushback after she'd explained a few of the potential side effects of remaining too close to the epicenter once the ritual was activated, and they hadn't even let her get to some of the interesting ones. The lugren honestly had no idea how likely any of this was to work. Well, she understood that the odds of the ritual going as planned were approximately zero, at this point there were more alterations than original components to the diagrams, but something would happen when they began the activation sequence.

Of the other six senior ritualists working with her, only two of them thought they had any real possibility of success. But the only thing they could do was speculate, so who could say who was right. They were well into uncharted territory, and due to resource limitations, they'd barely been able to perform any meaningful tests. More than out of a desire to succeed, or even survive, Elyx's recent efforts had been fueled by a kind of morbid fascination with the ultimate outcome. Whatever was about to happen, one thing was certain. It would be quite the spectacle.

Now, she stood in the shadow of a large boulder that protruded from the desert sands. The portal to the Labyrinth proper stood nearby, their exit point should something go disastrously wrong. If that happened, Elyx wouldn't be the first one through. Relying on a portal to save them from a dimensional mishap was a terrible idea, but it's not like she was an expert or anything. Why should anyone listen to her? The scorching heat of the ceaselessly shining sun beat down on the small crowd of demons, taxing even their innate resistance to such climates. Zertrolk stood nearby, flanked by a pair of greater demons that had arrived to witness the momentous event. The commander looked up from the scrying orb he was holding long enough to glance at her.

"How much longer?" he asked.

Was that a hint of unease in his voice? Could he be nervous? Elyx had never seen so much as a twitch of emotion on his mask of a face until this moment. She wondered what he was more worried about. The ritual failing or being torn to pieces by an errant dimensional schism.

Probably failure, if Lady Ira is on the other end of that orb.

"Only a few more minutes, commander." Elyx said, returning her attention to the complicated instruments in her hands. "We're ready to go, I'm just waiting for confirmation that the other site is synchronized with us."

"Proceed as soon as you receive the signal." He grunted. "Do not wait for my command."

"I wasn't planning on it. This has enough ways to go wrong without me introducing more variance into the ley line alignments. Timing is key."

She half expected some kind of rebuke, but the commander had stopped reprimanding her for her lack of deference more than a week ago. Was he growing to respect her? Or had her aggressive apathy towards her own fate simply worn him down? Elyx liked to think it was the latter. She shook her head and sighed. If by some miracle this worked, it would be in spite of his malcontent meddling.

Blink. Blink blink.

The crystal rod in her hand began to flash in a brief but distinctive pattern. That was the signal. The other site was ready to begin.

"Now!" Elyx commanded, channeling power into a rune-inscribed tablet at her feet. The other ritualists followed suit immediately, lending their power to the spell. Even the commander and the other greater demons joined them, feeding their own strength into one of the many tablets on the ground. Crimson energy blazed from the runes, flowing away from them along a pre-arranged path of similar tablets that lay between them and the primary ritual diagram. It was a rather low-tech and resource-intense solution, but more convenient ways of activating rituals remotely had a higher likelihood of failure.

Elyx turned her attention to another scrying orb set into a nearby table as the flow of magical energy disappeared into the mouth of the canyon. This one displayed an aerial view of the ritual diagram, a massive array of interconnected arcane sigils carved into a flat sheet of stone. The human artifact sat in the exact center of the spell formula, its sleek metallic body wholly out of place alongside piles of dimension effusion, powdered crystal, and other ritual components. She held her breath as the magical energy began flowing into the circle, lighting it up in a sequence that spiraled towards the center. It was out of their hands now. The explosive runes were in place, primed and ready to activate the device, but the timing had to be perfect. They'd laid it all out as best they could, layering multiple redundancies and mana gates to ensure that each one went off in perfect sequence. Assuming her understanding of the human device was correct, and she was pretty sure it was, the entire thing should ignite just… about…

A flash of light pierced the sky, bright enough to stand out even against the blazing sun. A deep rumble shook through the ground a moment later, strong enough to make some of the nearby ritualists fall to the ground. Elyx gripped the table for support, staring intently at the image projected by the orb. The ritual circle was intact and blazing with power, projecting a sphere of translucent force energy to contain the destructive force of the blast. More bright flashes shone through the barrier as the roiling cloud of plasma strained to break free, but… it held.

It actually held. Cheers broke out among the surrounding demons as the explosive energy held above the ritual circle began to shrink, consumed by the formulas surrounding it. Soon, the majority of the power was gone, channeled into higher dimensions to ignite the spell's main functions. The tremors subsided, and everything fell silent.

"Did… it work?" One of the ritualists asked, his voice a hushed whisper.

"We're taking power from the Labyrinth Core now…" Elyx said, monitoring her instruments. She shifted the orb's view to show an image of the large, floating gemstone. which now was surrounded by bands of purple and blue energy. They streamed from the ancient construct like writhing serpents, flowing into the blazing ritual circle. "It's… establishing the gateway…"

Elyx could hardly believe it. Could they have truly done it? Despite seemingly insurmountable odds, had they opened a gate to the home world? The terminus would be outside in the Labyrinth proper, not here in the corespace, so there was no way to tell from this position. They lacked the proper tools to observe the other end of the corespace portal from this end of the dimensional boundary. Triumph and trepidation warred in her chest, battling for supremacy. Was it working? For a moment, the demon ritualist entertained the dream that, somehow, everything was going to plan. That she would get to return home to the quiet halls of the caretaker conclave, leaving the outside world and its madness behind.

Then, as if it had been waiting for Elyx to lower her guard, the Labyrinth Core detonated in an eruption of gold and blue.

"Elyx! Elyx, wake up!"

The lugren came to slowly, fighting her way back to conciousness through a haze of mental fog. But… no… the fog wasn't just in her head; it was all around her. Thick clouds of acrid smoke blanketed the sky, blotting out the sun and casting the desert in ominous gloom. Elyx rubbed her eyes, trying to focus through the pounding pain in her head. Someone was calling her name, but she couldn't hear properly. Everything was muffled, like she was submerged in water. She blinked again, focusing on the slender lugren woman leaning over her.

"Lyxi…?" she asked. "What…"

"Get up!"

It looked like the other ritualist was shouting, but it didn't sound like it. She looked around, surprised to find their camp in ruins. Flaming piles of debris surrounded them, and the desert sand was littered with sections of glossy, cracked glass that radiated intense heat.

"The dimensional integrity of this space is collapsing!" Lyxi continued, hauling Elyx to her feet. "We have to get out!"

"Where is everyone? What happened?!" Elyx asked. She tried to stand on her own but found her legs were too weak. Lyxi held her up, pulling her bodily towards the portal arch that led back to the Labyrinth. Was it her imagination, or was the swirling energy inside shrinking as they approached?

That's… not good.

She hurried, lurching closer alongside Lyxi with all the grace of a drunken dragon.

"The Core couldn't withstand the energy siphon! It collapsed part way through! Everyone that survived the blast is already gone!"

The Core failed? Elyx tried to laugh, but it came out more as a wheezing cough. Out of all the possible failure points, they'd never considered the Labyrinth's own infrastructure wouldn't be able to handle the strain. The bars of their prison weren't strong enough to be leveraged against the cell door. Elyx sensed a certain irony in that, but she doubted her demonic companions would appreciate it. That being said…

"You came back for me?"

Most demons weren't prone to loyalty. They respected strength, and there were few among them that would deign to assist a fallen ally. Lyxi just huffed, not wasting breath on a response as she continued to haul her charge towards the shrinking exit.

"Just hurry! Shut up and hurry!"

Elyx grunted an agreement, finally finding the strength to stumble along on her own. Together, they reached the portal moments before the last remnants of the dimensional gate flickered out, flinging themselves through what remained of the aperture. The transition was nausea inducing, but luck was with them. They emerged amidst the other survivors on the mountain peak that had once anchored the corespace, battered, but intact. Elyx collapsed to her hands and knees, what little strength she'd managed to muster ebbing from her aching limbs. She spotted Zertrolk a short distance away and half expected him to storm over and execute her right then and there, but he barely even glanced her way. None of them did. As the last of the haze lifted from her mind, she finally saw why.

Far below, nestled in a forest valley between two nearby mountains, a jagged scar of crimson energy rent the air. Its boundaries were rough and uneven, constantly fluctuating as the rift stretched and contracted. One moment it was hardly a slit, barely visible even in the dark of night. The next, it expanded to fill half the valley, consuming a hundred yards of woodland. When next it shrunk, only blackened and charred earth remained.

"Gods be damned…" Elyx whispered, unable to tear her gaze away from the spectacle. She could see it now. A hint of familiar sky flickering in and out of sight through the twisting, unstable rift. Crimson skies, crowned by a bloated, blood-red sun.

Home.

Even as she watched, another rift opened. This one was closer, at the base of their mountain. Then, more, further away, stretching into the distance. Some were small, others large. Not a single one looked at all like the safe and stable portal they had been trying to forge. Hell, there shouldn’t be more than one in the first place, but that hardly mattered. Even this much was a far greater success than Elyx had expected. Who knew if anything would be able to survive passing through such a tear in space, but she'd never truly cared about the mission itself. The mere accomplishment of such an impossible task filled her with awe and pride.

Those feelings lasted right up until the closest of the rifts suddenly expanded in their direction, slicing a city-sized chunk out of the mountain beneath them. The other demons shouted, scrambling away from the unstable, unpredictable event horizon.

Right. Survival first, emotions later.

The door to the forest Sanctuary had only just come into view when the tremors began. Jade staggered, bracing herself against a nearby tree as the ground continued to heave. Tara was sent sprawling on the ground beside her and Naomi latched onto her arm to prevent herself from doing the same.

"What's going on?!"

Before Jade could answer, the notification appeared.

Global System Alert: Demon Containment Breach.

Location: Fourth floor, Ironpeak Mountain quadrant, Titan Stone zone.

Quarantine protocols initiated. For your own safety, avoid using any dimensional magic in the region of the breach.

Response force deployed. For your own safety, avoid interfering with Labyrinth Subjugation Golems, Adamantine Golems, and Stellar Core Golems.

Jade's heartbeat quickened, and she squeezed Naomi's hand hard. Before anyone could say anything, a new window appeared.

Global Quest: Stem the Tide

A dimensional rift to the demon home world has opened on the fourth floor of the Labyrinth.

Objective: Aid in stopping the demon armies from flooding back into the Universe.

Reward: Potentially avoid a cataclysmic cosmic war.

Bonus Reward: Large experience gain.

Jade's first thought was to wonder what was with those quest rewards. She didn't know if the Voice was trying to make a joke, but this really wasn't the time. Then her brain unfroze, and she shoved the irrelevant train of thought aside.

"We weren't fast enough…"

The others were talking, but Jade wasn't listening. She just kept staring at the notices, frozen. That fourth-floor zone couldn't be far from Earth. What was going to happen to her world? Her family? Could the people of the Labyrinth somehow stop this? Was there still time to prevent disaster?

"Jade!"

Luis brought her back to her senses with a forceful shake. The large man was standing over her, hauling her back to her feet. When had she fallen to her knees? She looked up at him, surprised.

"Pull yourself together." He said, the force behind his words surprising her again. "We don't know enough yet, and panicking won't help.""He's right." Tara said, clearly trying to keep herself composed. She pointed to the doorway just ahead of them. "Maybe one of the Labyrinth attendants can tell us what's really going on."

Jade's fist tightened around the small, dark token Skraag had given just a short while ago.

"You're right." She drew in a sharp breath, nodding once. Calm. She had to stay calm. Jade glanced down at the token, its dark surface glinting in the fading evening light. "This had better be good…"

Later, much later, Jade thought back to that moment and wondered. If she had known down which path that token would lead…

Would she still have been able to walk through that door?
