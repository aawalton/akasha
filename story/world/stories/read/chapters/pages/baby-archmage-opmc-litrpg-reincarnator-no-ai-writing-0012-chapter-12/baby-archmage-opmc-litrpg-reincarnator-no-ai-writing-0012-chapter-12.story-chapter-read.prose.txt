“Yes!” I exclaimed. This was the perfect new Skill to get. Now I was sure I was ready to cast spells, and I wouldn’t need a staff for it. I had been diligently practicing creating a mana circle and was getting decent at it.

I had gotten a Rare Skill! This was excellent. My parents would be especially happy. Rare Skills gave more Attributes and usually were more powerful. The substantial increase in Magic Power would surely allow me to cast increasingly powerful magical effects!

I had practiced with different sizes, different thickness of the ring, and my speed at which I could create one had improved drastically. If you couldn’t throw a fireball at the snap of your fingers, you were likely going to lose a wizard battle. My mind returned to the cultivators fighting in the night sky, and I wondered how they cast spells.

I figured the mana circle was the basis for each and every spell, so learning to create it with just a thought was central. So learning that first couldn’t be wrong. And I had been rewarded for that effort.

Naturally I started casting again. I made the circle and used the booklet as a cheat sheet for the Basic Water spell. Before it had been pretty shoddy work, and I understood why Piraka was having such a hard time drawing spells. It was intricate, and the old badger was working blind. He was rather impressive all things considered.

However Spellcrafting changed the game entirely. Whatever intuition-based Skill Danek had started to make sense to me. Spellcrafting was a Skill like that. It gave me impressions on whether the symbols and shapes I was drawing with mana were correct. I drew a J-shape inside the circle of mana because the booklet said so. What did it mean? The hell did I know, I was just a baby copying someone else’s homework!

I really wanted to understand what these symbols meant, but for now I settled for rote repetition so I could cast spells in my sleep.

Earlier, I had leafed through the spell booklet. Piraka had instructed me to learn one single spell and focus on that first. It seemed like sound advice, so I picked the most interesting spell. It was simply called “Basic Water” in the booklet.

I didn’t know why I chose this spell in particular, but for some reason it called to me. As an element, water was incredibly versatile. It could be used for offense and defense. I shook my head. The Immortals in the sky were clearly a bad influence. I was already thinking like I was going to battle other magic users…

What I learned quickly was that it wasn’t easy to imagine the whole formula in all of its intricacy, even when you could actually perceive the mana particles. Spellcrafting helped immensely, and I would applaud anyone trying to draw anything beyond a shitty dog without its intuition. I was now spending all day every day simply mastering the Basic Water spell. I still infused potions for Piraka, but I wasn’t using his refined mana anymore.

I learned a lot about my new Skill as I directed my focus on it. It would tell me if the formula was workable, and it would tell me to what degree. It was all pretty vague. Like if I drew that J-shape, it told me that the shape was shoddy work, but only the faintest impression of how to improve it. The Skill told me it was in the wrong position within the circle, but it only slightly nudged my attention to where the correct position was. I assumed these intuitions and impressions would strengthen once I leveled. Soon enough that happened.

The circle held form above my palm. In the middle of the formation, a single drop of water floated. I had created that water drop out of nothing. It hadn’t existed before I willed it into reality. What on earth could possibly be sweeter.

I reveled in this feeling of mastery and creation. There was nothing quite like the bliss of following my purpose and reaching the goals I had set out for myself. I could almost cry, it was so sweet. I couldn’t remember a single moment like this from my past life.

In this new life? I resolved to rack these experiences up.

The nice thing about the spell formation was that once it was completed, it sort of… solidified. I had noticed this when interacting with the mana particles and forming superparticles. Once a superparticle had formed, it didn’t require much attention for them to hold that form. The mana particles would try to drift away and find orbits when they were not attached to each other. But if they were shaped into a ring, the ring would be steady. It was sort of like playdough… Made of light… And hopes and dreams… And magic…

The important part was that now that the full spell formation was complete, it didn’t require my attention to hold the spell. That was neat, and I wondered about the implications.

My little droplet was apparently the shittiest dog the mana could accept. Elder Piraka’s spell had generated enough mana to fill a jug. But was it specific symbols I got incorrectly, or the whole shape? Were there symbols that governed the size of the spell specifically?

I spent my days changing the circle’s size and moving the symbols around. Sometimes I made a symbol bigger. Sometimes I removed one or added another. Spellcrafting gave me a vague sense of whether I was improving the spell or making a mess of it.

Two things seemed to make the water globe bigger. Using more mana helped, which I could do by making the circle larger and its symbols thicker. Drawing the formula more accurately helped too, although I was getting weird results with that.I was starting to suspect the sketch in the booklet wasn’t quite right. Sometimes I felt I had copied something perfectly, but it still produced shoddy work.

Even so, the booklet was immensely useful.

I also spent some more time studying the other spells to get more perspective. The symbols for Basic Water reappeared in the other spells. That meant that different symbols had different functions. So if you learned the language, you could cast all kinds of magic.

Unfortunately Piraka had made no notes about that. The only thing he had learned was copying the formulas.

I tried asking him about these things, but he offered little knowledge. He said he had been in a hurry when he had been copying the spellbook at the ancestors’ ruins. When he had visited the pyramid, Silverback had been younger and less dangerous, but it had still been a threat to avoid.

I really wanted to go see this spellbook at the pyramid.

But for now I could only learn the only way I knew how to: by playing.

So I tested the limits of the formula. I tried removing symbols and adding more symbols. The spell mostly broke and the water drop fell. I tried this a few times and decided that shooting in the dark wasn’t the best use of my time. So I asked myself what else I could do.

Then I made the circle bigger and the symbols thicker. I packed as much mana into the spell formula as I could. The problem was that when I was still constructing the formula, it required my rapt attention. It was not easy and fast, no matter how many times I had done it. It would require a lot of repetition to make spellcasting second nature.

I gradually started adding more mana into the formula and my accuracy grew.

After some work, I had now made the biggest and fattest spell formula so far. When I placed the final piece into its place, the spell activated. My eyes widened as half a cup of water materialized to float above my palm.

I was no god of the sea, but boy did that feel amazing. What had previously been a drop, was now a globe. And it seemed that the system also appreciated my efforts.
