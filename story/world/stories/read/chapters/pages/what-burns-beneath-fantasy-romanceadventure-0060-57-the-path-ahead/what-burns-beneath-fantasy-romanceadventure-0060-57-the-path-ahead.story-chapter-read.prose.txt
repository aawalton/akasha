Every argument I voiced was rebuffed.

I didn’t want Royce mixed up in all this. Not for a moment. I wanted him home. Safe in Fellbrook, where the greatest dangers were winter fevers and poor harvests. Where monsters didn’t lurk in every shadow.

“It’s my decision, and good luck stopping me,” he insisted for the fifth time.

“Royce, things are dangerous out here! The three of us nearly died last-”

“Sounds like you need an extra person, then! Someone to help keep you safe.” His gaze flicked towards our audience, cheeks tinged pink, before landing back on me. “All of you, I mean.”

Renner snorted. He’d been watching our argument with his arms crossed.

Royce rounded on him. “What? Why is that funny?” An uncharacteristic scowl twisted his handsome face. “I’ve got a sword, you know. I know how to fight, and I’ll wager I’m stronger than-”

I caught my friend’s arm, reclaiming his attention before any argument of that sort started. “Royce, it’s not about being strong! There are monsters out here. Actual monsters! If you’d been there last night, Baltha would’ve… ashes, Royce, she probably would’ve smashed your head in just the same as Renner’s!”

“In my defense,” the smaller man interjected, “I didn’t have an actual weapon.”

I glared at him. “You’re not helping!”

He smirked.

Royce spread both hands wide. “Well, now if something else shows up there’ll be four of us. Better odds.”

“It’s not about having better odds, it’s about being safe!”

“Well, you’re not being safe.”

“Because I can’t!”

“Well, then neither can I.”

I fought the very childish urge to stamp one bare foot onto the floorboards. Instead I went the slightly more mature route of throwing both hands up with a frustrated growl.

Teela yawned. “How much longer are you two gonna be?”

The urge to stamp came again. “As long as it takes for him to see sense!”

Royce grinned. “So… forever?”

“Royce!”

“Brin!” he echoed my tone, then laughed at the indignant look I aimed his way. Broad hands lifted, palms-up, but his apparent mirth didn’t fade. “And as for seeing sense; none of this makes sense! You’re a criminal, for one thing, and you’re being chased by monsters. It’s like something out of a story.”

“An awful one,” I mumbled.

“Maybe. But that’s why I’ve gotta come.” He towered over me, all smug dimples and sparkling eyes, and finished matter-of-factly, “To make it less awful.”

My lips parted in a resigned sigh. There’s no arguing with him, is there? I could probably shout until I’m out of breath, and he wouldn’t hear a word.

And… if the situation was reversed, I’d do the same. I’d never let him go alone.

I massaged my temples. “There’s really no talking you out of this?”

“Nope.”

“Even if I beg?”

“Not a chance.”

“Threaten?”

“I can take you.”

I smiled, despite myself.

“Folks?” Master Lewen’s muffled voice echoed from the hallway.

Renner shifted forwards, unbolted the lock, and swung the door inwards.

The innkeeper stood holding two large, steaming buckets of water. “Sorry to interrupt, but you’ve a visitor.”

A weary, broad face adorned by a lustrous red mustache appeared in the doorway. Davith Rell was clad in sap-spattered leathers and mud-caked boots. He dipped into a little bow. “Good to see you all doing well. ”

“You find Ward and Aveline?” Renner clearly wasn’t in the mood for pleasantries.

Davith leaned against the doorframe, sighing heavily. “I did not. Their trail led to the main road, but from there I found no trace.”

Teela puffed her cheeks. “Salt. So there’s two murderers just… out there?” She shuddered. “I hope we don’t run into them on the road.”

“I hope we do.” Renner growled.

I was much more inclined to agree with the young woman beside me. “They wouldn’t head north, right? Closer to the Order? That’d be madness.”

The captain paced, detouring around bodies and buckets. “I can’t say. Were the circumstances different, I’d ask you folks to stay in town a bit longer. Give written statements on all this mess.” He pinched the bridge of his nose. “But I don’t see much need. Miss Agnes isn’t saying much, but Josiah and Gil confessed everything.” He heaved another sigh. “Is your destination still Pinnacle? All of you?”

I nodded. “Yes, sir. To the Order.”

“Good. That’s good. I’ll beg you to be wary on the road, then. If a place like our little town is beset by horrors, I can’t imagine what the rest of the realm…” he fell silent, gaze drifting downwards, then squared his broad shoulders. “Mm. Be safe. And, if you don’t mind, ask the Order to send a few members to Snowmelt. We don’t have the manpower to transport three prisoners, nor to send a proper search for our fugitives.”

“Of course,” I promised softly. “But, captain, why do they need to be transported? If they’ve confessed, then surely…” Surely their fate would be carried out here. Their… saints, their execution. I didn’t have it in me to voice the terrible thought.

His mouth tightened at the corners. “If they were dangerous, I’d have leave to end things here in town. But the Order’s clear; anyone suspected of consorting with Fae, or known but held in check, is to be brought to the temple.”

I could feel Renner’s eyes boring into me. As soon as I glanced over, he looked away.

Davith gusted out another heavy breath. “I suppose I can’t complain, can I? It’ll be easier for folks if an outsider comes to deal with those three. Easier for me, as well.”

“I’m sorry, captain,” I murmured, unsure of what else to say.

“Miss, I’m sorry. To all of you. All this nightmare right under my nose for years, and me without a bloody… I’m ashamed. There’s no making up for what you’ve been through, but… anything you need, ask and it’s yours.”

“You don’t owe us-”

“Warm clothes.” Renner stepped forwards. “A cloth tarp. Hunting snares. A map of the area. Oil. Saddlebags filled with enough food to get four people to Pinnacle.”

“Good food, please!” Teela chirped. “And I’d like a new hat. A green one. Oh, and some money would be nice. My coinpurse got, uh, lost in all the-”

“We don’t need money!” My face burned.

Captain Rell chuckled. “I’ll make sure everything’s delivered tomorrow morning.”

He bade us goodnight. Lewen did the same after asking what we’d all like for breakfast in the morning and promising to wash any dirty clothes we left out. Once both men had left, Teela turned with a catlike grin.

“I like them. I gotta say, I’m really coming around to all you small-town folk.”

“There’s no beating us.” Royce cocked an eyebrow down at me. “Or changing our minds.”

I sighed. “I suppose there’s not, is there?”

“Nope.”

Teela stretched. “Sounds like it’s settled, then. Too bad, it was kinda fun watching you two yell at each other.” She snickered at my expression, then danced towards the door and sang out, “Have a nice bath!”

My gaze landed on the steaming, inviting water, then trailed to where Renner was slouched against the wall. He seemed to be frowning at Royce, who was slowly frowning back.

“You should go first.” I managed a weak smile when he turned the expression towards me. “I think you… that is, you deserve… you should… go… first.”

One brown brow rose.

My face warmed even as a knot wound through my belly. I’ve already thanked him. Several times, and once by practically throwing myself at him! And he looks far too tired to handle me launching into a speech about how grateful I am, so… “I just meant-”

“I know what you meant.” He stifled another yawn, then flashed me a wry look that made the knot untwist a bit. “You sure about all this?”

“Yes, of course!” I cried, quickly seizing this small, kind victory. “You’re… Renner, you must be exhausted! I’m sure you want to get some-”

“That’s not what I meant.” The wry expression faded as his lips pressed into a grim line. It was very similar to the look he’d given me downstairs, when he’d asked about staying up to keep an eye on me.

“Oh.” My shoulders drooped as I realized what he did mean.

What must this afternoon have been like for him? Sitting there with nothing to do but watch me. To wait and see if I’d ever wake up, or if I’d be torn to shreds right in front of him, or…

I shook the thoughts away. “Yes. Yes, I’m sure.” Before he could try to change my mind I mumbled a quiet, much-too-meager, “Thank you,” and practically fled.
