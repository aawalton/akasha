The inside of the Ligis Guild Hall was huge, the front room like an enormous tavern with tables occupying most of the center, but also a bar, food line, weapon and armor repair shop, and, most prominently on the back wall, a counter for official Guild business and turning in bounties. Up the stairs was some lodging, which was much cheaper than staying at an inn as long as rooms were available.

As for collecting bounties, it depended on the contract whether the Guild would hold the reward or be paid by a client directly. As registered hunters, they technically were under an agreement not to take freelance jobs without the guild getting a cut. However, it was an unspoken understanding that hunters often did so anyway. It was the kind of thing the guild wouldn’t really try to catch or punish anyone for doing as long as they didn’t talk about it openly.

Though, Briadon had said taking a freelance job directly out of Ligis would be very stupid, which is why they did the smither’s job on the books. “Don’t shit where you sleep,” as Briadon had put it. Besides, in the event that a client tried to rip you off and not pay, or if there was some other dispute with turning in the bounty, having everything official meant they could call on the guild for help.

Tressa sat at a table with Lisha and Briadon, shoveling down some bread with a bowl of stew in front of her. It had a rich broth that complemented the meat and potatoes, made from the marrow of a boar.

The first time she had eaten at a tavern and gotten the hard, stale bread, she was confused and disgusted until Lisha had shown her how to eat it. They let the bread dry out purposely, as it was a special kind of dough used to make it, so when paired with the broth, the bread wouldn't turn instantly soggy and fall apart. It was used to soak up the broth, or some even used it to place meat and potatoes on it and eat it in one bite.

Tressa had been blown away by it and had cautiously dipped the bread in, watching as the golden brown bread turned dark as it soaked up the broth, and when she took a bite, her tongue danced with joy, and she had let out a squeal at the rich flavor. The bread brought down the saltiness of the flavor and brought out the different hints of spice. It was simple and currently her favorite.

“Why do you always get the same thing?” Briadon asked as he stabbed at a plate of vegetables he was currently craving.

After a deep swallow, Tressa took a sip of ale to help wash it down.

“Well, it's different wherever we go. The last city was more sweet than salty. There, they used apples to make the broth while stewing the bones. Here it's salty. And the spices taste a bit different, and the vegetables are fresher, but they just have carrots and tubers.”

“Tubers?” Lisha asked.

“Oh, you would know them as potatoes. But they help add flavor. It's more like a bitter, earthy flavor. I didn't like eating them raw. But in stews and pastries, they are delicious.”

“Potatoes in pastries?” Lisha asked and gave Briadon a shared look.

“Yeah. Narella, my family's cook, likes to experiment with desserts and food. Everything she has made is amazing. And I can't help but love it.” She instantly went back to eating her stew, and a sad look crossed her face.

“You okay?”

“Yes. I just miss home. But I chose to leave. So I’m going to continue my journey. I'll see everyone again soon enough,” she answered Lisha.

“This is your first time away from home, isn’t it?” Lisha asked. “I felt the same when I left the islands for the first time. I couldn’t believe how the main continent is like another world, and I felt quite homesick.” She gave a toothy smile. “But it gets easier in time.”

“Yes, I’m sure it will,” Tressa smiled. “At worst, I will have to return five years from now.” She then made a mental note to make sure to send an eagle home with a letter about her leaving Ligis soon. She had only sent one back since leaving on her adventure, telling her mother all about learning to be a monster hunter. The letter her mother returned praised her and assured her everything was fine at home, encouraging her to be safe and letting her know how proud she was. She also promised not to inform her father of her location so that he wouldn't try sending anyone after her.

“Why five years specifically?” Briadon asked as he looked at her.

“Well, you see, I need to take my trials. And if I don't, I'll face punishment. It’s a rite-of-passage sort of thing. All elves take them. All travel to the capital to take their trials.”

“A rite of passage? What is yours like?” Briadon said with excitement.

“I’m sorry, I’m under oath not to speak of it,” Tressa said with an awkward grin.

“Under oath?” Lisha asked as her eyebrows knit.

“Yes. When High Elves reach a certain age, they must take an oath not to speak of how the trials happen or what they will be. So if, let's say, someone tried to force me to speak of it, I could not. The oath makes the words silent.”

“So like some kind of massive powerful spell?”

“Yes,” Tressa said, looking at Briadon after he asked the question.

“Hmmmm sounds stupid. Elves are surely odd. We hear strange things about you, but now that I know you, the more I hear the more your culture makes no sense.”

“Briadon, be nice,” Lisha hissed.

“What I said is the truth,” Briadon argued, waving a hand dismissively.

Tressa shrugged. “Do your cultures have anything like that? A coming-of-age trial or rite of passage?”

Lisha nodded. “Well, it is not secretive, as yours sounds. But we dive in the ocean to see who can go the farthest before having to use their gills to breathe. Or we craft weapons. I won the last round when I was young.”

Briadon leaned back in his chair, causing it to creak heavily. His tail gave a flick, and he rubbed his beard.

“Minotaurs don’t have a rite of passage, per se,” Briadon began. “But we do have a mating ritual any may take part in when they come of age.”

“Mating ritual?” Tressa asked.

“Oh yes! If you win, you get to have a grand wedding thrown together by the whole clan! Come to think of it, the mating ritual is coming up. My brother and cousin should be taking part. I hope they do well.”

Tressa awed at the concept. “Huh, that sounds romantic. Competing for your lover's hand and then the whole clan celebrates it? So one couple gets this grand wedding every year?”

“Every six months.”

“That must make it so special!” Tressa smiled. “So how do they win?”

“Tressa—” Lisha tried to interrupt.

“I’m glad you asked!” Briadon boomed, straightening up with a grin and thumping his chest. “First of all, they have to prove their strength, their endurance, and their passion! The festivities begin at dawn with a ceremonial chase. The whole clan comes out to watch! There’s music, drums, horns, and plenty to eat and drink! Everyone strips down to their waistcloths and—”

“Nope!” Lisha interrupted, snapping her fingers and pointing a claw at him. “Absolutely not! You’re not discussing this further!”

Tressa blinked, her spoon hovering in front of her mouth. “Wait. But what…”

“Don’t encourage him!” Lisha said, half groaning, half laughing.

Briadon looked genuinely offended. “You act as if it is obscene! It’s a sacred tradition! There’s music, shouting, mud—”

“Nope! Stop right there!” Lisha waved her hands in front of her face as if fending off the mental images. “Just stop. We’ve had enough cultural enlightenment for one meal.” She then leaned towards Tressa and groaned. “He invited me to attend one with him once as an observer. I had no idea what I was getting myself into…”

Tressa just blinked, still having not taken the bite that was still sitting on her spoon. She clearly wanted to hear the rest.

Briadon huffed and sat back, his chair creaking under his weight. “Here I’d assumed High Elves would be more prudish than Statiki. Guess I was wrong!”

“Minotaurs just have no filters!” Lisha shot back, stabbing a piece of meat with unnecessary force.

Tressa grinned mischievously, finally lowering her spoon. “Soooo… mud?”

“Tressa!” Lisha groaned.

“What? I’m curious.”

“Sex, Tressa. They perform for sex!”

“You mean…” Tressa’s head turned.

“In the mud!”

Tressa turned a deep red and looked at Briadon, horrified, as her ears turned the same shade as her face. Yet he sat with a huge grin on his face as he shrugged. “It is arguably the most important part of the festival! When else do you declare whom you have your eyes on and show them firsthand your prowess in—”

“No, thank you! I wish not to know any more!” Tressa declared, and started eating her food. She tried to get the image of a muddy minotaur men out of her mind. She let out a slight shudder of disgust.

But then again… she realized she really should be adding this to her book containing notes on minotaurs

Both Lisha and Briadon let out laughs. He reached over and patted Tressa on the back.

“Well, it’s not as bad as you think! It really is a great time! Much drinking and laughter throughout the day!”

“I’ll admit, I did have a good time after the ceremonial chase,” Lisha admitted, sipping her drink. “A little warning ahead of time would have been appreciated, though.”

“It does sound like a good time,” Tressa said, but she decided she could go without seeing the naked minotaur men.

“The weddings are lavish and beautiful,” Briadon continued. “We do the ceremony in our clan’s wide fields. There we throw wheat and grain. Then the bride and groom get to choose what sacred area to visit, and they get that area for a week,” he said, now being more careful with the details to avoid getting skewered by Lisha. He knew Tressa well enough to know she wasn’t your average elf, but still her sheltered upbringing was showing.

“Soooo, Briadon,” Tressa asked. “Have you partaken in your clan’s rites?”

“No. I have yet to find the woman I want, nor am I ready to settle down. But truly, our women are beautiful. So glorious in stature and strength! Many can outrun us males and can easily put us in our place. But no, I have yet to find my mate, from our clan or another.”

“You can go to other clans and find a mate?” Tressa asked.

“Aye. We can travel to other clans and fight to show our strength. If we pass, we can stay for a time and have the right to try and find a mate. But if we can’t, we must leave.”

“What happens to your clan if you leave? Can you just return, or are you banned?”

Lisha looked up, interested. “Yeah, what then?”

Briadon stroked his beard. “Well, of course you can! But you must once again prove your strength to be welcomed back.”

Tressa nodded, fascinated by their culture. It sounded quite barbaric. If she had heard all of this without knowing Briadon personally, she would assume Minotaurs were absolute savages. But she knew that was not the case.

She then looked to Lisha. “What about Statiki?”

Lisha picked at a scale under her jaw. “Well, nothing like minotaurs,” she laughed. “The men dive into the sea and search the ocean floor for certain types of rocks, then they bring them back and give them as gifts to the woman they like. If we like what we see, we choose them. We mate for life, so if they die, we don’t normally find another. We tend to remain widowed.”

“I see… but not just any kind of rock, right?” Tressa asked.

“No, shiny ones, ones filled with magic. It should be special; after all, it is the offering you give, and you must outshine her other suitors.”

“Wait, wait. Does that mean a Statiki girl can have multiple different suitors give them stones?”

“Yes. One of my older sisters got seven before she chose her mate. She is very beautiful…" Lisha looked up reflectively. “He won in every aspect, and in truth she had already loved him before the ritual.”

“That does sound romantic,” Tressa said, thinking she much preferred the Statiki traditions over minotaurs. “And I didn’t know you had any sisters.”

Lisha gave a toothy smile. “I have seven sisters and four brothers.”

“Waaaht? Really!?” Tressa asked excitedly. It was rare for elves to have more than two kids, having twelve would just be completely unheard of. She couldn’t believe it.

“Unlike elves or minotaurs,” Lisha went on, “We Statiki have more than one child at a time. And… well…” She couldn’t blush, but the bashful expression showed her embarrassment.

“They lay eggs,” Briadon said for her.

“Wooooah really!?” Tressa asked as Lisha averted her gaze. “How many do you lay at once?”

Lisha hunched into her shoulders.

“Yeah? How many, Lisha!?” Briadon bellowed with laughter.

“Three or four at a time!” she said quickly, looking around and twiddling two claws together. “Now can we please move on. This isn’t something we statiki just speak of so openly… it’s… embarrassing.”

Tressa raised her finger, grinning sheepishly. “Can I ask… just one more?”

Lisha sighed. “Go ahead, Tressa.”

“Have you ever…”

“No! I am too young!”

Braidon had barely gotten over his last fit of laughter, and that stirred him up again, pounding a flat hand on the table. Many people in the guild hall looked in their direction, much to Lisha’s mortification.

“So you have to be a certain age?” Tressa asked.

“Well, it is very uncommon for someone my age. There is no rule when we can choose a mate, but traditionally it doesn’t happen until we are four or five cycles—ah, that’s about forty years. You see, our people live by stars and ocean alignment. We don’t follow the year-by-year calendar. We prefer the alignment of the stars and the Wanderers, that is, the stars that change their place in the sky. When the heavens align with our bodies and the ocean currents, and while the water is warm and we have much to eat, we do our rituals.”

Tressa nodded, knowing that when she said Wanderers, she must be talking about the stars her own people believed to be other worlds and were truly much closer than the stars. Though there was still much debate on exactly what they were. Religious interpretations believed that some gods lived there.

“Do other races ever make it to your islands?” Tressa asked. She was very interested in whether she’d ever get the chance to see Lisha’s home.

“No, I’m afraid not,” Lisha said, giving her a toothy smile that said she knew exactly why Tressa was asking. “The waters are fierce and hard to traverse. The currents are powerful and hard for any boatman to cross. Not impossible, but only a few very skilled captains can make it to us for trade. More often, we swim to the mainland ourselves and do our trading at Yotount port.”

“I see.” Tressa said.

“So, Tressa, what of elves?” Briadon asked.

“Huh?”

“What do you do to find a mate?” Lisha chimed in, clearly ready to pay her back.

“Well. Um. We don't have rituals or rites or things like that.”

“Then what are the trials?” Braidon asked. “Are they not to show your strength and commitment for a mate?”

“No. Um, it’s more of a coming-of-age thing. It has nothing to do with getting a partner. Hmmm…” Tressa looked up as she thought about it. “I guess if someone likes you, or if you like someone, you just flirt and… see what happens? As far as rules, I guess there is an unspoken rule not to try to date or marry too far above or below your station.”

“What do you mean?” Lisha asked.

“So my father is a council member and a lord, or a nobleman. That makes me a nobleman’s daughter, so it's expected I would marry someone from a noble house as well. Like, I couldn't think of dating a chef, a guardsman, or a servant.”

“Elves truly are strange,” Briadon said. “Why should who your father is have anything to do with who you wish to mate with?”

Tressa blushed and waved a hand. “Can you phrase it a different way?” She sighed.

Lisha folded her arms, and her tongue flicked, her expression saying, Ha! How do you like it!?

“Anyway, that's just the way things are,” Tressa went on. “Um, do you have a chieftain or something? Would his daughter be free to, uh… court just anyone she wants?”

Braidon snorted at the word court, then answered. “Of course! Why would her father being the chief matter?”

“Well, because… um…” Tressa struggled to explain something so obvious. “Because then just anyone could become ruler. It, ah… I don’t know how to explain it. It just does not work this way.”

Lisha and Briadon looked at each other, confused. “I agree with Briadon,” Lisha said. “I do not see the relevance.”

“Okay, okay,” Tressa began, thinking of another way to explain. “So how does someone become chieftain?”

“If one wishes to become chieftain, there is a way to go about challenging the current chieftain, but this is rare and usually happens if the chieftain has done something wrong. But there is another way. When the chieftain grows old and cannot fulfill his duties to the clan, a competition is held. The winner will sometimes choose a daughter of the chief as his mate, for the sake of having her support in understanding his new duties, but it is not a requirement.”

“That’s just…” Tressa frowned, thinking how to explain all the problems with that system. “So you're saying just anyone can be chieftain, just by being strong enough?”

“Of course. I do not see why this confuses you.”

“Well… because nobility and succession are meant to keep things stable,” Tressa said, frowning slightly. “Power stays with people who are raised knowing how to rule. They are taught for it their whole lives. If you do not have those traditions, then… anyone can decide they should rule. People will fight for power, there can be civil war...”

Lisha arched a scaly brow, swirling her drink slowly. “Tressa, humans have a similar system, so I am familiar with this. And do you realize many wars were started specifically because of nobility and succession?”

“Yes,” Tressa admitted with a sigh, “but those are… what is the word. It’s just not how it’s supposed to work.”

“From what I’ve seen,” Lisha said, “There is nothing uncommon about humans killing each other over crowns and thrones. Many have died over nobles who believe they should rule.”

“Well… It's true that does happen with we High Elves as well…” Tressa admitted reluctantly.

Briadon grunted in agreement, folding his arms across his chest. “Shouldn’t those who wish to rule prove themselves directly? Why hide behind armies? Why let soldiers bleed and die for a leader who sits safe behind walls? That is just senseless killing when it can be limited to just the two who are in conflict.”

Tressa opened her mouth to argue but paused. She furrowed her brow, lips pressed together in a thin line. “...That’s actually a good point,” she muttered. “But still, then someone who is strong but evil could just take leadership whenever they want.”

“Then he will have many challengers and will fight to maintain his position constantly,” Briadon said. “A chieftain who is loved and puts forth the good of his clan will have respect, and none would dishonor themselves by challenging such a loved chieftain.”

Tressa sat back, thinking about it. She couldn't deny there was some logic to it. Why did entire armies die over who gets to rule when it could just be the few who want the position?

She then asked Lisha, “Do the statiki have a system like that as well?”

Lisha shrugged. “No, we don't have leaders in that way. The closest we have are wise elders, those whose experience and wisdom are sought out in times when we require guidance. We have been fortunate to have little war in the history of our people. Just some disputes amongst our tribes that turned to violence, but not to the scale of the human or elven wars I’ve heard of. As for outside forces, I suppose it helps that our island would be too difficult to attack, with little benefit to doing so. I imagine if a nation wished to attack us, we would easily sink their boats from below before they ever made it to shore.” She chuckled, flashing her fangs before taking a big gulp of her drink.

Tressa took a drink as well, letting out a satisfied sigh as the ale gave a slight burn to her throat.

“Seems you’ve really taken to drinking ale. Huh, elf?” Briadon laughed.

“Of course, it pairs so well with the food.” She grinned. “But if I find proper everwine, I will have you drink.”

He rolled his eyes and took a drink of his own. “Wine? I’ll pass.”

“So, Tressa, do wars happen among your people for the throne?”

Tressa paused, her thoughts drifting to the past.

“I guess during the mad king’s rule there was a lot of fighting. But he took part in most of the killing. And as far as I know, many tried to assassinate him but didn't succeed until the current king, Faldin. He tried to help our people to not hate him, so he created the council. It’s made up of nine members: three heads and six underseers. My father is a head. They have to take a blood oath that they will do what is best for our people. And if they don't, the broken oath kills them. And their child will succeed them, or someone else whom they deem fit will take that seat. There has to be an odd number to prevent unequal standings. And then Faldin will agree or disagree with the council’s decisions. But he tends to listen to the council.”

“You seem to understand this quite well,” Lisha said.

“I’ve been to meetings. My father said I should see how it works…” She grinned sheepishly, thinking of those days.

“Really?”

“Yeah. And I don't want to do anything with ruling. I don’t want to be in the high council. It’s not who I am. That's why I left…” She grabbed her mug and raised it high in the air. “I think I’m better suited to adventuring!”

“I would agree.” Lisha flashed her fangs, grabbing her mug as well.

“Aye! Cheers to that!” Braidon bellowed.

“To adventuring!” All three cheered, clinking their glasses together, then drinking.
