As the world grew dark, one thing stayed in focus.

Velidan.

It hung, a hard slash of unyielding black amidst the dim green and flickering flames. Then it grew. Stretched. Carved through smoke, slicing the very fabric of the air. One end cut down to the floor. The other slashed upwards.

And from beyond it I glimpsed a vivid black sky, one bedecked with endless silver…

He was there. Standing in the gap. Eyes wide, mouth ajar, for once looking not regal or amused or arrogant but utterly shocked. He whirled in a circle, taking in the rank cave he had been- pulled? called? what had I done?- brought to.

He stared. At me. At the hulking figure atop me.

I had seen displeasure on that perfect face. I had seen annoyance and anger.

Now I saw rage.

Baltha’s head rose, scorched skin bright with tears. Hissed, teeth bared, at the figure now standing over us.

And was torn away. Earth trembled as her huge bulk hit the wall. The air rang with a shriek as her claws swiped upwards, ripping flesh.

She’s bigger. Strong. Not safe. Get up.

Smoke filled my lungs as roars and snarls filled the room. My heart pounded unevenly. My very blood felt sluggish. Darkness swallowed the edges of my vision.

No. No. Stay awake. Teela. Renner. They need me. Stay awake.

The hag bellowed. Stones broke. Something ripped. He growled. The sound was thick with pain.

Renner groaned.

I rolled myself towards the sound. Blinked through the stagnant haze. Pressed my palms into the warm dirt. Pushed.

He lay against the cave wall. Eyes glazed. Unfocused. One bloody hand was dragging against the stone. Trying to pull himself upright. Failing. Crimson trickled down both sides of his neck and from his open mouth.

There was blood on the stones around him. Some black, some red.

Too much red.

I got to my knees. Crawled.

Renner’s eyes slowly focused on me. Then they lifted, sharpening with the clarity of fear, at whatever hideous sight lay in the shadows behind.

Don’t turn around. Don’t look. Get to him. Help him.

Stay awake.

Baltha screeched. Something snapped with a crack that rattled my teeth.

I made it to Renner. Touched his jaw, turned it with trembling fingers. The back of his hair was a mass of matted, sticky crimson

Bad. Really bad. Clerics. He needs help.

“H-have to go,” I rasped. My throat felt raw. “I n-need you to stand up. Okay?”

“Wha…” his words slurred. “What… nngh…”

I wrapped both hands around one arm. Muscle and sweat-slicked skin quivered beneath my touch. I tried to pull him up, but my own arms were shaking and his weight was too much. “Renner, I can’t… I need you to stand.” My voice cracked. “Please. Please get up.”

He tried. Torn, bleeding palms braced against the earth. He pushed himself into a kneel, then slumped back with a groan.

He can’t. Too hurt. Okay. Okay. Teela. Get Teela. We can help him together.

Panic at the sight of his wounds lent strength to my limbs. I stumbled back into the smoke.

The earth-shaking thuds and snarls had fallen ominously quiet. The two Fae were circling each other at the far end of the cave.

Teela lay face-up on the slab. Her skin was milk-white, chest rising in an uneven rhythm. Embers smoldered around us as I wiped the rancid plants from her face.

“Teela? Teela!” Every word was raw. “Wake up, come on, wake up…” I shook her. Nothing. Saints. What did Baltha do to her? My gaze swept down. There was a jagged gash along her pale forearm, as if great, flat teeth had bitten into her flesh. The foul-smelling wound leaked a slow trickle of blood into the stone basin. Crimson had mixed with strips of torn, familiar silver bark.

Something snapped. Baltha screeched.

“Teela! Wake up!” I shook her again, harder. Her head lolled, hair limp against the stones. Nothing.

There was a heavy thud from behind, followed by a dreadful, chilling quiet.

Then the sound of weeping.

“Mercy!” the hag wailed. “Mercy, please, please… Baltha begs…”

My heart nearly stopped. I sank forward against the slab, legs limp with the sudden flood of relief. He won. He won.

“Mercy?!” He sounded breathless. Furious. “How long have you been here?! You’d put us all in danger. And for what?!”

“Mercy,” she sobbed again, nails scraping against the ground. “Mercy. Don’t hurt her.”

Silence.

Then, in a voice like death, “Hurt… who?”

I turned, blood running cold, as the hag’s shining eyes fell on me. His gaze followed.

My gorge rose at the sight. Baltha knelt, the ground around her slick and gleaming. Her green skin was a tapestry of black blood and exposed red flesh. Both of her enormous arms dangled, their palms facing the wrong direction. One hip jutted out too far.

He stood nearby, panting. His clothes and chest were torn, a mess of ripped cloth and spattered, visceral black. Blood covered his hands and arms.

“Mercy,” she moaned, slumping forwards. “Mercy for the mortal.”

“Mercy… for… why?”

“Baltha remembered. So long, so long in the dark. So long without… Sweet, precious mortal.” The hag sobbed into the dirt, then lifted her wretched visage towards me as I stumbled back. “Thank you. Thank you. Baltha remembered. Don’t hurt her.”

He looked between us, utterly mystified. “Why would you care about a human’s life?”

She trembled, heaving against the ground, and gave no further answer.

He stared at me for a long time, too-sharp teeth flashing. Then, with thoughtful slowness, paced in front of her. “I don’t have the strength to drag you back with me, crone. Not now. I doubt you have it, either.”

Baltha’s blistered, glistening face tilted up.

Pale, blood-slicked fingers flexed. “So if you’re not going to answer…”

She snarled as hand fisted in her burnt hair. The other grabbed a knotted shoulder. Baltha screamed and my ears rang and flesh tore-

I staggered away from the grisly scene, vision spinning. The scream cut off into bitter silence. Something fell to the ground with a thud.

My palms hit the cave wall. I panted. Choked down bile as air rustled behind. I turned, slumping against earth-dusted rock.

He loomed over me, carved features twisted into something feral. “Explain this.”

“Explain-?”

“You just ripped me across… How?!” Bloody hands slammed into the stones beside my head. “Answer!”

“H-how?” I shrank back, heart pounding. “I… I used a r-rune. I thought of it, so I… I… and then you… you came.”

“A rune,” he hissed. “So you actually… A rune. What was it?”

“Vel… Velidan.” I raised my hand without thought, fingers twisting to offer proof. Carved air shimmered for one breath before his hand closed tightly over mine. Ending the magic nearly as soon as it began.

He released me, mouth tight, watching the fading dark warily.

Spots danced across my vision. I swayed, half-falling against an arm that dropped to brace me. “I… I just… it was just… there. I d-didn’t know what… and you’re here. You… you saved us.” My voice hitched. I could hardly believe the words. My split lips quivered as I gazed up, eyes rounding in dizzy astonishment. “You saved us.”

He searched my face, breathing raggedly. Obvious anger and confusion softened, something hungry rising in their stead. “I… saved you.” The words came out slowly. Thoughtfully, as if he was tasting each one. “I… I suppose I did.”

“Thank you.” I sagged against the wall, gasping in fetid air. “Thank you.”

Dark eyes slid downwards, settling on my bloody mouth. Then continued, tracing my aching neck. His voice became very soft. Almost disbelieving. “I saved you. No one… else.” He tilted his head, gaze slowly dragging away from me to peer over one shoulder. Examining the stagnant, smoke-filled cave, as if looking for someone. “No one came for you. You’re… alone.”

“No, I’m… please, my friends.” My spine straightened with the reminder, the urgency, of what lay behind him. “W-we tried to fight her. Please, please, can you help Renner? She hit him. His h-head, there’s a lot of blood… he needs help. A-and Teela. She doesn’t seem hurt, but I can’t wake her up. I don’t know what’s wrong with her.”

The Fae’s endless, glittering gaze settled back on me. “Old magic. An attempt to keep her alive. Fresh. Hags crave Fae blood, and so rarely are they strong enough to take any.” A smile tugged at the corners of his mouth, and he let out a little laugh. “One of their many ironies. Pitiful things.”

“Can you undo it? And Renner?” Every word hitched, thick in my aching throat and the desperation welling within it. “Can you save him?”

“Oh, Brin.” His voice was velvet and smoke. Soft and warm, curling around me. “Of course I can.”

“Thank you,” I gasped, close to weeping. “Oh, thank you. Hurry, please-” I tried to step past, towards my fallen friends.

Blood-slicked arms kept me caged.

The smile widened.

I froze. Listened to the echo of his words. Stared up with dawning horror.

“I was willing to be patient.” His tone dropped to barely a murmur, eyes searching mine, and the expression twisted into something thoughtful. “To sway you with charm. But… mm.” The Fae inhaled deeply, shoulders shivering with a little growl. “Blood everywhere, a dying man… It’s just like the first night we met. And I know just how to motivate you.”

“No…” I shook my head, cringing against the wall. “No. I… I thought…” What? That he was good? That he cared? The words were so shamefully foolish that they died on my tongue. And all around us… shadows. Everywhere. Endless dark. Spreading up the walls, blanketing the floor, swallowing the room, swallowing me. I bit back a sob, legs crumpling beneath me.

“Oh, poor thing.” Silk rustled as he knelt. A bloody hand cupped my chin. “I suppose you’re in no state to appreciate the irony.”

Every breath came too fast. My heartbeat roared in my ears, deafening and wild. No, no! He saved us, he can’t… he can’t…

“Don’t fret, Brin.” He tilted my face up. Dipped his own down, until my world narrowed to blood-spattered skin and endless, starlit eyes. “I won’t hurt you. You looked at me so sweetly, before.”

“L-let go.” I tried to pull away. Pressed myself into stone and earth. “Let me go.”

“No. Hold still.” One slick thumb slid up, parting my lips. Then the Fae gave a little grunt, and something thunked to the dirt nearby. He cast a glance over one shoulder, scoffing. “Stay down, worm.”

I flicked my gaze past him, and immediately wished I hadn’t. Renner was propped, half-sitting, against the wall. He was panting. One bloody hand clutched slowly, numbly, at a fist-sized rock. And on his face was the look I’d been dreading. Revulsion. Hatred.

“Please,” I whimpered. “I don’t want him to die. Please.”

“Then you should save him.” The grip tightened. His thumb traced my mouth, and cool silver flickered with the motion. “Or, if you’re feeling stubborn, we can sit here and just… watch, for a while.”

“No!” I wrapped my fingers around the iron wrist, straining. Futile. “Stop! W-what do you…?”

“Don’t be coy.”

“I… I don’t have it with me.”

“I know. This would be going very differently if you did.” The hand on my chin dropped, closing instead around my aching neck. Exactly where Baltha had held me.

Terror spiked through my chest at the sensation. I thrashed in immediate, blind panic. Clawed at him. Screamed through a raw throat and lungs ready to burst.

He snarled over me. “Hold still. Calm your… here. I’ll even take my boot off your other friend’s throat. Royce, wasn’t it?”

The fear didn’t abate, but his words did return some measure of calm to my limbs. I stared up, wild-eyed and panting, hands vice-like around his wrist. “W-what?”

“Consider it a kindness.” He gave a little chuckle. Shadows twisted around us and heat pooled through my stomach. A feeling of sheer relief, one that stood in visceral contrast to the memory of ice snaking around my belly only a few nights ago. His hand flexed against my skin, and the ache in my neck faded to a memory as I lay limp and shuddering. “There. Feeling more cooperative, now?”

Some distant part of my mind screamed out in denial. Railed for me to refuse. To stand firm, to deny this wicked creature what he wanted.

But I didn’t know how to help Teela. Renner couldn’t stand, much less make it back to the surface. And I didn’t have it in me to leave them here, bloody and broken, and crawl out alone.

I nodded.

His hand fell away. “Good. I’ll heal your friends, and you’ll give me the shard the next time you sleep.”

“Alright,” I whispered, closing my eyes in defeat.

“Say the words, Brin.” His voice lowered, pleased and thick. “Just like the first time. Bind yourself to them.”

“If you… if you heal them, I’ll… I’ll give you the shard the next time I sleep.”

I kept my eyes shut. I was too ashamed to open them and see that cruel, victorious smile. But I heard the hiss of silk as the Fae leaned in, felt the warm air on my lips as he murmured, “We have a deal.”
