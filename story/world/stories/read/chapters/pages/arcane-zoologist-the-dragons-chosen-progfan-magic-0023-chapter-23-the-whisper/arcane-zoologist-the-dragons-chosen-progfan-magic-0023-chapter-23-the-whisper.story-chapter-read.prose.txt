Tristan's weird behaviour didn't stop. As Callie cleaned the linens for the patients' beds, she would catch the boy occasionally glancing in her direction. But as soon as their eyes met, he would look away and act as if he hadn't been looking at all.

What's up with him?

Even Granny Ethel noticed, despite being busy with patients. During lunchtime, when they had a moment for a short break, the elderly woman said, "Tristan, what's up with you today? You're acting strange."

"I am not!" He protested.

"You're definitely acting weird," Callie confirmed.

"You're one to talk! You're the biggest weirdo in the whole village!" He spat, his eyebrow furrowed angrily, and his cheeks red.

"Tristan! You can't say something so rude. Callie, has he been bullying you again?" Granny Ethel demanded, crossing her arms over her chest.

"I have not!" Tristan protested.

"Well… I guess he hasn't," Callie said. "I think he tried to apologize to me this morning."

"Tried? What do you mean tried? I did apologize!"

Granny Ethel looked at Tristan in bafflement. "You apologized to Callie? Of your own accord?"

"You didn't force him to do that?" Callie asked in surprise.

At this point, poor Tristan was as red as a beet from head to toe. Callie couldn't believe it.

"Aww! I'm so proud of you, Tristan. You're finally growing up," Granny Ethel said and pulled him into a gentle hug.

"Stop it! Let go!" The boy squirmed out of the woman's grasp, but Callie couldn't help but laugh at the ridiculous scene. It was actually heartwarming to know that Tristan actually meant his apology in the morning. Maybe he wasn't so bad after all.

"Hey, Tristan," Callie prompted, thinking about the strange occurrence in the morning.

"What?" He glared at her, his skin color slowly returning to normal.

"Your cat. Is she very timid?"

Tristan shook his head. "Not really. She's very friendly and brave. I once saw her chase away Chief's wolfhound. That was a sight to see."

"That's odd…" Callie muttered.

"A little, yeah. But she's very protective of her home, you see. It's thanks to her that we never really needed a dog. She pretty much is one," the boy said proudly.

"No, not that. This morning, I saw her run into the barn and climb all the way up to the wooden beam. She seemed frightened."

Tristan narrowed his eyes. "That is odd. Are you sure it was our cat?"

"Is there another ginger cat with white paws in the village?" Callie asked.

"No, I guess not."

There was a heavy silence as everyone seemed to be immersed in their own thoughts. The cat was acting strangely, and the odd pull Callie had felt. Could it have something to do with the egg? Maybe it was hidden somewhere near Tristan's home?

"Y-You know, maybe in the afternoon we could go and check on her. The cat, I mean," Tristan said, awkwardly scratching the back of his neck.

"I wish I could, but I have lessons with Mister Blackthorn," Callie said.

"Oh…"

Did he sound disappointed?

Callie quickly dismissed that thought. Why would Tristan feel disappointed that he couldn't spend the afternoon with Callie? It was nice that he was making amends and even going out of his way to sate her curiosity about his cat. But interpreting it as anything other than politeness was silly.

In either case, Callie was busy thinking of her plan—the plan to find the egg. The first thing she had to do was pass Mister Blackthorn's shelter building class. After that, he would likely give her a few days during which she could search for the egg. So today she would focus on his instructions completely.

The problem was that once again, Mister Blackthorn had decided that practice was the best kind of instruction. Wordlessly, he just provided Callie with materials and watched as she struggled to make the shelter. Nonetheless, she didn't give up. She would try to strike up a conversation with Mister Blackthorn and learn as much from him as possible.

"M-Mister Blackthorn," she addressed the man who glared at her. Callie flinched, wondering if it was best to keep quiet and try to manage things on her own as best as she could. But no! She had to do her best to pass, so she continued. "W-Where's the best place to build a shelter?"

"Where do you think is the best place?" He snapped back.

This was going nowhere. She tried to make a few guesses and got a few grunts in response, some of which were likely yes and others – no. She also tried to ask how to best build the shelter, but the more she asked, the deeper and more annoyed his grunts sounded. Eventually, Callie didn't dare to ask questions anymore.

Somehow, he seemed more willing to answer questions when the other girls were around. He seemed far more irritated today. She noticed him occasionally tapping his good leg. At one point, he even shifted to his other leg, the one he usually limped on. It ended with him clutching it in pain.

"Are you alright?" Callie asked in concern. "Perhaps, you need an ointment for your injury?"

Mister Blackthorn shook his head. "This is an old injury and not something one of your herb ointments can help with. It was inflicted by a monster after all."

Callie wanted to protest, but had half the mind to stop herself, and instead she asked. "Which one?"

"The very same thing your mother nearly lost her arm to. Glauxis," Mister Blackthorn said, and then spat angrily on the ground. "The world would be a better place if creatures like that ceased to exist."

Callie's fist clenched. How could he say such a cruel thing? She could somewhat understand his anger towards the glauxis. It had mangled his leg after all. But it likely even wasn't the same glauxis. Wishing the whole species to go extinct—that was too much. She wanted to protest loudly.

But she didn't dare to speak up against this scary man. Who knew what he would do? It was best to keep her head down and work on the shelter. As she lined the roof of her shelter with thick, leafy branches, her mind once again wandered back to the egg and how it could be hidden in the forest in a shelter like this one, perfectly masked among the foliage but warm enough.

Another thought crossed her mind as she lined the inside of the shelter with moss. Would it really be feasible to keep an egg in it? What did a glauxis nest look like? Did they even build nests?

Callie's eyes landed on Mister Blackthorn. Would he perhaps know? He certainly had encountered one before, so it was possible he knew whether they built a nest or not. But asking him about glauxis nests out of the blue… Callie didn't quite have the courage for that.

She agonized over it for some time as she kept working on the shelter. Eventually, she managed to come up with a question that she believed to be safe to ask.

"Mister Blackthorn, are these shelters similar to the way birds build nests?" Callie asked.

The man looked thoughtful for a moment, then shook his head. "The very basics perhaps, but conceptually they are similar to dens made by larger monsters."

Callie had a feeling that was the case, so she pressed her question further. "Larger monsters? They make dens like our shelters?"

"Yes," the man said curtly, not explaining further.

This felt like pulling teeth. But Callie persisted. "How do they do it exactly? They don't have rope, do they?"

"Nobody sits around watching them make their dens. They likely use clay to keep the water out and moss to make the den warmer," Mister Blackthorn said, tapping his good foot impatiently.

"Could they dig in underground? To hide it from other creatures?"

"Enough, girl!" Mister Blackthorn snapped, standing up to his feet. "I am not here to foster your childish fantasies about monsters. I am here to teach you how to survive in the wild. No more talking!"

Callie shrank behind her shelter, trying to get out of the way of his glare. Why was he even so angry? She hadn't asked anything specifically about the glauxis, yet he had reacted so badly. Was she right to assume that a shelter could be dug underground?

He had said that a shelter couldn't be in a low place where water could gather during rainfall. But what if the shelter was dug on an incline? Like a small cave where water couldn't get it? It would be incredibly easy to hide the egg in a place like that, and it would be kept in a dark, warm and moist place.

Callie felt like she was getting closer and closer to finding the egg, so she did her best to work hard on her shelter, covering the roof meticulously so water couldn't get through and lining the inside with moss and ferns. Once she announced that she was done, Mister Blackthorn examined it with a clearly annoyed expression.

"You pass," he said. "But I suggest you drop your obsession with monsters. It won't do you any good. "

She nodded numbly in response and hurriedly left the forest. Why was he so angry today? Did he really dislike Callie so much? Or was it something else entirely? It appeared that he had grown more annoyed when she had mentioned the arcane beast nest. Callie couldn't make sense of it.

"What took you so long?!" Tristan snapped in annoyance, his arms crossed over his chest as he tapped his foot impatiently. He was standing not too far from their home.

"What? Why are you here?" Callie asked suspiciously. Had he been waiting for her? No way! Tristan would always do his best to leave Callie behind, bragging about how fast he was.

Tristan flinched, then awkwardly scratched the back of his neck. "I…"

He muttered something so silently that Callie couldn't hear him, so she stepped closer. "What? I couldn't hear you!"

"You wanted to see Millie, right?" He snapped, his eyebrow furrowed angrily.

"Millie?"

"Our ginger cat!"

"Oh!" Callie realized what he meant now. It wasn't that she wanted to see the cat. She was just curious as to why she was acting so strange. "I just thought she was acting strange, is all."

Tristan nodded, his frown softening. "Yeah, when I got home, I checked on Millie and found her huddled in the corner of my bedroom. She had never done that before. But that's not all. Our goats, for some reason, completely avoided grazing in their favorite spot."

"Their favorite spot?" Callie tilted her head quizzically.

"Yeah. You know my dad cleared away a large chunk of the forest to create a perfect place for our goats and cows to graze. They have the best milk in the whole village!" Tristan bragged.

Callie nodded. That much was true. Mom and Dad always opted to trade for milk with their neighbors, and Callie also believed theirs tasted better. Likely because the goats and the cow were so well cared for.

"They usually love grazing near the forest, but today they had been sticking close to the house. When I tried to coax them to go further, they began bleating and mooing. It was weird!" Tristan said.

"C-Could you show me where they normally graze?" Callie asked. Perhaps these animals were sensing something. The same thing that Callie had sensed in the morning.

"Sure! Follow me!" Tristan announced with a wide grin, and they headed into the backyard of his home.

As Tristan had said, the cow and goats were grazing near the house while the pastures closer to the forest remained empty. As Callie got closer to the end of the pasture, she could once again feel that tug. Very gently at first, but it got increasingly stronger the closer to the forest they got.

The feeling was unmistakable now. It was almost as if something was calling out to her. A whisper? A voice? It felt scared and lonely. Callie couldn't help but follow it, ignoring her surroundings completely.

"Hey, where are you going?" Tristan complained as Callie crawled over the fence and headed into the forest.

"I'll be right back," Callie muttered, afraid to lose her connection to the voice. It was very faint. She had to keep concentrating on it to understand where it was coming from.

"Carrothead?" Tristan called, also climbing over the fence.

She ignored him and kept going. The voice was somewhere close by.

"Calluna, your arm is glowing!" Tristan called in panic, and Callie finally snapped her attention to her arm. The mark on her hand was glowing brightly, and Tristan was looking straight at it, his face twisted with fear and shock as he took several steps away from her.
