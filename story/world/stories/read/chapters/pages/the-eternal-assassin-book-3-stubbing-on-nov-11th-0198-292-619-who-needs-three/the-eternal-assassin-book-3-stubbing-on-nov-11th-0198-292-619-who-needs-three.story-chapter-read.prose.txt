“That’s a mighty big smile you’ve got there,” Moxy said the next day, startling Asher out of his daydreaming as he caught himself looking at Samantha yet again. Despite having spent an amazing night with his new fiancé to celebrate their eventual union and it having been a few hours since having all that weight of immortality lifted off his chest, Asher couldn’t help but find himself thinking about how lucky he was. Samantha was doing a bit better of a job acting like nothing had changed than he was, but he still caught her smiling at him from across what had become known as the war table every now and again.

“I happen to be happy,” Asher shrugged, rolling his eyes as Moxy raised an eyebrow. “I know it might be a strange concept to you, being happy while not currently connecting my fist with anyone’s face, but it is possible.”

“Well, duh. Sometimes it’s my foot connecting with their skull that brings me joy!”

“Focus you two,” Rulfar said, frowning at them as he finished sketching out a map on the whiteboard. “Asher is allowed to be happy, and Moxy is allowed to be… Moxy. Regretfully. Right now we need to worry about taking down our next lich. But before we can do that, we have to actually find The Lost Lady.”

“Why are you going after her specifically?” Kroult asked, the lich looking surprisingly comfortable sunken into one of the beanbag chairs as he was. “With Hylm and the King of Death slain, and myself and Zygrul off the table, that leaves ten liches still on the Council. Why her?”

“The same reason we started with the King of Death,” Rulfar explained. “Hylm was set up for us by Zygrul, but we don’t want to tip our hands to the Council that we have journals filled with information on the other liches just yet. Some of the journals even have locations that will get us pretty close to where the other liches are hiding. Unlike the others, the King of Death and The Lost Lady are the only two liches who are somewhat publicly known. Our hope is that the Council just thinks we managed to figure out that those two are liches. The moment we suddenly swoop down and attack a base that is supposed to be a secret, they’re going to know their current locations are compromised.”

“Ideally, that means we can take out The Lost Lady and one other lich before the Council gets wise,” Samantha said. “Which brings the Council down to only eight members. Donvath told us he’d be able to convince at least a handful of the other countries on our continent to intervene and go on the offensive with us to take down the rest of the Council once they were down to roughly half of their original size. The thought of over a dozen incredibly powerful, immortal wizards with nothing to lose raining death and destruction on any one country is enough to keep everyone scared. But only five or six wizards? That’s a lot more manageable.”

“As someone who’s felt the power of only one of them at a time, that’s still a pretty huge ask,” Asher admitted, shivering at the memories of the different liches. Hylm had locked him out of his own brain, her Love element preventing him from saying or doing anything. Zygrul’s Void element had been capable of scraping out his tritessence and erased his arm in an instant. The King of Death had created countless powerful minions to serve under him and radiated a Death Aura that stretched for dozens of miles in all directions. It was no wonder none of the kingdoms of this continent were willing to take the fight to the Council, Asher couldn’t even begin to imagine the havoc the liches could cause if they attacked together.

“Duly noted,” Kroult said, nodding along. “In that case, how are we going to take down The Lost Lady?”

“That’s the million-shard question,” Rulfar said, tapping at the sketch of a cave system he’d made on the board. “We don’t know much, but some of it is still useful. The Lost Lady, whose actual name is Iphiane, lives inside this cave system. The true size of the cave system is unknown, largely because anyone who tries to venture inside has a nasty habit of never coming out again. The reason why Iphiane is even called The Lost Lady in the first place is because of an old wives' tale from the nearby village. Every couple of years she’ll emerge from the caves under the cover of darkness and snatch away an able-bodied man, dragging him kicking and screaming back into the caves with her. The villagers believe she's some form of ancient spirit who lost her own husband and is trying to find a replacement, they have no idea what she truly is. The timeframe between these kidnappings seems to grow longer depending on if any intrepid explorers recently vanished within the caves or not, lending credence to Zygrul’s theory that she experiments on them.”

“And the villagers just put up with this?” Samantha asked, frowning at the idea. “Why not go somewhere else?”

“People can get used to just about anything,” Rulfar shrugged. “Sometimes over ten years pass between her showing up to drag someone away with her. Claiming only a dozen lives every century means she’s probably not even in the top ten causes of death in the village.”

“I have witnessed villages put up with far worse during my travels,” Kroult said sadly. “Villages living in close proximity to monster zones that would kill dozens every year. Towns in the center of bandit-heavy areas responsible for so much theft and destruction. What seems strange to you or I can become a person’s normal quite easily.”

“I’m surprised they don’t fight back,” Moxy pointed out. “You said she works under the cover of darkness, but if people have seen her, then she’s really not being all that stealthy, right?”

“Correct. There have been plenty of sightings of her during her kidnappings,” Rulfar confirmed. “And people do try to fight back. It is due to the fact that she completely ignores anyone other than her latest target, shrugging off skills and weapons alike, that has led the people of the village to believe she’s some form of untouchable, vengeful spirit.”

“So she has some sort of powerful defensive skill,” Asher muttered, laying a comforting hand on his void dagger. He’d yet to encounter a single thing capable of stopping the blade made from void from cutting through it, but if such a thing existed, he bet it would be tied to one of the liches. “Do we have more details? Are we talking armor? Regeneration? Evasion?”

“Probably a mix between armor and regeneration,” Rulfar said. “She doesn’t make any effort to dodge attacks, all the reading I did says she just ignores them. Zygrul’s journal hypothesizes that she at least has the Flesh element, but he wasn’t confident in making any other predictions. Flesh would explain both the armor and regeneration.”

“So we’ve got a lich with two entirely unknown elements. Not to mention the fact that they’re a lich, which means they could have who knows how many other skills stored away they can replicate,” Samantha said as she stared hard at the whiteboard. “Do we have any good news?”

“We don’t have to worry about greater demons or a gigantic Death Aura that will kill us if we walk too far,” Rulfar chuckled. “Presumably, she’s been turning these men she’s been taking into minions somehow, but I doubt they will be as deadly as the greater demons. I think finding her is going to be trickier than actually killing her. Or at least I hope it will be.”

“Got any ideas on that front?” Moxy asked. “You need something she personally owns, or some of her blood, right? I don’t suppose we have access to either?”

“Unfortunately, we don’t,” Rulfar admitted. “Her last kidnapping was a full three years ago, so she probably won’t go after the village again for some time. We’re going to have to do this the hard way. Head straight into the cave system and try to find her ourselves. Most likely she’ll send her minions after us, and my hope is we can try to use those to track her down.”

“Seems like as good a plan as any,” Asher said. “Though I think we should swing by the village before we head into the caves. If her last kidnapping was only three years ago, memories still have to be at least somewhat fresh as to what attacking her was like.”

“I was going to suggest the very same,” Rulfar smiled. “Intelligence gathering is the key to any victory. The more we know, the less surprised we’ll be.”

“And the more boring the fight,” Moxy added, chuckling at Rulfar’s glare. “Relax, I’m not disagreeing. I know these liches are bad news. Anyway, sounds like we’ve got the battle plan worked out for the most part. What now?”

“All we really need to do is grab a few more potions from Polly’s and then we’re good to go. But before that, there’s one last matter we need to discuss.” Rulfar turned toward Kroult, and the lich sank deeper into his beanbag as the bloodhound towered over him. “Kroult. What actually are your elements? Donvath chucked you at us so last-minute we never really had this conversation. We know you have plenty of weaker spells from all your travels, but we need to know what you’re capable of.”

“That is fair,” Kroult said, the fires dancing nervously in his eyes. Asher found it more than a little weird how the longer he spent hanging around the lich, the more he was able to read his expression just from how the twin flames shifted. “If I am to be entirely honest… Pain is my origin element.”

“Pain?” Asher repeated, staring at the quiet lich in shock. “…That would not have been my first guess.”

“I have told you all before, I did not have what one would consider a very good start to my life,” Kroult admitted with a sigh. “Again, the horrid conditions of my childhood were what my master rescued me from. It was why I spent the rest of my life worshiping the very land he walked on, up until… until the end.”

“So what skills do you have? Not all the countless random ones you learned as a wizard, I mean from your Pain element,” Rulfar clarified.

“Hedge wizard,” Kroult corrected with a raised, frail finger. “I will not pretend I am something I am not. As for my skills, they are not all that useful. I can inflict an unnatural pain in those that I touch. I can also remove pain by doing the very same. If I concentrate, I can sense the location of those in pain over a significant area. I can detect what would cause any single person the most pain, be it in a physical, emotional, or some other kind. And I can convert my own physical pain into physical strength. Though that last one is largely useless now, as I do not feel such pain as an undead.”

“Good to know,” Rulfar nodded. “And your other elements?”

“I do not have any other elements.”

The room was silent as four pairs of eyes stared at the waiting lich. After a few seconds, Asher cleared his throat.

“…Seriously? You don’t need all three elements in order to become a lich? I guess I just always assumed that was a requirement.”

“I will not pretend to know how the ritual for lichdom functions,” Kroult shrugged. “Seeing as my master’s ritual worked, it would appear that is not the case. We were far from well-off during our travels, and the few times we stumbled into elements, my master always elected to sell them. I was more than happy to do whatever he asked of me. Not once did I think it was unfair.”

“Okay. I also never considered that Kroult only had his origin element to his name,” Rulfar admitted. “Still, good to know about those Pain skills. I doubt I need to say this, but just to be safe, if you ever detect someone feeling pain nearby where there shouldn’t be, make sure you tell us immediately.”

“I will do so,” Kroult nodded.

“In that case, all that’s left for us now is to send out another pegasus courier with instructions to carry something Asher’s left an Eternal Mark on to that village,” Rulfar said, looking eager to go after their next target. “Once they arrive… we launch our attack.”
