One in four dead!

— Diary of Xu Ziqing, Imperial Scribe, Tianqiu Plane – now known as The Origin, Outer Realms

Jianyi was cold.

Colder than he ever had been in his life.

Shivering, he fought his way out of the pond, his robes heavy with water.

Despite all of that, he had the presence of mind to pull the large beaver’s body out of the pond with him.

Once back on dry ground, he sagged to his knees and took a deep breath. He had done it. Had fought and triumphed over a spirit beast all on his own. There certainly were things he could have done better, but just in this very moment, it didn’t matter.

Shivering, he dropped his heavy coat into his storage ring.

With shaking fingers, he produced his spirit needle from storage and held it over the beaver. His lower dantian was quite empty of qi after having used two techniques in the fight, but there was more than enough left to create a thin thread that would connect to the spirit needle and hopefully to the remains of the spirit beast.

He reached out, hoping that the beast had had sufficient proficiency with its techniques for them to materialize in the form of cards… success!

Within moments, Jianyi made contact with the spirit, and a bundle of qi formed above the carcass, consolidating into the shape of a card.

With a broad grin, only marred by his chattering teeth, Jianyi grabbed the card and took a look at what he had gained.

Vital Vines

Technique

Common

1 x Wood

Creates a set of vines that can be controlled by the cultivator, lasts for five breaths

For a moment, Jianyi had thought that the beaver had been a Water-aligned creature, but the card proved that wrong. It was a good technique, one that could expand his capabilities in a fight significantly.

But.

There was a big but.

While his first step on the path of body cultivation required an Earth-based potion, the second one, focused on his internal organs, called for Wood as the basis.

If he moved the card into his upper dantian, how quickly would he be able to get another one?

Jianyi felt that the freezing cold limited his ability to think this through.

He put the card away and did the same with the whole body of the beaver—there would be time to deal with it later.

There was nothing he would like to do more than get out of his wet robes and return to their camp, though he hesitated with a glance to the large pine tree in the middle of the pond.

He was fairly sure that it was a natural treasure, and if he left now, who knew what beast would claim the location while he was gone.

If there was one thing he did not want to do, it was jumping back into the pond that had almost seen him drown.

A deep breath.

Three quick steps.

He jumped as far as he could into the pond.

The advantage of his body temperature being as low as it was, the shock of jumping back in was considerably less than before.

Still, he felt like this was unsustainable.

As fast as he could, which was a lot slower than he wanted, he swam towards the small island at the center of the pond.

The brown of the bark was pretty.

The last rays of the sun were, too.

The beaver had certainly been a cuddly creature, right?

Maybe he should just rest a bit longer in the pond…

The fingers he had hurt earlier touched the shore of the island, and the pain brought him back to reality.

He briefly wondered whether this was one risk too many for the day.

Then, he remembered a story. Was it one of the Silent Fox? No, he didn’t think so. It was about a cultivator who had fought on the frontlines and had gotten separated from his circle during a qi-infused snowstorm.

Supposedly, he had stayed alive by cycling qi for days without end.

Maybe he should look at the tree and leave quickly, but Jianyi took his spear and moved slowly through one movement of the Eternal Spear Meditation and cycled his qi accordingly through his pathways.

He felt qi enter his body, more than even in the inner sect—he was certain the tree beside him was the cause—and with the additional qi and the movement of it through his extremities, he started feeling significantly better.

He moved through the next form. And the next. Until he had done the full kata.

Then he repeated it all again.

It was getting dark, but the warmth that flowed through him was a boon for his body and his soul.

As the kata ended, he felt almost like he had woken up from a deep slumber. His lower dantian was partially replenished, his body felt much more resilient to the cold, and his brain worked again. He wondered what kind of thoughts had gone through his head while he had been freezing.

With fresh eyes, Jianyi looked at the tree. While the sun had mostly sunk below the horizon, there was still enough light to confirm what his spiritual senses had told him: there was something in the higher reaches of the tree.

But before even thinking about climbing the tree, he again pulled out his spirit needle and tried to connect with it. Theoretically, it was possible to find an affinity card or maybe even a qi card in a natural treasure, though the latter only started at uncommon, and he doubted that the tree was in the Qi Condensation Realm.

Unfortunately, he couldn’t get a connection to the tree’s spirit; its qi might have been focused into whatever was in its crown.

So close to his target, Jianyi didn’t let himself get deterred. He was strong and dexterous, and despite his hurting fingers, climbing the tree was not going to be a problem for him.

He was right, and within minutes, he had made it to the top, where he looked at a large dark brown pine cone that seemed to be the source of a lot of the qi in the area. With slight goosebumps, he reached out reverently and pulled the natural treasure from its branch and quickly into his storage ring.

Quickly, he was back on the ground of the island and looked with some trepidation at the dark pond in front of him.

The feeling of his impromptu cultivation session still vivid in his mind, he started to cycle the qi through his body without the spear movements, and the moment he started to feel the warm sensation coursing through his body, he jumped back into the pond.

It worked well.

The cold of the close-to-freezing water did impact him a lot less. Nonetheless, the moment he was back on the other side, he slipped out of his robes, dried himself off with a piece of cloth, and dressed in a replacement set of robes.

Despite his newly discovered trick of staying warm with the help of his qi and dry clothes, Jianyi was very much looking forward to the campfire and some warm food.

He raced through the woods, not caring too much about his presence and more about speed, and he smiled as he saw Minghao sitting at the brightly burning campfire.

“You are back,” the retainer said with a raised eyebrow.

“Yes! But I did it, Minghao, I defeated a spirit beast!”

“Well done!” he said with a smile. “Now, show me your fingers.”

Jianyi grimaced and sat beside the older man, who somehow had spotted his injury right away.

“It does not look broken; you can use your healing technique, or cycle your qi through it for a couple of hours over the next few days, and it should be fine.”

Jianyi thought about it for a moment. Given that he had already used two techniques and that Verdant Mending took as much qi as the other two combined, he would use up all of his freely available qi to activate the technique.

It seemed better to use Verdant Mending on the following day, should it still be needed, and get some more cultivation done in the meantime to replenish his reserves fully.

Decision made, he settled down and made preparations to grill a deer steak, but Minghao handed him a bowl of tasty stew to celebrate his successful day.

While he ate, Minghao asked him about the fight and what he could have done better. Amongst other things, a better first spear attack was on Jianyi’s mind; another thing was that if he were truly stealthy, he could have surprised the beast and dealt with it swiftly.

“That is true; however, not a simple ask. Against a mortal animal, sure, but it is hard to get really close to a spirit beast on your level unless that is something you specialize in,” Minghao cautioned. “Now that you are done with food, let’s see your finds.”

Jianyi eagerly moved a few paces to the side and dropped the beaver on the ground, then pulled out the pine cone.

“Very good, this was likely a second-stage Qi Gathering Realm spirit beast, and the pine nuts in that cone should be in the third stage, maybe even fourth. That is truly a great success for somebody this early on their cultivation journey.”

Jianyi felt warm inside, and it wasn’t just the campfire.

“What do you think about this card?” he said as he showed Minghao the Vital Vines.

“Another nice find. What were you planning to do with it?”

“I don’t know! I would really like to use it, but if I could create an essence from it, it would be perfect for the second step of my body cultivation. What do you think I should do?”

Minghao relaxed beside the campfire as if he hadn’t heard the question. “What did you say you were going to do with it?”

Jianyi growled.

He really did appreciate that Elder Sun and Minghao didn’t want to influence his path, but sometimes he felt like life would be much simpler if the experienced cultivators were to tell him what to do.

He sat down, thinking it through.

Was the technique good? Definitely! Was it unique? Probably not. Was it realistic that he would find many other Wood-aligned spirit beasts that he could slay and that would get him a card? He sighed.

“I will use it for my cultivation,” he grumbled.

Minghao leaned forward, rubbed his hands, and started on his frankly horrible impersonation of a greedy merchant. “Mhh, interesting, young cultivator, very interesting. I might have a deal to propose to you. I have come into possession of this potion here,” he said as he pulled a vial with a shimmering green potion out of his storage ring. “It is called—succinctly and creatively, I might add—Wood-infused Soul-Forged-Body Elixir for the Body Tempering Realm. For the low cost of a Wood-aligned card and a natural treasure, I would be willing to sell it.”

Jianyi looked at him wide-eyed. Not just because of the horrible acting, but also… Minghao had his potions?!

He had been so worried that he needed to break through to handling three threads of qi while being in the wilderness and doing everything else he had to do.

Also, the price that Minghao asked for was a bargain. He knew that the potion needed more than two ingredients, and that ignored that an essence was worth more than a card.

Given that it would have been his first attempt to convert a card into an essence, it would have had a high chance of failure.

Minghao winked at him and reverted to his normal personality. “Do you want it?”

Jianyi nodded quickly and handed him the card and the pine cone.

“Good. Now go and field dress the beaver; you should be able to get quite a few contribution points for the tail, fur, and teeth—and the meat of a spirit beast should be quite tasty as well.”

Jianyi got started on that right away. Now that he had what he needed for the second stage of the Soul-Forged-Body Path, he just needed to repeat what he had done today for an Earth-aligned creature and hope that Minghao would have that potion as well.
