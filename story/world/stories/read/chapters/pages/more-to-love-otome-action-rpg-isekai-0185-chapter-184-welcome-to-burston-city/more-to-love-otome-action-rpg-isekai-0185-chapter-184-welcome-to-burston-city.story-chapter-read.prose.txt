The trip from Buddington Town to Burston City had been peaceful, all things considered. Dev managed to gather Shon and Myk to escort her and Lily, with Dev taking point on leading the expedition while Arryn drove the golem: a van-sized wagon that looked more like a horseless carriage than any of the other golems Aida had seen thus far.

The journey had been long—it took all of Loam Day, even though the Talonwings had picked the girls and their luggage up early in the morning. There hadn’t been any monster encounters, but environmental damage from the monster outbreak had slowed their journey down significantly, with Arryn having to drive carefully around large potholes and troughs scraped through the road with massive claws.

Shon was weary by the time they finally made it to the City, since he had to grow wooden bridges across some particularly canyon-esque disruptions in the road. Lily had offered to help, but Arryn had firmly said the three of them were to act like the passengers they were, so that the Talonwings could experience what it was like to be wholly responsible for nonpractitioners during an escort mission. Needless to say, the boys had been grumbling they should have forced Pritchard onto the rotation for the better part of the journey.

Lily left their group first. Her employer was an herbalist, and her work location was essentially a jungle. They had nearly gotten lost following the signs that pointed them towards the herbalist’s lodge, and even got trapped in quicksand, requiring Dev to strain his Water Manipulation abilities to his limit as he attempted to save the golem from sinking while Shon used the last of his mana to grow plants that could boost the vehicle out. All they got for their troubles was an approving nod from Arryn.

By the time they dropped Aida off at Relief for Health Clinic, the Talonwings were too exhausted from the trip (Myk wore himself out screaming and cursing) to do more than bid her good night.

Burston City, unlike its name, was not nearly as city-like as Buddington Town. It reminded her of a sprawling suburb, with large homes on large swathes of land and businesses segmented into their own private compounds. Unlike Buddington, there didn’t seem to be a prevalent public transport system either; the primary mode of transport seemed to be bicycles, or even personal golems that resembled the Gullbeaks’ van Arryn was driving.

Fortunately for Aida, she didn’t have to do her own grocery shopping. Relief for Health Clinic had its own compound, although it was smaller than some of the others she had seen: a five-story dormitory building that housed employees who weren’t local to the City, or didn’t have their own travel golems; one two-story building that was open to the public, where half the building contained a treatment clinic for patients and the other half was a pharmacy that sold medicines and potions to anyone; and a third building that was only one floor, though it was still large enough that it had the footprint of a wholesale club.

The dormitory had a cafeteria on the ground floor, where grandmotherly cooks would serve warm, hearty meals. The treatment clinic was fairly exclusive: patients in the clinic were live-in residents, there because they had illnesses that were either unknown or unable to be treated elsewhere. The pharmacy, on the other hand, saw plenty of foot traffic: from citizens of Burston City dropping by to pick up minor medicines and supplements, to traveling Adventurers restocking on potions and antidotes.

The third building was the Clinic’s research center, according to the frazzled employee who had been assigned as Aida’s tour guide when she had entered the clinic/pharmacy. He had helped bring her luggage to her third-floor room (which she was thankful for, since there were no elevators), given her a verbal tour as he pointed at each of the three buildings in the compound through the windows, and then immediately bid her good day before he rushed back to the clinic.

After waiting in her room for an hour, hoping someone would come and formally get her onboarded as a Clinic employee to no avail, Aida had decided to go exploring. Luckily for her, one of the kitchen grandmothers had seen her lost expression and waved her over, giving her a more detailed overview of Relief for Health’s campus as she ate her dinner.

“Healer Bokar Evins? He won’t be here until tomorrow,” Gramma—as she had insisted Aida call her—said. “But when he comes in for breakfast, I’ll let him know you’re here already.”

Since there wasn’t much else for Aida to do, and it seemed like all of the other employees were firmly established in their own cliques, she had turned in early, hoping she would receive more guidance on the morrow.

Sixth Moon, Second Sun Day

Aida woke up mildly disoriented in a sterile white room until she sensed the muted heartbeats of her still-sleeping neighbors. Then she remembered that she was in her new Relief for Health quarters, a tidy little dormitory room furnished with a small bed, desk and chair, and bookshelf.

Wondering why she woke so abruptly, she realized there was someone outside her door—and another gentle knock confirmed that someone was looking for her.

Aida scrambled off of her hard mattress and peeked the door open to see a tall, middle-aged woman smiling down at her, as if it wasn’t the crack of dawn.

“Good morning! Are you Aida Loreh?” Her cheerful voice echoed along the hall.

“Yes, good morning,” Aida mumbled, opening her door just a bit more. “Um, you are…?”

“Pleasure to meet you! My name is Charlotte, and I manage the employees for Relief for Health. Head Healer Bokar asked me to make sure you were situated and then bring you to him. Are you ready, or do you need some time…?”

“Ah… Give me a few minutes, please.” Easing the door shut, Aida quickly changed out of her sleepwear, gave her hair a quick brush, and summoned water to rinse her face and mouth before she stepped out into the hallway.

Seeing that she was slightly more alert, Charlotte smiled and gestured for Aida to follow her down the slowly brightening hallway.

“I’m sorry I wasn’t prepared to greet you yesterday when you arrived,” she said as they walked. “It’s been all hands on deck for the past star cycle, so even operational personnel have been pulled into helping with the Clinic’s research tasks whenever we get any spare time.”

What suspicious timing.

“What happened?”

“The strangest things,” Charlotte sighed. “Inexplicable selective memory loss, lethargy, sometimes outright personality changes.”

The tall woman shook her head before she glanced down at Aida. “Very odd, and it makes a lot of us quite uneasy. What about you? Have you noticed anything like that where you’re from?”

“Now that you mention it…” she gave a quick summary of what she had observed with Agnes to the woman, who nodded thoughtfully as she guided the two of them through a maze of counters and cauldrons in the third building, some stations still staffed by researchers bent over their work.

“Interesting indeed… I’m surprised you haven’t heard of more unusual cases in Buddington, given the population density. I would have expected more occurrences from such a busy town, but the lack of cases is optimistic, I suppose. Anyway, here we are. Pardon me a moment while I try to get Healer Bokar’s attention.”

Charlotte had brought the two of them to a large glass room, within which Aida saw several figures dressed identically in long white robes with a hood, a full-face mask with a beak on it, and gloves. They were all surrounding a figure laying on a bed, though she couldn’t see much more than that.

Honestly, the scene looked quite surreal. Everyone in the glass tank looked like they were straight out of a horror movie.

Charlotte rapped on the glass, drawing the attention of all the plague doctors in the room. One particularly stout figure waved enthusiastically when they saw the two of them standing outside, and bustled their way over to the far end of the tank. Charlotte smiled and tugged Aida’s arm so that they met Healer Bokar at the entrance of the tank, where he was removing his protective gear.

He ran his hands down his face and body, and Aida saw a light purple color emanate from his hands as he quickly Detoxed himself before he opened the glass door.

“Aida, I’m so glad to see you made it safely!” He shook her hand with both of his with great enthusiasm and relief. “I was worried when you didn’t arrive on Shoots Day, hoped all that happened was that you missed the InterTravel golem and not anything worse—but that doesn’t matter now that you’re here!”

Bokar waved for the two of them to follow him into a corner that clearly served as his office: a large desk covered with papers and notebooks, surrounded by chairs for an air of collaboration. “Please, find a seat…”

Charlotte shifted some notebooks off of a chair and offered it to Aida before clearing a chair for herself.

“So, first things first: Welcome to Burston City, and welcome to Relief for Health! We are glad for you to finally join us.” Bokar beamed at Aida before his expression sobered. “Unfortunately—or perhaps fortunately, depending on one’s perspective—you came at a bad—or good—time. We are in the middle of an epidemic of unknown nature, so I may have to put you to work sooner than I had hoped.”
