James woke up to see the sun rising on the horizon. It was a new day in the dungeon—twenty minutes before the next wave. The encampment had progressed quite a bit; the palisade had been repaired, and trenches had been dug before it. Andrew had asked the civilians to dig them, just like a medieval army would.

“James, you’re awake! Just like yesterday, you’ll guard the north gate while we take on the rest,” Andrew said.

“How do you even know where they’ll appear? There might even be a third portal today.”

“In that case, we’ll trust you to deal with one portal, James.”

Unlike James, the rest of the mercenaries and Andrew had bags under their eyes from pulling an all-nighter repairing the encampment. Now that James was refreshed, he wondered why Andrew hadn’t just slept and let the civilians do the work. Civilians should handle construction, while survivors should handle combat. That’s how strategy games usually worked, after all.

As the timer reached zero, the dormant portal in the distance lit up again. The third wave brought imps alongside goblins—hundreds of them.

“Shit, there are tons of them.”

“Don’t worry, we have Sanders’ magic and Dehlia’s grenades. James, are you okay handling that side?”

“Leave it to me. I’ll meet them head-on.” James dashed toward the horde on the north side of the encampment. Defending from inside the palisade wasn’t his style; it restricted too many of his weapon skills.

He charged toward the portal, cleaving through monsters left and right. He didn’t even need to activate his sword skills. As more and more monsters were slain, fliers began spawning from the gate—twelve giant eagles, each over four meters wide and two meters tall, zipped out of the portal. They homed in on the settlement, intending to feast on the flesh of helpless civilians inside. Of course, James didn’t let that happen. Even as numerous monsters swarmed him, he still had his magic.

A purple ball of mind magic shot out from his back, aimed and launched by his Parallel Thought skill. The purple bullets were fast and precise, striking the soaring eagles in the sky. Intense pain assaulted the creatures—it felt as if stakes had been driven into their eyes. The agony spread through their entire nervous system, like needles sewing through their tendons.

The birds crashed to the ground as their bodies tensed up from the overwhelming pain caused by James’s magic. Some broke their necks and died instantly, while those that survived were skewered by ice arrows.

Two hours later, over three thousand monsters lay dead. They were mindless monsters, charging through a fortified position. The group cleaned up the remnant by selling them in the shop.

Please prepare for the next wave.]

James was about to pick the wings for his extra slot but hesitated. Something felt wrong.

“System, why is there no gliding or flying skill attached to the wings?”

[These wings have no magical enchantment. Normal wings are a hindrance to humans; they provide no lift and offer only minimal gliding or buoyancy.]

“So they’re completely useless? But I swear I’ve seen wings that could glide before—maybe not fly, but at least glide!”

[Yes, they are useless. Wings from humanoid creatures such as Birdmen and Fallen Angels possess enchantments that enable flight. When a Chimera takes those wings, the enchantments carry over—albeit at a reduced rate—allowing survivors to glide with them.]

James pouted at the news and left the wings and the rest of the corpses to be sold by the mercenaries. He called for his raptor and returned to the settlement, where Andrew and the mercs were shouting at each other.

“You’ve conquered a dungeon, right? Then why were your men fleeing? You should’ve fought the eagles that raided the camp! Because of you, three civilians and one mercenary are dead!”

“We did fight them! We even took casualties on our side. They were tough—our spears and weapons barely did anything.”

“You should’ve used a weapon skill! Why were you using normal attacks? That’s one of the most basic rules of dungeon raiding!” Andrew groaned.

James blinked at Andrew’s sudden aggressiveness. It was unusual—he hadn’t been this hostile in the other medium dungeon—but James shrugged it off, assuming it was because of the deaths. He sighed at how caring Andrew was, even though those mercenaries were nothing more than a burden.

“Tch! Acting all high and mighty just because you’ve conquered medium dungeons! We’re the ones who suffered casualties because you let those birds through! This encampment was your idea—you think you’re some kind of hotshot, don’t you?”

“...Hah… I’m sorry. Let’s just get some rest. We’re all tired; we haven’t slept for a day. It’s probably clouding our judgment,” Andrew said. He then turned to the civilians. “Please fix the palisade. James, you came at a good time—we could use your water magic. We’re thirsty.”

“Yes, yes.”

“How was your sleep? We could also use more logs. Now that we have some rope, you can use it.”

“Alright. Get a good night’s sleep—you look awful. It’s not like you to yell at people,” James said.

“Yeah… I think the pressure’s getting to me. Thank you, James. Please bring as many logs as you can,” Andrew said before huddling in a corner and falling asleep like a log within minutes.

Using his raptor, James brought back forty logs in two hours, which the civilians used to build a small barrack. Hunger began to set in—they hadn’t eaten in more than a day. Luckily, there was a huge chicken (the eagle they had beaten earlier), three meters wide, that Andrew had killed earlier. James ordered the civilians to prepare the bird before heading back into the forest to gather more logs.

“Leave one for me, alright?”

When James returned a few hours later with the logs, he found the camp partying—people were skewering the bird’s meat on sticks and roasting them over a bonfire.

“You ate without me?”

“James, welcome! Don’t worry, we saved several for you,” Andrew said, pointing to a pile of wooden plates filled with roasted chicken meat. “There’s still plenty of that bird left.”

“Hah…” James sighed. He unloaded the logs near one side of the camp and ate his fill.

“Sorry for my outburst earlier. I guess I was just tired,” Andrew apologized to Xiaoming and Bernard by the campfire.

“We apologize as well—that was pathetic. There was only one of those birds, and it still took one of our men. If it hadn’t focused on the civilians, we would’ve had even more casualties,” Bernard and Xiaoming said. “I guess you’re right. We need quality as well as quantity.”

After such an argument earlier, it surprised James that they were able to get over it in less than a day. James didn’t have such thick skin and kept some distance from them.

As he ate the roasted chicken while glancing at the timer and the glowing portal on the horizon, Xiaoming and Bernard turned to Andrew.

“That’s James, huh? Isn’t he a bit too powerful for a survivor? He handled an entire portal by himself,” Bernard asked. “What’s he like? I didn’t get a good feeling from him. He seemed… uncooperative, didn’t he?”

“Is it because of his unique skill? The system’s unfair, isn’t it—giving such powerful skills to only a few people?”

“James’ skill is certainly powerful, but it’s not as omnipotent as you think,” Andrew said. “I have a friend—Hain. His skill was similar, and possibly even better. He could augment his body using parts bought from the shop, but in the end, James still stood ahead of him. He has the wit and tenacity we didn’t. Since he’s always alone, he developed a danger sense we never had. That’s one perk of working solo, I guess.”

Andrew nodded to himself. He was a team player—he believed people should work together, that by specializing in different areas, they could complement one another. But ultimately, he had failed in that medium dungeon. Now he understood the kind of strength James believed in—and respected him all the more for it.

“Is that so? Did he have that skill? I thought survivors only had—”

“Of course! Boss James has been raiding and conquering dungeons solo! He’s saved our lives more than once.”

“Really? But his attitude… Even now, he chose to sit alone at the gate like that.” Xiaoming frowned. He believed attitude should prevail over strength. Andrew agreed, but James was an exception. It wasn’t that James was malicious—he simply preferred to be a lone wolf.

“That’s how it is with the world. If you want to succeed, get good. People don’t care whether or not you’re nice, as long as you bring results,” Andrew said.

As they talked, the timer hit zero. This time, the wave consisted of flyers. Humongous hawks, crows, and eagles spawned from the portal. They flapped their wings and dove toward the settlement—over fifty of them.

James stood up, activating Magic Parallel Thought and Mind Flay at the soon-to-be fried chicken. He stabbed his sword into the ground and drew his bow. Using his skill, [Homing Shot], he began picking off the birds one by one as ice spears shot toward the sky.

A golden meteor—one not from James’s skill—whooshed through the air, taking down a bird with it. A spear had lodged in the creature’s chest. When James turned around, he saw Andrew with an outstretched hand. He had thrown the spear with his skill.

“Similar to a boomerang but without the ability to turn back, huh,” James muttered as he continued shooting down the birds.

With that, the fourth wave on the second day was over.

[Congratulations. You have cleared the fourth wave. 0 deaths. The next wave begins in 12 hours. You and the rest of the survivors have obtained 500 coins. New area unlocked: Orchard.]

James now knew where to go. It was time to get a balanced diet of vegetables and fruit.
