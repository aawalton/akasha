The Ideograms term exam was shockingly difficult.

It wasn’t that Eola wasn’t capable of replicating the dozen First Order runes on the first set of folded parchments. She was. She even wrote up descriptions of half of them, from core rune to modifying marks.

It was the Second Order spells that tripped her up.

This time, they weren’t optional.

Light Lance came first. She’d had time to work on that one since the mid-term exam, and she was ready for it. But after it came more ideograms, with more complex language. Mixes of Emerald Cursive, Old Alemic, and Forsooth Script, but written in different patterns. It was one thing to grade other students on their work, and a totally different thing to work through the Second Order spells without her spellbook or other students’ work as references.

Still, she’d managed to solve three of the six when Instructor Tarik cleared his throat. “Time. Please bring your exams forward.”

Eola stared at her half-remembered Shield Wall ideogram. The rune sat in a place somewhere between Safe Shield and the siege magic Headmaster Tomlish had cast before her duel, and she’d only seen the actual spell being sketched once. She quickly moved her pen across the rune, adding one more part, then stopped. Her brow furrowed, and she scratched it out violently enough to tear a hole in the paper.

Then she marched to the front of the room and handed in her somewhat worse-for-wear exam.

“Frustrated, toddler?” Catrine asked.

Eola did her best to ignore her, but her ears still burned.

“If you can’t even remember Second Order spells by the end of the year, what good was that independent study?”

Eola opened her mouth, but before she could, Instructor Tarik intervened. “That’s enough, Miss Andrese.”

“Lady Andrese, please.”

“No, but thank you. I actually had an alternative exam prepared for Miss Lemiene. It was my mistake to give her an exam with the final two pages.”

“What? That sounds completely made-up!” Catrine’s eyes shifted to Eola’s suspiciously, and she shrugged. Truthfully, she had no idea what Tarik was talking about.

“It most certainly isn’t. Part of Miss Lemiene’s independent study with me included an exceptionally difficult ideogram, which she was able to solve more completely than any student I’ve had. Her independent study and apprenticeship have focused on the skills she still needs to work on, unlike yours with Madame Reyanna. Yes, I know exactly how your work with her has gone, and it would have been more beneficial for you to have focused on Introduction to Monsters. Now, turn in your exam and move along, Miss Andrese.”

Catrine’s mouth dropped open. She froze, then slammed her parchment onto the classroom’s desk and turned, storming off in a huff. The tension didn’t leave Eola’s shoulders until the door clicked shut.

And Eola found herself alone with Instructor Tarik.

Questions boiled up before she could stop them. “Why haven’t you been here for the last week? Were they really supposed to know those Second Order runes?”

“Ideograms. They’re called ideograms, Miss Lemiene. And yes, your fellow students were supposed to know at least some of them. You, however, are not. Your work with the Worldshaper was more than enough. And as for your first question, I was…otherwise occupied.”

“That’s not a very good answer, sir,” Eola said.

“Nevertheless, it’s one you’ll need to be satisfied with.” The professor paused. One hand reached up to touch his beard, then stopped. “I am sorry that Lady Andrese seems determined to make your life difficult, Miss Lemiene. There’s only so much I can do.”

“It’s fine. I’m managing her.”

“Yes, I suppose you are,” Instructor Tarik said, suppressing a laugh. “Now, you have other exams. Hurry, or you’ll be late, and Madame Reyanna is less forgiving than I am.”

Eola nodded and did as instructed, sliding into her seat in the diminutive professor’s room with less than a minute to spare.

The exam was all but identical to the midterm. Her classmates each cast Heavy Haul as she watched and scribbled in her notebook. The half-finished modifying mark had her full attention; its structure looked almost tantalizingly familiar, but she couldn’t place where she’d seen it. A spell sketch, of course, but which one?

She couldn’t figure it out, even in the twenty minutes the Heavy Haul test took. Even when Garreth Skagget finally gave up and the others tore into the offered treats to help refill their Mana, she only kept scribbling in her notebook. The chocolate squares from before were a distant memory, and one that didn’t tempt her.

Much.

She only took three.

Then the test of bars began.

Colin reached six this time, and Catrine made it to eight. She shot Eola a smug look. Eola ignored her. Of course, she’d be catching up; she had an independent study built around Mana Studies and taught by Madame Reyanna herself. Even so, growing from six to eight in a few months was impressive.

Then Garreth Skaget, Catrine’s teammate and fellow noble, walked to the bars. He pushed Mana into them, and they quickly filled, one after another. Seven, eight, and nine. The class went quiet as he started on his tenth, then fell back, breathing heavily and sweating from his brow. “That’s it,” he gasped.

“Nine. Nine, as a Journeymage. Impressive. You and Lady Andrese are almost ready to begin the trials for Truemage, and at the end of your first year. Congratulations.”

The boy named Olethe—Eola had forgotten his first name—reached nine as well. That wasn’t a surprise; he’d done well at the midterms, too. What was a surprise was just how far ahead the noble families’ sons and daughters were, though. While Colin was at six, and a handful of other students had reached seven, the few nobles all seemed to be at eight and above.

There had to be a reason—something Madame Reyanna either wasn’t seeing or didn’t care to see.

“Miss Lemiene, begin.”

Eola snapped her notebook shut and shoved it into her pocket, then walked to the bars and cast Mana Mark.

Just as before, the world went dark except for the bars and the spiral of her Mana. She began filling them instantly, not wasting time. The thin strand grew in size by the second, filling the first and second bars in a single push before flooding into the next half dozen, almost without stopping.

Only at the end of the eighth did Eola’s stomach start to protest. It lit up a moment later. Then the ninth. The ninth had been her limit just before Y’aer’s Day.

The tenth.

But the eleventh was twice as hard as the tenth, and Eola’s soul was empty after she finished. She tried anyway, but when she was only a quarter done with the twelfth, the bars drained, one after another, and she dropped to a knee.

“Full bars only, Miss Lemiene. Eleven. Acceptable growth.”

“Acceptable?” Colin asked.

“Mr. Tremory, control your outbursts. This exam differs from the previous one in that it measures growth, not capacity. The initial test offers a baseline to measure how hard you’ve worked and how much your soul has grown. Miss Lemiene has achieved acceptable growth, as have most of you.”

“And the fact that she’s already approaching Archmage levels of Mana means nothing? Most mages slow down as their souls grow. You said it yourself, three lectures ago. Eola isn’t. Don’t you think that’s—”

“Mr. Tremory,” Madame Reyanna said, her voice sharp, “if you cannot control yourself, you will be asked to leave. Miss Lemiene’s level of Mana is irrelevant. She has not passed the Journeymage trial, and is therefore still—in terms of her skill with magic, at least—a child.”

As Colin sat down and Eola walked to the snack table for more chocolate squares, she glanced back at Madame Reyanna—and at Catrine. She expected to see anger, annoyance, or anything at all. Instead, both the professor and the noble girl wore carefully controlled, expressionless masks.

Still, scoring eleven on the bars test was pretty good.

Fortunately for Eola, the Introduction to Monsters exam was the exact same eight pages Instructor Clearance had given at midterms. Eola remembered the ‘2’ she’d gotten on that exam. She also remembered a good portion of her answers, and spent extra time making sure she fixed the ones she was pretty sure she’d gotten wrong. That, plus the benefit of a half-term of focused instruction, was enough. Probably.

She wasn’t the first student finished, but she wasn’t the last, either, and she ended up waiting outside for Colin to wrap up his own test.

Then it was time for Magical Dueling.

“Lemiene, Irisbreeze, Skaget, and Ferrin,” Maestro Yarrowbloom said, and Eola tensed. Last time, the elf had been a tough opponent for her. Not badly matched at all, but he was a natural with the sword. Garreth Skaget was one of Catrine’s friends—and the only one in her group who was probably a true friend to her instead of a coattail-riding lackey. He’d probably be a tough fight. He was one of the most generally talented students in the first year.

And Ferrin…Eola didn’t know anything about the mousy-looking woman with the simple, messily-cut bangs. An unknown.

At least she didn’t have Wenderly. She’d lost against that girl, and lost handily.

“Your groups have been assigned based on your performance during the previous exam. If you wish to improve your score, simply do well in these bouts,” Maestro Yarrowbloom said.

This time, Eola’s group fought fifth. Colin’s fights were fast, furious, and ended in two losses and—to everyone’s surprise—an actual, honest-to-Y’aer draw. Not even Maestro Yarrowbloom could tell who’d made contact first.

Patrice, however, was a force of nature. She flattened the small, yellow girl she was assigned against first, then tore through two boys without a problem, all without using a single bit of proper Taron-Li form. Eola was pretty sure the redhead had bent her practice sword on one of their helmets.

Then it was Group Five’s turn on the pitch.

Just like her first fight with Irisbreeze, Eola found herself with a sword tip jammed against her sternum for his fifth point an instant before she could hit him for her fourth. This time, however, the bout lasted almost forty-five seconds longer. Eola had been practicing, after all, and only First Order magic had given Irisbreeze the victory. If she ran into Eddarn during the library exam—or any time next year—she wouldn’t lose because of her swordplay.

She shook hands with Irisbreeze as Skaget and Ferrin took the pitch. Skaget got the first four points in a flurry of blows, but then something shifted in the mousy girl’s posture, and less than fifteen seconds later, the bout was over. Lord Garreth Skaget pulled his helmet off, beaming. “That was amazing!”

“Thank you, m’lord,” the girl said, curtsying without taking her own practice mask off. “You fought brilliantly, too.”

Eola fought Garreth Skaget next. The noble man outweighed and out-reached her, and she found herself backed along the pitch even as she landed blows. With the score three to one, she finally ran out of room to retreat. Her eyes locked on his practice mask, and she knew exactly what he was thinking: with nowhere to run, it’d just be the two of them hammering at each other.

Eola used her Held Chromite Cloth. Skaget’s first blow bounced off without counting, and she grinned, then retaliated.

A moment later, it was over; the moment he lost the initiative, Skaget lost track of the fighting. “Good job,” the boy said.

Eola nodded and sat down, pouring water into her mouth. It wasn’t a snack, but she still had the hardest bout to go. Food would only slow her down.

Besides, there wasn’t any here.

Garreth beat Irisbreeze four to five, and Ferrin handled the elven boy; Maestro Yarrowbloom had balanced the group amazingly evenly. Eola watched, trying to figure out what the girl was doing. Every fight, she’d fall behind, then wrench victory from the jaws of defeat. It was almost like she was two different fighters.

Or…

Maybe…

Eola took the pitch, pulling her helmet on and nodding to Ferrin. The young woman nodded back. The fight started…

And Eola did nothing.

She didn’t aggress. She didn’t take a step back for distance. She just stood there, watching Ferrin. The other girl’s sword tapped hers lightly. She ignored it. All of her attention was on—

There!

While her main hand—and her blade—stayed between her and Eola, Ferrin was casting. And she wasn’t just casting a simple bit of Child’s Magic. Nor was it a First Order spell. She was casting a Second Order spell in a spellsword’s duel, the maniac!

But it had clearly worked for her. If she finished that spell, Ferrin would have a huge advantage, one Eola couldn’t overcome.

Eola only had one option. She attacked—but instead of aiming for body hits, her practice sword went for the other girl’s casting hand.

It took only one bit of disruption for the Second Order spell—it was Stilled and Silenced, so Eola couldn’t tell what it had been—to fall apart. Thirty seconds later, it was over, and a sweaty, gasping Eola reached a gloved hand down and helped her opponent up.

Ferrin leaned in until their masks touched and her lips were only inches from Eola’s ear. “You saw it, huh? That makes you the first. Don’t tell anyone—I’m worthless with a sword and no magic, but I really want to be a spellsword. That spell’s been covering for me.”

“Your secret’s safe with me if you’ll share the rune you used,” Eola whispered back. “That’s a clever trick you’ve got. It almost got me!”

“Gladly. Dualiste Principalis, by Renault. Floor Two, Row Twenty-Three, shelf—“

“Fourteen, right?” Eola asked. She’d been there before, after all.

“Yes. Dueling guides. It’s in that book, but you’ll have to translate the text itself from Old Alemic,” Ferrin said as she separated from Eola. “I’m Jeanette.”

“Eola.”

“Nice to meet you. Be seeing you in the library tomorrow?”

“You might, yes,” Eola said.

Then she left the dueling pitch and stripped off her practice armor. The sooner she was back in her room, the sooner she could clean up and take one last, desperate attempt at understanding the mysterious, half-remembered modifying mark.

Dualiste Principalis would have to wait.

For now.

When Eola returned to their suite, Colin was already there. He sat on the couch, a letter on his lap; water ran in the washroom, and a slow melody drifted from Roth’s open door. “I’m last, then?”

“You are. Letter for us all.” Colin handed the envelope over, and Eola pulled the folded paper out and skimmed it.

Prospective Combat-Track Students,

The Library Exam will take place at ten on Last-Day morning. All students interested must report to the library’s first floor thirty minutes prior. Any teammates must also be in attendance so escorts can be assigned.

Bring all desired weapons, armor, trinkets, and other equipment with you. Once your team passes the gate to the third floor, leaving without completing the exam will result in disqualification.

All students attempting the Library Exam acknowledge that, by entering the library’s third floor, they accept the risk of injury or death.

Headmaster Tomlish

“Well, that’s short,” Eola muttered. When she’d read the first line, she’d thought it might include some sort of information about the test itself, but Madame Reyanna and Instructor Tarik had both already given them the same information. The only thing Eola hadn’t known was the exam’s start time.

“The professors must really be worried about this, huh?” Colin asked as the water shut off in the washroom and Patrice’s terrible singing voice started echoing from it.

Eola read through the letter two more times, then tucked it back into its envelope. Roth emerged during her second read, glared at the washroom door, and awkwardly read over her shoulder.

“It can’t be that bad, right?” Colin asked. “I mean, if students died every year, that’d get Varin’s in a heap of trouble. Parents would be upset about it…right?”

Roth rolled his eyes. “Dad wouldn’t.”

“My parents would, but they know the risks, and it’s worth it to them. Besides, I’ve got an older brother already.” Patrice opened the door. Her hair was wrapped in a towel, but she’d gotten dressed. She took one look at the letter in Eola’s hand and rolled her eyes. “Already read it.”

“Were those supposed to be hymns?” Roth asked.

“They weren’t supposed to be hymns, they were hymns.”

“No. No, they couldn’t have been.”

Patrice drew herself up. “Y’aer doesn’t care what hymns in their name sound like, and neither do I. That letter—and all the professors’ warnings—are just to make us overthink and panic.”

Eola shook her head slowly. “Mmmm. I don’t know. I’ve almost died three times in that library, and I’ve only been to the third floor once. Maybe they’re actually serious.”

“Our parents would never—“

Patrice touched Colin’s shoulder, and he stopped talking. “I don’t know anything about your parents, but mine absolutely would let it happen. Anything to further House Clerk’s position,” she said bitterly. “Besides, I’m broken, remember?”

Roth rolled his eyes. “You can always back out if you’re scared, Colin. No shame in that.” The way he said it made it clear that he, at least, thought there was plenty of shame in it.

“No one’s backing out,” Eola said before Colin could consider his options. “We’re going to succeed, and we’re going to do it together.”

She sighed and glanced longingly at her door. All her work was inside, and she was close to a breakthrough. Maybe even as little as an hour more, and she’d have it. Then she looked Colin in the eyes. “Let’s get some dinner and talk over what equipment we want to bring so we’re ready for tomorrow.”
