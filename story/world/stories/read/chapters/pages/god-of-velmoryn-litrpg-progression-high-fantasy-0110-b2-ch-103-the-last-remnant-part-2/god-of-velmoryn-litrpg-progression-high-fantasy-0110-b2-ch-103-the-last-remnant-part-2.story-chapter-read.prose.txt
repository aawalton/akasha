[Six Shards of the One Whole ]

These five shards were once fragments of a single whole. Their true function will remain hidden until all six shards are gathered.

This was disappointing.

After raising my Rank, Guidance had mostly started giving me direct answers and stopped offering vague explanations. I had gotten so used to it that I had almost forgotten how it used to torment me with hints and roundabout answers that barely explained anything.

This time, however, the vague answer might have been enough.

I can clearly sense the Goddess’s divine energy from those stones now. I probably missed it before because there is so little energy in each one that sensing them separately is almost impossible. But now I know. There is no doubt these stones were left behind by the Goddess.

I could not know whether gathering all six would transform them into a memory stone or something else entirely, but if these gemstones had truly been left behind by the Goddess, then they were worth collecting.

I looked back at the Window, watching the silvery-cyan hue shimmer faintly above Othrien’s head. I even opened his Status Window, wondering if the crown had granted him some unique power or status.

Nothing had changed.

If it does not affect the wearer, maybe it really is meant for me. Or perhaps its true purpose will only be revealed once all six stones are brought together.

In the end, I decided not to waste time speculating. Once I gathered all six, the truth would reveal itself anyway. I already had too many things to worry about without adding another mystery to the pile.

The velmoryns cheered for Othrien’s crowning, though their voices were not filled with joy. Well, not entirely at the very least.

From the corners of their eyes, many were still watching Nelya and Freya, who now stood with their heads lowered after being released.

Dariel was no longer beside Freya. He was near Tekla instead, the two separated from the rest of the velmoryns.

“Thank you, Priestess,” Dariel said awkwardly, staring at the tips of his boots.

He was still holding the rope that had bound Freya only a few minutes earlier. Not all of it, only the short piece left in his hand after he cut her free. His thumbs kept pressing at the rough fibers.

“Do not forget the vow you made,” Tekla said stiffly. “Family ties can be stronger than anything else, but I still trust you. If Freya… if your wife dares to sow chaos in Eriavel again, I trust that you will take the responsibility.”

Dariel clenched his jaw.

His fingers stopped moving against the rope. He let the piece fall and finally raised his head.

“I will,” he whispered.

Tekla nodded and walked past him, gently touching the giant velmoryn’s shoulder as she went.

“Lord, I hope my judgment was right. I hope the mercy we showed today brings light into the hearts of those who can see nothing but darkness.”

Her expression hardened.

“Otherwise… I will execute them all myself.”

She strode toward the temple, leaving the gathered velmoryns behind.

As she walked, Lucas’s figure suddenly blurred into existence beside her.

Tekla flinched, but otherwise did not react. By now, she had grown accustomed to his abrupt appearances.

“I sent the Crimson Root as you ordered,” Lucas said, matching her pace.

“Make sure someone is always watching them…” Tekla paused, pressing her lips together.

Then she sighed.

“Leave Freya to Dariel. I promised him my trust. If I break my word now, we might lose his loyalty. It is better to trust and regret it than to doubt someone and regret that instead.”

She hesitated.

“I think…”

Tekla trailed off.

She missed the shadow of dissatisfaction that crossed Lucas’s face, though he chose not to say anything.

They continued in silence until they reached the threshold of the temple.

“Lucas,” Tekla said at last. “Tell my fa…”

She stopped herself.

“Tell Mirion to come find me.”

“Any message I should pass along?”

“No.”

Tekla watched Lucas’s shadow blur away as he moved with incredible speed toward the center of Eriavel.

She remained beneath the dark columns of the temple entrance, her gaze shifting toward the Crimson Guardian. For a long moment, she stared at the enormous tree, her eyes fixed on its distant crown.

“We must prepare for the war to come. It is my duty to lead the velmoryns while Father is recovering.”

She exhaled with the thought, turned around, and entered the temple.

A few weeks passed uneventfully.

I kept a constant watch on Pintre, hoping it would meet its new friend again. But the woman was nowhere to be seen, despite Pintre doing everything it could to find her.

Eriavel was also busy preparing for the war against the Night God.

After the last battle, after witnessing the descent of an actual god, the velmoryns had become far more serious about what was coming. Before, they had no real idea what kind of enemy they would face. Even if they had never exactly underestimated the humans, learning that they might have to fight deities had changed their attitude completely.

The mages only rested when their mana was completely depleted. Quite literally, they trained until they lost consciousness.

With Aria back in Eriavel, they could finally fight without holding back. Aria herself was also testing the limits of her new power. She had been a formidable opponent even before I blessed her, but back then, she could only properly handle one strong enemy at a time.

Now she could fend off several mages attacking her simultaneously, and her mere presence became an impenetrable barrier for any velmoryn below Gold Rank.

She had even created an entirely new spell that only she could use.

Dariel and Mirion were training the warriors to death.

With Vaelari mass-producing minor healing potions, the poor warriors were forced to get beaten, cut, and crushed over and over again.

“As long as you don’t lose a limb or die, it’s all good,” Mirion would often say while swinging his enormous axe.

He had even forced everyone to train with real weapons. According to him, they needed to become accustomed to their actual weight. Wooden swords, axes, and spears were far lighter than weapons forged from steel.

That naturally meant more wounds, but it also exhausted the velmoryns much faster.

The archers were no different. Their routine had not changed as drastically, though they now focused more heavily on moving targets and channeling mana into their arrows.

I was not without work either.

Constantly monitoring Pintre and Eriavel was already pushing the Window to its limits, but I was also watching the rest of the forest, keeping track of the dam’s construction, and gathering my most powerful subordinates closer to Eriavel.

Unfortunately, Ninali was still inside the dungeon.

But I could no longer justify leaving Karla there to guard the entrance. Instead, I decided to send an ordinary velmoryn in her place. If Ninali emerged wounded, whoever was stationed there could simply pray to me, and I would intervene.

There was no reason to waste one of my strongest believers on guard duty.

The problem was that I could not communicate with Karla directly and order her back to Eriavel. I still needed everyone to believe I was in slumber.

So I contacted Avenor instead.

A short message written across one of Orrvyn’s roots was enough to pass along the order, and I did not have to worry about anyone probing Avenor’s thoughts either.

I even contacted my basilisk and ordered it to remain near Eriavel.

It was mostly hunting with the smaller, newborn basilisk.

The little one was green, and although my markings covered its body as well, their crimson color was so faint that they looked closer to pink than red.

Its size and shape were also far less imposing.

I assumed it had inherited only a small portion of its parent’s strength, which was very reasonable. If the basilisk I had created could reproduce and give birth to creatures just as powerful as itself, they would become an absurdly dangerous species within only a few generations.

But that was not all I had been doing while waiting for Noah to make his move.

Little by little, I had been seeping my divinity into the mortal world.

It was horribly inefficient.

Almost all of the power I sent into the forest was lost before I could store it there, but it was the only method that guaranteed I would still be able to intervene in the battle if necessary.

Well, there was another way.

I could accept whatever punishment followed and directly influence the mortal world when the time came.

But what if Noah had some way to sever my connection at the crucial moment?

This pitiful amount of divinity I could hide within the mortal world without triggering a punishment or exposing my presence would become my final card if everything else failed.

The Pantheon will probably punish me for using it anyway. But whatever happens afterward, at least I’ll know that no one can completely prevent me from using my power when I need it.

My only other ace was one I was still uncertain about using.

Amon.

I had ordered him to remain quiet while I pretended to be asleep. But once the war reached my forest, once Noah finally made his move, I would use everything I had to defeat him.

A few more weeks…
