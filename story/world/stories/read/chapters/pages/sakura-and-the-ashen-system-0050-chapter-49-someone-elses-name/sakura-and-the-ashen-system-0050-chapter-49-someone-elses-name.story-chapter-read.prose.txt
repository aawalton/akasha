The prison wagon moved unevenly. The wheels kept dropping into old ruts, then climbing onto stone with a dull creak, and the iron walls trembled from every impact. Along with them, the magic-suppressing bracelets on Gilbert’s wrists trembled too. The metalwork, if he was being honest, was not bad. The young runic mage from the administration apparently really knew his work. That was a little irritating.

The plan, seen from the outside, had gone indecently smoothly. Kelvin had taken the bait, burning with the desire to prove he was smarter than everyone around him. Harn had received a dangerous prisoner, the Greymost administration had exposed a magical underground that had never existed in the city, and the Castle of Justice was now receiving a supposed participant in a rebellion. All of that had been done by only two people: Gilbert and Sakura. The rest had required only a few weak scrolls, smoke, shadows, an inscription on the wall that had required slaughtering a perfectly decent pig, and one frightened guard. Everything else had been filled in by fear inside the city administration all on its own.

Gilbert sat on the narrow wooden bench, leaning his shoulder against the wall of the wagon. His hands rested on his knees. The bracelets suppressed the magic in his body, as they had before, but the good news was that this was temporary. In the prison, they would remove them as unnecessary. The core under his skin pulsed dully and wrongly, like a second heartbeat that did not match his own. Sometimes it made him nauseous, and his chest grew tight. But for now, it was bearable.

Panicking was pointless, and thinking about escape was too early. That was not why he had allowed himself to be caught at the old dyehouse. Eight people rode outside. Six on horseback, one on the driver’s bench, and one by the rear door. Four had crossbows, but only two held them with confidence. The keys were not with the ones sitting closest. The leader, with a soldier’s voice and short commands, rode to the right of the wagon. Gilbert immediately estimated that this one would not be easy to throw off balance.

The wagon was slowing for the fifth time in the past hour. Twice, they had stopped on an incline, once at an outpost, and once on a bridge. Now the road became more even again, and the air filled with the smell of water, wet stone, and something mineral. Most likely, the mists.

One of the guards outside spat.

"They say he dropped three before they threw the net over him."

"Not three," another answered. "Two. And he didn’t drop them. They’re both alive."

Someone gave a short laugh.

When the wagon stopped the next time, the guards spoke more quietly. Cold seeped through the gap by the door.

"From Greymost," the leader outside said. "Order of Commander Harn. Dangerous mage. Delivered alive."

"Documents."

Paper rustled.

"Suspected of organizing a magical underground. Illegal runic circuit, attack on the guard, attempted recruitment of an administration employee."

"Resistance during arrest?"

"Yes."

"Losses?"

"None. Two wounded."

"Accepted. Inner courtyard."

Gilbert exhaled almost imperceptibly. His legend was alive. Now it did not need to be maintained every second. People would start filling in the missing pieces themselves.

The wagon did not move right away. First, someone approached the door again from outside. The lock of the inspection hatch clicked, and cold light struck through the narrow slit.

"Prisoner inside. One. Conscious," someone said.

"Bracelets?"

"In place."

"Greymost seal?"

Someone checked the outer fastening on the door.

"Intact."

The hatch closed. Only after that did noise rise outside. Someone gave a short command, and something heavy was stretched across the roof of the wagon. A tarp fell over the top with a dull slap, then they began tightening it along the sides, covering the gaps and seams. One of the guards cursed when a metal clamp pinched his finger.

"Faster. The gray mist is moving today."

"I can see that."

"Then don’t stand there without a mask."

Gilbert sat motionless, listening as they fastened straps and checked the mounts outside. Cold still seeped through the gap by the door, but with every new layer of fabric pulled over the wagon, the air inside grew heavier and drier. Gilbert knew that prisoners were not given protective suits in this place. They were simply locked inside, and the guards made sure the mist did not find its way to them. The tarps stretched over the wagons were special, made from the same material as the protective suits worn by the guards. That was why it worked. Most of the time.

Then the wheels jerked again. The wagon moved slowly. Outside, no one spoke loudly anymore. Gilbert could not see the mist, but he still felt it. The air around them seemed to grow colder, and its foul aftertaste became stronger.

They did not enter the Castle of Justice immediately. Gilbert could not see it, but he knew the layout of the place roughly. First came the outer gates, then a courtyard, where the prison wagon was stopped again. The wheels creaked over stone and went still. Someone checked the locks outside again. Then a stranger’s calm voice sounded near the wagon:

"Open it."

The door swung open, and light struck his eyes. Gilbert blinked, but did not turn away. In front of him stood three Castle workers in gray uniforms without extra insignia. One held a folder of documents, the second did not stand out at all, and the third was different only because thin runic marks were embroidered in lines across his gloves.

He was the one staring at Gilbert too intently.

"Gilven Neld," he said.

One of the Greymost guards behind him let out a noisy breath. Gilbert did not turn his head.

"Human," the worker added. "External verification passed."

Gilbert smiled inwardly, receiving confirmation. To them, he was Gilven Neld. The name sounded unnatural even in someone else’s voice, but that was enough. The verification had not exposed Gilbert. It had once again accepted the role created by the foreign core stitched into him. Here, the plan was working exactly as it should.

"Inside," said the worker with the folder. "To intake."

The leader of the escort nodded to his people. Gilbert was taken out of the wagon. Because of the core, the mage’s legs obeyed worse than he would have liked. But no one paid attention to that, the way people rarely paid attention to a criminal being in bad condition after an arrest. More precisely, after a battle with lawkeepers during an arrest.

He saw the first inner arch at once. It had been built into the wall without any beauty. It was a wide stone opening covered in thin lines, runes that did not glow brightly.

"Remove the bracelets," the Castle worker said.

The Greymost guard frowned.

"He’s dangerous."

"Everyone here is dangerous. Remove the bracelets."

"Commander Harn ordered him transferred under suppression."

The Castle worker looked at him with faint irritation for the first time.

"Commander Harn commands in Greymost. Not here."

The argument ended at once, and after that, the key turned in the lock. The magic-suppressing bracelets were removed from Gilbert. The metal came away from his skin, leaving red marks on his wrists.

They led him under the arch. The magic inside Gilbert did not disappear, but it seemed to grow heavy. Gilbert cautiously reached toward the inner movement of power, not gathering a spell, but rather checking whether it could be gathered at all. Usually, magic responded at once, like breathing or moving a finger. Now it lay somewhere deep, viscous and still. To raise it, he would have had to pull through wet cloth, through stone, through dense suffocation. His Inventory did not respond at all.

Gilbert inhaled slowly and made sure that he was truly inside, and that everything here worked exactly the way he and Sakura had discussed. At least as far as magic suppression and Inventory blocking were concerned.

The primary processing took less time than he had expected. He was not simply searched. He was forced to remove all the clothing he had been brought in wearing from Greymost, then given a gray prison shirt and matching trousers made of rough cloth. The outer cloak, boots, belt, and everything that could have seams, buckles, or hidden pockets immediately went into a separate box. That was exactly why Gilbert had hidden nothing in his clothing, knowing how pointless that was against the Castle of Justice.

After that, they examined him and checked him again. They worked quickly and without embarrassment, like people who had done this dozens of times. Once, one worker paused near his collarbone, where the core was hidden under the skin. Gilbert allowed himself to jerk in pain before the man could press harder.

"Hit there during the arrest?" the worker asked.

"If you care about the list of injuries, ask the ones who hit me," Gilbert said dryly.

The worker snorted and moved his hand away. Gilbert lowered his gaze. They had taken his clothes. Everything that could be carried in by normal means had been taken from him before the first door. But they did not know what else, besides the core, was hidden just under his skin.

The interrogation room was deeper inside. There was no window, and the walls were covered with the same thin lines as the arch, only denser. Runes stretched along the floor, ran under the legs of the table, climbed to the ceiling, and converged behind the back of the man waiting for him inside. The man was elderly, solid, with short-cropped gray hair and an entirely emotionless face.

"Sit," he said.

Gilbert was seated in a chair, and ordinary straps fixed his hands and chest in place. The elderly man opened a folder.

"Gilven Neld."

Gilbert remained silent. That too was part of the role. He did not need to look like a frightened loner or a mad fanatic. The Castle needed a leader of the underground, and Gilbert intended to give them exactly that kind of person: cautious, stubborn, used to not naming names and answering in such a way that every word seemed to hide ten more unsaid ones.

"You will be asked several questions. Answering now will be more useful than answering later."

The elderly man lifted his eyes.

"How many of you are in Greymost?"

"Enough."

"How many?"

"More than you would like."

"Who wrote the message on the guardhouse?"

Gilbert stayed silent.

"Who was with you at the old dyehouse?"

"People you failed to catch."

The elderly man made a slow note.

"What does 'mages will take what is theirs' mean?"

Gilbert looked at the runes on the wall, which were glowing almost imperceptibly. The room pressed down on power so evenly that most mages surely tried to resist for the first few minutes, then tired and broke faster.

"It means what it means," he said.

The elderly man set down his pen.

"Many like you have sat here before. Dodging questions. But not for long."

He nodded to one of the guards.

The pain began without any unnecessary words. The guards did not try to cripple him right away, since this was only the primary interrogation. They needed to probe Gilbert, to understand where he broke, what he feared, and which questions made him react faster.

First came blows calculated to knock the air out of him without breaking bone. Then they poured icy water over him, pressed on old bruises, on his ribs. One of the guards took his hand, pressed his fingers to the table, and drove a thin needle under the edge of his nail, pushing it deeper and deeper.

Gilbert jerked from the pain, but still remained silent.

The elderly man did not raise his voice the entire time. He simply asked questions. Sometimes he repeated the same one or jumped from Greymost to scrolls, from scrolls to letters, from letters to people who did not actually exist. The old man was not waiting for the truth right away. He was looking for inconsistencies in Gilbert’s words.

He tried to beat the names of accomplices out of Gilbert, but received nothing. Then he pressed on something else: places of resistance, other bases and shelters.

Gilbert stayed silent. The guard behind him grabbed him by the hair and forced his head back. For a second, the world slid apart. The core under his skin struck so sharply that the pain from the stranger’s hand almost got lost in it. The mage’s body was betraying him, but he could not allow himself to lose consciousness now.

"Locations," the elderly man repeated.

Gilbert closed his eyes and waited a little longer. The legend had to cost blood. A confession that came too quickly would look cheap. But if he stayed silent for too long, that could complicate everything too.

"Northern soapworks," he said at last. His voice after the torture was weaker and hoarser.

The room grew quieter.

"What?"

Gilbert opened his eyes.

"Northern soapworks. Old basement under the drying hall."

The elderly man picked up his pen again.

"Who is there?"

"I hope no one anymore."

"What will they find there?"

Gilbert smiled almost imperceptibly.

"What you deserve to find."

The guard struck him again, this time mostly for form. Gilbert exhaled heavily from the blow. Sakura had to have had enough time. If everything had gone according to plan, candles, cheap reagents, and drawn circles were already waiting in the soapworks. Everything looked like another place for underground meetings.

The elderly man asked another dozen questions before closing his folder. Gilbert answered little. Mostly, he stayed silent. At one point, he allowed himself a mistake, human enough for them to notice. He said "the lower group," though no lower group existed, but the elderly man immediately seized on the word.

After some time, the interrogation ended, because the guards had worn him down enough and gotten something they could verify. Naturally, this was not the end. They would definitely return to it.

"Intake block," the elderly man said. "Not the general one. And make sure he lives until the next conversation."

Gilbert’s straps were undone, but he did not rise immediately. No one rushed him. Most likely because rushing a person in that condition was pointless. Or they wanted to see if he could stand by himself. He could. Two guards led him down the corridor.

Now he had to look. From the interrogation room to the first turn, there was one door. A dedicated runic mage opened it, the same way Harek Harlow had once opened Gilbert’s bracelets. Fortunately, not all prison doors required that approach.

Gilbert also noticed that the runes by the staircase leading down glowed stronger. That meant the magic sealed in them was stronger than even the magic around the cell or post. It was an interesting detail, one he definitely had to remember.

Beyond the next turn, it smelled of metal and dampness, and then medicine. The bitter smell of cleaning infusion, old bandages, and something sour. The infirmary was somewhere nearby, but not on the main route. Voices drifted from there.

"I said after the check," someone said irritably. "Not before."

"And I said that if he collapses on my table again, I’ll leave him on your cot. I’m warning you."

Gilbert passed by without turning his head. The intake block turned out to be above the lower levels, and that was expected. They were not going to throw Gilbert in with the most sealed prisoners after the very first interrogation. First, they would keep him close at hand for the next checks and interrogations.

The door into the block took a long time to open: with a simple key, runic magic, and a second key. Gilbert had already understood that the prison’s runes were tuned so they would not suppress the local workers’ runic magic, but would suppress everything else. The tired overseer, who was also a runic mage, was old and almost lazy. He did everything mechanically. Gilbert liked that man, because he could become his first crack in this prison. But there was still more to study.

The cell was narrow, dry, and cold, made up of stone walls, a low bunk, and a slit under the door through which a strip of light passed. Runes stretched along the floor in thin threads and disappeared under the door. Here, the magic inside Gilbert lay even heavier.

"Face the wall," the guard said.

Gilbert obeyed. The door closed, and the key turned. The second lock clicked, short and indistinct. The overseer did not even pull the door after closing it. Footsteps retreated.

Gilbert sat on the bunk slowly, so as not to provoke another wave of nausea. The core pulsed under his skin. He ran his tongue over his split lip. Lir was below. He had known that before, but now he knew it differently, being inside this prison anthill.

He needed to move through the prison to study it better. He needed to get into the infirmary.

The core helped him. Gilbert allowed the weakness to rise higher. He did not steady his breathing or hide the tremor in his fingers. For several minutes, he sat motionless, listening to the corridor. Then he stood too quickly. The world tilted, but he held on. He went to the door and braced his palm against the stone beside the slit.

"Water," he said.

The overseer outside went silent.

"What?"

"Water."

Surprisingly, the overseer did not mock him.

"Wait."

The footsteps began moving away. Gilbert waited a little longer, listening to the corridor. Then he raised his hand to his face, as if he wanted to wipe blood from his upper lip, and sharply pressed his thumb under the cartilage of his nose. Pain flashed white and brief. His nose had already been damaged during the interrogation, and he did not need to break anything seriously. It was enough to tear what was already barely holding together. Blood came at once. It was hot, thick, and there was a lot of it. Good. Gilbert lowered himself to one knee by the door and let his breathing break.

The overseer returned faster than he had expected. A mug clinked behind the door.

"Asked for water?" he muttered.

Gilbert did not answer. He only lifted his head just enough to be seen through the viewing window. On the other side, it became quiet.

"Well, damn," the overseer said in a different tone. "Hey! He needs the healer."

"What is it?"

"Nosebleed. A lot of blood."

"Everyone bleeds after interrogation."

"Don’t be smart. This one’s important. If he dies in here, you’ll be the one writing the explanation."

Someone cursed in the corridor. Then quick footsteps sounded.

Gilbert lowered his gaze and pressed his fingers to his nose again. It had turned out convincing enough. Partly because he barely had to pretend anymore. After the core and the interrogation, his body truly was too weakened.

Now he would be able to get into the infirmary. Everything was going according to plan.
