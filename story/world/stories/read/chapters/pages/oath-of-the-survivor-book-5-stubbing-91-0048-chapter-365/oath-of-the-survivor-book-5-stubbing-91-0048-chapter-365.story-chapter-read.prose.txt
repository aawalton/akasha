“There’s no shame in turning back, Courier.”

The orc standing guard at the gate was a recently evolved D Grade, and Kyle had to give him credit for having the courage to try and bar his path.

“I was hired to complete a job, and I intend to do it,” Kyle said cooly. “I know my own limits better than anybody. You and your colleagues trying to convince me to quit is just wasting both of our time.”

The orc shifted uncomfortably, and Kyle sighed as he pushed past. It was likely a comical sight to anybody watching, as the orc stood nearly a meter taller than Kyle, but the disparity in Strength was extreme, despite Kyle’s lack of investment. The orc stumbled to the side, catching his balance as Kyle strode confidently through the gate to the path beyond. This main road would split off to the ancestral path only a few kilometers outside the village, and the variant hodag’s nest would be less than half a day travel from there.

Kyle felt eyes on him as he began his trek, but nobody made any further moves to hinder him as he departed. C.H.A.D.D. made clear note of the fork in the road to ensure Kyle didn’t miss it, but it turned out the drone needn’t have bothered. The delineation between the main trade route and the ancestral path was dramatic. The main trade pathway was large, flat, and well-maintained. By contrast, the ancestral path’s entrance was marked by two large sapphire pillars before it wound up into the crags above.

A quick check of the reference guide told Kyle that there had been a brutal war in the distant past along this route, which caused the pressurized sulfur beneath the ground to erupt, wiping out all the E Grades and below from both armies. It was a catastrophe, and one of the historic events that led to the formation of the Tournament of Ascension.

Wildlife was plentiful as Kyle navigated the crags, oversized lizards climbed the dark stone, while mammals that looked like a cross between a yak and a goat moved in small herds, grazing the squat shrubs that dotted the stony landscape. The trek was peaceful, at least for now.

It didn’t take long, however, for them to see the first hints of the hodag’s presence. Jagged blades of stone that seemed incredibly out of place jutted from the ground in haphazard patterns, the gray rock stained with dried blood. A quick use of Identify confirmed to Kyle that these were not natural, instead having the telltale signs of being created through mana manipulation. Just a little farther away, Kyle saw deep gouges in the stony ground, and a small crater where it looked like a massive paw struck down.

[AN EFFICIENT HUNTING STRATEGY,] C.H.A.D.D. noted.

“Can you even call it hunting?” Kyle asked. “It just skewered everything it wanted, then wandered up to eat.”

[I BELIEVE IT STILL QUALIFIES, YES. AND WE SHOULD BE THANKFUL FOR THIS INFORMATION. IT WILL UNDOUBTEDLY PROVE USEFUL TO US AS WE ENGAGE THE CREATURE.]

Kyle nodded his agreement. He’d noted before that most C Grades seemed to have at least one larger domain skill, and this hodag’s would almost certainly involve manipulating the stone around it. And if it had some of the other hallmark techniques the other C Grades did, the surrounding area wasn’t going to look pretty by the time they were done.

“Any signs of it on your scanners, C.H.A.D.D.?”

[NOT AT THIS POINT, DR. MAYHEW. HOWEVER I EXPECT THAT WE WILL SEE IT SOON AS WE CONTINUE DOWN THIS PATH.]

After passing more macabre spires, the drone’s words proved to be prophetic. [I DETECT THE BEAST APPROACHING, DR. MAYHEW. IT IS LARGER THAN EXPECTED.]

“Can you show me an image?” Kyle asked, working to suppress his aura.

Two projections appeared; one of a standard C Grade hodag, the other showing a creature nearly twice its size. If Kyle hadn’t seen the genuine article, he would have almost thought he was staring at some type of dragon. The hodag’s horns were long and curved, like a bull’s, protruding past a bear-like snout, which hid a maw full of jagged fangs. Its body was rippling with muscles, thick plates of natural armor covering it almost like scales. Its front shoulders were particularly pronounced, flowing down into oversized forelimbs with wickedly long and sharp claws. A thick bone protrusion tipped its long tail like the head of a flail with small bony spikes running up its spine.

“Certainly looks like a powerful creature,” Kyle said after a moment of comparing the images.

[FROM WHAT I CAN DETECT, I WOULD PLACE THIS CREATURE’S POWER A TIER ABOVE THE OTHER VARIANT HODAG YOU FACED.]

“Then it’s a good thing we’re stronger, too,” Kyle said with a smile. “Do you have a good sense of where it’s headed?”

C.H.A.D.D. highlighted a series of potential paths on the map, all leading to a small depression in the ground. [THIS AREA APPEARS TO BE ITS NEST; ALONG THE ANCESTRAL PATH, BUT NOT EXCESSIVELY CLOSE TO THE SULFUR PITS. THERE ARE INCREASED NUMBERS OF SPIRES ALONG THE APPROACH, WITH RELATIVELY FEW WITHIN THE CENTRAL AREA.]

Kyle nodded along. “It’ll work out well if it’s resting when we arrive. We’ll launch an ambush with Model: Cannon to see how it holds up, and then plan on single combat from there.”

[SO LONG AS NO OTHER HODAGS JOIN IN THE BATTLE, THAT PLAN SEEMS TO BE OUR BEST CHANCE,] C.H.A.D.D. agreed.

“Are you detecting any others?”

[NO. HOWEVER, IT WOULD BE UNWISE TO ASSUME THAT WE WILL BE FIGHTING THE BEAST ALONE. IT IS MUCH MORE RESPONSIBLE TO PREPARE FOR A WORST-CASE SCENARIO.]

“I can’t argue with that,” Kyle said, continuing down the path at a slower pace. While his mana control allowed him to suppress his natural aura without much difficulty, his lack of a real stealth skill prevented him from being able to use other skills without giving away his position. The fact that Jarberry could accomplish so much while being veiled to all senses was a testament to his mentor’s prowess. He’d never be at Jarberry’s level, but he’d still picked up a few tricks.

As they walked, Kyle took some time to look out at the sulfur fields in the distance. A thick mist clung to the ground over them, the gray stones tinged with yellow and brown. Small cracks could be seen on the surface, and he thought about the horrific fate that met many of the warring orcish soldiers in this very spot. He’d experienced many horrors in his relatively short life, but the thought of the ground opening up to swallow entire armies still sent a chill down his spine.

C.H.A.D.D.’s prediction turned out to be correct, as the hodag stopped moving as it made its way to the nest. Even better, there was no sign of any other hodags nearby. While it was no guarantee, the beasts weren’t particularly speedy. If he was able to deal with the variant hodag expediently, he was confident in disengaging before getting pinned down by potential reinforcements. His ability to retreat was also why he was willing to go out of his way to fight the powerful C Grade. Even if he and C.H.A.D.D. underestimated the creature, they had plenty of tools available to aid in their escape.

Careful to keep his presence concealed, Kyle crested the ridge to look down at the massive creature. It was curled up in its nest, crunching on the carcass of one of the oversized lizards.

[ARE YOU READY TO BEGIN, DR. MAYHEW?]

“As I’ll ever be,” he said, willing a pre-prepared net of vines to come out of the World Tree Seed and cover him. Stonebloom bark covered the vines, creating a mana-rejecting zone around Kyle as he prepared Model: Cannon and his own armor. Taking a deep breath, he aimed the weapon, then activated Genesis Engine, boosting his attributes the moment before he launched his attack.

When he did, the hodag’s head snapped toward him. Still, Kyle knew the creatures well enough to know that they didn’t have the speed necessary to dodge at this distance. What he didn’t expect, however, was that a wall of stone would erupt from the ground, intercepting the projectile midair. The razorpine seed, enhanced by a blast from Aura Force, smashed through the earthen wall before colliding with the hodag. It still staggered the beast, though a quick use of Identify showed Kyle that the beast had taken almost no meaningful damage.

It let out a massive roar, and a shockwave carrying thousands of shards of stone came flying back toward Kyle in reply. He launched himself skyward to evade the blast, swapping out Model: Cannon for Model: Shield to intercept the shrapnel on the far edge of the hodag’s attack.

[DR. MAYHEW, THIS IS GETTING DANGEROUS.]

“Yes it is,” Kyle agreed, soaring overhead.

As they watched, blades of stone rose from the ground to float around the hodag. Fist-sized chunks of rock flew toward him, forcing him to evade as the ground seemed to flow around the giant creature, forming a thick layer of earthen armor. Kyle dealt with a similar technique from one of the shrike alphas on Er’Mithren, though to a much weaker degree. It was a bad matchup for him then, as the inorganic material made it difficult for Parasitic Resonance to break through.

As more projectiles flew toward him, he used Aura Force to disperse the attacks, pulverizing the larger stones to gravel. Flying overhead, he dropped large several larger trees toward the creature, relying on the mass and pure kinetic energy to damage the thick armor. Compared to the more specialized munitions, these were much easier to grow within the World Tree Seed’s space, making them strong disposable options.

Unfortunately, the strikes didn’t seem to do much to the hodag at all. It responded by launching larger and larger stones at Kyle; blades, boulders, and spears flying toward him at ever-increasing speed. The increasing size and velocity of the attacks made them more and more difficult to neutralize with Aura Force alone, and he had to rely on evasion.

As Kyle watched, the armor surrounding the hodag grew thicker by the moment, though it didn’t seem to weigh the creature down at all. Instead, it used the stone like an exoskeleton, moving it about with its mana and constantly repairing the small damage Kyle was able to inflict. One misstep could be deadly, and Kyle knew that it was time to change the status quo.

“C.H.A.D.D., are your bombs ready?”

He felt a surge of excitement from the drone, as one of its favorite projects would finally have its time to shine. [I THOUGHT YOU WOULD NEVER ASK, DR. MAYHEW.]
