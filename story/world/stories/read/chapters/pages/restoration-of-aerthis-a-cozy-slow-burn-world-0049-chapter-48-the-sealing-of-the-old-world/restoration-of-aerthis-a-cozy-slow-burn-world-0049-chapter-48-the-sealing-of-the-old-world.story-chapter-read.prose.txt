It took a long time before either of them could bring themselves to move again.

The crystal’s projection had long faded, but its weight lingered in the air around them, the sorrow of the recording having taken up quiet residence beside them. And it just refused to leave.

Elynara sat hunched over her staff, arms wrapped around it like a lifeline. Her eyes were still puffy, swollen from crying, skin blotchy where she had tried – unsuccessfully – to wipe away the tears with her sleeves.

Every so often her breath hitched in that uneven, fragile way that came after the worst of a cry, when the body wasn’t quite sure whether it was finished yet.

Starpuff nestled in her lap, its glow barely more than a soft ember, matching her mood with rare, subdued stillness.

Aru hadn’t quite shed tears – though Elynara could see her eyes moisten up too – but more visibly… she trembled, every so often. The shivering pulses that ran through the fine lines of her plating. Tiny fluctuations in her internal light flickered irregularly, blinking out and returning in uneven intervals.

Her hands, normally so steady they could thread feathers, twitched when she tried to fold them neatly in her lap.

For the longest time, neither of them spoke. The ruins around them breathed only with wind, soft and lonely.

Eventually, in the quiet, Aru shifted first.

“…We should continue,” she said softly – her voice a tired, gentle suggestion. “If we stop now… it will be harder later.”

Elynara nodded without lifting her head.

“Yeah,” she murmured. “We should. We promised to finish.”

But her voice was thick, still raw at the edges.

So they worked.

Slowly at first, hands moving with the weight of everything they had just seen.

The holographic device flickered to life again, its blue glow painting their faces with a cold light that made the shadows under Elynara’s eyes look even darker.

Despite having briefly looked through all the crystals they gathered by now, the girls weren’t quite done copying everything properly yet. Their need to learn more about the Elthusians and faster, made simply organizing the checked ones into piles look like a better idea.

Crystal after crystal was placed inside.

Copy. Wait. Transfer.

Check. Set aside.

Repeat.

The motions grew rhythmic, meditative in a somber way. Elynara learned to operate the device better – even if it was still mostly Aru doing the work. For the most part she simply needed to supply the mana, but that was fine too.

Starpuff hovered nearby like a tiny lantern, keeping close to Elynara’s hair whenever another video appeared it didn’t like.

It was late into the night – long after the last twilight had bled out of the sky – when they finally reached the bottom of the pile.

The moons hung low overhead, shining through the broken courtyard walls in faint, fractured beams.

Some crystals had been too full, too dense with information, and Elynara’s crystals not enough to contain everything from them.

So they had to overwrite some.

The first volunteers were the excessive safety guides – Elynara’s heart did not ache for those, not one bit!

Hundreds of crystals had dwindled down to a few dozen.

Aru arranged them in neat rows beside the wall, the “important” ones copied twice into separate blanks.

“Just in case,” she murmured, her movements careful and measured.

Elynara nodded. Although it was a little hard to tell whether she was agreeing or just acknowledging the motion.

Yet, over time, the work let their turbulent emotions calm down into a gentle simmer. And slowly… life began to return to their bodies. Their actions becoming more fluid, reactions more animated.

Starpuff getting up to no good again.

Sometimes healing just needed one to focus on work, until the fresh wounds closed up properly.

And when the last crystal clicked into place and the device finally dimmed with a soft sigh of mana, Aru quietly powered it down.

Neither girl smiled, but… they were no longer brooding either.

Despite all the promising discoveries – the manuals, the lessons, the glimpses of knowledge that could reshape everything Elynara thought she knew – joy still felt distant.

Tender, but far away.

Tucked behind the memory of the little girl whispering, Daddy, I’m cold.

Elynara pulled her knees up to her chest, resting her chin atop them, her staff laid across her feet.

“…That was a lot,” she whispered, her fingers idly petting Starpuff – to its obvious happiness.

Out of the three of them, her little puff of a friend sure bounced back up the fastest.

Aru folded her hands in her lap, no longer trembling.

“…Yes.”

The crystals gleamed softly beside them, Elynara watching them with mixed thoughts warring at each other.

Treasures, in any other moment.

Yet tonight, they felt like gravestones.

The night air brushed past, cool and gentle, trying to soothe what words couldn’t. The girls sat together – quiet, tired, and carrying the weight of a lost world in their hands.

And in the dim silver glow of the moonlight, neither tried to push away the heaviness.

Sometimes the past wasn’t meant to be swallowed quickly.

Sometimes it needed to sit with them awhile, settling in deep before becoming something they could walk forward with.

Tonight was one of those times.

And it wasn’t until Elynara finally lifted her head – eyes still a little puffy – that it finally clicked.

A small quiet spark of determination flickered in her chest.

“…We can’t sit like this all night,” she murmured, voice rough from crying.

Aru turned toward her, inner lights dim, waiting for her to continue. She didn’t argue, simply looking lost, unsure what it is they could be doing instead.

So Elynara stood up!

Then she reached down, grabbed Aru’s hands, and gently pulled her to her feet. The automaton girl rose stiffly, her motions hesitant and almost delicate.

Perhaps she was worried she might accidentally break the moment?

No matter, Elynara had long since learned that movement helped with mood a lot. And so did rest. And she would have her friend learn that lesson too!

Once Aru stood, Elynara turned and tugged her across the courtyard toward the discarded pile. It was the heap of mundane things she had emptied out of her satchel earlier: some cloth bundles, cooking scraps, spare clothes, herbs she picked up at some point, leftover gifts from the villagers – none of them food, and…

Her sleeping gear.

Aru blinked, thrown off.

“I… what are we doing?”

“Letting the night rest,” Elynara said, trying her best to sound confident despite her uneven voice. “And you’re coming with me.”

Aru tilted her head, confused.

“But I do not require sleep.”

“Yes, well.” Elynara knelt to gather the bedroll and bundle of blankets. “I do. We do.”

She cast a look over her shoulder.

“You are hereby upgraded to my pillow for tonight.”

Aru froze.

“…Your what?”

“Pillow!” Elynara declared, as if this answered every possible question. “And you will be compensated fairly.”

“…Compensated…?”

“In hugs and mana!” Elynara said triumphantly, the offer surely the most convincing!

Aru’s entire body seemed to stutter.

Soft lights brightened along her cheek plating, an adorably glowing blush forming, and if she had blush subroutines, they would have been completely fried now.

“But– I– the parameters of–”

“Too late!” Elynara cut in, already pulling her close again. “Decision made!”

It took only a few minutes to set things up.

They found a sheltered spot beneath a broken overhang where the wind passed over softly but didn’t sting their faces. Elynara spread her bedroll on the flattest patch of stone she could find, tugging the thicker blanket open with a practiced flick.

She settled onto one side, leaving space behind her.

Starpuff burrowed under her hood at once, letting out a tiny, relieved chirp.

Aru stood beside the blanket, her hands moving and twitching – the girl unsure whether she was meant to lie down, sit, or flee.

Elynara reached out and tugged her wrist gently.

“Aru,” she said softly, “come here.”

Aru obeyed in small, rigid motions, lowering herself with obvious uncertainty.

Elynara shifted, pulled the blanket up around both of them, then guided Aru’s arms into a loose circle.

The moment Elynara leaned back into her, the warmth surprised her.

“…I never told you this,” she murmured, settling her cheek against Aru’s shoulder plating. “But you’re very warm.”

Her eyes fluttered tiredly.

“And very much hug-shaped…”

Aru went still.

But only for a heartbeat.

Then the tension in her limbs softened, and her arms slowly – very slowly – tightened around Elynara in a careful, protective cradle.

The soft glow under her plating steadied, warming into a gentle muted simmer.

Starpuff peeked out from the hood, blinked once, then nestled down again, bobbing softly with Elynara’s steadying breaths.

The automaton girl watched her friend’s face relax – the faint tear tracks drying along her cheeks, eyelashes fluttering in the first threads of sleep.

When Elynara’s lips parted in a tiny, soft exhale, Aru reached a hesitating hand up and brushed away the last faint streak of dried salt.

It was a touch light as dust, barely enough to stir skin.

She didn’t want to wake her.

Didn’t dare to.

Merely watched as sleep took her friend deeper into their grasp.

And when Elynara slipped fully into sleep, Aru let her hand hover just a moment longer before lowering it back around the girl’s waist.

Aru didn’t need sleep.

Even in the new body, she could most likely remain active for weeks without rest.

But now… she let her internal body processes slow, her primary functions dimming until only a light alert remained – enough to watch over the girl resting against her.

Enough to know if danger came.

Dreams took her again, warm and strangely bright, filled with the presence of the girl who had brought her back to life.

At some peaceful point in the night, she stirred.

Her optics flickered open, and she looked down at the soundly sleeping girl curled in her arms, face pressed to her chest, hair spilling like a golden river with a hint of greenish hue across the blanket.

The dreams had worked wonders already, she could feel. Her mind far more relaxed, less ready to pounce and react to every new sound around.

Aru whispered – for no one’s ears but the ruins around them:

“…Says the most hug-shaped girl I ever knew.”

A small smile touched her lips – a rare, soft thing, the first of what would be many. The promise of the good to come.

Her belief strengthened, the first decision of her new life entrenching itself deep into her psyche. Once more, she was certain. Once more, convinced.

She would keep her savior safe. As she was sure her friend would do for her.

Her glow dimmed again, peaceful.

She powered down once more, arms wrapped securely around the sleeping form.

And for the first time in centuries, Aru slept with neither fear nor worry.

The next morning greeted them gently.

Soft light broke across the ruins in pale streaks, warming metal and stone just enough for the air to lose its bite.

Elynara woke first, stretching under the blanket with a long, low groan that felt like it came from the depth of her bones. Her muscles ached, but in the easy, relieved way rest always left them.

Aru stirred beside her – more a flicker of inner light than any external motion – her body optics powering on one by one in small, soft pulses. She didn’t jolt awake as one might have expected from a robot. She simply… eased into consciousness, warm beneath the blanket, her arms still loosely wrapped around Elynara’s middle until awareness finally caught up and she carefully let go.

Elynara pretended not to notice the shy way Aru’s hands recoiled, or how the girl’s glow flared slightly brighter in embarrassment.

But it did make her mood just a little better, and Elynara patted herself on the back for the great idea of pulling Aru to rest with her.

Seeing how the automaton girl reacted – and just the fact that she slept at all, contrary to all those claims of ‘not needing to sleep’ – had her grin internally.

Even the events of yesterday – still sitting heavily in her chest – no longer felt quite as daunting.

“Good morning, my dear friend… Convinced yet of the glory of sleep?”

Aru stared at her, confused. Frozen for just a single moment that Elynara noticed anyhow.

“...Yes.”

Her smile widening, she gave her robot friend a quick hug, and immediately got up.

There was a day to get to, and bad thoughts to chase away still!

After a full night of sleep, the ache had edges she could handle again. One tragedy among so many. This one just felt… far too personal.

Her stomach grumbled.

And with that very mortal reminder, the moment of calm shattered.

Elynara grimaced dramatically as she unwrapped her last dreadfully unexciting ration bar.

“…If I ever meet Celestia again, I’m asking her why she didn’t stock my satchel with better food.”

Of course, the complaint completely ignored the fact that stocking up all the other things for the journey here was her own handiwork.

Nobody needed to know that!

Aru sat nearby, folded neatly, hands resting on her knees as if she wasn’t sure what she was allowed to do yet. But she was less rigid now, a softness remaining from sharing a blanket all night.

Elynara took a bite of her ration bar and frowned at it like it had personally wronged her. And it probably would, if it could!

Starpuff peeked out from under her hood like a nosy little spy. Elynara flicked a blob of her processed mana upward, and the puff caught it with a delighted trill before immediately dive-bombing straight into Aru’s forehead.

Aru startled, her hands shooting up and then stopping mid-rise.

Elynara cackled.

“Finally! I’m not the only one being attacked by that little menace!”

But then she noticed something else entirely – something that tugged at her focus with the same intensity as mana itself did.

Aru wasn’t looking at Starpuff.

The automaton’s eyes followed the blob of mana instead… and only reacted to the spirit when the fuzzball smacked between her eyes.

Elynara blinked slowly.

“Aru.”

“Yes?”

The girl straightened, a little too fast.

“Catch.”

Before Aru could question it, Elynara reached out with her magic. Mana flowed from her fingers like warm water, sinking into the girl’s core with an ease that was surprising to them both. Aru gasped – a tiny, soft sound – and her glow flared in a shy burst.

“You– you do not have to–”

“Nuh-uh,” Elynara said firmly. “I’m not letting my friend go hungry.”

Aru’s glow pulsed again.

“…Um. Thank you.”

Elynara grinned and squeezed her hand.

“None of that now. But that does explain a few things.”

She looked – really looked this time – letting her mana sight spread through her vision.

What she saw made her exhale slowly.

It hadn’t been obvious during the past few days. She had been topping Aru off after every trip into the bunker, so of course the deficit didn’t show. But now, with distance from the sand and time to breathe…

“…Your mana generation really is low, isn’t it?”

Aru lowered her gaze, quiet.“I, uh… was never meant to be able to gather any. I relied on external sources, the Source, and city batteries. The Elthusians built different cores and networks for different purposes. Mine…” She hesitated. “…Mine was not designed to gather or generate much on its own.”

Elynara squeezed her hand again, smile softening.

“I don’t mind helping. Really. If anything, it’s good practice. But if I’m being honest–” she held up her pitiful ration bar “–I desperately need something better to eat soon or I might go insane.”

Starpuff trilled in agreement, already nibbling at its mana piece with unearned smugness.

Aru tilted her head thoughtfully, perhaps a little worried on her behalf.

But Elynara wasn’t done.

“So,” she said between bites of the awful ration, “what do we do about the bunker? And the rest of the ruins?”

Aru stayed silent for a long moment, optics flaring then dimming in thoughtful cycles.

Elynara looked over the horizon, then back at the yawning entrance far behind them. The memory of hollow skulls staring outward still made her shoulders tighten.

“Whatever is still out there…” she began, “it won’t be easy to find, even with your help. The ages destroyed too much. And we already recovered the most important thing: their knowledge. Their memories.”

Aru nodded.

“As for the bunker…” Elynara hesitated, chewing on the last of her ration bar. “We can’t clear it… At least not properly and without spending far too long here. There’s no way to bury, and no way to honor everyone inside. As for the white sand…” She shivered. “That thing cannot be allowed to spread. Not for curious wanderers, and it’s too dangerous to leave for somebody to just walk into.”

“…We should close it,” Aru said quietly. “Seal it. Make it safe. And hidden.”

Elynara lowered her gaze.

“…And hope the souls of those buried there forgive us.”

Aru placed a warm hand over hers, gently.

“We will carry their memories with us,” she whispered. “I am sure they would understand.”

Elynara swallowed and nodded.

The day ahead still waited for them, but now they had a plan on what to do next. And perhaps a decent meal to look forward after. Elynara would make very sure of it. Even if she has to hunt the ingredients herself!

The sealing itself took them most of the afternoon.

Aru worked in focused silence, her hands moving with a mixture of precision and quiet determination as she re-aligned the inner locking mechanism. What was left of it, at least. Elynara held the staff-light steady for her, its soft glow catching the dust motes drifting lazily in the stale air.

The doors – massive slabs of metal that had once been the pride of a civilization – now stood half-open only because Elynara had forced mana into their ancient cores. Even with Aru’s help, they refused to budge more than a slow grinding inch. Age had claimed their grace. The white sand had claimed the rest.

“Here,” Aru murmured, sliding a piece of reinforcement plating under the edge. “Once I wedge this… they should not move again.”

Elynara watched her work. The girl’s motions were steady, but her hands shook just a little whenever the dust hissed across her plating. She still remembered the cold, hungry pull on Aru’s warmth after the first trip inside.

She felt it too when she stood too close. The Deadzone influence was truly too harsh, too cruel for those unprepared for it… if one even could prepare for something like that.

Her idle thoughts halted as Aru stepped back.

She supplied mana to the bunker once again, and watched Aru work her magic.

The doors groaned – a low, deep sound that vibrated through Elynara’s ribs. Metal scraped, dust puffed, the faintest echo carried through the corridor, the last sigh of a closing tomb.

And then the bunker sealed shut, the wedge Aru talked about soon clinching it such that any attempts at reopening the mass grave would be far more problematic.

Aru then exhaled, placed both palms on the cold metal, and muttered something almost prayer-like under her breath.

Elynara let her have her moment.

She then set her hand beside Aru’s, touching the slab lightly.

“…Rest well,” she whispered. “We won’t forget you.”

Aru nodded silently.

They spent another hour reinforcing the outer frame – wedging debris, twisting beams together, collapsing some of the hallway behind them to make sure nobody curious, foolish, or unlucky would wander inside. By the time Elynara stepped back, brushing dust from her hands, the entrance was unrecognizable.

A tomb, a warning, and a promise.

No more white sand would escape its confines. No more souls would be lost to curiosity.

Only then did Elynara finally allow herself to turn fully toward the ruins.

The silence pressed in again, but this time it wasn’t as cruel.

She looked at everything – the broken beams stretching like ribs across the collapsed skyline, the cracked stone half-swallowed by debris, the faint remnants of pathways that once led between towering habitats now buried in time.

Her heart tightened.

When she entered these ruins, she knew so little. Barely anything, truly.

Merely hoping for anything useful, not even knowing what to expect. Only that she needed to learn.

And now…

She had fought monsters in shattered halls. Grew to new heights, learned so many things. And pushed her mana to new heights.

From a lone wanderer to someone who could mend spirits and metal alike.

Or at least one person who was both.

Elynara had found a friend in the old spirit – and then lost them.

Only to save them and their machine friend both at once. Two turning into one, saved yet not the same. Old turned to new.

And that new friend of hers…

Elynara smiled.

It was worthwhile to save her. To see the curious and shy looks the girl was giving to everything, even when she didn’t know Elynara was watching. She looked forward to how Aru would grow.

Watching Aru breathe life back into a city long forgotten, and give context to its workings… It was quite an experience.

And despite now having seen horrors she would never forget… Elynara now held those precious memories in her hands.

Anything from the funny cooking videos from a world that no longer existed, making her smile and shake her head. Up to the dreary sight of a father soothing his daughter in their final moments.

And she now had access to so many books and lessons made for people who were now bone dust.

Elynara would not waste that chance.

She looked at the streets around her again.

At the half-buried gigantic buildings.

At the glitter of glass shards catching the light in places, scattered between debris, and many in weirdly good shape, despite centuries of abuse.

And the broken angles of what must have been quite a metropolis, from an age when prosperity was a given.

And for a single heartbeat, the holographic projections they had watched flickered over the ruins in her mind.

Tall towers of pristine white. Blue lights weaving along walkways.

Gardens tended by automatons.

Children laughing.

The city breathing with life.

Bright. Warm. And whole.

Her breath hitched.

Then the image faded.

Leaving only collapsing stone and silence again.

“…I wish I could have seen it,” she whispered. “All of it. Before everything went wrong.”

Aru didn’t answer. She didn’t need to.

Instead, the girl’s hand slipped quietly into Elynara’s.

Warm, careful, and shy – though not as shy as before.

Elynara blinked and looked at her.

Aru’s glow flickered, but she held her gaze this time.

Comfortable enough to stay close, and to reach out without being asked.

Elynara smiled softly, her eyes twinkling at her friend’s growth.

Trust took time.

But they had plenty of that ahead.

She squeezed Aru’s hand back, and faced the broken road winding away from the ruins.

“Ready?” she murmured.

Aru nodded.

Starpuff swooped in with a triumphant chirp, nearly hitting Elynara in the forehead before darting a playful loop around Aru’s head.

Elynara laughed, while Aru flinched in response. Starpuff preened like a happy mischief incarnate.

And together, they took their first steps away from the sealed bunker.

Away from the dead city, and toward whatever came next.

There was so much left to learn about the world.

And now she wasn’t walking alone.
