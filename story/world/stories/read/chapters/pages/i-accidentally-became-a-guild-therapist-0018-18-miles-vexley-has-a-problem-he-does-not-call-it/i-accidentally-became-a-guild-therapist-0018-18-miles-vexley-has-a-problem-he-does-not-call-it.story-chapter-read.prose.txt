I ACCIDENTALLY BECAME A GUILD THERAPIST

Chapter 18 – Miles Vexley Has a Problem (He Does Not Call It That)

ARC II: THE WORLD OUTSIDE THE CAMP

SYNOPSIS: Miles Vexley requests a strategy meeting. It is not a strategy meeting. Livia knows it is not a strategy meeting. Miles knows that Livia knows. Neither of them says this out loud for quite some time.

The request came through the guild’s official channel, which Miles almost never used for internal communications because the guild’s official channel generated a log and Miles preferred things that did not generate logs.

It read:

Strategy meeting. Planning room. Tomorrow, 9am. Agenda: guild resource allocation Q3 and external consultation review. — M.V.

Livia read it twice.

Guild resource allocation Q3.

He sent this through the official channel.

He wrote agenda items.

Miles Vexley has never in his life cared about resource allocation Q3.

He used the official channel because he wanted a paper trail.

He wanted a paper trail because he needed a reason to be alone in a room with me that is not the real reason.

She wrote back:

Confirmed. — L.Q.

[Therapist’s Insight: Patient: Miles Vexley. Status: Approaching something voluntarily for the first time. The system notes this is significant. The system notes you already know this. The system suggests letting him lead. He will not get there if he feels pushed.]

Let him lead.

I know.

I have been waiting for this for three months.

I am going to be very calm about this.

The planning room was the small one off the main hall, used for actual strategy meetings and, on two documented occasions, Nyx’s post-mission decompression sessions when the main hall was too loud. It had a table, four chairs, a tactical display that Miles turned off when she arrived, and a window that looked out onto the guild garden where Glimmer was currently attempting to climb a decorative stone pillar for reasons that were unclear.

Miles was already there when she came in. He had, specifically, placed two mugs of coffee on the table, which meant he had thought about this enough to prepare for a conversation longer than ten minutes.

Two mugs.

He made coffee.

He is more nervous than he looks.

I am going to look at my notepad.

She sat down. He sat across from her. The tactical display was dark. There were no notes on the table. There was no agenda visible anywhere.

“Resource allocation Q3,” Livia said..

“Right,” Miles said..

A pause.

“We can do that first if you want,” Livia said. “Or we can do the other thing.”

He looked at her..

“What other thing,” he said. Not a question. The flat register of someone testing whether she was going to name it..

Livia picked up her coffee and did not push..

“There’s a lot of guild activity in the next quarter,” she said. “Echo Cavern contract is done. The Eclipse Covenant consultation wraps up at the end of the month. Quest board volume has been high. Probably worth reviewing.”

Miles was quiet for a moment. Something in his posture settled—not relaxed, but recalibrated. He had expected to be pushed. She had not pushed. The quality of his stillness changed.

“The Eclipse consultation,” he said. “Caelric asked if we’d consider a standing arrangement.”

“What did you say?”

“I said I’d ask you.”

“Do you want me to take it?”

He turned his coffee mug. Once. “I want you to have the option.”

That’s not the same as wanting me to take it.

He knows that too.

“I’ll think about it,” she said..

He nodded. They drank coffee. Outside, Glimmer had reached the top of the stone pillar and appeared to be surveying the garden with satisfaction..

The resource allocation review took twelve minutes, because it was not complicated and Livia had already reviewed it before coming and Miles had clearly reviewed it before coming and they both knew it was not the reason they were here. When it was done, Miles set down his pen and looked at the tactical display for a moment as if it were showing something, which it was not.

Then he said, to the dark display: “The guild’s in a different place than it was six months ago.”

“Yes,” Livia said..

“That’s partly the dungeon work. The external consultations. The quest board.”

“Partly,” she agreed..

He was quiet..

“The individual members have been—” he stopped. Started again differently. “Bron doesn’t wake up at 3am anymore. Nyx comes to dinner most nights. Phina’s wedding planning doesn’t derail raid prep the way it used to.”

He was not looking at her. He was cataloguing changes, the way he catalogued things that he did not know what to do with emotionally but could document precisely.

“Alaric still bets on everything,” Livia said..

“Alaric bets on better things now,” Miles said. “There’s a difference.”

He’s noticed all of this.

He has been watching and cataloguing and not saying it.

This is what he wanted to say.

“It’s good,” she said simply. “What you’re describing. It’s good.”

He turned the mug again..

“I know,” he said. Then: “I’m not used to it.”

Livia waited..

“When things are—” he searched for the word, —“stable. Functional. I keep waiting for the other part.”

“The part where it goes wrong?”

“The part where it was always going to.”

There it is.

Not a dramatic confession.

Just the thing that lives underneath all of it.

When things are good I keep waiting for the part where it was always going to go wrong.

She did not say: that’s the betrayal talking. She did not say: you were abandoned by people you trusted and now your nervous system treats stability as a warning sign. She knew both of those things. She also knew that saying them now would make him close the door he had just opened, because that was too much to hand back to someone who had not asked for it yet.

“How long have you been waiting for the other part?” she asked instead..

He thought about it..

“Three years,” he said..

The same number. Three years ago. The raid. The guild. The trade.

She did not say that either..

“And when things go wrong,” she said, “do you know in advance that’s what’s happening? Or does it always feel like a surprise?”

He looked at her for the first time in several minutes..

“Always a surprise,” he said. Quietly..

“So three years of waiting,” Livia said, “and it still surprises you every time.”

He was still..

“Yes.”

“That’s exhausting,” she said..

The word landed the way she had meant it: not as analysis, not as a technique. Just recognition. It is exhausting. It must be.

Miles looked at his coffee..

“Yes,” he said again. Different register. Something had shifted in it..

They had approximately four more minutes before the door opened.

It was Bron, which was not a surprise, because Bron’s internal schedule had never aligned with anyone else’s and he operated on the assumption that rooms he wanted to enter were available until proven otherwise.

“Strategy meeting?” he said, looking at the two coffee mugs and the absence of any tactical materials. “Doesn’t look like a strategy meeting.”

“It’s wrapping up,” Miles said, in the tone of someone who had already reclosed everything that had been briefly open..

Bron looked at Livia. Then at Miles. Then back at Livia with the specific expression he got when he had understood something that nobody was saying out loud.

“Oh,” he said..

“Resource allocation,” Miles said..

“Right,” Bron said, very seriously. “Resource allocation. Sure. I can come back.”

“We’re done,” Miles said..

He stood, picked up his coffee, and walked out with the unhurried composure of someone who had not just said the most honest thing he had said in three years in a planning room over municipal logistics.

Bron watched him go. Then looked at Livia..

“Was that—” he started..

“Resource allocation,” Livia said..

“Right,” Bron said..

He sat down in the chair Miles had vacated, picked up Miles’ remaining coffee, and drank from it with the serene confidence of a man who did not think this was a problem.

“He’s doing better,” Bron said, after a moment. Not a question..

“He’s trying,” Livia said..

“For him that’s the same thing,” Bron said..

For him that’s the same thing.

Bron said that.

Bron, who I have been treating for existential dread for three months.

Sometimes the people in therapy become the sharpest observers of each other.

That is not something they teach you in the half of psych class I actually attended.

Filed.

The guild had the particular quality of late morning on a low-intensity day: most members doing maintenance tasks or optional content, no active quests requiring coordination, the general ambient noise of a large group of people occupying shared space without urgency.

Livia sat in the planning room for a while after Bron left, notepad open.

She wrote:

Session: Miles Vexley. Unofficial. Initiated by patient. Disguised as logistics meeting — patient used official channel, created documentation, prepared for conversation he needed a structural reason to have. This is the first time he has come voluntarily without a crisis as the presenting reason.

Content: Hypervigilance in stable conditions. Anticipatory dread of loss even when nothing is threatening loss. Three years. He said three years without framing it as the betrayal but it is the betrayal. He is not ready to call it that. That is fine.

Progress: He named the exhaustion. That is not nothing. For Miles, naming anything is not nothing.

Note: He made coffee. Two mugs. He was going to be there when I arrived. He thought about this.

She closed the notepad.

[You held space for someone who arrived calling it something else. +1,200 EXP. Patient: Miles Vexley. Progress: 12%. The system notes: 12% is more than double what it was. The system notes: he made coffee. The system also notes this.]

[Mental Supporter Lv. 7: 3,300 / 7,000 EXP.]

Outside, Glimmer was still on top of the stone pillar. It had not come down. It appeared to have decided the stone pillar was home now.

Noted.

I am adding Glimmer’s escalating real estate ambitions to the list of things I am monitoring.

The guild board had a new posting when she passed through the main hall on her way to lunch.

Not a quest slip. A note, pinned with slightly more force than necessary, written in a handwriting she did not recognize: sharp, angular, the kind of handwriting that matched someone who had strong opinions about letter formation.

The plants in the eastern corridor have been speaking for three weeks. I have logged seventeen distinct utterances. Nobody in this guild has mentioned this. I have been saying for two years that the flora in Aethelgard has latent sentience capacity and everyone called it “naturalist nonsense.” I require a conversation with whoever is responsible for the talking plants. — T.P.

Livia looked at the note for a moment.

T.P.

Someone named T.P. has been logging utterances from sentient plants for three weeks.

Someone named T.P. has been telling this guild about sentient plants for two years.

The eastern corridor plants started talking around the time of the Luxemira incident.

I am responsible for the talking plants.

I am not going to put my name on this yet.

She left the note on the board..

Then went back and took it down..

Then put it in her pocket..

I will deal with this later.

The subreddit had a new top post when she checked it that afternoon.

Not a fan thread. A video. Posted by an account called:

u/MortyCalvarium_IYKYK, which had 847,000 followers and a verified badge that said "Certified Undead Content Creator."

The video was titled: "POV: You Are One of Livia Quinn’s Guild Members and Your Guild Leader Just Had a Strategy Meeting That Was Definitely a Strategy Meeting" and was two minutes and forty seconds of footage of Miles walking out of the planning room, coffee in hand, face completely neutral, while ominous dramatic music played underneath.

It had 200,000 views.

Someone was in that hallway.

Someone filmed Miles leaving.

Someone has 847,000 followers and a verified badge.

Someone is definitely in this guild.

In the comments, pinned by the creator:

For anyone asking:

yes I live here. No I will not elaborate. Yes she is as good as the internet says. I have been telling you all for months. — Morty

He lives here.

Morty Calvarium lives in this guild.

I have apparently met him and have no memory of it.

He has 847,000 followers.

He filmed Miles.

Miles is going to find out about this video.

I need to find Morty before Miles does.

[Achievement Unlocked: Your Guild Has a Celebrity You Didn’t Know About. +0 EXP. +999 Chaos Points. The system suggests introducing yourself. The system notes you live in the same building.]

She was writing a message to Miles — there is something you need to know about before you find it yourself — when a different notification appeared. Not from the guild channel. Not from Aldous. From the quest board’s public registry, which flagged her account because she was a registered practitioner.

A new session request had come in through the official portal.

Category: Grief (Guild-Related).

The request field read:

I used to be the one they came to. I was good at it. I left for reasons I thought were good reasons. I have been watching from the outside for seven months. I think I made a mistake. I don’t know if they’d want me back. I don’t know if I deserve to ask. I’m not sure this is something therapy can fix. But I don’t know who else to ask.I’m not going to give my name yet. I hope that’s okay.— Anonymous

Livia read it twice.

I used to be the one they came to.

Left for reasons I thought were good reasons.

Seven months.

Watching from the outside.

She looked at the request for a long time.

Then she wrote back, through the portal:

That’s okay. Take your time. I’m here when you’re ready. — L.Q.

She closed the portal.

I used to be the one they came to.

Seven months.

I arrived eight months ago.

She opened her notepad to a fresh page and wrote, very carefully, at the top:

Someone who used to do what I do. Someone who left one month before I arrived. Someone who is watching. Someone who misses them.

I don’t know who this is yet.

I think I might, though.

She closed the notepad.

She sent the message to Miles: There is something you need to know about before you find it yourself. It’s about a video. Come find me.

Then she sat with the two notifications—the video and the anonymous request—and the particular feeling of a day that had contained more than she had expected.

Tomorrow.

I will sort all of this tomorrow.

Today has been enough.
