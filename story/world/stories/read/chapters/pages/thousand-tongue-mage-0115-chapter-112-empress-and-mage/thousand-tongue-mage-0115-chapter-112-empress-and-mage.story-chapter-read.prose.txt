It was strange, Zora thought, how quickly a city could decide what to call itself when it wanted to keep living.

A mere month ago, it’d been the ‘Divine’ Capital in the mouths of trembling courtiers and undead nobles. Tonight, it was simply Salaqa Ik’Balam—a capital city reduced from godhood to something far, far more honest. It was now a city that admitted it could bleed. The Salaqa Region and the old Salaqa Ik’Balam north of the capital had been given to the Nohoch Lord, the younger brother to the late Salaqa Lord, so the new Salaqa Ik’Balam could remain unique as the bastion of the Attini Empire.

It was a good and simple name. Nothing fancy. The same couldn’t be said of the festivities, though. The last hammers and scaffolds had gone quiet since an hour ago, and in their place came drums and flutes that danced like butterflies in summer air. Thousands of feet stamped. Thousands of voices laughed, cried, sang, and danced across the city.

Nobody ever said a coronation ceremony shouldn't be fancy.

Where Zora stood on the elevated dais at the base of the new ‘Earthen Temple’ with his staff in his hand, wind dragged up the smell of roasted fungi skewers and sweetened maize from the celebrating food districts. He smelled incense burning in clay bowls from the smaller praying temples. He heard the massive banners fluttering in the wind across the hundreds and thousands of roofs across the city, and while he couldn’t see the crest stitched onto them, he felt he had an idea which household’s crest they were flying.

Why do we have to be standing here while the rest of the city’s having fun?” Ifas muttered behind him, standing at attention alongside Enki and a dozen other Outer Regional Lords upon the royal dais.

“Just let the people have it. We’re not locals anyways,” Zora replied. “In the Tlahuacan Valleys, they say when the river recedes, the village sings not because it is now safe, but because it remembers it was afraid. Were you ever afraid for the Attini Empire much in the first place?”

“... You’ve got a saying for everything.”

“Why, yes, I do have a saying for everything.”

It wasn’t just them standing upon the royal dais before the Earthen Temple who were thickly dressed in layered regalia, adorned in ceremonial necklaces heavy with chitin beads and polished bones. The twenty Spore Knights standing below the dais were also fully armored and stoic, sawtooth greatswords stabbed into the ground as they waited for the royal procession to arrive—and on that front, Zora did share Ifas’ frustration a little.

The new Empress’ carriage was taking its time winding through the city from the northernmost earthen wall to the Earthen Temple, and it didn’t seem like it was in a hurry, so all of them on the royal dais simply had to keep standing and waiting for it to arrive.

How does the coronation ceremony go again?

‘From the mouth of the stone jaws she comes, dawn-thread bound in gold and maize.

‘The city exhales. Drums wake the streets, and shells cry to the sun.

‘Her carriage rolls on living hands. Petals bruise the road. Old women lift ash and prayer, children lift dust and laughter. At the temple’s feet she descends, where earth kneels to sky, crowned by voices, sealed by footsteps.

‘She enters as Empress—and the city follows behind her.’

The royal procession was a parade, in essence, for the people of the empire to give their blessings and respects to the new thronetaker. Tonight, there were hundreds of thousands of them taking the streets, cheering and waving and praying for the health of their new Empress. Judging by the uproar of sounds slowly weaving through the city, Zora could vaguely tell where the royal procession currently was—almost here at the Earthen Temple—but he could also vaguely tell there were plenty of strangers mixed in amongst the crowd.

He heard thick, weighted steps. Too measured, not local. He heard the rasp of massive fur and beetle-chitin coats, heavy layers that didn’t belong to warm capital air. He heard men who breathed as if they were used to thin mountains and colder fronts. He heard others who wore almost nothing at all—shirtless bodies with their skin scarred into maps, tattoos dense enough to be armor, their laughter too throaty and raspy in the wrong places.

Scouts and spies, he decided. Observers from other fronts who wanted to see whether the Attini Empire had truly put a new spine in its back.

But they won’t dare to do anything to interrupt the procession.

All I have to do is keep an ear on them.

The sound of the procession finally changed as it rounded the final corner and entered the long, straight avenue that faced the Royal Temple.

The cheers across the city stopped breaking against walls and instead flowed forward in a single, unified surge, rolling like a living tide toward the temple. The royal carriage. It was a massive stone vehicle carved with ant charms and layered banners meant to flaunt lineage instead of wealth. Four giant silver ants pulled it forward with Machi—the new Royal Attendant—at the reins, while the new Empress-to-be herself stood upon the roof of the carriage, lifting her hand to the crowd following her.

Kita Salaqa was garbed in ceremonial robes Zora had never seen her in. Feathers and embedded beads swished and clinked with every little movement she made, and that was intentional. To the commoner, the Empress must be the brightest, most dazzling person in the room.

When the carriage reached the base of the Earthen Temple—stopping right before the elevated dais where all of the empire’s most important figures were gathered—Machi stepped off the driver’s seat first and offered her arm to the new Empress-to-be. The new Empress-to-be accepted. She stepped off the carriage, careful not to trample on her own robes, and then walked to the dais before kneeling without being prompted.

The swearing-in began with a shallow stone basin. One of the Spore Knights brought it forward, filled with darkened water mixed with ground obsidian and crushed chitin powder. Kita placed her hands into it and waited until the surface stilled. Machi presented the ceremonial blade next, and Kita drew it across her palm just enough to let blood fall into the basin.The mixture hissed faintly.

Blood offered, not taken.

As Kita stood and raised her left palm to the sky, each of the Outer Regional Lords upon the dais took turns speaking, calling on the earth, the ancestors, and the royal duties the Empress was beholden to. Kita answered each vow clearly, but Zora didn’t follow every word of the formal oaths. Promises of protection and rules blended together in his ears as they always did—what mattered was the end.

When Machi finally placed the crown of the Empress upon her head—an obsidian crown made of forged ant chitin—she turned and faced Salaqa Ik’Balam proudly.

“For the Attini Empire!”

The response came back instantly. Hundreds of thousands of voices roared together, shaking the earth beneath the city, and as the sound washed over the night, Kita glanced back at Zora.

In those robes with blood still drying on her palms, she truly did look like a proper Empress now, but…

Four hours before the coronation ceremony.

At Zora and Enki’s behest, Kita had long since emptied one of the grand bedchambers in the Earthen Temple and stripped it of anything soft. No tapestries, no incense bowls, and no cushions. What remained was bare stone and a single, dominating presence in the center of the room: a giant block of mist-releasing ice that made the air feel like winter had crawled into the temple. It never stopped hissing. Cryogenic mist kept leaking from hairline cracks that threatened to widen every passing hour.

Inside the ice, Eria still slept.

Enki placed his palms against the ice, reinforcing it and burying her behind such thick cold that Zora couldn’t quite hear her. Then again, the ice was slowing her heartbeat to a crawl. He shouldn’t be surprised he could barely detect her presence.

“... This will hold for another week,” Enki finally said, stepping back once he sealed all the cracks with his cryogenic mist. “After that, it will thaw again.”

Zora, Ifas, and Sora already knew that much. They hadn’t spent three months with Eria in the northeastern regions for nothing. Kita and Machi, however, still looked up at the ice with bitter expressions.

“Is there truly no way we can cure her of her affliction?” Kita asked softly, placing one palm on the ice. “Can we not return her to a normal human girl again?”

Zora tilted his head at Ifas, who was leaning against the doorway with his arms folded. “The Hayawari are the only people with natural resistance against the mutations caused by consuming bioarcanic essence. I hear the Rampaging Hinterland Front has researchers working on a medicine—an inoculation, more accurately—using Hayawari blood as a base. Their goal is to eventually create a medicine that can stop people from getting afflicted either accidentally or intentionally, but… it will take a while.”

“Why?”

“Because the Hayawari are rare. They’re supposedly more common in the Hellfire Caldera Front, but good luck getting men who live their lives in perpetual summer to go to a place in perpetual winter.” He shook his head slowly. “As things stand, I hear it will be at least four or five decades before they can complete their research on Hayawari blood.”

“And even then, Eria cannot stay in the empire,” Machi chimed in. “She is a Grafting Bug Afflicted. News of the siege has spread far and wide, and rumors of humans capable of grafting bug parts to themselves are circulating across the continent. No doubt the other fronts are already at least aware of her, and they will want her for research, defence, or leverage. The Attini Empire is not on gentle terms with the other fronts. As long as Eria remains here, she will be harassed. You will be harassed, my lady.”

Kita didn’t deny it, but her jaw clenched hard enough that Zora heard her teeth grind.

“Three to four decades…” Kita turned to look at Enki glumly. “But you can’t afford to stay with her that long just to keep reapplying the ice to her, right? After all, you’re… you’re—”

Enki looked at her sternly. “I can.”

“... You can?”

He raised his left ring finger. “I can leave one of my clones behind to stay with her, and all I have to do is take control of the clone once a week and reapply the ice. It does not matter where my real body is on the continent.”

“Really?”

“Yes.”

“And,” Sora popped in, lifting her quill. “I did a little digging into little Eria’s background using a few notes and papers I managed to salvage from that lab. I believe I might—”

“You what?” Kita’s fury sharpened so fast it almost hurt Zora’s ears. “You kept notes from that lab?”

“Just copies,” Sora corrected quickly. “Not to worry, though. I’ll hand them over to you later so you can destroy them yourself, but my point is…” She lifted her notebook and flipped through the pages before showing it to all of them. “I found out where Eria came from. I found her hometown.”

All of them immediately leaned in and squinted at the page. Zora, of course, didn’t bother. He couldn’t read it anyways.

“That’s…” Kita began.

“A small town in the Sharaji Desert north of the Attini Empire,” Sora finished. “It isn’t within the purview of any one of the Six Swarmsteel Fronts, and the desert’s a pocket that gets ignored because it’s inconvenient to claim. If we move Eria out fast and without fanfare—tonight, even—she can vanish from the Attini Empire without anybody noticing she’s gone. I believe the townsfolk of her hometown would be glad to take her back in.”

“And if one of Enki’s clones can stay there in disguise to maintain the ice…” Kita murmured, finger on her chin, “it’s unlikely she’ll ever be discovered by anyone looking to take advantage of her?”

Sora gave her a thumbs up.

Apprehension settled into Kita’s face instantly—the thought of being separated from Eria certainly pulled at her in a way duty couldn’t easily cut through—but Zora knew the truth, and he knew she knew it too.

“The cruelty of the Grafting Bug Project is not something the world should ever see repeated,” he said. “Eria cannot be a precedent. She must be the first—and the last—human to carry the essence of the grafting bug.”

Kita turned back around to stare at the ice, jaw tight.

Then she leaned in, resting her forehead against the frozen surface, and for a long moment she simply stayed like that, breathing softly.

“… Alright,” she whispered. “We’ll move her.”

Enki dipped his head. Machi and Ifas followed. Sora nodded, already flipping through her notes and talking about routes and establishing contact with the townsfolk, while Zora stayed silent about the lone tear slipping down her cheek.

Some grief deserved privacy.

And as Kita glanced back at him—the Attini Empire roaring her name as she did—she gave him a small, sincere smile.

Zora smiled back.

She truly looked like a proper Empress now, but after everything they’d done together the past year, he wished the child who chose the hard path at every turn of her life had a little bit more time to live as a child.

Nevertheless, Kita Salaqa was the Empress of the Attini Empire, and just for tonight, Zora bowed as her Magicicada Mage.
