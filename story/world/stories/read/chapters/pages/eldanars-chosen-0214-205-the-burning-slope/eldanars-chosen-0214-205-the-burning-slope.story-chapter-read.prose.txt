Fighting on the flat, freezing mud of the sinkhole floor had been a test of sheer, immovable endurance. Climbing the newly formed, highly unstable thirty-foot slope of shattered limestone, however, was a brutal, chaotic scramble for basic survival.

The massive, jagged boulders that Kaelen and the human geomancer had sheared from the cliff face were loose. Every single step required intense, exhausting focus. Heavy steel boots slipped wildly on the wet stone, sending showers of loose gravel and foul-smelling dirt cascading back down the ramp. To make matters worse, the intense, lingering heat from Brett’s cascading plasma spell had turned the entire slope into a suffocating, steaming oven.

"Keep moving!" Josh roared from the very back of the retreating line.

He was acting as the rearguard, standing shoulder to shoulder with the exhausted tank of the Iron Gauntlet party. They were walking backwards up the steep incline, their heavy steel shields raised to block the narrow path.

The undead horde was completely relentless. The mindless dungeon spawn and corrupted native beasts ignored the searing heat of the superheated rocks. They scrambled wildly over the burning, blackened remains of their own front ranks, their glowing green eyes locked entirely on the retreating humans.

A heavily corrupted forest cat, its flesh completely burned away by the residual magical fire to reveal blackened bone, launched itself up the slope. It cleared a massive limestone boulder in a single, terrifying leap, aiming directly for Josh’s throat.

Josh did not have the physical leverage to swing his longsword on the steep incline. He simply raised his shield, catching the burning skeletal cat mid-air. The impact shoved him dangerously backward. His steel boots slid a terrifying six inches down the loose gravel before finding purchase against a solid tree root. He shoved his shield forcefully outward, throwing the snapping, burning beast back down into the swirling steam below.

"I am slipping!" the Iron Gauntlet tank yelled to his left, his voice laced with pure panic.

The massive man was drained. His knees were shaking violently under the immense weight of his dented rectangular shield and heavy armour. He missed a step, his heavy boot sliding off a slick, angled rock. He fell hard onto his chest, crying out as the heated stone burned directly through his padded gambeson.

"Get up!" Josh bellowed, reaching out with his sword hand to grab the heavy leather straps across the man's back. He hauled the towering tank back to his feet with a terrifying surge of raw, physical strength, his own shoulder screaming in protest from the earlier blow dealt by the Bone Goliath. "Do not stop! The fire is dying down!"

Further up the slope, the rest of the rescue squad was physically hauling the critically wounded up the steep incline. Ronald had sheathed his twin hunting daggers entirely. The lithe beastkin was using his impossible agility to navigate the loose boulders, practically dragging Elara up the ramp by her good arm. The injured skirmisher was completely silent, her face the colour of chalk, focused on putting one foot in front of the other. Two rogues were supporting the Iron Gauntlet cleric and mage between them, practically carrying the exhausted casters up the treacherous slope.

"Almost there!" Perberos called down from the ridge.

The elven scout was kneeling right on the lip of the sinkhole, his silver eyes narrowed in concentration. He was firing a relentless volley of heavy steel-tipped broadheads down the slope, picking off the fastest, most agile ghouls that managed to slip past Josh and the tank.

Josh took another grinding step backward, his chest heaving with ragged, desperate gasps. The air was incredibly thin, completely choked with thick rock dust and the foul, oily smoke of burning necrotic flesh. He deflected a rusted spear thrust from a scrambling skeletal warrior, using the edge of his shield to shatter the creature's arm before kicking it down the incline.

The slope was acting as a natural bottleneck. The horde was vast, but the ramp was relatively narrow. The undead were actively tripping over the massive, jagged boulders and the burning corpses of their kin, significantly slowing their terrifying momentum. It was the only reason they had not been completely overrun.

Suddenly, Josh felt a cool, biting wind wash over the back of his neck.

The suffocating heat of the slope vanished, replaced instantly by the freezing, ambient temperature of the corrupted forest. He took one final, heavy step backwards, his steel boots leaving the loose, shifting gravel of the ramp and finding solid, moss-covered earth.

He had reached the top.

Josh immediately spun around, taking several rapid steps back from the treacherous edge to ensure he did not trigger a secondary collapse. The Iron Gauntlet tank stumbled up right behind him, instantly dropping his massive, ruined rectangular shield into the dirt and collapsing entirely onto his back, gasping wildly for the freezing air.

The rescue squad was scattered across the lip of the ridge, catching their breath in the dark forest. They were a battered, bloody, and completely exhausted collection of fighters.

Josh sheathed his electrified longsword, the raw blue lightning fading with a sharp crackle of ozone, as the rangers took his place on the lip of the wall, firing down into the mass attempting to climb the slope. He raised the visor of his helmet, spitting a mouthful of hot, metallic blood onto the dead moss. He looked across the small clearing and locked eyes with Brett.

His friend was leaning heavily against the trunk of a dead pine tree. Brett looked absolutely terrible. He was completely pale, his skin sporting a sickly, greyish tint that spoke of severe, dangerous mana depletion. He was hunched inward, wrapping his heavy fur cloak tightly around his shoulders as if trying to physically hold himself together. He pushed his non-existent spectacles up his nose with a trembling, dark red drake-scale glove.

"I knew you had not abandoned us," Josh stated. His voice was incredibly hoarse, barely more than a ragged rasp, but it carried absolute, unwavering conviction.

Brett offered a weak, entirely exhausted smirk. "It was a purely practical decision, Josh. The logistical nightmare of finding a replacement meat-shield of your exact density was simply too much for me to handle."

Josh let out a short, painful bark of laughter, instantly regretting it as his bruised ribs flared with sharp agony. "Good to know my utility remains high."

"Sit down before you fall down," Carcan ordered, stepping smoothly between the two friends.

The elven healer was a vision of absolute, serene calm amidst the chaotic, bloody aftermath. Her pale green and white travel robes were remarkably clean, completely untouched by the freezing black mud that coated the melee fighters. Her silver hair remained perfect in its tight plait. She carried her wooden staff in her left hand, the large amber crystal at its peak glowing with a warm, pulsing, comforting light that pushed back the suffocating darkness of the trees.

Josh did not argue. He unclasped the heavy leather straps of his dented steel shield, letting the massive piece of metal drop heavily to the forest floor. He sank down onto a protruding tree root, leaning his head back against the rough bark. Every single muscle in his body felt like it had been beaten with an iron hammer.

Carcan did not ask where he was injured. She could clearly see the deep, jagged gash across his left cheek and the unnatural, painful way he was holding his left shoulder after the Goliath's devastating strike.

She knelt gracefully in the freezing mud beside him. She raised her right hand, her elegant fingers glowing brightly with concentrated, pure golden holy magic. She pressed her hand gently over the terrible wound on his face.

Instead of speaking an incantation, Carcan began to hum.

The melody was incredibly soft, a gentle, swaying rhythm that sounded like a slow waltz. It cut through the harsh, ragged breathing of the exhausted fighters and the distant, clicking shrieks of the undead horde below.

“Bind the flesh and seal the bone, Drive the winter from your blood. Let the golden river flow, Washing clean the blackened mud.”

The effect was instantaneous and profound. The golden light flared brightly, feeling like the warm, midday sun pressing directly against Josh's freezing skin. The jagged edges of the deep cut on his cheek began to actively knit together, the bleeding stopping completely in a matter of seconds. The raw, throbbing agony in his bruised ribs began to recede, replaced by a soothing, dull warmth.

“Rest your heavy, weary head, Let the rising dawn awake, Weaving light in golden thread, Mending every bitter ache.”

Carcan shifted her glowing hand down to his left shoulder. She poured a concentrated burst of holy mana deep into the joint, forcibly repairing the torn muscle fibres and realigning the heavy bone. Josh let out a long, shuddering breath as the terrifying numbness in his arm vanished, replaced by the familiar, heavy ache of normal exhaustion.

She gave him a gentle, reassuring nod before rising smoothly to her feet. She immediately moved on to the exhausted Iron Gauntlet tank lying in the dirt, her soft, melodic humming continuing seamlessly as she began to knit the terrible burns on his chest.

"Do not get too comfortable," Perberos warned from the edge of the sinkhole.

The elven ranger was still kneeling in the dirt, his longbow fully drawn. He had stopped firing down the ramp, his luminous silver eyes fixed intently on the dark, rotting canopy of the forest surrounding the opposite side of the gorge.

"The bottleneck on the ramp is holding for now, and the terrain is helping," Perberos reported, his voice entirely flat and devoid of emotion. "The fire has died, but the rubble is unstable. They are struggling to climb over the corpses of their own front line."

"That sounds like excellent news," Brett muttered, rubbing his temples slowly.

"It is a temporary reprieve," Ronald corrected sharply, stepping out from the shadows near the treeline.

The feline beastkin's short-cropped ears swivelled constantly to track the ambient sounds of the corrupted forest. His jade-green eyes were narrowed into dangerous slits. He pointed a sharp claw towards the thick, thorny briars to their immediate right.

"The Crypt Weavers are not stupid," Ronald stated, his tail lashing restlessly behind him. "They realise the ramp is a choke point. They have stopped sending their fastest units up the rubble. They are directing the corrupted native beasts to scale the walls of the sinkhole further down the gorge. They are actively circling the rim."

Josh stood up from the tree root, testing the repaired joint in his shoulder. The dull ache remained, but the structural integrity was back. He picked his heavy steel shield up from the dirt, sliding his arm back through the leather straps.

"How long until they flank and cut off our route back to camp?" Josh asked, looking at the beastkin.

"Five minutes, perhaps less if they have Crypt Ghouls in the mix," Ronald calculated instantly, his feline instincts processing the terrifying mathematics of the dark forest. "We need to run. We cannot establish a defensive perimeter here. We are completely out of position, and half this squad is out of mana. We break for the camp immediately."

A heavy, oppressive silence fell over the gathered adventurers. The pure, adrenaline-fuelled high of surviving the sinkhole completely evaporated, instantly replaced by the grim, undeniable reality of their situation.

They had to run a mile through a dark, highly corrupted forest, navigating knee-deep freezing mud and tangled roots, while being actively hunted by a massive, tireless horde.

"There is a significant flaw in that plan," Kaelen, the dwarven Stoneweaver, pointed out. She was leaning very heavily on her massive granite staff, her broad chest heaving. She wiped a thick layer of grime and sweat from her brow. "We cannot run. Not fast enough to outpace corrupted wolves."

"She is right," the human geomancer agreed. He was sitting in the mud, his nose still bleeding sluggishly. "Shaping that bedrock completely emptied our reserves. I can barely walk in a straight line, let alone sprint through a dark forest."

Brett did not say anything, but he looked down at his trembling hands, silently agreeing with the earth shapers. The mages were wearing heavy robes and thick cloaks. They did not possess the physical stamina or the enhanced agility of the rogues and rangers, nor did they have the raw, brutish endurance of the heavy fighters. In a dead sprint through a swamp, they would inevitably fall behind.

"Yeah, that plan won’t work," the human mage wearing deep blue robes stated, his voice completely devoid of panic. He looked at Ronald with calm acceptance. "If we try to move as a single unit, the casters will slow the entire column down. The flanking horde will catch the rearguard, and we will all be slaughtered in the mud. You have to leave us behind."

"No," Josh stated flatly, his voice echoing loudly in the small clearing.

The blue-robed mage looked at the heavy fighter, clearly surprised. "It is the only logical choice to ensure the survival of everyone else."

"I do not care about the logic," Josh growled, his storm-grey eyes hardening into cold, uncompromising steel. He stepped closer to the exhausted casters, his heavy armour clanking loudly. "You just exhausted your entire mana pools to tear a hole in the earth to save my life. I am not leaving you here to be butchered in the dark."

"It is not a matter of sentiment, Josh," Ronald argued softly, his ears flattening slightly. The beastkin clearly hated the mathematics of the situation, but his pragmatic training demanded he voice the harsh truth. "If they cannot keep the pace, they become a fatal liability to the entire squad."

"Then we change the pace," Josh countered instantly, looking directly at the beastkin. He turned his gaze to Brett, Kaelen, and the other exhausted casters. "You run as fast as you physically can. If you stumble, if your legs give out, or if you fall behind the main column, you do not stop and accept your fate. You yell."

Josh looked over at the massive Iron Gauntlet tank, who was slowly pushing himself up from the mud, and the other heavy fighters in the squad.

"If they fall behind," Josh ordered, pointing a heavy steel gauntlet at the mages, "we scoop them up. You throw them over your shoulder, you drag them by their robes, you do whatever it takes. We are the heavy lifters. We carry the weight. Nobody gets left behind in this forest. Is that completely understood?"

The Iron Gauntlet tank let out a low, rumbling grunt of agreement, retrieving his bent rectangular shield from the dirt. "They built the stairs. The least we can do is offer a ride home."

Ronald looked at the stubborn, heavily armoured fighters, then looked at the exhausted casters. The beastkin let out a long, frustrated hiss, but a faint smirk briefly touched his fanged mouth.

"Fine," Ronald snapped, taking absolute command of the formation. "But we do not linger here a second longer. Rogues and rangers take the front. You clear the path and cut through the brush. Mages and wounded in the centre. Heavy fighters take the rear. If you have to carry someone, you keep moving. Do not stop to fight unless we have to."

"They are cresting the ramp," Perberos warned sharply, drawing his bowstring back in a single, fluid motion.

The elven scout was correct. The bottleneck had finally broken. The sheer pressure of the hundreds of undead pushing from behind had forced the front ranks over the difficult terrain. The first skeletal hounds and heavily armoured ghouls were now scrambling wildly over the lip of the sinkhole, their claws scraping loudly against the solid limestone rim.

"Give them a parting gift," Ronald ordered, looking at the mages. "Clear the immediate drop zone so we have a head start."

Brett pushed himself away from the pine tree. He did not have the mana left for a massive, sustained torrent of plasma, but he had enough for a single, focused burst.

He stepped up to the edge of the pit beside Kaelen and the blue-robed mage. The three casters did not need to coordinate their actions with words. They were seasoned veterans.

The blue-robed mage raised his wand, firing a concentrated, violent blast of raw, kinetic arcane energy directly into the centre of the emerging undead pack. The invisible force hit the ghouls like a solid wall of moving iron, violently throwing them backward and grouping them tightly together at the very top of the ramp.

Kaelen slammed her granite staff into the ground. She did not shape the bedrock this time. She simply forced a dozen massive, jagged spikes of solid limestone to violently erupt from the earth directly beneath the clustered undead. The stone spikes impaled the ghouls, completely pinning them in place and creating a physical barricade across the top of the ramp.

Brett finished the combination. He snapped his fingers, summoning a small, highly compressed sphere of roaring white fire. He threw it directly into the centre of the impaled, clustered monsters.

The explosion was deafening. The sheer thermal shockwave shattered Kaelen’s stone spikes into deadly shrapnel, incinerating the pinned undead and violently blowing the charging ranks immediately behind them back down the steep slope.

A massive, thick cloud of dust and steam instantly completely obscured the exit, buying them the precious seconds they needed.

"Run!" Ronald screamed, turning his back on the sinkhole and sprinting directly into the dark, corrupted forest.

The entire squad broke into a desperate, chaotic sprint. The heavy clanking of steel plate armour, the ragged, exhausted gasps of the wounded, and the rhythmic thudding of boots filled the freezing air. They plunged straight back into the suffocating, pitch-black nightmare of the trees, racing frantically against the closing jaws of the flanking horde.
