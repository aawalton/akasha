Ferris’ missing leg hurt.

Recently, phantom leg pain was frustratingly common. He’d trained for years to fight, only to lose his leg before ever getting the chance. Some days he couldn’t decide what was worse: losing his purpose, or his leg. Today it was the leg.

“All strapped in?” asked his father.

Ferris nodded. His father, Alan, went back to triple check everything anyway. The man knew Ferris was secure, he had tied the harness himself. It’s probably just nerves, Ferris thought, clenching the straps. I just wish he hid it better.

Life on the frontier was hard. At least, that’s what everyone said. Having been born here, it was all he’d ever known. The deadly Wild pressed in from every side, and bastions were the only safe areas. Unfortunately, they were few and far between, which meant most people never traveled. Ferris had never even stepped one foot outside the bastion, even back when he had two legs.

“Are you sure about this?” His father asked, again. “It isn’t too late. We could find another way.”

Ferris smiled at him and shook his head. “No regrets, I want this.” He was leaving home, leaving his family, all for a chance to learn magic that could restore his leg.

Traveling between bastions was dangerous. The most direct path of going through the Wild would be a death sentence. Instead, travelers would open a rift portal between two bastions. The rift offered a safer path than the Wild, but only by comparison. Rifts were full of monsters, after all.

Rifts were strange; each one different. You never knew exactly what lurked inside. The same route might have rocky hills one trip, and then the next dense jungle. The creatures living within would be equally random, with everything from normal plants and animals to strange and diverse magical monsters.

“Drink every last drop,” his mother, Marris, said as she handed him the water breathing potion. The stern woman watched like a hawk as he drank it. He tried not to gag at the taste of sour fish and forced it down. It would keep him alive underwater while it lasted. She gave a firm nod once he finished, showing none of father’s nerves. She’d always been the rock the family relied on.

Ferris blinked the tears away, pretending they came from drinking the potion. They had all cried the night before. Now was the time for strength.

His two sisters stood beside him, grasping each other’s hands for support. Both tried to match mother’s stoic strength. Ellis, his older sister, did a reasonable job. Ferra, on the other hand, failed miserably. He didn’t blame her. She’d never quite escaped being treated as the baby of the family, being born almost six years after him.

Growing up the middle child had always felt like the worst of both worlds—at least to Ferris. Never trusted with anything like Ellis, yet never able to get away with what Ferra could... As his eyes met theirs, he gave them each a small nod. One final goodbye, and a promise to see them again one day.

Ferris handed his mother the empty potion bottle, trying to forget the taste. Travel through the rifts to and from their home—Bluereef Bastion—was more difficult than most. While rifts were inherently random, the bastions imposed certain predictable traits. Almost all rifts leaving Bluereef started underwater.

His father finally stopped messing with the harness, and Uncle Winton finished tying the other end of the rope. His uncle, as the only close family coming with him, would pull Ferris behind as they swam.

A sharp crack came from the gate, followed by a low hum. Their group leader had finished opening the rift, so it was time to go. Ferris had said his goodbyes already, but his family each gave one a last hug. Pulling himself away, he followed Uncle Winton up to the gate, trailing behind with what little slack the rope gave him.

Although Ferris was missing his left leg, he had grown accustomed to using a crutch the last several months. He had trained to fight for most of his life, so he moved quickly even with the crutch. He was as fit as could be asked of a seventeen-year-old boy. If he hadn’t lost his knee, he might have been able to use a peg to get around… but he had lost the knee.

They joined the rest of the team in front of the gate. Ferris held the rope tight while Winton and the others lifted spears. The gate looked like a distorted portal, surrounded by a large stone arch. Water rippled on the other side, as expected. The surface of the portal was strange, somehow keeping the water from flooding out yet allowing people to cross over.

Only five people could use this gate. He didn’t know the details, but the magic that opened rift portals determined how many could enter before the gate closed. His uncle and the other three members of their group were all experienced delvers—strong fighters that hunted monsters and treasure in the rifts—but this would be a difficult trip. Even if he had both legs, Ferris would be dead weight, and his uncle would be guarding him. It was up to the other three to do the work of five.

His uncle and the others had cultivated their bodies to the second tier, while Ferris had only just taken a single step into the first. Ferris was still as weak as a normal human, while they were superhuman. They were stronger, faster, and tougher by a large margin—although monsters in the rift would be even more so.

The team was led by Valda, a tall woman with short red hair. Long hair would tangle in the water or get in the way of a helmet, so most delvers in Bluereef Bastion kept it short or tied down. She was young to be so strong—stronger even than his uncle—everything Ferris had dreamed of being.

The other two members were the brothers: Dan and Dean. It was uncommon for two siblings to both be cultivators. Barely one in ten that tried were able to step into the first tier… and everyone tried. A single family member beating the odds was celebrated, but two siblings making it was rarely seen. Then, many died in the rifts, but Dan and Dean had survived together this long. Both older than Uncle Winton, they were veteran delvers with years of experience hunting in the rifts around Bluereef Bastion.

Cultivators had many mystical skills and magic that would help them survive—like the potion Ferris drank—but fighting underwater required specific expertise. Many strong warriors had fallen to weaker monsters simply because they were not trained to fight in water.

The spears the delvers carried were special; bonded weapons embedded with water cores granting magic. They were the specialty of his clan—aptly named the Bluereef clan after the bastion—and the reason they could survive the rifts around here. The spears had special abilities to unleash powerful attacks, while the water magic let them move and breath underwater. Ferris should have bonded his own spear after he reached the first tier of cultivation, but losing his leg had prevented that…

Valda entered the rift first, followed by the brothers. Ferris pulled his goggles down over his eyes, the tinted glass turning the world a shade of blue. They restricted his field of view, but it was better than nothing. He took measured, deep breathes as he tried to look through the portal.

“Hold on tight boy,” were the last words he heard before Winton picked him up and jumped through the portal after them.

Suddenly submerged in water, Ferris held his breath and closed his eyes, not yet trusting his eye protection. The others had water magic to protect their eyes and see in the dark waters, but Ferris would have to rely on the goggles.

Tentatively, he opened his eyes and looked around. They were in a small cave with no natural light. Valda, shining like a beacon in the darkness, had a small glowing stone attached to her. Normally delvers would only rely on magic to see, but this light was for Ferris’ benefit. It had the additional advantage of luring monsters away from him; he would not survive even a glancing blow from a tier two monster.

In the dim light, the four delvers floating around him had changed. Instead of the casual clothes they were wearing outside the rift, they were now covered in plates of shimmering metal. From head to toe, the full heavy armor covered every inch of them. Ferris hadn’t seen it happen, but he knew they had all activated abilities to instantly be armored for battle. Even their faces were covered, without even a slit to see through. They must rely on some ability to see, he thought, or maybe the helmet is enchanted? At least he could still tell them apart, each set of armor being slightly unique.

Normally it would be a terrible idea to swim in heavy armor, but cultivators weren’t normal. Either they had magical abilities, or the armor itself was enchanted. However they did it, the four swam gracefully, unbothered by the burden.

Water rushed by as his uncle pulled Ferris over to join the other three waiting at a hole in the wall. They exchanged some hand signs, and then spread out to guard.

This was the part Ferris feared most, his first breath. The potion usually worked, but he had never used one before. They were too expensive to use casually, and some people had problems with them. If he tried to breathe and failed, it would be impossible to stop choking while underwater.

‘Breathe now’ Uncle Winton signed in the water. Every child of Bluereef learned hand signs from a young age. Telepathic magic also existed, and the more experienced delvers used it regularly, but it had drawbacks and could be blocked. Hand signs were reliable—at least outside of the chaos of battle—despite being mundane.

They had gone over the plan extensively: Ferris would hold his breath until he got the signal, and then give confirmation once he could breathe. If he started choking, Winton was ready to get him out of the rift.

With a force of will, he inhaled. The water was brackish in his mouth, but somehow only air reached his lungs. It was the strangest feeling.

After a few tentative breaths, Ferris gave the ‘all good’ sign. Winton nodded calmly and turned to the hole. The four delvers, plus Ferris, entered one by one. Valda led the way, followed by Uncle Winton with Ferris in tow. The brothers covered the rear.

The darkness pressed in as they swam through the small—but long—passage. Fleeting glimpses of the light attached to Valda came in and out of view. Ferris had trained to crawl through tight places, but reality was worse than he'd imagined. His heart raced with each bend of the tunnel, barely able to see anything.

Fish occasionally bumped into him as they swam by, squeezing through any gap they could find. The rifts were full of life, entire ecosystems of mundane plants and animals living alongside the monsters. Ferris didn’t know where it all came from, or how so many different rifts could all exist around the same area. He’d been told it didn’t all magically get created and destroyed with the rift portals, but not much beyond that.

Moving through the tunnel, he soon lost track of time. The walls felt closer with each twist and bend. The rope pulled him relentlessly forward, not caring for his desire to stop and regain his bearings. Part of him wanted to just close his eyes and focus on something other than the tunnel pressing in from all sides.

But that wasn’t who he wanted to be. I need to be more than just baggage, he thought. I might be weak, but I should at least be aware and ready to react.

After what felt like an eternity, but was probably only ten to twenty minutes, they stopped at the opening of a new cave.

‘Clawlings, three tier two,’ Valda signed.

Ferris thought back to his lessons. Rifts contained an endless variety of monsters, but each area had common types. The clan did its best to categorize and teach about local monsters. Usually, the names were just a broad category for similar creatures. Even when they were more specific, a particular monster could have rare or special abilities. A delver had to be ready for anything.

Clawlings were a common—but vicious—monster. Squat four-legged amphibian scaled beasts; they swam through the water by kicking their powerful back legs. They were not very agile, but their sharp claws on each leg would rend through anything they could catch. Their strong legs allowed them to push off the walls for a sudden burst of speed through the water. Mostly an ambush predator, they liked to hide along walls and ceilings. A single tier two clawling would rip a new team of delvers to shreds… although that was true of any tier two monster.

Thankfully, Ferris’ group wasn’t new. With confidence based on experience and skill, the three delvers entered the cave. Winton and Ferris stayed back near the tunnel, only moving far enough to let the brothers pass.

Several yards out into open water, the delvers stopped and leveled spears. Valda raised her hand and shot a bright light into the middle of the room. He assumed she was attempting to draw the monsters out. Even with the light, Ferris still couldn’t see any sign of the beasts.

Suddenly, what had looked like three rocks started moving. The water muffled most sound, but Ferris watched small waves pulse out as each beast jumped off a wall. Faster than Ferris could follow, Valda and the brothers dodged. A normal human would have trouble maneuvering from a standstill underwater, but a small burst of propulsion shot out of each of their spears, pushing the delvers out of the way.

One on one, a clawling would overpower any of them. Thick scales on their front could deflect the delver’s spear, and powerful legs would push them until pinned against the wall. Meeting the charge head on was folly.

Instead, the three used their superior agility to swim around the clawlings. The nimble humans only attacked when it was three on one. Two of their spears would bounce off scales, but one would usually score a hit on a weak point: the monster’s softer belly or under one of the leg’s joints.

The clawlings were most dangerous when they reached a wall and could charge again. Turning in open water was difficult for the monsters and left them open to attack. With a little teamwork the clawlings could have covered for each other and been much more dangerous, but instead the dumb beasts attacked blindly. The delvers smoothly swam circles around them, hitting and then quickly retreating.

This continued for a little time, until one of the clawlings left itself a little too exposed. Valda unleashed a powerful thrust—boosted with a mystical skill from her bonded weapon—into its right leg, hobbling it. The three delvers then avoided the slowed monster to gang up on the other two.

Victory seemed only a matter of time as the clawlings took more hits. The injured one couldn’t catch up, and the delvers danced around the other two, inflicting more damage.

Frustrated, the injured clawling turned towards Winton and Ferris. They had not moved since the battle began, staying as inconspicuous as possible, yet the monster still decided to approach them. With one leg limp, it was unbalanced and slow. Still, it could move. Even if the beast only attacked Winton, Ferris might die from just being nearby. The difference between Ferris and a tier two monster was too great.

Ferris hunched himself up as his uncle leveled his spear. Should we run? He wondered. No, even hobbled it could probably pull itself through the tunnel faster than me. Glancing at his uncle, the man appeared to agree, showing no signs of retreating. If he were alone, Uncle Winton might be able to outrun the monster. Ferris wasn’t sure. Even if he could, the man would have to move too fast for Ferris. Either the rope would break, or he would. That was assuming he didn’t hit his head on a sharp turn first.

Right when the monster was halfway to them, Valda and the brothers broke off and sped towards them. The three delvers coordinated to approach from different angles. The hobbled clawling turned to face the attack, and was able to mostly defend itself, but Winton used the distraction to pull Ferris off to the side, slipping away.

The rest of the fight progressed smoothly; the superior teamwork and speed of the humans over the creature’s brute power won out. Winton slowly pulled Ferris away anytime a clawling got too close, but he was wary of going too far. There could be more monsters around, after all. Every move was a risk.

After the battle, the brothers Dan and Dean stood guard while Valda collected the bodies. She would place a hand on each corpse for a few seconds until it disappeared into her soul storage. Just like her armor had magically appeared, now the dead monsters were pulled into that same space in her soul. Thankfully it took less than a minute to gather everything, as the potion Ferris drank had a time limit.

Each rift was different, so they didn’t know how far they would have to travel underwater. Most teams would take at least an hour—often longer—to reach the surface, but they would explore and look for resources along the way. The potion should last at least twice that, but it wasn’t an exact duration. Uncle Winton had a way to track time, and they would try to return to Bluereef Bastion if it took too long.

Still, he thought, anything can happen. The way back could be blocked, or a monster too strong to fight could get in the way. Ferris shuddered, focusing on his breathing and not how it would feel when the potion ran out. Down here, walls on all sides. Unable to breathe. Trapped.

Shaking his head, Ferris was glad to see they were ready to move on. He looked forward, grasping for any distraction.

Their group swam through four more caves. Each tunnel had several branches, but the cultivators—except for Ferris—all had a pathfinding ability to lead the way. Two more groups of clawlings attempted to ambush them, but without success. Valda would sense them every time, and the team would deftly kill the creatures. Almost all rifts would have a wide variety of different monsters, but Ferris’ group stuck to the main path and didn’t see anything else.

At the entrance to the next cave, light pierced down from the top of the water. That is the surface! Ferris rejoiced. He’d been trying not to wonder if he would ever breathe real air again.

‘Clawling, one tier two’ Valda’s hand sign brought him back to reality. Looking around, for the first time Ferris quickly spotted it. There was only one, but it was twice the size of others he had seen. Even if two monsters shared the same tier, there could be a large difference in strength. A tier three would be suicide to confront. Luckily, while larger than any they had seen so far in this rift, Valda claimed this massive clawling was still only tier two. The beast wasn’t even attempting to hide, lounging on a large rock.

Somehow, its casual disregard for the ambush tactics used by the other clawlings unsettled Ferris. The way it just lay there, sharp claws glinting in the rays of light. Relaxed. It made his stomach churn.

Valda and the brothers approached slowly, surrounding it. He wasn’t sure if it was just the odd situation, but Ferris felt a physical pressure given off by the creature; like a constant low tremor that traveled through the water and into his bones. Real or just his imagination, the feeling slowly increasing in magnitude as the delvers advanced.

The beast sat still on its rock, watching the three delvers slowly approach. Valda and the other two stopped, expecting a powerful charge, but the beast was content to wait.

After a short standoff, Valda gave a quick signal to Dan. He nodded and swam further back. Valda and Dean moved in to cover him, while Dan’s posture changed to a throwing stance.

Like every child back home trying to be a delver, Ferris had learned everything he could about how the clan groups fought.

They wanted the beast to come away from the wall, he guessed, into open water. Approaching while it sat along on the floor or near a wall would expose them to a charge with no time to dodge. Since it won’t cooperate, he watched Dan’s stance, they are attacking from range to force it out.

Ferris knew that­ range attacks were common, but they didn’t match the martial style of Clan Bluereef. The clan had developed several abilities to cover this vulnerability, but they were only used when the situation demanded.

Water started swirling around Dan, building up intensity until it hit a crescendo of power. With a pulse of pressure, a translucent blue spear shot out of Dan’s real spear, jetting forward through the water. A vortex of force magically propelled the crystal-like spear as it raced towards the clawling.

While the attack appeared powerful, it was clear the monster was ready for it. The moment Dan launched his spear the beast leaped out of the way, directly at Valda. Prepared for the move, Valda dodged, deftly dancing to the left while Dean attacked from the side.

Right when the beast was passing by Valda, an explosion blasted out from its claws. The water pulsed and a mass of bubbles erupted. Valda and Dean were both hit by a shockwave, disoriented.

Dan was still out of position, unable to help after launching his attack. Valda and Dean quickly tried to recover from the shockwave, but not before the clawling was able to take advantage.

Rushing at Valda, the monster pulled its claws back to attack. Ferris held his breath, watching a buildup of red energy on the thing’s arms. Another special attack? The beast was too close. She tried to dodge, but there was no time. It swung at her—just as another crystal blue spear slammed into the joint of its left shoulder. The clawling’s blast went off prematurely. Valda was caught in a shockwave, going limp. At least she hadn’t been hit directly. Hopefully that mattered.

Ferris could feel the water vibrate as the monster screamed. It turned to face Uncle Winton, whom Ferris belated realized had just sent the crystal blue spear at it. The same kind of attack Dan had used, although this one hit. It was now embedded a foot into the beast’s shoulder. In what Ferris could only interpret as rage, the creature’s entire body began pulsing red. The water bubbled and swirled around it, distorting his view.

With powerful strokes—somehow not affected by the translucent spear sticking out of it—the monster swam towards them. Uncle Winton immediately pulled Ferris back up the tunnel. Alone, the man would likely be able to dodge or outmaneuver the creature in open water, but not while protecting Ferris. Instead, he chose to retreat up the tunnel.

At first Ferris wasn’t sure why his uncle had decided to flee up the tunnel now, and not against the earlier monster. It’s too big, he realized, it won’t fit! We can get away!

They made it a couple yards in before the beast slammed into the hole and started tearing through the walls. Ferris felt his earlier excitement die as he watched the tunnel collapse around it. Soon only the thing’s head and arms were visible through the chaos, reaching in further each time to pull more of the walls out.

Ferris held on as best he could, his uncle pulling them further away. Each of the clawling’s vicious slams as it widened the tunnel came with a burst of heat. His leg started to burn. The magic that allowed him to breathe water did nothing to cool the heated liquid, making each breath more painful than the last.

Then, all of a sudden, the beast’s head jerked in pain. It let out a scream that shook Ferris to the bone. With a mighty heave, it turned away and vanished.

Ferris watched for a few breathless moments, trying to peer through the muddy water. The tunnel behind him was dark, but some light from the cave pressed through the dirt. Occasional bright flashes of red let him know the fight was still happening. More of the walls collapsed in the monster’s wake, but at least it didn’t return.

Uncle Winton pulled him close, then carried him back towards the cave. The man used some kind of magic to push through, the water moving around to protect and buffer them.

Why risk returning? Ferris wondered, wanting to just stay in the tunnel. Does he fear getting separated from the group, trapped by a cave in? He didn’t know, and Uncle Winton wasn’t explaining.

As they emerged from the cloud of dirt back into the underwater cave, Ferris saw the giant clawling charging at Valda again. They must have attacked it from behind, when it was trying to chase us, he thought.

Valda and the brothers had repositioned themselves to meet the attack. As the clawling’s charge almost reached Valda, she dodged to the side. This time she was ready for the blast, using water magic similar to Uncle Winton. A precisely timed wave countered the shockwave, pushing her far enough away to evade the clawling’s follow up attack.

With the monster adrift, the three delvers circled and resumed their hit and run tactics. Uncle Winton pulled Ferris off to the side. The blue crystal spear the man had shot was gone. Ferris didn’t know if it had just fallen out or faded away to nothing.

The delvers danced in and out as the clawling released more blasts. The water was still getting hotter with each attack, to the point Ferris was feeling faint. He tugged on the rope to get his uncle’s attention and signed ‘heat.’

Ferris couldn’t see his uncle’s face through the helmet, but he saw the man clearly understand. I bet he barely notices the temperature, Ferris thought, how long will it take for me to reach his level of body enhancement?

Giving a small nod, Uncle Winton held his spear up and concentrated. Slowly, a current of cool water started to come from further up the tunnel, flowing around Ferris like a whirlpool. The swirling, muddy water further distorted his view.

Ferris floated there, at the bottom of the cave. Cool water surged around him as he watched the hazy shapes darting through the water, interjected with bright blasts of light that thundered vibrations through his body. This was what he had dreamed of all his life. To see the wonders of the rifts and rise above its challenges, both magnificent and terrifying.

The water nearby was now hot enough to boil, but cooler streams wrapped Ferris in a protective shell. Only Uncle Winton’s magic kept Ferris from being cooked alive. The others seemed fine, able to endure the more intense heat next to the monster.

One of the delvers—Ferris could no longer tell them apart through the violent water—scored a deep thrust to the creature’s body. It let out a final muffled roar and went dark, bright red glow fading to black. Two more spears made sure the thing was dead.

It took a few moments for Ferris’ heartrate to slow, as he waited to see if the battle was truly over. Two figures swam back towards him, revealing themselves as Dan and Dean once they were closer.

The brothers wordlessly stood guard around Uncle Winton and Ferris at the bottom, constantly vigilant for other threats as Valda stored the monster’s body.

Ferris was excited to leave the water, but his uncle waited patiently for Valda to go first. She poked her head above the surface far away from the shoreline, slowly moving forward until her feet could touch the ground. The turbulent, muddy water made seeing anything hard, and she soon faded out of sight. After half a minute, Uncle Winton turned to Ferris and signaled ‘all clear.’ Valda must have informed the group mentally.

Breaking through the waterline, Ferris had never been so happy to breathe air. I thought I was ready for this, he thought, but those endless dark, cramped tunnels… He shuddered, turning the motion into a shake of his head. Hopefully the others thought he was just shaking off the water.

Surrounding them was the light of the Wild, a soft glow that shone down from the rift’s barrier above. The Wild surrounded both bastions and the rifts on all sides—you couldn’t even dig under it.

The barrier would shine bright during the day and then dim at night in an endless cycle. It had been hard for Ferris to keep track of time underwater, but he guessed from the brightness of the Wild it had taken more than an hour.

“That wasn’t so bad now, was it?” Uncle Winton laughed, somehow having magically removed his helmet. The man cast another water spell, this time to dry and clean the two of them. Ferris was grateful, the cave water had been grimy and unpleasant, especially in the tunnels.

“Easy as pie,” Ferris lied. He had looked forward to his first delve for most his life—although he never thought he would be pulled through it. “That was amazing,” he admitted, thinking back to the fight. “Is it always that intense?”

“It’s never boring.” Valda walked up to them, also with her helmet gone. The side of her face was red and raw, scorched from the clawling’s blast through her armor. It looked unpleasant, but not debilitating. As a tier two cultivator, she would be healed within a day. “It should get easier from here,” she said. “The closer we get to Scalefort, the weaker the monsters will be.”

Scalefort Bastion was the closest city to Bluereef, and their destination. The city, and the bastion containing it, were both much larger than Bluereef. The rifts around it were also less dangerous. It took a lot of resources to grow the bastion and push the Wild back far enough for a city. Bluereef was only large enough for one moderate sized clan, but Scalefort was home to many larger and stronger groups.

In addition to people, Scalefort offered opportunity. The opportunity to heal Ferris’ leg. Otherwise, he would never have risked this journey.

While Clan Bluereef had several members with healing magic, none of them could regrow another person’s limb. When Ferris’ leg was bitten off, he had been rushed to one of the healers. They closed the wound and saved his life but couldn’t do anything more.

The problem was that regrowing another person’s limb was beyond most basic healing magic. It would take an extremely powerful or specialized ability to grow back his leg, which the healers in Bluereef did not have.

There were probably a few healers in Scalefort that could do it, but the Bluereef clan didn’t have the money to pay for such an expensive service. Especially not for a boy not yet bonded to his spear, Ferris bleakly thought.

Of course, the fact that Ferris had not yet bonded a weapon was also his saving grace. A bond always gave special abilities, but each cultivator could only have one bond. While magic that healed others was limited, there were easier bond abilities that could regrow one’s own limbs.

Unfortunately, knowing an ability existed was one thing, but acquiring it was very different. The Bluereef clan specialized in bonded spears embedded with a water core and a style of supporting water magic. This let them take advantage of the natural tendency for underwater rifts around their bastion, and the clan had extensive research on abilities the spear could provide. As far as the clan knew, water spear bonds didn’t give the ability Ferris needed.

The groups at Scalefort were not as specialized, and the larger ones had extensive libraries detailing many different bonds. Uncle Winton had tried to find one willing to sell the information on his last visit, but he was refused. Not like we were surprised, Ferris thought. Knowledge is power, and thus viciously guarded.

Winton had, however, secured a chance. One group that Bluereef had good relations with was willing to help Ferris on the condition that he agree to a retainer contract and participate in a research project. Thus, he was strapped along for a ride; off to sell his soul—or at least his time and service—for a leg. Someday he intended to walk home healed, a true delver.

Uncle Winton handed over Ferris’ crutch, which was pulled out of the man’s soul storage, and then untied the harness. Ferris pulled off the goggles and looked around, secure in his own ability to move around again. Above the water there was a path leading through a thick forest. Winton still stayed close to guard him, but Ferris could finally travel under his own power again.

As the group traveled along the path, Valda’s words proved prophetic; the remaining monsters were weaker. There were more of the amphibian clawlings, but they were not as tough as before.

The delvers fought much differently on land. Standing on the ground gave more leverage to thrust their spears, but less mobility than shooting around underwater. The weaker clawlings still relied on large leaps, but these monsters lacked the strength to overpower the humans like the earlier beasts.

No longer worried about the water breathing potion’s time limit, Valda and the others took their time carefully engaging every group of monsters. As before, each corpse was carefully stored away for later sale or trade in the bastion. Unlike the normal animals found in a rift, monster meat was poisonous to normal people. Still, monsters contained valuable parts that could be dissected and processed into magical resources or food for powerful cultivators.

Eventually, Valda stopped the group.

“This should be close enough,” she said. “We can open the portal to Scalefort here.”

Rift magic was universal enough that any cultivator could learn it, and everyone learned at least a basic escape ability before leaving the safety of a bastion—unless you were unbonded and being escorted like Ferris. If you got stuck in a rift without anyone to open a portal, the only way out was through the barrier to the Wild. That way is assured death, Ferris shuddered.

After a few minutes of concentration, Valda opened the portal in front of them. This one didn’t have the protective structure of a stone gate, so it pulsed around the edges. Touching the edge of a portal was a good way to lose a hand. Ferris didn’t feel like losing any more limbs, so he watched the edge carefully.

The delvers wasted no time. Once Valda had stabilized the portal, they all jumped through. As if a point of pride, Valda was the last one out—just as she’d been the first one in.

On the other side, Ferris took one last look back through the rift. He did not know how long it would take to regrow his leg, or what prices he would have to pay, but he promised himself: One day I will return home, whole again.
