It wasn’t the first time Xavier had trapped someone inside a spatial storage device.

When he’d first encountered Palini, he’d trapped the man in his Roving Seed Base so he could fight him there instead of inside the isolation room. This was different, however, as Xavier didn’t intend to enter the spatial storage device.

He wondered what he would call it.

Perhaps Pocket Prison?

Xavier peered inside the Pocket Prison, watching as the Class C7 Void Dungeon-created beast slammed its head into the door over and over again. It tried to cast a spell, but it wasn’t able to. The Pocket Prison had enchantments—runes embedded into the walls—that made it impossible for this thing to cast any spells.

At least, he hoped it would be impossible. This was the first time he was testing it. Hell, he hadn’t even known what he was making when the idea came to him and he’d executed it.

Not on a conscious level, at least.

There were other enchantments at work inside the Pocket Prison, and as Xavier watched he activated them, wondering if they would work. Runes all along the walls glowed. The Ragatni paused, looking around the room fearfully, seeing the glowing runes.

Recognition gleamed in its eyes, then those eyes were filled with the same rage he’d seen in the other beasts of this place before it.

The Void Dungeon’s rage.

It redoubled its efforts to break through the door as great big spikes started to reach for it from all sides of the Pocket Prison. While the spikes weren’t strong enough to get to its brain, Xavier didn’t need them to. He merely needed to get to its energy pores, which—like just about every other being—were right at the surface.

The spikes stuck into the enemy. First one, then another, until it was stuck in the middle of the room, held slightly off the ground, every single one of its limbs and its entire body rife with the spikes.

Another aspect of the enchantment activated, and the spikes began siphoning energy out of the Ragatni. Energy that poured directly into Xavier. He breathed it in, feeling its warmth, and sent it all straight to his Reality Core.

It didn’t take long for the Ragatni to die. Without its energy, it could no longer live inside the environment of the Pocket Prison—a caustic environment that lacked breathable air. And without its energy, the toughness of its hide was greatly diminished, allowed the spikes to reach deeper into it. Its bones, too, no longer possessed the same structural integrity as before.

Xavier winced the spikes sunk deeper.

A painful way to go.

Though he felt no sympathy for the Void Dungeon-created beast, a beast that would have killed him without a second thought, he didn’t relish the pain he caused it.

I wonder if I can kill these things a little more… humanely.

When the beast was dead, Xavier had the Pocket Prison drop it out onto the floor of the dungeon. He stared at the beast for a little while, contemplating what he’d just done, thinking about the word he’d ascribed his powers not so long ago:

Godlike.

The prison he’d just created… he knew it wouldn’t be able to ensnare every enemy he came across, but these creatures weren’t exactly weak and yet he still managed to imprison them. And he was only going to get stronger…

He moved on to face the other Ragatni in the cavern, taking them down with ease one by one, rejoicing in the energy they offered him, filling his Reality Core more and more.

Xavier pushed farther through the dungeon to the next cavern. Ragatni seemed to be the main event in these parts. Sometimes five, sometimes ten. Rarely more than that. He was beginning to get bored of facing their hammerhead-like heads, beginning to tire of coming up with new ways to kill the same Void Dungeon-created beast.

Surely this dungeon would be more creative than this…

Soon, he stopped using the wave of temporal energy on every single enemy, instead leaving two unfrozen. He could lock one inside a Pocket Prison while he inserted a ball of bone that exploded into spikes into the other’s brain.

Not long after he felt comfortable taking two on at a time, he made that three, then four, then five, using different tactics against each of them. Not every enemy would be susceptible to his every attack—and there would be many out there he wouldn’t be able to simply lock in a temporal cage or place inside a Pocket Prison.

So, he attacked with melee attacks, with ice attacks. With lightning and shadow. With teleportation and phasing. With telekinesis and pure, destructive energy strikes.

It was with the pure, destructive energy strikes that he ended up hitting gold. These things didn’t have souls, so getting past their physical armour had been a difficult thing. But there were other means—pure, concentrated, destructive energy, similar to the energy he’d used against the True Dragon, though of course nowhere near as much, was enough to push through its defences and sink straight into the Void Dungeon-created beast.

There were several different ways that he could manage this.

The first way that he’d tried was with a long-ranged attack. A beam of destructive energy would slam into the Ragatni and damage its defences. Enough beams of destructive energy hitting the exact same place would weaken it enough for him to break through.

Once he had, it was only a matter of time before the thing weakened and died.

As he killed his fifth Ragatni this way, he recalled how he’d defeated the World Destroyer. He’d brought in the power of two galaxies from two different universes into himself, pooled it into one place—his arm—then had punched straight through the World Destroyer.

He’d needed a physical attack against the World Destroyer because of its ability to consume energy, but that didn’t mean there wasn’t merit to attacking other enemies in the same way…

The Ragatni sunk to the ground, dead, and Xavier faced another one, releasing it from its temporal cage but doing little else. At present, he wasn’t wielding any weapons, and he didn’t want to. As powerful as his bone weapons had become, he didn’t need them in this instance.

Xavier pooled energy into his fists. When he called it “pure, destructive energy” what he was really referring to was Celestial Energy. And not just raw Celestial Energy, but refined Celestial Energy—which was just about the strongest energy he could think of. Except the refined Celestial Energy was altered in such a way that made it ready to explode the moment it interacted with other energy or matter—to the point where simply wielding it at all was a danger to him. If he made the wrong step, if he flowed it out of his body in the wrong way, it would sooner explode his arm than the enemy.

And if he had enough of that energy pooled inside his arm, it might do a lot more than just explode his arm…

But he was confident this would work. At least, he was confident enough to try. If it exploded in his face, he’d deal with the consequences. As long as those consequences weren’t his imminent death.

Xavier didn’t waste any time. He teleported straight up to his enemy, so he was hovering in the air above it, then slammed his fist into its stupid hammerhead-like head. The instant his fist made contact, he teleported away, leaving the enemy behind.

The explosion was deafening. Or, it would have been, if he were able to hear in his current state.

When Xavier turned around, he saw half the Ragatni’s head was missing. Blood, bone, and brain matter had been utterly obliterated, leaving nothing left to splatter the walls. The beast didn’t have time to roar in agony; it simply slumped dead to the ground.

One strike… I killed that thing in one strike…

He looked down at his hand. It was still intact. The energy hadn’t done any harm to him at all. He had the exact right delivery method…

This is at least 500 percent more efficient than using it as a long-ranged beam energy attack.

Xavier released a breath, cycling the energy he’d just received through his body and into his Reality Core. Though he didn’t send all of it there—the energy he received was Celestial Energy, raw Celestial Energy. He’d summoned several drones to work as, well, energy refineries, and he sent half the energy to them so they could refine it for him, allowing him to use more of that pure, destructive energy against the next enemies.

He couldn’t help but clap his hands together and grin.

When he came upon the next set of Ragatni—five in all—he didn’t stick any of them in a temporal cage or a Pocket Prison. He stepped around the corner and into the cavern with a bow in hand and a smile on his face.

He had three arrows, and he was hoping it would be enough. Each of the arrows had pure, destructive energy held within. He’d altered the bone arrows in such a way as to ensure they wouldn’t explode when he released the string—the energy was all compacted inside the arrowhead.

Nock, draw, loose.

Nock, draw, loose.

Nock, draw, loose.

By the time he’d loosed the third arrow, the first hadn’t reached the enemy.

Thunk, thunk, thunk.

All three arrows slammed into the Class C7 Ragatni’s head, exploding on contact. The rush of energy he received told him it was enough to kill the bastard. He noticed he hadn’t gotten that rush until the third arrow hit.

More effective than as a beam, less effective than a straight, melee strike.

He deposited the bow into his Storage Ring the teleported to the second Ragatni.

Slam.

His right fist impacted its head. He teleported a fraction of a fraction of a second before the energy exploded.

Bam.

His left fist hit the third Ragatni’s head. Another explosion.

Two more teleports. Two more strikes: a kick with his right; a kick with his left.

All five Ragatni fell to the ground roughly at the same time, their massive bodies making for a loud thud against the hard stone of the Void Dungeon’s floor. The energy flowed into him, and he breathed deeply, his chest expanding, his back straightening, eyes closed to enjoy the feeling to its fullest.

That was the fastest time that he’d managed to kill five Ragatni. The “problem” with using pure, destructive energy in this way was that it wasn’t as easy to “reload” as other energy might be.

He could store the energy easily enough inside his body. Not in any of his cores, of course, and certainly not cycling around his energy channels—that would not end well for him—but inside a pocket space he’d created to be a sort of makeshift core. Similar to the Void Core he possessed.

He could also store the energy well enough in arrows or other weapons, but the problem with that was that he couldn’t store them there for long.

It took a little bit of time and concentration to pool the energy back into his limbs. Not a lot of time—a second, perhaps—but that could be a long time in the middle of a fight, especially against enemies like these.

Especially against enemies stronger than these ones.

Still, he could pool the energy into multiple limbs and weapons at once, just as he’d done in this fight.

He opened his eyes, switched his spiritual sense and his hearing back online, then got busy with extracting the beasts’ Void Dungeon Mini Cores.

It was as he was extracting the first of the cores that he sensed something. The rage that he’d been feeling from these beasts was thick in the air, despite all of them being dead. It wasn’t coming from any of their corpses—these things might be able to summon undead hands, but they’d never become the undead themselves—it was coming from the wall.

He abruptly raised his head, staring at the source of the rage, and found that something was staring back at him.

A glowing red eye. It looked exactly like the rotten-smelling, strange runes he’d discovered embedded into the stone walls, and was clearly created by the same being—except for the red glow about it. That was unique.

Xavier tilted his head to the side and took a tentative step, then another toward the eye.

“What are you, I wonder…”

“I AM THE DUNGEON.”
