Chapter LXVI (66)

“More! You have another?” the half-goblin boy begged. His entire face was soaked in a glowing juice and his eyes glowed slightly from the fruit he’d just gobbled up.

All around Mitsuko, children devoured the fruits Juniper had given her. Mere minutes from now, she doubted even a seed would remain of the garden’s spoils. It certainly was a different kind of treasure to that of Sterling’s wealth back in Tempus City. Not for the first time since the revelation, Mitsuko wondered what exactly Mauve’s clock tower had contained as its treasure. Likely, enchanted clockwork wonders that had long ago been plundered and sold. Scattered throughout the world, lost at sea, destroyed. The thought of the historical loss made Mitsuko’s heart pang. Despite all her temporal magic, she saw no way to ever recover the long lost treasure.

Both of the other treasure vaults had been wondrous in their own way. It made her curious about what sorts of treasure the next three islands held in their temples.

“When I get out of this loop, I’m going to make certain everything is preserved,” Mitsuko swore quietly. “Nothing else will be lost.”

“What?” a young boy named Quinn cocked his head at her. “Loops?” His eyes widened. “Can the fruit make me fly in loops?” He jumped in the air, desperately flapping his arms. But gravity tore him back down to earth, the same as always. His friends laughed and he joined them. Then several others tried flying. Then a young goblin girl insisted that Mitsuko must have meant spinning loops. She turned on her toes to show them all her spin, only to collapse on the ground giggling a few seconds later.

“You intend to found a museum?” Sterling asked.

“This all deserves to be seen by all. Your church and Juniper’s garden. I don’t want to see them ransacked like the clocktower, but they should still be appreciated by scholars.”

The children, most of whom were now spinning with their arms out reached, paid her comment no mind. They were absorbed in fits of laughter. Weirdly, not a single one of them seemed even the least bit concerned about being stranded in the jungle.

Sterling nodded slowly. “I agree with this sentiment. Perhaps the world would be better if the people remembered more of what came before.”

“Who are you?” a new voice said. “Why are you in charge?”

Mitsuko turned her head to see the acolyte Hallie sizing her up. While the other children described leaving Anemone’s control like having a foggy mind clear, Hallie had stated she had absolutely no memories beyond the dome appearing overhead. Even the other acolytes were more cognizant of their surroundings while under Anemone’s spell.

After some time to consider the matter, Mitsuko had come to a hypothesis. With Anemone currently dead in this loop, all of her influence had flaked away into nothingness. But that would change when the next loop began. Anemone would be banished. No longer dead. The same as Rodrick and Prince Bupre. And that was the difference between Rodrick puppeteering his acolytes and Anemone’s being normal for the moment. It meant that, after this loop, Hallie could not be trusted.

“I’m just a traveler,” Mitsuko replied. “Not anyone special. I found the garden and freed everyone’s minds completely by accident.”

Hallie frowned and looked down at Mitsuko’s feet. “Where are your shoes? And your shirt doesn’t look to fit right at all. I know this sounds rude, but they look like cut up rags.”

Mitsuko opened her mouth to state a rehearsed excuse she designed to distract from her weird clothing, but then Hallie continued speaking.

“Once we get back to town I’m taking you to my ma. She’s a tailor and will fix everything! We need to get properly fitted clothing on you!”

“I don’t need—”

“It doesn’t matter where you came from or who you are,” Hallie decided. “You brought us food and saved us and need a nicer outfit!”

“I happen to agree with Anemone’s acolyte’s opinion on this matter,” Sterling said from beside her. “You do look like a rather disheveled vagabond at the moment. Might I add, a bath would also do you some good. While several of these children do reek worse than you, that does not stop your scent from being offensive to my feline scenes.”

Mitsuko stood stiffly. Completely resisting the urge to sniff herself while Hallie continued to chat with her about different fabrics and options for practical but fashionable shoe styles.

As they all left the camp by the pyramid behind and began their walk back to town, Hallie took control of organizing and keeping track of all the children. A duty Mitsuko was happy to delegate to the older girl. While Mend continued to heal her physical body and keep it in peak efficiency, Mitsuko’s mental exhaustion was an entirely different beast.

The fruits proved Juniper honest as not a single jungle creature approached or leaped out at them as they traveled. In fact, Mitsuko noticed several birds above them flee on sight. A marvel when compared to her previous misadventures trailblazing through this area.

When they finally stumbled back into civilization, the children all took off, retreating back to their homes. While Hallie insisted that Mitsuko stay over, the girl had gotten distracted by the sight of her weak-kneed parents, allowing Mitsuko an escape. Parents wept as they reclaimed their lost children. Soon, only Mitsuko and Sterling remained, wandering the streets together.

She found a familiar babbling green creek and decided to take some time to watch the water flow below her. Her legs danged over the canal’s lip while Sterling sat back on his haunches beside her.

“Thank you,” Sterling said. “For what you said to Juniper. I do not deserve the words you wielded for me.”

“You’re my friend,” Mitsuko said simply. “All of my friends deserve to be defended when belittled.”

“I’ve made horrible mistakes, Mitsuko.”

“I’m sure I’ll learn about them eventually. But today? This loop, let’s just enjoy what we’ve accomplished. Revel a bit in victory, you know?”

The verdant water below them slowly churned. A crocodile floated up and eyeballed them and then drifted  away. Goblins and humans rushed past them in a hurry to reunite with the children of the town. Nobody paid any mind to the girl in rags and her cat.

The sun never moved from its perch above them as the days passed. Three sages freed from their guardians. And three more to still go. Porcelain, Amber, and the mythical Azure Island.

While uncovering information on the islands was still a priority, Mitsuko let herself relax for the remaining days of the loop. She played with the freed children and traded stories with them. They told her myths about the other islands while she told about her adventures working for Emperor Sasaki. Though she phrased them as stories told to her rather than anecdotal tales.

The loop closed with little fanfare. The sun never set. The world simply slipped away. Before she fell back onto the deck of The Selcouth Sable, a familiar flash of words and sounds greeted her.
