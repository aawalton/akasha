Chapter 52: Comfortable Lies

The feast had been going on for two hours, and Ambrose was still ravenous. Finding something acceptable among all the bugs and tough, chewy roots was challenging, if not impossible. It wasn’t like he didn’t sometimes slum it up. He went to Meli’s Place as often as anyone else, and it certainly wasn’t for the quality of the cuisine. It took him the better part of first year, but he let his standards drop considerably.

He didn’t see any way they could drop low enough to eat worms and beetles. The pungent molekin seemed to love it, to his horror. They thought nothing of scooping up a handful of grubs and worms with their big claws and shoveling it into their mouths. And then there was the squishing and crunching, and bits of bug falling from their messy maws. Disgusting, horrifying, unacceptable.

So Ambrose wandered around, looking to see if any of the cooking stations had anything better. He’d already been hungry, but dancing with Raeva had made it so much worse. His previously decent mood turned more sour by the second, until the clearing of a throat made him pause.

“Do you need anything, honored guest?” Elder Marphy asked him from beside a long table, half the food cleared off. He was joined by an equally hefty and graying molekin with long hair pulled back into a braid. “My wife Ruta and I were just about to settle in for dessert.”

Before he could help himself, Ambrose said, “Oh? And what’s for dessert? Crickets dipped in chocolate? Spider eggs?”

Ruta frowned, and said, sounding hurt, “No, it’s deepberry pie. I made it shortly after you brought our Mulch home.” Her voice was creaky and raw, and he instantly felt like he just kicked someone’s grandmother.

“So, no bugs in it?” Ambrose asked.

They shook their heads.

He looked around at the feast. It showed no signs of winding down, and he was sure if they could have alcohol, half the moles would be three sheets to the wind by now. As unhappy as he was, Ambrose did appreciate his host’s decadence and ability to have fun. Even if they did smell funny.

“Then sure, I’ll take some.” He sat down across from the elder and his wife, and Ruta served him up a slice of what was a very ordinary looking pie, save for how big it was. One slice on a clay plate was big enough to be its own meal. The problem was, while the molekin seemed to use spoons for that soup that nearly set his mouth on fire, they preferred to use their claws instead of actual forks.

He decided to throw caution to the wind and eat with his hands as well, grimacing at the hot, sticky innards of the pie clinging to his fingers. He brought up a piece and, before he could change his mind, popped it into his mouth. An explosion of sweetness swirled around his tongue, followed by a gentle bitterness that tiptoed and lingered.

“Huh,” he said, pleasantly surprised. “That’s really, really good.” He grabbed another chunk and then licked his fingers clean.

Ruta smiled, perhaps a little smugly. “It’s my specialty. I’m sorry our best food isn’t up to your standards, but at least this was good enough for such an esteemed hero.”

Elder Marphy coughed again, and said, “Would it be okay if we were to ask you a few questions, regarding your group and your visit? For our own peace of mind, understand. We’d never accuse Hermanic boys of anything fishy.”

“Of course,” said Ambrose, partially amused, but mostly tense. Lies like this always involved scrutiny, but somehow he had all of their attention. It may have been part of the plan, but he found himself not as prepared as he would’ve liked. “Do keep in mind that there are certain things I cannot tell you, but your people have been good to us, so I see no reason not to indulge your curiosity.”

He thought of Raeleq, telling him he was the only one pompous enough to pull this off. It didn’t matter that it was Ambrose’s idea first, the fact that everyone jumped on board was as funny as it was a little annoying.

“Well, of course,” said Ruta. “We’d never expect you to spill everything you know, sir. We know you’ve got bigger, more important things to worry about.”

The constant deference, clearly forced, was beginning to annoy him.

“Ask your questions,” he said, taking another bite of pie and washing it down with nearby juice. It might have belonged to someone, he didn’t care. He was currently representing the Empire, and they took what they wanted.

“Well, what all do you need from Stonskarr, and how can we help?” Elder Marphy asked, still tense. “We’re good, simple people here in Crag. We keep our heads down, do our work, and stay out of trouble. Mostly. I guess I just wanna know…We’re not in trouble, are we? Or you’re not going to bring trouble down on us, right?”

Ambrose took his time answering, licking his fingers clean. It was so uncivilized, but they were hardly in high society. Maybe it made him fit in a little better. “We have no intention of causing any more trouble than we have to. We’re pursuing fugitives, and a fight led to us crashing here. As soon as we get the parts we need to repair our ship, we’re gone.

“All we require from you is the use of Mulch to escort us. We’ll go to Bedrock Spire, get what we need, come back, and leave.” He let a bit of a chill enter his voice. “The number one thing you can do is not talk about us to anyone, and just forget we were here. We have no interest in this world. Not on its own. Soon, you’ll be able to go back to normal, and work on atonement for your peoples’ sins.”

Elder Marphy swallowed, but his wife cocked her head to the side. “What about Boss Parl? Does he know you folks are here? Even if it’s a secret mission, I can’t help but think he needs to know.”

Ambrose took another sip of juice and pushed the mostly eaten pie away from himself. It really was delicious, and it helped his mood tremendously to get something in his stomach, but now he had to focus on not blowing their cover.

“My company and I, we’re not based in this region,” he said. “Who is Boss Parl, and why should we care what he knows or doesn’t know?”

“Well, he’s the overseer of Stonskarr,” said Marphy, scratching under his chin with a long, brittle looking claw. “Only one above him is Raynor, the governor of this region. He used to be a Crag boy, when he was younger. And smaller. Now, he’s in charge of keeping the peace and makin’ sure all the shipments are on time. He can be a little…” he trailed off.

“He used to be a good boy, but he’s got a nasty temper,” Ruta added. “And doesn’t like not knowing stuff. Whatever fugitives you’re trying to find, he’d probably want to know about it.”

“Maybe, but we don’t need the help,” Ambrose scoffed. “This is bigger than him. If we do things right, we’ll be in and out before he knows we’re here and it won’t be a problem. Trust me, there is nothing I want less than to have to deal with some petty local administrator with an oversized ego.”

The two old molekin shared a nervous look. “Well, that may be,” said Marphy, “but you might want to think of what you’ll do if he catches wind of your presence and decides you gotta answer to him. He’s not the type to take no for an answer, understand, and the Empire gives him a lot of leeway.”

Ambrose filed that away for later. This was absolutely not something they wanted to deal with in any shape or form, but he had no clue what to do about it now. Mulch or that foul-mouthed kobold would likely know how to get around while keeping a low profile.

Then there was that other scout ship, who had gone to check in with that world Rihzor, he thought. They’d said it could take a few days, but chances were that was just something to irritate them into compliance. There was a real chance there was an alert out for them already. But that didn’t necessarily mean that they’d get caught. For all that other scout ship knew, they disappeared entirely, and there would be no need to suspect they were on Stonskarr.

“Thank you for the warning,” said Ambrose slowly, “but I have my orders. We will not budge on them. If this Boss Parl interferes, he’ll regret it immensely and immediately. There are big things happening,” he added conspiratorially. “I cannot tell you what they are, but life is going to change soon, and for the better. Emperor Turhan cares about his subjects, and things will improve.”

It wasn’t strictly necessary, but a bit of hope and intrigue would hopefully shut them up and keep them busy while they were there.

Ruta rubbed her claws together with interest. “I look forward to that, sir. We’re a good people, and we’ve been faithful and earnest in our atonement, I promise you.”

Ambrose nodded and stood. The best way to succeed was to give them just enough bullshit to be distracted and then leave them before they could ask too many questions. “One last thing, honored Elders. If our mission is successful, we’ll be back around these parts to give you a boon, and take care of some of the more troublesome monsters. If you can keep quiet about us.”

“We understand,” Elder Marphy said with a solemn nod. “And we thank you for your kindness and answers. Anything you need, you have but to ask it of us, and Crag will provide.”

Ambrose nodded and departed, with no real destination in mind. Unlike his friends, most of the molekin gave him plenty of room. That suited him just fine. It gave him the run of Crag, and it let him check in on the others from a distance.

Jessica and Dexi’Lynn were thick as thieves, throwing darts at targets on a far wall. The kobold missed wildly, and Jessica dissolved into laughter, leaning on the only one in their group shorter than her. He worried about Dexi, but didn’t dislike her nearly as much as Jack. He was going to be a problem, regardless of what Luthor said.

Raeleq had finished giving [Reggie] rides and now was feeding the damned thing soup. Ambrose had no clue whether Signature Creatures benefited from eating, or if it would affect them in any way. It just figured that the wild wizard would do it anyway. Not like the moles were in danger of running out of food; if they were fine with throwing it away, it wasn’t his concern.

Luthor and Mulch chatted animatedly at their table. They now had a bunch of Mulch’s friends hanging around, laughing at something he’d said. Ambrose had never seen Luthor look so relaxed and comfortable in a big gathering. He chose to take that as a good sign.

And Raeva, when he found her, had finally taken a break from the dancing and schmoozing to just lounging near a band, relaxing. He caught her gaze, and she smiled for a second before rolling her eyes. Message received.

They still had some day left before they turned in, and they weren’t due to get on the train until mid-morning. While the feast had worn out its welcome already…he was still hungry. With a bone-weary sigh, he found another table that still had some remaining food at it, and sat down.

“Whatcha hungry for, Hermanic Man?” The moles seemed to love calling him that.

Ambrose looked. Bugs, insects, worms, and more bugs. He was almost hungry enough to give the BBQ grubs a try. Sighing, he said, “Do you have any of that mushroom stew left?”

“We sure do!” The friendly mole served him up a fresh bowl.

Despite his misgivings, it did smell good. Mushrooms were harmless enough, even if he didn’t care for them. Bad texture, like chewing on a dirt-flavored sponge. Ambrose braced himself and spooned a bite into his mouth.

The spice assaulted him, filling his sinuses and burning throughout his skull. He let out a surprised, pained cry, and dropped the spoon. One of the nearboy moles handed him a cup, and he drained it, coughing and sputtering.

“You alright?” the mole asked, obviously trying not to laugh.

Ambrose eyed the rest of the food, throat throbbing with a burning beat. “At least there’s two good dishes here,” he muttered.
