The small, dirty room in the boarding house was silent. Ysara stared at the man standing before her, unable to speak. He wore the face of her dead friend, Kaelin, a perfect and disturbing copy. Yudursh, a trained soldier, stood completely still, his mind unable to make sense of what he was seeing.

Arthur, wearing the mask of Kaelin, let the silence hang for a moment longer, allowing the shock to settle. He needed them off-balance, but cooperative. "I need to become him," he said, his voice already the quiet, level tone of the man whose face he wore. "Down to the last detail. Start from the beginning. Tell me everything."

It was Ysara who broke the spell. She took a shaky breath, her royal composure a shield she desperately clung to as she began to speak, her voice barely a whisper. "He was quiet." She sat on the edge of the lumpy mattress, her hands clasped tightly in her lap, her gaze distant as if summoning a memory from a place of deep grief. "He didn't speak unless he had something important to report. It wasn't because he was shy, but because he believed words were a liability. A stray comment, an unnecessary detail, he thought they created risks."

Arthur listened, his expression unreadable, but his mind was a whirlwind of activity. He wasn't just hearing her words. He was absorbing them, filing them away, building the foundation of his new identity.

"I found him when he was just a boy," Ysara continued, a flicker of a sad smile touching her lips. "Twelve years old. He was an orphan from the Gutters in Valoria, surviving on his own. He was caught stealing from a palace vendor, so his crime was a royal matter. I saw the commotion in the barracks courtyard as they brought him before the Captain of the Royal Guard for judgment." She paused, the memory sharp and clear. "For stealing a single loaf of bread, they were going to send him to the mines. He was just a boy, so small and clearly desperate. I couldn't stand by and watch his life be thrown away for the crime of being hungry. It felt wrong. A waste of a life before it had even begun."

She looked at Arthur, her eyes pleading for understanding. "So I intervened. I ordered the Captain to place him in the royal guard's training program instead. I think he felt indebted to me for that, and from what I saw, his loyalty was absolute. But he was never an idealist. He didn't fight for the kingdom, or for some grand notion of justice. He fought for me."

Yudursh, who had been standing silently behind her, finally spoke, his eyes fixed on the face of his dead friend, the sight still unnerving. "He was the best scout we ever had. His primary role was to move ahead, to see without being seen. In combat, he avoided the main fray. He would never charge in. He’d use the chaos, find an opening, and neutralize a target from the shadows. A quick strike to the throat, a dagger in the back. He was pragmatic. He believed a fair fight was a fight you had already lost."

A flicker of grim approval passed through Arthur. Kaelin wasn't a warrior. He was a survivor. A man after his own heart. The sentiment was immediately soured by a familiar, booming voice in his skull. ‘A man after your heart? So you’re both rats who stab from the dark. How noble.’

"He had skills," Yudursh continued, ignoring Arthur's internal conflict. "System-granted. [Silent Step], to muffle his movements. [Heightened Senses], which is why he always seemed to know when something was wrong before the rest of us. And he could use [Shadow Meld] to melt into the shadows."

"What about his habits?" Arthur asked. "His tics. What did he do when he was thinking? When he was nervous?"

Ysara frowned, trying to recall the smaller details. "He was rarely nervous. But when he was concentrating, assessing a situation, he would often rest his hand on his dagger."

"The one on his right hip," Yudursh added, his voice firm. "Always the dagger, never the sword. The sword was for parrying, for defense. The dagger was his true weapon. He would trace the pommel with his thumb, a slow, circular motion. It was the only sign he ever gave that his mind was working."

Arthur’s own hand twitched, and he resisted the urge to mimic the motion. He filed the detail away. A "tell." Invaluable.

"And his voice," Arthur said, the sound a perfect imitation of Kaelin's quiet tone. "It was always this quiet, I assume?"

Ysara flinched at the sound, nodding slowly. "Yes. He never raised it. Even when delivering an urgent report, it was always controlled."

Arthur walled off the remnant’s voice, his focus entirely on the two people in front of him. He needed more. The shell was forming, but it was still hollow.

"His relationships with the others?" he asked. "Braska? Thorne?"

"Respect, but distant," Yudursh answered. "He saw them as the frontline, the 'battering rams,' as he once called them. He trusted them to do their job so he could do his. There was no camaraderie. No tavern stories. He was part of the team, but always slightly apart from it."

"And what about his personal life?" Arthur asked, probing deeper. "Any friends outside your team? Was he seeing anyone?"

"He had no one," Yudursh said flatly. "The team was his life. There was nothing else."

This was the core of it. The isolation and professional detachment made Kaelin a perfect cover for a spy. A man with no connections was a clean slate, allowing Arthur to observe and gather intelligence without the burden of feigning friendships or shared histories. ‘Perfect?’ Drogan’s voice was incredulous. ‘You’ve just described a tool, not a man. He had no life beyond his duty and died serving someone else’s cause. And this is the life you want to steal? By the gods, you have no ambition.’

Arthur let the silence hang for a beat, then his gaze settled on Yudursh. "And you? What was your relationship?"

Yudursh was silent for a long moment. "We were two sides of the same coin," he said finally. "I was the shield, he was the shadow. We understood each other without needing words. I knew he would always be watching my back, and he knew I would always be standing between the princess and whatever was coming. It was a professional trust. The deepest I've ever known."

The weight of the warrior's grief was a tangible thing in the small room. Arthur felt a flicker of something, a ghost of an emotion he couldn't afford to acknowledge. He crushed it. He was not here to mourn Kaelin. He was here to become him.

His posture shifted, the soldier's rigid readiness melting away. He let his shoulders slump into a rogue's coiled potential and began to pace the small room, his gait changing, his footfalls becoming silent. It was in that moment of quiet transformation that Drogan's voice returned, dripping with disdain. ‘Look at you, playing dress-up. A legend in the making, learning how to walk quietly. Why don't you practice bleeding silently next? That seems to have been his best skill.’

He turned, and his eyes, which were now Kaelin's eyes, swept the room in a constant, subtle scan. He reached into his ring. After the ambush, he had stripped every corpse of value. His memory was perfect. He knew exactly which blade among the dozens in his storage had been Kaelin's. He produced a simple, well-worn dagger and held it in his right hand. His thumb began to trace a slow, circular pattern on the pommel.

Ysara watched, her breath caught in her throat. Yudursh’s face was a mask of stone, but his eyes betrayed a storm of disbelief and horror.

It was Kaelin. The way he moved, the way he held himself, the quiet, unnerving intensity in his gaze. It was all there.

Arthur stopped pacing and turned to face them. He had absorbed every detail, every nuance, every ghost of a memory they had shared. The performance was ready. His gaze held Ysara's. "From this moment on, I am Kaelin. Arthur is a ghost. A name on a leaderboard. Nothing more. Do you both understand?"

Ysara could only nod, her voice lost to her.

Yudursh, his face pale, gave a stiff, formal bow. "Understood."

The transformation was complete. Arthur let the dagger vanish back into his ring, the motion fluid and silent. His posture remained that of the rogue, but his eyes held a new, calculating intensity.

"Good," he said, his voice now the perfect replica of Kaelin's quiet, professional tone. "Now that I know the man I need to be, you will tell me about the world he must navigate. I want to know everything about the power structure of this kingdom. The noble houses, their allegiances, their rivalries. The king's court. Tell me what I should expect when we arrive at the palace."
