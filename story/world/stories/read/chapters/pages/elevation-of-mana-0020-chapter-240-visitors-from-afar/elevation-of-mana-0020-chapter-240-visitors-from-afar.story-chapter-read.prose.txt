I saw three men and three women coming out of the forest the next day, and of them two, a pair male and female were the odd ones. I took my time to examine them, to really look them over. First were their clothes, not fancy, not modern, but definitely proper clothes, woven clothes, not the skins we sore. At the man's hip there was even a sword, nothing fancy again, but a sword, and then my eyes worked up, they were darker skinned than us and... pointed ears.

Elves!? There were elves here? That was a true surprise, a real and complex one. These weren't quite like I would have imagined from fantasy books back in my era, but they were definitely elves, and young, both looked barely out of their teens.

Old Pa was send as our representative, she hobbled over, looking at the two. They seemed unsure of what to make of her, respectful, but also... something else, something in their eyes like worry, or disdain, it was hard to mark. It could be a mix of all of it.

They spoke, in a language I didn't understand, then old Pa spoke, then the newcomers spoke, then everyone spoke at once, going back and forth.

“What are they saying?” I asked my mother, not being able to make heads or tails of it.

“Not sure, don't think Pa knows what they want either,” she mumbled to herself, looking at the ancient woman trying to negotiate.

There was no clear explanation of what they were trying to do, but the elder woman seemed interested in a few things herself. She broke down to motions, since these people had things she'd not seen before, and managed to get them to trade some of our food, for one of their trinkets.

It was a small knife, which the men of our village seemed very, very interested in, recognizing a cutting tool. The roots and dried meat she traded weren't too much, but it was some clear sign of interaction. A few moments later the pair of elves did something I didn't expect over the food, though I really should have.

One of them held a hand over it, and they had a small light move over it. They were checking it, making sure it was... something, maybe edible to them, maybe safe? I wasn't sure, or sure how they'd know.

“Shamans,” my mother said, very low. “They're shamans...”

As I looked on I saw around them small bits of an... aura of some kind. It wasn't a natural thing to look at, more like almost squinting my eyes to see it.

“Different because shamans?” I asked her, since they were clearly very different from us.

“Maybe, maybe not,” she said, unsure.

One of them looked up, the female, her eyes landing on me. She was approaching, and my was she pretty. Oddly pretty, perfectly, like something out of a story, with waves of pink coming off of her, undulating in the air around us both.

She got close enough to lean over and say something that had at least the sound of a woman playing with a baby. That was pretty reassuring, because my mother, and all the women here, had tensed up. What did they think about shamans? Well, I knew they believed them to be scary powerful, and this woman certainly was one, and I could see her aura, and she'd come right over to me, which meant....

A bubble drifted before my face, and my heart dropped, she could see mine. A single finger reached out to touch it, though it didn't the aura or whatever it was didn't truly exist on the same kind of physical wavelength we did, at least it didn't seem to. It still responded, maybe to her magic.

“Yeah, see it,” I mumbled in English.

She looked at me and smiled, patting my head and taking something out of a pouch on her belt to give me. Magic or not I seemed to be a mumbling baby, and she passed me a small item that looked more like a little toy than anything.

It wasn't much more than a metal animal, but it was an animal, with a hole through it so it could be strung on something, maybe so it could be pulled along? I didn't recognize the type though.

“Thanks,” I said in the local language and she smiled again.

She said something to the others, which nobody understood, then walked away. Men from my previous world would have fallen over themselves for the view of her leaving, and some of the men here seemed equally smitten. However nobody said or did anything other than look, shamans were dangerous after all and horny or not nobody wanted to be killed.

The pair shortly left, and one of the men, whom had never spoken to me before came over. “What do you have Op?” he asked.

“Shiny rock,” I answered, not having the words for toy, if there was one.

“Pretty, why'd she give you that?” he asked again, voice kind and gentle.

“Dunno,” I lied.

“Op, are you sure?” my mother asked, picking me up and holding me off the ground.

“Nope, she talked odd.”

That got me a good laugh out of everyone around and the man patted my head. For a second I thought he might take my little toy, but he did not. He did however give me a deep, searching look, as if trying to see what the woman had seen. I got the feeling he suspected something was different about me.

“Op, everything is okay, regardless of what it is, everything is okay,” mother said as she put me down. “But don't run off.”

“Okay,” I said, nodding, which wasn't a thing here, but I was doing it anyway, and at least one or two people had picked up on the habit.

“You are strange my child.”“Shamans are often strange, and they often know their own kind,” old Pa said as she passed by, low enough she didn't think I heard her, but my mother certainly did, and the look she gave could cut glass. “But that is a good thing. We really do need a shaman. And if we don't get one, perhaps a strong hunter in a few years.”

I let them talk, the question of how to reveal what I could do and if I should still bouncing around in my head while I played with the little metal toy. It looked like someone had taken a boar and mixed it with a rock, then cast the whole thing in metal, so weird, why would anyone make that?
