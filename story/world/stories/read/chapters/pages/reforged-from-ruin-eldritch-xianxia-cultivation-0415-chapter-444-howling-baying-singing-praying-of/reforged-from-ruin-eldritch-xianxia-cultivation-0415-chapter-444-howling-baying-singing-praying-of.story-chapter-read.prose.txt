These have been strange times indeed.

Gao Li is sure that, given a few more centuries of life, he’ll see more like them, but that’s the burden of being young. Like any newborn, all of the things with which he has no experience are overwhelming and frightening, and always, the best resource open to him is to listen to those older, wiser, and more powerful than he. Even they have acknowledged that the times indeed are strange, but they say they have been stranger, and that these times too shall pass.

Gao Li has heard stories about times like this, though. Interesting times. Times when the barrier surrounding the world would waver and shake, would flash with distant colors that whispered of battles between terrible beasts and great masters. There are myths about times like these, generations past, where the sky itself looked fit to burst like a bubble of soap.

Ironically, those tales give him some security. The mere fact that such legends have had to survive through generations means that there are generations which lived to tell them, and what’s more, there were elders who lived those times directly. Who survived them, overcame them, and are now even better equipped to handle them.

He does not mind that the immortals are up in their palace while he stands here upon the castle walls. They’re more important than he is, so it only stands to reason they should be better protected. And by their very existence in this castle, they protect him in turn. They know better, are better, and it’s comforting to be able to trust in the guiding hand that shapes one’s life.

Many of them have lived through times like these before, and they stand tall. They may even have descendants living in this very castle, seeking to protect it alongside ancient ancestors so many generations removed they might be unknown to each other.

Gao Li holds to these convictions as he watches the air above his head roil, and finds them to be enough.

He is not enough on his own. No soldier is. But that which they serve can overcome anything, even time itself, and what better use is there for such a victory than to learn and be even further prepared?

His hand comes up to hold the necklace tied around his throat. A simple brass star, shaped by human hands, hangs there, its edges just that little bit too sharp for him to properly squeeze. When he received it, he was pointedly informed that that was on purpose, so he’d have more trouble worrying at it. More trouble is a solid trade, though. There’s a little scar at the base of his thumb from where he’s squeezed it a bit too hard one too many times.

“What’re you worrying for?”

Gao Li turns to look at the man beside him, holding a spear with a casual ease that he’s always envied.

“Nothing at all, martial brother. An old habit, harder to break than most.”

Fei Lao laughs, his smile easy and comfortable. “As you say, as you say. I wouldn’t blame you for a bit of unease, though! Look at all this mess– I doubt anyone can sincerely say they can see the sky falling and not be a bit perturbed!”

“The sky isn’t falling, and I’ll thank you to refrain from saying so. You know there are plenty here who look up to you in spite of that ridiculous hairdo, your terrible attitude, your general halitosis, your violent snoring, your–”

The haft of the spear comes up to knock on the back of Gao Li’s knee, which earns Fei Lao a grunt and a more genuine frown.

“Better to acknowledge it than to wallow like you, old man. If our juniors see my confidence in the face of even so dramatic an omen, surely they’ll feel emboldened in turn!”

Gao Li snorts. “Maybe you’re right, but I find it interesting that your theory happens to so closely resemble an excuse to act as you choose.”

“And until ordered otherwise, I’m afraid you’ll have to deal with my actions for as long as I please.”

“When has it ever been otherwise?”

The two friends smile at each other, the elder of the two shaking his head in faux frustration.

But only a second later, Gao Li’s eyes have drifted back to the barrier.

“Does it seem more agitated to you?” he asks.

Fei Lao lets out a laugh, crowing in his victory. “What a joy it is to always be right, old friend! So much for not worrying, hmm?”

“I’m not worried. Whatever happens, all will be well. We stand on one of the most secure bastions in the Jiang’hu, supported by all five of the Peaks and the Valley below, with great masters behind us ready to lead us to victory always. I’m just asking you if it seems a bit more agitated than before.”

Fei Lao huffs, like he’s about to make another comment, but relents. Instead he turns to the barrier, looking up at the impossible haze in the air until its height tilts his head into vertigo.

All around the Republic of Morae, within which the Jiang’hu grows and prospers, the sky is coated in a thin layer of fog. The translucent membrane encompasses the home of hundreds of millions, enchanted into the land by thousands of stones, arrays, and anchor points like the very castle they stand in, dedicated to repelling foreign intruders and ensuring the purity of the airs that enter the Jiang’hu.

It is, to Gao Li’s credit, a bit unruly.

The closer one looks, the harder it is to see past the barrier, at least to those who possess the ability to sense Qi. From further away or with less intent, the translucence increases closer to transparency, but in truth, it’s just a more sophisticated illusion. The barrier is a perfect bubble, misleadingly delicate-looking.

But it is roiling.

Patches of it seem to change colors just a bit, now and again. Darker clouds of bruise-purples, little flashes of sun-gold and fire-crimson, intermingling with flushes of green and blue and orange. Like any proper fog, it holds rainbows of colors– unlike fog, it’s not supposed to. Clear but impenetrable to all but the highest senses, an invisible and sight-obscuring purity, not something that anyone should associate with colors.

More often than not, as those colors manifest above them, the barrier ripples. Little things, like a rain droplet impacting the surface of a lake, leaving no sign of its passage save a bit of added motion.

“I think it’s rather pretty,” Fei Lao says.

“I prefer the stillness.”

“Yeah, you would. Better on your joints not to have to move too much, isn’t it?”

“You do remember I am all of one year older than you, yes? Sometimes I worry that your fool’s mind forgets, the way you dig into a joke whether or not it’s funny.”

“Age is not a measure of time but of spirit, my friend, and you have the spirit of an absolute curmudgeon.”

Gao Li snorts, flicking the haft of his spear at Fei Lao this time. Unlike him, though, Gao Li’s spear hits empty air, missing the leg he was aiming for by virtue of it having moved.

“See? Slow to boot!”

“If you keep acting like a fool-”

“What? Someone might experience a bit of levity around here? The slightest bit of a release of tension? Would you rather we all stand still as dolls until all but the strongest start to quiver and quail at imagined terrors?”

Gao Li turns to stare his friend in the eyes, his gaze turning serious.

“I’m not imagining the barrier moving like that. I know you’ve heard about the invaders. I know you’ve seen the way that cultivators have gone from one castle to the next lately. They’re preparing for something, and I can’t imagine that the barrier moving as it is isn’t a sign of that.”

“Maybe so, but what benefit does it do us to fear what we do not know and can’t control?”

“Mmh.”

Fei Lao pauses their conversation to give a smile and a wave at another passing duo, patrolling their section of the castle walls behind the stationary guards looking out. Gao Li merely nods, and gets a nod in return.

“You could do with being a bit friendlier sometimes. It’s good to have friends when you’re stressed like this.”

“I have friends. And I have you. I’d rather stay focused on the job otherwise. Friends are for drinking with, for sharing burdens, not for neglecting duties with.”

Fei Lao laughs. “Oh, you have me, do you? Just because we’re assigned more often than not doesn’t mean we’re tied at the hip, old man. Any day now I’ll be off to find a fresher soldier to impart wisdom onto.”

Gao Li scoffs. “Anyone else would find you insufferable.”

“While you just find me endearing.”

POP

The sound is loud enough to throw both of them from their feet, shoving them bodily against the castle walls behind them as they tumble. Wind rushes past them violently enough to tear at Gao Li’s skin and make his eyes water and his head is ringing from where his helmet smacked against the wall he’s fallen against, his spear awkwardly pressed down on him by the sheer air pressure of the burst.

When he manages to inhale air back into his lungs again, it’s been a few seconds. His eyes struggle to see past the tears, but they turn unerringly up to where the sound came from.

There is a hole in the sky.

Where before the comforting formlessness of the horizon was unbroken, now it shines bright. He can see the sun beyond it, brighter and more violent than any he’s known, burning at his retinas. He sees the shadows that obscure that hateful sun, which writhes like snakes curled in a ball, and feels them move closer over his line of sight.

They are so vibrant that they too hurt to look at. Colors he’s never seen before. Shapes that don’t work right, that should not be according to every law of existence he knows. Forms that skitter across the back of his mind and tastes and smells and textures that he doesn’t have words for.

They stay like that. It has been perhaps a second, perhaps two, since the horizon popped like a soap bubble and opened a hole from reality out into the madness outside.

Something that Gao Li thought was a mountain proves him wrong as it takes a step forward, making the earth shake hard enough to hurt his teeth.

Just like that, the dam is broken.

Gao Li survives another fifteen seconds, mostly due to the majority of the beasts simply passing him by. He experiences the sensation of his lungs bursting from the pressure as something moves past so quickly that the air cannot accommodate it. He feels bones break as he’s crushed down by the weight of a set of wings flapping, and flapping, and flapping, endlessly looping an infinite moment. He gets to see the castle’s palatial keep light up as the millenia-old monsters inside leave him behind, leave all the thousands of mortals and cultivators in the castle behind, hunted by and hunting the things that have cme into the world.

He lives long enough to notice Fei Lao lying beside him, dead from the moment he fell and hit his neck at a bad angle against the low parapet.

Before he can even drown in his own blood, he sees a shadow blot out the light above. His failing mind, flooded with adrenaline and pain and fear, takes a second to recognize it, but recognize it he does.

A reptilian foot pushes through what’s left of the fog of the horizon, the leg behind it as thick as one of the mountains the great sects rest upon, and takes a step forward.

The castle, the forward operating base of the defenses of the Republic of Morae, a part of a vast coalition of alliances and millenia-old defenses, sustains its structural integrity for zero-point-three seconds as the foot comes down, shattering magic and stone and bodies alike without so much as noticing.

Something out in the wilds gives a roar that echoes, calling forth the primal, animalistic fear of everything that can hear it for hundreds of miles.

The Pack joins in the howling.
