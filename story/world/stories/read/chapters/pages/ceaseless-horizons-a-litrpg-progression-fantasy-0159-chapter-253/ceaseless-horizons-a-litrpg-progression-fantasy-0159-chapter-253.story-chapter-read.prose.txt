It took Lev a great effort of will not to immediately move out of the way. Time slowed to a crawl due to the activation of his passive skill, letting him see the foot rocketing towards him with extreme clarity.

This better work…

When it was mere meters away, Lev made his move. Large amounts of lightning coursed through his armor, and he made use of that to dart away just as a long barrier spike tore out of the ground.

Creating traps like that spike on the fly, was tricky. He wanted to practice making them for a while now, but the actual creation process held him back.

Speed was of the essence in such fights, and he had finally found a way to manage these. Below them, a few meters underground, dozens of barrier plates had been distributed as Lev tried to weaken the monster as much as possible before trying.

Each had rings of runes carved on its surface that, by themselves, didn’t do much when activated. In that random assortment of runes, a configured Arcane Barrier formation was also present, which, when powered, instantly created a long spike.

Formed of the plate itself, all the present runes were dragged along its creation. That was when the random arrangement truly shone, for when they were distributed on the spike’s surface, the runes slotted themselves into neat, layered formations.

Now, as the Ebonwalker’s foot descended with an aura of assured victory, it had no way to dodge when the spike’s extremely sharp tip cleanly slid through flesh and bone. Nearly two meters of the construct was buried in the foot when the other functions came alive.

The enchantments demanded a destructive mana, and Lev had lightning in spades. Torrents of it rushed into the spike, and all of it was channeled towards the spike’s tip, guided by a heat sense formation.

Blue, crackling mana rushed up the walker’s leg. Paralyzed and roaring in pain, the trap also opened up the monster to two disks that rushed the limb at the same time from opposite sides.

From the left and right, both disks cleaved through the charred and weakened leg, dismembering it after losing most of their momentum and all the lightning mana stored inside.

And since Lev was aiming for it this time, a new General Skill slotted itself into place.

The hell? Already!?

Lev gritted his teeth, then abandoned his lead. The monster was exposed, and he could potentially disable its other arm too.

But the new skill he had earned demanded his attention. He wasn’t expected to gain it so quickly, except he also hadn’t realized that it was a base skill, one more fit for Classers than Masters.

It shouldn’t have come as a surprise. The rapid level gains also affirmed that.

The monster’s aura and gaze sliding back to him brought Lev back to the present. He had already flown hundreds of meters away. The EbonWalker’s regeneration was far from enough to give chase with a missing arm and leg, and frankly, Lev knew that the fight was already over.

Lances took form above him. He refrained from using Stargazer’s mana too much, for his own regeneration could be buffed greatly by Kora’s auras.

Lightning once again rushed into them in droves. Thick roots shot out of the ground to create defensive walls, one that moved in tandem with the boss as it tried to close the distance.

A Fracturing Wave cleaved through a large portion of the defense near the base, moving in a straight line. The EbonWalker jumped out of the way with great difficulty, the lost limb not healing nearly fast enough to be usable again.

That also opened it up to a salvo of lances. The first landed squarely on its remaining and freshly vulnerable eye as most of the root wall crumbled. It vanished in a large blue explosion before fire messed up with all of its remaining senses, letting an insanely heavy Stargazer sink into its head and brain, eviscerating most of it in a large localized blast of lightning.

539? The combined sense of danger I got from the monsters was way higher, though?

Lev turned abruptly towards the other clash shrouded in an icy mist. From inside, he could hear the clang of metal, as well as the intensity of it through the vibrations destroying the nearby ground.

Not taking any chances, he prepared himself again and moved closer. A pulse of lightning left Stargazer, just enough for Melvine to acknowledge it with a ball of mist that shot towards him out of her dense dome.

Eventually, after another few minutes, the dome cleared with a loud bang. Melvine panted with her greatsword resting on the ground. The few cuts on her being were closed shut with solid ice, and it was obvious that she wouldn’t be able to hold out for long.

In comparison, the Steelfeather stood tall, its aura flaring proudly. Lev’s spine prickled. It wasn’t the Ebonwalker that screamed danger. The bird monster was expertly hiding its aura before, and now that it was revealed, he wasn’t sure how Melvine had survived for so long.

At least level 600…

The humans shared a glance, reaching an understanding with just that. Neither of them could realistically take down the monster by themselves. Fortunately, Lev was already prepared for that scenario, and he made the first move.

A lance filled with a decent bit of lightning flew at the monster. Feathers with sharp metal coating cut through it with ease, only for several more lances to push through the blast, too close for the monster to dodge the next few blasts.

Due to how wet it was from Melvine’s element, the monster didn’t want to risk lightning so close to it. Melvine herself also realized that Lev had a plan, especially after he let out a pulse of shadow mana from his bubble.

The Steelfeather took flight and came in contact with three different orbs filled to the brim with lightning. Shadow mana and stealth enchantments that were struggling to keep them hidden eagerly gave up control, and a single command made them explode.

Each explosion empowered the last, covering the Steelfeather from head to toe as it plummeted towards a waiting warrior.

Talons screeched against Melvine’s greatsword, chipping its edges slightly. The impact was enough to unbalance the monster slightly. Lev darted in from the side and had to halt immediately when a veritable rain of feathers came his way.

Stargazer furiously hacked away at them. Most of the feathers were deflected. His hackles rose when the glaive’s blade cracked with each blocked impact, only remaining whole through a large infusion of pure mana constantly repairing it.

Shields didn’t help. They were pierced through without an iota of struggle, and Lev had to bring out all of his meager talent with melee weapons to defend himself.

Flying back at full speed, he released a wave of lightning to halt a few feathers closest to him. Dozens still sought to cut him to ribbons.

Cursing the stupid precision of the feathers in his head, Lev prepared himself. All of them unnervingly chased his heart, but not too fast to dodge.

Stargazer was swung wide. Three feathers were slapped away by the swing, each with a reverberating impact, shooting up his arm.

Spinning with the glaive’s momentum, another feather missed, almost grazing his armpit, whereas two more passed between his legs.

Too close…

The moment Stargazer came back in front of him, he released all of the stored lightning in another Fracturing Wave. Fatigue made his shoulders slump, especially from the willpower usage. The skill used far too much of it.

It had the strength to match, however. The large slash roared through all of the feathers, and combined with Melvine’s good foresight and planning, parted through the renewed mist dome and landed on the Steelfeather’s back.

This time, there was no escaping the large amount of lightning that clung to its form. More lances joined the fray, carrying lightning payloads, and yet, a scowl remained on Lev’s lips.

This isn’t working. Lightning needs more than just strong willpower to evolve to a higher tier. Fire was a breeze in comparison.

No new leads had presented themselves, no matter how closely he observed the element. It was destructive, erratic, sought out conductive materials, which were flesh and blood in this case, and also served as an excellent tool to wreak havoc with.

Lev wasn’t delusional enough to think that he was giving it his all. Given the sheer amount he had used in his lifetime, he didn’t doubt that embracing lightning as a concept and a singular element would yield far more results.

But that wasn’t his path. Barriers weren’t limited to any one element. They excelled at making use of everything while maintaining a unique identity.

As it stood, none of the constructs he was willing to use right now were designed to really dive into the element. He did have one theorized in his head, but he would rather not use it before confronting the Night Hunter again.

Before the fiery explosions could clear fully, Melvine darted in again. Hidden by the blasts, she got as close as she could before another dome of mist erupted around her. The rapid change of temperature further shrouded it all in an explosion of steam, and Lev knew that his role in the fight was over.

Two shrieks marked successful hits from Melvine’s sword. The bird’s warbling cries became more and more desperate, and each time it tried to fly away, the mist thickened, seeping into its injuries and locking its muscles.

And the injuries were many. The few flashes Lev could observe of the fight, his lightning had done a number on the monster. Many of its feathers were still in the process of regrowing, as their metallic edge no doubt took a lot of mana and time to reconstruct.

Melvine made fantastic use of that. Mana Slashes cut into its wings at every little opening. Tinged with her frosty element, even the pure mana attacks were deadly, only furthering its inevitable demise.

Lev brought his bubble closer and stepped inside. Kora immediately started buffing his regeneration, so he sent an appreciative nod her way.

One thing they had all neglected was the absence of two beings. A painful reminder reached them in the form of a thundering roar that originated from quite some distance away, yet far too close for comfort.

Shit, shit, shit, shit.

Melvine ditched her battle immediately. A distant part of Lev noticed how the Steelfeather, a mighty third-threshold creature, desperately wobbled away from the roar. It had literally momentarily forgotten how to fly.

She hadn’t even made it a third of the way when everyone staggered, their senses overloaded by a sickening amount of blood mana. All of it was gathered at a spot nearly a dozen kilometers away, at the edge of the hilly region they were fighting in.

The Hunter was nowhere to be seen. Lev gritted his teeth, glaring at the monster that was almost prepared to fire its attack.

With a thought, the input points of his bubble’s enchantments became visible. He sent Isabella a message.

“Take over the bubble if I can’t.”

She didn’t acknowledge it, too busy preparing a ridiculous amount of fire with her team. Melvine was about to as well when all of Lev’s and Stargazer’s mana was pushed into the lightning converter.

The humans gasped again, first due to the sheer amount of power in the forming Fracturing Wave, and then due to Lev’s armor vanishing, revealing his body covered in blood. Both of his legs had almost exploded, and his arms were only bruised, for he still needed them.

Willpower was tricky, at least in practice. In theory, it was simply meant to empower any attack it was thoroughly wrapped around. Of course, one had to command it to act that way, but those theories didn’t cover the amount of mana Lev was using in the attack.

He gave the order to his willpower, which was done converting all the mana, to wrap around Stargazer’s blade. Her pole almost slid down his hand as his vision darkened around the edges. Too much was at stake however, and he stubbornly clung to his consciousness, tightening his palms around the glaive.

The slash was prepared. Lev’s still-intact feet trembled on the barrier platform he was standing on, on the verge of giving out. He didn’t let it get to that point. Two ropes held him in place as he locked eyes with the stag, and both of them fired their skills at the same time.

A red crescent attack erupted from the boss’s antlers, eerily silent in its passing. In comparison, Lev’s slash roared as it tore through the air, leaving a thick trail of lightning and fire that almost engulfed the caster.

Strength fled his limbs the moment the attack was launched. As his vision blackened, his mind noted one curious detail.

It also used a Mana Slash…
