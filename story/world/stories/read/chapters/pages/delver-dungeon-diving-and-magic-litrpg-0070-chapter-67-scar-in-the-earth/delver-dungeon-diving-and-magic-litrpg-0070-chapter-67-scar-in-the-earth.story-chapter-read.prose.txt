Another portal opened, this one larger than any I’ve faced before. A priest with us told me that it leads to the First Realm Of Hell. It certainly felt like it. I’ve never seen so much destruction from something that looks so close to human. The creature that stepped out was capable of magic I’ve never seen before, nearly to the level of an Archmage, but I couldn’t read any of the spell structures. The runes were like nothing I’ve ever seen, like cracks in space to spell out a language I couldn’t read. I’m not sure I would’ve survived if a battalion of Oathsworn hadn’t been training nearby.

I’m afraid I have some bad news from all of this. Louis, my familiar, passed away during the battle. I know how much you loved the big wolf. He sacrificed himself so that I could land the killing blow on the strange demon. We’ll have to have a proper funeral when I see you next.

Eridor’s Journal To His Sister

I don’t like leading such a large group. Yes, our combat power has increased, but it comes at the cost of drawing more attention from the many predators of the wilderness.

I keep our progress slower than strictly necessary. Thankfully, the threats out here tend to be big and obvious predators. When one turns its attention to us, I can use the Angel Statuette.

That’s how our first day is spent, hidden in the illusion of the statue, as a large Wolfpack searches the area for us.

And by large, I mean each individual wolf is roughly the size of the horses. With wolves, you can’t exactly trust them to drop a scent either, even once they’re gone from sight.

It’s a few hours of waiting before Shar flies over to tell us that they’ve moved off. At that point, I still have Tanya mix together something that’ll hopefully cover our scent.

Once more, we start down the path, the ancient road giving the horses enough solid ground for a soft trot. Poor Jasper isn’t used to the increased pace, but he’s adjusting slowly with constant whistling. He has a lighter load than usual, which means that he should keep up with the slow trot, even if he complains a bit during it.

I was right to worry about the wolves, since a couple hours into our traveling, Daron tells us that he’s spotted them on our trail again.

I decide to push our pace, try and cover as much ground as we can before they catch up to us again. I’d rather things didn’t devolve into a fight, even if we’d win, that many wolves means they might be able to take some of us with them.

I don’t want anyone dying on this trip.

We cover ground quickly, the large trees of the Living Forest slowly giving away to the conifers I’m more used to. It’s a gradual change, but noticeable as the glowing flora slowly becomes less and less. A heavy rain starts to pour down, warmer than I’m used to.

That seems to make the wolves give up, as I’m guessing we leave their territory. At the same time, we find a nice clearing where I decide to make camp. Making camp in the rain is never fun, but better than doing it in the rain and in the dark.

One of the tents wasn’t put away properly, letting it get rather soaked. I decide to give up my tent, instead trusting in my enchanted cloak to keep me warm through the night. The little wood we manage to gather is soaked through, and we’re forced to go without a fire that night. People aren’t much in the mood for talking in the wet, and soon enough we’re all in our furs and trying to sleep.

The next morning, we’re up and leaving soon after dawn. I look over to the Dralk curiously as we pack up.

Lady Kala is bundled up in heavy white fur of some kind, watching as Commander Vakar and his men pack up their section of the camp. We hadn’t really managed to talk much beyond basic communication for traveling, but I do have many questions for them.

We set out, only to pause midday as we come across a familiar, giant scaled creature.

The snake ignores us as it crosses the old, overgrown road, traveling on its own path.

Thankfully, the presence of the monster does seem to scare away a lot of the larger animals that like to hunt the woods. Birds I recognize also start to return, their whistles helping me navigate many dangers as we make our way down the old road.

An hour or so after encountering the snake, the rain stops, and the sun shines through the clouds. Rays of light fall to the world, illuminating the beginnings of spring growth. Sprouts poke out from the wet earth, their leaves just beginning to unfurl, and I spot a heavily pregnant dear in the distance, an aligned stag keeping watch over her.

I make sure to give them a wide berth. Since the stags can be extra aggressive for their younglings. Leaving the Living Forest also means that we aren’t constantly covered by the foliage above, shadow and sun mixing to illuminate the forest beautifully. Despite myself, I ask the mare to slow down, enjoying the scenery of nature.

“Is there a problem?” Lady Kala asks, navigating her Elk towards us.

I glance over, then shake my head, “Just have to remember to pause sometimes. It’s easy to forget how stunning the world is behind it all.”

She follows my gaze quietly, the side of her mask hiding her thoughts. “It is easy to forget.”

I pat the mare under me, and she seems to get the message, continuing forward slowly. A whistle signals to everyone else to continue moving as well.

We make better time today, as with the whistles of the birds, and the familiar territory, I feel more comfortable pushing us. Still, Shar warns us to hide three more times during our travel as behemoths make their way across our path.

When we settle down that night, we manage to get a fire going. Everyone gathers around this time, enjoying the meal that Tanya prepares for us. It’s quieter than our delve, but I suspect that has something to do with the presence of Lady Kala. The Dralk around her are respectful to the point of quiet as we all slurp at some surprisingly good noodles the Dralk had brought for the journey.

To my surprise, the Lady herself is the one to break the silence, “What can I expect from your Seer at the Stronghold… Keve, correct?”

Keve shrugs, “She knows many things. What do you ask, Lady Kala?”

The Vishten Lady moves the food in her bowl quietly for a moment, before continuing. “What will she want to allow us in?”

Keve looks to me, as if I’d better be able to answer her question.

I shrug, unsure myself.

Keve hesitates, then continues, “Friendship is not… easy. I do not know what she will ask.” He sets his bowl aside, staring across the fire into the tall woman’s eyes. She still wore her mask, lifting it slightly so that she can eat. “Truth. No matter what, give her truth, and she will give you truth in return. That is the first trade that must be made with our Seer.”

“She’ a nice lady.” Grildor agrees, drawing out attention to him as he slurps up the soup from his bowl.

“When did you meet her?” Libby asks in surprise.

“We both did.” Grildor slaps Daron on the back, making the young scout look at Libby guiltily, “She wanted to know all ‘bout us.”

“And you didn’t tell me?” Libby narrows her eyes at the two knucklehead mercenaries.

“Nothin’ serious like.” Grildor holds his bowl out to Tanya, and she hesitantly refills it, “Jus’ a lil meeting. Asking ‘bout bein a mercenary.”

Libby rubs her eyes, letting out an exhausted sigh.

“Grildor’s right. It wasn’t anything important. We kept our mouths shut about-“ Daron pauses, looking over to Lady Kala. The Dralk looks at the mercenaries with a tilt of her head. “About the things we gotta keep our mouths shut about, y’know?”

Tanya snorts, and Eyla hides a laugh behind her hand. I can’t help myself, and start laughing as well.

Libby glares at us, her gaze finding me in particular, before she shakes her head.

I glance over to Lady Kala to find her eyes softened.

“It is good to see your group’s friendship.” She looks over to Vakar, who nods at her, “The Commander told me of your…” she pauses, then continues once she finds the word, “companionship. But I have not spent time with humans, all I could imagine was-“ she stops, her eyes hardening again as she looks at me, “you’re horrors.”

“Their teeth should be sharper.” Keve agrees from across the fire.

A laugh escapes the woman, jingling like soft bells. “Indeed, and their claws are so short. Useless little nubs.” She holds up her own clawed hands for comparison.

Keve shares a knowing look with the Dralk woman.

“I haven’t forgotten you two knuckleheads.” Libby whispers to the two other mercenaries.

And those words seems to break the quiet as multiple conversations start around the fire. Grildor loudly protests his innocence, while Tanya and Keve speak with the Vishten.

Eyla scoots closer to me, her hand finding mine as we set our bowls to the side. Her grip is tighter than usual, and I look over to see her staring at the fire, her hair tucked behind her ear.

“You alright?” I ask quietly.

She glances at me, “Just thinking about home. I don’t know what I expected from the ruin, but…”

“You’d hoped for more.” I squeeze her hand.

“Yes. Something that would’ve made all this worth it. People are trying to kill me, Leo. And I’ve done nothing to deserve their hatred, except own a stupid map.”

Very gently, I pull her into me, her head resting on my shoulder. She stiffens for the slightest moment before leaning into the hold. “I’m sorry you’ve had to face that. I can’t know what your life was like, but I have seen evil people. And that’s who they are. And the sad truth is that as long as you fight for the right thing, they’re going to come after you. Everyone here will stand beside you as long as you keep fighting.”

“I’m not ready to fight them.” The words are whispered, “I thought in the ruin, there would be something that made me ready, but the truth is that I’m very scared.”

I squeeze her closer, “So am I.”

She looks up, “you are?”

“Terrified.” I let out a small laugh, “Eyla, I’m willingly entering a secret fight with an ancient society of necromancers and cultists. I’d be dumb if I weren’t shaking in my boots.”

“You hide it well.”

“So do you.”

She smiles, “I guess that’s something we’re both good at.” Her expression turns cold as she glares at the fire, “I need to get stronger.”

“You’re already a terrifying Ice Witch.”

She looks up at me seriously, before reaching up and booping me on the nose. She laughs, then pulls away to stretch. “I’m going to practice some more.”

I nod, then watch her head over to a nearby tree. The disappearance of her warmth against my side leaves a noticeable absence. I can’t practice with her, since I’m still keeping my magic secret from the Dralk. That means I’ll have to just read, and watch for now.

Speaking of, I reach into my bag, and pull out a book, holding up my light to read the words.

Conversation around me fades as Grildor drags most everyone into some kind of game of dice. Eridor’s Journal draws me into its words until a welcome message pops up.

That puts me at 19. One more level until I gain my next class spell.

The next morning we set out early, traveling through gentle showers mixed with sunshine. I have a very special love for spring, ever since I was a little kid and I saw the world growing again after a harsh winter. No matter how hopeless things seem, how dead the world might appear, it’ll alway come back, sprouting and growing once spring comes again.

Our travel is interrupted only once by a large bird attempting to catch one of our horses. Thankfully, Daron shouts a warning in time, and the shadow horse seems to disappear from sight, causing the bird to miss its swoop, and be cut down by our party moments later.

Towards midday, we arrive at a familiar gap in the earth, the giant ravine like an ancient wound to the forest.

I stare at the opening, remembering the close call we’d had last time we tried to cross it as I stop our troupe.
