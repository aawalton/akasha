A man wandered the wasteland. Sand rolled on ahead and behind him, piling in endless rolling drifts. The sun beat down on his shoulders, but failed to burn, his face and upper body cloaked in a ragged, threadbare cloth. He crested a dune and staggered to a halt, lifting his hand to peer at the distant horizon. As far as the eye could see, nothing but sand. Burning, aching, searing sand, bright as the sun that beamed down overhead.

“Damn,” he murmured, swaying slightly on the spot.

The sand shifted at his feet, then exploded upwards. An enormous worm with a thousand teeth burst out of the earth and snapped immense jaws shut around the man. It lunged into the air, thrusting another hundred meters of its immense length out of the sand on momentum alone, then plopped to the ground, chewing contentedly.

It paused. Its jaw stopped working, and its tongue moved around in its tubular maw, searching for the tasty tidbit it’d bitten into.

A small form touched down atop the worm. Purple lightning flickered around him, grounding here and there on the worm’s skin and fizzling off harmlessly. The man yawned wide, lifting his hand to his mouth, and looked down. “Too slow. Try harder.”

The worm threw itself into the air and slammed down again, smashing its side into the sand. It rolled, flattening the dunes around it and throwing the entire landscape into disarray. For a thousand meters all around, the earth was transformed as the worm smashed apart even the stone hearts of the long-standing dunes. It didn’t matter how fast the man was; the worm thrashed far and long enough to strike him anyways.

Sand flew into the air, darkening the sky. It hung there, as thick as a sandstorm. The worm laid there, feeling the earth as much as watching with its tiny pinprick eyes, but it felt immense satisfaction anyways. The man had been vanquished. He couldn’t live through that. No one ever had.

Out of the black heart of the sandstorm, purple lightning flashed. A black comet streaked forth, piercing out of the storm with lightning speed and piercing the thick armor of the worm like a nail punching through corkboard. The worm’s body deformed, falling in around the point of impact. Yellow ichor splattered over the sands, and the worm thrashed, screeching in mad pain.

“Oh, shut up.” Lifting his fist again, the man slammed it down on the worm’s head over and over until the creature finally stopped moving. Ichor dripped down its body, sizzling in the sand, then finally going quiet as it wetted the sand around it. Under the darkness of the sand cloud, the sun didn’t immediately evaporate it away. A dozen tiny creatures, and even plants, awakened from the sand and darted forth, burrowing into the worm in search of mana, food, and water alike.

Ike lowered his hood for the first time in a long time and looked around, then huffed. Ever since the System had changed and allowed any being, bug, plant, person, beast, or otherwise, to access skills and magic, it had been like this. Nothing but madness as everyone and everything scrambled for their little scrap of power. Mana was scarcer than it used to be, but richer in the beings and creatures around him. Many mages had taken up eating again. As for him…

He sunk his hand into the worm’s ichor, then pulled in, drawing at the raw magic within it. It flew to him, fleeing the worm’s body. There was no longer a delineation between mana, aether, or any other type of energy; all energy had coalesced back into mana. The little creatures screeched in shock and indignation, but there was little they could do to stop him. One, a tiny version of the worm, turned about and leaped at his hand. Its tiny teeth closed on his skin, then bounced off, unable to pierce through.

“Idiot,” he chided it half-heartedly. It didn’t stand a chance. Turning his eyes upward, he gazed to the heavens. Is it better like this?

There was no way to know, and he wasn’t qualified to determine it, anyways. Sighing, he removed his hand from the worm’s insides, shaking it clean, then lifted his hood again and trooped on, out of the sandstorm and back into the sun. This wasn’t his destination. It was just another stop on the way.

Where was he going? He wasn’t sure. He’d woken up in this wasteland of a desert a few days ago, with no clear destination and no one in sight. All his old friends had vanished in the blink of an eye. His memories of immediately before were blurry; as far as he was aware, he’d settled down to rest in a forest and woken up in a desert. He had the sensation that something was very wrong, but what? Why? He couldn’t say. All he knew was that he could either sit in the desert and wait to die, or walk on, so he walked on. If he was right, then there were answers somewhere at the end of this path.

He lifted his hand, checking his status sheet. He still wasn’t used to having one again. It was a little jarring to see it after so long without it.

Name: Ike | Rank: 5

Skills:

Stormclad [Mastered]

Sandwalking 5

Wormkiller 2

It didn’t reflect most of his old skills, just the new ones. It was fine; he still had the old ones even if they didn’t appear. If he did something a lot, the skill appeared. It was somewhat surprising to see a new skill pop up without having killed or bought anything even now; he was still too used to the old skill orbs of the previous System. Things had been different. Only a select few had access to the System, the lucky ones who had either found or purchased a skill orb, and even beasts had to hunt one another or spend ages in magic-rich landscapes to gain skills. Now, he walked over the desert and two new skills popped up. It boggled the mind.

Before long, the world would only know this System. There were children being born today for whom this would be the only System they ever knew. He was one of the last who would recall the old ways. It was still something he struggled to wrap his head around.

That, and the fact that he could command it now. He lifted his hand. “Suppress the weaker skills.”

The display changed, and both Sandwalking and Wormkiller vanished. He gazed at it a moment longer, his eyes tracking to the top line. He’d Ranked up at some point. The last he remembered, he was Rank 4. Something was missing. There was a chunk gone from his memories, as if someone had taken a big bite out. And he didn’t like it. It reminded him of bad times, of things he’d rather not recall.

His eyes tracked to the bottom of the screen. His only hint beamed back at him.

Hunt the Divine Skills: 1/7

A quest. He didn’t recall having those before, and he didn’t know what this one meant. It had been handed out by the System, so he was pretty sure he could trust that it was important and for a good cause, but he didn’t know why he should hunt them, or what he’d get for accomplishing it. Or even what it meant to hunt skills any more, in this strange new System he found himself a part of.

“Couldn’t give me any more hints, could you?” he groused to no one. As usual, there wasn’t a reply.

A moment later, Ike lowered his hand, dismissing the display entirely. He turned all his attention to his feet instead, steadily trooping out of the desert. Answers laid ahead. He had to convince himself of that, or else he’d never make it out of this blazingly hot hellhole. Answers laid ahead.

He walked on. The sand shifted under his feet, infiltrated his clothes, and ground at every crevasse of his body. At first it hurt, but then he ceased to feel it. A message popped up, alerting him to the fact that he’d gained Desert Resistance, but he dismissed it without checking it. It would get suppressed like all the rest.

It’s a bit overkill. Not all of these things need to be skills. Desert Resistance, honestly? When am I going to use that? Now, obviously, but aside from ‘now.’ It wasn’t going to do anything for him in ninety percent of situations he found himself in.

At last, he crested a dune and found himself gazing down on a rickety hut and a few scraggly brush trees, the first signs of civilization or an end to this endless desert he’d seen since he’d woken up. He hurried down to it, almost falling over himself in his haste to reach the hut. People! Maybe one of them would know what was going on. He kind of doubted it, but it was worth a shot.

A sign clung to the building by one hinge, declaring it The Oasis Tavern. Ike pushed the door open and all but fell into the shade within the building, exhausted. “A drink. Anything.”

The room had been rowdy, but as he entered, it fell silent. He lifted his eyes. A bunch of rough-looking men crowded into the space in matching leather armor, even the spiked studs on the shoulders and chest the same. Almost as one, the men reached for their weapons.

Ike put his hands up. “Sorry, didn’t realize the shop was occupied. I can go.”

“Wait.” From the back of the crowd, a figure shoved his way forward. The other men parted to let him through, and he faced Ike, crossing his arms. He was huge, roiling with muscle, and he sported a huge sword that he probably couldn’t swing without bringing the tavern down around him—whatever was left of it.

Now that Ike wasn’t sun-blinded, he could make out more of the tavern, and it wasn’t a pretty picture. The walls were fragile, with big gaps the sun streamed through. Scars marred the bar, cut so deep and in so many places that it looked more like a used cutting board than a countertop. There were no drinks to be seen, nor a bartender, and most of the tables were either tipped over or being used as seats. In the case of one particularly acrobatic leather-clad man, both. There was a particular scent on the air, medicinal and sharp, and the blueprints of a building he really doubted the men owned spread out on one of the most complete tables.

He nodded slowly, giving the men another look-over, only this time their ugly mugs looked like shiny gold coins. I bet they’re wanted. I wonder how much I could get for their heads?

He’d lost all his gear somewhere along the way. All his fabulous wealth, too. They hadn’t missed any of his dimensional bags or storage rings. No, they’d been so thorough that he’d begun to wonder if he hadn’t been mugged by some real professionals. Then the question became why they were hanging out in the remote desert, and why they’d targeted Ike. He was—had been—rich, but he didn’t look like it. Not even before he went to sleep. His robes were hardy and threadbare from use, and the only jewelry he wore was functional; storage rings and the like.

Either that, or someone had taken him for a corpse and looted his body. Between the two, the second was far more likely.

The leader tipped his head at Ike. “Show me your face.”

Ike paused. Something in the back of his mind told him that that was a bad idea, but he couldn’t remember why. He hesitated for a moment, then decided to be direct about it. “Why?”

“Show me your face,” the man demanded again.

Shrugging widely, Ike lifted his hands to the rag he’d been wearing over his head. It was mostly there to protect him from the raging sun; in the desert, it was strong enough to burn even his Rank 5 skin. He hadn’t meant to hide his face. That was just a side effect.

He gripped the rag and began to raise it. His chin appeared. His nose. Abruptly, he flung the rag at the men and darted to the side, racing toward the wall. He didn’t know why they wanted to see his face, and he didn’t care. One way or another, he’d already decided to defeat the men, so why give them the right to decide when the fight started?

The leader shouted, and the men jumped to their feet. Ike smirked. Lightning crackled around his body, and he leaped off the wall and cleared the gap in an instant, sliding in between the men and their leader. Without bothering to kill his momentum, he smashed his fist into the first face he saw. The man reeled back. Ike grabbed his collar and belt and lifted him, smashing him headfirst into the next man without a moment’s hesitation, then dropping him on that same man.

Arms wrapped around his neck, and huge muscles squeezed as one of the men sought to twist off his head. Ike grabbed the man’s arms and swung his whole body forward, then back, lifting his legs as he swung to drive both heels into the man’s undercarriage. The man grunted. His grip weakened, and Ike slipped out. Dropping to the ground, he spotted one of the downed men’s sword lying uselessly on the man’s unconscious back. Rather than continue to let it rot, he yanked it out of its sheath, gripped it backhand, and smashed it up and behind him, splitting the man from the lower belly-upward as he stood.

By that point, the room was a forest of blades. All the remaining men had drawn their swords. Ike snapped his head left and right, searching for an opening, but there was none. He was locked in.

He snorted. Fools.

The sun pouring in through the holes in the walls dimmed. Thunder crackled, and the sand whipped into a frenzy. One of the men looked up, startled. “A storm?”

The sand beat against the walls of the hut, piercing through the holes to lash the men inside, and curled up under the roof, pattering down from above. Ike kept his newfound blade raised and at the ready, but he didn’t attack. Or rather: he was already attacking.

Ike swept his hand, and a great blast of wind sheared the roof clean off. A bruised purple sky roiled overhead, brown with sand and flickering with lightning. Without the roof to protect the tavern, sand and wind poured in. The men hunkered, instinctively protecting themselves. In that moment, Ike could have taken all their heads, but that would have wasted all his effort in whipping up this storm, so he punched his fist upward instead. “Come!”

Heeding his call, the sky spat forth a great tongue of lightning. It smashed down on Ike, splitting at his fist to ground on all the men around him instead. From a roomful of combatants, he suddenly stood in the ragged remains of a shack full of burned, trembling bodies instead.

The men’s leader remained standing. Black burns carved into his flesh, and his sword sparked with the remnants of lightning, but he remained on his feet. He pointed at Ike. “You’ll never…”

Ike dashed past him, his sword flashing as he passed the man. He landed on the other side of the tavern. “Never… what?”

The man gurgled. His head slid off his neck and bounced against the ground.

“Oh, sorry. I got bored.” Ike spun his stolen sword in his hand, then wandered over to steal its sheath. Putting it away, he slung his rag back over his shoulders and looked at the mess he’d made.

Do I have to have all their heads, or just the leader’s? He considered for a minute, then shrugged. Drawing another sword out of the pile of bodies so as to not dull the edge on his chosen blade, he got to work. One of the men had a storage ring; he cleared it out, dumping the mysterious powders into the desert, then filled it back up with the men’s heads, adding them as he cut them. After a moment’s consideration, he added the blueprints, too. Someone would probably pay a pretty penny for them. Maybe it’d even be righteous mages, on the side of the law. Depended on who was the highest bidder, really.

As he’d walked around, he drew whatever remained of the men’s mana from their bodies. In this strange new world he found himself in, he couldn’t leave behind a single precious scrap of mana. The mana people gathered in life left their bodies more quickly than that of beasts, making it a better deal to kill beasts, but beggars couldn’t be choosers. Right now, he needed all the mana he could muster.

“Well. Back at it,” Ike said to no one. He looked at his shoulder and sighed. “Where on earth did you go, Wisp?”

Nothing but more silence. He shook his head at himself and left the tavern, slamming the door behind him. The force of the door closing reverberated through the walls, and they all crashed down to the sand. Only the bar remained standing, a lone counter piercing out of the sand. Almost instantly, creatures welled up out of the sand and scrambled toward the bodies. A whirlwind of hungry animals tore into the corpses, splattering blood even further across the remains of the floor.

“Whoops.” Feeling like he’d maybe done a bad thing, Ike walked away. At least he’d finally found signs of civilization. More would surely await ahead. Then again, is it a good idea for a savage like me to head to civilization?

He didn’t have an answer for that, either. In no time, he left the tavern behind, until it was nothing but a bump in the sand, and even that vanished as he climbed down a dune. Onward. Always onward.
