The world shifted, and Viktor found himself standing inside an enormous hall.

Towering white marble columns rose all around him in symmetrical rows, their polished surfaces gleaming beneath the soft radiance that poured down from the high vaulted ceiling overhead, where countless streams of pale golden light descended to bathe the vast chamber in a serene, almost dreamlike glow.

A long crimson carpet stretched across the floor from the entrance toward a massive raised dais at the center of the hall, layered with an abundance of luxurious cushions and coverings of silk. And, beside it, stood Galatea’s projection.

“What is this place?” Viktor asked. “I don’t remember seeing it when I visited your cathedral last time.”

“My bedchamber,” Galatea said with a shrug.

Wait, what? This is her bedroom? Viktor glanced at the ridiculously big platform occupying most of the chamber. And this is the bed? Who in the world even slept on something like this?

“Anyway, what do you want to talk about?”

“The goblin,” Galatea replied.

Huh, goblin? Viktor frowned. “What the hell are you talking about?”

“Your hypothetical goblin, Master. We just discussed it yesterday, and you already forgot?”

Ah, that.

But... why did she bring it up now? What exactly was she trying to accomplish?

“What do you want?” Viktor grumbled.

Instead of answering his question, Galatea turned away, drifting across the marble floor at a leisurely, unhurried pace.

“You know, Master. I’ve been watching you ever since the day I came here.”

Why did she change the subject? Viktor narrowed his eyes. “Why?”

“To learn more about the man I have pledged allegiance to. I’m certain you have also been observing me for the same purpose.”

He gave no response.

“And I believe I’ve gotten a fairly complete understanding of your character. You are intelligent, resourceful, and highly disciplined. When you set yourself a goal, you pursue it with complete attention. You explore every possible way to get what you want, and then you execute your plan with flawless precision. You are determined, so setbacks do not easily dissuade you. You are focused, so you are not easily distracted from your path—”

“Did you call me here just to flatter me?”

“—and you are also a coward who runs away from your problems when they become inconvenient.”

“...What?”

Galatea went on as though she hadn’t been interrupted.

“When something makes you uncomfortable, and you can’t figure out a way to deal with it, your solution is to pretend that it doesn’t exist. You just look away. You do something else. You immerse yourself in other tasks. It appears, at first glance, like discipline or focus, but in reality it is simply avoidance.” She chuckled. “It is almost as if that strength of yours I’ve just mentioned is not actually a strength at all. You do not focus because you are free of distraction; you focus because it helps you ignore what you don’t want to confront.”

Viktor felt heat rushing to his face.

“You think that is an appropriate way to speak to your master?”

“Yes, I do,” Galatea casually said. “You said it yourself, Master. That as long as I do my job, you do not care about anything else. That I may speak freely whenever I have something to say, and that you won’t get mad no matter what happens.” Her lips curved into a smile. “So, I am. Speaking. Freely.”

Ugh.

Viktor’s jaw tightened. “That’s not what I meant. I did not give you permission to insult me.”

“Oh, so you’re mad now?”

“Anyone would get angry if they were insulted!”

“Not because what I said is true?”

“How the fuck would that be true? You’ve been here, what—three months? And you are acting like you know everything about me?”

“Of course I don’t know everything about you, Master. But I know enough. Enough to see the patterns. As I said, you are the kind of person who always focuses on the present, on the problems immediately in front of you, while rarely, if ever, pausing to consider the past. Because, as you often repeat to yourself, the past cannot be changed, and therefore it deserves neither concern nor effort.”

“And how is that wrong? Are you suggesting that we should keep living in the past?”

“That’s a fallacy, Master. Why do you act like there are only two choices? To dwell fully in the past, or to ignore it altogether. It could be something in between, you know? Besides, the issue you have at the moment is not even in the past. It is happening. Right now.” Galatea paused. “It’s about your... goblin. Or should I say your sister?”

“She’s not my sister.”

“Oh? You don’t care about her?”

“I do not.”

“Then why did you ask me how to heal her? Please, don’t tell me that your so-called hypothetical scenario isn’t about her. That would insult both our intelligence."

“Shut up!”

“Oh, I don’t. If you want me to stop talking, you’ll have to make me.”

“You think I can’t?”

“Of course you can. In fact, there is a very simple way to silence me forever. We could terminate our contract. Right here. Right now.”

“You want to leave?”

“No, I don’t. I’m merely offering you a way out if you want one, Master. If my words make you uncomfortable, if you find me unbearable, you can just walk away. The door is always open.”

Ugh... Why do those words feel so irritatingly familiar?

Well, yes. He could absolutely throw her out right now. In fact, by all reasonable standards, he should. The sheer amount of disrespect she had shown him was so absurd it was almost unbelievable. A subordinate with that kind of behavior could not be tolerated and had to be removed immediately.

So, why didn’t he?

Was it because he had invested an enormous amount of resources into constructing this cathedral? No, that was not it. Even if Galatea disappeared this very moment, the fourth floor would remain unchanged. He would simply need to find someone else to put in charge of the place. She was useful, but she was not irreplaceable.

Was it because he did not wish to make a hasty decision while being angry? No, that was not the reason either. He was calm right now. Very calm. Besides, even if he didn’t want to make anything final yet, he could still end this stupid conversation. He could simply leave. He could ask Celeste to teleport him away. There was no reason for him to stay here and argue with this insufferable woman.

There was no reason, except that—

Doing so would be the same as conceding she was right.

But... it was not like continuing the exchange would be any better. Because he knew, more than anyone else, that this was not a fight he was going to win.

“Fine,” Viktor said. “I’m upset about what happened to Claire. There. I admitted it. Are you happy now? Now, enlighten me. How exactly is making me admit something like that supposed to change anything? Is she suddenly going to wake up just because I say I care? Is this pointless conversation somehow going to miraculously heal her condition? At the moment, there is no way to wake her up. You yourself made that perfectly clear to me. So the only thing I can do is to find that bastard and fucking kill him. Which is exactly what I am doing anyway, so why do you complain?”

“Ugh... you’re so frustrating,” Galatea muttered. “But at least this is one step in the right direction.” She stepped closer, looking him straight in the eye. “Master, why do you refuse to visit your sister? Clearly, it’s not because you don’t care about her. So why?”

“I do visit. Every day. I bring her meals. I ask people how she is.”

“But you don’t actually go see her.”

“There’s no reason to. Nothing changes. Her condition remains exactly the same every day, so what is the point?”

“We both know that’s not why you’re avoiding her. Master, if you don’t say it yourself, then I will do that for you.”

“...Fine,” Viktor said. “I don’t want to visit her because seeing her in that state makes me unhappy.” He looked away and muttered under his breath, “I really hate you.”

“Master,” Galatea said, her voice softer now. “It’s not that I don’t understand your feelings, but I don’t think that’s the right course of action.”

“Why? Is me visiting her supposed to change anything? Would it help her recover in any way? What is the point of torturing myself for nothing in return?”

“What if... what if she dies tomorrow?”

Viktor clenched his jaw.

“Her condition may still be unchanged,” Galatea continued. “But that doesn’t mean it’s never going to change, and not necessarily for the better. So, what if she dies? What will you do then? Will you regret not seeing her one last time? Or will you just write her off as ‘the past’ and move forward without looking back?”

“...Fine. I’ll go see her tomorrow, if that makes you happy.”

“It’s not about me, Master. Stop making excuses. I’m not asking you to do anything. If you don’t want to go, then don’t. My only wish is you confront your problems instead of running away, and find out what you truly desire.”

Viktor let out a deep sigh. “I’ll think about it.”

He felt drained. Even the spar with Sebekton hadn’t left him this exhausted.

Without another word, he walked past Galatea’s projection. He climbed up onto the massive raised dais and sank into the sea of oversized cushions. The softness swallowed him, and he simply lay there, staring up at the impossibly high ceiling where pale light drifted down in a dreamlike haze.

For a long while, there was only silence.

“Is there anything else you want to talk about?” he finally asked.

Galatea drifted to his side. “I believe I’ve said everything I needed to say,” she said. “Unless you now want to discuss my contract.”

“You want to leave?”

“No. You want to kick me out?”

“No.”

“I thought you hated me?”

“I do. But you’re useful, and I can still use you. How about you, then? Why do you want to stay anyway? I thought you found me frustrating?”

“I do. But if I leave, then who is going to keep you on the right path?”

Viktor frowned. “That’s not what I hired you for.”

“You’re right,” Galatea said. “It’s not my responsibility. The problem is the person who is supposed to do that job can’t do it properly. So I have to step in myself.”

“Who?”

“You think who?”

“Celeste, huh?”

“Master, you don’t know how much she worries about you. But she couldn’t fully commit to stopping you, so everything she did was half-hearted. That’s why I am doing this in her stead.”

“Is that so, Celeste?”

[Not at all, Master. I have no idea what she is talking about.]

Viktor chuckled, glancing at his luminous Guardian. “She betrayed you.”

“Of course she did.” Galatea snorted. “You’re so frustrating. Both of you.”
