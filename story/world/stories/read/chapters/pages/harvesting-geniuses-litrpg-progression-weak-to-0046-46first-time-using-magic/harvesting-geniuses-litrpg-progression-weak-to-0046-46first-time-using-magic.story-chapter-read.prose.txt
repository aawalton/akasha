"MAMA!!!" A cry begging for help from the mother he loved. Even though his brain understood it was pointless. Visibility inside the fog was limited to a hundred square meters. No matter how loud he screamed, his voice would only carry within that same radius.

Even if he screamed until his throat tore apart, his voice would never reach his mother.

The only one who heard that pitiful cry was Luciria, running for her life with Gretel in her arms. Her lungs were working overtime from the exertion. She might be a swordswoman who trained for hours every day, but her body hadn't grown enough to outrun a rampaging monster while carrying another child.

How pathetic. Why am I this pathetic?

Luciria thought about herself. What was it that made her so afraid she couldn't even draw her sword?

She thought of her mother. Yes—the mother she loved. The mother who'd raised her through hardship on the outside.

Luciria was Sven's younger sister. A true daughter of the Halfrin family. But her background was anything but simple. She was born out of wedlock—the result of the Halfrin patriarch's affair with a woman outside the household. Her mother was an ordinary woman of modest means, but she'd done her best to raise Luciria as her only daughter.

It wasn't luxurious, but it was happy. Luciria was embraced by her mother's warmth every single day. They played hide and seek. She still remembered the scent of cheap fabric softener clinging to the laundry line where she'd hide. A freshly sun-dried blanket—still warm from the sun. It felt better than any grand mansion with servants.

At ten years old, Luciria was tested and her talent was discovered. She'd set her heart on training hard, studying diligently, and getting a job so she could support her mother—to show her gratitude. But then she fell gravely ill, completely without warning.

Her mother couldn't bear the medical bills. In the end, when Luciria next regained consciousness, her mother was gone. She saw a beautiful woman who regarded her with cold eyes, and the senior butler James, who told her that from this day forward, Luciria would carry the Halfrin name. Luciria never went back to her old home. She was never given the chance to see her mother again.

She'd heard that her mother had sold her to the Halfrin family for an enormous sum.

Luciria didn't want to believe it. She refused to believe her mother had sold her for money. She'd been too young then to understand. There was a period when she'd burned with anger and resentment toward her mother. She just kept training—thinking that if she succeeded, her mother would see her in the newspapers and regret selling her to the Halfrins.

Luciria wanted to find her mother and ask: You didn't sell me for the money, did you? Day by day, the doubt gnawed at her. She wanted to go back and try living with her mother just once more. But the family wouldn't permit it—she was told it was beneath her to associate with commoners. One who walked the heavenly path should not mingle with the lowborn. Especially not with inferior bloodlines. That's what her grandfather said.

'I want to see Mom.'

Not because she wanted to scream a question. Not because she wanted an answer. She just wanted to see her mother. Just once.

When the Locust Demon had been about to kill her, all she'd felt was fear. Fear of dying without ever seeing her mother's face again. She'd wanted to survive and go back—but she'd been too exhausted to draw her sword and fight. The stress of studying twenty hours a day had eaten her soul hollow, turning her into an empty vessel that had lost all meaning in being alive.

A life spent taking orders from others at every turn. A life where she couldn't decide anything for herself—not even which teacher she wanted to learn from. It was utterly miserable.

Deep down, Luciria might have been seeking death. But when she heard Gretel cry out for his mother, it hammered home just how small and young he truly was. Her cowardice—her fear of dying without seeing her mother again—felt so cheap compared to this tiny child's life.

Luciria twisted her body and hurled Gretel with everything she had. Gretel, sailing through the air, watched the twelve-year-old girl draw a white sword.

"Sis Lucy..."

"I'm sorry. This is all I can do." Luciria chose to draw her blade against the charging Eltharok. She used the skills she'd trained to sidestep the hammering hoof-fist by a hair's breadth.

The Eltharok turned its full attention to Luciria, ignoring Gretel entirely.

"Run!" Run. Please. Don't let my sacrifice be for nothing.

Luciria pleaded—but Gretel didn't move. His small face was white as paper. He tried to push himself up, but his legs wouldn't cooperate. The girl's heart plummeted. Had she thrown him too hard and broken his leg?

No... I keep making one wrong decision after another and can't help anyone. All I do is cause trouble for other people...

I really do deserve to die.

"HRAAGH!" The Eltharok swung its massive fist straight into her torso. Its reaction speed far exceeded what Luciria had anticipated. The fragile body of a twelve-year-old couldn't withstand the blow. She might have had the sword skill to handle Level 30 combat dummies with ease—but her body had never been trained to absorb real damage.

Luciria tumbled across the ground. Rocks and broken branches tore at her body. The jarring impact—like being hit dead-on by a sledgehammer—left her too broken to stand. She couldn't even roll over to face the killer coming to end her life.

Gretel's eyes brimmed with fat tears. No... I don't want to see this. I don't want to watch anyone else die.

Someone... Mama... Mama, please help me.

An image of Rina flashed through Gretel's mind. Then it shifted to another person.

If fate truly meant for us to be father and son... then please... come out.

"PAPA!!!" Gretel screamed with every fiber of his being. The Eltharok was reaching for Luciria when a figure launched itself from one tree to the next. The newcomer's eyes swept down to take in the chaos below. He changed his trajectory mid-air, kicked off hard, and swung his sword from range.

Song of a Hundred Flowers. Third Form: Wandering Butterflies.

Jinyo's blade slammed into the back of its skull. The sword cut through nerves and spinal column where head met neck. Bright red blood gushed out, followed by clear cerebrospinal fluid seeping through the wound. The Eltharok shrieked and whipped around to face the intruder—eyes blazing with fury—but couldn't find a trace of the bastard.

By the time it turned back to the girl, she was gone too.

The fragrance of wild flowers brushed its nostrils. Peach blossom petals drifted on the still air like rain falling from the sky, painting the ashen fog with a hint of color.

"You..." Gretel watched Jinyo carry Luciria in a princess hold. The young man set her down as gently as he could.

"Thank you. I didn't see it myself, but you protected my son—didn't you?"

"N-no... I didn't help at all." Luciria shook her head. She didn't dare accept his praise. She was just a stupid kid who kept making mistakes.

"You did help. Even if you think it didn't amount to anything right now, the kindness you showed already saved Gretel. I believe that." Jinyo held Sonneteer in his right hand. His left arm was splinted with vines and sticks, with no usable strength remaining. He faced the Eltharok, which had already finished regenerating. It was twice as furious. Its level had risen to 32—higher than the one that had nearly killed him.

"I'd love to just hack your head off. Lucky for you, I can't manage that right now."

"HRAAGH!" The Eltharok bellowed in rage. It flexed some kind of body-enhancement art, swelling its muscles even larger. The condition for defeating an Eltharok was decapitation. As long as his left arm was out of commission, Jinyo had to abandon his previous strategy entirely.

If the sword couldn't get the job done... it was time to set aside the swordsman's pride and try magic!

"In the name of the King of the Forest and the Mother of All Living Things—I, the owner of the Nameless Garden forsaken by God, hereby claim dominion over this unowned wasteland. I swear to drive out all intruders with the honor and spirit of the forgotten deity."

"Establish perimeter... Otherworldly Domain."

A thousand square meters were enclosed by a strange, palpable force. The fog that had been smothering visibility in every direction was swept away in an instant, leaving everything clear and open. Gretel and Luciria had been suffering under that grey fog for what felt like an eternity—its sudden disappearance in a single breath left them stunned.

The sensation of being threatened by an illegitimate power drove the Eltharok into an even deeper frenzy. It was uneasy. This was the forest where it had been born and raised—yet suddenly, it felt as though this place no longer belonged to it.

The thousand square meters were now saturated with pure Spirit Essence. The Garden of the Sword management window appeared just like when Jinyo was at home—only without the green rice stalks budding into ears, or the straw-hatted rabbit tidying the grass. Jinyo had spent a full 1,000 Planar and was burning 10 Planar per second to maintain the Otherworldly Domain.

Except—when Jinyo activated the Nature Synthesis Technique, the 10 Planar he lost every second was replenished by exactly 10. The maintenance cost effectively became zero. And it wasn't just his stamina recovering; even the pain in his left arm was gradually easing.

Gretel steadied his breathing. Jinyo wasn't the only one benefiting. As a son—a direct blood heir—the system granted Gretel access to the domain's effects. His injured knee began to heal.

"Is he... a mage too?" Luciria's jaw wouldn't stay shut. What she'd just witnessed was domain-type magic—the most complex category of all spellcraft. An ancient form of magic ranked at the same level as Grand Sorcery.

Only a true archmage should be able to cast domain magic. Yet Jinyo—a swordsman—had used it without breaking a sweat. He was a genuine prodigy.

"I've got three new spells! Let's see how well they work."

"Three new spells!" Gretel's eyes lit up. At last, his unreliable father was looking cool for once.

"!!??" Luciria watched with anticipation. Magic was like swordsmanship—it required constant study and practice to master. For a single mage to have three offensive spells was already impressive. Jinyo was a swordsman, and he could cast three?

"Spirit of the Rice..." Jinyo focused and summoned the Immortal Rice Spirit. Grains of rice were pulled from the granary in the Garden of the Sword. Hundreds of golden husked rice grains floated suspended in the air. Within them, the spirit of nature concealed a power that should have been fearsome.

"Nature's Creation—Rice Grain Bullet." Jinyo channeled energy into the grains. They fired outward like magical projectiles, pelting the Eltharok as it charged. It stumbled in confusion—not from pain, but because every single grain bounced off. They barely even dented its hide.

"Hmm." Jinyo smiled stiffly.

"....." Gretel.

"..." Luciria.

"Something must have gone wrong. Why does it seem so... weak?" Jinyo pulled up the skill description. Fire rice grains as projectiles. That was correct, wasn't it? So where was the error?

"HRAAGH!" The Eltharok felt insulted. It had been terrified half to death—and this was all the human could muster? It was being played with. This human dared to toy with the heart of a deer?! The Eltharok launched a barrage of punches at Jinyo. Fortunately, he'd gathered enough movement data from its brethren to dodge more fluidly now.

"Because they're just rice grains! Even if you fire them like magic bullets, they're still just rice grains!" Jinyo wanted to cry. He'd thought he understood Nature's Creation perfectly—but he'd clearly misinterpreted something. A grain of rice wasn't a bullet. Even at bullet speed, it couldn't cause real injury. Especially not to an Eltharok with hide as thick as a water buffalo's.

Had anyone ever been killed by having rice thrown at them? No! Never!

It was a completely useless skill!

"Spirit of the Carrot... Nature's Creation!"

Carrot Weapon!

Jinyo summoned a Happy Carrot and fitted it over his hand. He imagined the form of a sword. A green glow enveloped it. The carrot reshaped itself into a long, orange blade. The same feel as using a wooden sword. Yes—it was a weapon he could actually swing.

[Carrot Sword]

Grade: F Durability: 2/2 Attack Power: 1

"....."

God help me. This is literal garbage.

Jinyo tried infusing Sword Intent into the Carrot Sword. The result: the Carrot Sword snapped with a pathetic pop.

"....." Jinyo.

"..." Gretel.

".." Luciria.

"." The Eltharok.

Damn it—

It had just turned a carrot into a sword-shaped carrot. Nothing more.

My magic is completely useless!
