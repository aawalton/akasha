Chuck held his head in his hands and groaned. Despite being back in Sanctuary, his wireframe form had continued to flicker at a higher rate than normal.

“Anything I can do?” Dent asked, hovering over the seated Architect. “Feel kinda useless.”

“It’s like… having a migraine, but over my entire body. Still need to get my… Sea legs, I suppose.” He looked up at the swordsman and winced.

“Hate to say I told you so, but…”

Chuck waved him away and sank into his chair. Traveling to another world was a risk, although it wasn’t really him who had traveled. It wasn’t even a clone or splinter of his true form. A fully descriptive term eluded him in this pained moment, but perhaps the closest thing was a dream. A vivid one, where he recalled everything that happened on the other worlds, while still being aware that he was here in the tower - engaged in conversation with Dent.

While the man had been worried about the safety risks of trying this method of consciousness projection out, it was a necessity.

“Seeing Archie in his new form was worth the risk.” He shook his head and sighed. “That amount of power has the potential to change… everything.”

“Not really worth it if you die in the process, is it?” Dent rolled his eyes and returned to his own chair within the control room.

“I don’t know.” The Architect’s face flickered slightly. “It could be something bigger than me or my position here. Even more so than the Outsiders as a whole.”

“Now you’re just talking to fill the silence. Warping around the Sea has clearly weakened your resolve. You need to be worried about getting our friends back home.”

Chuck winced. “How did two routine quests turn into do-or-die adventures? Not that I expect any of them to fail, but the stakes are…”

He paused to watch screens of data fill up, lines of code sifting downward in several different arrays. After a moment, a dry smile crossed his face. It was, after all, par for the course at this point.

“Chaos, to bring about order,” he murmured.

The battle in the courtyard was a mess. While no stranger to a bit of combat flare himself, Theo found himself occasionally distracted by the efforts of the magician duo.

Max was a blaze of purple cards and various weapons drawn from his Inventory. A demonic imp that he had summoned threw fireballs from the side, while the occasional pop of faux stage lights and colorful effects blinded the fish-like monsters. Ren was no less of a new experience, with the powerful sniper rifle firing out explosions of elemental effects.

With a blaze of pink energy, Theo finished off the last of the three opponents near him with a quick chained [Novice Strike]. As he flicked the gore from the warhammer, he turned to see the large figures stepping out of the temple gates.

Perhaps distant cousins to these bipedal fish. Only these three monsters were over ten feet tall and were more shark-like.

Ren put a bullet straight through her husband’s head, exploding it and showering the remaining minions with napalm and gray matter. As the burning magician slumped to the ground, the real Max appeared out of invisibility beside the vampire.

“Not a bad warm-up,” he said. “Though these new audience members look harder to impress.”

Theo nodded as he read through the information the STAR fed him. “You could say that… on account of them enraging for every one of their smaller brethren we’ve slain.”

“Ah.” Max removed his sparkling purple top-hat to wipe the sweat from his brow. “Seems as though someone set the stage for us to fail.”

The elf worked the bolt of her sniper rifle, ejecting an empty casing onto the stone ground. “A show-stopping performance could only be a trigger pull away.”

To prove her point, she did just that. A bullet of bright golden light shot from her weapon, leaving a trail as it snapped across the courtyard. The middle monster—a Jawgore—stumbled back, wide head jolting back from the impact.

It regained its footing and looked back toward her. A slim line of crimson ran along its forehead, as if the bullet had just ricocheted away.

“Ah,” Ren stated. “Just how much of a buff do they have?”

“Enough to make this temporarily interesting.” Theo pushed his glasses up, the lenses catching the light. “One each? Last to kill is buying the first round at the tavern.”

Max gave the vampire a bow before returning the hat to his head. A bright smile illuminated his face as purple energy danced in his eyes. “Quite the ask, going against someone who called himself the Aspect of Death. I’ll be sure to put on an unforgettable performance, either way.”

Theo held his hammer out, pointing the heavy end at the monster on the left. “On your marks, get set…”

Sally’s boots dug into the ground as she powered forward. The eight-foot long chainsaw narrowly whizzed past her, scuffing the rocky mountainside before lopping through two trees during the follow-through.

The elite Radoch turned to get a bead on the elusive zombie, only to slip on a sudden wave of small ball bearings swarming around them. With a loud clang, the cockroach fell flat on its face, arms askew as the mechanical blade sparked against the stone. Before they could push themselves up, zombie hands broke through the ground and held them in place.

Sally hopped over the bladed weapon and delivered a precise strike to the back of the Radoch’s head with her Skeleton Key. The dagger cut through the chitinous plate like paper, killing the oversized bug instantly. She landed awkwardly, stumbling as exhaustion slowed her legs.

With a glare, she scoured her surroundings. There had been no sight of Archie or Hunter since the fight began. An explosion had pushed her from the mountain plateau and down into the forest. Other than the occasional shot of dark, destructive energy or bright flash of something elemental, she wouldn’t have known if they were doing well or not.

Her energy had waned enough to where her Life powers couldn’t even pick up the vitals of her companions. With a grunt, she pocketed her risen zombies as soon as they had finished crawling up from the dirt. It was too much of a drain to keep them out constantly, with the dwarves on the mountain still present.

Worse than that, however, was something in the air. The delicious smell of a ripe brain ready for consumption. As she couldn’t usually smell brains, this was concerning. It also made her stomach growl.

All she had to do was-

Her hungry thoughts vanished as something barreled out of the bushes to her right. Far too fast. The monster struck her, and she tumbled back through the air before hitting a tree.

White thread circled her broken arm, snapping it back into place with a sharp click. Sally wiped the blood from her mouth and scowled at the new foe. She had expected to wade through some chaff before getting to the tougher opponents, but it seemed the head bug had only saved an elite cadre of powerful underlings. There had been one squad of armored Radochs as she plummeted from the mountain, but then it was just boss battle after another.

This new opponent was no different. Much thinner than most of his kind, but with the same look of malice in his dark eyes. His upper set of arms ended in spiked club attachments rather than grasping hands. Mechanical parts covered most of their legs and were probably to blame for the incredible speed he was capable of.

“You're fighting a losing battle,” the zombie growled. “No ships to retreat.”

“I exist only to serve the will of my betters,” came the reply.

She rolled her eyes and adjusted the grip on her dagger. Deathforged floated up beside her, the tome flicking open and fluttering through pages at the ready.

Trying to talk with the Radochs was an exercise in futility at the best of times. They had a one-track mind, and the one in front of her was somehow a few steps below that. Now she didn’t even have the energy for a couple of quips or goofing around.

The fast bug moved in a blur. Both arms snapped forward like the crack of thunder, obliterating zombie flesh and splitting the tree trunk. Sally rolled away from the mashed zombie she had quickly summoned, and a skull burst into flames over her book. She sent out Mortis Bomb toward the Radoch.

Static filled the air as the bug hummed and dodged to the side with ease. The flaming skull sank off further down the hill before striking a tree.

“Rock-paper-scissors is pretty terrible if you can’t change type weakness, huh?” she said, adjusting her footing once more. “Give a damsel in distress a little time for a knight to arrive?”

The only response from the cockroach was soulless cackling.

“Figures.”

With another crack of displaced air, the monster hit her.

Sally blinked a few times, the stars around her head dissipating as she avoided falling unconscious. She had struck the ground hard, and… oh, was currently missing both of her arms. After grimacing at the bloody stumps of her upper arms, she looked up at the Radoch.

He stood, slumped toward her slightly. Some manner of ichor drooled from his slack mandibles. Arms hung limp at his sides, twitching slightly. The spear that she had braced against the ground had pierced straight through the slim Radoch.

“Ran right into it, dumbass.” Sally scowled. “All these stolen technological marvels and you operate like medieval peasantry.”

Eat Brains snapped her upright, tearing the pitiful gray matter from the heavily wounded Radoch. She chewed through the brain before spitting it out onto the ground. White thread snaked across the forest floor from her body, like roots. Wrapping around her pulped pal, she pulled his rotten arms off and gradually brought them over to attach to her own body.

With a dull expression, she flexed her new fingers.

“Now I have to eat more of your rotten kind to change these into my own arms. That’s… unforgivable.” She turned her head, sniffing out that strangely alluring brain-smell. “Although one suspects a trap, you have to meet people where they are. That’s business one-oh-one.”

She picked Skeleton Key up from the dirt and the remains of her obliterated right arm.

“Let’s go see what’s on the menu today, little one.”

With some slightly delirious humming, Sally wandered off deeper into the woods.
