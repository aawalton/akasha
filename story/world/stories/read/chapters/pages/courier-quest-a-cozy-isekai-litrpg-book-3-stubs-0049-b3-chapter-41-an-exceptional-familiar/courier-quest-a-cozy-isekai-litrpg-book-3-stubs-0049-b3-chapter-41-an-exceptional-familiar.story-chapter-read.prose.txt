Even though it was dark out, the trip back from the viewing area didn’t take much longer than it had to get there. Trevor had allowed Adabelle to drive a little bit, though they never went above forty on the speedometer. She had then declared that riding in a car was more fun than operating one and let her husband back in the driver’s seat.

Trevor had been paying more attention to Clay’s feelings after they got back onto the road, too. The earth elemental was happy and interested in whatever they were doing, but rapidly getting tired as well. Whatever Matilsa had them doing, it was wearing them out.

Considering Clay wasn’t working with their husk, it was only a matter of time. His familiar could work earth, metal, and gems that weren’t attuned to their core now that they were a bonded familiar, but it was a much more arduous task. They hadn’t even been capable of it before they met Trevor, either. It was something new.

All in all, Matilsa wasn’t having Clay doing anything they didn’t like. The closer they got, the more there was some frustration in the bond, but Trevor knew his familiar well enough to know that they were getting grumpy because they were tired. He had found it rather endearing, actually. They would mentally huff before throwing their body onto the ground, their form somewhat flattening as if they didn’t want to put up with being solid anymore.

A text box from the Quest Master appeared after he drove Rambunctious C into the garage, but he ignored it for the moment. A team of demons began preparing to look over the car as he parked it near where he started. Unbuckling his seatbelt, he stepped out and patted the machine fondly. He hadn’t realized how much he missed driving and going fast.

Maybe it was time to focus on his Speed stat.

Trevor smiled as he discarded the thought. He would eventually, but he had a build in mind and he knew it would be for the best to stick with it.

“You two looked like you had fun,” Lora chirped as she greeted the newlyweds.

“We did, thank you,” Adabelle confirmed as she walked around the car to join her husband, who was waiting for her before heading towards Clay.

The earth elemental was laying down between two silver engines, partially flattened on the ground. They reached for their master with one paw like a man trying to grasp water in the desert, and Trevor chuckled.

“Look at you, hamming it up,” he said with amusement as he reached down and picked Clay up. The earth elemental clung to his shirt, nuzzling his chest, and he patted their side. “How’d the impromptu quest go?”

Matilsa had a pair of goggles over her eyes and was inspecting the engine that looked cleaner than the other, probably because it had just been created. She didn’t answer right away, opting instead to raise a finger to tell them to give her a minute, and Trevor let his gaze wander.

The engines weren’t like anything he had ever seen before. He had been under the hood of his own car enough times to know what a motor looked like back on Earth, and these were almost completely alien to him.

For one, there weren’t any pipes. It was a solid block with ridges, canyons, and holes placed seemingly at random. It was almost as if someone had taken a motherboard and turned it into a cube. There were no seams to indicate that it could be opened, and the only thing that was different on the older model was a glowing crystal that pulsed with power.

Beside them was a motley assortment of metals and stone, most of which seemed to have pieces missing. The courier could tell where Clay had tested and nibbled at certain materials before deciding they weren’t what they needed. They had clearly brought out a good amount of things for the earth elemental to test.

There was also a stack of short rectangular pieces that started with the same silver as the engines, then something black, something white, and then a few stones that were almost indistinguishable from each other. Those nearer to the floor were larger than those higher up, and Trevor came to the conclusion that his familiar had set them up so the mechanics would know the ratio of materials that the engine was made with.

That realization put a proud smile on his face. His little buddy was a smart one. Now that he figured out the situation, he opened the message the Quest Master had sent him.

[[Quest complete! Clay has a rock solid set of clearly exploitable skills.

Seriously. This little guy has completely fixed the bridges and walls of Tosa with no trouble to the town’s population, they’ve molded metal odds and ends for the local wainwrights to help them get over that little fae debacle, and they’ve got combat power to boot. As if that weren’t enough, they can be ready for a fight in seconds because the Summoned they’re attached to can carry around their husk no problem thanks to their Power.

They can solve so many mundane issues that it’s almost silly, and now they go ahead and replicate a portion of another Summoned’s Power? I know, I know, it’s already been made and this is a fairly straightforward solution of figuring out the materials and then injecting the right amount of magic, and sure they’re tired from it all, but this bonded earth elemental is…

I don’t regret things often, and Clay’s been good for you, but your familiar is a little bonkers even by my standards. Not completely overpowered—I can’t say that when your wife is Adabelle—but the sheer amount of utility they provide makes me wonder sometimes.

Anyway, it’s not often that a familiar outshines their Summoned. That’s a pretty novel happening. It’s also not why we’re here. We’re here because they made an entire engine.

Reward! A rod of Sammie Steel, which is an alloy that can’t be found anywhere else because she invented it.

You are now at 6/9 quests for a level up!]]

There was nothing about the text box that didn’t make Trevor smile. He imagined some other Summoned might be upset at the call out that their familiar outshined them, but he knew it was correct. He felt nothing but pride in Clay, who clearly noticed through their bond as they nuzzled his chest. At least there was no taking it back now whether the Quest Master would truly regret it or not.

Trevor nearly jumped back as Matilsa turned away from her inspection and closed the gap between them. Her brown eyes were wide, and there was a manic look on her face. She stood so close that he could feel her breath.

“You, Trevor, have an exceptional familiar!” she decreed as she gestured towards the engines with both her hand and her tail. “We’ve been trying for decades to figure out the right alloy to reproduce Sammie’s original designs. Literal years of hardness tests, melting it down, calling in wizards, and countless other experiments that got us close but never—”

Adabelle put her hand on the mechanic’s shoulder and pushed her back. She was gentle about it, but it was also clear that she wasn’t trying very hard. “Your proximity is making my husband uncomfortable, and he’s too nice to tell you.”

“Thanks, love,” Trevor said as he smiled at her. He turned his attention back to Matilsa. “I’m glad they were helpful, and thank you for being restrained. The only discomfort I felt through our bond was near the end when they were getting tired.”

“I made sure that everyone was real respectful,” Lora added as she joined the conversation. “I think I speak for both of us when I say that it was a real treat to watch, though. Seeing Clay ping pong between the engines, building it up like he was making something out of glass was glorious. It’s no wonder they’re tired after all of that.”

Matilsa nodded. “It wasn’t just glorious, it was extraordinary! Figuring out the composition of the alloy, putting it all together, fixing it just right. We still need to test it with a battery, possibly in Duck E since that would be the easiest one to put it in, but that’s just a matter of time. I’m not going to rest until I’ve tested it.”

“At the very least, they look similar,” Adabelle observed. Her attention was on the engines. “Magically speaking, at least. It’s hard to tell with power coursing through one of them, but—”

Before she could finish, the mechanic almost skipped over and pulled the pulsing crystal out of the engine. A low hum permeated the room as she held it up. “How about now?”

The noblewoman’s ears twitched as she looked at the engines again. She began to walk around them. Matilsa was quick to get out of her way.

“It’s taking some time for the leftover magic to leave the first engine, which is good because it means there’s a solid system in place. Leaks are bad, obviously,” Adabelle stated before squatting down and looking at them closely. It took her a few seconds before she nodded. “Clay’s attempt has a little less magic in it. That could be because they’re a familiar and not a Summoned. Their engine might work with the same power source, or it may need one that is slightly more powerful.”

“I did receive the quest complete screen, and the Quest Master mentioned that they’ve replicated a portion of Sammie’s Power,” Trevor said. “That means that it should work, right?”

“Should, but there are always complications when magic isn’t exactly the way it’s supposed to be,” the demonkin mused, tapping a finger against her chin. Her tail swished against the ground behind her as she thought it over. “It could be that the engine won’t last as long, take more power to operate, or not move as quickly. There will have to be substantial testing done.”

Matilsa practically squealed at those words. “No worries there. I’m planning on pulling an all-nighter working diagnostics with a team of fresh mechanics. They’re already bringing Duck E over from the museum and I simply will not be able to rest until I get to the bottom of this.”

“But you probably should, right?” Trevor asked.

“To that I would say… good luck,” Lora said with a smile. “There’s not many people out there as passionate as Matilsa when it comes to, well, most things.”

“Why do you make it sound like there’s something wrong?” the mechanic asked, pouting. “Passion is passion, and my passion is for these glorious machines. There’s nothing wrong with that!”

“Do whatever you like,” Lora replied with a shrug. “So long as you’re happy and healthy, does anything else really matter?”

Matilsa nodded. “Yes, these lovely cars do, too.”

Standing up, Adabelle walked back over to Trevor. Her tail wrapped around his wrist before he unconsciously took her hand. “If you don’t mind my asking, how advanced is the wizarding community here in Aletander?”

“It’s… not great,” the demon replied with a thin smile. “We have them, of course, but they’re not up to the standards of your continent. No matter how hard we try, most of the wizards we’ve attempted to recruit from over there have been reluctant to make such a drastic move. Well, that’s not entirely true. There have been several who are less than qualified, but those we think would be able to help us have hesitated.”

“I see,” Adabelle said with a nod. “A lot of the more serious, and powerful, magical researchers are comfortable because they’ve already built their towers and settled down.”

“The towers are surprisingly important, aren’t they?” Trevor asked, thinking back to Melvirn’s home and the plethora of enchantments laid upon it.

“Circles are the best way to regulate magic, and towers are layers upon layers of circles,” she explained. “They’re often used to help augment, control, and refine the ambient mana in the environment. Let’s take our local wizard, for instance. Melvirn specializes in extradimensional spaces.”

“Like Trevor,” Matilsa said, glancing at the courier.

“Yup,” he confirmed.

“Indeed. In fact, Trevor’s Power helped Melvirn make several breakthroughs in his field that he hadn’t been able to figure out before, pushing his research far enough along that he was able to make a more powerful product within a year of my courier showing up.”

Trevor smiled. “Darling.”

“Yes?” Adabelle asked, turning to him.

“I don’t think that’s pertinent to towers,” he said. “Plus, I didn’t really do much other than exist, really.”

“Oh hush, you. Let me be proud of my husband’s contributions, even if you never think it’s a big deal,” she replied teasingly. The courier gestured for her to continue, and she offered him a warm smile. “What I was getting at was that, even before Trevor showed up to advance the field by simply being a Summoned, what Melvirn had already worked on was considered the pinnacle of extradimensional magic.

“His tower was instrumental in that. The way it was built to accommodate the flow of mana, to trap and change it, and more was the culmination of years and years of trial and error. Like what you’re doing here with the engines. To them, these are secrets of the highest order and it would take a lot more than material wealth to make any of them waver in rebuilding their home on the other side of the world.”

Adabelle took a breath as she looked at Matilsa again. “That being said, I believe we can work something out.”

“You and I?” the demon asked, pointing at the other woman and then herself.

“Yes. While my husband didn’t introduce me by my title, which is fine because we are on vacation, I am a noblewoman and mayor of the town of Tosa, in Lucent. We are the foremost country in magical research with a focus on summoning, but we have one of the best academies in the world and that’s not hyperbole. While I cannot promise anything at this moment, I’ve already seen enough of Aletander that I would not mind proposing some kind of exchange program with King Eskretet. Some of our promising young wizards that haven’t anchored themselves to one place, who can focus on your magical technology, for some of your engineers and mechanics.”

For a moment, Matilsa didn’t say anything. She watched the noblewoman carefully. Trevor wasn’t sure if she was thinking things over, looking for any deception, or something else. After a short silence, she sighed.

“That’s way above my pay grade,” the mechanic admitted sourly. “It’s a fantastic offer. A great one. I’d accept it in a heartbeat if it means we get people who have already been trained by the best to focus on our field. It’s just out of my hands.”

Adabelle smiled. “But I’m sure you know exactly who to ask.”

“That I do,” she replied with a grin. “And as soon as I’m done fitting Clay’s engine, I’m going to march into their office and give them a piece of my mind. It should be late enough by then that they’ll be in.”

“Since I don’t expect something this huge to happen overnight, though I appreciate your fervor, I’ll simply say that I’m ready to extol the technological virtues of Aletander to my country’s king upon return,” the noblewoman stated. “That should grease the wheels for when it’s finally time to make things happen.”

“And I appreciate that,” Matilsa returned before she looked at the earth elemental with hungry eyes. “Then the only question is, when do I get to borrow Clay again? There will doubtless be more tests, maybe another engine, and—”

“Right now, they’re tired,” Trevor interrupted with a raised hand. “So if they’re up for it in the morning, then we’ll let you know. We’re going to Lemon World and I don’t think Clay will react to the rides any better than he did the car, so it’ll be up for discussion then. So long as Lora’s okay with it.”

The demon turned to the teleporter, who smiled and shrugged. “I’m happy taking anyone where they need to go, within reason. This sounds reasonable to me and I can probably squeeze a quest out of Matilsa to make it so, and that means I’m good to go.”

“Oh, yes, quests! Yes, if Clay comes back, I’ll certainly be giving more of those,” the mechanic said, turning to Trevor again. “I have no illusions that I’ll be able to entice you to stay longer than you planned to, especially since you’re married to another country’s nobility, which makes this a once in a lifetime opportunity for me. There will be quests. As many as I can spare without losing the qualifications the Quest Master has given me. Just consider it, will you?”

Trevor looked at the pleading woman’s gaze. No matter what she promised, it would still not be up to him even if it was enticing. Still, he offered her a smile.

“All I can do is promise to try,” he responded. “But, I wouldn’t say this is a once in a lifetime opportunity.”

“No?” Matilsa asked, hopeful.

“No. It might take a while to happen, but I already know we’re going to want to come back here. You’ll see us again even if Clay doesn’t want to continue working on the engine for whatever reason, I’m sure of it.”
