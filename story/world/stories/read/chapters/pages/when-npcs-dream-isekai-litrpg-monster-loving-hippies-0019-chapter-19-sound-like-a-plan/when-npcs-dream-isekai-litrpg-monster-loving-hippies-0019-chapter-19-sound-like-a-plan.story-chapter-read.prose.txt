Juliette did not take the surprise of seeing a scorpion suricata in her room with light-hearted humor.

She jumped up, actually grabbed a knife, and looked truly distressed.

“You promised, Geshem!”

Well, shit. That’s when I realized I was an absolute jerk. After all we’d been through, for me to show up with another suricata without explanation—it really looked like I’d broken our no-sting promise. Totally insensitive.

“Shit, Juliette—sorry! I didn’t get stung, I swear! It’s just… one of Jane’s kids wanted to join us. He argued with his mom about it, and eventually she agreed. I know I can’t exactly prove I didn’t get stung, and I should have thought about that before showing up like this. I’m stupid.”

“Well, I’m not stupid,” Juliette snapped. “And I actually have a way of knowing whether you got stung or not.”

“You… do?” I asked, genuinely curious.

“Put the scorpion thing down and come closer.”

I set Kim Jong-un on the floor. I walked up to her, and she leaned in, so close our faces were just a few inches apart. My heart jumped—was this going to involve a kiss? Some secret kiss technique to know if my brain was altered? That didn’t make sense…

“Okay. You’re clean,” she said flatly.

“Huh?”

“The pupils,” she explained. “They dilate after the sting, stay enlarged for few hours. I noticed last time. A lot of drugs cause that effect. Yours look normal.”

Of course. She’d been staring at my pupils. I felt dumb.

“So then,” Juliette continued, still frowning, “if you didn’t get stung, what on earth made you think it was a good idea to adopt a scorpion suricata?”

Kim Jong-un buzzed excitedly, vibrating his fur as if he desperately wanted to add his two cents, but we ignored him.

“Well…” I brushed a hand through my hair. “They sort of… convinced me.”

“What?” Juliette snapped, annoyed.

“Look,” I tried again. “I did think this through. He promised not to sting humans, and honestly, I think we can trust him. I don’t think it’s actually that dangerous for us. We know enough about scorpion suricatas that if he did betray us, we could react before the venom takes effect—it wouldn’t end well for him. And also, there’s the potential…”

“Potential?” Juliette asked, skeptical.

“Yes! Imagine this: we can recruit forest creatures to our party. When we head out on that long trip to the magic city, picture us traveling with a couple of care-bears at our side, armed with spears. We’d be so much stronger.”

Juliette frowned but didn’t dismiss it outright. “Well… I see the advantage. But—wait. Did you actually name him Kim Jong-un?”

“Yeah. Silly story.”

“You realize Hyun-woo is from South Korea, right? Don’t you think that might, I don’t know, offend him?”

Ah. Hadn’t thought of that. Not my choice anyway—the little guy insisted.

“Well… I’m sure he’ll get over it,” I muttered. Then I backed toward the door. “Anyway, I’m off to work. Still time to hit my underpants quota for the day. You two get acquainted while I’m gone.”

“Hey! Don’t just run away, we need to talk about this more!”

“We’ll talk tonight at the tavern!” I called as I left. Kim Jong-un was already buzzing excitedly at Juliette, eager to chat with her. Honestly, I figured she’d warm up to him soon. He was a good boy.

At Ysabel’s Clothier I worked like a maniac. Ten pairs of underpants in Adequate quality in under four hours. Not bad at all. I wanted to make sure I wasn’t late meeting the others at the tavern.

By sunset we all gathered at The Eyeball & the Spoon—the best (and only) tavern in town.

“They let fourteen-year-olds in here?” Juliette teased as Samar stuck out her tongue.

“I’ll get drinks,” said Hyun-woo. “Ale for you two, and fruit juice for Samar, right?”

“Yes,” I said. Then I glanced at Samar. “Honestly, nobody would probably stop you from ordering alcohol, but I guess it’s not right for your age.”

“My age has nothing to do with it,” Samar shot back, annoyed. “I’m Muslim. We don’t drink alcohol, remember? Egypt?”

“Right, yeah… I should’ve thought of that,” I admitted.

“So, do you do the five prayers a day thing?” Juliette asked, curious.

“The Salah,” Samar corrected. “Most days I manage three. But… there’s a problem.”

“What problem?” Juliette asked.

“We’re supposed to pray facing the Kaaba in Mecca. Or at least try. But… well, this is not Earth. I thought maybe one of you knew stars, could at least figure out where Earth is, and I could at least face it.”

I shook my head. “Sorry. I actually do know constellations, but I looked. Nothing familiar. We could be really far.”

Samar looked down, suddenly sad.

“Hey,” Juliette said gently. “If magic brought us here, maybe there’s magic to get us back. We’ll find it.”

“Yes,” I added. “That’s one of our goals: to learn more about this world, and if possible, find a way home.”

Just then Hyun-woo returned with the drinks.

“Oh, that reminds me,” I said, grinning. “You won’t believe what I found in the forest today—actual goblins!”

Juliette raised an eyebrow. “You mean some weird forest creature that just looked like goblins?”

“No! Real goblins. From Earth lore. And I talked to them! The system translated their speech!”

“Hold it,” Hyun-woo cut in sharply. “You two are always secretive, dropping bits here and there, but never the full picture. You tell us the forest is deadly and your friend died there, but then you talk about it like it’s a fun camping trip. I think we’re missing a lot here.”

I looked at Juliette. She gave me a small nod.

“Okay,” I said. “We trust you. It’s time we told you everything.” Well… almost everything.

“Thank you!” Hyun-woo said eagerly. He and Samar leaned forward.

“Well… here goes.” I launched into it. “When we first got here, Juliette worked at the mansion. I… did something stupid and ended up in jail for a week. Then we met Ramon, a great guy from the Philippines. We tried to hunt low-level monsters, but Ramon got brutally killed by an armadillo care-bear controlled by a tribe of brain-altering creatures called scorpion suricatas. But then—we befriended another scorpion suricata tribe. Actually joined them. Then came a big battle between tribes… some stuff happened… and we sort of left. But one young scorpion suricata wanted to join us, and I agreed. He’s in Juliette’s room right now. His name’s Kim Jong-un. You’ll like him.”

Hyun-woo and Samar just sat there, mouths open. Juliette buried her face in her hands.“What?” I asked defensively. “Did I explain it wrong?”“No,” Juliette groaned. “It’s just… hearing it out loud makes me realize how insane our lives actually are.”

“So…” Samar said slowly. “After brain-altering monsters killed your friend, you became friends with another tribe of the same monsters?”

“Yes.”

“This brain-altering monster…” Hyun-woo hesitated. “Maybe… did something to you?”

“We don’t want to talk about that,” me and Juliette said together, almost in unison.

“But our brains right now aren’t altered—to our knowledge,” I added quickly. “You’ll just have to trust us. Oh, and Kim Jong-un is friendly. He won’t sting you.”

Hyun-woo pinched the bridge of his nose. “Why would you even name him Kim Jong-un? Never mind. That’s honestly the least weird part of this story. So they’re like… smart? Like humans? And the system actually lets you talk with them?”

“Scorpion suricatas aren’t smart like humans,” I explained. “They only understand simple concepts. Think of it like this—if we could freely understand chimpanzees or dolphins on Earth, it’d be kind of similar I think. Until now, they were the only sapient species we’d seen in the forest… well, at least until today, when I met the goblins.”

I told them about the goblins: the sorry state they’d been in, and how they recognized humans from the place they’d come. “And you said Alice said all the things from Earth are new here,” Juliette added thoughtfully. “It’s like… this place is some kind of mix. Maybe alien?”“Exactly!” I said.

“You don’t know they’re alien,” Hyun-woo countered. “They could just be… made-up creatures, invented by whoever designed this world.”

“That’s true,” I admitted. “But look at the flora and fauna in the forest. The insects, for example—they don’t vanish when you kill them, and they don’t seem to serve any game purpose. They just look like they’re from another planet. And then you’ve got dwarves, elves, goblins—straight out of Earth fantasy handbooks.”

“I see,” Hyun-woo said slowly. “That does make sense. But then why haven’t we met an alien player yet? If monsters are from other worlds, shouldn’t there be players from those same worlds too?”“I think this place is divided geographically,” I suggested. “This area seems built for humans—human-lore monsters, NPCs, towns, and players. But maybe monsters wander across borders from other regions. If we ever meet an alien player, that’d basically prove this theory.”

“But why would anyone even do this?” Samar asked suddenly. “Build this whole world, kidnap people from their homes, make them fight monsters… Is this some kind of alien reality TV show? Some asshole species out there watching us on space-Netflix while eating alien popcorn?”

“I haven’t the faintest idea,” I admitted. “But you might be spot on. What I do know is that—unlike you guys—I don’t want to go back home. Despite all the crap I’ve been through here, I actually… like this place.”

They all gave me strange looks, so I explained. “Back home, life sucked. Shitty parents, prices going up, endless pressure to get into college, student loans… everything. Here? Here we’ve got magic and elves. Sure, it can be cruel and awful, but it also has the chance to be awesome.”

Hyun-woo looked thoughtful. “I can… probably relate. In South Korea, the pressure on young people to succeed is insane. But still… I don’t know.”

“Even if I don’t want to return,” I said, “I promise I’ll help Juliette get home. We’ll keep searching for answers. If it’s possible, we’ll find a way back.”

“Yes,” Juliette said firmly. “We’ll help you two as well, of course.”

“So… do you have, like… a plan?” Samar asked eagerly.

Juliette and I exchanged a look. “We’ve been kind of occupied,” I said. “But there’s a city northeast of the forest—famous for magic. What was it called?” “Kuriolis,” Juliette offered.

“Right. It seems like the logical next step—learn more about the magic system. Look at this.” I said “Najaaa,” and a glowing line flickered in the air.

“Whoa! Teach me that!” Samar said.

“Sure, but it doesn’t actually do much,” I warned. “There’s a ton to learn before you can do anything useful with it.”

“So to get to this magic city, we’d need to cross the forest?” Hyun-woo asked.

“Yes,” I said. “Probably a two or three-day trip.”

“Well, you still haven’t explained why the forest isn’t dangerous,” he pressed.

“The forest is extremely dangerous,” I admitted, “but not for us. All that time we spent with the suricata tribe made us into super bad-ass forest people. I’m not exaggerating—we actually know what we’re doing out there now.”

“I want to go to the forest,” Samar announced.

Hyun-woo shot her a look, then turned back to me. “If this place really is like an RPG, shouldn’t we be doing quests to advance? Wouldn’t that should be the logical next step?”

And honestly, the way he put it, it did sound logical. I’d been suspicious of the system, but avoiding quests altogether was stupid. If they were dangerous, we could start with easy ones just to test the mechanics—and the rewards. Could be good money. Could even be EXP.

“You’re right,” I said. “There’s no reason not to try. But we can also do quests once we reach Kuriolis—maybe even magic-related ones.” I really want to go there.

“I won’t be going to Kuriolis,” Hyun-woo said with a sigh. “I trust you, but I still think the forest and a new city are unnecessary risks. Life here is stable. And honestly, I don’t think Samar should go either. But… I’m not her parent.”

“I… haven’t decided yet,” Samar said quietly. “I understand,” I said, though it surprised me—I’d started to think of us as a party.

“There’s another reason for someone to stay,” Hyun-woo continued. “Since you arrived, a new person shows up here about every five days on average. If nobody’s around to guide them, they’ll be lost, and can easily get in trouble.”

That was… a very good point.

“That’s good thinking,” Juliette admitted. “I feel selfish. I didn’t even consider that.”

“Anyway, we’re not leaving right away,” I said. “We need supplies, maybe save money for Kuriolis. I also want to help Kim Jung-un train two Carebear soldiers. Not sure how long that’ll take.”

“And in the meantime,” Samar suggested, “we can try some low-level quests.”

“Good idea,” I said. “And you two have to come with us to the forest tomorrow. Not too deep—just a short trip. It really can be beautiful. We’ll go while Kim Jong-un and I search for a wild armadillo care-bear.”

“That does sound interesting,” Hyun-woo admitted. “As long as you’re sure it’s safe.”

“It’s extremely safe!” I said. …Probably.

“I can’t wait!” Samar said brightly.

And with that, we actually had something like a plan for the future.
