Trevor and Adabelle descended the mountain the next morning hand in hand, taking their time. Except for when the courier pulled out a sled he borrowed from the people of Froslan Heights. They went a little faster, then. When they arrived, they were met with some familiar faces.

“I had set up a perfectly good room for you and the only one who managed to appreciate it was Clay,” Lora pouted, arms crossed. “You see one yeti and decide to go and live out in the wilderness, is that it?”

“Yeah, you’ve got us figured out,” Trevor answered with a nod. “We’re mountain people now.”

“You might as well go on without us, because we’re going to live here from now on. Sleeping on the top of the world was quite the experience,” Adabelle continued before smiling and letting the joke drop. She turned to the minotaur Lora had brought with her. “It’s good to see you again, Klarkartar.”

“I’m glad to see that you’re enjoying the Froslan Peaks,” the merchant prince said with a laugh.

While the rest of them were wearing casual clothes, he wore a thick yellow parka. It seemed as though he hadn’t been subjected to Shaman Brown’s magic. Clay was standing on his shoulders, completely focused on polishing the coins that hung from the minotaur’s horns. They had been clean before, but the earth elemental was making them shine.

“You might not want to take those to Paladona,” Trevor informed his friend, amused. “An alumrae might snatch them right off of your horns.”

“That’s a fair warning. I’ll keep it in mind, though I may visit for a little luck if that’s the case,” Klar said with a grin. “Come, let us sit in the garden as we talk. I have some things to discuss with you.”

“Everything good?” the courier asked as they began walking.

“Yes, but only because you’ve caused such a stir, my friend.”

Froslan Heights wasn’t large, and it only took them a few minutes to reach the garden by town hall. The gouge left behind by Hans was already fixed thanks to Clay’s efforts, and the stepping stones had even been mended together. Once they all sat down, the familiar jumped onto the table and returned to their people, laying belly up across both Trevor and Adabelle’s lap.

“I’m assuming that you’re here because there are several people who want to poach my husband and his familiar,” Adabelle said more than asked, a sage smile on her face.

“I would say you don’t know the half of it, but you’re a smart woman, Lady Adabelle. I’m sure you do,” Klar laughed as he relaxed, leaning against the table. “So far, the only place that hasn’t requested him was the Wildlands and the port in Garend, and that’s only because no one met him there.”

“He is a hot commodity,” the demonkin said, pleased. Her tail wrapped itself behind his back.

Trevor remained silent. He knew his worth, and simply smiled even if he did believe that Klar was stretching the truth a bit.

“Oh, don’t I know it,” the merchant prince said. “You even had Surtla requesting your return, and—”

“Ugh, Surtla,” Trevor scoffed, unable to help himself. He pointed at Adabelle. “Did you know he was rude to my wife?”

“Trevor, honey, it’s not like you to hold a grudge,” the demonkin said.

“He was mean to my wife!” he reiterated, feeling completely justified.

“Yeah, I heard about what happened with him, and I’m very sorry one of my business partners treated you in such a manner,” Klar said, lowering his head so much that his snout almost touched the table.

“Hey, no, it’s okay,” the courier responded, holding up both of his hands. “You don’t have to go that far, man. It’s not like you told him to try and rip us off or anything.”

“No, but I should have vetted my contacts better,” he said. His coins clinked together as he raised his head again. “We’re still using him for now because he is, unfortunately, the best talisman maker in Paladona. You might be happy to know that we are putting more money into his competitors to see if anyone can reach his level, though. In the meantime, we’ve cut most of our contracts with him to facilitate the change.”

“I’m more than okay with a gradual comeuppance,” Trevor replied as he placed his arm around his wife’s shoulders. “How did he feel about that?”

Klar shrugged. “Not well, but he didn’t have as strong a negotiating position as he used to, so we’ll see how it goes. It’ll cost me a little trouble in the future, but the competition should be good for the city overall.”

“That’s usually a good catalyst for growth,” Adabelle agreed.

“Indeed! Now, the reports I’m getting from Aletander about Clay blew my mind,” the minotaur said with a chuckle. “You might be a hot commodity, Trevor, but so’s your little buddy.”

“Always has been,” he replied proudly as he reached down to stroke his familiar’s belly. They wiggled and shifted, enjoying their master’s affection.

“I believe it. Matilsa has been reaching out like mad, trying to figure out when you and Clay will return to help bridge the gap between what they can make now and the artifacts Sammie the Gamer made with her Power.”

“Did she manage to get the engine in full working order while we were at Lemon World?” Adabelle asked curiously.

“Not to the same degree, but closer than before,” Lora answered as she drummed her fingers on the table. “That woman says a lot of things and she’s more than a bit obsessed. She was even talking about moving a whole production plant to Lucent just for Clay to work their magic. Said they can ship the products back to Aletander after.”

The earth elemental raised up and stared at Lora as she spoke.

“Don’t worry, it was a fancy that passed real fast,” the teleporter said with a wry smile. “Though, if you’re up for it, we would like to send you and Trevor over there once or twice or three times a year, collect some material, and then you can head back to try at your leisure. They’re willing to offer Sammie Steel for it.”

“What do you think, little buddy?” Trevor asked.

Clay tilted their head for a moment before nodding.

“Sounds acceptable,” their master translated.

“Good. Matilsa will be happy with that,” Lora said with a smile before it faltered slightly. “You also left an oversized impression with the customs clerk, Carrarl.”

“Well, I am, like, twenty Anderson Bags in one, and one of my Annexes is an actual Anderson Bag,” he replied with a shrug. “I’d be surprised if the thought of what I could do didn’t stick with him.”

“The boss just wanted to let you know.”

Trevor arched an eyebrow. “Any particular reason?”

“Carrarl wanted to put your passport back under revision until they could discuss it further,” Lora explained. “Because you could be some kind of smuggler or something. With Lady Adabelle, it was easy. The Power of Disaster? No, ick, keep it away! No offense, of course.”

“None taken, I fully understand,” the demonkin said.

“Thanks. But, the Power of Inventory? Sounds innocuous. Harmless. Like you’ve just got a bunch of spatial bags with you. Whoever approved it lacked the foresight to realize they’d just have to trust you to say when your inventory was empty.”

“Okay, I’m picking up what you’re laying down,” Trevor said, frowning. “And what does that mean for me?”

“Right now, probably nothing,” Lora admitted with a shrug. “They already approved your passport and they don’t like walking things like that back because it makes them look like they made a mistake. Kinda proud that way. Plus, the boss did a lot of vouching for you.”

“I don’t expect much to come of it,” Klar confirmed, sounding unworried. “Word will get back to the other merchant princes and their meddling would be the most you’d have to deal with. They’ve known about you for a long time since we’re business partners, but hearing of your capabilities from an official source is different from the rumors they had picked up along the way.”

“Is that something we need to worry about?” Adabelle asked, her tone stern.

“They won’t cause you any trouble. At least, not in a harmful way,” he chuckled with a shake of his head. “I just didn’t want you to be blindsided with offers to go and work with them when they randomly show up at your door.”

“Eh. I’ll just turn them away, anyway. They may be merchant princes, but they’re no Klarkartar,” Trevor said with a wave of his hand.

“At least hear them out first,” Klar replied, grinning at the loyalty of his friend. “I’m trying to spread the virtues of Summoned to my people, remember? The merchant princes are the gateway to making that happen, so someone as amiable as you working for them would be a great boon towards advancing my political agenda.”

“There sounds like there’s a but in there,” Adabelle said.

The minotaur laughed again. “But don’t be afraid to charge double, or even triple your going rate. They’ve got more than enough money, so feel free to strike their wallets to your heart’s content. The older ones, at least. I can provide you with a list of the good ones if you want.”

“Man, now I’m going to have to come up with rates,” Trevor joked. “But if that’s what you want, then I can help out. Especially if it means my wife can get that passport. We’ll expect more ballabroody once that happens.”

“Deal,” Klar said with a nod. “Ma will be happy to hear that, too.”

Lora cleared her throat after checking her watch. “Boss, looks like your meeting with Mayor Nester will be starting soon.”

“There’s always something to do, isn’t there?” the minotaur sighed.

“I understand that deeply,” Adabelle commiserated.

With a smile, he stood up and brushed off his parka. “I did receive a message that you wanted a trade between the hearts of ice and Firesap, Lady Adabelle, and I’ll see what I can do for you.”

“That would be appreciated,” the noblewoman said. She picked up Clay so that she could stand, too. Her husband joined her. “Trevor is carrying a good amount of them in his inventory and I’ve already given him a quest to deliver them home along with the rest of the cargo meant for Tosa. However, I wanted to thank you for this trip. It’s been very good to us both.”

“Absolutely,” the courier agreed. “You’ve done right by us, so I hope our work has helped you out.”

“My friends, I am just happy to hear that our plans for you were a hit,” Klar said happily before gesturing to Lora, who stood up. “And now, I believe it’s time to send you both home. We can talk about your trip in greater length once you’ve settled.”

“Sure thing,” Trevor said, smiling. “Happy negotiating.”

“Alright, shall we get you back to Tosa?” Lora offered as the minotaur headed into town hall.

“As nice as that would be, we actually wanted to head to the Feywood first if you don’t mind,” Adabelle informed the teleporter.

“Oh, right, for the dragon to dragon love letter! I had forgotten about that.”

“Not a love letter according to Croal,” Trevor corrected with a chuckle. “But yes. Glyrphizith shouldn’t be hibernating yet, right?”

“Not yet,” Adabelle confirmed. “She’ll be preparing, though.”

“And she’s near the Grandmother Tree?” Lora asked.

“Yes.”

“Alright, that should be easy enough!” she announced brightly. Adabelle was about to hand Clay off to Trevor, now well used to the stipulations of teleportation, but she shook her head and proudly jabbed her thumb against her chest. “No need for that. While you two have been enjoying yourself, I’ve leveled up and can now take a small amount of magical goods with me.”

“Congratulations, Lora!” Trevor said. “Magical bags, too?”

“Thanks! And, yes, but just small ones. I should be able to take Clay with me, too. They’re tiny," she replied before holding out her hands. “Ready?”

“Ready,” Adabelle said, reaching for the teleporter with her husband.

Lora once again scrunched up her face, but then relaxed slightly as she felt things out. With a smile, she activated her Power, and the snowy peaks disappeared for the Feywood. There was a slight cold permeating the forest, but nothing unpleasant. Shaman Brown hadn’t been kidding; they felt the weather immediately after leaving her influence.

The magical trees were still vibrant, but the leaves of the others had already changed color and were starting to fall. Ahead of them, the Grandmother Tree stood tall and proud. They had been taken directly to the center of the forest.

“Thank you, Lora. I don’t know if you wanted to stick around or not, but we can make it home from here,” Adabelle said with a smile.

“Are you sure?” she asked hesitantly. “My job was to get you three home safe and sound.”

“Don’t worry, we’re home. Plus, Clay can get us places very quickly,” Trevor assured her. “You’ve been great.”

Lora beamed brightly before lunging at them, capturing them all in a hug. She stood on the tip of her toes to kiss Adabelle’s cheek, then Trevor’s, and then leaned down to kiss the top of Clay’s head. Then, she backed away.

“Thanks for having me. It was a lot of fun!” she said happily. “I’ll try and get another assignment with you again soon!”

“You’re always welcome in Tosa,” Adabelle replied.

“Don’t be a stranger, alright?” Trevor requested.

Clay waved a short, stubby arm.

The teleporter seemed to hesitate for a moment before waving. “I won’t. Bye!” Lora said before disappearing from sight.

“I’m going to miss her,” Adabelle said once they were alone.

“Same.”

Clay nodded, also agreeing.

The demonkin’s tail wrapped around her husband’s wrist. “Shall we get going?”

“Sure. How far away is Glyrphizith from the Grandmother Tree?” Trevor asked, allowing her to lead him away.

“Not far. We actually passed by when we came for the ball.”

The courier tilted his head. “Did we? Come to think of it, I don’t remember seeing her when I was checking out the scenery on top of the tree. That seems a little strange to me now.”

“Nothing strange about it,” Adabelle replied. “Glyrphizith can’t exactly have all those clocks of hers exposed to the elements, can she?”

“Okay, yeah. I can see that.”

“Right. That’s why she lives underground.”

“Must be one heck of a cavern if she fits inside,” Trevor chuckled.

The newlyweds walked together in comfortable silence. They enjoyed the brisk weather, the colorful leaves, and the fact that they were so close to home after being away for so long. With every step, they could feel the weight of their travels behind them. It pushed them a little faster, ready to be home where they could rest amongst their family.

True to Adabelle’s words, Glyrphizith’s cavern was not far from the Feywood’s capital. They had only walked for half an hour before the road was swallowed by a hole wide enough that Trevor thought they could fit the Summoner’s Tower inside of it. As they descended, the demonkin made a series of whistles.

“So she knows we’re coming,” Adabelle offered.

“Okay, cool.”

The tunnel was relatively short, and they arrived inside a cavern half the size of Tosa. There was a large bed made of trees in the center, which Glyrphizith rested on, as well as a few other odds and ends.

Then there were the clocks.

They were absolutely everywhere. If they were freestanding, then they were on the ground. If they weren’t, then they were placed on or inside cubbyholes in the walls or on any of the myriad tables covering the floor. There had to have been over a thousand of them.

Croal had inherited his collection of colors from his mother, but they had nothing on the green dragon of the Feywood.

Despite the sheer amount of clocks, there was only a slight ticking noise that tickled at the corner of their ears. It seemed as though magic was dampening the sound, which Trevor could appreciate.

“Oh, if it isn’t my favorite pair of newlyweds!” Glyrphizith greeted warmly. “It’s not often you arrive unannounced, my little dragon slayer, but you are welcome regardless. How was your honeymoon? Shall I expect some little demons sometime next year?”

Adabelle bristled slightly at the nickname, especially as her husband stifled a snicker, but smiled regardless. “Thank you, but I would temper your expectations on that. We’ve already decided not to have children.”

The dragon brought a clawed hand to her chest. “Then I’m sorry for bringing it up. I am curious about your honeymoon, though.”

“It’s okay, and we had a wonderful time. There were many things we saw that I had only heard of from other Summoned before.”

“Yeah, we got to go all over the place, like another continent, and meet a ton of interesting people,” Trevor added.

“I bet you have,” Glyrphizith said before stretching out her neck towards them. “It smells like you’ve met another of my kind.”

“You dragons have good noses,” the courier remarked.

“We are the top of all food chains for a reason,” she said with a toothy grin.

Trevor just chuckled. “His name is Croal, from Paladona, and he lost his mother about two hundred years ago.” He paused, looking at his wife. “It was two hundred, right?”

“Roughly,” Adabelle answered before turning back to Glyrphizith. “He’s never met another of his kind before that wasn’t family, and he smelled you on us just as easily as you did him. We have a letter requesting a meeting.”

“I see,” she replied, tilting her head. “Well, you can leave it on the pile over there. I’m quite busy getting ready to hibernate, as you know, so I likely won’t be replying to him any time soon. It has been a while since I’ve met another dragon, though, so I’m intrigued. Paladona, you say?”

“Yup. It’s far to the south and, apparently, warm all year round,” Trevor said.

“Then consider me interested. I’ll sleep on it, and decide after I wake,” Glyrphizith responded.

The courier smiled. “That’s all he wanted. Over here, you said?”

“Yes, young man. Thank you.”

Trevor walked over to a pile of other envelopes. Some were fresh, but most of them were yellow from age. It didn’t seem like the dragon checked them very often. Probably due to her size. He made a mental note to return in spring to read it to her. It was the least he could do for Croal. The quest was completed the moment he set it down.
