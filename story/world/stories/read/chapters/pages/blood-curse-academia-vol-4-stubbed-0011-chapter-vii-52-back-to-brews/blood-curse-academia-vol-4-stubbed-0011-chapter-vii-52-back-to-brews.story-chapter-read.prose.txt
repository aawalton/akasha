Chapter V.II (5.2) - Back to Brews

Kizu took in a deep breath, internalizing the scent of his newest concoction. Often novice brewers relied too heavily on sight and ignored other key signifiers of a brew’s effectiveness. Every scent offered a different perspective. A lesson taught to him by the crone after she removed his eyes for a week. He’d nearly lost an entire hand’s worth of fingers in the process, but he had learned a lot in that week.

“Can you add a few more drops of Dragon blood?” he asked his current assistant.

Harumi, the first year who’d begged him for lessons in brewing, immediately scampered off to do his bidding, returning with a vial of the blood in hand.

“Blood is an extremely dangerous material component to work with,” Kizu told him.

“Because it’s the essence of their magic?” Harumi asked.

“That’s part of it. But it also is part of their soul. Their very being is mixed into blood. Distilling that can be difficult. Especially with sentient species.”

“But not impossible?”

“No.” Kizu gingerly took the vial from the younger student. The contents sloshed slightly as he palmed it. “Definitely not impossible. Unethical? That’s more debatable. But witches use all sorts of blood in their rituals and brews. Human key among them. It’s the most powerful of all materials, and therefore also the most deadly. It goes for all species though.”

The thought made his mind go back to the bloodspawn he’d met in his first semester at the academy. They’d been able to replicate the spells of the mages’ whose blood they’d consumed. They had a very unique relationship with blood. He wondered if he used bits of them in a potion if he’d be able to replicate that effect in a normal mage. Could he use them as a middleman to grant a mage the abilities of a different mage?

“I don’t usually use sentient creatures in potions,” Kizu clarified. Both to Harumi who was looking at him with wide eyes, and also to himself after going down that macabre line of thought. “This Dragon’s blood is one of the first I’ve used. Or any Dragon materials, for that matter.”

Yesterday Kizu had gone out to the other side of the island with Taroe to check in on Sojan. The knife, now firmly rooted in the roof of a Dragon’s mouth, had offered him a wide variety of fresh materials from his current body. Even offered to slice off a toe. So he had basically unlimited access to Dragon parts for the foreseeable future. The only thing not on the table was blood, as Sojan required that to sustain himself. So Kizu’s current plan was to use up the blood gifted to him by the headmaster while it was still fresh.

“I’ve heard of some being bought on a black market trade in Tross and Hon,” Harumi said. “Succubus is a popular one for illegal love potions. My dad said that there was a big problem with it in Hon back when he was a kid. Lots of his friends were a result of the boom in its popularity.”

Kizu blinked, surprised by Harumi’s insight. But he supposed this leaned more heavily into politics than it did traditional brewing knowledge. “Interesting. I knew about succubus blood. But not about laws. What sort of laws are in place in Hon?”

“Um. Just ones to keep really bad potions off the streets. Regulations and all,” Harumi said, sounding less certain. “I don’t really know specifics. But everyone says that succubus blood is really powerful stuff and not to get spiked with that.”

Kizu scowled. He knew about getting spiked first hand. The effects of just the drop or two of that potion he’d been affected by had not been subtle.

Deciding to leave it at that, Kizu stirred in a more rhythmic, up and down motion for a minute, then returned to normal. Biting his lip and holding his breath, he carefully applied one drop of the Dragon’s blood. A bubble ruptured, releasing a hiss of steam. But it soon became absorbed into the concoction.

Kizu let out his breath in a sigh of relief. “That went far better than I expected.”

“What?” Harumi looked from the cauldron, over to Kizu. “Did you think something bad would happen?”

“Well, hypothetically it could have erupted into an explosion of fire and boiling liquid.”

Harumi’s face paled so Kizu quickly continued.

“I put a few different safety precautions in place, but I’m not infallible. All the records I’ve found for brews using ingredients from Dragons were centuries old. And not all that reliable. So I was mostly making due with stories I heard growing up.”

Harumi gulped then his eyes drifted down to the brew in question. “What is it supposed to do? You didn’t explain it.”

“Last semester I experimented with a few transformation brews. I was toying with a concept back then, but didn’t have the proper ingredients to put it into action.”

“You want to transform into a Dragon?”

Kizu chuckled. “Nah. I have a different goal. I want to shift my body into a living element. Truly reach the pinnacle of both elemental spellcraft and brewing together.”

“You’re…transforming yourself into fire?”

Kizu raised the large stirring spoon he’d been using. The cauldron’s contents had worn away at the enchantments and fried it into a blackened husk. He swiftly replaced it and silently reprimanded himself for the oversight.

“That’s my plan. But I’ll need to perform some experiments first. It’s not a completely novel idea, but there are no modern records of brewers pulling off the feat. At least not that I’ve found in the academy library. But none of them had access to my materials.”

“Wow,” Harumi said, eyes wide. “You’re like, revolutionizing the art of brewing.”

“More like trying to revitalize it,” Kizu amended. “A friend of mine is tutoring me in history right now and he just explained that there was a period of fifty years where a Hon Emperor went on a crusade against witches. I’d known about him in passing before this, but apparently the man was so insane he started burning all records of brewing and astronomical maps the Empire got their hands on. It destroyed a lot of our art’s history. And also explains why the education here seems so much weaker than what I received in the Hon Basin. So much has been lost and willfully destroyed.”

“Oh. I heard about him. A witch stole his daughter from the cradle and ate her.”

“That’s one of the stories I came across,” Kizu said. “But there are a lot of varying accounts on what exactly happened.”

Personally, Kizu still believed the story the crone had told him about that princess entering a coven on her own accord and her father being pissed about it. But he decided to maybe keep that to himself rather than risk his words getting back to Krimpit and returning to the top of the grumpy professor’s ‘try to get these students expelled’ list.

Kizu added another drop of Dragon’s blood, only slightly tensing as it made contact with the concoction this time. Meanwhile, Harumi began to wander around his brewing laboratory, examining his budding collection of materials and equipment with absolute awe and fascination. After Kizu’s birthday party, he’d decided to start trusting the younger student more. Owl’s Respite wasn’t exactly his best kept secret anymore and the boy had been eager to learn more about brewing. No real reason had popped into his head not to allow him. Well, beside Kizu’s ship being more noticeable by Harumi’s father, the town’s constable. The last thing Kizu wanted at the moment was more adults with overblown senses of authority showing up on his deck and snooping around the ship. Even if everything Kizu was doing was mostly legal, Aoi’s experiments in the former captain’s cabin was…more of a gray area. Kizu wasn’t sure of the local government’s exact legislation around growing clones in vats, but for some reason his knee jerk reaction was to assume they wouldn’t approve of the hobby.

Regardless, he counted on Harumi to not rat him out to his father. The boy was so overjoyed by the invitation that Kizu doubted he’d knowingly betray him.

“This is what you used as a base for your potion?” the first-year asked while peering at Kizu’s water supply. “Is it just normal water? Why is the area under it so wide?”

“It’s a bath,” Kizu explained. He remained focused on the brew in front of him while he explained to Harumi. “I installed it about a month ago. I connected it to an abandoned hot spring village in the mountains of Hon near Kyonaka. Minerals aren’t my forte as a brewer taught by a witch, but I thought it might serve as a solid base since the water originated from a thermal heat source.”

“And it still works?” Harumi asked.

“Why wouldn’t it…” Kizu’s words trailed off as he picked up on what Harumi was implying. He pivoted to look at the flowing source of water before forcing his attention back on the brew. Despite the border closure of Hon, his portal remained open.

What reason was there for that? Could it be because the inn his gate linked to was owned by ghosts and considered sovereign land by some ancient ruling? But no…Tross was split because the spell Emperor Sasaki had unleashed was tied to the continental plate, not the country of Hon.

“That’s weird. I’ll think about it more later when I don’t have an unstable brew in front of me. Can you pass me the phoenix feather?”

“Phoenix feather?” Harumi’s already wide eyes turned to saucers.

“I talked Professor Knoff into letting me use one after explaining my experiment.”

Suddenly Harumi’s body slackened into a more relaxed position. “Oh. Professor Knoff knows about your potion?”

Kizu considered mentioning it was the more… eccentric of Knoff’s personalities that approved his studies, but decided to withhold the information. Most people didn’t realize Knoff’s soul was split and explaining the reason for his rapid personality shifts seemed like a conversation which would distract him from his current project.

A project Kizu noted was now on fire.
