It is said that when a giant mountain boar and a giant plains bull collided, they created the Boarbull of Shangra. There is no record of spontaneous boar-bull fusions anywhere in recorded history and this explanation is largely ignored by the general scientific community. More important than where it came from, was where it was currently located. As the Boarbull through forest, and mountains and plains, she searched for the one thing that would satiate both a boar and a bull. Truffles. Truffles for the monstrous ungulate, and it would ravage anything in it’s path to get what it wanted. In the past it had laid waste to Shangran topside towns. But n the last few years, she had made her way to Ulmund territory, recently spending time in the pastures outside of Milik, hunting madly for truffles.

The divisive nature of the boarbull came two schools of thought. The first group, the vast majority of people, believed the boarbull to be a wild animal, and its search for truffles would lead to ruin in its wake. Thus, the remedy prescribed is and has always been that they should kill the Boarbull before it finally made its way back to Shangra and razed the villages to the ground.

The other group, however, felt that the boarbull had more of a plan than it let on, and that the truffles were a metaphor for larger truth. That searching for the truffles was merely symbolic, and what it actually wanted was an imperialist expansion for Shangra. These people understood that the boarbull could be wily at times, but was mostly good, clearing human settlements and keeping humans from naturally establishing order on that side. These people were convinced that the murderous boarbull uprooting trees, cliffsides, and whole barns actually knew what it was doing, and was thus making a powerful statement. This small but growing minority was often disregarded for their foolishness and unwillingness to compromise.

Kip watched from on high as the Boarbull stuck its lower right tusk under the earth of an expansive oak tree. She craned her neck, uprooting the tree with ease. Then, she sniffed with her pig-like snout for the goods.

Kip yelled into the chlorophone wrapped around his ear and cupped against his mouth, “I’m starting to think this is a bad idea.”

He was sitting on Swiff, Azami’s broom as Azami held Kip and Jasssper up high in the air above the Boarbull, she yelled back into the chlorophone, “Do you want to turn back!? I have a slow roast in the oven.” “N-no!” Said Kip, “It’s just… this is a bad idea, right?”

Azami said, “I’m just here to assist.”

“What’s your thoughts on if it’s a good idea or a bad one?” Kip asked again.

“Uhh,” Azami said, “I think it’s one of those ideas you can’t think about too hard. You know? Do you have another idea for how to save Rexie?”

Kip shook his head, “I suppose I don’t. So… you think it’s a bad idea?”

She handed him a cleaver. “I got it from the castle blacksmith. Enchanted so it should take the boarbull’s meat off clean without it realizing. Swiff will fly in front of the boar, and Jasssper and I will keep it distracted while you take a chunk out! Then the Jasssper’s Ladder and we’re out of here!!”

“Okay,” Kip said, “If you think it’ll be easy. What do you think, Jasssper?”

“If anyone’s up for the task, it’s you!” He said.

“Okay,” Kip said, “I’m dropping!”

“Kip!” Azami said before he did, “If she notices you… I’m willing to admit it’s a bad idea!”

“Right,” And with that, Kip dropped from the broom. He dropped down while the behemoth boarbull shook her head to rid herself of a stuck bush on her tusk.

As Kip dropped closer, he spread his wings and glided. He approached the Boarbull.

The current of the wind kept him in place as he upturned his wings to stay still. The creature dug through the ground, unaware as Kip lightly pressed his feet to the Boarbull’s back. Kip grabbed to the tufts of the boar.

“Remember!” Jasssper hissed through the chlorophone, “You can’t kill it. But you can’t let it know you’re on there either.” Jasssper said, “The boarbull is a political maelstrom, killing her and not killing her both considered inflammatory stances.

“I understand,” Kip said.

He clutched the cleaver he’d been given. Dubbed by the blacksmith, Eonor, as ‘The Butcher’s Best Friend.’ It would allow you to take the meat off of any beast of burden without damaging them.

He shot an ‘okay’ symbol at his compatriots.

“What do you see?” Azami asked. “He’sss flashed the symbol. Now, to dissstract.”

Azami nodded, “Do you have the truffles?”

Jasssper looked at the small bag of truffles, “Yesss.”

Jasssper gulped. It had been discussed with a certain person when Jasssper informed them of this plan. He had been given clear orders. He was to sabotage Kip’s plans with the boar.

He wrapped his tail around one of the truffles, and thought to himself, “I’m sssorry, sire.” And dropped the truffle down as Azami lowered her broom.

“Okay!” Azami said, “I’ll cast a spell that amplifies the scent!” She stuck her wand down and cast it on the truffle. The scent boomed from the tiny truffle, reaching the nose of the Boarbull.

As Kip was about to cut the skin, the boarbull suddenly shifted directions to the truffle. Kip’s foot slipped and he dropped off the back, but held onto the tufts of hairs. He looked down at the powerful hooves as one landed on a stump and splintered it into a million pieces.

“Drat.” He placed his feet on the boar and cast ‘Burst.’ He toppled over and landed back on the back. The boar found the truffle and ate it.

“Okay!” Azami said, “Keep dropping the truffles one at a time so it’s curiosity stays piqued.” Swiff dropped lower, and Jasssper dropped another truffle. Kip sliced into the meat of the boarbull, the blade went in cleanly. Kip started to work around the boarbull’s back as it came toward the new bait.

Kip had worked the cleaver’s way to about a half circle, it’s radius as long as Kip laying down. He continued cleaving back to the starting cut.

Jasssper dropped another truffle. Kip made his way faster, finishing the circle early.

“Got it.” Kip said, then he lifted his thump in a second ‘okay’ symbol.

“We got it,” Azami said, “Come on! Let’s go! One more truffle!” Azami whirred her broom up, and Jasssper dropped one more truffle, he held up the bag, staring at it. He shook his snakey head as he realized what he must do.

Azami brought her broom up above the Boarbull, then lowered herself.

Kip grabbed the broom and said, “Stretchy hands.” Azami lifted the broom, and pull at Kip’s stretchy hands. Kip’s other hand held to the piece of bologna. They moved upward, and the once the extent of Kip’s stretchy hands reached, the bologna piece peeled off the boarbull.

“Success!” Kip said, “Now, let’s peel out of here!” Jasssper sighed, he looked at the piece of truffle in his hand, and back at Kip.

“Right Jasssper?” Kip asked.

“R-right.” Jasssper said, “Let me help you up, Sire.”

“No, I’m quite alright.”

“I insssist.” And Jasssper slithered to wrap himself around Kip’s arm, and dropped two truffles from the bag, landing on the boarbull.

They plopped on her forehead, and rolled down her nose, where she crossed her eyes to see both of them. She looked up and noticed noticed Azami on the broom.

She’s found us!” Azami screamed, “We have to get out of here!”

Azami turned Swiff, as Kip held on, flailing in the air as the bologna hung behind him, the boarbull ate the truffles, and was suddenly interested in the mysterious truffle lady and her companions.

It stamped its hoof, and chased after Azami’s broom, following them back to Shangra
