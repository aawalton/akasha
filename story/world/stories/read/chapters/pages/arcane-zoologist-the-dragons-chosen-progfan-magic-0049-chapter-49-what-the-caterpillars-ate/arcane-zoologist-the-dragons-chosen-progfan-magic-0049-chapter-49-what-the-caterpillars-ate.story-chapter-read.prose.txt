"What is going on?" Callie muttered in surprise at her mark. Mom, Dad, and Callen gathered around her, staring at it as well.

"A-Are you trying to do something with magic?" Callen asked.

Callie shook her head. "No. But it feels… strange. Like it's reacting to something."

"Is it an arcane beast?" Mom's gaze sharpened.

Callie tried to feel the magic within her for a moment. It wasn't the same all those times Callie had been with arcane beasts. Neither did it feel like the time she had sensed the egg.

This was more wild. The magic surged within her like wind—blowing and howling one second, then calming down the next. In fact…

Callie ran up to the window and listened to the storm outside. As the wind picked up and threw snowflakes at the window, the glow of her mark intensified. When the wind calmed, the glow of the mark also seemed to calm.

"I think it's reacting to the storm?" She said, unsure.

Mom, Dad, and Callen also came up to the window, looking at the howling winds and then at Callie's mark in turn. It really was quite difficult to tell whether it matched the winds, since it flickered a lot, but that's how it felt to Callie.

"This isn't a normal storm…" Mom muttered glumly.

"What do we do, Maeve? If we can't get the fire going…"

"Maybe I can try?" Callie suggested.

"Are you sure you can control your magic in this state?" Dad asked.

"I… I'm not sure, but it's worth a try, isn't it? We need fire," Callie said.

"Let me try for a bit longer. If I can't get it going, then you can try," Dad suggested.

Dad once again tried his magic to get the fire going, but just like before, the fire just refused to remain lit. It was getting colder and colder within the home, and Callie soon found herself wearing the coat and still feeling cold.

Once it was clear that magic wouldn't work, Dad tried the flint and still, but that didn't do any better. It was as if the wood was soaked in water, although it felt dry, and suspiciously cold to the touch. Almost as if it were frozen. But that couldn't be. The water in their bucket wasn't frozen, so the wood shouldn't be either.

"Okay, you can give it a try. Maeve, be ready with water," Dad said.

Mom nodded, walking to stand behind Callie. Dad placed a bucket of water next to her, just in case Callie's magic went wild.

Normally, her parents wouldn't be so worried. After all, her recent practice with growing carrots has helped her to better grasp the flow of magic. Or rather, she had gotten better at putting herself into the right mindspace.

For fire magic, it was always riskier. It required her to feel frustrated and angry, and these emotions were always quick to take over her mind and run out of control. Like wildfire. She needed to keep it to the steady burning of the hearth. Warm and strong, but not destructive.

Maybe I don't need to imagine anger to get the fire going. Maybe I can use the same method I used for growing carrots.

A gentle flame wouldn't need a lot of power. It needed wood, like the one in the fireplace. It needed air, which was also in abundance thanks to the all-powerful wind that found its way through even the smallest of holes. Most importantly of all, it needed a dry, comfortable place.

However, the magic within her roiled unsteadily with the storm. At some points, it felt like it would just break out of her in an angry blaze. Callie held it back—she didn't want to burn her house down. Then the magic retreated again within her, not wanting to come out.

It was most frustrating. As if magic wasn't difficult enough to control already, this storm was throwing it into a complete disarray. Callie just wanted to let it out in an angry burst, as this feeling was making her queasy.

But that would be dangerous, so she took a deep breath to calm herself. Callie waited for the right moment when the magic was neither trying to burst out nor withdraw within her. At just the right time, she released it.

The fire came out in a powerful stream, more powerful than Callie had wanted, but not so large as to threaten burning anything it shouldn't. The magical fire engulfed the firewood and then fizzled out.

"What?" Callie gasped. Did she not use enough energy? Did the fire not have enough time to catch?

But then a little flame appeared by one of the logs and began to grow in intensity. Callie and her family watched as, slowly but steadily, the fire spread from one piece to the next until the fireplace was properly burning.

"It worked!" Callie cheered.

"Good job, kiddo!" Dad said with a grin, ruffling her hair.

"It's strange… Why didn't your fire work, yet Callie's did?" Mom asked thoughtfully. "It didn't seem like yours would be any weaker, Rowan."

"Is it because Callie's magic is different?" Callen guessed.

"That might be it. Hmm…" Mom looked thoughtful. "I'm going to go into the village. Check how others are doing. Whether they can get the fire going."

"Let me go," Dad said, stopping Mom. "You've never been good with cold."

Mom looked like she wanted to object, but nodded and allowed Dad to go.

Meanwhile, Callen decided to make breakfast, while Mom and Callie did their best to keep their home warm. They brought the frozen firewood from the storage by the fireplace, where the frost quickly melted, leaving behind perfectly dry wood.

It was incredibly odd. One would have expected the logs to be at least a little moist after they had frost on them. But they weren't. Meanwhile, the frost on the firewood in the storage only grew into a thicker and thicker layer.

No matter how hard they looked, they couldn't find any holes where the cold could be coming from. It was just growing on the logs like green grass was growing after a warm summer shower. It made no sense.

After eating a modest breakfast of flatbreads and cheese, Callen and Mom decided to go and check on the animals. They worried that this strange storm might have impacted the animals as well.

Normally, they wouldn't worry about them. The coop and stables were both stacked to the brim with hay, which protected the animals from the worst of the cold. But this wasn't a normal snowstorm.

"Can I go with my brother?" Callie asked, worried about the little birds. Gale was much sturdier than the chickens, so he was likely fine.

"The storm outside is horrible. It's better if you stay at home," Mom said.

"Please… It's not like it's far…"

Mom sighed. "Okay, okay. But don't stay out for long."

Callie and Callen both huddled up in as many layers as they could and headed out into the storm. She could barely see anything. It was white all around, and to make matters worse, the wind was constantly blowing sharp snowflakes into her face.

If it wasn't for Callen's sturdy hand and him making a path in the deep snow, Callie wasn't sure if she could have even made it to the coop that was in their own backyard. That's how bad the storm was.

When they got into the coop, the chicken clucked in annoyance at having their home torn open in this storm. But Callen closed the door quickly. Despite it being daytime, it was rather dark in the coop. The windows had been covered with wooden shutters to preserve as much warmth as possible.

To Callie's surprise, the coop was warmer than she had imagined. The chickens were huddled close together but seemed comfortable enough.

"They seem fine. Thank the heavens," Callen said in relief.

"It's amazing how well hay keeps the cold out. Maybe we should line the house with hay," Callie joked.

"And have a house full of mice? No thanks," Callen chuckled. "Let's check if there's none of that weird frost anywhere."

Callie nodded, and they went through the coop, checking as they could in the poor light. Since she couldn't see much, Callie took off her gloves and checked the corners with her fingers, checking for any of the weird frost.

Suddenly, her fingers came upon something squishy and slightly fuzzy, so she yelped. "AAAH!"

"What's wrong?" Callen came running to her right away, and the chickens clucked nervously at the disturbance.

"Sorry, I just touched something squishy," Callie said, and felt around carefully. Eventually, she came upon the squishy thing again, picking it up.

Warm. Warm.

Callie nearly dropped the squishy thing when she heard its soft, minute voice. Bringing it closer, she saw that it was a frost caterpillar, peering up at her with its large eyes.

"Oh, sorry! I didn't mean to disturb you," Callie said apologetically and put the caterpillar down.

As her vision adjusted to the dim light, she saw that this wasn't the only caterpillar in the coop. It seemed like two dozen of them had somehow made their way inside.

Food. Food. Food.

Their little voices sang in unison.

"What is it?" Callen asked.

"Frost caterpillars. And they seem to be eating something," Callie puzzled, but couldn't make out what.

"Not they hay! If they start eating hay, the chickens could freeze!"

Tasty. Tasty.

The caterpillars were none the wiser at Callen's outburst. But Callie did wonder if they were eating hay.

"Have you ever seen them eat hay?" Callie asked her brother.

He paused and scratched his head thoughtfully. "Hmm… I don't think so. No. Maybe they are doing so out of desperation. Because of the storm?"

Callie crouched down, looking at the frost caterpillars. Despite there being a lot of them, they were clumped together in one spot. The hay was scattered all over the place, so they could have easily spread out if it were the hay that they were eating.

"I don't think they are eating hay, Brother," Callie said.

"What are they eating then? There's nothing else here except… It's not chicken poop, is it?" The disgust in his voice was clear.

Callie frowned. Would caterpillars really eat poop? Some bugs did that, but she'd never noticed frost caterpillars doing it. In the dim light of the coop, she just couldn't see exactly what it was that the caterpillars were eating.

"C-Can you ask them?" Callen wondered.

"I guess I can try."

She cleared out her throat and crouched down close to the frost caterpillars. "Excuse me, could you please tell me what you are eating?"

Tasty. Tasty.

Food. Food.

Eat. Eat.

They chanted the same words on repeat. Their words were very primitive; it was likely that they couldn't even say much beyond that.

"They just say "food" and "eat" all the time. I don't think they can speak well enough to tell me. If they can even understand me."

Callen fell into thought for a moment. "Maybe let's check the stables. If they eat hay, then they should be in there too."

Callie nodded, and together, hand in hand, they trudged through the snow again, making their way to the stables.

"What are you doing here, kids?" Mom asked in surprise. She had been brushing Gale. It appeared that the strong wind had unsettled the stallion, making him pace nervously around his pen.

"We came to check on something. Have you noticed any frost caterpillars in here?" Callie asked.

"Frost caterpillars? I haven't checked, to be honest. Never would I expect them to be inside," Mom admitted.

Callie and Callen shared a glance and began looking through the stables. It was dark in here, too, but not as dark as in the coop. Mom had lit an oil lamp, which hung by Gale's pen.

Before long, Callie found a cluster of the frost caterpillars in the corner of the stables, while Callen found another cluster in another corner.

Food. Eat. Tasty.

"They are eating something here, too," Callie said.

"Not the hay!" Mom exclaimed nervously.

"Definitely not the poop," Callen noted with a bit of relief.

"Can you bring the lamp over here?" Callie asked.

Mom came over with the oil lamp, and they peered at the caterpillars. So close together as they were, Callie couldn't see what they were eating.

"Excuse me, I'm going to move you for a second. You will be able to return to eating just in a moment," Callie warned the caterpillar and gently lifted them.

Underneath the cluster, they saw that frost had formed on the hay. It looked very much the same as the frost that had appeared on their firewood.

"They are eating frost!" Callie announced. It made sense. Frost caterpillars were always eating frost from trees and bushes, so now they ate the frost that formed on hay. Would they also eat the one that was formed on firewood? What even was this strange frost that could appear even indoors?
