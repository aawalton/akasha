Cade’s fist plunged through the skull of the final guardian—a skeletal monstrosity with too many jaws and not enough eyes. Lava coursed through his veins and erupted from his knuckles, melting bone and ancient magic alike. The creature collapsed in a heap of smoldering remains as Cade shook droplets of molten rock from his fingers.

“That’s the last one,” he panted, eyeing the grand staircase that spiraled upward toward the cathedral ceiling. Toward Chubbs.

Behind him, his crew gathered their breath and weapons. Nora’s massive greatsword dripped with spectral ichor. Elena wound her whip-dagger back into a loose coil. Gav’s partial transformation receded, the fur along his forearms melting back into human skin.

“Everyone still alive?” Cade asked, scanning them for injuries. “Everyone still have all their extremities?”

“Depends,” Elena quipped, spitting blood onto the ancient stone. “Does dignity count as an extremity?”

“If it did, we’d have lost it floors ago,” Gav muttered, adjusting his somehow still-pristine ascot.

Evie drifted between them, touching shoulders, checking wounds with gentle fingers. Her eyes had regained their usual sparkle after her prophetic trance earlier.

Cade’s gaze drifted to the staircase again, and his expression hardened. “Bernard’s up there with Chubbs. Whatever ritual he’s performing, it’s connected to this entire crypt. Every trap, every ward, every monster—they’re all part of it.”

“So what’s the plan?” Nora asked, sliding her blade into its sheath across her back.

Cade’s lips curved into a smile that made his crew simultaneously relieved and terrified. It was his heist smile—the one that preceded either brilliant success or spectacular disaster.

“Bernard thinks he’s clever. He’s got his lieutenants guarding him while he performs the ritual. Augustus, Elizabeth, Nash, Rufus—they’ll form a perimeter.” Cade began pacing, his mind racing faster than his words. “We split up. Each of us takes one. Elena, you’re fastest—you take Nash. Gav, you’ve got history with Rufus—he’s yours. Nora, you handle Elizabeth. Evie, you distract Augustus with that goat staff of yours.”

“And you?” Elena demanded.

“Bunny and I will slip through the chaos and get to Chubbs.” Cade looked down at the white-scaled dragonling, who chirped enthusiastically. “Bernard’s focusing on the ritual. He won’t expect a direct assault.”

Nora frowned. “Against a possessed gold-ranked mage? That’s suicide.”

“Not if we break his concentration first.” Cade turned to her with barely contained excitement. “That’s where your Pandemonium Box comes in. Remember Evie’s prophecy? ‘When chaos sleeps within the flame, and order breaks its chains.’ It’s time we fulfill that prophecy. We release the box at the heart of Bernard’s ritual, and the resulting chaos will disrupt everything he’s built.”

The crew exchanged glances—half hopeful, half skeptical.

“That...” Gav started, “might actually work.”

As they gathered their weapons and prepared to ascend, Cade turned to Nora. “So, how long to unlock the box?”

Nora rubbed the back of her neck, suddenly intensely interested in her boots. “Ah... well... that’s the thing...”

Everyone slowly turned to stare at her.

“I haven’t exactly... solved the riddle yet.”

The silence was deafening.

“You… You’ve had it for weeks now,” Cade repeated flatly.

Nora pulled out the obsidian cube, its surface etched with arcane symbols. “What? It’s not like I can just smash it open.”

“And you’re telling us this now?” Elena hissed. “Why didn’t you, oh I don’t know, ask literally anyone for help? I fucking love riddles!”

“I’ve been trying!” Nora protested. “But I’ve always hated word games. Back in the Fateweavers, they strongly discouraged us from even attempting to solve Fate’s mysteries. ‘Record, don’t interpret’—that was practically tattooed on our eyelids.” She sighed. “I’ve been recording Evie’s prophecies for years, but actively solving riddles? That was heresy.”

Gav threw his hands up. “If one more riddle is going to determine whether we live or die, I swear I’m going to hunt down and kill every single god responsible for this cosmic injustice.”

Everyone blinked at him, startled by the vehemence in his voice.

Evie patted his arm gently, her expression laced with concerned worry. “Oh, honey. Who hurt you?”

“The universe,” Gav muttered, smoothing his ascot with what little dignity he still possessed. “The universe hurt me.”

Cade pinched the bridge of his nose. “New plan. Nora, you stay back and work on that riddle. The rest of us proceed as planned. When—not if, when—you solve it, bring it to the cathedral. We’ll buy you as much time as we can.”

Nora nodded grimly, turning the box over in her hands.

“Bunny and I will create the initial distraction,” Cade continued. “The rest of you find your targets and take them down.”

As they gathered at the foot of the stairs, Cade turned to look back at each of them—his family, forged in blood and fire.

The people he’d die for. The people he’d live for.

“We’ve lost Jer. We’ve lost Orro.” His voice caught, but he pushed through. “I’m not losing anyone else today. We’re going to steal back our Chubbs and ruin whatever Evil Bernard is trying to conjure, because screw that guy. We go in, we get her, we get out. We blow shit up.” He reached out, placing his hand in the center of their circle. “For Jer.”

One by one, they added their hands to the pile. “For Jer,” they echoed.

With a final nod, they separated, moving up the stairs toward the cathedral where Bernard waited with Chubbs. Cade, with Bunny perched on his shoulder, caught Nora’s eye one last time before she disappeared into an alcove with the pandemonium box.

Bunny nuzzled Cade’s ear and made a soft chirping sound.

“Yeah, buddy,” Cade whispered, his eyes fixed on the glow emanating from above. “Let’s go get our girl.”

White light exploded across Orro’s vision as the ghostly figure slammed into them.

When consciousness returned, he found himself sprawled on a crystalline surface that hummed beneath his body. Each breath burned like inhaling shards of glass. Beside him, Jer groaned, curling into himself as though trying to gather scattered pieces of his own existence.

“Oh. C’mon, Jer. That was a breeze compared to that stew Elena cooked us,” Orro managed, testing each limb and finding them remarkably intact despite the sensation of having been torn apart and reassembled.

“Nope. This is worse,” Jer croaked, slowly pushing himself upright. His eyes widened as he took in their surroundings. “Sweet merciful gods...”

They stood in a deep chasm, mist swimming across their vision. Far above the sheer wall that now dominated their vision was the crystalline deposit they’d first arrived on.

That meant that they were now in the abyss of swirling, impossible, colors. What passed for sky writhed with energies that hurt to look at directly. But most striking were the lines of power stretching around them—radiant veins that pulsed with vibrant magic.

The Grassblade exile counted seven vines of energy in total, with one extending from where they’d arrived and toward some distant point in this massive canyon. The six others came from different directions, but they too were connected to some central point far into this abyss.

“Where are we?” Jer whispered, raking his fingers through his wet red hair.

Orro steadied himself against a crystal spire that looked like it had fallen from the cliff above and wedged into the strange black sand beneath their feet. “The spirit realm. Still. But different. I think the ghost creature teleported us here.”

At the thought, he swiveled, searching the chasm for the crackling silhouette. It took him longer than he would’ve liked to spot the strange entity, but he eventually zeroed in on a floating mirage of heat and rippling air.

The figure approached. As it drew nearer, details emerged—a vaguely masculine form composed of golden-red motes that coalesced and dispersed in constant motion. Its eyes—if such features could be called eyes—burned with radiant red energy.

“I don’t think it means to harm us,” Orro said, surprising himself with the certainty in his voice.

The figure gestured for them to follow, and the creature started to enter the mist that led deeper into the chasm. One of the massive vine-like tendrils throbbed over their head, briefly lighting up the black sand in that direction.

“What choice do we have?” Jer shrugged, rubbing his wrists. “Lead on, sparkly nightmare person.”

They followed the wavering specter into the canyon. Around them stretched an impossible landscape—a web of luminous strands that pulsed with colors Orro had no names for. The strands had energy flowing inside in a loose facsimile of rivers, converging at a central point where something was glowing more brightly with each pulse of the ethereal vines.

The figure pointed forward, its arm stretching unnaturally long as it flickered.

“I think it wants us to look at that,” Jer said unnecessarily.

The figure nodded, its face briefly forming a smile that felt achingly familiar, though Orro couldn’t place why. Then it dispersed into a cloud of golden-red particles that rained down over them before vanishing entirely.

“Well that was—”

“Helpful,” Orro cut in, peering over the edge. The strands of energy—ley lines, he vaguely recalled from old texts—were being pulled toward a central point. Something unnatural was happening. “We need to get over there.”

“In there? You want to follow the spooky canyon guide’s advice? You know that this is the spirit realm, right? For all we know, that’s where the gods have their orgies, or torture idiot mortals for venturing this far into their domain!”

“There,” Orro pointed to a crystalline deposit that formed decent cover near the lightworks. “Would you prefer to stay here and wait for whatever was hunting us before to find us again?”

Jer sighed dramatically. “After you, O suicidal assassin.”

The trek through the black sand was harrowing. Twice, crystal shards hidden just beneath the surface shattered beneath Orro’s boots, nearly sending him plummeting into bottomless sinkholes. Three times, Jer’s injured ankle threatened to give out entirely. By the time they reached the kaleidoscopic lights, both were bleeding from a dozen minor cuts where sharp crystal edges had sliced through clothing and skin.

“Next time,” Jer gasped, “I’m staying manacled to the wall.”

The ley lines stretched before them like a glowing roadway, leading toward the central convergence. Some pulsed with familiar colors—the vibrant green of life, the deep blue of water, the angry red of destruction. Others glowed with hues Orro had never seen before, colors that seemed to exist between the known spectrum.

“We need to get closer,” Orro said grimly. He shaded his eyes over the pulsing lights overhead.

“Oh, is that all? Just stroll in there? Again, Or… Torture chamber. Evil god-orgy. Maybe both. Mortals don’t delve here for a reason—Oh, you’re already going in. Great.”

Orro took a tentative step into the light’s circle, still using the crystalline construct as cover to block one side of a potential attack. “See? Not dead.”

“Your confidence is overwhelming,” Jer muttered, but followed suit.

They made their way across the web of energy, each step an exercise in endurance and insanity. Orro knew he was tempting fate, but he needed to know what was going on in this place. If it offered in the slightest chance of exiting this place, he needed to know. He owed it to Jer to get him out of this mess.

They walked around the circumference of the strange lights, the ground here a latticework of partially glassified black sand. When they made it nearly to the opposite side of the circle, a strange figure emerged from around the pillar of vibrant energy. Orro’s mind went into overdrive as he spotted a familiar mustache.

“Bernard,” he whispered.

But it wasn’t quite Bernard. The Elysian crime lord’s body hung limp, like a puppet dangling from strings. Around his head, a crown of twisted metal gleamed with unholy light. And attached to the crown was... something else. A creature of darkness and malice, its form constantly shifting between humanoid and bestial. Its eyes—all six of them—glowed with ancient emerald hunger.

“What in all the hells is that?” Jer breathed.

“I don’t know,” Orro admitted. “But it’s controlling him somehow. Through the crown. Oh gods, I really hope that isn’t the same artifact we’re supposed to steal.”

As if hearing them, the creature’s head snapped in their direction. Six golden eyes fixed on them with predatory focus.

“Move,” Orro hissed, shoving Jer forward. “Now.”

They abandoned stealth for speed, racing across the black sand and back to the cover of the crystals. Each step sent fire coursing through their bodies, but fear drove them onward. The creature controlling Bernard didn’t pursue, but its gaze never left them—calculating, patient, ancient.

The seven ley lines flared as the Not-Bernard lifted his hands into the beacon of light that connected to all of the magical cords. Orro winced and had to duck under their cover to prevent his eyes from going blind. When the light dimmed to a bearable level, he risked a peek into the circle of energy. A skeletal structure of pure magic rose from where all ley lines met—branches spreading upward in the unmistakable shape of a tree. With each pulse of the lines, it grew more substantial, more real.

“That can’t be good,” Jer observed.

Before Orro could respond, a tremor shook the entire realm. A fissure appeared near the base of the forming tree—a tear in the fabric of reality itself. Through it, Orro caught glimpses of stone walls, flashing green ritual light, and for the briefest moment, a blinding golden glow centered around a small form.

“Chubbs,” he breathed, recognizing that distinctive energy.

“That’s our way out,” Jer said, pointing to the widening fissure. “Has to be.”

A terrible screech tore through the realm, so piercing that both men dropped to their knees, hands clutched over their ears. When Orro managed to look up, his blood ran cold.

A shape moved through the mists at the edges of the web—a creature of shadow and nightmares, its form constantly shifting between humanoid and something far more alien. Rippling muscles were briefly exposed as the shadows danced off of its broad shoulders and back. Twin serpentine eyes gleamed in its approximation of a face, and each movement left trails of darkness in its wake.

“What… what is that?” Jer asked, his voice barely above a whisper.

The entity glided across the ley lines with disturbing ease. It was beelining for the fissure while black tendrils smoked out of the growing rip in reality. Orro had a horrible, sinking, feeling.

Something—or someone—was drawing this creature toward it.

Worse, if that thing entered through that portal, his friends would have to face it in addition to whatever traps or defenses Bernard had set in place. And given his impression of the monster’s powers, it had to be at the very peak of Celestial rank, if not higher. For all he knew it could be a lost god from this accursed place.

“C’mon. We have to beat it there,” Orro said, pulling Jer to his feet. “We have to warn them.”

They ran, ignoring the agony of magical energies searing through them with each step. The fissure grew larger as they approached, reality stretching thin where the tree’s power strained against the boundaries between worlds.

Behind them, the beast accelerated, appearing to sense their intent. It moved with the inevitable purpose of an avalanche—unstoppable, patient, deadly.

“He’s gaining,” Jer panted, his injured ankle threatening to collapse with each stride.

Orro didn’t waste breath responding. The fissure was close now—so close he could almost feel the cold stone air of the crypt beyond. Through it, shadows moved: his crew, his family, fighting for their lives.

The monstrosity dove for the portal. They were too late.
