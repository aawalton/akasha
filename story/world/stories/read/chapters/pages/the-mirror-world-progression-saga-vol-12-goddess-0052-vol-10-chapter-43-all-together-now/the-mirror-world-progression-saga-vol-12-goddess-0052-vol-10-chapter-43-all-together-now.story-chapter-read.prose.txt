With the dawn came the fateful hour of battle once more. Sadly, the sun hardly shone, the sky gray and overcast in that way you knew would not even bother raining. Preserved dreariness, and most days, that was how that region was, with little exception.

Oddly, one single star shone through it all. She didn’t recognize it.

An omen, surely.

The battlefield at Matrazere was quite different from Rivermount. The Dominion’s forces were more extensive still, and the place they hoped to defend was a far more concentrated location. The fortress was composed of four towers, one larger and more central, and surrounded by high walls that were themselves a big problem to get troops over or through.

The armies spread out in all directions, including a network of undead and gargoyles hovering in the air to screen against any fliers hoping to bypass the ground approach. Most forces were concentrated facing the approach from the south and the southwest, but Samantha couldn’t and wouldn’t take the bait of a flank, as the approach to the fortress had to be secured for this battle, not blitzed into.

With their enemy outnumbering them yet again, they could find themselves surrounded and enveloped. As such, they had to carve their way more ploddingly. They had to decimate their enemy’s deliberately presented, expendable troops. If they couldn’t, they would have to get desperate.

Before the endless hordes of the enemy were engaged, final preparations bloomed over the Samanthian and alliance forces like a storm of magic and shuffling, wheeling troops. Their allies from Burnstire fell in beside them as they presented a united mass front to march forward.

Last minute, Tashome contacted her mentally, out of the blue. “I have something of a breakthrough that might be ready.” He seemed poised and trying to contain excitement, of all things. “I still need to execute it. Tell me: do you need extra oomph for Matrazere? I need to know now. You’ll need to expedite me there soon, too.”

Samantha felt nothing but alarm. His excitement was disturbing. He didn’t get excited about anything. “For the love of all holiness, Tashome, do not rush whatever the hell you’re brewing.” She thought quickly for an excuse to help sway him. Quickly, she realized she had a great one. “We still need things for the battle at the Capitol. Or some other unforeseen place. I think our plans here so far remain sound.”

“You mean they still have a snowball’s chance in hell?”

“A ball of unknown composition. Hopefully, it can take the heat.”

“Right. If things get bleak, back out, Samantha. I can be the backup plan.”

“Seriously, whatever it is, don’t. I want to discuss it beforehand. Later.”

“Fine. But tell me you’ll back out before it’s too late. Don’t overcommit.”

“I won’t,” she lied. “I’ll back out if things go to shit, I promise.”

“Alright then.”

Samantha breathed out a sigh, shook off the distraction, and re-focused on the extremely imminent battle. She had a zillion final buffs to cast, and he was bugging her with some abominable plot that would probably turn him into Cthulhu or something! He really thought she’d save her own skin just for some rush job sacrifice of his in her place?

The Hanged Man is trying to hang himself. Isn’t he? Fuck my life sometimes, I swear…

The first of the fighting was not very epic, and it was on the ground; flyers on both sides held back. No convenient champions presented themselves in the foot-bound vanguard. Instead, it became sheer chaos as elite, heavily armored and buffed troops clashed largely as they had been in skirmishing for days on end — only now it was more a matter of her swift shock troops trying to break into unyielding zombie pike formations like a dreadful porcupine of steel and wood.

But they had to fall eventually, from the sheer press of protected bodies. Enchantments such as wound negations took a lot of the sauce out of the unapproachability of a pike wall. Men and undead fell, and the chaos of long-distance magic interacting sizzled in the air. As usual, Samantha’s core, the ‘middle’ of the pack that had to stay stable, endured the magic barrage without issue, countering with Antimagic, counterspells, and sheer resistance, as aided by the ‘bedrock’ artifact they’d leveraged for the third time in a grand battle.

If the enemy counted on claiming the buff themselves, they were soundly disappointed. Tashome had perfected it once more, honing it down to the core of its intent, and that insane buff only applied to allies of the minotaurs. They’d made sure that applied for every group of their own.

And she was grateful for that. In addition to helping them, the buffs claimed by the enemy were plenty high enough. If they could’ve claimed the bonus, arcane magic would’ve been dubious to use.

Samantha wanted to directly partake, but it wasn’t her role, and on this scale, she was better off layering buffs until the time came to step things up. In addition to wound negations draining like candy, in wide-scale battle, the ‘5 seconds per level’ type buffs would fall off many, many times over the course. Besides that, she needed to get a feel for things.

Samanthian side magic attacks as a whole were mostly via efficient divine light attacks, on the presumption of being better against undead. Just as with their side, countermagic was thick in response where the fighting was fiercest. Any arcane specialists could use fire just fine.

In both cases, attacks began to shift further away from the fighting, seeking hollows of the concentrated protections. And so they traded seemingly irrelevant ‘casualties.’

Not good. Not how things must go. The Dominion will take this all day. But we had to establish our baseline, didn’t we?

Through it all, Samantha felt justified in her determined role for herself. Now that the battle was engaged, the feel of their coyness clung in the air. Now that blood was drawn, its spilling was like hematomancy gleaning her purpose. And this was all mere confirmation of the Brain Trust’s analysis and determination of the obvious truth: she was still the target.

They lay in wait to do what they could not help but want to do: cut off the head of the snake. End it all with the death of the ‘parasite’ herself. They wouldn’t stop trying until their will was entirely broken. They’d keep coming for her throat as the only threat to their rule. And why shouldn’t they? They were fucking right. She’d seen just how much the whole balance of everything in her order came down on the pinhead of her own divine leadership. She’d contrived this weakness to things. It was the only way to protect everyone else, and the only way forward was to endure. As always, to force the weakness into strength. Take it. Eat it all.

We’ll see who and what is unbreakable by the end of this.

All too soon, the previously ‘unassailable’ pike wall of the enemy became a melee of hand weapon use in closer quarters, and internal regrouping on both sides, and then more engagement, at most to the length of a spear. Samantha’s ‘medical evac system’ took the grievously wounded, mauled, and delimbed away on floating stretchers, headed for the backlines and varieties of more extensive medical care. Lighter wounds rarely lasted long, with Resemblants saturating the field, slinging healing wherever needed.

The other side was doggedly difficult to put down, naturally. The gray-skinned, dead-eyed, emotionless, and silent hordes kept working no matter what. They just kept fighting, even if their torso or face was half ripped apart, or even if they were missing an arm. A surprising number of fallen zombies caused wounds despite missing legs, from daggers pulled while they lunged at enemies from the ground. Soldiers had to be notified to watch for lurkers, and preferably follow-up fighters should go for decapitations or head shots. Neither was easy with a struggling, armored zombie.

Holy crap. Need a bunch of guys mixed in two-handed picks or warhammers, to serve as zombie executioners.

Meanwhile, Burnstire’s side was already tearing ass in utilizing elite blue- and green-skinned Daenriar trolls wading into zombie masses and making them explode. The trolls already liked swinging around huge two-handed weapons well suited to splatting zombies. Meanwhile, the high-level, magic-resistant warriors were further hyper-buffed by alliance magic and basically ignored the magic erupting in their midst.

Seeing the sight of their wanton group slaughter, the tall warriors, proud, smooth, and almost elegant in their dance, it once again struck Samantha at how odd the term ‘troll’ was for them. They were just bad-ass fae orcs. Most of them were decked out in bronze armor, with fearsome bestial masks.

Their king was not yet fighting. Samantha didn’t see the point in wasting the ‘elite of the elite’ chopping up rabble with his troops, much as he loved the idea. He hadn’t much liked the separation, at first — until Azure pointed out that his warriors could grab their own glory for a while instead of him hogging it all. That made the giant laugh, and he seemed satisfied enough about it. Samantha also promised that if he wanted battle, he’d get it, just at the opportune time, in her ‘special assembly’ yet to reveal.

Possibly very soon…

The Burnstirian knights were not spared glory, either. They charged in curving wheels and dipped into the ranks on the flanks, as broken up by the Daenriar as well as ranged fire from their light cavalry. They quickly showed themselves to have some of the most effective ‘conventional’ ranged fire to the task, as the ammo made endless small detonations in the ranks, like fireworks going off. The pikewalls where they concentrated fire were broken to hell from a single volley, making for quick work from the charging heavy knights smashing into the smoking remainders.

With them being so effective, Samantha declared into the global realm gestalt, “Alright, the Burnstirians are showing us up too much, folks. Time for Phase Two. Time to get epiiiic!”

On cue, ranks parted as if elephants were coming through on the charge. Metaphorically speaking, they were. Hundreds of their strongest built into an ironclad block regiment, flanked by champions, headed by living legends, new and old: Zadkiel, Redberry, Gelmak Por, Bast, Deikmorn Brakka, Nyomel, Canmore, Bammondy, Sir Remus, Eupraxia, Mr. Agatolis, Michael, Gabriel, Seraphiel, Athos, Dart, Porthos, Azure & Deathsting…

The newest addition was among the most impressive. A giant stood two-and-a-half meters tall, decked in breath-stealing authentic black dragon scale, his helm like a fearsome dragon head with a glittering crown atop it. His large, natural horns flared up out of the helm, and he wielded a greatsword that glittered like silver in the moonlight. Even Zadkiel looked impressed.

Ahead of the others, the giant paused and raised his sword high…

Oh yeah. Here we go, here we go, let’s get hyped!
