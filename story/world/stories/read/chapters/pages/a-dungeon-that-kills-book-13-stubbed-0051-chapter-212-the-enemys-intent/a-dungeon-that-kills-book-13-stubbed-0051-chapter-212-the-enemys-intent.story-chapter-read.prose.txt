Damn it!

So the bastard could even turn himself into a lump of meat as well? Was there any limit at all to what he was capable of?

But to be fair, this one still fell under body manipulation, so it was not like he had pulled a completely new trick out of nowhere. Then again, if he could do this, outright shapeshifting was hardly a leap too far, was it?

Anyway, there was no use whining about it.

And so, Viktor moved his vision to follow the glistening blob of flesh as it slithered onward, past the threshold and into the Chamber of the Dead beyond.

It came to a halt just past the entrance, and there, under the restless, flickering glow cast by the torches lining the walls, the mass of tissue began to quiver. Its slick surface rippled and shuddered as though a thousand unseen muscles buried beneath had all decided, in perfect unison, to clench at once.

Then, it surged upward.

Flesh stretched into a thick column, swaying for the span of one heartbeat before splitting cleanly down the middle. One end divided into two legs that planted themselves against the stone floor, while the upper half swelled outward to form the beginnings of a torso. A pair of shoulders pushed free, and arms followed soon after, peeling away from the greater whole. At the very top of it all, a neck rose out of the widening chest. The featureless lump that crowned it bulged and shifted as the contours of a face pressed outward from under the skin.

And then.

That half-formed face was vaporized by two beams of sickly green light that lanced from the far end of the chamber.

What?

The now-headless body immediately threw itself sideways, and more of that same green light came searing after it, chasing the thing across the room as it darted and twisted and flowed about. At the same time, a fresh head grew from the stump of its neck. Eyes blinked open, ears unfurled, a nose pushed through, a mouth split wide across the smooth flesh, and last of all came that familiar cascade of grey hair tumbling down over the shoulders.

The mouth curled into a smile. “Straight to violence, huh? I never thought a priest of Death would be so impatient.”

“You are an enemy of the Sovereign,” came a raspy voice, “and you have presumed to intrude into this chamber. Give me one cause why I should not have smitten you where you stood?”

That voice, of course, belonged to the master of this hall, who had risen from his golden throne. And obviously, it was also he who had loosed those deadly bolts of green.

What kind of magic is that? Viktor wondered. And why had Khenemhotep never once mentioned such a thing to him?

“Good thing I hadn’t moved my brain up there yet,” the Apostle said with a chuckle, seemingly unbothered by the fact that he had just been shot in the face. Even as he spoke, the blob’s remaining flesh flowed upward like liquid wax, filling out the contours of muscle, smoothing the surface of skin, refining every last detail of his body until he stood there whole once more. “What a dangerous ability you have there, priest. The greatest threat to me in this dungeon, in fact. Which is precisely why I thought it best to remove you from the board as fast as possible.”

“Have you known me before this day?” Khenemhotep asked.

“Not you personally, no.” The gray-haired man gave a casual shrug. “But the moment I saw this pyramid of yours, I knew at once that one of the priests of Death was serving as a Guardian in this dungeon, and there was a possibility you knew a spell capable of doing me real harm. I lamented my luck considerably.” He let out an exaggerated sigh. “But then it occurred to me that you might not have informed your master about that particular ability of yours. Which would mean he hadn’t factored it into whatever plan he was devising against me. Which would mean I still had a chance—a chance to reach you and put you down before that changed.”

Viktor frowned. What on earth was this man talking about? Why did even Khenemhotep hide things from him? And why did his enemy know more about his own Guardian than himself?

“Of course, I know full well that I can’t kill you permanently,” the Apostle went on. “Even if I were to destroy the vessel standing before me right now, so long as your phylactery remains intact somewhere safe, you will reform after a while, won’t you? But that’s fine. It’s not as though I hold any personal grudge against you. I just need you out of my way long enough for me to reach the Core Room unopposed.”

That much was true. On the day Khenemhotep was summoned into the dungeon, he had brought with him what he called a canopic jar—a funerary vessel that contained the embalmed organs removed from his body during the rites of mummification. After he went back to the world of the living thanks to the Bearded God’s power, that jar had come to serve as his soul’s anchor. To put it simply, it was much the same as Galatea’s skull, while the desiccated body he wandered about in was the equivalent of her suit of armor.

Of course, Viktor had decided to leave that jar inside the Core Room, the most secure part of the dungeon, which meant Khenemhotep was effectively unkillable as long as the dungeon still stood. Better still, none of the tomb guards or other skeletons he had already raised beforehand would be affected by his temporary defeat, and they would continue fighting as though nothing at all were amiss. The only real consequence was that he wouldn’t be able to repair the old ones, create new ones, or go around casting his own spells, not until his own body had finished its reconstruction.

And that, apparently, was exactly what the Apostle was aiming for. There was a spell Khenemhotep possessed that could pose a genuine threat to him, so he came here to remove him before he became a problem.

Now, what am I supposed to do with this information?

The ancient priest’s secrets could wait, and so could the mystery of how the Apostle had come to know so much. Viktor was going to have a long talk with his Guardian later, once the battle was over, assuming that they managed to win and were still alive by then. Right now, the only thing that mattered was surviving the present.

The gray-haired man considered Khenemhotep the most dangerous obstacle standing between him and the Core Room. Yet, he came straight here to challenge him. Even now, after finding out that the priest was not nearly as helpless or as unprepared as he might have initially assumed, he hadn’t hesitated. He hadn’t reconsidered his approach. He hadn’t even looked particularly concerned. That meant only one thing: he was certain he could win this one-on-one fight.

So, what should I do?

Viktor could see three courses of action.

The first was the safest option. He could just have Celeste teleport Khenemhotep away from that chamber. After getting the priest out of immediate danger, they could regroup, exchange information, and come up with a better strategy for the next encounter.

The second option was for him to teleport to the great tomb himself and join the battle alongside Khenemhotep. By combining their power, there was a chance they would be able to finish off this great foe right there and then.

The third one was to send reinforcements. He could divert some of the forces currently engaged in the Great Hallway and send them into the Chamber of the Dead to assist Khenemhotep. But of course, the Apostle had already taken this possibility into account. Which was exactly why he had left Xander and that abomination behind as a delaying force. Sebekton would need to fight through that fused monstrosity in order to reach the top of the ramp, while Galatea was required to remain there to hold the spike-haired bastard off.

So those were the choices: three paths, three possible decisions. But which of them was the correct one?

No, wait.

Something about this situation was wrong. There was something about the Apostle’s behavior that did not make sense.

The man had claimed that Khenemhotep was a serious threat that needed to be eliminated as fast as possible. And yet, he didn’t act like someone who was in any particular hurry. He just stood there and talked and talked. He didn’t treat this at all like a desperate battle where every second counted.

No, I cannot take any word coming from that mouth at face value.

The Apostle had proven he was no ordinary enemy. Not only did he possess a terrifying ability, but he was also cunning and far more knowledgeable than Viktor had initially expected. Well, no surprise there. He was among the greatest weapons in the arsenal of the Children of the Abyss, after all. Of course he knew much more than the likes of Khalid or Xander. And if the worst were to be assumed, then there was a possibility that his knowledge was comparable to beings like Nephra or the Eternal Flame. In other words, he might have already known that the Dark Emperor, the dark spirit, and the lord of the dungeon were all the same person.

Which meant the Apostle knew he was watching, and the words he had spoken were not merely meant for Khenemhotep. It was entirely possible that this whole conversation was a trap—an attempt to manipulate Viktor, to bait him into making the wrong decision his enemy wanted him to make.

Every single one of the three courses of action he had been considering could be exactly what the Apostle wanted to happen.

Viktor could have Khenemhotep teleported away from the Chamber of the Dead, yes, but there was a chance that the Apostle was actually in a bad position. After he realized that he couldn’t defeat the priest as easily as he had expected, the man was simply bluffing to make his opponent run away and give him free access to the third floor.

Viktor could go there and personally engage the Apostle in a direct battle, yes, but there was a chance that doing so would be playing perfectly into his opponent’s machinations. The gray-haired man might be pretending to be vulnerable in order to lure him out, to goad him into making a hasty judgment and entering a fight he hadn’t adequately prepared for.

Viktor could order Galatea to leave her post and instead go to help Khenemhotep, yes, but there was a chance that without her, the carefully constructed containment for Xander would collapse. The bastard would be able to break through, and the Apostle would suddenly have another powerful ally by his side.

Ugh.

Any of them could have disastrous consequences.

“Celeste. The High Priest still has his own troops inside the chamber, correct?”

[Yes, Master. Most of the undead mages are being held there as reserves, and in addition to them, there are also no fewer than two dozen skeleton warriors. They remain hidden behind the walls, ready to intervene whenever he requires their aid.]

So it was not a pure one-on-one fight, then.

“Very well. Have Sebekton move up the ramp to kill that abomination. Galatea will remain where she is and keep focusing on Xander. Send some Templars into the Chamber of the Dead to assist the priest. Everyone else in the Great Hallway will continue with the original plan.”

[So we are going to have Khenemhotep stand his ground and fight the Apostle in that chamber, then?]

“Yes,” Viktor said. “If he can kill that man right there, then that would be the best possible outcome. But if he cannot... teleport him away immediately the moment he’s in any danger.”

[Understood, Master.]
