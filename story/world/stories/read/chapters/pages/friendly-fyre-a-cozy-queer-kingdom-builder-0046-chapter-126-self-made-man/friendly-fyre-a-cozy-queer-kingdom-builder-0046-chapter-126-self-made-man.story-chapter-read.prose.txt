In the few days since we've been back, Kanin hasn't had time to restore his body, so he's still just a floating blob of ink and broken glass—or as Sandro has (accurately) described him, a living lava lamp. In fact, this form is the only one Travelers have ever encountered him in; I imagine seeing him as a humanoid will be even more strange to them.

When Kanin arrives, I manufacture three giant blocks of glass, each about one and a half meters cubed, as Aquenno watches. I have the Dungeon Core make one standard cube of transparent glass, one cube of metallic glass, and at Kanin's request, a third made of black obsidian. Kanin alights on top of the first, touching his core to the glass so he can settle in for an Attunement, and I settle in with a book, happy to take the rare opportunity for some leisurely reading.

“Holy shit,” Kanin says.

I sit up straight, glancing sharply around the room. “What? Is everything okay?”

“Oh, yeah, sorry,” Kanin says. “It’s just, it looks like it’s only going to take me fifteen minutes to Attune this whole block. When I was level one, it took me hours to Attune just a couple shards of glass.”

I tip my head as I process this. “You started at level one?”

“Yeah.” He pauses. “Wait, you didn’t?”

Huh. “No, I started at level twenty.” And thank goodness for that! I never would have survived the caves without the mana pool I had accessible to me that first day. Not to mention, the Dungeon Core, of course.

“Twenty!” he cries. “What a rip off.”

Curious. I wonder why there was such a significant difference? Of course, all the Travelers seem to be at slightly different levels. It could be based on their age—either that of their body or mind. I suppose Level 1 would match someone who starts as a simple ink bottle.

“Anyway,” Kanin grumbles, “it looks like I'll be Attuning these blocks a lot faster than I was anticipating. You may have less time to read than you’d planned.”

“Not to worry,” I assure him. “I'm rather good at squeezing in time for books whenever the opportunity presents itself.”

“Alright, then,” he says. “Be back in a few.” His glass sinks down to lay flat on the cube, then he’s still.

I Check him out of curiosity.

As anxious as the Shuddering Shroud is, it reacts defensively, in an attempt to protect itself—and Sandro.
