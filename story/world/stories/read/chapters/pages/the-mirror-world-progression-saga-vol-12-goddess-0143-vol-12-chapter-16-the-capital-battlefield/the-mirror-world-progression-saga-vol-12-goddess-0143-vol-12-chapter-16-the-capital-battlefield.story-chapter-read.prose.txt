Samantha’s first look at the battlefield was sobering. The vast fields surrounding all sides of the great and majestic walls and towers of the City of Heavenly Order were stacked with endless numbers of troops. They’d gone heavier on the south-facing side that would receive the primary alliance force, but the north-facing side couldn’t be ignored by any means, as a smaller but primarily minotaur-based horde was coming for it. Ironically, that side might end up making better progress over time. Samantha hardly cared — if they had the role of absorbing all of the attention as the other army blew through the walls, that would just make things easier as a whole.

The strategy everyone agreed the Dominion had for the first leg of this was simply ‘weathering the storm.’ Stalling and attrition, forcing time sinks. All around the city were multiple hundreds of little hard points — small, deceptively humble-looking tower-fortresses behind circular walls like mounds. From intelligence-gathering, they knew these were an engineering marvel like a sandwich of protective layers.

On the outside was magically packed earth that claimed all sorts of ancient protections itself, but then behind it were separate ‘panel-like’ thin force constructs in multiple layers, so even when you carved through it, you had to have someone who could breach the force wall, which usually required a dispel. But these required multiple dispels to get them all. Finally, behind this was their typical reinforced stone.

Inside the fortresses were crude spell circles that would launch magic from their trusty ‘observation orbs’ at the top of the towers. Otherwise, the wizards were sealed in without a direct path to get to them. The structures had multiple pockets within them for a sparse collection of defenders, but no pocket led to another. They were simply there for enough sniper types to be annoying on top of the magic threat.

Samantha had to admit they were an innovative way to frustrate progress and annoy everyone. They all had to be destroyed, as taking them over was a no-go for the near-certainty of waiting booby traps on top of the issue of sealed-in defenders. Taking care of them was very doable, but it was a drain on resources. They also served as a point of hope for enemy troops. They could rally around these defensive structures.

The biggest goal at the end of it all was the primary ‘gates’ in the front of the walls. Southgate was its humble name, but it was far more. The walls were massive and strong in the Capital, and the front portion governing the gate was equivalent to a fortress only ever eclipsed in its durability as compared to Matrazere. There was a massive and storied hall one traversed to go through that gate, in which there were actually ten gates of different varieties, and surrounded by endless mechanisms of defense from every direction.

Once again, Samantha didn’t want to navigate such a deathtrap at all — she just wanted to destroy it. In this case, it would likely need to be the strongest of them. Her, Six Moons Falling, Bast, Gelmak, and other such heroes.

They only had the bulk of a possibly million-man army to get through first. Tedious, but they had advantages. One thing was clear from the configuration: the Dominion was not trying to win the skies. They had all of their aerial power held back to the walls, the bulk at Southgate.

Intelligence suggested the most ancient and strongest of gargoyles protected the walls, in fact lending strength to them while they persisted. In seeing the articulately-made constructs herself, some giant-sized, Samantha knew it was true. Those things would come alive when the walls were threatened.

Exactly what the Dominion planned to do about aerial dominance before the walls wasn’t clear. They did have the gazillion spell fortresses which might be trouble for flyers, but it likely wasn’t the end of it. Sadly, their intelligence hadn’t uncovered anything further.

The typical general restrictions they expected were in place: teleportation was heavily banned, and underground manipulation was next to impossible. Earth magic in general would be debuffed and difficult to perform. Ripping up walls that way wasn’t going to work, at least not for anyone not named after meteors. In her case, though, she’d likely be smashing or ripping directly at the stone. She’d already deduced and informed them that attacking the walls wouldn’t be easy, and best done in coordination with others to weaken it.

The special issues around the Capital, and its unique spiritual, magical aura, had to do with two primary things: inherent restrictions on the impact of any form of magical resistance, and limitations of ‘proximity.’

The first issue was fairly simple and planned for; it just tipped the numbers a bit more in the Dominion’s favor than most places, even compared to Matrazere. It functioned like diminishing returns for total resistance, halving it at the higher levels. Wince-worthy, as they needed every level they could get, but they planned for it as best they could.

The other issue, the limit of proximity, was a bit stranger. Apparently, the reason the Capital was so hard to scry from a distance in general was due to this aura, one of outward magical pressure. The intensity of all power diminished in a wide radius around the Capital, as it specifically approached the Capital’s walls. Literally, the direction toward it, though anything inside ten meters was not penalized at all.

It made long-bombing them from standard angles impossible, and changed the strategy for how to use ranged effects, particularly magi. You used them either simply at shorter ranges (not that uncommon) or you had to get more tricksome and dig furrows into the defenses, firing laterally or away from the walls.

Basically, they had a major disadvantage that sucked ass, but had ways of working around it. Samantha certainly would. In no other battle would air superiority be more advantageous. The other side had to be kicking themselves for not doing a better job when it came to flyers.

Or do they think they’ll get me just because I turn my back to the walls? Good luck with that.

In any case, the final struggle of numbers was going to be a gamble no matter what. Neither side fully understood everything the enemy would do.

Dax was sure the opposite side’s general was very good, and perhaps for the first time, the sort that didn’t conduct the battle with hubris. Bad news, essentially. Samantha knew what non-arrogance within the Dominion did, as she had the mental scars to prove it. Matrazere’s primary general was competent, sure, but also leaned on the standard hubris that had seemed to come with the place. Meanwhile, the psychics that had attacked Samantha had spared not even a drop of effort to kill her, and had only narrowly failed.

They’d successfully made the fate of the world hang on a wire. If she had been overcome, nothing that was happening would be. No army would be at their gates, and Matrazere would still be standing proud. It would’ve been a complete reversal of fortunes.

The Inquisitors had not gotten that close by being arrogant about their enemy. They’d used all bits of information to make their play, even trying to bait out her most powerful success-altering abilities while holding their last big strike back. However much that she reviled those bastards, that was just fucking brilliant, as was their ploy to strike from afar via Sympathy, and their strategy would’ve worked in the end… if not for the trump card that was Azure.

And Jeeves and the Brain Trust in general. They were the ones who did everything in their power to ensure my Alter Fate uses kept up the pace, and made me avoid use except in emergencies. Little did I know how right they’d be about that.

She had no expectation they would bother with the same strategy again. They would know she’d gotten stronger while they were heavily depleted. Would smarty-pants cowards risk their necks after getting burned once already?

No ‘collective’ would defeat her individually — there just wasn’t one strong enough anymore. It was Entropy bullshit or bust at this point, and she was fairly certain there were only two who had any sort of chance: The True Sage, who was apparently just going to wait below whatever happened, and the Sage of Truth, who would likely just hide like the coward he was.

Mmn. No. No, that’s an arrogant assumption, isn’t it? Let’s not do that, Samantha. We have to remember the vision, and half the reason why I have this Entropy-killer eye in my socket. He’ll crawl out of his hole at some point, try to ‘season’ me for his master or whatever. Likely out of some kind of sudden ambush, hoping to slither away afterward. Hopefully one Cyclops Blast is all I need for him.

This was all tangential to what they were looking at for a battle start, though. She was fairly sure the enemy general planned to simply endure her. They’d be stupid to plan and depend on taking her out.

For her part, she planned on baiting them and being boldly out there, terrorizing them. She wouldn’t hide, not this time. Decoys, yes — there was no point making things easy when you summoned matter on a whim — but sneaking around? No. Her entire army would watch her carving the enemy like a pumpkin, and that would drive them forward. If the Dominion thought they could stop it?

Let them try.
