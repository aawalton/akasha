I did need the wakeup call, James thought as he moved through the dark passage that transported him through time once again. Even if Abhi thought he came too early, I needed it now. I have gotten complacent. Somewhere along the way, I lost the plot. As soon as Luna was branded by that angel, and it became obvious there were more of this group out there somewhere, with the possibility of someone out there to seek vengeance, coupled with those visions of the future—of what happens to Mina and my people at their hands—I should have scoured the Earth until I wiped them all out.

He shook his head. I could have crushed them when they were just a small bud. Maybe. The future isn’t truly deterministic. That’s what my time-traveling kid said. Now I’ll have to kill many people to get out from under this. Not just the leaders. Thousands. Millions? Well, whatever I have to do. It’s a costly lesson, but I’ll pay the price if it keeps my family safe.

The darkness disappeared from around James, and he found himself back in the same place he’d left off.

“What the fuck?” the Assassin King exclaimed. He looked annoyed and surprised, and his eyes darted away from James toward something behind the Fisher Emperor.

James quickly looked where the Assassin King’s eyes had landed, but he saw nothing. He returned his gaze to the Assassin King, bracing for an attack—perhaps the man was doing the old made-you-look trick—but no, the Ruler was still in the same place he’d been. He was actually stuck there, now that James looked closely. Of course he was.

I trapped him in my silk. The Assassin King was stuck in a web of James’s making. He was missing an arm and still bleeding from where the dragon had severed the limb. Helpless. Harmless. Could just let him go, and…

James blinked. Wait, what the hell am I thinking? What do you mean, let him go? He tried using Soul Magic when he ambushed you and Zigfried. He’s obviously… huh. It’s strange. When I look at him, he just feels really harmless. Mental influence?

The Fisher Emperor ordered Roscuro to transform into his longsword form. As he looked down at the Soul Eater, he collected his thoughts.

The Assassin King was so committed to killing me here, ideally permanently, with Soul Magic, that he was willing to target a dragon when he wasn’t getting paid for it, just because Zigfried was in his way. He definitely needs to be erased from existence.

James turned his head slightly. Where’s the bastard who was helping him? No way the Assassin King was the one who sent me on that trip through time. That’s who he must have been looking at. But I can’t sense a damn thing that way. Is the time manipulator that good at hiding? Or did he just recognize that I’d be pissed off when I came back, and he’d be on the chopping block? He doesn’t seem to be ambushing me with additional attacks, so I’ll kill the Assassin King before I think about finding him, I guess.

He looked back at the Assassin King. The man emanated an orange-red aura. Fire Mana. He was trying to burn his way free of James’s magic silk.

But he would find it would take him longer than he might have expected.

Warp Step.

James took a single step, appeared in front of the Assassin King, and his Soul Eater shortsword stabbed at the enemy’s heart—only to stop inches away.

The Assassin King held the blade’s tip in between both hands, the blade penetrating through one palm and slowly moving closer and closer to the target.

He had regenerated his missing arm while James was looking elsewhere, then ripped through the armor covering both limbs to save himself.

He understood the threat Roscuro presented, at least.

As James stabbed closer to the Assassin King’s chest, the man twisted his hands, altering the trajectory of the blade from his heart to his shoulder.

James kept pushing. The blade entered the Assassin King’s shoulder and kept going, slowly stabbing further into him.

“I have you now,” the Assassin King said through gritted teeth.

The mana around his body changed its nature in an instant. Too quickly to make sense.

Did he hide the truth with an illusion?

Suddenly, what had appeared to be Fire Mana appeared as a darker colored red that arranged itself into a sort of magical pentagram symbol in the air.

The blood streaming from the Assassin King’s shoulder and palm levitated through the air and entered the symbol.

He was planning to get stabbed?

The symbol glowed once, twice, and then shattered.

A trio of monsters that resembled crimson-colored great white sharks with dragonfly wings appeared in midair behind the Assassin King.

“Kill!” he ordered.

Oh, he’s getting desperate. Unless these are some kind of demon sharks that eat souls, he went from trying to permanently kill me with Soul Magic to trying to kill me in the melee so he could escape this engagement.

James danced backward as the shark creatures dove for his head.

Zigfried, rushing through the cloud of smoke that had surrounded him, leaped upon the rear shark, claws and teeth flashing, and James guessed he would only need to worry about the two closer to himself.

Roscuro reshaped himself into a kite shield strapped to James’s arm, and James used it to take the first bite attack from the lead shark. Its jaws clamped down uselessly on the black material of the Soul Eater, doing no apparent damage.

James started using his own magic.

He sensed the Assassin King doing something in the distance, but he was forced to keep most of his attention on the sharks in front of him.

The monster with Roscuro in its mouth released the Soul Eater and lunged for James’s other side. James turned Roscuro into a hard glove with spiked knuckles and threw a punch at the side of the shark’s head, spinning James’s own body to the side at the same time.

The punch didn’t do much damage. The creatures were physically robust at least.

That’s strange, James thought, dimly aware of what the Assassin King was doing. Soul Magic again, huh? Even though he’s not free from my silk.

A burst of flame from Zigfried took the shark monster that was further from James. It was just him and the other one now, dancing in a dance of Soul Eater and shark teeth.

Bite, block with Soul Eater shield, pivot, slash with Soul Eater dagger, dodge, stab, block, chop. James managed to sever one of the dragonfly style wings from the monster’s side, but other than that, he found it difficult to inflict real or lasting damage.

Zigfried had flown over to try and intervene, but James and the monster were moving back and forth too quickly, trading blows at such high speed that the dragon could not reliably hit the one he wanted to aim at with his fire breath.

The dragon instead zipped back to the Assassin King, who had finally burned his way free from the web. James tracked the movement of dragon and Ruler with a corner of his attention even as he focused the bulk of it on the monster in front of him.

Zigfried was preparing another burst of dragon fire, the Assassin King had surrounded himself with Mana again, the shark creature was slowly being whittled down, and James could feel the momentum of the fight was about to change. He was waiting for it.

The flying shark dove for James’s head, and he made a mistake by moving out of the way, landing the creature behind him, with James’s back mostly turned to it. Space warped in the fraction of a second after the shark had changed its position.

The Fisher Emperor was aware that the shark disappeared and reappeared where the Assassin King had been. Zigfried’s flames bathed the shark monster, sealing its fate.

But that meant the Assassin King was left floating in the air right behind James.

He flitted into close range before the Fisher Emperor could even turn around, and the halo of Soul Mana that surrounded him gave away his intentions. He reached out, and with a surge of Mana, enveloped James in the magic.

“It’s over,” the Assassin King whispered as his power crackled around the Fisher Emperor. There was nothing that could be done now. “That was harder than I expect—”

A blade exploded through his chest, tearing through his heart.

“What?”

The figure that had been surrounded by Soul Mana fizzled instantly into nothingness. There was no scream, no feeling of wrongness from a soul being destroyed in close proximity, no nothing. Because there had been nothing there. The Mana itself vanished a moment later.

The man who had been in control of it was hardly in any condition to redirect it. He wasn’t even in control of his own bowels anymore.

James peeked out from behind the Assassin King and smiled humorlessly.

“Surprise.”

The Assassin King realized what had happened a second after it was too late to do anything about it. Just as he had used Illusion Magic to disguise what kind of Mana he was using in different moments of the later part of the fight, the Fisher Emperor had used illusions to give the impression that he was in a different place than he was as he sparred with the summoned shark monster.

The difference was that James’s illusions were almost as real as reality, virtually impossible to see through for any entity on the same tier of power as the Fisher Emperor.

They could even inflict real, small injuries on opponents. That was why the shark had never suffered any serious harm while in the whirl of battle against the Emperor. The real body was hidden, invisibly, a few feet away from the action the entire time.

James had been able to detect the Assassin King using magic again, and even if he couldn’t break through the other Ruler’s illusions, he could use his battle sense to guess what the Assassin King might do. Teleporting around was one of the tricks the hired killer had resorted to over and over through the course of the battle. His summoned sharks weren’t so powerful as to justify their use as anything more than a distraction—and a marker for a place he could teleport to, switching places at a key moment.

You really outsmarted yourself, James thought. With all your tricks, you never considered that others would use deception against you, too.

He looked into the Assassin King’s eyes and saw the look of dawning desperation there.

“No,” the enemy Ruler silently mouthed.

“Farewell,” James replied, his smile flattening out into a line. Though it was necessary, there was still something dark in what he did. It was hard to take much joy in it.

Then the Soul Eater did his work. A fatal injury had been inflicted. The Assassin King’s heart had been ruptured by the Soul Eater longsword. A soul was due for destruction.

A horrendous scream filled the air as the Assassin King’s body and soul shattered permanently.

Zigfried set down a few feet from James a second after the Assassin King had been consumed.

“That was… intense,” the dragon said. “What do you intend to do next?”

“The one who helped him earlier is too well hidden for me to detect them,” James said. “Can you find him?”

“He left,” Zigfried replied immediately. “We can’t follow where he went.”

James frowned and shook his head. “Fine. Well, I’m going to go after everyone who hired them. I need to contain this situation as quickly as I can. Sparring over, all right?”

The dragon nodded in return. “Yeah, this is a little too intense for me. People trying to destroy each other’s souls. Dragons wouldn’t casually do that.”

“I’m glad you’ll be out of it, then,” James replied, smiling thinly.

“It would be irresponsible of me to try and continue,” Zigfried said, sounding for once every one of his fifty-six years. “My father said that if something happened to me, he’d destroy the planet.”

We shouldn’t have been fighting in the first place, James thought.

“Farewell, Zigfried. Enjoy the rest of your stay on Earth.”
