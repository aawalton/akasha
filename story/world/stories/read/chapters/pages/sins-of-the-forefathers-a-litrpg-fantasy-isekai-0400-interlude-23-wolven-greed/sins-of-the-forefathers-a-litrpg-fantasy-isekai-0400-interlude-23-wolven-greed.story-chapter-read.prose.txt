All around him raged an orgy of violence and death so violent and bloody that it threatened to drown out the world.

Bleddyn hated that a part of him loved it.

In normal times, his path dictated that he would need to dwell on that. There could be no hesitation, no doubt, to be found in the midst of the storm. If you wavered from your convictions for even a single moment, the tempest could descend on your heart and tear it to pieces. There was little room for weakness of spirit in the Mynnyd clans, but there was none allowed for the future Chief of the Clan.

Of the pack.

But he was a little busy right now, so Bleddyn figured the ancestors would forgive him for putting off his meditation.

For a while, at least.

He twisted out of the way of an impossibly fast fist as large as his torso, as it rocked towards him. To his irritation, he was only a fraction of a second too slow to completely dodge the blow, and the knuckle of the giant fist clipped his rib cage.

The entire right side of his torso stove inwards in response to even that glancing blow.

For most people, that would have been the end of things. Judging from what he could tell, the impact had completely obliterated his right lung. Ah, and a kidney, too.

Luckily, he wasn’t most men.

In only seconds, the regeneration factor gifted to him by his bond to Mighty Taran had totally repaired all of the damage. In the meantime, Bleddyn hadn’t even been moved an inch from the punch, thanks to how deeply he had gauged his clawed feet into the stone beneath him, unflinching from the pain of his regeneration.

Before the fist had even retracted, Bleddyn lunged forward, sank his razor-sharp, dagger-length fangs into the index finger of the fist, and tore it out. The owner of the finger staggered back from the mutilation, letting out a bellow loud and strong enough to rattle the walls of the hell pit he had found himself in.

Unfortunately…in only moments, the finger that Bleddyn had ripped out with his teeth was already regenerating. At a speed comparable to his own, at that. Bleddyn spat out the finger in his fangs, as in moments, an entirely new one had grown out from the ragged stump. The giant wagged that single finger at him mockingly, in response to the almost reflexive growls issuing out of Bleddyn’s throat.

Bleddyn narrowed his eyes, incensed despite knowing it was a taunt.

He couldn’t help himself, and not just because of his current bestial nature.

It was because it was such a fucking pain in the ass to run into a combatant that almost mirrored his own abilities.

Things weren’t helped much by where Bleddyn had encountered the massive, towering mutant Dwarf.

Right smack dab in the middle of an arena. All around him and his opponent, Bleddyn’s men clashed with a seemingly unending tide of Skarned, with only a handful of oddly thin, lanky, docile Karvokar standing at the back directing them.

Bella, the pirate woman, and her crew were fighting the rest of the men that his own Unchained weren’t. The little Bleddyn had been able to see that their own battle seemed to show they were doing well, helping to back up his more frontline fighters. The woman herself had initially tried to help Bleddyn with his own battle, only to pull back not long into it. Not only because she was better needed elsewhere, and not only because the giant was a bad match-up for her…

But because Bleddyn had asked her to.

Oddly, the woman had seemed to understand his pride well enough and backed off.

Bleddyn was thankful for that, despite how little the two of them knew each other. He needed to prove that he was still strong. That his men, not only the ones who had followed him north on this damn fool expedition of Nathe’s, but those he had left behind at headquarters…

They could rely on him.

The leader of the Unchained hadn’t forgotten just how much he had slapped him around, back in Rhoscara. That his own blood had not only joined forces with the godsdamned Savoy, but he had dared to strike out against the Clan, and its allies in his maddened grief.

It burned at him to know that his own blood was helping Anguis fuckin’ Savoy, the Serpent himself.

And Bleddyn knew that at the heart of that rage was the knowledge that this was Daffyd.

Daffyd, who had been there for him when his Father hadn’t, in the wake of his Mother’s death.

Daffyd, who had taught him to fish when Father would only teach him to hunt.

Daffyd, who had smiled at him so encouragingly, the first time he had embraced the transformation.

Daffyd.

The Archmage was his responsibility now. And Bleddyn had never been one to shirk his responsibilities.

So, when his and Bella’s battle group had stumbled into this spire after splitting from Nate and the rest, Bleddyn hadn’t been tired in the least. He may have been on the front lines of the fight through the Hold, but none of it had satisfied him. Not one of the Karvokar or their legions of Skarned had been enough of a fight that Bleddyn felt he had proven his worth once more, through the rigor of battle.

That was, until they had found the arena.

It was nothing more than a giant pit that spanned half of the enormous ground floor of the spire. Sand of all things lined the floor of the bowl, but it wasn’t alone.

Bones were everywhere, bleached and somehow dry as if they had been baking under a hot sun for decades. They lay everywhere within the bare arena, while surrounding it were, at the time, Bleddyn had reckoned they were nothing more than rents in the stone. Huge holes that led deep in the depths of the spire. Who knew what these odd bastards had created them for? For all he knew, they were using it as meat storage or something.

Turns out, he was wrong.

As soon as he and Bella’s battle group had set foot inside the oddly empty arena, Skarned had started to pour out of the holes in the ground like ants from a disturbed mound. They climbed over the edges with raw, bloodied fingers. These Skarned were very slightly different than the countless hordes they had already slain on the way to the lake, though. For one, all of their mutilations looked fresher, somehow, like they had only just been inflicted with the loss of their ears, noses, and lips.

For another, they were both completely naked and covered in blood.

Bleddyn could have lived without seeing that.

But ultimately, they fought the same as the rest of them. Their Karvokar were a bit different, looking oddly tall and stretched out even for their kind, but they also acted oddly. Nearly none of them chose to engage in the fighting, instead hanging back at the fringes of the near-instantaneous battle that erupted within the formerly empty pit. They towered over the rest of their raving Skarned hordes like singular tall trees upon a plain, unmoving and expressionless. Even more disturbing was that they didn’t even fight back in the rare occurrence that either he or his warriors managed to fell those trees. They simply let themselves be cut down.

And the tide of them didn’t cease. They simply kept pouring out of the burrows in an endless wave.

That was, until the entire world started to shake around them.

Bleddyn still didn’t know what had caused it. He’d been in the middle of felling one of those odd Karvokar, and then a cacophonous noise of crumbling stone had rung out. It had almost sounded like it was coming from the spire next to the one they were fighting in. Everyone, even the nearly mindless Skarned, had stopped in their tracks at the noise to gaze around in confusion. There was nowhere to run, and nowhere to hide from it, because the rumbling had lasted for nearly ten minutes before being followed by a wash of blood that had oozed its way in through the doorway. It was as if something enormous had splashed into the lake outside of the tower. Bleddyn had his own thoughts on what that might be, but he didn’t have time to even consider the matter.

Some of the blood had reached the burrows and streamed down into their depths.

In response…

The single largest Korvok Bleddyn has seen yet had exploded out of the hole in the ground, smashing the edges of the stone around it into a thousand shards. It had soared high into the air, hanging like a great maroon star of hardened flesh.

Before plummeting straight toward the center of the arena, a massive fist as large as any man led the way.

Right at Bleddyn.

He had no trouble saying the implicit challenge had raised his blood. Even when that same plunging fist had carved an impressive crater, after Bleddyn had dodged.

The fully transformed werewolf had merely loosed a howl of defiance before lunging for the nearly twelve-foot-tall abomination’s throat.

They had been stalemated against each other ever since. At the speeds the two of them were trying to kill each other at, sometimes it was hard to tell just how much time had passed. But if Bleddyn was going to take a wild guess…

Maybe about five minutes. And in that time, both of them had suffered over a dozen different life-threatening injuries that would have felled anyone else.

Yet, neither of them seemed to care all that much. The damage was regenerated in mere moments.

Bleddyn wasn’t enjoying having his own tactics turned against him like this.

After a few more mortal wounds exchanged between them, the giant finally backed off long enough to gaze at Bleddyn in disgust. The werewolf was surprised when the jaw on the Korvok finally opened for anything more than a grunt of effort, and actually spoke to him. “Begone from this holy ground, mutt,” He spoke in a startlingly melodic voice, for such a monstrously large existence. “I’ve no time to discipline you further.”

A rolling chuckle barked its way out of Bleddyn’s chest, as the battle continued to rage around them. Albeit at a safe distance. Not even the Skarned seemed to want to get within range of this giant’s fists. “Oh, a mutt, am I?” Bleddyn suddenly snarled. “Better a mutt than a mindless monster. And I ain’t goin’ nowhere, behemoth. I’m here fer some answers, and I’m gettin’ ‘em if I have to drag ‘em out of your corpse.”

The giant’s lip curled in distaste. “Mindless, am I? Know that you address Draskul, Rendarch of the Cleaving Path, beast. I am the farthest thing from mindless. I am he that educates. I am the lord of discipline, cleaving the worthy from the rabble and carving them into the righteous. You stand upon hallowed ground, a sacred classroom, an altar to the Red Truth. And I. Do. Not. Have. Time. For. Your. Questions.”

As ‘Draskul’ lowered himself into a new, different combat stance than the one he’d been using earlier, Bleddyn knew the time for testing and probing strikes had come to an end. This Rendarch character was ready to end things in a single exchange. No doubt the malicious fucker thought he’d gotten Bleddyn’s measure.

Fat fuckin’ chance of that. He wasn’t the only one who’d been holding back.

Bleddyn angled himself into a new stance of his own. This was a good chance for him to try out a new little trick of his. Something he’d been brooding on, ever since his traitorous Uncle had revealed his ugly maw. A combination of Capstone Skill and a Cultivation art that he had once, upon a time, seen his Father wield against a Prime. He took a deep breath, angled his body so that his left shoulder faced the Rendarch, and curled his right claws together until they reached a single point.

A spark of bright yellow lightning flared into being between those claws and then spread.

And spread.

And spread.

Until there was a massive corona of crackling lighting arcing away from the spear that his claws had become. They flew away at odd, random angles behind him, lashing at any Skarned that thought to charge at his ‘unprotected’ back. And yet, while it may have appeared uncontrolled, it was anything but. Bleddyn had complete control over every single bolt of lightning that strengthened and lengthened behind him, until they were arcing behind him, almost akin to a giant spider web.

And then he drew it all back down in an instant into a singular point that glowed brighter than any star, and he vanished forward.

Bleddyn didn’t know what Draskul had been planning, but it wasn’t for him to more than triple his speed and appear in front of the Rendarch with a boiling fistful of plasma clenched between his claws, floating in midair before the titanic Korvok from the sheer momentum of his leap.

The Werewolf looked up briefly, meeting the gaze of the startled Korvok, and then thrust his claws into and through the hardened muscle of the Rendarch's chest. He immediately leapt away…

And the bomb Bleddyn had left deep within the chest of Draskul detonated, bisecting the giant Korvok at his midline.

As the Rendarch’s top half fell to the floor of the arena in a downright obscene explosion of blood, Bleddyn was satisfied to see that his warriors seemed to be nearly done with their own battles. There were only remnants of the Skarned and their Karvokar handlers left within the spire, and they wouldn’t last long.

Something did surprise him, though. A voice called out from the direction of the doorway to the spire, carrying across the distance.

“Well,” A wry, though relieved, feminine voice sounded. “I see that you didn’t need much help after all.”

Bleddyn looked up to find that Sylvia, of all people, was standing in the door to the spire, the Sculpted woman’s arms crossed across her chest. At his regard, she shook her head and gestured for him to meet her.

He shrugged.

No reason not to, he supposed.

Bleddyn had already won his victory, and he was feeling pretty good.
