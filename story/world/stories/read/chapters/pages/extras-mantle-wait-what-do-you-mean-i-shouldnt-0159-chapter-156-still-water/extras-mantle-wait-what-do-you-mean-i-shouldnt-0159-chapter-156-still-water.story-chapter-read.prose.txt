The Engine did not go along with his demands the way the Matrix had.

Where the Matrix had rebuilt Jin’s internal essence channels with something almost like care—straightening, widening, quietly fixing all the bad decisions he made till now—while he lay still and let it work.

But the Engine wanted none of that. It just wanted room. More of it. All of it.

It reached out through him the instant the token melted into his chest and set about remaking his body. Jin felt the whole shape and promise of the Engine’s ambition arrive at once, and because his new channels firmly said no.

So the Engine took a different road.

This time the pain was the real thing. His skin went pink, then red, lit from the inside like a coal held up to a lamp. Steam boiled off him and the silver-bronze lines threading his arms pulsed and braided tighter, drinking the overflow, and the temperature in the room climbed so fast and so far that the ceiling's fire ward shrieked and dumped a cold shower down on top of him.

The water did almost nothing for the heat. It did a great deal for Jin—enough to knock the edge off. Enough to grab the reins—and for his cryo affinity, which woke up and started cycling cold chill through the system.

He tried to stand but tripped from the imbalance the upgrade left him with. His bare hand hit the floor, and the floor hissed, a curl of smoke rising off the tile where his palm had been.

“Damn it!” Jin grunted through the pain and jerked back. He started dumping his essence into his channels, calling upon cryo affinity to keep the engine from overheating, because he had unleashed the Engine in the bastion before, and he remembered in vivid detail how that had ended.

"Not the walls and definitely not the floors," he ground out through his teeth. "Illiana'll kill me."

And then, it settled, just like that, and the system panel bloomed across his vision.

The first thing he did was get out of the ruined clothes. The second was to get to the bathroom, which he managed at a careful shuffle, his bare feet sizzling faint black prints into the tile with every step.

« You got your floor design sorted! »

“Really!” Jin rolled his eyes and got the tap running. “Sometimes you make the lamest joke, Angel.”

« That’s cuz your first and second brain cells are fighting for third place, that I have to dumb my jokes down. »

Jin started at the water… he had no response to Angel except. Damn! When did he become so snarky?

He sighed again for the umpteenth time and climbed in. As soon as he did, the water just hissed right off him before it could even sit there.

“Oh c’mon!” Jin cursed.

It took him five minutes of continuous cryo essence channeling and conjuring ice into the tub, and only then the heat finally came to heel. He sat there steaming in cooling water when he noticed the new readout hanging quietly in the corner of his sight.

Engine Charges: Thermal 4% | Kinetic 1%

"Ohh." He nodded slowly, turned the readout over in his head, and told Angel to start running scenarios against the new limits. Unlike the Matrix, the Engine wasn’t a static skill. Jin knew of the engine’s capability… sure, but not the new limits.

Then he pulled the full skill tab and let out a low whistle.

"Alright," he murmured. "Alright. That's solid work."

He raised his hands.

The veins stood out sharper than before. Same silvery bronze, yes… but he could sense them now, the way he sensed his own pulse, or the newer, deeper hum of his essence channels.

It was a third circulation system, laid neatly over and intertwined with the other two. The engine had claimed the outermost layer of him for its own, and through those lines he could feel the charges sliding like molten fire.

"That's cool," he mumbled, one hundred percent sincere, and opened his status.

He wasn’t angry, but he was sure as hell pissed. What was the point of prompts when someone could force something onto him, anyway?

And then he felt a cold chill. Is that what Joe was talking about? Why he didn’t like sponsors?

He steadied himself and slid into the grey of the sovereign’s indifference as he waited for the vision to lock in place.

He braced himself for a memory… they always used a memory… to hold a mirror up to somewhere he'd already been, let him look at it with older eyes, pat him on the head, and send him back to work feeling appropriately grown.

He didn't know why his mind shivered at the possibility of being back at his hospital room… for the smell of disinfectant and a flat white ceiling and that he hated the bracing worse than he'd ever hated the place.

The shapes resolved, and Jin knew at once this was nothing like the visions before… There were no borrowed eyes, no room he could stand in and endure. To be honest, he didn’t even know if he had a body at all. He could not move himself or any part of himself, just let himself be pulled.

And sure enough, after ample focusing, he saw himself as a smear of translucent light adrift in open dark air and nothing else.

Below and ahead of him, rising out of the dark red night like a blade, stood a massive Gothic-style cathedral… something he would have known anywhere.

No. No—

He tried to scream it, and nothing came. He had no mouth to shape the word and no breath to carry it, and worse—far worse—when he reached on pure reflex…Angel wasn't with him.

The absence sat where his whole self should have been, a cold hollow the exact size of a friend. Alone. Voiceless. Bodiless.

Damn it! He cursed and cursed again when he felt what and where he was getting dragged towards.

Oh, hell no!

Something deep inside that cathedral had a hand on the thread of him and was reeling him in.

And as it drew him down over the walls and past the spires into the deep of the Ward, Jin felt the shape of the pull, and he stopped fighting.

He knew this feeling.

Not through his eyes and not through his skin, but through the two states that had sat in his status since the very beginning, and no one had ever once bothered to explain themselves.

Karma and Insight. The invariable stats.

This was the sensation they made… a silent, weightless resonance, one soul recognising the nearness of something vast and structured and divine. The same impossible tug he'd felt once before, when divinity had brushed past his own soul.

Something down there was calling to that part of him. Speaking in the only tongue it shared with him.

And there was only one thing beneath this city that could.

The ritual’s nexus, Jin understood, drifting down through cold cathedral air toward a glow he couldn't yet see. A divine essence.

He should have been nothing but afraid. Part of him was.

The rest of him—the large part of him—was still under [Sovereign’s Indifference] and ran the protocols it always ran.

If I'm already here, and I can't leave, then I'm going to make sure the trip pays.

He stopped fighting the pull. He gave himself over to it—trusting that the Eternal One had not carried him this far to feed him to a ritual—and the instant he quit resisting, the descent went smooth as falling water, and Jin opened himself all the way and drank.

He took the whole of it in one hungry sweep. The single causeway, choked and narrow… one road in, and only one.

The outer wall, and the curses laid into its stone, faces and sentries locations.

The precinct beyond it.

The stairs, going down, and down, and further down.

And at the very bottom of everything, under all of it, a sound like the largest heart in the world beating slow in the dark.

Remember it, he told himself, observing and remembering every angle for a battle soon to come. All of it. Every door, every wall, every stair down. Remember —

The heartbeat stopped.

And then, unhurried, a vast gaze locked onto him, and he broke out of the vision back into the bastion.
