A single flickering candle lit the dark room, casting long, wavering shadows against the stone walls.

Two figures sat at a table laden with untouched, luxurious dishes.

A broad-shouldered man idly ran his fingers through his spiked beard, his bald scalp reflecting the candlelight like polished bronze. He appeared deep in thought.

“And you’re certain of this?” he asked, his deep voice cutting through the oppressive silence.

“The source is reliable, Boss. There’s no reason to doubt it,” the other figure replied, head bowed low in deference.

Ming Hu’s frown deepened, displeasure etched across his face. The tension in the room thickened, pressing down like a stormcloud ready to burst.

The subordinate dared not speak further.

“We cannot let them succeed,” Ming Hu muttered, his tone like iron wrapped in velvet. “I’ve yet to extract enough benefits from this battle of succession.”

He paused, then added, “Place spies in each camp. Have them monitor the princes’ movements. Tell them to intervene only if their lives are truly at risk.”

“Yes, Boss.” The man bowed deeper and turned to leave.

But just as his hand reached the door, Ming Hu’s voice echoed again.

“Fetch Little Qin for me.”

The man nodded and disappeared into the shadows.

Some time later, a soft knock sounded. A young woman stepped inside, her beauty undeniable—but her red-rimmed eyes betrayed her recent tears. The sight tugged at the heart.

“Little Qin,” Ming Hu said, his deep voice uncharacteristically gentle. “What’s wrong? Is the Second Prince bothering you again?”

Fairy Qin shook her head, rubbing at her swollen eyes with the sleeve of her azure robe. She looked delicate, like a child seeking comfort after a nightmare.

“Then what troubles you, my dear?” he asked, concern flickering across his usually stern features.

She hesitated, indecision flashing across her face. That moment of silence struck Ming Hu like a blade. Did she not trust him? He, who stood second only to the king in Ralis City?

As if sensing his inner turmoil, Fairy Qin finally spoke.

“Father…” Her voice quivered. “I-I’m being bullied by the noblewomen. They say I smell bad. That despite my looks, I’m nothing more than… trash.”

The candlelight danced across Ming Hu’s face as a storm brewed in his eyes.

The table shifted slightly under his hand.

Then—CRACK!

A deafening crash split the air as his massive fist slammed down, shattering the table. Dishes clattered to the floor. Spilled wine soaked the stone. A leg snapped clean off.

Fairy Qin flinched, stumbling backward in fright.

The door flew open.

A towering figure burst in, sword drawn. Her eyes scanned the room with razor precision.

“Hilda!” Fairy Qin gasped.

The warrior woman swiftly pulled Qin Hu behind her, blade raised, ready to strike.

Her gaze landed on Ming Hu—and narrowed.

“What is the meaning of this?” she asked coldly, her voice like ice over steel.

Ming Hu said nothing.

He simply stared at his hand, now laced with blood from a jagged splinter that pierced his palm. The wound throbbed, but he didn’t wince.

“Hilda,” Ming Hu began, his voice low and icy, “it seems these noblewomen have forgotten their place in Ralis City.”

Hilda’s expression flickered at the mention of noblewomen. But then her eyes softened, a subtle sign that she understood the true nature of the outburst.

“Boss… is this about the perfume situation spreading through the noble circles?” she asked carefully, her tone probing yet respectful.

At the mention of that foreign word, the two others in the room reacted in strikingly different ways.

Ming Hu raised a brow in confusion.

Fairy Qin, on the other hand, lit up with excitement.

“You know about perfume, Hilda? Do you know where I can get some?” she asked eagerly, clutching Hilda’s thick arm with both hands.

“Hold on,” Ming Hu said, standing up from his shattered chair and the ruins of the table. Towering and broad, the full extent of his intimidating frame became clear—a living mountain, the true face of the Black Tiger Gang's boss.

“What is this perfume? And what does it have to do with the noblewomen disrespecting my daughter?” His tone left no room for lies or evasion.

Fairy Qin launched into an explanation, detailing the perfume’s rise as the newest luxury craze sweeping the noble circles. Her description—tinged with bias and emotion—was impassioned, but sufficient for her father to understand the gist.

“And you’re saying… these noblewomen are intentionally denying you access to this perfume?” Ming Hu asked, his displeasure seeping into every syllable.

Qin Hu nodded enthusiastically, clearly pleased that her father was siding with her.

His voice turned cold. “Which merchant is selling this product? Perhaps I need to remind them who truly runs Ralis City.”

But Qin Hu quickly shook her head, her expression falling.

“Apparently, the person who makes it doesn’t distribute it through merchants. I heard they don’t want to sell it directly at all…”

Ming Hu’s frown deepened. Her excitement made it clear the product was valuable. So why would the creator not sell it?

“If they aren’t working with any merchant groups, then there’s nothing I can do,” he muttered with a sigh, folding his arms.

Both father and daughter looked disheartened. The room fell into a lull—until Hilda’s voice broke the silence.

“If you’re talking about the creator of the perfume… I actually know him.”

“Huh?”

The father and daughter turned to her in unison, eyes gleaming with surprise. It was as if the heavens had dropped a miracle into the room.

“Well?” Ming Hu prompted, stepping closer. “Who is it, and where can I find them?”

Hilda shook her head solemnly. “I’m sorry. He’s a dear friend of mine, and he values his anonymity. I can’t reveal his identity—not even to you.”

Ming Hu’s massive body tensed at the refusal. The air seemed to thicken with impending rage… until Fairy Qin quickly stepped between them.

“Hilda! Can we please buy some perfume from him?” she begged, her wide eyes shimmering. “I promise I’ll only use it for myself. We’ll help keep his identity a secret—please!”

Hilda looked down at the pleading girl, her expression softening noticeably.

“Actually… my friend asked me if I’d be willing to help broker a deal between him and the Black Tiger Gang.”

The moment the words left her mouth, Ming Hu’s body relaxed. A grin spread across his face, growing wider as he saw his daughter practically bouncing with joy. Fairy Qin’s smile stretched from ear to ear, her pearly white teeth gleaming under the candlelight.

“Oh please, Father! Can we do business with Hilda’s friend?” she begged, rushing over to him and wrapping her small hands around his massive, callused ones.

For a moment, Ming Hu stood still—lost in the warmth of her touch. Her soft hands reminded him of someone long gone… his late wife. The woman who had gifted him the greatest treasure in his life: their daughter.

“Little Qin… return to your room while Hilda and I speak,” he said gently, brushing away a lingering tear from her cheek.

“Mmm, yes, Father.” She nodded obediently and turned toward the door. But just before leaving, she spun back around and flashed him a radiant smile.

“You’re the best dad.”

The door closed with a soft click, leaving the room in silence.

“…You didn’t enjoy your dinner?” Hilda asked, gesturing to the wreckage of the table and dishes scattered across the floor from his earlier outburst.

But rather than showing regret, Ming Hu let out a booming laugh. His mood was clearly still lifted by his daughter’s affection.

“Oh, Hilda… you bring me such joy. If I hadn’t vowed to my wife to remain faithful after her passing, I might’ve married you myself,” he said with a crooked grin.

“I already have a husband,” Hilda replied flatly, lifting her chin with mock indignation.

That only made the large man laugh harder.

“Then he’s a lucky whoreson,” he said cheerfully. “Now, tell me about this friend of yours. What kind of deal does he want to broker with us directly?”

But Hilda shook her head.

“First, allow me to clean up this mess. Business shouldn’t be discussed in such squalor.”

Servants were called in swiftly, cleaning the broken dishes and food-stained floor with practiced efficiency. Soon, only a few sturdy chairs remained at the center of the now-cleared room.

Rather than sitting immediately, the muscular Hilda took the lit candle and walked around the room, lighting the others one by one. The increased glow pushed back the gloom, giving the room a renewed warmth.

Once finished, she returned to the chair and parked her broad frame into it without hesitation. Thanks to Ming Hu’s own imposing size, the chairs in his receiving room were built strong—none so much as creaked beneath her weight.

“Now, where were we?” she said, flashing him a relaxed smile.

Amused, Ming Hu lowered himself into the seat across from her and crossed his arms, nodding for her to continue.

“Firstly,” Hilda began, “the most important condition—my friend’s identity must remain hidden. He has no interest in being brought into noble society or placed under scrutiny.”

Ming Hu nodded slowly.

“That makes me think he’s not just some commoner. Perhaps he has noble ties himself, and wishes to avoid entanglements. Am I close?” he asked, his grin turning smug.

Hilda simply shrugged, choosing not to respond to the speculation.

“Secondly,” she continued, “my friend doesn’t want to deal through any middlemen. He wishes to work exclusively with the Black Tiger Gang—using me as his only intermediary. In return, he’s willing to grant you exclusive purchasing rights to his product. As for payment… he’s asking for seventy percent of the resale price.”

“Seventy percent!?” Ming Hu scowled, visibly displeased. His expression made it clear he was ready to reject the proposal outright.

“Boss, if I may…” Hilda said, her tone dropping slightly. “The perfume is just the beginning. My friend has other products lined up—ones tailored for noblewomen, and they’ll drive them wild. If you position this correctly, you’ll control who gets access. You could turn favor and vanity into leverage.”

A brief silence settled between them before Hilda added softly,

“If you play your cards right—”

“I don’t need you to spell it out for me, Hilda,” Ming Hu snapped, cutting her off. His tone was cold, his pride pricked. “I didn’t rise to become the Lord of Ralis City’s underworld by being naive. Do you think I need help reading the board?”

Hilda offered a small shrug, unfazed.

“I was just laying out the opportunity, Boss. No offense intended.”

Ming Hu exhaled slowly, his hand moving to pinch the bridge of his nose.

“…I’m sorry, Hilda. Things have been tense lately. I know you’re only trying to help.”

His voice softened, the weight of recent days slipping into his tone.

“The deal sounds lucrative,” he admitted, offering a wry smile, “but I’m a petty man. I won’t settle for a thirty percent cut—even if the benefits outweigh the cost.”

“Is that your only sticking point?” Hilda asked, her eyes narrowing in thought. “What if I talk him down to sixty percent? Would you consider it then?”

“Fifty,” Ming Hu said firmly, flashing a sharp, toothy grin. “Take it or leave it.”

“Then consider the deal done,” Hilda replied, mirroring his grin.

But behind her eyes, a flicker of smug satisfaction glinted—like a player who’d just forced her opponent to walk right into her trap.
