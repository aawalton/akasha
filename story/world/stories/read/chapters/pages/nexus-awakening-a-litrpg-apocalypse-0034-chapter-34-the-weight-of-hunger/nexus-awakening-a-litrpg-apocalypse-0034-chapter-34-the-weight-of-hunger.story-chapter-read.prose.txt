Chapter 34: The Weight of Hunger

I found the cave relatively quickly. It wasn't hard to locate, considering the sprawling camp of initiates positioned outside like guards at a fortress gate. The cave itself was carved into the dark stone of the dungeon wall, its entrance a perfect black circle that looked nothing like any natural formation. The opening yawned wide enough for three people to walk abreast, its edges smooth and precise as if some massive drill had bored straight through the rock.

This wasn't geology. This was architecture. Another piece of the dungeon's impossible construction, designed by forces that thought in scales and purposes beyond human comprehension. The darkness within seemed to drink in the crimson light that filtered through the alien atmosphere, revealing nothing of what lay beyond the threshold.

Outside the cave mouth, at least thirty initiates had established a miniature city of violence and ambition. Canvas tents stretched between makeshift poles, their dark fabric rippling in the strange air currents that flowed through the dungeon's depths. Wooden tables salvaged from defeated camps supported crude maps drawn on salvaged paper, marking territories and resources like generals planning a siege.

Smaller campsites dotted the area between the larger structures, each one centered around a flickering fire that cast dancing shadows across faces I couldn't quite make out from my distant observation point. The initiates moved with the casual efficiency of people who had been here long enough to establish routines, long enough to believe they owned this piece of the dungeon's twisted geography.

Once I confirmed the numbers, I walked deeper into the alien forest to ensure I wouldn't be discovered. The twisted trees provided excellent cover, their impossible branches creating a canopy thick enough to hide a dozen watchers. I settled against one of the larger trunks, feeling the warm pulse of whatever energy animated these alien plants. My respirator continued its mechanical rhythm, the sound muffled by the strange acoustics of this place where normal physics held no sway.

Hunger was a type of pain, this was true. I had learned that lesson during the essence trial, when poison ate away at my lungs while starvation gnawed at my belly. But hunger was also a void, inevitable as long as you drew breath and your heart continued to beat. All of us would feel it eventually. All of us would know its sharp-toothed embrace, the way it whispered promises of satisfaction that could never quite be fulfilled.

My bloodline carried its own hunger, a desire to consume aspects that grew stronger with each feeding. I could feel that alien appetite stirring within me as I considered the camp ahead, recognizing the feast of power that waited to be claimed. Each initiate represented experience, advancement, another step forward.

The hunger wasn't entirely physical. It went deeper than the body's need for sustenance, reaching into parts of myself I had learned to acknowledge. It was the hunger for strength, for control, for power itself. Simple. Honest. Undressed of pretty justifications.

More than understanding my own appetites, I had learned something crucial about the nature of power itself during my fight with Keth. Others closer to my level, those who had developed attributes near or above mine, were essentially immune to my attempts to use the Flux against them directly. It put the entire system in perspective, revealed the brutal mathematics that governed advancement in this place.

Stats weren't just numbers on a screen. They were the fundamental architecture of existence, the framework that determined who lived and who died when the killing started. A few points difference might mean the gap between crushing someone's heart with a thought and having your own techniques turned back against you.

Due to my relatively low Containment attribute, I was certain others would be able to use the Flux against me in ways I couldn't reciprocate. If it wasn't for my ability to consume enemy techniques, to steal their gathered power and turn it to my own purposes, I would likely be feeding the alien soil of this place by now. My bloodline was the only thing keeping me competitive against opponents who had focused their advancement more carefully than I had.

Defeating Keth had given me a strange sort of pleasure that I was still trying to understand. The satisfaction that came with it went beyond simple relief at having survived another encounter.

I wanted to tell myself it was merely the pleasure of continued existence, the natural euphoria that followed any close brush with death. But that would be another comfortable lie, and I had grown tired of those. The truth was simpler and more disturbing: I had taken genuine pleasure in conquering the challenge Keth represented. In coming out ahead when the odds were stacked against me.

That hunger for victory wasn't new. As much as I told myself the old Varus Thorne was dead and buried, that particular appetite had always been part of me. When I worked through a particularly tricky construction problem, when I managed to lift something that should have been beyond my capabilities, when I found a solution that others had missed, there had always been that moment of quiet satisfaction.

The pleasure of competence. Of proving to myself that I could handle whatever the world threw at me.

I was finding combat carried the same addictive quality. Even during Vash's brutal training sessions, there had been moments of genuine satisfaction when I successfully executed a technique or lasted longer than expected against her relentless assault. I hadn't wanted to acknowledge it at the time, had told myself I was merely enduring what was necessary for survival.

Another lie. I had enjoyed those moments when I proved myself capable, when I demonstrated that I could adapt and overcome and grow stronger through adversity.

The blue notification that had been patiently waiting in my peripheral vision finally demanded attention, its familiar glow cutting through my contemplation.

You are now Flux Level 19. You may assign 2 attribute points.

I stared at the text for a long moment, considering my options with the careful deliberation of someone whose life depended on making the right choice. Two points weren't much, but they could still make the difference between victory and death when the margins were this narrow.

Logic suggested I should invest in Containment. My recent fight with Keth had shown exactly how vulnerable I was to direct Flux attacks, how my neglect of defensive capabilities could have proven fatal if not for my bloodline's unique advantages. Shoring up that weakness made tactical sense, the kind of measured decision a smart fighter would make.

Except the same fight had also demonstrated how crucial Resonance and Projection were to my combat effectiveness. When I couldn't affect an enemy directly, when their defenses were too strong for conventional assault, I needed overwhelming power to find alternative approaches. The fire that had consumed Keth hadn't responded to moderate influence. It had required the absolute authority that came with superior Resonance.

The truth was that I could consume most abilities used against me, my bloodline granting me a defense that couldn't be measured in simple attribute points. I didn't need conventional Containment when I could steal enemy techniques and turn them to my own advantage.

But I hadn't been able to affect Keth through normal means, and I was willing to bet Molech would present the same challenge. If anything, the draxi would be even more dangerous, his faction larger and better organized than Keth's small band had been. When that confrontation came, and it would come eventually, I would need every scrap of power I could muster.

I allocated both points to Resonance, feeling the familiar surge of energy as the Flux flowed through newly opened channels. The sensation was becoming addictive, that moment of expansion when raw power settled into my bones and sinew. Each advancement settled into my bones like it belonged there, power finding its natural home.

That done, I turned my attention to the tactical problem spread out before me. A direct approach similar to what I had used against Keth's camp might work here, systematically eliminating patrols until the numbers became manageable. The strategy had proven effective before, and there was something to be said for sticking with methods that produced results.

However, that approach required the enemy to actually send out patrols to begin with. If they were disciplined enough to maintain their position without ranging far from the cave mouth, I would need to find another way to reduce their numbers.

I returned to my previous observation point, settling into the alien undergrowth with the patience of a predator studying its prey. The strange plants seemed to respond to my presence, their leaves shifting color slightly as if recognizing something kindred in my awakened bloodline. I masked my Flux aspects as best I could, drawing on whatever authority my heritage granted over the energy that connected all things.

With my bloodline, this concealment came easier than it should have for someone of my supposed level. I couldn't be certain my camouflage was perfect, but it would have to suffice. Discovery now would mean facing thirty opponents simultaneously, and even my enhanced capabilities had limits.

I watched for what felt like hours, tracking the patterns of movement within the camp, memorizing faces and noting who seemed to hold authority among the assembled killers. The artificial lighting that illuminated the dungeon's depths never changed, making it impossible to judge the passage of time by any normal standard. Here, minutes and hours were measured by the rhythm of violence and the slow accumulation of power.

Some initiates disappeared into the cave's black mouth, presumably to scout or guard whatever lay within its depths. But no patrols ventured beyond the immediate vicinity of their camp, no hunting parties ranged into the forest where I might intercept them individually. They seemed content to hold their position, probably under orders to guard the cave entrance by a superior power. It would demand cunning, creativity, the willingness to fight dirty when clean fighting wasn't sufficient.

The mechanical rhythm of my breathing filled the silence as I planned their deaths, each exhalation a promise of the violence to come. They had something I needed, something that would bring me closer to my ultimate goal. The fact that claiming it would require wading through an ocean of blood was simply the price of advancement in this hostile reality.

I had learned to pay such prices without hesitation. Such concerns were behind me now.

Something that understood the only currency that mattered in this place was power, and power was always paid for in blood.

The hunt was about to begin in earnest, and I found myself looking forward to it with an anticipation that should have disturbed me more than it did. But hunger was honest, at least. More honest than the lies I had once told myself about the fundamental goodness of the universe.

And I was very, very hungry.
