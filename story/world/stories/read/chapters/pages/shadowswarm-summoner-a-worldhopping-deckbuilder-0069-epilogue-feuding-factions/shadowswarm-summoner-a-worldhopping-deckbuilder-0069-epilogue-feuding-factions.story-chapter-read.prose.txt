Epilogue: Feuding Factions

Charles Vanderborn’s backup airship wasn’t glamorous, comfortable, or fast. There was enough room for maybe three people total, and it didn’t have his cozy, enlarged cabin. It was, in fact, just a place where he and his daughter couldn’t escape each other ever, and the uncomfortable silence was starting to get to him. Two days alone in the Aetheric Expanse could make anyone crazy.

“How is Harrison?” he asked, seemingly out of nowhere.

Elaine sat a foot away in one of the passenger seats at the front of the ship. Without his golem to help, he had to control them whenever they were moving. The entire time she was silent, judgmental company.

“Oh, you know,” said Elaine, voice bereft of interest. “Doing foolish artist things instead of being big and important. He’s the king of his group of friends. I know it doesn’t beat being king of a world, but…”

Charles took a deep breath and held it. He supposed she had every right to be angry at him. She’d gone from being his favorite to him disapproving of her husband, and then her soft-hearted daughter. He’d never been quiet about his distaste of either.

“You know I don’t mean it like that,” he protested after exhaling. “When one has the power I do, it comes with certain obligations -- “

“Ah yes. It wasn’t your choice to become a conqueror, you had to do it. Dad, I don’t judge or hate you for being a monster. I’ve always known what you are. I judge and hate you because you’re too much of a coward to admit who and what you are. We’re going to get Jessica back, and then you’re going to use every ounce of political power you have to protect her. Am I clear?”

There was no heat in her voice. There rarely was. Elaine was capable of being cool, cold even, at all times. Fierce but not ferocious. It was one of the many things he treasured about her, and would’ve made her a worthy heir…Until she’d fallen in love.

“Then I wanted to do it. Is that what you want to hear, Elaine? I love power and I want immortality. What powerful wizard doesn’t? Other than you, obviously.”

She didn’t answer immediately. Instead, she stared out into the sea of stars and odd cosmic lights, as beautiful as they were haunting. “Something’s been bothering me. Why were you handing out Wildcards as tournament prizes?”

Charles blinked. The question had eaten at him as well too, but he hadn’t been one to question orders. “The Emperor’s top advisor believes that it will fuel our expansion faster. Specifically, Wildcards for people who are young and not far enough along in their advancement to be set in their ways. The power in the cards must react differently to youth and potential, so we’re trying it as this next century’s expansion technique.”

“Aside from plaguing a continent, you mean.” Elaine’s lips twitched at the sound of exasperation from her father. “So that’s it? You have the rarest form of card, and you want to give it to young people to see what happens?”

“When you have the kind of wealth, power, and lifespan the Emperor does,” said Charles testily, “you start to see things differently. The scale is something we cannot grasp. If the advisor, who has been around nearly as long as Turhan, says that it matters, then we’ll try it.”

Elaine nodded quietly, then resumed her search for the universe while the Backup chugged along. “Are you sure this is the right direction?” she asked, not for the first time.

“I am. They were not quiet or subtle about their departure, and it looks like they burned full speed ahead the entire time. It wouldn’t surprise me if the ship buckles and fails before too long. Foolish children.”

“Oh yes, so foolish, afraid for their lives and looking for a way to survive.”

“Will you stop giving me an attitude about that?” Charles snapped. “It’s the way of the world, and you are a fool for walking away from it. And for what?” He scoffed. “A painter of all things. But we’ve argued this a million times. Let’s just worry about bringing Jessica home. And I will keep her safe.”

Unless it ruined his career. Elaine’s presence meant that Jessica would have an easier time surrendering peacefully, but there was a real chance that this got back to the Emperor and would destroy his reputation if they managed to do any damage or interfere with a local world’s business.

The thought had plagued him off and on the past two days, but now it came back with a vengeance. And as soon as it did, an instrument on the control panel rang.

Charles’ stomach twisted as he answered the call. It crackled to life, and he heard breathing come from the speaker. “Agent Vanderborn, passcode Silent Alpha Strike. Who am I addressing?”

He knew, before the laugh. Before the smug, lazy, pretentious laugh. “Looks like you have a problem on your hand, Charles.”

He counted to three before saying, “Raynor. I take it you have found my wayward granddaughter?”

“Indeed I have. Or at least, one of my men has. She and a friend are being held captive on Stonskarr. Her other friends were due to be executed, but survived and got away. But only after killing several dozen of my men. Care to explain, Charles, or should I pass it along and let the next tier figure out what to do?”

That son of a bitch was positively gleeful. Even Elaine looked amused at his anger, but Charles forced himself to be calm. “The explanation is that things got out of hand and I could use a favor. What do you want from me, Raynor? Don’t be coy.”

“Mmm. Tough to say. Part of me wants to think it’s enough to let you crash and burn, this close to your governorship. Wouldn’t that be funny, knowing you almost made it before you pissed it all away?”

“Hilarious.”

A wistful sigh came through. Charles nearly cut the communication, but he waited. Raynor couldn’t resist baiting him, but he’d get to the point eventually.

“Well, as funny as that would be, it’s Empire first. We’ll deal with the pests like we do all the others. But if you want your granddaughter alive, then I’m keeping your ship.”

“The hell you are!” Charles snarled.

Elaine shot him a dirty look and then took over. “Excuse me. Raynor, is it?”

A pause. “Yes Ma’am. And who do I have the pleasure of addressing?” Pure amusement, not a worry at all.

“Elaine Vanderborn. It’s my daughter you have captured.”

“Ahh, of course. My sympathies to you, Elaine. It’s nothing personal, as I’m sure you understand.”

“Of course. But it is personal for me. So let me make this clear: if anything happens to my daughter, I will end your entire bloodline. I don’t care if you’re a quasi-immortal governor with ten worlds or whatever nonsense you arrogant bastards love to claim. I will end them all, and then you. Do not fuck with me.”

Charles shook his head. Threats never worked on Raynor, they just made him more of a cheeky jerk. But to his surprise, the man laughed.

“Mmm. I like you, Elaine Vanderborn. You’ll get your daughter. But if you want this to stay quiet, The Wanderer is mine.”

“Agreed,” said Elaine.

“What? No,” Vanderborn protested, heat flooding his face.

“Fantastic. Come see me at the Obelisk, and we’ll discuss terms. Byyyyye.” The sound cut out.

“What the hell do you think you’re doing? That ship is worth -- “

“More than your granddaughter’s life?” Elaine challenged. “Don’t. This is why I came with you. I know for a fact that you would’ve let Raynor hurt Jessica. Work with me and we’ll double-cross him when the time comes. We’ll get my daughter back, you can take out a rival or whatever it is you feel you need to do to soothe your precious ego. We all win.”

Things had been better once, and Vanderborn longed for the days when his daughter shared his ambitions and excitement to cross into different worlds and enjoy a healthy win against rivals. But maybe that could still be.

“Do you really mean you’ll help me take out Raynor?” he finally asked.

Assassinating a rival could get him in trouble, and taking out the leader of a cluster of worlds would be chaotic, but there was a lot to gain from it as well. Namely, Raynor’s cards and artifacts. It would be possible to pick his bones clean. All they’d need was a bit of help from one or more of his underlings who wanted to rise up.

“If I have to,” said Elaine. “I don’t want any part of this, but I’m not coming home without Jessica.”

Charles sighed and continued to fly the ship. The Backup was a slow, plodding thing, and they were a ways off from Rihzor and Stonskarr.  It would take nearly a week to get there, and then more to deal with Raynor and get a Levi-Box to not crash on Stonskarr. What a headache.

“If we bring her home safe and sound, will you finally stop treating me like this?” He finally asked.

Elaine shrugged. “Only one way to find out.”

They had their goals. Arrive, get Jessica, kill Raynor, and return home before the Emperor found out and interfered.

And, of course, he’d make sure to kill Ambrose Adams for getting them to this point. The thought brought a smile to his face, and he resumed flying in renewed, resolute silence.

The End.
