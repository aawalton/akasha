It was dark. The sun had gone down hours ago and only the light of the stars and moon remained to guide their steps. Still, Jia and Eui ran. Paradoxically, rather than slowing to fatigue, the pair had steadily been getting faster and more confident in their strides, and for the last hour, they had been running at a full sprint. The other disciples had long since left—a few stayed behind briefly for the spectacle of it, but they were just running and it got old fast. Now only one observer remained.

Master Ienaga Yumi continued to watch the pair impassively. She hadn’t told them to stop, and she hadn’t left. Lee Jia wasn’t sure why, but she was determined to keep going until Ienaga said otherwise. She didn’t know what Ienaga was waiting for, or what she was expecting, but Lee Jia didn’t plan to disappoint. Besides, this was fun.

It was the first time since she’d come to this academy that she actually felt the magic of it. Sure, it was awe-inspiring to gain an entire new sense, and to use magical formations like the stove or the shower. But people had those back home—not Lee Jia, she couldn’t afford them—but they weren’t uncommon. Sharing memories with Eui had been...odd. A little bit harrowing, but this—this was different. It was such a small simple thing, to just run and never stop—never get tired. But it made Lee Jia feel like she was more.

It was also, unfortunately, excruciatingly painful. The bursts of energy got stronger and lasted longer each time, but the same was true of the walls she had to break through to achieve them. Once the soothing effect of the flood of ki ran its course, Lee Jia’s entire body would throb with pain. Only the promise of another burst of energy—and her determination to meet whatever vague expectation Ienaga had for her—kept her going through the agony.

There was something else as well. Each time the ki flooded Lee Jia’s body, there was an indescribable pressure left behind. It had been steadily building more and more as she ran, and now it felt as though she was about to explode out of her own skin. She wasn’t sure what was going to happen when she broke through the next wall, but she had a feeling it would be the last one—whatever that meant.

When the burst of energy finally came, something was different. Instead of the soothing relief she had been expecting, she was brought to her knees by a pain that made the last twelve hours seem downright relaxing by comparison.

“Don’t lose focus! Sit down, concentrate on that feeling.”

Ienaga’s voice was a shallow comfort as Lee Jia tried to force herself into a lotus position and begin meditating. Concentrate on that feeling? It felt like every pore of her body was being stabbed by a red-hot needle.

“Breathe! Follow my voice. I know it hurts, but you have to stay focused.”

Lee Jia did as she was told. Gritting her teeth through the pain and concentrating on her breathing. The strange pressure she had been feeling continued to build, the pain growing more and more intense as it did. Just when Lee Jia thought she was going to explode, as if a dam had burst, the pain fled her body and took her consciousness with it.

When Lee Jia awoke, the first thing she noticed was the smell—she sat up urgently as she felt her gorge rising. As she turned to retch, she saw the source of the smell. A sticky black sludge was covering her from head to toe, and smelled of all the worst things she could imagine put together. She threw up and, to her dismay, produced more of the disgusting sludge in the process. She wasn’t even going to try to describe the taste, she knew she’d just vomit again if she did. Her retching and coughing was interrupted by Master Ienaga’s voice.

“Congratulations on your awakening. My apologies for not warning you, the process of purging bodily impurities is...very unpleasant. Especially the first time.”

Lee Jia groaned in dismay.

“The—cough—the first time!?”

Master Ienaga averted her gaze and cleared her throat.

“Ahem. Anyway, you should take the next few days off. The high from awakening will last for a few hours, but afterwards today’s training is going to take its toll.”

Lee Jia took a moment to process what Ienaga had said as she tried in vain to wipe the disgusting sludge off of herself. Aside from the nausea, she felt pretty amazing. Despite spending the entire day and most of the night running, she didn’t feel hungry or thirsty or even tired. In fact, she felt completely refreshed—as though she’d had a hundred good nights of sleep all at once.

“Uuuugh, ancestors, what the fuck!?”

Lee Jia was interrupted from her reverie by Eui’s cursing. Master Ienaga held a fist to her mouth and Lee Jia thought it looked like she was trying to suppress a chuckle.

“That was an interesting technique you discovered, Lee. It resembles one of my own more advanced combat meditation techniques, except you did it before even awakening.”

“Eh!? W-well, I couldn’t have figured it out without your advice so…”

Lee Jia demurred, but Ienaga just smiled and shook her head.

“I only offered guidance—your accomplishments are your own. What’s more surprising is how you shared the technique with An. What was that?”

Jia shared an uncertain glance with Eui before turning back to Master Ienaga with a shrug.

“That’s hard to explain. Especially since I don’t actually understand it, myself.”

Eui nodded in agreement and Ienaga seemed to accept that.

“Very well. You two should get home and clean up before the exhaustion catches up to you. I thought this might happen, so I had new robes delivered to your dorm—you should probably burn those.”

Lee Jia looked down at her filthy uniform and grimaced. It was such a waste, but she couldn’t imagine how she would ever get the smell out of it, even if it was magical.

“Rest for the next two days and then start attending with the advanced group. You’re dismissed.”

Jia and Eui bowed to their instructor and took their leave. On the way back, Lee Jia could feel the ki thrumming within her body. It wasn’t quite a new sense like her mana sense had been, though perhaps it could be described as an extension of it. It felt more like learning to see a new color, rather than gaining an entirely new sense.

If she focused she could adjust the flow of ki within her slightly, but it wasn’t full control. She also felt that with the impurities removed, there was now plenty of room in her body for more ki. From her previous discussions with Dae, she recalled that this was the ki gathering stage of martial arts.

So she was a martial artist now—not that she knew any martial arts. Come to that, she was also a mage who didn’t know any magic. Still, it felt good knowing that she’d had her second awakening so soon. Were they the first? Almost certainly they were within the academy, but Lee Jia wondered whether someone else out there had already learned how to combine the disciplines, or if she and Eui had started something brand new. Those thoughts were dismissed as soon as they arrived back at the dorm.

The shower! Jia and Eui both ran straight for the bathroom. Neither was willing to wait, nor were they about to waste time arguing over who would go first. With a silent agreement, the pair forgot their modesty and entered together.

It took nearly an hour of scrubbing to clean themselves of the vile black goo, and the uniforms were completely ruined. Luckily, the new robes promised by Ienaga had already been delivered by the time they arrived, and soon the girls were comfortably clothed, warming themselves by the fire, and fully prepared never to speak of this again.

Still feeling the buzz from awakening and unable to sleep despite the lateness of the hour, Jia and Eui investigated the other materials that had been sent along with their replacement robes. In addition to the clothing, there was a jade slip and a note. Jia sat next to Eui and looked over her shoulder at the note, though she couldn’t read it.

The note explained that unlike the basic lessons which focused on awakening, the advanced lessons would focus on techniques. It stressed that they would still have to spend time meditating to build up their ki. The jade slip contained the breathing technique that Ienaga had guided them through during their awakening. It was much simpler to understand than the one Elder Qin and provided, and Jia had to wonder if Qin’s technique had been intentionally difficult to grasp.

Still, the abundance of free time suddenly made a lot more sense. The lessons were meant for learning techniques and theories, while they were expected to spend their free time cultivating. Jia yawned as she wondered just how much meditation it would take to reach a new stage.

She leaned into Eui as she felt a wave of exhaustion wash over her. Her eyelids were growing heavy, and it seemed like the exertion was finally catching up to her. She’d get up to go to bed in a moment—first she just needed to rest her eyes for a—

Lee Jia stirred awake to the warm light of the sun in her face. When she moved to sit up, she felt something shift against her side and turned to find herself face to face with Eui’s sleeping visage—close enough that she could feel Eui’s breath on her face. Eui’s eyes slowly cracked open, the motion having jostled her awake. Their eyes met and for a beat they just stared at each other—both of them slightly disoriented from the unusual awakening. The spell broke as their brains caught up to the present and they jerked upright, making space between them.

“H-how long have we been asleep?”

Eui’s face was so red it was practically glowing, and Jia felt like hers was probably a match. Neither of them met each other’s gaze as Jia answered.

“U-um, I don’t know. The sun seems pretty high so it must have been a while. I don’t even remember falling asleep.”

In the awkward silence that followed, Jia had time to take stock of herself. She felt stiff, but not as sore as she had been expecting, and though she hadn’t eaten since yesterday, she only felt mildly hungry. After stretching and brushing the hair out of her eyes, Jia noticed Eui staring at her intensely. Jia froze, a blush slowly creeping onto her face.

“W-what? Is something weird? Why are you looking at me like that?”

“Your hair is longer.”

Oh! So it was. Somehow Jia’s hair had grown out during her awakening, and she now had a horrible, messy mop of hair that fell down to her shoulders. She sighed. She supposed she’d just have to cut it again. Eui was still staring.

“Well so is yours!”

Unlike Jia’s wavy, frizzy mess, Eui’s hair was perfectly straight and silky smooth—albeit slightly sleep-tousled—and it now reached halfway down her back. Jia was a little bit envious of that. Apparently, Eui took immaculate care of her hair—it reminded her of her mother.

Jia shook her head. Since she had no memory of her own mother, that thought must have come from Eui. That was still happening? While she wondered if they should ask Magus Hwang about it, Jia noticed that Eui had gotten a lot closer while she was lost in her own head. She tried to back up, but there was no more room on the couch.

“E-Eui? What’s wrong?”

“You look ridiculous. Let me fix it.”

“Fix it!?”

Jia scrambled over the side of the couch and backed away from Eui as she stood up and produced two items from the sleeves of her robe. In one hand she brandished a knife, in the other a comb. Jia wasn’t sure which one scared her more.

“H-hold on! Let’s talk about this!”

Eui advanced on her.

“Jia you can’t keep it that way! I’m not going to hurt you, don’t be a baby!”

“I’ll just cut it back to the way it—”

“Absolutely not!”

Jia’s back bumped up against the wall as Eui finally cornered her, her eyes glimmering with a menacing aura.

“Don’t I get a say in this!?”

As it turned out, she did not. After over a half hour of torturous grooming, cutting, and more than a few rebukes from Eui about taking better care of herself, Eui presented Jia with a small pocket mirror. Jia regarded herself and—wow, she had to admit that Eui knew her stuff. She looked good.

Her white hair was now cut evenly just above the shoulder and styled in such a way that it wouldn’t fall into her eyes anymore. With proper grooming, her natural waves looked smooth and silky rather than the frizzy mop from before. Before she could finish admiring her new look, something else caught her attention.

Jia’s tan had faded far more than a few cloudy autumn days could possibly account for. Furthermore, some of the nicks and scars from a life of living on the streets had faded or even disappeared—even a notch in one of her ears had vanished. Was this a benefit of her awakening as a martial artist? If scars could disappear, then what about Eui’s—

Eui’s brand was still there, just as prominent as before, and it hardly took much inspection to see why. To Jia’s mana sense the scar was an intricate weave of mana—a formation of some kind. Eui noticed her staring and covered the scar self-consciously with her hair.

“Sorry! I didn’t mean to stare. I just noticed that some of my own scars had faded and—”

“It’s fine! I checked too, but I think it’s magical.”

“Yeah...still, isn’t this kind of amazing? Takeda mentioned something about martial artists having tough bodies but this is totally different from what I expected! Old scars healed, I’m not sore from yesterday’s training, I’m hardly hungry after more than a day without eating—all that just from being able to control our ki?”

Eui nodded and rubbed at her chin thoughtfully.

“I wonder if it was the same with mana. We’ve been distracted by other things but don’t you find that you’re thinking clearer ever since we awakened? Like it’s easier to focus, or you notice small details you might have missed before.”

“Yes! It’s subtle but I think you’re right. Is it like that for every discipline? I wonder what a spiritual awakening is like. We should spend today trying to figure out that jade slip from Elder Qin. It’s not like there’s anything else to do toda—aah!”

“What?”

Eui quirked a brow at Jia’s outburst. Jia buried her face in her hands and groaned.

“I forgot! I was supposed to meet Dae today to study calligraphy! He was going to teach me how to read and write so that I can start learning talismans! Oh ancestors ashes! It’s already past noon!”

Eui covered her mouth and snickered at Jia.

“You’d better move fast or he’ll think you stood him up. You’re going to break the poor boy’s heart.”

“What? It’s just studying, Eui. Don’t be so dramatic.”

Eui’s smile dropped into a look of confusion, and then further into a frown.

“Jia, you cannot be that dense.”

“I don’t know what you’re talking about Eui, but I’ll have to find out later—I have to go. Ugh, I don’t even know where I am supposed to meet him.”

Lee Jia left her bewildered friend behind and rushed out the door to start running for the central courtyard. Stupid! She should have arranged a meeting place ahead of time.

“Ok, Jia. If you were a book-loving overachiever, where would you go to study?”

Her first thought was to try the lecture hall. Were they allowed to access it while it wasn’t in use? Maybe there was another class today. Lee Jia was so lost in her thoughts that she nearly ran headlong into someone on the street.

“Oof—sorry! I was in a hurry and wasn’t paying attention!”

“Tsk, watch where you’re going you—wait a minute, I know you!”

Lee Jia took a step back and regarded the person she’d nearly toppled. A tall boy with long dark hair, a topknot, and a sleeveless uniform. Between that and the fiery impression the boy’s aura gave off, Lee Jia had no doubt that this was Yan Zhihao—the same boy she had seen defeat Takeda in a duel on the first day.

“Sorry, I don’t think we’ve met. You must have mistaken me for someone else.”

Lee Jia tried to put her head down and go around the boy, but Yan reached out and grabbed her by the arm, stopping her in her tracks.

“No, I don’t think I am. You’re the beastkin that’s been the talk of the town all day—or one of them, at least. I heard about the scene you made yesterday, and there’s an interesting rumor behind that which I’d like you to confirm for me.”

Lee Jia didn’t like where this was going. Yan Zhihao had been one of the disciples to drop out of Ienaga’s training during the first session. She had not seen him during the second.

“A few disciples have been speculating that you and your friend managed to figure out a trick to awakening your ki, is that true? I suspected the savages were only giving us half the story, but that would confirm it.”

Lee Jia narrowed her eyes at Yan, and decided she didn’t like him. She might have just told him if he’d asked nicely, but to waylay her on the street like this? Calling her names and insulting their instructor?

“I don’t know what you’re talking about. We were only able to run like that because of—aaagh!”

Lee Jia’s response was cut off by a searing pain in her wrist, where Yan Zhihao held her. The burning sensation seemed to penetrate into her skin and spread through her veins like a poison. Reflexively, her ki pushed back against the intrusion and after a moment the pain was gone, as if it had never happened. Lee Jia snatched her hand away from Yan Zhihao and glared at him, sweat forming on her brow. Yan regarded her with a smug grin.

“I knew it! So there was a secret after all. To think some of the disciples are still letting that savage torture them into figuring it out themselves. Obviously you didn’t discover it yourself, so where did you get your information? Was it that savage that I put in her place the first day? I hear you were the one who escorted her home after that.”

Lee Jia’s eyes widened in surprise. Yan was surprisingly well informed, albeit also completely mistaken. She had seen his type before, though—so completely confident in his own narrative that nothing she could say would convince him otherwise. He wouldn’t believe the truth, nor would he be satisfied by a lie that didn’t match his preconceptions.

“There’s no secret—nobody told me anything. I just followed Master Ienaga’s instructions, the same ones she gave to everybody.”

Yan scoffed and shook his head, his smirk never fading.

“You’re right to keep your secrets. Of course I’m not asking you to just tell me. There are civilized ways to do these things.”

Yan Zhihao spread his arms wide and towered over Lee Jia’s diminutive frame.

“I, Yan Zhihao, formally challenge you to a duel!”
