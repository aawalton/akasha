The consciousness of the Origin Planes never spoke. Never advised. Never helped. But even Origin Storms recognize Authority. And in its presence, the laws of becoming are not broken. They are bent—ever so slightly.

Fragment from The Codex of Origin Phenomena, Volume VII

Year 2002, East Gordon.

Crixis shuddered as the amethyst drop splashed against his forehead, cool against his skin yet scalding like hot coals. And then it dissolved into his mind, a raging river of lightning that sent his consciousness into overdrive.

He stumbled from the force of its power. The chamber door slammed shut. Time stopped, and the world slowed to a crawl.

His ears popped. His eyes burned. And his mind—choked full of knowledge on his path and bloodline—began expanding, stretching past its natural bounds until it completely broke through the natural and into the supernatural.

Thoughts bubbled and gathered as it expanded, merging into fragments of insights that further converged into understanding. Into inspiration.

The world fell away, crumpling like paper as the Origin Plane of storm revealed itself in all its tumultuous glory—thunder crashing, lightning striking, and winds howling. It reigned supreme, dominating the sky in its fury.

Nothing was above it. Nothing should have been.

And yet, even as he basked in the path of superiority, another called to him. A stronger path. A path that didn't need physical prowess to stand above. A path rooted in identity. In presence and control rather than mere strength.

The path of Ruinous Authority.

Aodhán's path.

It called to him, seducing him with the prospect of unquantifiable control and presence. The result of a four-path merge. It was greatness on a platter. If he chose that path, he could become like Aodhán. He could become a cornerstone of the Storm Sect.

But as great as the path was, Crixis loved his path too much to leave it.

It was his father's path, after all, and his grandfather's before him. But more importantly, it was his path. And he wasn't ready to abandon it, not for all the control in the world.

The origin plane thundered in response to his thoughts. Chaos surged. An overwhelming consciousness loomed, and Crixis prepared his mind to be invaded.

However, rather than delving into his mind like he expected, the consciousness offered him something else entirely.

An alternative.

A vision seized him in that moment, and Crixis found himself atop a mountain peak, a raging storm overhead and ravaged earth below. Winds howled around him, thunder boomed, and from the storm, thousands of lightning bolts struck down, wreaking havoc on the earth and ripping jagged furrows through it.

The storm was relentless—a titan of fury that held the sky in its grip, turning day into night with clouds so thick they choked out the sun. Lightning carved the world below into fragments of light and shadow, each bolt striking with the certainty of divine judgment.

But it wasn't just the destruction that caught Crixis's attention.

It was the inevitability of it.

The storm didn't ask for permission to rage. It didn't negotiate with the earth or plead with the sky. It simply was—an undeniable force that arrived, dominated, and departed on its own terms.

You could see it gathering on the horizon. Feel the pressure drop. Watch the clouds roll in like an army assembling for war. You would see it coming, and yet, there was no stopping it.

No turning it back.

No negotiating its departure.

It would come. It would strike. And all you could do was endure.

The realization hit Crixis like a physical blow as the vision crystallized in his mind.

Superiority was great. The storm certainly dominated all beneath it. Authority was greater—a path that granted complete command over the sky and all its phenomena.

But this... this was something else entirely.

Inevitability.

A storm that, once formed, could never be denied.

It was greater than mere superiority. So much more than the power to crush one's enemies or command the storm. It was certainty—the power to arrive—to become a force so inexorable that resistance felt like fighting the tide itself.

The path of Ruinous Inevitability.

The name whispered through his mind like the chaos itself had spoken it, and Crixis felt something shift deep within his spirit. The path called to him, drawing him with the promise of a storm that would always arrive. A storm that would always strike.

For a long moment, he hesitated.

His father's path. His grandfather's before him. The path of superiority that had defined his family for generations.

Could he truly abandon it? Even for something greater?

But then the vision pulsed again, and Crixis understood. This wasn't abandonment. This was evolution. A branch, not a betrayal. The storm would still reign supreme—it simply wouldn't ask permission first.

He could already imagine it—every attack finding its mark, every strike rendering resistance futile, every bolt inevitable.

The thought crystallized in his mind, and chaos surged in response. The vision released him, and once more Crixis found himself within the Origin Plane of Storm, chaos whispers rushing into his mind.

They tried to sway him, made it hard to think, and pressed against his resolve like a physical weight.

But Crixis didn't need to think anymore.

He had already made his decision.

He looked up at the consciousness roiling above him and smiled. "I am inevitable."

An overwhelming sense of acceptance engulfed him in that moment, and then a scalding heat as a seal was branded to his chest.

And then another.

Back in the library, his body spasmed, locking in place as all the insights he had gathered began condensing into inspirations. Power radiated from him in waves, and on his skin, his bloodline began to manifest, strange markings of gold and amethyst appearing on his body.

Aodhán watched it all with a small smile, noting that Crixis hadn't taken his path after all.

He had created his own.

He turned to Mirelle. "Will you stay with them? I need rest."

"Go," Mirelle nodded. "Recharge. You'll need your strength in the next few days."

Nodding, Aodhán left the chamber. He bowed to Elder Kudrov as he stepped out, but the woman barely heard him, her gaze fixed intensely on the nine disciples who were seemingly gaining seals at the same time as a result of one man's skill.

No doubt she would spread this news among the other elders, who would in turn tell it to the High Elders, but Aodhán didn't care about keeping the skill secret. It would be a great boost for the coming war, and they needed every advantage they could get.

He reached his house a few minutes later, sighing wearily as he shut the door behind himself and summoned Varéc out of his spirit.

"Ugh,” he placed a hand on Varéc’s snout and groaned. “I’m so tired. I need like ten hours of sleep."

Varéc growled affectionately and raised a wing, inviting Aodhán to snuggle, and with a weary smile, Aodhán did, lowering himself against Varéc’s rippling torso. He had exhausted four whole seals on that skill alone, not to mention the immense amount of energy and willpower.

His spirit was in severe need of replenishing, and though he could make himself feel better with a quick activation of Ruinous Baptism, Aodhán decided to settle for a few hours of sleep instead.

Varéc’s wings covered him, and two minutes later, he was snoring softly while Varéc quietly turned his gaze to the opposite wall to practice his speech.

However, barely two hours later, Aodhán was roused by the persistent ringing of a notification in his mind. With a weary groan, he lifted his communication ring to eye level and opened the message.

He read it once. Twice. And then blinked awake.

“It worked!” He jerked to his feet immediately, a wide smile on his face. “It worked, Varéc! The pendant worked.”

Varéc growled happily, Aodhán's excitement infectious as he read the message again. "I can't believe it. Well, I can, but I can't." He shook his head and began heading for the door. "Come on, Varéc. Let's go see."

He raced toward the door, excitement and adrenaline pushing him forward—only for his steps to falter as his core sense flared, detecting an unfamiliar presence just outside his compound.

He paused, hands halting on the door’s handle in caution.

He knew the energy signature of every special in the sect, so he knew this wasn’t any of his friends. Yet the blazing intensity of the core was undeniable—the weight it exerted on reality impossible to ignore.

The person outside was a transmigrant. And one far more powerful than Sophran.

The thought gave Aodhán pause, and he took a deep breath before turning the handle. The door opened slowly, and when he stepped out, it was with a cautious smile. "Good afternoon. I take it you must be Dravion."

The boy turned to look at him, crimson hair cascading past his shoulders. He couldn't have been more than two years older than Aodhán himself, yet he seemed older—ancient, even—with crimson-gold irises that hinted at deep wisdom.

His gaze lingered on Aodhán for a moment, then he frowned faintly. “Good afternoon. Who are you?”

Aodhán's smile widened. "I'm your newest neighbor." He stepped out of the house and stretched a hand to Dravion in greeting. "Aodhán Brystion. Nice to meet you."

***

Dravion Ashscar stared at Aodhán's outstretched arm for a brief second before tentatively grabbing it. "You already know who I am," he stated in lieu of an introduction.

Everyone in the storm sect knew of him. Hell, nearly everyone in Gordon knew of him. He was the Storm transmigrant who had gained a thunderbird familiar at the lowly tier of twenty-five. He wasn't just famous. He was legendary.

Which was why it came as a shock when, rather than jump in excitement at the handshake, Aodhán simply smiled. “I have heard of you. The other specials speak highly of you.”

“As they should,” Dravion replied, brushing a crimson lock away from his face with a hint of practiced arrogance. “I am a transmigrant.”

Aodhán's smile thinned. “Right. I heard you were delving a rift.”

“A world rift, actually,” Dravion corrected, a faint crease forming between his brows. “But it's only for transmigra—”

The rest of his words were cut short as Varéc stepped out of the house, lightning rippling and midnight scales gleaming in the evening sun.

Dravion froze.

His eyes widened as he stared at Varéc's imposing form, feeling the earth shudder slightly beneath him as the dragon walked toward them.

“You… you have a familiar,” he said slowly, his gaze darting back to Aodhán.

Aodhán smiled.

“Yes, Dravion. Meet Varéc. He's a Storm Fury. I gained him at the seventeenth tier.”

This time Dravion didn’t respond immediately.

His gaze flicked back to Varéc, who seemed to cut an even more imposing figure as he drew closer. His muscles tensed with each tremor that passed through the earth, and after a few more steps he lifted a palm to halt Varéc's approach.

“That's close enough.” He cleared his throat, glancing briefly at Aodhán. “Birds and dragons aren't particularly friendly.”

But Varéc, being the annoying creature that he was, took another deliberate step forward—clearly done for the sole purpose of making Dravion uncomfortable.

Almost immediately, crimson-gold wings of pure lightning burst from Dravion's back, and a massive thunderbird, nearly rivaling Varéc in size, appeared behind him, its eyes flashing with lightning.

Both Aodhán and Varéc jerked back a step, eyes widening as they took in the magnificent creature. The thunderbird was absolutely beautiful, its wings of healing and destruction nearly spanning the entire length of the courtyard.

It too radiated a sense of ancient power, its crimson-gold eyes seeming to hold a depth of ancient wisdom. But nothing in the world could have prepared Aodhán for what the creature did next.

It spoke. Not screeched. Not shrieked. But it spoke—its voice almost indistinguishable from that of a human.

“Good evening, Aodhán Brystion. I am Giselle. A pleasure to make your acquaintance.” Her avian gaze flicked to Varéc. “And yours, Storm Fury.”

It would be an understatement to say Aodhán was shocked to hear it speak. He had never heard a familiar speak, even though he knew it to be very possible. There were so many factors attached to their speech, but the two major factors were age and the number of advancements the familiar had undergone alongside its bond.

And at the eightieth tier, Giselle had undergone fifty-five advancements alongside Dravion. She was also significantly more advanced in age than Varéc, which showed when Varéc responded to her words with polite growls.

Aodhán nodded, then smiled up at Giselle. “Nice to meet you too, Giselle. I don’t think I’ve ever met a talking familiar before.”

“Then you haven’t met many familiars old enough to speak,” Giselle replied. Then she glanced at her bond. “Dravion is lying, by the way. Thunderbirds have no enmity with storm Furies.”

“Giselle!” Dravion hissed, glaring at his familiar. “This is not the time to be insubordinate.”

Varéc growled then, images of flapping wings and endless skies cascading into Aodhán’s mind.

Aodhán shook his head and whispered, “I’m not sure Dravion would like that, Varéc.”

Despite the whisper, Dravion heard him and glanced over. “Like what?”

Varéc growled again, and Aodhán sighed. “Varéc wants to take to the sky alongside Giselle. I think he might like her.”

Dravion scrunched his lips in distaste, but before he could respond, Giselle did.

“I would like that very much.”

She took to the sky immediately, completely ignoring Dravion’s sharp command for her to return. When she didn’t, Varéc glanced at Aodhán and growled, excitement evident in his tone.

Aodhán glanced at Dravion, searching his gaze for a moment before nodding. “You can go, Varéc.”

Varéc launched himself into the air before he could even finish, lightning exploding beneath his wings as they snapped open, the force of the takeoff slamming into the courtyard like a thunderclap.

Aodhán braced himself instinctively, a hand rising to shield his eyes as wind tore across the ground, sending dust and gravel skittering across the stone. Beside him, Dravion staggered half a step, crimson hair lashing across his face as the shockwave rolled past.

“By all the gods!” he cursed, a scathing glare on his face as he watched the two familiars streak through the air in twin trails of midnight and lightning. “Why can she never listen?!”

Aodhán watched them for a moment before glancing back at him. “I get the feeling she’s… not particularly pleased with you.”

“I can see that,” Dravion growled, and then his expression slowly sobered. He sighed. "She's mourning my underling." He glanced sideways at Aodhán. "He… he died two days ago."

Aodhán noting the tight line of Dravion’s jaw and the faint tremor in his hands. “I… I’m very sorry,” he said softly.

Dravion nodded, though his gaze remained distant. “I’m sorry too. But Jorvik knew what he was getting into when he accepted to be my underling.” He shook his head, a shadow of sadness crossing his features. “I told him he shouldn’t have.”

Silence fell over them, broken only by a slow, measured sigh from Dravion. “I would’ve stayed in the rift a couple more weeks… given her time to grieve… but the war just—”

He trailed off, his expression cracking for a moment before hardening again. “That’s not important.” He glanced at Aodhán. “Where are the others?”

Aodhán studied him briefly before gesturing. “They’re in the seal chambers in the library.”

Dravion blinked in surprise. “All of them at once?”

Aodhán’s lips tugged into a small smile. “All of them except Mirelle.”

Dravion’s expression fell in disappointment, and he nodded. “Well, I guess I’ll just have to see them whenever they come out.”

Without another word, he turned and headed toward his house, leaving Aodhán to watch him go. Losing an underling couldn’t be easy, especially if they had been together for years. More than anything right now, Dravion needed comfort.

Aodhán, however, had none to offer.

He did have a distraction, though, and after a moment of hesitation, he spoke. “We created a pendant that lets one hear the whispers of chaos. Elder Vasmos just used it to gain a familiar.”

Dravion turned toward him.

“I was heading there before you arrived,” Aodhán continued. “Would you like to come with me?”

Dravion opened his mouth, closed it, then shrugged. "Okay. I would like to go with you."

Aodhán smiled.

So maybe Dravion wasn't the politest of persons. But he was a fellow storm cultivator. And Aodhán couldn't simply leave him to grieve alone when he could help it.

He created a storm platform large enough to comfortably fit the both of them and, without delay, launched toward the administration office where Elder Vasmos lived, wondering what animal she had gained as a familiar.

***

Back in the library, Mirelle stared at the seal chambers with envy, wishing she were one of those inside. Sophran’s miracle rain had been impressive—great, even—but it paled in comparison to the primal droplets of power Aodhán had created.

Barely two hours in, and every single person within the seal chambers had developed seals, most gaining two, and an outlier like Crixis, who had gained three.

Elder Kudrov had nearly gone ballistic the moment Crixis’s third seal manifested, promptly summoning half a dozen elders to witness what she called an unprecedented phenomenon.

She wasn’t wrong. As far as the sect was concerned, this skill was completely new. There were buff skills, enhancement skills, and then there was this—a skill so powerful and dangerous it could raise the average cultivator from a five to a seven, or even an eight if they were lucky.

The skill wasn’t just an enhancement. It was insight distilled. It was—

“Outstanding,” one of the summoned elders whispered in amazement, eyes wide. “If someone at the advanced class can do this, imagine how much more powerful a skill like this would be in the hands of a Mythic.”

“Or Calamity,” Elder Kudrov added, shaking her head. “It would reshape the course of this war. It would change everything.”

Mirelle shuddered at the weight of her words. She wasn’t alone; the elders themselves stilled, minds racing as they considered the benefits of such a skill on the sect. On the war.

A beat of silence passed before an elder spoke. “We have to report this to the High Elders immediately. If we can apply this skill to everyone, the effects would be tremendous.”

The elders exchanged glances, a thousand words unspoken. Then a white-haired elder spoke. “I shall secure a meeting with the High Elders tonight—and inform them of this.”

The elders nodded in agreement. However, before any of them could speak, the white-haired elder's ring chimed with a notification. Moments later, his eyes widened.

“Elder Vasmos just gained a familiar!” he exclaimed, excitement lighting up his features. “Oh, what joyful news. I must go see her.”

“Can I come with you?” Mirelle blurted out before she could stop herself, and all the elders turned to look at her. She stuttered, “I… I’m very sorry, Elder Qarris, but if you will, I would like to come with you.”

Elder Qarris nodded. “Well then, the more the merrier. Come on—let’s find out how Vasmos did it.”

But Mirelle already knew how Vasmos had done it. What worried her was how soon she could do the same.
