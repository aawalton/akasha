Enlittlement Pt 2

*Yorin*

I take a long, deep breath, pulling the air fully into my lungs until my ribs protest, and I release it in a slow and composed fashion as I try not to think about one of the Five Heroes chosen by the Heavens to do battle against the Darkness sitting on the knee of the archer across the table from me in a children’s linen dress and sandals, cheerfully munching on a cookie as if nothing is the matter with the above situation.

And then I pour myself another cup of herbal tea for good measure.

“So allow me to go through this once more in assurance that I understand,” I say as I lift the warm mug up between my hands.

Remmi has actually made her own ceramic cups that have little loops on the side for holding to, but I still prefer to feel the heat radiating into my palms. Young Ayre had teased her again at their reveal for her supposed disdain of crafting, but I suspect these, at least, were nothing more than an appeal to familiarity, as one might reach for chopsticks in a land of spoons.

I focus on the issue of reduction sitting past the mug after a reinforcing pull of the hot tea. “You discovered a new spell created by the Essence System, personalized explicitly to appeal to you, complete with references to specific past instances where it would have been of direct benefit to you. Trusting fully in the System, you purchased the spell without hesitation. Is that the sum of things before today?”

The girl nods sharply. “That’s right! The System has never led me wrong when it comes up with a special offer like that, so I see no reason to change course now!”

I take this next step carefully, as, while my next question feels obvious, I am compelled to preface it. “Certainly, Remmi, I, of all people, should be encouraging that mentality. However, can you truly say this time has had no ill consequence?”

She frowns, and it makes her look even more like a precocious child in thought than it normally does. “Yes? I mean, it did exactly what it said it was going to do,” and then her face splits into that grin, “and I’ll never pay full price for dining out ever again!”

My brows furrow as I allow myself to frown. “You believe the appropriate use of this state is to defraud those people attempting to ease the financial burden upon young families?”

“Uhhhh, mmm …” It is obvious when she immediately begins to backtrack, but the way her eyes dart about tell me that it is not penance she seeks, but escape, and my eyes narrow further.

* * *

*Remmi*

Yorin throws my front door open hard enough to rattle the wall, and as she storms out onto the front deck, I have no choice but to follow her, on account of the firm twist she has on my ear that sure stings for somehow not doing any health point damage.

“Ah, Yorin,” I cry out with my neck bent at an angle chasing her brisk, angry pace, “if you keep pulling on my ear like that, people are going to start mistaking me for an elf!”

Ayre is trailing behind us, his hands knitting together and pulling apart again as he frets over what to do. I can’t say I blame him, it’s not like taking on the Sacred Priestess is high on my list of preferred ways to get re-isekai’d, either.

Yorin, however, ignores my pleas and seems to pay no mind to Ayre’s plight. “You spoke true, Remmi,” she pronounces as she drags me down the stairs and through the garden path toward the road. “This spell was a blessing from the heavens. It has given me a second opportunity to instruct you properly! If you so wish to be treated as a child, then I shall oblige you!”

“Where are we going?!”

“To the temple. Children’s Service will be starting at the hour, and we must get you donned appropriately.”

“Do you intend on dragging me by the ear the whole way?! It’s gonna come off by then!”

She finally comes to a stop, just at the beginning of the road into the village, and frowns down at me as I’m twisted around to look up at her while minimizing the torque on my ear.

“... If you try to run, know that no amount of boost provided to you will allow you to cross more than half a dozen paces, and then I will be quite cross that you broke my trust and attempted it.”

I swallow. “Yes, ma’am.”

Promise extracted and fear instilled, she releases my ear with a nod. “Then, during long walks, it is appropriate to hold your guardian's hand to ensure that you do not wander off.” And she holds hers out to me, her expression expecting obedience.

… Well, it's better than going by the ear …

In that manner, we proceed down the long hillside road into the village, making our way, the three of us, toward the temple. Ayre continues to look uncomfortable and out of place, but Yorin walks as if this is all just another casual, pleasant stroll home. Rather than give her the satisfaction of doing the same as Ayre, I strive to imitate her.

I’m quite used to being recognized by the villagers at this point. There's a very real possibility that I am the only blonde in the entire empire, so even if I wasn't also famous as the eccentric neighborhood Hero with the funny ideas and friendly helpfulness (or, at least, that's how I like to think they view me), I'd still be instantly recognizable in this little bunny village.

Sure enough, it's not just the sight of the head of the temple escorting an unknown child that's getting everyone's attention, and soon, murmurs loud enough to be understood start to reach us.

“Who's that child with Sacred Yorin?”

“... Wait, that hair, is that Hero Remmi?!”

“Oh, heavens, she's so small!”

“Did a dungeon curse her?”

“What dungeon could have done that, and when could she have gone?”

“I heard she can basically teleport anywhere she wants now. She could be clearing a dozen dungeons all over the empire, and we’d never see her leave!”

“Oh, who cares how it happened? She’s adorable!”

“I care how it happened. It’s the Fountain of Youth!”

“Backs it up a little too much for my tastes!”

“Aww, look, she’s blushing!”

“... Are we being too loud? Can she hear us?”

“I don’t care! I just want to pinch those little cheeks!”

If I can hear the villagers, then I know for a fact the elves with me can. Yet, while Ayre is casting his gaze about in every direction, Yorin remains unmoved. It could be normal afternoon market chatter, for all her response gives. She walks along with me in tow as if she did it every day.

Yorin comes to a polite stop when one of the merchants steps out to approach us. The woman's face is a contrasting mix of schooled worry and impulsive smiles every time she glances at me in my linen dress.

“Sacred Yorin!” The woman’s greeting has a convincing sound of forced positivity. “This … this is Hero Remmi, isn’t it? Has something happened to her? Is everything alright?”

“Miss Jera. Everything is fine.” Yorin’s tone is calm and steady, and her smile is gentle. “This is the Gunslinger Hero, yes, but she has not been harmed in any way. She simply encountered a new status, and, once we confirmed that it was not to be concerned over, she thought to take this sudden opportunity to experience Children’s Service at the Temple. As you are aware, she came to us from another land and missed the chance in her own childhood.”

“Ah, Children’s Service?” the woman repeats, and her gaze drifts slowly back to me, as if she is still trying to keep it from doing so. But then she seems to realize she's doing it, anyway, and all restraint evaporates as her face lights up in a broad smile, like the face people make when they see a stray kitten sitting in their flower pot and looking up at them without running away.

“Why, Remmi, you can't go to service dressed like that! The children have to wear their special smocks to the Temple!” Her tone is pulled up in pitch and almost singsong at first, but then she frowns and her tone saddens. “Oh, but I suppose you wouldn’t have one, would you?”

I get it, I look like a child, I know. But she seems to be having fun with it, so I guess that’s okay.

“She does not,” Yorin is quick to confirm. “It is why we are not heading directly to the Temple, we intended to purchase one.”

Jera wheels sharply back to the elf, her face leaping back into a bright smile. “Oh, there’s no need to buy one! If it is for an adorable little Remmi, I’m happy to give you one! Come in, come in! I was just about to close for the evening, but I’m not about to miss this!”

Yorin starts forward again, following Jera, who happens to be a clothier by trade, toward the doorway to her storefront, and with a tug on my hand, I come along, as well. “We cannot afford too much time to shop,” she warns. “We must still actually reach the Temple, and must do so at least a little early so that I may tend to my own preparations.”

“Oh, don’t worry, don’t worry,” Jera insists as she reaches up to the wall to turn the interior lamps back up to full brightness. “We won’t need to do anything custom,” and she actually reaches down and pinches my cheek, “more’s the pity!” She stands up straight again and makes her way toward the doorway past the counter. “I always keep a few smocks in stock for situations like this!”

She stops in her tracks as those words leave her lips, and she looks back at the two of us, Ayre having stayed outside, probably because I'd be changing clothes. “... Well, maybe not this situation exactly, but you understand. For when a child needs a new one in a hurry.”

Yorin gives a reassuring smile and nods, but just before she shuts the door behind us, I hear the other villagers start gossiping again. Instead of positive, however, I could swear they’re grousing about Jera getting to me first, like I’m some sort of doll they feel they have to fight over. I don’t know why; it’s not like any of them run a clothing store. Where else are we going to go?

In short order, my linen dress has been transferred to my storage, and in its place, I find myself wearing a simple, undyed cloth that I feel should be coarser than it is. Fortunately, it seems like the inside has been deliberately weathered to a down-like softness that feels nice against my skin. It’s almost completely a straight sheet of cloth, except for the inclusion of large, loose three-quarter sleeves. It then wraps around my body so one side is drawn over the other across my chest, and is then secured with a thin, red rope, the color I’ve come to associate with the Temple. Matching red thread embroiders the collar, but the outfit is otherwise completely unadorned.

I almost feel like I’m wearing the world’s thickest, baggiest cheongsam. The way the two sides cross over makes it a little more complex than the simple blue and gray robes we received at the Grand Temple, but not by much.

I look down at my sandals from the dress as Jera is taking it upon herself to pull my hair out of the outfit’s neck and arrange it in a presentable manner.

“Do I need special shoes, too?”

Yorin shakes her head. “You only wear your shoes to the Temple. As when you go to another’s house, you remove them as you enter. The children are barefoot on tatami mats during the service.”

“Oh, that makes sense,” I agree, then, once Jera finishes, I hop down to slip back into the flip-flops. “Thanks again, Miss Jera!” I would normally just call her Jera, but something about having to crane my neck to look up at her makes that feel rude.

She chuckles at the properness, but waves my gratitude off. “Getting to dress you up was payment enough, dear! You’re too precious! I told you, it’s a gift!”

“Well, where I’m from, I was always told to thank someone for a gift.”

“As you should,” Yorin interrupts. “And you have my thanks, as well, Miss Jera. But we must be away, before we are late.”

Jera waves goodbye to us as Yorin leads me back out the door once again.

Then it’s off to the temple, and it occurs to me that this will be my first time inside of it since it’s been built. Unlike the ones back on Earth, or at least the Christian ones, the worship area isn’t at the front of the complex. Instead, there’s a reception area that almost makes it feel like an onsen crossed with a doctor’s office. It makes a kind of sense, I guess. Tolestean temples offer a lot more services than Sunday service, counseling, and the occasional potluck. It’s part emergency room, part psychiatrist’s office, and part community center.

To my delight, when Yorin leads me to a room down a side hallway, I find myself standing amidst colorful walls adorned with simplistic depictions of scenes that are clearly religious, even if I, of course, have no reference for them.

And some of the village children are already here. Even Yorin’s eyes widen a little when they turn to see us and greet me by name.

“Is that Remmi?!”

“It’s Remmi! Remmi’s here!”

“Remmi, you’re small!”

I’m surrounded in moments and my hands grasp theirs as we laugh and spin.

“What are you doing here, Remmi?” one girl asks me. “Children’s Service is for kids!”

“Well, don’t I look like one right now?” I ask in return. “So I thought I’d come check it out! Is it fun?”

“Yeah,” a boy replies off to my side. “Sacred Yorin tells us stories and then asks us questions about them to make sure we understood them! And if we answer right, we get honey candy!”

I’m sure my eyes sparkle at that, but suddenly, Yorin’s hand clamps down on my arm and pulls me off to the side. It’s uncomfortably tight, if I’m being honest, but it’s the universal sign that I’m in trouble again.

“Remmi, why are you so familiar with the children?”

Oh, was that all the issue was? I give a big smile back to her. “Oh, I play with the kids all the time when I come into the village! They don’t give me nearly all the stuffy attention the adults do, and they teach me about all of the interesting parts of town!”

That drives Yorin to lean back, taking a long, deep breath to assert control over herself once more, then she leans back down to me again. “I remind you that you are here as educational discipline, not to play with friends! You will not wind the children up. You will sit through the lesson, you will listen, you will accurately answer any questions asked of you, and you will not be receiving any candy.”

I’m not pouting. I’m not puffing out one cheek and jutting out my lips or averting my eyes to the side, and I’m certainly not doing it over the denial of candy. “That’s fine,” I insist. “I can always make my own later.”

“Good,” she confirms with a sharp nod. “And after service is over and we return home, if you have behaved yourself, I will undo your transformation for you, as I doubt Purge will suffice. If you do not do as I have told you, you will go to bed early, as you are, and be awakened in the morning to assist me with chores. This will continue until your behavior improves, am I clear?”

“Yes, ma’am.”

“Good.” And she releases me. “Go sit down.” And Yorin’s motherly smile is back on her face as she moves to the front of the room once more, spreading the instruction to the children. “Everyone, gather around. Let us begin.”

Needless to say, I intend to do exactly as she says.
