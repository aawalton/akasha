The Fae lunged, but not at me.

Kallian’s bloodshot eyes swept to a man near the middle of the table. They widened. Then he launched forward with a guttural cry.

They were all ready. The intended victim scowled, seemingly more annoyed than anything else, as five other forms blurred to intercept the charge.

Kallian roared and screamed. Foam spattered his lips as he tried to claw past. Though outnumbered, the frenzied force behind each movement was enough to make it an almost-even fight. One slashing hand came away slick and wet. Then he twisted free of restraining fists and bore one of his opponents down, slamming him to the glass with a heart-stopping crack.

Panic sent me bolting upright, only to be immediately pulled back down by an unconcerned Velidan.

“That was a rib, lamb, not the floor,” Embriel sniggered, spearing a radish. “Just enjoy the show.”

I gaped at him, then turned wide-eyed to the figure beside me.

Velidan watched the brawl as calmly as if we were watching a card game. Then, almost absently, he murmured, “Most creatures woke up, Brin. Not all.”

The implication behind that turned me cold. The sensation only worsened when I realized the raw screams sounded furious and fearful in equal measure.

Kallian’s opponents corralled him further back in the cavern, grunting at him to ‘snap out of it,’ while he raged and railed.

Alora stormed to Embriel and drew him up by his vest. Her face was white. “And what of him, then? What of the other Fae who still fall to madness? What harmonious,” she spat the word, and clenched one fist as if she wanted to strike him, “role will they play in your new realm?”

Saffine, watching from across the table, choked on her drink. She wiped her chin while covering a grin.

“They’ll have a place, as all things do.” Embriel disentangled himself, looking up with an expression of utter sincerity. “I’ve thought of everything, Alora. I’ll swear it on my life; after today, I won’t let him harm a soul. I’ll carve a vow out in my own blood, if you like.”

She searched his face while her own clouded with confusion. “After today? What in the blazes does that m-”

“I’m calm!” A ragged snarl tore from the far end of the cavern. Kallian shoved away from his opponents, chest heaving and blood-streaked hands swiping through his already-tangled hair. “Back off!”

Wary, quiet voices murmured at him.

“I am. Spare me the pity.” He bared his teeth and shoved the nearest Fae away, then stalked towards us. “What’s the meaning of this? Why are you all meeting without me?”

“Kallian,” Embriel’s voice sank to a coaxing, honey-smooth pitch, “please, no offense was meant. I sent a messenger about today. I’m sorry if they didn’t reach you.”

“I’m sure you are.” Kallian reached the table as Alora took up her position between him and Bernard.

The human man sat with his head bowed and both hands clasped in his lap. Shay and Sera seemed similarly deferent; lashes lowered and lips pressed together. Both women were leaning against their broad-shouldered Fae. Shay had one dainty hand curled over his forearm, and Sera kept nuzzling her head against him.

My gaze drifted to Liana. She alone had her chin up, and had watched everything with obvious curiosity. She hadn’t shown a shred of fear at the outburst. Even when Kallian stomped nearby, passing just behind her chair, she didn’t flinch.

Embriel continued, “How about instead of you breaking more bones, I make it up to you?”

Bloodshot eyes narrowed. “And how do you intend to do that?”

The bronze-haired Fae stood. His voice rose as he addressed the room. “I’ve got a busy day planned, and of course some of you have already been invited for the fun; some bigger predators are due for some blood sport, and I’ve a few things that need to be culled.”

A chorus of responses rang out; disgusted snarls, delighted laughter, and everything in-between.

“Join me on my dais,” Embriel continued smoothly, moving towards Kallian with his hands outstretched. “You’ll get the best view, and I’ll have anything you like brought up. Dawn, everything you like.” He winked.

They went back and forth, Kallian’s growls met by inerrant calm, as more voices joined in. I watched it all, trying to puzzle out what any of this had to do with attacking a human city, until a tiny whine drew my attention.

One of the little gray Fae stood where smooth stone transitioned to the jade glow of water. It was staring at its fallen kin with glassy eyes. The creatures trembled a bit, let out another whine, then crept back into the hallway.

Conversation buzzed around me as I watched the unmoving form. The air thinned, suddenly not enough to fill my lungs.

I waited for it to get up. It did not. I waited for it to move, twitch, anything. Nothing. I waited for someone to move towards it, to help it, to see if it wasn’t too late.

No one did.

And when I rose to try, an unbreakable grip pulled me back down. A few heartbeats later the whimpering creature reappeared, this time with another, slightly larger Fae beside it. The large one stared for a moment, then gathered up its limp kin and carried it away.

“This is as fine a note as any to end on,” Embriel announced, one arm around the mollified newcomer’s shoulders. “As I said, I didn’t hope to sway all of your minds today. All I ask is that you consider everything I’ve said.”

“Enjoy your blood sport,” Alora bit out. “And that’s what any attempt at coexistence with a terrified mortal realm will become. We all know it.”

“Harmony, Alora,” Embriel crooned. He ran his thumb along his lower lip, then moved lightning-fast to stand before her. He stooped, gathering Bernard’s cane, and held it out. “That’s all I want. You and dear Bernard achieved it so easily.”

The man rose, grunting and teetering as he accepted his cane. Embriel steadied him with hands that could snap bone while Alora stood rigid.

Her side of the table, along with several Fae from the middle, began to disperse. Shay and Sera hurried forward to clasp my hands and whisper goodbyes. Bernard gave me a tight-lipped smile as Alora seized Velidan next, hauled him to the archway, and spent some time speaking in a quick, low voice. She gestured towards me several times. Whatever their conversation, it ended with her looking incensed and him calmly walking away.

And a short while later, I sat still and cold as half the Fae left. The rest convened around Embriel.

I watched them, gnawing my lower lip. All this, and I haven’t learned a thing. Well, not anything helpful; I suppose I’ve learned he’s even more of a monster than I thought. Saints, how can a person lie through their teeth like that? How can he laugh about people being slaughtered, then turn and pretend to mourn in front of an audience?

“Don’t worry.” Liana’s soft voice made me jump. She stepped near, offering me a small smile. “I’ll stay with you.”

I glanced over. The remaining Fae- a group of ten men and Saffine- were finishing the last of their drinks. Embriel offered Kallian a full cup. The bedraggled man took it with a waspish ‘you’ll have to do a lot better than this,’ then downed the whole thing in one gulp.

“I asked my lord Aravis if I could hold your hand when we go. To help you not be frightened.” As if to demonstrate, she drew me up and kept her fingers tangled in mine. “He said that was very sweet of me.”

I searched her doe-like eyes. A thousand questions hovered on my tongue, but we were called away once more.

We followed the group back into the outdoor cavern. Several of them shoved and jostled each other, giving rowdy whoops or dark laughs at comments I was too far back to overhear.

Two more gray-skinned Fae waited at the rune. One held a knife, and at its master’s request carved a crimson line into its own arm. Blood spattered to the stones and Embriel’s murmur of “Pit, ground floor,” brought a flare of blue light.

Velidan’s shoulders stiffened. “The bottom? Why?”

Embriel chuckled as, one at a time, his guests stepped through. “Don’t fuss, Vel. You won’t get too dirty.”

His lip curled. “I’m more worried about the stench.”

The light dimmed. The little Fae winced, lifted its knife, and began the process anew.

“Of course you are. But that’s where,” one burnished eyes winked, “the first bit of fun is.”

Velidan’s displeasure was evident, but he said nothing else; instead he gave a little growl, grabbed my wrist, and hauled me through the light.

Straight into a nightmare. Sin-black stone curved above and around, broken in one place by a large gaping hole. Frosty, still air pebbled my skin and flooded my nose with sharp iron, stale musk, and the pungent smell of urine.

And in the distance… shouting. Groaning. Hissing. Growling. Weeping. All of it very muffled and faint, as if carried to us by echoes upon echoes.

“H-Henry?”

I whipped my head towards Liana as she appeared beside me. She was white-faced, with both hands pressed to her mouth.

“Who?” I gasped.

“Henry!” Her voice broke. “S-saints, did he make-”

“Shh, you’re safe.” Aravis stepped from the glow and drew her close, voice satin-smooth and one large hand clasping her jaw. He tilted up until her panicked eyes met his. Then, in a tone so viscerally familiar it sent ice tearing through my insides, “I have you. I’ll keep you safe. Be calm, sweetheart. Be content. Don’t think of anything painful.”

Her jaw slackened. Her breathing evened out. Then she sank against him, lashes fluttering and one cheek nuzzling his palm.

He kissed her forehead, then followed the other Fae through the opening.

Liana turned a dazed, placid little smile towards me. “Oh, you look frightened again. It’s alright. Lord Aravis will keep us safe.”

“No.” One foot dragged back. “No.” My lungs burned on the stench of choking air. Chains rattled in the distance. Something wailed. My head swayed from side to side because no, she couldn’t be glamoured and of course I must have misunderstood. That couldn’t be what was going on, it was too sick, too cruel, too evil, how could-

Iron curled around my waist as my knees buckled. The world dimmed to numb, fuzzy gray. When it returned I was upright, though not by my own strength.

“Go away,” Velidan ordered the blinking young woman. Once we were alone, he breathed in my ear, “Fainting is also unacceptable.”

The world blurred. “She’s-”

For the third time in a single day, he pressed a palm over my mouth. “Not your concern.”

No words had ever been so wrong. I shook my head, trying to scream out a thousand denials, but he smothered them all.

“You lot go on up,” Embriel’s smooth drawl echoed from the chamber ahead. “There’s drinks and an attendant on every dais; order whatever you like. Saffine, lovely, make sure Kallian’s comfortable.”

She giggled.

“And what of you?” The ragged voice growled back.

Embriel’s oily laughter snaked through the air. “Oh, the rest of us will be down here for a bit. We’re going to chat with some Thalessians.”
