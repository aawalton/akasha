Xavier contacted his old party, bringing the three of them to the house he and Adranial had built together on the mountaintop. He also contacted two more people—well, one person and a dragon.

Howard, Justin, Siobhan, Romalda, and Volkarin showed up within seconds of each other.

“What is it?” Howard’s tone was serious.

“I’m leaving again,” Xavier said.

“You’re not going after Jhanku already?” Justin asked. He waved a finger behind Xavier. “Also, when are we going to talk about this?” He looked at the others. “This wasn’t here before, was it?”

Xavier smiled. “That my and Adranial’s new home. We, uh, designed it together.”

“Your home? Does that mean—” Justin raised his eyebrows up and down. “Are the two of you…?”

“Justin, don’t be a pain,” Siobhan chided, then brightened. “Can we have a tour?”

“Something tells me that’s not the reason he called us here. Is it, Xavier? You said you’re leaving,” Howard said.

“There are things I need to do before I go after Jhanku. And with Jhanku setting his sights on me, that means everyone in Silver River and Ventorin will be in danger. With me gone, I need the five of you to be able to pull off what I did with the World Destroyer.”

Justin’s eyes widened. Siobhan’s mouth fell open. Howard just stared.

Romalda cackled.

Volkarin released a puff of smoke from his nostrils. “You mean to share with us that power?”

“I trust you all.” Xavier’s gaze rested on Romalda a moment. “Even you, Romalda.” He shrugged, smirking. “Well enough, at least. The last thing I want to do is leave my empire vulnerable, and I’m trusting the five of you to be its true defence.”

I don’t want to leave any of you, or your families, vulnerable.

“What about Adranial?” Siobhan asked.

Xavier raised his chin. “I’ll train her up for this too, but she won’t be staying behind.” He smiled. “We’ll be returning to the Tower of Champions together.”

Xavier wasn’t sure he would be taking her with him to many of the floors—there was too much he needed to do, and the rate at which he needed to progress… he hated to say it, but he knew that having anyone else along with him, even Adranial, would simply slow him down.

He’d spent a long enough interlude on Earth as it was, especially since he knew that Jhanku had access to time mages.

Xavier had advantages other time mages didn’t—he didn’t have to worry about spell cooldowns, for one, something that would stop any other society from taking advantage of time dilation fields in the way that his empire had—but that didn’t mean he could take it for granted and slow down too much.

He needed to get moving.

Xavier didn’t train the others how to cultivate refined Celestial Energy. That, like the ability to peer through his Immortal Soul Lens and control Celestial Energy outside himself with his Living Soul, wasn’t something he wanted falling into the hands of the enemy.

For some time, he’d intended to share his powers with everyone under his rule, and to an extent he’d done that—everyone in the sectors he ruled possessed a Reality Core, sending them down another path of advancement—and they possessed the ability to create spell patterns, too.

But it was only those closest to him who he’d taught how to manifest spell patterns inside their body.

And he didn’t teach them the other abilities he’d picked up.

Xavier felt as though he was betraying some of his ideals by not sharing all his powers, but there was no denying that he needed an edge over the more powerful A Grades of the inner sector. An edge he couldn’t let leak out to them.

A Grade? Am I even sure that’s all they are?

He’d heard more than one whisper over the years about there being a level of advancement beyond A Grade. S Grade had been referenced, but only ever speculatively—not even Roln had known what the highest advancement in this universe was, but only because it hadn’t been something he’d needed to know.

But even though he wasn’t willing to train his friends and allies in these abilities, there was a lot he was willing to share with them. The two Defender spells were things he could take advantage of already.

And the sharing of energy? All he needed to do for them was teach them was how to create a portal to every world, and how to use a few spells: Energy Funnel and Energy Transference.

That, and strengthen their bodies with refined Celestial Energy.

It was this last task that would take the longest.

To take full advantage of the powers at his disposal, Xavier activated Territory Defence and Unity Defence, and he had those spells active for the entire time he trained his people. This allowed him to make the time dilation field they trained within even more powerful than before.

More powerful than the A Grade time mage’s time dilation field back in Arrack’s base. Much more.

Being inside his territory truly was an incredible advantage, and the rest of the universe knew that. Something told him their plans would consist of taunting him to leave his empire or trying to attack him while he was expanding it—that would be when he was most vulnerable.

Several years passed inside Xavier’s strongest time dilation field as he farmed refined Celestial Energy from his drones. The process still wasn’t something he was willing to do inside his own body, not when he hadn’t perfected it inside the drones—he could pull more and more refined Celestial Energy from the drones he could create than he could originally, but the problem was, they always died in the end.

This was a problem he’d yet to overcome, and until he did there was no way he was going to lift the restriction on his own soul the System had put in place when he’d been integrated.

As he collected all of this refined Celestial Energy, he didn’t only give it to Howard, Siobhan, Justin, Romalda, Volkarin, and Adranial—he gave it to himself.

Instead of focusing on a single spot to enhance, as he’d done in the past, and as he was doing for the others, he bonded the refined Celestial Energy to every molecule in his entire body and brain.

The changes were subtle at first but became more and more pronounced as the time passed.

The refined Celestial Energy was essentially what Denizens gained when they gained levels—it was what Mastery Points ended up giving them—and yet no matter how much he put inside himself, the “Mastery Points” counter for his next level didn’t move.

Not only that, he never gained a single level.

Yet he did gain stats.

Xavier’s attribute points were boosted the more his body and mind became stronger, yet not once did any of this improvement—an improvement that was to his base attribute points—lead to an advancement to B Grade. He knew his natural body had already been as strong as a weak A Grade, and the longer he spent bonding more and more of the refined Celestial Energy to his molecules, the stronger it became, making him sure that at the end of those few years he would have been able to defeat Arrack even without a time dilation field at his disposal.

Yet he hadn’t gained a single level.

This is how they used to gain power, before the System. But I guess it isn’t my path to B Grade.

It made him think of it as a kind of third path of advancement—something altogether different than his Denizen and Cultivator paths.

On the last week, when he’d decided that the others were almost strong enough to handle the energy from the two sectors he ruled, he’d hit a roadblock.

He could create more refined Celestial Energy, but it was no longer bonding to his molecules.

“What’s happened?” Adranial asked, able to sense the dismay in Xavier without him having spoken a word. They were sitting on the snow—all of them—each deep in meditation, reliving the memories of how Xavier had wielded the energy from two sectors in two galaxies to defeat the World Destroyer, as Xavier had been bonding refined Celestial Energy through the use of his Immortal Soul lens to all of their molecules, when Xavier had let out small gasp and opened his eyes.

“I’ve… hit a limit.” Xavier’s forehead creased. “My body won’t take any more refined Celestial Energy.” His frown deepened, head dropping as he looked at the snow in front of him.

Volkarin growled low in his throat. “You sound… disappointed. Did you think such an ability would go on strengthening you forever?”

“Well…”

“You did think that!” Justin said. “Really?”

Xavier ran a hand through his hair. “It’s only… I suppose I didn’t know there would be a limit.”

“Of course there’s a limit,” Adranial said.

Xavier looked at her. “Why?”

“Well…” She faltered for a moment.

“Because it would be too easy, otherwise,” Siobhan said. “You’ve said yourself, this ability of yours… it must be how those before the System advanced their powers. It sounds as though to do that—and to create the System in the first place—they must have been far more in tune with Celestial Energy than most beings are today. Yet it hadn’t been enough for them to save the universe in the end, had it?”

“They can’t wield as much power as the ultimate threat,” Howard said.

The Interdimensional Dragon.

Xavier vividly remembered the revelation of discovering what it was he would be fighting against at the end of his universe—a dragon bigger than an entire fricken galaxy, and he’d seen a vision of it from billions of years ago.

The entity was even stronger now.

“I thought the limit would be energy consumption,” Xavier muttered.

“You thought you could just keep bringing in energy and continue to get more and more powerful and never stop?” Volkarin asked.

Xavier nodded slowly, feeling a little silly for thinking in this way. He’d discovered some completely wild abilities, things that made him incredibly unique—unique enough that powerful beings from outside his corner of the multiverse had taken notice, not because they’d never been done out in the multiverse, but because no one had ever done them at his stage.

He supposed he was beginning to think exactly that—that there was no limit to how powerful he could become.

In a way, he still believed that, but he supposed it made sense that he couldn’t keep pushing his power farther and farther with this new ability without hitting a plateau.

The others were right, if that was possible, then someone would have done it by now, and they would have used that power to take down the Interdimensional Dragon.

But isn’t that exactly how the Interdimensional Dragon has become so strong in the first place? By continuing to absorb more and more power? Consuming everything, worlds, galaxies, universes, in its path? I haven’t consumed energy from a single world…

So what’s stopping me from pushing forward?

He didn’t know, but he doubted he was going to find the answer through deep thought alone.

Several days after he’d hit that limit—a limit he kept trying to push through despite it feeling impossible—he was standing outside his and Adranial’s new house, saying his goodbyes to the others once more. Adranial wrapped an arm around his, leaning her head against his shoulder as their friends disappeared through different portals, heading back to their lives, a new weight of responsibility on their shoulders.

“Do you think it will be enough?” Xavier asked.

“I think it has to be,” Adranial replied. “You can’t be afraid to leave. That’s how rulers stagnate. You must step out from your empire to improve, to face new challenges. That’s how you will be able to protect your people the most. The Tower of Champions is the perfect place to do that.”

Xavier sighed but didn’t argue.

Truth be told, he was excited to get back to the tower. Giddy, even.

It was a feeling he wanted to hold into.

The notification, called up by his will, hovered in front of him.

Do you wish to return to the Tower of Champions?

“Are you ready?” he whispered to Adranial.

She held him tighter. “Ready.”
