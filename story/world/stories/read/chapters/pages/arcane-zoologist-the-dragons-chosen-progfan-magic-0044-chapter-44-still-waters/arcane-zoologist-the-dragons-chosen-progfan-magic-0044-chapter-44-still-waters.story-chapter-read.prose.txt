Callen hated this. He hated to be a burden to his family. He would be turning sixteen soon, an adult by any standards, yet he was still fully reliant on his parents for even something as simple as a winter coat.

But Callen refused to just sit back and do nothing. So he went to the one man who could provide assistance. Silas.

"A winter coat, you say?" The man drawled out as he blew on the hot stew in the spoon. Callen had caught him during lunchtime, which made him feel bad. The merchant was already busy up to his ears with the sudden arrival of winter.

"Yes! I'm ready to work for it! Whatever you need, I'll do it," Callen said, bowing before the man.

Silas put the stew in his mouth, chewing it thoughtfully as if considering whether helping Callen would be worth the trouble.

"I do have an old coat of mine that I was thinking of selling," Silas said.

"I… I don't have any money," Callen admitted, his head low. "Please, let me work for it."

"Work for it, you say. It would be easier to sell it, though. With winter coming as early as it is this year, many would pay quite a bit of coin for it."

Callen clenched his fists. Yeah, it would be much more profitable for the merchant to just sell it. He couldn't hold it against Silas for putting his own livelihood first.

"But… I do like you. You show real promise as a merchant, so I'm going to make you an offer," Silas said.

Callen perked up. "What? Whatever you need!"

"Because of the sudden cold, I have been busy at my stall the whole day. I've never had a chance to get into the forest to gather a tiny little something that a wealthy client from Drymon requested. If I could get it, then it would easily make up for the price of the coat."

"What is it?"

"Swamp lantern mushrooms," Silas said.

Callen blinked. Those wouldn't be easy to get. As the name suggested, the mushrooms grew only in swampy places, filled with water. Their value is known to all, but because they are so difficult to gather, most people just don't risk it.

In fact, it is nearly impossible for an adult to collect these mushrooms. They grow in the tiniest of crevices. The only way to reach them would be with the help of small hands. Like Callie's, for example.

Together with his little sister, Callen had no doubt that he could collect these mushrooms. He would keep her safe while she would just have to reach into the crevices and pluck the mushrooms.

"I think I can do it!" Callen said firmly.

"Excellent! You do need to hurry, though. My client isn't going to wait forever. Two days is the most I can give you. I'll have to head into town and restock by then anyway," Silas warned.

Callen nodded and ran back home. With this, the issue with his coat would be solved. All he had to do was gather those mushrooms.

"Swamp lantern mushrooms? Absolutely not!" Dad said fiercely.

Callie watched her brother deflate on the spot.

"But—"

"No buts! Only Callie would be able to reach into the narrow crevices where they grow, and the swamp at the current time of year is far too dangerous!" Dad said firmly.

Callie didn't know much about the swamp lantern mushrooms. She'd seen a few adults boasting about finding a few, and apparently, they were worth quite a bit of money. But she had never actually found them herself.

"Dad, it's just a few mushrooms, and then I'll get a coat in return. I'm obviously going with Callie and making sure she remains safe," Callen said.

"No, Callen! It is far safer for us to sell the frostmoth cocoons," Mom shook her head.

"But the frostmoths will die!" Callie protested. "We would need to gather a lot to get enough money for a coat. Hundreds!"

Mom hesitated. "It… is much safer that way!"

"Safer doesn't mean better. Those are live creatures you are killing for no good reason. Their cocoons are just valuable after they emerge. Killing them for them is pointless and cruel!" Callie protested, feeling magic bubbling within her in response to her emotions.

"Gathering swamp lantern mushrooms is too dangerous! If the two of you go together, there is no guarantee you will come back safely. There's a thin layer of ice in the swamp that would make it even more difficult to traverse," Dad resisted. "No, we are collecting the cocoons, and that is final!"

Callie stared at her Dad angrily, her frustration bubbling within her. "Why are you so cruel?!"

The heat within her rose, and she felt that her magic would burst out. Oh no! This was dangerous! Callie clutched her chest, trying to keep it contained within her, but it was too much. It was threatening to burst out of her again, and this time it was unlikely to turn into a field of flowers.

"Callie?" Mom noticed the change and came up to Callie, rubbing at her back gently. "Are you alright?"

"Th-The magic… I can't contain it…" Callie whispered.

Callen rushed up to her as well. "Just take a deep breath. Calm down."

Callie tried to, but all she could think about were the poor frost moths. "We can't kill those frost moths!"

Dad looked taken aback by her firm stance, and he also seemed worried, crouching down in front of Callie. "I just don't want you to get hurt… or worse…"

Callie looked at her Dad, doing her best to keep her raging magic contained. "But is it right for us to kill the moths when there is another way available? If we search for mushrooms, then nobody will get hurt."

Dad clenched his fists and pursed his lips. It didn't look like he would agree.

"Dear, why don't we let them try…" Mom said, still rubbing Callie's back. "You see how important it is to Callie to protect arcane beasts. We should leave the cocoons as a last resort."

Dad got up and growled, then whirled around, pointing his finger at Callen. "You must guard your sister. Proceed with utmost caution, and should you feel that the place isn't safe, you must turn back. And you will only get one chance. If you can't find those mushrooms, that's it!"

"I would never do anything to endanger Callie. If it's too much, I'll turn back immediately," Callen confirmed.

The magic raging within Callie began to calm. She took a deep breath and let it slowly calm down. Mom and Callen rubbing at her back actually helped to soothe her.

"I'm sorry… I nearly lost control again…" Callie muttered.

"It's okay. You managed to get it under control," Mom soothed, although her eyebrows were still creased with worry.

"We really need to get it under control," Dad grumbled. "I don't want one day to find the house on fire."

Callie slumped. If she had lost control, would she have set their own home on fire? The feeling was certainly similar to the one she had when using fire magic—anger ruling over her.

Maybe we could also search for the dragon while we are out.

She didn't dare to voice this thought aloud. Mom and Dad would surely disapprove, but Callie felt she needed the dragon's help. She didn't understand her magic and thus was forbidden from studying even the frost caterpillars.

But they were literally everywhere. Callie wanted to learn more about them. How could these little guys that everyone believed to be just another bug be an arcane beast? Or was it better to call them arcane bugs?

"Callen, you must make sure Callie doesn't get distracted," Mom said, as if sensing Callie's thoughts. "Don't cave into Callie's whims just because she made puppy eyes at you."

"You can trust me, Mom!" Callen said dutifully. "Now let's go search for those mushrooms. Silas needs them within the next two days."

"Will you be alright without a coat, brother?" Callie asked.

"Take mine for the time being," Dad offered. "I'll be working in the forge the whole day, so I'll hardly need it."

As Dad passed the coat to Callen, he whispered something to him with a stern expression. Callie couldn't hear what it was, but judging that Dad had glanced in Callie's direction, it was likely something along the lines of "protect your little sister".

She wasn't so small and stupid that she would just step into a mire. Besides, Callie had learned how to traverse a swamp properly through Master Blackthorn's lessons. She wasn't as clueless as she was at the start of the summer.

Callie took her own new coat, which felt a little too large for her, but at the very least, it was warm. Callen put on Dad's coat, which, while it was large on him, actually wasn't quite as large as Callie had expected. When had her brother grown so big? He was basically an adult now.

"Let's go, Callie!" Callen said, grasping her hand.

"Be careful!" Mom called after them as they headed in the direction of the Deep Forest.

"We're not actually going into the Deep Forest, are we?" Callie asked nervously.

"No, of course not. There's a swamp not too far from that dragon cave of yours. We're going to look there," Callen explained.

"Really? I never knew," Callie noted.

"It's quite a distance away. Mom and Dad likely didn't want you going in so deep."

Callie nodded. That made sense. Granny Ethel didn't send her far for herbs either. Then again, there wasn't much need to. A lot of the herbs they got in the flower meadow.

She hoped that Granny Ethel managed to gather some herbs before the winter. With how busy Callie had been with winter preparations, she didn't even have a chance to help her.

Tristan likely didn't have time either. She hadn't seen him much during these last three weeks, but she had caught glimpses of the boy running through his yard, carrying hay or feed for animals. His family was likely busy, much like everyone else.

Walking through the forest, Callie could see that it wasn't ready for the early winter either. Some trees still had plenty of colorful but frozen leaves hanging onto their branches. Blueberry bushes had a few frozen berries, and as a whole, it looked like the land had frozen too soon.

"Why do you think the winter came so early?" Callie asked her brother as they walked deeper into the forest.

"I don't know. But it is certainly strange. I just hope it ends as quickly as it came."

Callie rather doubted that, and judging by the serious expression her brother had, Callen did too. But the best they could do for now was gather those mushrooms, so Callen could have a proper coat for the winter.

They had to walk quite a distance before they finally reached the edge of the swamp. When they passed by the dragon's cave, Callie couldn't help but throw a quick look inside. But the dragon wasn't there. Not a single trace, scale, or anything.

The swamp itself was frozen. But it wasn't yet frozen enough to hold their weight. It would still be a while before the swamp would be completely safe to traverse. But currently, they would have proceeded with utmost caution, sticking close to trees.

Callen got a very long and sturdy stick, which he used to feel the ground in front of them. Very often, the stick would sink very deep, and they would have to choose a different path. Sometimes Callen would have to hoist Callen over some mire or carry her on his back, where the path was too narrow.

The pace was slow, and Callie saw no sight of the mushrooms. Maybe it was too late already, and they had all died with the arrival of the cold. Or perhaps the swamp creatures had already eaten them. Were they safe to eat?

"Brother, what are swamp lantern mushrooms even used for?" Callie asked as Callen carried her over yet another narrow path.

"They glow in the dark, apparently, so apparently rich folk like to grind it up and use it as decoration? Or something like that," Callen said.

That seemed like an utter waste of money in Callie's opinion. Why spend your money purely on something that just… looks pretty? Shouldn't they be spending it on something actually useful for winter? Callie felt she would never understand such wasteful people.

But if finding these mushrooms meant that Callen would get his coat, then so be it.

"Wait, I think I see something over there!" Callie said, noticing a slight glow at the side of the path ahead. It seemed to be coming from a hole in the ground.

"That must be it!" Callen said, excitedly. "Hold on!"

Callen kept walking slowly, feeling out the swamp. There was a path leading to the mushroom. Callen stopped right before it.

"I don't think it can hold both of our weight…" he muttered.

"Should I go alone?" Callie suggested. It looked large enough for her.

"No, it's too dangerous. What if you fall in?" Callen grumbled.

"Do you think I am not able to walk at all?" Callie huffed. "Give me that stick and stay here. I'm going to get that mushroom."

"Callie…" Callen growled, holding her tightly and not letting her slip off his back. "No, we'll keep searching."

"We don't know if there are even any other mushrooms here. Please, trust me a little, brother. If it feels like it can't hold my weight, I'll come back," Callie said firmly.

Callen hesitated, but after a moment, let Callie down. He took a rope that he had secured to his belt and tied it firmly around Callie's waist. "There! If you fall in, I can pull you out."

Callie nodded. She felt safer this way, so with the stick in hand, Callie proceeded forward on the narrow path.

The surrounding ice broke as she moved forward. It felt as if she stepped a bit too much to the left or right; she would fall in. But the stick was helping her keep her balance, so she kept moving forward.

At this pace, she soon reached the shining hole she had noticed before. Peeking inside, she saw several glowing mushrooms growing inside.

"I found them!" Callie cheered and reached in to grab the mushrooms. There was a small cluster of them, so Callie put them into the satchel tied to her belt.

Do not trust the still waters.

A low, barely audible voice resounded in her mind. Callie looked up and hurriedly looked around herself. What, or rather who, was that?
