After a fierce two-handed swing of the Razor, the glass now dark with demon blood, Ethan simply let his exhausted body collapse. The few brief seconds as he fell were a welcome break, and splashing into a foot of water and muck was relaxing after hours of brutal combat. Prime kindly sealed a dark helm over Ethan’s face to keep out the filth and rain, and Ethan just lay there gratefully for a few moments, pieces of gigas still raining down around him.

“Ethan, are you alright?” Selina asked. At some point she’d joined Apollo in helping filter through the undead rushing through the gates. “I could see you a minute ago, but everything just went dark.”

Ethan hauled himself into a sitting position. Breaktime over.

“I’m fine, Selina. Just took down the gigas for the fourth time. Any luck on your end?”

“We still haven’t found the true undead,” she said. Since Ethan was trapped in a purgatory of perpetually healing undead, Selina had volunteered to focus on searching for the undead in charge of this army.

“It’s probably in the hills, or the forests, watching from where it’s safe,” Ethan said with some envy. He’d been forced to focus on the gigas personally, giving Revan free reign to rush where he may, keeping down the smaller undead with endless flame. Ethan could handle the giant on his own, but it was difficult, and his powers didn’t serve him particularly well. Even [Internal Combustion] refused to layer properly, and he suspected either the gigas itself, or the undead ruling it had given it some kind of impressive fire resistance.

That meant cutting it apart, piece by piece, over and over. “We need a better plan,” he muttered.

Ethan looked around at the chaos the battlefield had become. There was no telling undead from demons anymore, as the muddy plains where they’d stationed the army had been completely consumed by the battle. Everything and everyone was covered in blood, and muck, lit only by spotlights from the Fable Train, and constant flashes of lightning.

As he surveyed the area, it looked to Ethan like the landscape itself was alive, quivering and undulating constantly.

Again, he found himself asking a single question. “How do the demons possibly survive this? Even if they just ran away, the undead forces would just keep growing. Is there some kind of immunity, or antibody? Something they’d be able to do if we weren’t controlling them?”

“We’re running out of time, Ethan,” Apollo said from his communication rune.

“How bad is it?” Ethan asked.

“Soon this side will look like that side,” Apollo said. “More and more undead are finding ways through, we’re past putting them down cleanly. At this point we’re always exterminating a section of our forces.”

“We can’t take much more of this,” Ethan agreed. “Tomo said the storms can last weeks, so we can’t just hold out, and running right now would be suicide.”

He noticed the gigas’s corpse beginning to quiver, as the unseen master of the undead army was focusing on it once again. Ethan retrieved the Razor from the mud at his feet, and began hacking at the pieces like an overworked lumberjack.

“I’m open to ideas,” Ethan said.

“Well…” Selina began, but clearly it wasn’t something she wanted to do.

“No bad ideas in brainstorming,” Fable said brightly.

“Thank you Fable,” Selina said indulgently. “It’s just…this is a bad idea. We can just unleash Fable entirely. Your spirit is so bloated with energy that it could fuel the defenses at full blast for days. Things are going this badly because we’re still trying to save as much of our army as possible. We could…we could stop doing that. Destroy everything on your side that moves.”

No one said anything, but Ethan knew she was right. They’d been avoiding this plan for some time. The train could unleash dimensional beams, which annihilated nearly anything they were pointed at. Extraordinarily mana-intensive, when not used for short, controlled bursts at least, but Selina was right about his spirit as well.

Though, he sure as hell hadn’t been accumulating all this power just to turn it on his own forces.

“Damn it,” he cursed. “I really don’t want to do this. We’ve spent so much time and effort building this force. Just destroying them all…we weren’t even okay with letting the stronger ones eat the weak,” he said, then his eyes nearly bulged out of his head in realization.

“You’re right,” Selina said, “I’d really rather we find a better solution. Maybe–”

“Selina!” Ethan interrupted. “Everything here eats everything else!”

There was a pause. “Yes?” the Runemistress replied.

“I’ve been an idiot! I’ve been thinking about this like a doctor, looking for immunities, or environmental factors, but it’s so obvious! Selina, what would the demons do if we weren’t here, and they killed an undead?”

Another pause. “They’d…well they’d probably eat it? I suppose you’re right, everything eats everything. Why do you–oh. Oh! Yes, you’re right! This is so unbelievably disgusting, but you’re right!”

“You want our army to eat the undead?” Apollo asked.

“Think about it, what else could explain the undead not ruling Potentia at this point? If everything just ran from them, or fought them endlessly while they regenerated–like we’ve been doing–what then? The undead would eventually turn them all!”

“But if they encountered a stronger demon, or a herd of stronger demons,” Apollo said, “their forces would be eaten in turn. No different from any other creatures on this horrifying planet.”

“Exactly. We just need to let these vicious little bastards do what comes naturally to them. And then probably vomit about it later.” He considered. “This might even draw out the big one. They’re fully committed here, which I’ll bet they don’t often attempt. It’s likely working better than they expected because of our retreat. If they suddenly start losing all their hard work…”

“There’s another problem, Ethan,” Apollo said. “There aren’t enough demons left on your side to pull this off.”

“Then we open the floodgates,” he said. “It’s this or kill most of our own army–if we’re lucky–while draining our energy reserves at the same time.”

“When you put it like that…Fable, you heard the man.”

“Do it, Fable, open the whole line up. Apollo, let them loose, only draw the line at fighting one another.”

“I hope this works,” Apollo muttered.

Ethan turned away from his unenthused chopping to look back at the train. Sure enough, the wall was separating back into distinct cars, then the cars themselves were being absorbed back into Fable. The remaining demons of their army flowed out like a rushing tide, letting out roars loud enough to be heard above the booming thunder.

They leapt upon any undead they saw, often five or more on a single target. They tore them to pieces, devouring the greying flesh greedily. It was as effective as it was horrifying to watch, and Ethan realized he was retreating slightly by instinct.

“This is the right choice,” Tomo said from the Astral. “This has become…natural to these creatures. More will survive this way, and much of the power of Potentia that we have accumulated will be saved.”

“Yeah, that doesn’t make it feel right, does it?”

Tomo growled low in his deep, draconic chest, then nodded. “No. This does not feel right.”

Ethan looked around, noticing immediately that the gigas’s healing had slowed. Curious, he looked around at some of the other large undead, and noticed they were all covered in swarms of demons, and regenerating rapidly to survive.

“I think we’re pushing the unseen leader to their limit. They can’t heal all of them, not through this,” Ethan said.

He was turning to look at Tomo, but suddenly a weight slammed into Ethan from the side. He went soaring through the air, bouncing and splashing against the soaking, muddy ground. He was forced to dig a hand in to arrest his momentum, as if grabbing a hold while falling from a mountain. He regained his feet just in time for a dark form to crash into his chest, Ethan hurling back again.

This time, hundreds of [Karmic Threads] turned his momentum into a wild swing, and he spun in a wide circle around his attacker, landing with his chain sickle in hand. The weighted end was already spinning, the bladed end held defensively, and glowing with mystic power.

A Twilight demon looked back at him, eyes glowing with an inner fire. Ethan looked back, surprised that he recognized this thing.

When he’d fought the undead in the Viridus mountains, he’d eventually faced a Dusk rank threat. A true undead, as they’re called, the ones in control, and holders of the awful affinity. It had been a powerful beast twice his size, looking like a werewolf covered in scrappy grey cloth. Ethan had no doubt this was the creature’s evolution.

Smaller, and dense with muscle, the Twilight version brought the beast closer to a man, though its face was still recognizably lupine. Its hands were also clawed, and it held them out threateningly. It still had grey cloth covering most of its body, cut into strips so they wouldn’t hamper movement, but would disguise it.

Tomo appeared, Nator-sized, and with twin katanas drawn. He and Ethan shared a nod, then stalked forward. The undead attacked, and it was fast. Faster than Ethan, certainly, and he found himself immediately on the defensive. He worked his sickle furiously, trying to keep it ahead of those deadly claws. Only Tomo attacking the beast from the opposite side kept it from gaining the upper hand.

It was ferocious, and fought with a mad abandon that was hard to predict, and harder to lock down. Ethan had to think back to his training with Rothavaro to keep his stance balanced and his mind focused, but ultimately he had the advantage. Between his weapon’s chain, thousands of [Karmic Threads], and the undead fighting two against one, it was inevitable that the creature was pushed slowly back.

Its abilities were clearly meant for controlling and supporting its minions, and it had nothing beyond its speed and strength for a head-on battle like this one–although, that speed and strength was quite significant. Still, Ethan suspected its attack was desperate, possibly due to becoming enraged by the true deaths of its army.

He and Tomo worked methodically, slowly acclimating to the creature’s pace until it couldn’t keep up. Its own incredible ability to regenerate saved it then, as Ethan watched slash after slash reknit in heart beats.

Eventually he had no choice, and activated [Avatar of Ruin], each slash becoming an enhanced [Knife in the Dark], and tearing the creature down. After Ethan managed to [Obliterate] each of its arms, the fight was clearly over. Ethan could only frown at the disturbingly human look in the creature’s eyes as he finished it off.

He fed the remains to some nearby members of his army, then looked around at what remained. It was bad.

“Two thirds,” Apollo said over the communication rune. “We lost two thirds, in one day.”

Ethan could only nod. He was looking for a place to slump down when his eyes were drawn to something in the darkness of the nearby forest. Through the raging storm and his own exhaustion, he just barely made out two burning eyes, watching him from a distance.

Then they were gone.

“There was more than one,” Tomo said, obviously having seen the same thing.

“Unfortunately, I agree,” Ethan said. “Could the first one have attacked to give that one a chance to flee?”

“Demons do not think like that, Ethan Bishop.”

Ethan smiled up at his friend. “The good ones do.”
