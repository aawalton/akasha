Chapter 168 / 13: Freezing Frieze

The voice spoke again, but it went in one ear and out the other as Joe tried to unravel the labyrinthine mural. He could tell it was trying to depict a story, but it was doing so in such a bizarre and unfathomable manner that it was driving him nuts. It wasn’t until a hand landed on his shoulder that Joe finally unfocused from the ancient imagery and tuned into his team.

“What has gotten into you, Joe?” Kenda appealed. “We’ve been trying to get your attention for ten minutes.”

“It was more like two, but it felt like ten,” Tezeno droned.

Kenda didn’t break eye contact with Joe but carped back to her long-time co-worker, “It’s an expression, Mister Stopwatch.”

“Sorry, guys,” Joe apologized, “but there is something here. I know it’s important, but I can’t figure it out. The images don’t seem to connect.”

“It’s a fey mural, Joe. You have to know how to read them,” Wen explained. “The fey don’t think like most folks. It’s like my dad says, ‘The stories of people walk on roads. Fey tales weave the woods.’ Show me which part you are interested in, and I think I can help you.”

“If anyone can follow feyish logic, an elf would be your best bet,” Tez murmured.

Joe tapped the section of chimeras and stated, “This part. Where the chimeras are invading the Feylands.” The image depicted movement across the Veil, and the various chimeras seemed to be rampaging.

The slim elven archer scanned the blue stone wall and then tapped a small mark. She then walked the whole length of the mural, quickly sliding past the immediately adjoining sections. She stopped halfway across the mural, then turned and moved to a different wall.

“Uh, Wen? What are you doing?” Joe confusedly prodded.

“Sorry. I should have explained. These four murals are actually at least two dozen narratives woven together and spanning all four walls. Each mural is connected by a talesign. See this mark here.” The silver-haired ranger tapped the mark on Oakbaron’s wall again. It looked like an upside-down peace symbol, surrounded by a teardrop instead of a circle. “That is the mark for the story you want. And it is threaded across all four walls. That’s why you couldn’t figure it out.”

“WHY?” Joe blurted. “Who tells stories that way?”

“The fey,” answered most of his teammates in unison, with only Yuk staying silent. Earcellwen scanned each wall and then returned to the group that had slid to the middle of the vast chamber.

“I have it. The forked blood-drop is the feymark for chimeras, so I just had to find that mark on each wall and then figure out where to start. As it happens, you started on the second wall, and you had the direction backwards.” She pointed at the summer wall and began to narrate, “The Summer Queen Gloriana sought more, as is her nature. She is the Giver of Abundance, the Grower, the Mother. Her realm, her court, her house, and her family all must expand for her to be fulfilling her purpose. Rather than upset the balance of the Feylands, she turned her sights on Robin Goodfellow's realm.”

“Wait a minute? Corran Loigen told me the queens did upset the balance when they went to war with the Erlking.”

The archer pointed at a different section of the wall and stated, “That’s true, but later. Remember, there are many stories being told here. The chimera’s tale is centuries older than the Fall of the Erlking.”

“Oh. Okay. Sorry, please go on.”

“The problem is that the fey cannot live in the Lands of Stone, Illuminaria, for long. So each of the rulers created beings that could remain on the other side of the Veil from the Gossamer Lands. The Summer Queen created us, the elves, as well as the bucca. We were shaped to resemble the most common race of Illuminaria, humans, but with the elegance of the feylands.” The slim ranger stammered to a stop before embarrassedly adding. “That is how the tale is told. It’s not me bragging.”

“We know, Wen,” Hah’roo breathed. “That is how I have heard it as well. The bucca were created in the image of the noble leprechaun clans.”

Joe still thought of bucca as halflings, or hobbits from The Lord of the Rings, but he could see how they could have a leprechaun-like ancestry.

“Right,” the elf continued. “The Winter Queen, Morrigu, created witches, beings much like the hags of her court. The Erlking, the one who sought loveliness in all things, created the nymphs; creatures so beautiful that they could bewitch the minds of mortals who simply gazed on them.”

“And by nymphs, you are including dryads, sylphs, naiads, and the like, right?” Kenda specified.

“Exactly so. Which brings us to chimera’s,” Wen explained, pointing at the section of wall that started this whole thing. “The Oakbaron created the chimeras to hunt this world. That is what is depicted there, Joe. It is not the chimeras invading the Feylands but them surging out of the Feylands and into Illuminaria.”

“That’s it?” Joe asked in a disappointed tone. He scratched his stubbly cheek, confused. That did not seem like enough to get both the wildness and the Mark of Death so worked up over.

“Well, no. That is just the beginning of the story, but since we are in a very dangerous place, let me summarize the rest.”

“Good idea,” both guild lieutenants agreed at the same time.

“The invasion was a complete bust. The nymphs were too reclusive to work with the other races. The elves found a deep bond with the wilderness of Illuminaria, but there were so many forests that we saw no need to fight over them. We easily found unoccupied groves to build our holts in. Yet what really foiled the invasion were the chimeras. They were completely unruly. They were supposed to be the force to drive back mortalkind, yet they were just as happy to turn on the elves, bucca, and witches. Maybe the nymphs, too, but the forest maidens vanished so quickly no one knows for sure.

“Because they were so defiant, the Oakbaron gave up on them and withdrew his lordship over them. Without protection, they were fair game. After the monarchs gave up on conquering the Lands of Stone, Morrigu was so enraged that she cursed them and all their lines. So, ever since, each race of chimera has never known peace …,” Wen’s voice trailed off, and she spun to face Joe. “Finn. Oh! Of course. The pack side of you needs to help its packmate. That’s why the story was important. At least, I think that’s partially why. I don’t know how the prophetic marks work.”

“Honestly, your guess is as good as mine, Wen,” Joe admitted. “I’ve skimmed what the guildhouse library has on the marks, but I have been too busy to do a deep dive yet. The marks are pretty enigmatic, even more so with my mark, the Mark of Change or Death. I read that it has been documented to act differently for different mark-bearers. The only thing that all the marks seem to have in common is they enhance potential, kind of like a quest.”

“So you are always under the enhancement of a quest,” Tezeno droned. His expression looked intrigued, even though his flat voice gave no such inflection.

“I don’t think so. The mark only reacts to things that interest it. The only thing I know for certain is interactions with other marks getting it going. Other than that, it's been pretty potluck as to what the mark notices.”

‘Time to wrap up discussions. Footfalls coming this way,’ Yuk announced telepathically.  ‘A decent-sized pack coming from the way we came from and even more in the direction we were heading. Guess that’s Winter and Summer. The ones behind us … Winter … are moving faster.’

“Then let’s go right or left,” Kenda stated. “Since we are here for the Erlking, Spring makes the most sense, right?”

“Agreed. I was about to suggest the same,” Hah’roo breathed, already starting to move towards the doorway in the left-hand muralled wall.

They retreated at a fast jog into another long hallway that stretched into the darkness ahead of them. Joe could see his breath in the air as they moved in the faint glow of Tezeno’s light. The air in this passageway was much colder than it had been on the upper level.

He felt bad for Wen and shook his head at the galeling, who was still dressed in her sleeveless duster, capri-length leggings, and crop top. Physical armor interfered with her fighting style, so her protections were all skill-based or magical. This meant she could wear whatever she wanted, which left large swathes of skin bare, even in the biting chill of the ziggurat. Hailing from the soaring jagged peaks of the Sapphire Coast, she seemed immune to the cold.

Joe wasn’t the only one to notice the incongruity of the white-skinned ranger's outfit.

“For crying out loud, Hah’roo,” Kendell swore in a hushed voice. “You are making me shiver just looking at you.”

Joe glanced over at Wen, meeting a set of miserable emerald eyes. He extended the hide again, and the lithe archer dove back under the heavy pelt. Walking together under the single skin would normally have been awkward, but it wasn’t. Between Wen’s high Dexterity and the hide’s ability to change shape, they moved along the dark passages easily side by side, letting Hah’roo scout on her own.

The corridor beyond the Spring doorway descended and bent to the left. The curve was not a tight corkscrew, but it clearly was a descending spiral. Down and down, the team crept ever deeper into the dreaded lair, until Yuk spoke into their heads again.

‘Company coming upon Kendell. Fast. Nine of them, but we are counting the tremors of footfalls, so it’s not an exact count.’

“Ok, let’s clear these guys before we move further. Hah’roo, quiet this area so they can’t call out. Joe, I want your paws behind the pack to make sure none get away. Wen, oil up your arrows,” the skill savant pronounced, tossing the elf a glimmering vial.

“[Still the Wind],” the galeling huffed, spreading her {Air} magic around them. The atmosphere of the passway grew heavy. Even though they were not making much noise already, even the slight sounds of their preparations were further muted. Hah’roo’s effect was not a true silence spell, yet Joe doubted any sound they made would escape her field of thickened atmosphere.

With the two guild lieutenants in the front, the team waited as Yuk counted down their enemies' approach.
