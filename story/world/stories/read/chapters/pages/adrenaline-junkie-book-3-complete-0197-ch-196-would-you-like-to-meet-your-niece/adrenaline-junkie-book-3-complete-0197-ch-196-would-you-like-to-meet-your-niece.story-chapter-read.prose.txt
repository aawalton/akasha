Branches whipped at his jacket, scratching past as Archie tore through the woods, crouched low over the mana bike’s frame. The Nature Attuned Runic Mana Engine gave a low hum beneath him, just as it did before he’d taken out its Power Core to power up the teleporter he’d made on Fractal.

I wonder how Bralmir perfectly put the engine back together in the exact way I did it, Archie mused as oak leaves blurred in his peripheral vision. His eyes were locked ahead, weaving through narrow gaps in the trees like he was being chased.

Which, technically, he was, but being chased by a pack of peak E-Grade Martens wasn’t really something he could consider to be in danger of, considering that they looked to weigh 25 kilograms at most and were the size of a sloth.

He banked hard to the right, narrowly missing a tree root that jutted out like a tripwire. His right pulled back ever so slightly, and the wheels responded, giving him just enough of an acceleration for him to pull his bike from under him for a moment to leap over yet another root that the Martens were controlling.

He then swerved to the left to avoid a moss-covered boulder the size of Bralmir, then yanked back right, carving a clean crescent through a pile of fallen leaves and skirting the edge of a gnarled root that rose from the earth like some sort of gnarled hag finger.

Why was a pack of Martens chasing him down?

How would he have known that the parent Martens wouldn’t like it if he saved their young from getting down a tree and distractedly got lost in playing with their toe beans for longer than they would have liked.

In the back of his mind, deeper, quieter, Volos and Arsenic stirred within his soulscape, waking up from their five-hour nap, having fallen asleep after being unable to move while he was on his mana bike. He could feel their presence, coiled and watching him as he zig-zagged in between large uprooted stumps that now littered the forest.

He didn’t slow. And they kept hissing within the soulscape, wanting to come out and enjoy the speed, but he kept them tucked inside his soulscape. The speeds at which he was traveling were more than what they could handle, not to mention the sharp turns and adjustments he was making every couple of seconds while in the forest would have most definitely given them whiplash at the very least.

“Hey, you two went wild in the tavern earlier,” he muttered, half-gritting his teeth as he ducked through a particularly tight cluster of trees. “Consider this payback.”

The forest thinned without warning. Archie saw that the forest, thick, lush forest, vanished, giving way to much shorter grass and a few scattered bushes with a sparse amount of trees littering the patch in front of him.

It was as if he’d passed through an invisible veil, like back when he’d attempted to spy on Oliver on the cliffs.

I really need to improve my mana detection skills, and really get back into mana exercises, I wonder if Bralmir has a couple of… nah, I can ask him if I can’t find anything on the Aetherglass Bralmir gave me to use so I wouldn’t have to use my guild membership card and have my searches and stuff monitored every time I access it, Archie mused to himself, somewhat annoyed that he was unable to detect the veil even after manipulating mana as well as he did when forging.

Archie squinted as the late afternoon sunlight flooded the clearing and almost blinded him, by how sudden and bright it appeared to him.

Before him, nestled in the heart of a shallow valley surrounded by low hills and swaying dull green grass, stood a squat, log house. Smoke curled lazily from the chimney, rising like a soft ribbon into the sky. A wide perimeter fence wrapped around the entire property, made of thick wooden beams, and a small workshop pressed against the side of the outside wall of the log house, not with any windows beside it, along with various other logs, planks, and various other wood working bits and bobs.

He eased the mana bike to a gentle stop, its humming engine quieting until only the soft ticking of cooling metal could be heard.

Then he flicked back open the lid of his Compass of Desire on his bike and saw that its crimson needle was vibrating excitedly at said house.

Archie stared at it, lips parting slightly.

“Finally,” he murmured.

Archie revved the mana bike almost instinctually, pulling him out of any thoughts that would have once more trapped him in hundreds of assumptions. The hum of the engine buzzed through the soles of his boots and up his spine, grounding him after the chaos of the chase.

“Speaking of which,” he muttered to himself as he activated Earthen Grasp to hold the Martens that were still chasing after him in place before he took out his Mana Revolver and activated Runic Amplification atop it, flooding its mana chamber and container with his mana. Archie felt it strain to hold his much denser mana.

Letting loose six separate shots, each one found its mark in the eyes of the six Martens, and letting loose another six shots, he killed the rest of the Martens that chased after him.

*[Marten Lv 87] has been slain – XP has been given.*…*[Marten Lv 91] has been slain – XP has been given.*

That was easy, Archie mused to himself as he deactivated Vital Sight. While he knew they had pretty weak vital energy outlines, he didn’t think they were that weak. I wonder how they were able to survive that long if they were killed that easily?

Not sparing them another glance, Archie looked down and saw that his Mana Revolver was smoking. Shit, that shouldn’t, he thought to himself before activating Gaze of the Forgefather to look inside the revolver without having to open it up, and saw that the mana of the mana pathways was fried from how much dense mana he was channeling inside of it.

“Well, crap,” Archie muttered, his eyes lingering on his first-ever completed gun he’d made for a couple more seconds before tucking it into his Spatial Storage. He would have to repair it later.

But now…

He leaned forward slightly, eyes fixed on the fence up ahead, where the clearing opened into the quiet homestead.

As he and his bike coasted toward the gate, he saw a figure emerge out of the door facing the fence gate, close the door, and walk a few feet forward on the patio, staring at him – seemingly having sensed his arrival or heard the screeches of the Martens. Either one was likely if he was being honest.

Something about the way the guy stood,  the coloring of his hair, his frame;  it was all too familiar to him, and the longer Archie stared, the clearer it became he knew who that person was.

The person in front of him was his brother, it was Henry.

His hair was messy and unkempt, as was his beard – something practically inconceivable to Archie, who had always known his brother to take pride in being neat and well-presented, unlike himself.

Suddenly, an image of a much younger Henry overlapped the figure standing outside the squat log house’s door. This version of Henry looked just as exhausted as the one before him – utterly drained, with barely four hours of sleep in the past week, thanks to finals month and a brutal schedule where all his exams were packed back-to-back and compressed into a week, some even on the same day.

It was uncanny, but he didn’t doubt it for a second.

“Henry?!” Archie called, voice cracking slightly over the soft rumble of the bike.

He raised one hand and waved, his grin stretching across his face almost before he realized he was smiling.

The figure didn’t move, nor did he respond.

For a second, Archie thought he’d gotten it wrong… maybe it wasn’t Henry. Maybe he’d been away too long, maybe time and distance had warped the memory, maybe–

Then, suddenly, the axe and sword in the man’s hands vanished.

“Archie?” The man shouted back in surprise before breaking into a full sprint towards the main fence gate of his house and keeping the wards that protected his house under full force protection as he activated Identify on the man in front of him.

He’d only exited his house because he felt someone go past the proximity wards of his house and crept closer to it. This was the last thing he’d expected to see.

His voice was much deeper and rougher than he remembered it being, but it was unmistakable.

Archie barely had time to slow his mana bike to a halt before Henry was at the gate, and leapt over it.

Henry’s chest rose and fell like he’d just run a marathon, but his eyes didn’t leave Archie’s face. He looked like he was trying to make sense of something that his brain didn’t quite compute. Like the Archie in front of him was some sort of figment of his imagination, and that he was going to disappear any second now, or that he was under some sort of illusion.

He looks older, Archie noted as he stared at his brother’s face. Not biologically, but…

Like whatever had happened since they last saw each other had put him through the wringer and still had him in it.

Archie opened his mouth to say something. Anything at all, but before he could, Henry beat him to it.

“Who are our Parents?” he asked, his voice tight. “Their names.”

“Sarah and Matthew Gracefield,” Archie answered without a second of hesitation. “What’s Mom’s maiden name?”

“Smith,” Henry answered, despite the lump that had settled in his throat. “How many years?”

“2,000 to 2,003 years,” Archie answered, dismissing his mana bike into his Spatial Storage without another word and taking a couple of steps closer.

Henry still hadn’t moved, instead, his eyes locked on Archie’s face with disbelief, as if he were to blink, his brother would disappear before his eyes.

“You...” His arms hung stiffly at his sides, with his fists half-curled, like his body was waiting for some kind of signal to act on, and for a moment it looked like he might just punch Archie in the jaw.

But instead, all he said was, “You look like shit.”

“I could say the same about your ugly mug,” Archie shot back with a crooked smile tugging at his lips as he came to a stop just a few paces away from his brother. "It's good to see you, too, you crayon thief."

The final words Archie said, seemed to have broken through whatever hold Henry had been holding himself together with as he lunged forward and wrapped his arms around him with all the strength he could muster.

Then he raised a hand and clapped it against Henry’s back, returning the hug with a solid, grounding thump. His other arm came around, and he pulled him in tighter.

And as he did, he noticed something: Henry was trembling ever so faintly as he hugged him, like someone who’d held it together for years and couldn’t anymore.

Archie froze for half a moment as the realization came to him.

… He should have come sooner. If he had, his younger brother wouldn’t have had to suffer for as long as he did.

“You didn’t die,” Henry muttered, voice muffled against Archie’s shoulder. It was hoarse and full of something raw, something splintered. “You bastard... You didn’t die. You’re alive.”

Archie’s eyes slipped shut.

“Always,” he murmured, barely loud enough to be heard, but to Henry it was as clear as a whistle.

When they finally stepped back from each other, Henry stifled his nose, looking at his brother for a moment before looking back at his house, then back to him. “Would you like to meet your niece?”
