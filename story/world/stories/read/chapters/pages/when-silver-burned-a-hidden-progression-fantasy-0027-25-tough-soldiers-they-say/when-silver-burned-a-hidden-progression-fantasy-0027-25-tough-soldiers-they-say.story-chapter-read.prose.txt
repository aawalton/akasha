Losing control over the direction of your life is a curious thing. It can be a frustrating experience, yet once you let go and give in, it invokes an odd sense of comfort, like warming your cold hands over the flames of a wildfire blazing through the forest. Agency over what happens next is out the window. Responsibility over your well-being, as well as the well-being of those around you rests on the shoulder of someone so high up the ladder you'll probably never meet them. It's a scary thing, especially for one so reliant on plans for the future.

The following week should've given Kalsin plenty of time to think, but between physical conditioning, integrating into a new branch of standards and training to march, fighting in formation and learning discipline, there was precious little he could discover about his curse.

Was there a limit on how many scars he could leech from others? Were they permanent? Was there a correlation between the way scars stuck to him and his inability to surge? Worthless questions with no possible answers, and it wasn’t like he could experiment to find out.

It was a piss-poor situation altogether that only complicated his day to day life. Mounting anxiety. Sleepless nights. And a bad case of keeping others at arm's length—even more so than usual—to avoid accidentally brushing over a stray scar.

He never asked for this, damn it. And he was terrified of what people would ask of him, if anyone knew.

Another concern on his mind was the Civil House itself. He had no idea if the monocled agent at the gate had put things together—the fact that Jack’s exact scar was claimed by him, and that she now enjoyed a life free without it. Did he manage to take a look at Kalsin’s face while he was out and inside the vision of Jack’s memory? He had no idea.

Yes, he evaded the machinations of the upper crust's secret service for the time being, but they sure as hell wouldn't be sitting around with thumbs in their Clenn asses while he went off fighting the eastern invaders. They had resources beyond Kalsin's comprehension. Perhaps enough influence to reach him in the middle of the Scarrd Division and hundreds of miles away from the archipelago's mainlands.

Kalsin hated being in the dark. He hated not having Jack and Cinder by his side to face whatever came next. With them serving in the Clenn Division, there was no way to reconnect, not yet. But there was one little spark of hope he kept alive, knowing they were both going toward the same destination. To the east.

So, yes. Things weren't rosy. His own body was playing a cruel game on him, his friends were out of reach for the moment, and daggers were likely being sharpened against his own good self. Oh, and he might or might not have seen an actual, true-to-life, godsdamn fucking ghost in the military office.

Not rosy at all.

But on the bright side, he wasn't alone in his woes.

His mates had plenty of hardships adjusting to proper 'soldering'. Crawl was lagging behind in sword training even with both Kalsin's pointers and Littleguy's private sessions, and it took a minor miracle to get through the day without Brownweather snapping at him. Shutter, for some reason, couldn't comprehend that you were not supposed to lather the steel blade in an entire jar of dripping wax, and Kalsin promised himself he would beat some proper weapon maintenance into him before the man depleted the supplies of the entire Ember Republic. As for Fals, she treated the whole thing as a joke, some holiday resort where you could sing, loiter and just generally be a pain in the ass for all recruits involved. Actually, the singing part wasn't half bad. She used to be a bard if her word could be believed. It was strange she would swear on earth and sky about her past profession, everyone instantly dismissing it as a lie because, well, Fals being Fals, then hear an expert strum of lute as she launched into a bawdy song.

Other than Brownweather, they rarely came across another ranking officer around the camp, which meant the recruits were pretty much forced to teach one another if they found holes in their knowledge. For Kalsin, who had been a loner in school growing up, it was a pleasant surprise to see that interaction between soldiers-in-training was an entirely different beast. Though people still gravitated toward their circle of mates, there were no childish cliques or hostile gangs. Sure, disagreements were aplenty. Fights broke out, and there was a good deal of bad blood to go around. But at the end of the day everyone knew the next person lying in their bunk could be the difference between life and death tomorrow, and that belief only strengthened as the date for their inevitable mobilization drew closer.

These things, they're not something you discuss, yet everyone's aware of the untold truth like the undeniable fact that humans cease to function without air. Kalsin would've never admitted to Ringtail—who still tried besting him in duels with a fierce hatred exclusively reserved for him—he never would've admitted that he wanted to see her improve, a small part of him feeling proud whenever she came out victorious. It happened rarely, Kalsin's body hurting all over afterwards, but he found solace in the fact that she wasn't trying to kill him for real.

At least, he hoped she wasn't.

Since Kalsin's arrival, a little more than half a dozen new recruits joined their ranks and earned a fresh new identity from the depths of Brownweather's twisted imagination. Kalsin didn't envy them; getting sent off to war was bad enough already, but with only a few days of training under your belt? A shitty situation from all angles. Sure, each recruit technically volunteered to be here, but for many of them it was basically a choice between likely death and certain death, whether by defending the Republic or executed by a headsman's axe. As for the patriot types? Eh. In Kalsin's opinion, only the unhinged would join out of nothing but the good of their golden hearts.

One quizzical character such as that was a polearm prodigy with the fitting name Pike. At first, Kalsin thought he was some weapon expert payrolled by the army as a halberd instructor, but soon he found out the fellow who always had a helping hand ready for anyone in the vicinity was, in fact, just another recruit not unlike themselves.

"Thrust, don't let it waver," Pike said next to Kalsin in line. "Good, now chop backwards like this." Pike demonstrated, showing the strange technique that was unique to halberds. The axe-like blade, at the base of the spear point, had an odd crescent shape designed to inflict damage when pulled back after a thrust, Pike had explained. It gave the wielder the chance to turn a defensive move into an offensive one.

Kalsin followed his example, arms grasping the pole shakily after a long hour of practice. Though the weapon felt foreign in his hands, he couldn't deny its supposed effectiveness against large, immobile targets. Or against a crowd.

"Good, now swipe back. Lively, Ghost! Mythos hide ain't gonna open itself up."

Aching and out of breath, Kalsin still couldn't hold back a snort. "Ah, yes. The army and their prowess against mythical creatures, heroically rescuing villages ravaged across the length of the Republic."

"Ouch. Touchy subject?" Pike drawled.

"Coy doesn't suit you. But anyway. Everyone knows hunting mythos requires finesse rather than brute force. Soldiers might put down a mindless beast tearing through the population, provided it's big, loud, and alerts every human soul in a hundred mile radius. No debate there. But the most dangerous creatures are the ones you don't see coming. The ones intelligent enough to lay low. These types of nightmares, they wipe out a handful of families, then scurry the moment they hear the marching steps of military boots shaking the earth. The army has no subtlety, no creative thinking. Soldiers are novice in the art of monster-killing."

Kalsin wanted to say more, but he stopped at that. There was no point letting his companions know about his past as a hunter. He didn't have a reason to keep it a secret, per say, but giving away information with no gain whatsoever was something an amateur would do.

Pike rotated a shoulder. "I wouldn't know. But seeing how you serve in the army now, I'm sure you understand if I find that take a little hysterical."

Kalsin didn't put up a debate, instead thrusting his halberd forward in the practiced motion, then pulling it down and back with protesting muscles. He sneaked a glance around the training yard. "I still think Brownweather should give you a salary, seeing how you're here doing his job while he always disappears to Gods know where."

Pike shook his head, dark skin barely sweating as he put another length of steel through an invisible opponent. "The lieutenant has enough on his plate already, and me, I can afford to take a small portion off of him." He wretched his halberd downward, cleaving the nonexistent creature in half and bringing the weapon back to ready position. "Someone has to. I swear. The whole lot of you respect him 'bout as much as an inflamed pimple, and that won't do much good in the long run. Nor for us, not for him." he said, though cushioned the edge with a sour smile.

"Maybe," Kalsin allowed. The lieutenant wasn't his favorite topic of discussion, and their impending march was weighing heavily on his mind. It was only a few days before they would be sent off toward the border. "I just hope we'll make it in one piece."

"Batshit said he had visited a fort along the Inner Line of Defense once," Crawl butted in as he moved past them toward the weapon racks, balancing a halberd in his arms. "Said the place looked cheerful enough. The ale served wasn't all that special, but the storytellers made up for it. The Clenn side had the largest surge-training ground he'd ever seen. He could hear explosions from a mile away."

"What about Scarrd establishments?" Pike asked.

Crawl shrugged, shouting over the noise of recruits stashing away their gear, training done for the day. "Didn't say. He'd been Clenn, back then." He smiled. "But we'll get to see for ourselves during the honor parade."

"The what now?" Kalsin asked.

Pike snorted. "It's customary to send recruits off with a little something as they march through the Inner Line. Drinks, celebration, rose petals, that sorta deal. Supposed to warm our innards with the last pleasant memories we’ll ever acquire, when sitting there in the Old, out in the grim vastness and waiting for the knife to fall."

"We'll be fine," Kalsin said, putting enough resolve in the words to sound genuinely reassuring. He stole a last glance at Pike's exemplary stance with the long weapon, and for the third time today, came to the conclusion the sword was more his speed. "The Old, hm? That's what they call us, the other soldiers. Older boys in training."

If Brownweather's constant bitching had any basis in reality, the recruits would not survive the bloodshed that was the daily norm in the Outer Line of Defense. As an older boy, the life of a soldier was short, brutal and isolated. The lieutenant liked to mention the dirt. Dirt that carried on the wind and across the barren land, coating the inside of your mouth when you talked, ate, drank. Dirt that slid past the folds of your uniform and filled up your boots. And the Scarrd, they were treated like dirt, used as bait to hold off the Veiled until reinforcement from the inland forts arrived. The older boys were, by all meanings of the word; expendable.

"It can't be that bad," Pike said. Maybe he was serious. Maybe he was convincing himself. Kalsin couldn't tell either way. "Older boys are the toughest bastards in the army, and respect is hard to come by for people of our social standing."

"Renown, more like," Kalsin said. No Clenn would ever respect a Scarrd.

As was proper, he heard his mother echo.

And he hated the fact that a part of him agreed with her.
