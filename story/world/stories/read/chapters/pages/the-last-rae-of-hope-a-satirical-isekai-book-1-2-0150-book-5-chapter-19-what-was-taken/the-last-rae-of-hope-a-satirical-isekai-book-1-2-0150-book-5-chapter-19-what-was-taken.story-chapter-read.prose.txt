The dark, vine-shrouded temple came alive all at once. Children spilled out of narrow, crooked gaps between the stone walls, shouting as they scrambled over roots and loose rocks. Bundles flew into the outdoor pavilion, most landing with the soft thud of linen, though one cloth bag landed with a bit of an unfortunate shatter. A girl yelped as others laughed, their stomping at odds with the seriousness of our collective exodus.

“Only grab the important things!” Tetora barked. “If it doesn’t keep you alive, it stays behind!”

“Aww…” Kiko said, eyeing a vase that she must have purloined. “Oh well.” She trudged back inside with it, only for us to hear it break a moment later. “Oops!”

Tetora sighed in defeat and headed for one of its many uneven entrances. “I’ll be back. Wait here.”

Nora frowned, then immediately poked her head inside the temple the moment he was out of sight.

“Well?” I asked as she returned to the awning.

“It’s a disaster in there,” she confirmed, shaking her head.

“And that’s saying a lot, coming from you and your housekeeping routines…” I murmured, prudently stepping out of strike range as Olethros chuckled softly.

Tetora reemerged a few minutes later with a bag and his trusty iron claws. Nora squinted at them, her fingers tightening around her staff.

“We can try stripping off the rusty bits later,” I whispered. “I have a feeling his hands have been full, and the humidity here probably didn’t—”

“I’m not worried about rust,” she cut in, holding out her hand. “Lemme see ’em.”

Tetora blinked, then handed the iron claws over. Nora winced under their weight.

“Don’t do anything weird to them,” he advised, already turning back toward the chaos inside. “Hey! That scroll doesn’t belong to you! Put it back!”

Nora started to glow a faint purple. “Hmm…”

“What’s wrong?” I asked.

“A lot of things,” she muttered. She shoved them into my hands. “Feel for yourself.”

I hefted them, sensing a slight spike in amity. “They’re still blessed, but I think it’s wearing off.”

Nora’s expression hardened from concern to a full scowl. “That’s the exact problem I’m referring to.”

“I’m sure we can find another priest to re-bless them, eventually—”

“No. They can’t come with us like that.”

“Why?”

Olethros tilted his head. “Remember who blessed them last.”

Relias!

“Oh… The bad man,” I said almost absently.

“The bad man?” Nora echoed.

I nodded. “I don’t like saying his name. Edric summed him up pretty well, though. He’s just… a bad, bad man.”

Nora glanced at Olethros and gave a firm thumbs-down. He just shrugged in response, then straightened.

A realization came to me, slow but sure. “Wait… He blessed my weapons, too? What should we do about that?”

Nora hesitated. “We checked them before we gave them back. No amity blessing associated with him.”

Was it all for show? Or could he somehow rescind his blessings?

Whatever. It doesn’t matter.

Olethros tapped his cheek. “Nora, what if you—,” he started, then leaned in and whispered to her in detail.

She listened for a few moments, then said loudly, “Why don’t you just do it?”

He cleared his throat. “I’m sure Tetora would prefer I keep my hands off his personal equipment.”

I knew what he meant, but I hadn’t gotten all that much sleep, and a naughty snicker somehow escaped me. I didn’t have to look to feel Nora’s eyes rolling in response.

“Steel might do it as an ideal form,” she said, mostly to herself. “Stainless steel to prevent rust? Oh, but I don’t know what the outer layer of that is made of, so I’m not sure how to convert from iron.”

Oh!

“Chromium oxide,” I promptly replied. “That’s what makes it resistant to rust. But not all stainless steel is as strong as iron. And I think its forging can be a bit complex, so imagining its transformation might be hard.”

“Again,” she said slowly, “I need to know why you have this weird knowledge.”

“Baking! Don’t you remember me ruining your mom’s stainless-steel pan?”

“Among other things, yes. I now recall several kitchen disasters associated with you.”

I coughed. “Anyway. Just go with tempered steel. It’ll still rust, but that’s a minor inconvenience of upkeep.”

Nora considered this, then turned back to Olethros. They whispered again, conspiring quietly. By the time Tetora returned, the transmutation was complete.

“I said don’t do anything weird with them!” he admonished, even as Nora handed the claws to me for a final check.

“We’re making them better and bless free,” I explained, nodding my head in final approval. “All clear of amity blessings!”

“Well, how am I supposed to fight demons with them?!”

Olethros folded his arms. “You’re not.”

Tetora twitched, then grimaced. “I wasn’t referring to you… But the bad ones!”

I coughed. “Right now, we need everyone we can put our hands on, and that includes demons—and their lords. So, let’s not lose sight of our real goal—the downfall of the bad man.”

Tetora’s eyes clouded with concern. “Whatever you say, Rae—Rachel.”

I smiled slightly in response, knowing he was doing his best to meet us where we needed him to be.

***

If Galenus was surprised that we returned to the jungle outskirts with a small procession of children in tow, he didn’t show it. Under Tetora’s watchful gaze, he adjusted something on his gauntlet before opening a larger portal than we had started with. The dozen or so kids with us froze at first, but Kiko was the first to march forward, stepping through with only the tiniest of shivers. A few went for Tetora’s hand, and Edric went for mine. Sadly, no one grabbed Olethros for reassurance, but Nora nabbed one dawdling child without a second thought.

“In you go!”

Chester greeted us from a tall pedestal intentionally positioned a few inches above my head. He looked across at us gathered in the large, empty room, then hopped down lithely with a single bounce.

Several children moved forward, chattering excitedly.

“Mraaw-raar!” he declared, and the children froze.

“This is Chester,” I said. “My cat. Please treat him well.”

Tetora eyed him warily, then flinched the moment Chester returned the stare. With a flick of his ears, Chester turned and began licking at a tuft of fur, prompting a chorus of squeals.

“What does he like to eat?” Edric asked.

“Where does he sleep?”

“Can I pet him?”

“Why is his name Chester?”

“How old is he?”

“Does he like to be held?”

Chester mewled once more, stealing their attention before I could answer. Then he sauntered through a nearby mirror. With a collective gasp, the kids followed him.

“That isn’t a cat,” Tetora said flatly.

“Well, how was I supposed to know?!” I shot back. “Uh—I mean, I know that now, sure! But just play along. He’s trying his best to be one.”

Tetora considered this. “I suppose there are worse creatures to emulate. Felines are of the highest order.” He hesitated. “He won’t hurt them, will he?”

“Chester? No. He’s a sweetheart. You can mess up his fur any way you like, and he never gets mad.”

None of them were dog hybrids, either. Thank goodness.

Galenus cleared his throat. “We meet again, Iron Tiger. I hope we may put aside any past differences in the spirit of—”

“Why are you all standing around like idiots?” Beigaldi shrieked as he seeped up through the floor. “Bodhil is out there again, and none of you give the slightest damn about—”

He froze, his eyes wide at Tetora. “You!”

Tetora snarled, both claws snapping into position. “Forget blessings. I’ll take him out with sheer—”

Olethros snapped his fingers.

Beilgaldi collapsed inward, reduced instantly to his core.

“I warned you about embarrassing me, Nephew,” Olethros said mildly as he watched the core bounce on the floor. “Try again. This time, with a modicum of restraint.”

Tetora stared at him.

“I do not tolerate rudeness from my subordinates,” Olethros explained, “regardless of relation. Should he address you so poorly again, I will order him to the Southern Ocean.” He paused. “He may decorate a coral reef for the next century or two.”

“You don’t understand, Uncle!” the core wailed.

“It is you who doesn’t understand,” Olethros replied. “If you wish to save your core-mate, you will do precisely as instructed.”

“Core-mate?” I asked.

“Bohdil,” he clarified. “He is able to track her because she is his core-mate.”

“Is that like… a soulmate?” I stared at the core. “What, you’re married to her?!”

He snarled as he reformed. “And what’s wrong with that? I don’t need permission to exchange core shards from anyone, especially a fool like you!”

Olethros put his fingertips together again, but I waved him off.

“I just… Never really saw her returning your affections, that’s all,” I admitted. “Even if you can turn furry like Tetora.”

He turned white—literally. “You heard that?”

“Uh huh. But more importantly, where’s Bodhil now that’s got you so stirred up?”

I don’t think I’m ready to fight her again yet, but…

“Northwick and Southwick,” he answered sullenly.

“The strangely divided, double-yet-desolate town full of hybrids? Why there, of all places?” I glanced at Olethros. “Is it some secret demon haven?”

Olethros frowned. “I have never heard of such a place…”

“We’ve been there before!” Tetora turned to Galenus. “Well? Open a portal already!”

Galenus glanced at Nora, who nodded in affirmation. He then summoned a translucent map. “It appears there is sufficient animus in the region… I will put us near the shared border.”

We were too late. Northwick and Southwick had both been half-razed, with random buildings reduced to nothing but smoldering piles. Hybrids stood stone-faced and ashen, each surveying the destruction that was once their collective home.

“They took Lala!” a woman shrieked from behind.

They?

As in more than one?

I turned to see a familiar hybrid rabbit tearing at her sooty apron, her ears drooping.

Lulu!

“They said… They said she had funny eyes…”

She was talking to the ground, her face stricken.

“I’ve known Lala all my life… She’s not a demon.”

Tears started to pour down her cheeks.

“Even if she was… She’s my sister… My little sister.” Then she looked up at Paradise and screamed, “They took my little sister!”
