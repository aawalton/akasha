The Optio said nothing else and walked out of the barracks. Senior Cassius followed.

The thirteen legionaries herded the recruits out of the building and followed from a distance as they walked in the darkness. It was nearly pitch black with clouds that hid the stars and most of the moon’s light. The shadows were fought back by only the tall lantern poles and odd brazier of flame that dotted the camp grounds.

Max still found it difficult to see. The lanterns were to spread out and left large gaps between them.

The legionaries surrounding them had no such problem.

Arnie walked up to him and got close. “What do you think they meant with brass balls? Are we throwing brass balls or crushing them to show our strength?”

I hope not!

Max shrugged. He was not about to comment about himself.

Captain Samson had done all the story telling necessary for the next decade and a half.

“I heard that they’re going to make us fight something…”

Max blinked as he turned to look at Arnie. The boy had a talent for gathering information better than anyone else.

“How do you figure that?”

“Some of the legionaries said they wanted to come watch the fights against something when we were getting our Patron Inheritances. They got yelled at by one of the Captain’s I think… he had the helmet on with the red plumes–”

No wonder they were all so quiet by the time I got there. They already used up all their conversations before I could hear them except for the cook…

“–you think they’ll give us armor and a sword? Wouldn’t that be something.”

Max frowned and shook his head. “If we end up fighting, then I doubt they give us anything special or they would have told us to wear the clothing set. They expect us to go through the ones we have on, if I had to guess.”

Arnie stared at his face for a few seconds. He didn’t say anything else.

The group looped further into the forest, moving for nearly half an hour before they broke into a clearing surrounded by large boulders and thick copses of trees. This place was a single structure large enough to house multiple barracks and half the camp they had been in.

Max and Arnie stared in awe at the spiraling amphitheatre going downward and made of gray stone.

It could fit thousands in here…

The central battling grounds had a rusty red soil packed hard and tight.

Giant braziers dotted the highest level. Lantern poles covered the ground in between and along the stairs down from every side. Unlike the camp, this place blazed with light and nary a shadow.

“This place looks like it was carved from a crater,” Arnie said.

“A what?”

“A crater. Big rock falls from sky and causes a large… it’s not important. Cratere Urbem, the city I’m from was founded on a massive one that looked like this. There were hundreds more outside of the gates that covered the surrounding plains and hills.”

Huh… You learn something new every day. Craters. Big rocks falling from the sky.

The recruits were guided down to the lowest seating area.

Senior Casius was sitting a few meters away from the group above them, two levels higher.

The thirteen legionaries spread out to form a wide circle around the amphitheater next to the braziers. Seven more stood at attention on the rust colored soil of the battle grounds next to the Optio, who had arrived before them. They looked small next to him.

Optio Ignacious waited until they were all seated with a thick bag in front of him.

Arnie sat next to Max.

While Max was busy keeping an eye over his shoulder. He had noticed Caleb getting closer with every step they took to get here since they left the barracks. Moving gradually enough that it would have been impossible to notice had Max not been intentionally working on keeping his wits about him at all times.

The eagle eyed boy was sitting only two seats away from him to the left.

“Look,” Arnie pointed to the far side of the battle ground.

Max turned away from eyeing Caleb and looked where Arnie pointed. There was a giant gate made of thick metal with two legionaries standing to either side of the central opening. Nothing but solid, unadorned metal that could likely have taken a charging bull without being worse for wear.

“What do you think is in there?”

Max frowned, remembering Arnie’s words while they were being led here. “Probably whatever we’re going to fight.”

Max noticed Caleb perk up at his words. He ignored it.

The Optio took a step forward and waved his hand. Three legionaries separated, from the seven beside him, and marched toward the massive gate. A pair grabbed each handle while the last one pulled out their blade and waited for the signal.

“Today is an auspicious day!” the Optio shouted.

The legionaries grunted as they pulled. The gate screamed with old hinges as it opened.

“You stand in our Cursed Legion’s Coliseum!”

A cold draft washed over the entire amphitheater from the insides of the gate. The legionaries pulled their weapons out and marched into the darkness within.

“Grounds where kids become men!”

A loud crash echoed, followed by the screeching of metal being dragged and the roars of hundreds of monsters. Creatures shouting and cursing in languages Max had never heard before nor could he understand.

“And men become legends!”

Max and Arnie leaned forward on their seats to get a better look. The legionaries stepped into the light pulling long, thick chains attached to something that caused the screeching metal on stone sound. They figured what it was a moment later.

A large cage with a heavy tarp was being pulled out and dragged to rest before the Optio.

Ignacious stepped closer and grabbed the tarp, never once looking away from the recruits. The giant man smiled and pulled it off. “Today, you either survive and become men, level 1, or you ascend to meet your maker!”

Max felt his blood go cold.

Arnie grabbed his shoulder and the rest of the boys gave various reactions as they stared at the contents of the cage.

One solitary GreenSkinned; a goblin.

Savagery, evil, and thoughtless violence given physical form. Red eyes, thick green hide-like skin, sharp teeth, and nails thick enough to function as proper claws.

It was everything the stories claimed.

Less than the tales his mother had made him read, but it was close. There were no Orc’s or Trolls charging the walls as they sat there. No goblin shamans and hobgoblin armies marching as though they were sapient and rational creatures that could learn to fully coordinate.

That’s the ultimate enemy. Mother had made sure to make me read on them constantly.

Max doubted the rest of the boys knew as much as he did about the tales. Armies clashing with hordes of GreenSkinned. Heroes of old standing bright against the tide of death and darkness that threatened to consume humanity from whence it had been born.

An enemy centuries old.

Razing cities, burning farms and crop fields, and slaughtering entire cities of hundreds of thousands of people. With no purpose but to see the final calamitous ruin of all human civilization and life. And when they were done with them, they would fight the elves, then the dwarves, then the beastfolk, and then every single race until only they remained.

They watched the goblin’s red eyes scan back and forth. It snapped into a fit of violence and unadulterated rage. Slamming into the cage bars, screaming, shouting, banging, clawing in the hopes to reach the Optio and the legionaries to rip them apart.

It cursed and spat in a guttural language that drove fear into the hearts of every recruit, Max included.

The Optio stepped away from the cage while the goblin continued its fit of terror and paused in front of the bag he had been standing in front of. He kicked it, spilling its contents. Daggers of various sizes spilled from it.

A legionary dropped a short sword he had been carrying near the gate.

The intent was obvious.

“You have one task. Kill the goblin with nothing more than a knife… while it wields a short sword. The thing has been starved and dehydrated. If you can't kill it, then you don't deserve to be here. I pray for your success.”
