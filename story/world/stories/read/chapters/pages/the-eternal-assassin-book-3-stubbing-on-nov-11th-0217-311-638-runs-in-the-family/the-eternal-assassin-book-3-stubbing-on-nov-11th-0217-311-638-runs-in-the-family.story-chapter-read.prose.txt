Asher and the rest of the team took Donvath’s advice, taking a few days to rest and recuperate after their encounter with The Lost Lady. Not even Moxy complained about the break, going so far as to switch to light sparring with Christian after she regained strength in her body again. Overheal was a powerful skill indeed, but the cost of pushing her Life and Air elements to such an extreme for such a short burst of power was drastically overshadowed by the fact that she could barely even move for the rest of the day.

The more blended skills Asher ended up witnessing, the more he understood why he didn’t often see people utilizing them. Of course, the primary reason was the simple fact that very few people even managed to reach the Stage of Bleed in the first place, due to the shard cost and three element requirement. But even with that in mind, it made sense such skills would be more of a last-resort option. He’d gotten lucky that his Remote Control didn’t really seem to have any major downside, but that was probably because the downside was a requirement to use the skill rather than a result of it. After all, the skill was worthless until he’d been dismembered.

Donvath’s suggestion that Samantha see a therapist ended up being a fantastic one, as Asher got to hear all about how wonderful the woman Samantha was talking to was from the hunter during their day-long excursion with Brian. While Brian was eager to tell them all about what he’d been up to in the truthseeker program, the two of them would trade off back and forth, excited for their own experiences and hearing what their sibling had been up to. Asher got to sit back and watch the two of them share with a smile on his face, happy that Samantha was doing better and that everything was going so well for Brian.

“I got to fire a ballista!” Brian exclaimed, laughing maniacally as he mimed a giant explosion with all the enthusiasm that only a twelve-year-old who had been trusted with handling a siege weapon possibly could. “It was awesome! I hit a target almost a quarter of a mile away!”

“How large even is the truthseeker academy?” Asher muttered as Samantha congratulated her brother on his impressive accuracy.

As Donvath had promised, Brian had also come with a Ring of Honest Ears, just like the old one Bordan had lent him. Only this one didn’t have the same restriction of needing to be recharged by a specific person every twenty-four hours. Asher was already at his limit when it came to artifacts, so he tucked it away within his rift for the time being. He didn’t actually want to be pinged anytime someone told him a little white lie anyway, he only cared about knowing people were telling the truth when his own life or those of his teammates were at risk.

While everyone rested up in preparation for launching their next attack on the Council, Asher snagged another few lessons with Mornamol, making sure to keep his skills sharp and sneak in a little improvement anywhere he could. He’d distinctly noticed his skills with his daggers improving little by little during all of his recent fights, so the lessons were definitely working.

However, this time around, he had an ulterior motive beyond just learning how to fight better.

“Alright, break!” Mornamol called, panting as he wiped some sweat from his brow and threw him a crooked smile. While Asher still hadn’t managed to land a hit on the old slayer, he was definitely getting closer with every lesson. “I see you’ve been doing your homework! Some nice moves out there.”

“Really?” Asher scoffed, yanking a dagger out of his thigh and sighing as he watched his flesh slowly knit itself together in real time. He was half tempted to ask Mornamol to just stab him in the heart in the hopes of forcing Eternal Regeneration up to level 40, but some part of him warned him to be patient. Everything he’d read and everyone he talked to seemed to agree that skills leveled better and achieved greater effects due to legitimate combat as opposed to training. It would happen soon enough. “I hadn’t noticed.”

“Sure! Normally you’ve got three or four daggers sticking out of you by now!” Mornamol chuckled, tilting his head back as he took a long swig from his canteen.

“Mornamol…” Asher said, glancing around before throwing up a Spatial Lock to block sound and light from escaping. He didn’t think anyone had actually been watching them practice in the slayer guild’s training grounds, but one could never be too sure. “...How much do you know about the High Prince?”

Raising an eyebrow, Mornamol held up a finger, finishing his drink before letting out a hearty sigh. Shaking his head, he gave Asher a pointed look. “Is this a curiosity kind of question? Or more of a heresy kind?”

“It’s a concerned question,” Asher decided. “You don’t have to answer if you don’t want to.”

“Before I say anything, I think it’s only fair that you tell me why you’re asking in the first place,” Mornamol said, tossing his canteen down before he started picking at his ear. “I’m no noble, but even I’ve heard how The Reaper and the High Prince are tight as twins. Something happen?”

“Nothing so extreme that I’d want to start any rumors. Just… I realized I don’t really know much about Donvath beyond what I’ve read in books, or what he’s personally told me,” Asher admitted. “It’s sort of because you’re not a noble that I wanted to ask you. You’ve worked as a slayer in the Noala Kingdom for decades, since before Donvath was even born. Surely you must know a thing or two about him, or his family, or whatever.”

“Yeah, I remember his father,” Mornamol admitted, flicking away some earwax as he grunted at the memory. “Good ruler, that man. His name was Ophello. He wasn’t quite as fruity as Donvath from what I’ve heard, he was much more serious. Though I suppose he sort of had to be. Things were a bit dicier back then. The kingdom had lost one of its greatest fighters to old age, and Winged Death hadn’t made a name for herself yet. With only a single greater power to our name, the Lambit Republic was much more aggressive in pushing boundaries. I was sent on more than one mission by the slayer guild to take out advance scouting units.”

“Wait, slayers can be instructed to kill people?” Asher asked, blinking in shock at the news. “I thought you hunted monsters?”

“We hunt whatever we’re told to hunt,” Mornamol shrugged. “Sometimes that includes people who are up to no good. Anyway, things ended up stabilizing on their own eventually. Winged Death’s power became well known, and shortly after receiving his origin element, Donvath started making a name for himself. There was a brief period of time, a good couple of years, where Donvath and his father practically ruled the kingdom side-by-side. Then, not long after Donvath became recognized as a greater power in his own right, it happened. Ophello fell ill.”

“He got sick? Seriously? Surely there has to be skills or potions for that sort of thing. Hell, what about Gran?”

“Not ill in the sense that there was something wrong with his body. Ill in the sense that there was something wrong with his mind,” Mornamol corrected him, tapping his head with the blunt side of a dagger he hadn’t been holding a moment ago. “The extent of just how bad it was ended up being kept secret from the people, but I overheard some important nobles talking during one of my missions. From what I heard, the man didn’t just have a screw loose. He was missing the whole workshop. Apparently, it was bad enough that he became a danger not only to himself, but to everyone around him. One moment he’d lash out in violent fits of rage as if he were fighting a war deep in enemy territory, and the next he’d be sobbing his eyes out like he were attending the funeral of a loved one. It got bad enough he was all but locked away in a special room designed to contain him. You can’t just let someone with skills in the third-tier run wild like that or people die.”

“Wow… what happened next?”

“He died,” Mornamol shrugged. “The disease kept progressing until he couldn’t even think for himself. Eventually, he was nothing more than a corpse with a still-beating heart. Rather than keep him like that, Donvath made the impossible choice to allow his father’s life to come to an end. You wanna know the worst part? I haven’t heard any mention of this in ages, but at the time, people were talking about how much of a tragedy it was. Donvath’s grandmother, the ruler of the Noala Kingdom before his father, ended up facing a similar fate. I guess it’s some sort of genetic thing they have to deal with. Poor saps.”

“Poor saps indeed,” Asher muttered, starting to realize why Donvath had seemed so unnerved at the fact that he’d forgotten about the note he’d added for him about Rosh. If his father and grandmother had both passed due to some sort of genetic cognitive disorder, that meant he probably lived his whole life with the fear of the same thing happening to him someday.

However, there was another possibility that Asher couldn’t rule out.

“Mornamol, do you actually know what elements Donvath has?”

“He’s got the Knowledge element, he’s made that one quite clear,” Mornamol said, scratching at his chin with his dagger. “But the other two are national secrets. All I know is that he’s known as The Immortal among the greater powers, so whatever they are, they must be pretty strong.”

Most people don’t know about Donvath’s Time element, so it’s no wonder they think it’s just some sort of genetic disorder. But if the secret to obtaining a Time element is something passed down through his family…

Is that what caused his father and grandmother to go insane?

From the very beginning, Donvath had made it clear that most of the other rulers of kingdoms on their continent knew precisely what element he had, as did the heads of the noble houses in the Noala Kingdom. However, he’d also mentioned how they all knew precisely what came with the element, which was why none of them were jealous of him and what he could do. Asher hadn’t really understood what he’d meant at the time. But if the Time element came with the caveat that whoever gained it would eventually lose their mind and become a danger to themselves and everyone around them…

“Thanks, Mornamol, this has all been really helpful,” Asher admitted. He wasn’t sure what he was going to do with this new information just yet, but at the very least, he’d be able to keep an eye on Donvath and maybe help him if he started to worry. Despite the friendly vibe the High Prince constantly seemed to give off, he was a surprisingly private person. After all the help he’d been to him, Asher wanted to be there to lend the man a shoulder if at all possible. But he couldn’t do it unless Donvath was willing to accept his help.

“Anytime!” Mornamol grinned. “Though fair warning, if I get visited in the night by a couple of truthseekers demanding to know what you and I talked about, I’m going to throw you under the carriage so hard it’ll dislocate my shoulder. I’m retired, I just want to spend my evening drinking booze and throwing dice down in the tavern.”

“And yet here you are, taking my shards and stabbing me repeatedly,” Asher pointed out. “Not exactly what I would call retired activities.”

“Is it so wrong for a man to have a hobby?” Mornamol argued, motioning with his dagger for Asher to step back. “Now get into position! You already paid me for today, so I need to stab you at least a thousand shards worth or you’ll start complaining!”
