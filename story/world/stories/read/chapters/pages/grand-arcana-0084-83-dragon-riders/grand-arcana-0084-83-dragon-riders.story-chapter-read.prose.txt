There were no rules in war. Everything was a flexible guideline that shifted depending on the need. One thing that always remained the same was that regardless of the circumstances, participants were always mercenaries.

When it came to army hierarchy, mercenaries were at the bottom of the pile, just above peasants. The one advantage they had was that, strictly speaking, they weren’t under anyone’s command and, as such, didn’t need to take part in any formation. It all depended on the orders of the nearest noble within the vicinity, and thanks to Eleonor, all the nobles were reluctant to give us orders.

The march started at noon. Thirteen thousand men left the protection of the city and set off in the direction of the mountain. At noon we were joined by a few thousand elves.

The hatred and disgust emanating from the creatures were palpable. It took considerable strength of will for me not to unleash a few thread strikes in their direction. I strongly suspected that they were exerting considerable effort on their part as well. In their eyes, we were cannon fodder they had to endure just long enough until the aelves showed up.

“Remember, we need the dragons,” I told Marcus.

He nodded, but knowing him, I was convinced he was already running the numbers. With an army of thousands, he could level up his blood weapons considerably after a friendly slaughter.

I looked at the sky. There was no sign of any dragons. Of course, there wouldn’t be. The aelves were smart enough to hide their presence until the very last moment. Dealing with jungle elves, they were used to enemies with the eagle eye special. Always flying in from the direction of the sun, they had relied primarily on speed to cause chaos within an army. In my past run, they had routed the remnants of the human forces before we even had a chance to react.

“Greg,” a voice said beside me.

I glanced to the side. It was Cathy. She looked a lot different now in her mercenary attire. The leather armor made her look strange. While she could pull off being a noble, I strongly doubted anyone seriously bought her mercenary act. Then again, they hadn’t seen her shoot.

“They’ll be here soon,” I said. “They always come from the sun, so—”

“That’s not what I wanted to discuss,” she interrupted, forcing me to give her my full attention. “I’ll be taking a different path after the trial is over.”

A different path? I didn’t even know what that meant. Participants didn’t have a choice in where they went. The only freedom we had was whether to be first or last.

“Are you sure?” I asked, maintaining the pretense I knew what she was talking about.

“I don’t know what your story really is, but you’re too far ahead. If I’m to catch up to you and Marcus, I’ll have to make sacrifices.” She looked around.

The rest of our group was resting on the ground, patiently waiting for something to happen. I found it curious that she gave me and Marcus as examples. The woman didn’t even know what an achievement it was to get chosen by Diana. I had known participants to kill each other in the hopes of attracting the attention of a major deity.

“The goddess told me what I need to do in order to progress.”

The only time I had heard similar talk was from Avon. Everyone else focused on only one thing: survival. Hearing conversations of progressing seemed outright weird.

“The next time we meet, I promise to be stronger,” she said.

“I don’t doubt it,” I lied. “For now, just focus on the trial, okay?”

I heard the sound of wings flapping. It was faint, barely noticeable, but the Listener card had amplified it, allowing me to identify them among the many others.

“They’re here!” I shouted, staring right at the sun.

Tears formed on the edges of my eyes as I tried to spot the dots that corresponded to the approaching aelves. After a while, I found them.

Aelf Dragon Rider

Level: 15

Cards: Paladin, Rider, Twelve of Rings, Nine of Chalice, Fourteen of Swords

Specials: Animal Understanding, Fearlessness

Familiar: Snapdragon

Weapon: Aelf Saber

There you are, I thought.

Never before had I seen the status box of a dragon rider. They seemed a lot less threatening than I had imagined. No doubt the trial imposed certain limitations, reducing the number of cards they had, but still… five cards? I had more after my first trial. Granted, two of them were Major which changed the calculation a bit.

“Eight of them,” I said, brushing off the gathering tears. “There might be a second wave behind.”

I looked away from the sun and used the Two of Chalice to heal my eyes. Normally, I would be fine after such a short amount of time, but I didn’t want to risk it affecting my eagle eye.

Everyone prepared for the clash. There was no panic, no abrupt and chaotic actions… they just stood up, drawing their weapons. In terms of the Grand Arcana, they remained rookies, but were a lot better than the ones I had been with during the first years of my previous run.

Seeing us, some of the other soldiers stirred. They had no idea what we really were, but were also mindful that the nobles treated us with such high regard.

“I see them,” Cathy said beside me.

“Shoot a few arrows,” I said. “You don’t have to hit. I just need you to attract their attention.”

The woman nodded. With Diana unable to interfere during the trial, there would be no divine punishment.

The arrows split the air. I watched them fly straight at the leading dragon rider.

“They’ve got snapdragons, so they’ll try to cause as much panic as possible.” I turned to the rest of the group. “You should be fine. It’s a lot better than the Emperor.”

Sparta chuckled.

“I’ll take care of the beasts once they get near,” Reginald said. “Leaving the nobles to you.”

Seconds later, a deafening shriek filled the air. Composed of several dragon screeches merged together, it hit the entire army like an avalanche. Back in my previous run, that had felt like the most terrifying experience possible. Of course, that was long before I had come across the effects of the Emperor.

“Withstand,” I shouted, using the main skill of the card.

My current strength wasn’t enough to fully negate the effects of our attackers, but at least the participants were alright.

Beside me, Cathy tripled her shooting speed. The trickle of arrows turned into a torrent, flying in the general direction of the riders.

They had gotten close enough for me to see them clearly. There remained only eight of them, all dressed in the fancy clothes and dark silver armor typical of aelf nobles. Overconfident in their abilities, they wore no helmets, proudly displaying their pale faces and long silver hair. That was one of the things that I hated most about their kind. Not the cruelty, nor the strength, but the overbearing arrogance.

Serp, feeling like starting the fight with a bang? I asked.

The lack of pestering since his battle with the shark blade had me slightly concerned.

Sure, he replied with lukewarm enthusiasm.

There’s a good chance that there’ll be a treat, I said as if I were convincing a child to do its homework. Just don’t harm any dragons.

What do you take me for, old man? A spark of his rebellious nature shone through. Just like a child, it didn’t take much to bring him back to his usual self. I can kill all of them without touching the dragons!

I only need you to kill the one. I summoned the sword, then thrust it in the direction of the approaching riders.

Even as they were being shot, the snobs didn’t think much of us, relying on their beasts to inflict most of the casualties. In the past, they would wait until the panic had taken root to fly down and reap through the soldiers, killing as many as possible.

Noticing the blade, several of them flew slightly to the side. Likely there was a certain thrill in letting enemy projectiles as close to them as possible. That turned out to be a mistake. A few dozen feet from its target, the serpent blade changed form. The width of the blade doubled, curving in such a way as to take advantage of the air resistance. In a fraction of a second, its trajectory changed, causing the blade to take a sharp turn and strike the dragon rider.

I couldn’t see the face of the aelf, but I imagined what his expression might have been: the sort of disbelief that came seconds before one’s inevitable death.

I could have taken more, you know, Serp said in my mind. Treat was nice. Couldn’t get the cards.

The cards weren’t my concern. I unsummoned the weapon. The whole point of my action was to get the riders’ undivided attention, and by the looks of it, I had. No longer casually flying towards the army, they turned our way. Several drew their swords as they focused on our small group.

A wave of air swept through us. In a single instant the riders had crossed miles, reappearing a mere few hundred feet away. It was just the same thing that my first dragon rider target had done. That’s how we had lost two of our strongest veterans at the time. Having the monsters suddenly emerge had stopped everyone in their tracks, causing them to lose focus in the vital first moments of the fight. Before we could snap out of it, three people were gone. This time, it was they who had fallen into our trap.

“Withstand!” I shouted, using my Emperor card. This time I wasn’t alone.

Several people followed my lead, multiplying the effect.

Oblivious, the aelves continued approaching. Some let out thread slashes.

Standing perfectly still, I watched the threads of destruction mow through the mass of soldiers behind us. The unfortunate souls never stood a chance, having neither the means to block nor the speed to attack.

Still the same tricks, I thought.

The dragon riders were so arrogant that they didn’t want to waste mass attacks, choosing to finish us off close and personal.

“You can use mega spells, right?” I whispered to Cathy.

“How…” she turned to me. “Yes, I can.”

“Wind only,” I said. “Follow my lead.”

The dragons swooped down right at us. Each had chosen their specific target, with three showing me the honor of choosing me as their prey.

Got you! I cast a mega spell.

The giant spell circle formed on the ground, sending a torrent of wind upwards. A split second later, a second one followed. Cathy had understood my intent and copied the spell.

All seven dragons spontaneously shot up like paper planes flying by an industrial aircon vent. The shock was strong enough to send them off their mounts.

“Get the beasts!” I ordered as I summoned Serp again.

Reginald was already ahead of me. Half the snapdragons broke off, flying towards us. The rest scattered, flapping eagerly to safety. Similar to their owners, they too hadn’t encountered any real resistance.

How does it feel to be the prey now? I threw the serpent blade at one of the disoriented elves.

“Kill them all!” I shouted.

The massacre that followed was burned into my mind. It wasn’t the first time that I had seen creatures torn to shreds. By far it wasn’t the largest or the most gruesome. In the ten years of my previous run, I had seen slaughters that had kept me awake for months. However, this was the first time I had witnessed players achieving such a feat.

These weren’t goblins or undead we were dealing with. Before us were what could be considered aelf elites. In a one-on-one fight, they should have presented a nearly insurmountable obstacle. Even as I watched, I expected them to deflect most of our attacks unscathed. Nothing of the sort occurred.

Confused and separated from their dragons, the nobles didn’t get a chance to react as arrows and aether projectiles drilled through them. Unleashing her power as a deity’s champion, Cathy pierced through armor and flesh, killing two aelves in the blink of an eye. Eleonor followed with her crude, yet effective “drill” attack, bursting holes in anything she set her sights on.

Marcus and Sparta also joined in charging at our opponents. Leaping into the air, they took advantage of the constant air current from the spell circles and proceeded to disembowel the ones that were left out. The psychopath’s blood dagger ripped through the creature’s throat, while Sparta used her thread daggers to elegantly slice and dice another, keeping some distance between them.

During all that, Serp spread out like a vine of steel, using both the living and the dead to constantly grow.

One after another, elves burst into red smoke.

SERPENT BLADE (Grand Arcana)

New skill acquired:

VENOM STRIKE: strikes performed with the SERPENT BLADE inflict poison

You greedy little jerk. I smiled to myself.

He had practically stolen all the booster spheres, though in a way that no one could have any complaints.

“Get the cards!” I shouted. “We get them, then we storm the castle!”
