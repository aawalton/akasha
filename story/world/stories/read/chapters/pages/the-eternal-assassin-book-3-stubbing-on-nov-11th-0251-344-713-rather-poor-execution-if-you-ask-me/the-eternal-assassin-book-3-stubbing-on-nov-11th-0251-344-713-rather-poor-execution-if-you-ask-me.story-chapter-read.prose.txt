Asher’s awareness returned to him much quicker than it had the last time as he suddenly found himself drifting within that empty galaxy of space stretching out for eternity in all directions. Yet again, he was without a body, or he would have sighed when he realized what had happened. Something had killed him without warning. He probably should have been more concerned about the fact that he’d just been murdered in the middle of the street in the capital, but he’d died so many times by now that one more death wasn’t really that big of a deal. He was far more worried about the fact that Samantha was now forced to deal with his attacker all on her own, but she was more than capable. Months of training and fighting constant life-or-death battles had honed her abilities like no other. And at absolute worst, she could simply take off and fly away.

That said, he still wanted to get back and help her as fast as possible.

Hello again! It’s me! He tried thinking toward the space around him, knowing that the deity would ‘hear’ him without issue. I don’t suppose there’s any chance you can send me back, is there?

Just like it had the last time, Asher felt that gentle tendril of power stretch out and touch the back of his mind, bringing forth a memory of his and emphasizing a few select portions in order to communicate. In the memory, he and Samantha were in the floating city of Poltar, eating dinner with Sefta after accidentally reuniting the noblewoman with her mother.

“Girl, that’s not how I swim,” Sefta snorted, raising an eyebrow as she started on her second plate of food. “Trust me, I’m built for fighting. I don’t do that whole settling down thing, and if I had to put up with a smaller version of myself, I’d probably try to drown them.”

The memory faded, and Asher put the pieces together as he focused on the deity once more.

You don’t do that whole settling down thing… So you don't have any sway over when I actually go back to my body when I die?

Asher suddenly found himself walking through Whikoga with Kree, the young thief with a Shadow element who he’d made an impression on months back. Kree was currently working under Draken, but he had been quite helpful back when Asher had been trying to find Eight to take the Finger down.

“Nope, just been a little busy,” past-Asher said, double checking his Incorporated map of the different gangs within the city as he corrected his course. “A lot has happened since we last spoke…”

No kidding, Asher said after the memory ended. He wished he had a head to shake at the understatement. My life tends to be filled with a little more excitement than that of the average person’s from what I’ve seen. A sudden thought struck him, and Asher grew worried as he asked his next question. Last time I was here with you, almost a month passed by in the real world while a new body was being created for me. I’m not about to miss out on another month, right? I mean, my death happened pretty fast, but I’m pretty certain it was just a head wound. That’s not all that much that my body needs to regenerate.

Asher was whipped back to the disease-riddled forest, where he watched Sedrick the ancient druid make his pitch for Samantha to become his pupil a second time.

“That will let me find you,” Sedrick explained, giving Samantha a warm smile after growing the wooden bracelet around her wrist. “Do not fret. It is not an artifact, merely a facet of one of my skills. You and you alone can break it quite easily if you so desire, but should you decide you wish to learn from me, simply hold the bracelet against a tree and I will take care of the rest. Take as much time as you need, but not too long!”

Do not fret… you break quite easily… but I will take care of the rest… you need not too long… Asher repeated, rolling his nonexistent eyes at the unneeded comment.

Yeah, well, I imagine flesh and blood bodies tend to break a little more easily than… whatever the hell you are, he countered, trying and failing for the umpteenth time to observe the deity he was ‘talking’ to. It was as if the deity was the vast, infinite space he was drifting around within. As if it were simultaneously all around him, and yet nowhere all at the same time. Still, thank you for all your help. I know that you sort of only exist because I do, according to what you told me the last time I was here at least, but I appreciate the help nonetheless.

Asher found himself sitting on the back of a large mogrant with Samantha and Brian as the three of them ate their caravan-provided meals on their way to Poltar. They’d just finished up a bout of wrestling, and Asher felt a warmth spread through his disembodied self while he took in the happy, content looks on all their faces as they enjoyed each other’s company.

“Thanks for the help,” Brian whispered to him.

“Anytime,” past-Asher whispered back, retrieving their meals and handing them out. “Though I will say, I’m looking forward to seeing you come back from that academy strong enough to take on your sister without my help one day.”

Asher opened his eyes, startled to suddenly find himself with a physical body once more. Switching between that strange, disembodied state and a physical one without warning was jarring beyond belief, and he blinked as he spotted Samantha’s angry face looking down at him, her focus on the side of his head.

“Well you don’t look happy,” Asher coughed, causing Samantha to jump. Letting out a sigh of relief, she leaned down and gave him a tender kiss.

“That might have something to do with the fact that I just watched my fiancé get shot in the head right next to me,” she pointed out, holding a bloody crossbow bolt over him.

“Fair enough,” Asher said as he sat up and took the bolt from her hands. Giving it a curious look, he turned it over in his fingers.

[Crossbow bolt] - Iron

The bolt wasn’t enchanted by any means, and the material wasn’t even anything unique. Whoever had shot him had either been relying on a skill in order to put him down for good, or they didn’t have even the first clue about what he was capable of. While Asher did his best to keep his Eternity element under wraps, all the people he could currently think of who might want him dead should have known that he could shake off a crossbow bolt to the head like it was nothing.

“What happened after I went down?” Asher asked, finally noting that the two of them were back in their bedroom in the palace. “Did you see the attacker?”

“No, I was just as surprised as you were,” Samantha admitted with a frown. “The first thing I did after you got shot was manifest my wings and use Winged Shield’s second-tier effect to cocoon the two of us. Once I realized you were already dead, I dropped the skill, but by the time I could see our surroundings again whoever had attacked you was already gone. After that I flew us back here, where I yanked the bolt out and started using Soothing Spring to speed up your recovery.”

“Thank you for that,” Asher smiled. “And don’t worry about not spotting the attacker, you made the right call focusing on defense first. Let’s just be glad they shot me instead of you. I know you prefer keeping a low profile as much as I do, but it might be smart for you to start keeping your wings manifested more often than not.”

“I know,” Samantha sighed. “Though doing so does take a little bit of energy, so I can’t exactly keep them out all day even if I wanted to. More importantly, who in the world tried to kill you? And why?”

“Your guess is honestly as good as mine,” Asher said, turning his focus back on the crossbow bolt. “My first thought would be the Head finally coming out of hiding after all this time, but I don’t know why they’d pick now of all times when there are so many heavy hitters in the capital, and this isn’t their style in the slightest. I’d expect Owl to snipe me from a mile away with a silent arrow, not some dirty crossbow bolt to the temple. And no way this was the Council. This is way too simple, and they would have done whatever it took to retrieve my body for their extraction ritual.”

“Then who does that leave? The only other ones who tried to kill you recently were the Shadow Squad members from the Lambit Republic, but they’re part of the coalition now. They wouldn’t try again while we’re working together, would they?”

“I don’t know…” Asher muttered, thinking back to Hounth and how annoyed he seemed to even be a part of the coalition in the first place. “Hounth did come right out and tell us he didn’t want to be here. Maybe the Lambit Republic only joined the coalition in the first place to try and get another shot at me after their Shadow Squad failed. Though after all the prep and resources that went into the first hit, this one seems pretty pathetic. I mean, they left my body behind. Even if they didn’t know about Eternal Regeneration, surely I’m important enough that they would expect Donvath to shell out the shards for a healer to bring me back from the dead, wouldn’t they? I just don’t get it.”

“That’s still something that takes time,” Samantha pointed out. “Maybe this was just some sort of message? I mean, isn’t this the whole reason why the nobles keep the Assassin’s Guild around in the first place? They don’t treat death like a permanent thing.”

“Or maybe they wanted to take me out of commission for a little while,” Asher muttered, scratching his head with the bolt as he tried to piece it all together. “It would take Donvath time to get a healer all the way over here capable of resurrection. Other than Gran, I think there are only one or two others with skills capable of doing so in the entire kingdom, and they’re probably constantly moving around to help all the noble families out. But if that was the case, what benefit would they gain from me being dead for a couple of days?”

“Can you use Eternal Knowledge on the bolt?” Samantha asked after a moment. “Sorry, your skill is so new I keep forgetting you even have it.”

“Tried that already, no dice,” Asher admitted, grinning sheepishly at her as he spun the bolt between his fingers. “It only works on objects of personal importance. Ironically, it would seem I’m so used to dying and coming back at this point that I don’t even view the bolt used to kill me as an important object. I’m hoping when I evolve the skill it will become a bit more flexible, but for now, I’ve got nothing. We could try bringing it to Rulfar and see if he could track down whoever shot this, but I can't imagine they were that incompetent not to take such a basic precaution.”

“So what do we do?” Samantha asked. “Just act like nothing happened?”

“Well, it is pretty late,” Asher shrugged before tossing the bolt into his Personal Rift. “The palace itself is so heavily guarded I doubt anyone else is in any real danger. And even if something big did happen, Donvath would just use Timeline to undo it. Weirdly enough, my soul actually feels a little bit better after dying and coming back. Why don’t we call it a night and see if anything interesting happens? If it doesn't we'll ask Rulfar for help, just to cover our bases.”

“Only you would think grabbing some sleep after being assassinated was the right choice of action,” Samantha snorted. “You seriously want to just leave things as is?”

“Just for the moment,” Asher decided as he started getting ready for bed. “I’ll give Donvath a quick call just to inform him what happened and let him know to be on the lookout for anything, but I think that’s all we really need to do. The other team should be contacting me before too much longer to finish off the lich they were sent after, and I’d like to heal up as much soul damage as possible before that happens. Let’s just wait and see if any of them look surprised to see me when I show up!”
