It can’t be Horace. It can’t. It… certainly looks like him, but… It’s not even dark yet. He’s sitting in sunlight, for salt’s sake!

The owl perched half-in-shadow on a still fir branch. Mellow golden light glinted off one sleek wing and traced the silver curve of his talons.

He was perfectly still. Perfectly calm. Watching the scene before him with all the disinterest of an actual owl.

Because it is an actual owl. Of course it is. It just looks similar.

“Brin?” Royce waved a hand in front of my face.

I blinked rapidly, disentangling myself from his grip and taking a step back. “I heard you. Saints, I heard.” My skin prickled with the heat of another gaze, one I just knew was boring into me from the side, but I couldn’t bring myself to look at Renner.

How many? How many people has he killed? How much blood is on his hands?

Thom’s face flashed before my eyes. So sudden and clear that I jumped, and a deluge of other unwanted sensations followed the image. The crushing pressure of his weight on my stomach, his ragged cries of pain and rage. The throbbing heat of bruised skin and fresh blood.

I could never fault him for killing Thom. I’m glad he killed Thom! Or… or at least I should be. Of course I should be.

And what if they were all like him? People who would’ve beaten someone to death, or dragged them away to be devoured in the night? What right have I to… to…

The words all made sense. The reasoning was there, voicing its logic with perfect calm, but my heart refused to stop pounding.

“Renner, w-why...” My throat felt like it was full of mud. Thick and dry, every word struggling to claw its way up. I blinked rapidly and took a shaky breath. “Why are you telling us this?”

For a long moment the only answer was hissing, shadowed pine boughs in the evening breeze. Finally he said in a rough voice, “We haven’t known each other very long. I’m not a good man-”

Royce rounded on him. “You’re a murderer!”

“Yeah, I am. Dozen times over. But my point is that I never enjoyed it. I just slept easier knowing I made someone die quicker than they would in Pinnacle.” Leaves crunched as he stepped towards us, voice dropping low. “That’s why I’m telling you. All three of you. You can hate me, think the worst, whatever you want. I’m a salted murderer, but I’m taking the moral high ground when it comes to consecration. Let that mean something to you.”

“All this means is that we shouldn’t be listening to you!” Royce sputtered. His voice rang off the trees. “That we should be doing the opposite of what you say!”

The owl blinked. Calmly, slowly, still undisturbed by the noise, and sweat pooled at my temples as I looked up again. I met the little bird’s gaze and, when it held mine in return, stepped back with an audible gulp.

“What?” Renner followed my eyes, one hand dropping to a knifehilt. He examined the bird, then frowned at me. “What’s wrong?”

And still, it didn’t look away. Soft gold highlighted the growing curve of a too-sharp beak. A tiny, sly smile.

My heartbeat swelled to a roar. “Don’t…” I whispered, bringing a hand to Royce’s shoulder. Trembling fingers curled into muscle, drawing him back. “Don’t make him angry.”

Royce threw his hands skyward. “Why, ‘cause he’ll murder us? Like all those other people who should’ve had a trial before he slit their-”

“Shut up.” Metal missed. Renner shifted back, still following my gaze with his own. “Brin?”

I couldn’t seem to find my voice. The owl’s smile grew and Renner went stiff.

“Don’t-!”

It was too late. His hand snapped out. The dagger flicked towards the tree, flashing silver amidst evening shadows. The flicker was almost too fast to follow… until it wasn’t. His aim was true, but the blade never met its mark. It slowed and sank before the branch, as if he’d thrown it through water instead of air.

And then it fell. Wicked, impotent metal glinted on dark moss as the four of us gaped.

Stillness reigned for one breath. Then Renner swore, Teela dove behind a tree, Royce shook his head in disbelief, and I lunged. My hands closed over Renner’s arm as he grabbed his crossbow with one hand and a belted witchwood bolt with the other.

“Wait!” I cried, shoving his wrist down. Not for fear of the Fae being harmed, but for fear of what retaliation could follow.

Familiar, soft laughter filled the air. “Hello, morsel.”

Renner yanked his arm free, slammed the bolt into place, and with a click and a hiss a shaft of silver flashed past. Once again, it slowed upon nearing the tree and fell to the moss below.

He swore again, stepping back. His gaze darted to my pack and I half-wondered if he was considering throwing the shard next.

“Wait,” I said again, turning to face the feathered Fae. I eyed him warily, breath trapped in my throat as I waited for any sign of displeasure or anger.

“Wait?! Wait for what?! It can talk!” Teela squeaked from behind her tree.

Horace chuckled.

I swallowed. Right. He’s here. Talking. Here. In the sunlight, no less, and… and… and I should remember to be polite this time. “Um… good evening, Horace.”

“Good evening, little one.”

“Um.” Royce sounded vaguely strangled. He stepped back, skin white as milk and blue eyes bulging. “Brin?”

My chest swelled. “H-Horace, are you… that is, um… you’re… here?”

Round, shining eyes blinked. “I am.”

“In the… l-light.”

“Mm.” He sidestepped further into the sunlight. Sleek sable shone beneath the gold. “Anduin and I have no quarrel.”

I opened and closed my mouth, entirely at a loss.

Renner cocked another bolt, aiming at the owl, but didn’t fire. “What is this thing?”

“It’s… Horace,” I croaked, feeling faint. Because if this little Fae from the Night Court could casually appear in the mortal realm, in the sunlight because apparently he and the salted sun ‘had no quarrel,’ then everything I’d always known about monsters simply wasn’t true and-

“I gathered that,” he snarled, prowling to the side. Each step crushed loud, crinkly leaves. “I was hoping for a more specific answer.”

Horace chuckled. “Oh, I’m not here for answers. Only advice.”

I sagged against a pine. Took a moment to catch my breath as the Fae watched me with obvious amusement.

“Um,” Royce said again.

A slightly hysterical giggle rose up my throat at that. I cleared it quickly and straightened. “Horace,” my voice somehow remained steady as I looked up, biting down on a swell of dread, “You… you didn’t hurt Slip, did you? After I left?”

He cooed. A hundred crickets and evening songbirds chirped in response, bringing the forest around us to momentary, vibrant life before they all fell quiet again. The curve of his beak widened. “I did not.”

“Good. That’s… that’s good.” My lungs filled with brisk air as I fought to calm my racing pulse.

Teela’s red eyes and shadowed hat appeared from behind a pine. She peered up, jaw hanging open.

The Fae twisted his head to face her. The dark feathers of his neck ruffled. “Mm. You are lovely. No wonder the hag was so enamored.” Silver talons clicked against the wood. “It’s been an age since I’ve smelled kobolos blood.”

Hiss. Another bolt whistled through the air and, as before, fell.

“Perhaps your fourth attempt will succeed,” Horace said wryly as Teela ducked back behind her tree.

“Please stop, Renner.” I crept forwards as the man in question growled another oath. “Horace, you’re… not here to hurt any of us, are you?”

He chuffed a laugh. “No, little one. Only to watch.”

“What are you watching?”

“Mm. Vultures gather in the aftermath of a disaster. I prefer to linger before. To… savor.”

I gnawed my lower lip. “Saints, are you… do you mean the city? Sedgewick Vale?”

He blinked slowly and offered no answer.

I eyed him for a long while, mind racing. “Well… you said you could offer, er, advice? Is it for one of us?”

“For all of you.”

My pulse stuttered. “For… you want to… help us?” I shook my head. Wintry air bit at my cheeks and nose. “Why?”

He let out another little coo, but this time it held a hollow note. Mournful, almost, and the forest sang sadly in response. “Why does anyone do anything, Brin? I wish to see a future come to pass. Right now it’s… more distant than I’d like. Than I promised.”

My mind raced. The future. Saints, is that how he knows so much? My rune, my name… is he like my mom? Does he have visions? “Horace, can you… s-see the future?”

“Versions of it,” he sighed, sounding impossibly weary. The shadow of a smile crossed his feathered face again, but there was no sharp, wry cast to the look. He simply looked sad. “And one in particular I’d like to guide you towards.”

My mouth went dry. “With… advice?”

“We’re taking advice from a monster. Sure.” Renner’s voice was dark. One hand now held the witchwood knife and he prowled a half-circle, as if debating how quickly he could climb the tree and stab our visitor. “Why not?”

“You needn’t listen, though I’ll advise nonetheless.” The Fae snapped his beak. His soft voice hardened, falling like a stone. “Vengeance lies close at hand, little man. Its eyes are empty. It will not know your face. Expect no salvation.”

Renner froze, squinting up. His jaw went tight and he said nothing.

Royce cleared his throat. “Is… is that the… er, could you… be more specific?”

Horace chuckled. “Oh, that was only for him. For you…” he flapped his wings once, landing before the blonde man with a rustle of feathers.

My friend paled even further, inching back from the diminutive figure. “Um-”

“The eloquence of men is astounding.” The owl’s soft voice was as dry as the leaves beneath him. “Folly and love go hand in hand, young one. But fools who throw themselves into fire may be glad to burn.”

Royce looked at me helplessly. “Er… that doesn’t sound very good.”

Teela poked her head out again. She regarded the owl with a mixture of wary fear and obvious curiosity. “Do I get one, too?”

He chuckled. “You do not. You don’t need it. But I would like to thank you.”

“You… huh?”

He hummed, talons curling into the soil. “The steps of mortals are ever uneven. They pause, stumble, and veer so very often. Points in time that trail away and muddle the paths to…” he grew very still, then shook his head as if to clear it. The sly smile returned. “Mm. A hundred paths lead to the door, yet at its threshold your choice never falters. You always follow. She was grateful, and… I suppose I am, as well.”

She blinked. “Huh?”

Silence was the only answer. Finally he turned to me, feathers cast almost fully in shadow and gaze heavy.

I swallowed and, with a lightness I did not feel, said, “Horace, I’m not sure I can take any more ominous predictions from you. I’m still worried about my heart being eaten.”

He huffed another laugh. “A troubling thought, indeed. No, Brin, I only wanted to remind you of something.”

“Oh? What’s that?”

“Mercy, little one. Mercy and cruelty. Your heart may love one as it loathes the other.” He regarded me gravely. “But remember this, mortal… sometimes, they wear the same face.”
