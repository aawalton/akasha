Magda, Paragon of the Sliding Surge, was old. Old enough to have recognized exactly what Kade was seconds after her initial attack failed. This man wasn’t simply a threat to her. He was a threat to everything she knew, relied on, and convinced herself she loved.

So, as his guard slipped, Magda made her move. The sword dropped, and she was close. Very close. Close enough that her smooth skin could almost touch his armor. This close, her scent would be in his nose, and that would buy her time to finish the job.

She lunged. Her body shifted.

And then—

Fear and pain.

No.

Terror.

Agony.

She hit the tangled wooden floor of her home, screaming in pain. Her eyes locked on a twitching, purple-and-green fin. The part that should have connected to her flank was…burnt.

Fear. Fear overwhelmed her. Not just fear from the shadow mage’s aura, or even of Kade’s sword as it punched through her skin and left a burning, twitching wound in its wake. She screamed again, but the agony was secondary. It was fear that this lunatic, this maniac of a delver, had seen her ambush, been ready for it, and countered it flawlessly. Fear that her lover and master might both be in danger.

They had to be warned.

She had to flee.

Square chunks of her flesh burned. The shadow mage had cast something. Skin and muscle vanished. More pain wracked Magda’s body.

She made a choice. Threw herself into the water. Became part of it as she dove. The water filled the square holes in her flank and tail. Her missing fin tried to beat against it and propel her deeper. As far down as she could go, then make for the exit. She’d flee to the God of Thunder first. Plead her case to him. Between her master and her lover, she’d rely on her lover’s mercy any time.

As she dove, though, something shifted.

Her Mana started to drop. Static filled the brackish water around her. She looked up at the flickering ocean surface overhead. It was dark. Black. Like night.

The lightning exploded around her, boiling the seawater as a blossom of electricity rippled out from her. It pulsed over and over, and Magda sank to the bottom of her borrowed portal world. Darkness took her before she hit the mud below.

Ellen and I left quickly.

Our exit from the purple-tinted portal only met one delay. As we approached it, a powerful aura entered. Stronger than the Paragon I’d just killed. I recognized it even as it washed over the world. Vern Kellen had arrived.

“Mission accomplished,” I whispered through gritted teeth as I suppressed my own. We found the floating corpse of a treant near the portal and waited as close to a dozen Guardians pushed into the portal—including their second-in-command. The A-Ranker was still battered and bruised from my attack on the portal, and as her glare washed over our hiding spot, I tried not to meet her eyes.

It’d be a hell of a fight to escape the portal with all twelve Guardians on us.

Then they were gone, pushing toward the floating wooden island. We slipped out of the portal, hit the three A and B-Rankers guarding it like a thunderbolt, and disappeared into the humid, oversized rows of corn and wheat. The mission had been a success.

With Vern Kellen back in Wickenberg, the Guardians wouldn’t have the firepower to push against the Portal Tyrants. Even better, they’d overplayed their hand, and Jessie would—when she woke up and felt better—work on bringing the Coyotes and Roadrunners back into the GC’s alliance. With all four guilds, we’d all be able to protect each other if they tried something else.

In the longer term, Vern Kellen might be a problem. But for now, he was as solved as he was going to get.

By the time Ellen and I got back to the Desert Wind Building, the mud and salt had dried in the Phoenix heat, forming a stinking crust over our skin and Ellen’s robes. Jessie took one look at us and shook her head. “Showers. Now.”

“Why are you awake?” I asked.

She rolled her eyes. “Because the Portal Tyrant’s going to be here in twenty minutes. Get rinsed off and presentable. Hurry up.” My sister was still in her pajamas, sitting in her wheelchair and looking exceptionally grumpy.

“He’s coming here? Now?” Ellen shook her head. “Seriously?”

“Yes. He contacted me five minutes ago. Debrief from the ‘guild war,’ he said.”

“Fine. Let’s go.” Ellen led the way, not to the elevator, but to the guild’s locker room. There, we found our workout clothes, stripped down, and took quick showers. Five minutes later, we were up the elevator, swamp-stained gear in the laundry, and heading for fresh, presentable clothes.

“This is killing me, Kade,” Ellen whispered. “I’m ready for S-Rank.”

“We could just let Jessie deal with Terrel Young while you push to S-Rank. It’s a tough one, but even so, it doesn’t take too long. I’ll coach you through it. But…”

“No. Your sister has no business being awake. She’s in a ton of pain, and if I had my way, we’d handle the Portal Tyrants ourselves while she slept. I doubt she’ll let us, though, so we need to be there to help her out. Besides, I’m curious about the rest of the fighting. How Jeff and Yasmin did, and who got hurt and killed. I’ll wait. It’s just killing me, that’s all.”

“Your call.” I leaned in and kissed Ellen’s forehead. “Thanks, by the way.”

“For what? You did everything for me tonight.”

“For helping with Jessie so much. I know she can be…a handful, but—“

“Kade,” Ellen said. She put her head on my shoulder and rested it there briefly, then looked me in the eye. “Kade Noelstra, you care about your sister, and so do I. We’re in it for the long haul. That means dealing with each other’s families—whether they’re money-mad CEOs or secret hacker pre-teens.”

“She’s sixteen,” I said automatically, then sighed. “I see your point, though. Let’s get this over with, then we can push you to S-Rank after things calm down. It’ll be interesting to see the process when there’s less pressure involved.”

Ellen shot me a look, then shook her head. “Ignore that. Terrel Young is probably waiting by now.”

The Portal Tyrant’s axes were both notched. His armor was cracked in half a dozen spots, and one of his eyes was swollen completely shut. “Gotta say, when Guildmaster Gerald—“

“You can call me Jessie,” my sister said, forcing the pain out of her voice.

“Fine. When Jessie called to tell me you were launching an attack on the Wickenberg Fields, I almost switched sides right there! When she said you were going after the portal, it was even closer! But you’re a madman, Kade, and it paid off. We lost a handful of delvers with longer-term injuries, but no one’s dead on either side.”

“Is that…normal…for a guild war?” Ellen asked.

“Not zero deaths,” Terrel said. “But no, they’re usually not high-casualty affairs.”

The four of us—plus a half-dozen men and women in white lab coats—sat around a conference table near Jessie’s office. We’d never used it before, to my knowledge. Every one of our in-guild meetings had been couch-and-chair affairs. But hosting an S-Rank guild leader required a little more formality.

Terrel belched loudly enough to shake the windows.

Not much more formality, but a little bit.

“Do we need to be concerned about the Guardians making another move?” Jessie asked. “I’m going to…”

“You okay, Guildmaster Gerald?” Terrel asked, unblackened eyebrow raising.

“Yes.” Jessie winced and squeezed her eyes shut. I watched her breathe. After a few seconds, she shook her head. “No. You know about my joint condition. It’s especially bad tonight. I’m sorry, but I’ve got to—“

“It’s fine,” Terrel said before I could open my mouth. “I’ll debrief with Delvers Noelstra and Traynor. They can get you caught up tomorrow. The Guardians won’t make another move, though. Not tonight. Kellen’s not usually this impulsive.”

“That’d be his second-in-command,” I said.

Jessie slowly wheeled herself to the door before Ellen stood. “I’ll help you get back to your room. Be back soon.”

“Thanks,” my sister said.

As Ellen and Jessie made the trek up to Jessie’s bedroom, I debriefed the Portal Tyrant on Gina Smithers, Magda the Sliding Surge, and the events inside the Wickenberg Portal. I didn’t keep Ellen’s impending S-Rank status a secret, and I told him almost everything Magda had told me. “I don’t think we can trust that monster, though. She’d aligned with both of my biggest enemies.”

“And she didn’t actually get any information from you,” Terrel pointed out.

“True. I think she was just trying to get close enough to kill me quickly, in an ambush. If she’d succeeded, Ellen would probably have been in trouble. She’s not built for one-on-one fighting.”

“An Angelo-typed mage?” Terrel asked. Grief flashed across his face for a second, then faded just as quickly. “Take care of her. Mages like that are powerful, but they tend to be glass cannons. Even someone like the Light of Dawn can just…die.”

“I’m sorry.” I didn’t know what else to say, and the Portal Tyrant didn’t say anything until Ellen rejoined us.

Then he cleared his throat. “I’m not only here to debrief. These six doctors—Reynolds, Quintana, Perot, Miyazaki, Nabers, and Elm—are the Portal Tyrants’ core break team. When I told them you two had both had partial or full breaks repaired, via different methods, they were wildly interested. Of course, they’ve also dug through what you shared with us before, Kade, but under the current circumstances, we’re hoping for a more complete picture of both of your breaks.”

I stared across the table, then closed my eyes and sighed. We’d committed to this, after all. It was the cost of doing business, and of making allies. “Alright. Let’s do this. I’ve got just one request. There’s a delver—Garrett Fritch. He was a delver with the Traynor Corporation’s guild, but he got pushed all the way to core break. I think he’s still alive, but his brother blames me for his break, and it’d be great if, when you figure this out, you try to fix him.”

“Will do,” Terrel said. “Now spill it.”

Ellen and I spilled it. The process took a good, solid hour, with all six doctors digging into us for answers, clarifications, and as much information about the devices we’d used and the processes we’d endured to fix our cores. Then, when they finally ran out of questions, Terrel raised a hand. “I think that’s enough. I’ll have the R&D team email you any further questions as they dig into the repair process more. You’ve got a lot on your plate, Kade. We appreciate the help with the Guardians and with the core break issue.”

“One last question,” I said as we stood. “Do you have a good long-term plan for the Guardians?”

“No. The big guilds are hard to displace. I’ve got a feeling Vern Kellen’s going to be in charge out there for a while, and based on what you’ve said, it’d be better to keep him than to let his second-in-command take over. The best play might be to let him know what she’s been up to.” He stuck out a massive hand. “Thanks again, and if you need me, I’ll do what I can against your monsters.”

“You’ve got it, Terrel. Thanks.” I waited until he and his scientists left, then headed for the elevator, Ellen right behind me.

It was time.

Ellen’s core had been humming for hours.

Vibrating inside of her.

It was…not painful. Not even uncomfortable. But…like she’d eaten just a little too much, and she could feel the fullness. The shadow spread through her body, and some—but not all—of her focus had to be on holding it back. She’d ignored it in the portal, then ignored it while she cleaned up, took care of Kade’s little sister, and sat through the meeting.

But now…

Now, she could truly focus on it.

She sat, cross-legged, on the empty floor, cool air blowing across her shoulders and seeping up from the tiles into her legs. The lights were off, the shades were drawn, and the very world around her reflected the shadows within her core. She breathed, centering herself on her shadow core. In and out, in and out.

Kade stood nearby, watching, body completely still. When she opened her eyes after ten minutes, she couldn’t even tell he was breathing.

“Are you ready?” he asked quietly.

She shook her head. “No. There’s one thing that bothers me.”

It was a little detail. They’d been in the Wickenberg Portal for maybe two hours. A drop in the bucket as far as portal time went. And in that time, they’d killed a lot of monsters. But…

“What’s up?” Kade asked. He moved, and his hand was on her shoulder as he squatted down behind her. “Nervous?”

“Not about this. Not at all.”

“Good, because you’re ready. We’ll get you through this, get you to S-Rank, and from there…”

“From there, you’ve got two massively powerful monsters for us to fight,” Ellen said quickly. “I’m not worried about them, either.”

“Then what’s up?”

Ellen paused. Then she stood. “Did you actually kill Magda?”

Kade’s arms stiffened around her. She tensed, too. For a long time, the room was silent. Then Kade shook his head. “I…don’t know. Let’s get you to S-Rank, and do it fast. If she’s one of the Placid King’s servants…”

“Then he’s going to come for revenge, and do it soon,” Ellen finished. She closed her eyes, breathed, and let the S-Rank trial begin. And behind her, Kade sank into his own trance. They’d be as ready as either of them could be.

Ellen just hoped it’d be enough.

As she drifted through the water, Magda’s Health slowly, excruciatingly healed her wounds.

The puncture wounds in her flank and chest. The cubes of missing flesh. The burns that ran in spiderwebs of agony all across her beautiful purple and turquoise skin. Magda had been gorgeous. Fins the color of an oil slick in the sun. Lithe, smooth body. The scars would fade with time, but this fight had been one of the most disastrous in her life.

Not that she could see the damage. Her eyes had been destroyed by Kade Noelstra’s attack, and they healed slowly. For the longest time, she floated, blind and unable to move properly with a fin that stubbornly refused to regrow.

She’d never been this helpless.

She’d never been this furious.

But most importantly, she’d never been this afraid.

Was that monster still in her world? What did her current condition mean for her deal with the Guardians? Would her core still satisfy the Thunder God? And would the Placid King—

Something touched her.

She twitched, trying to escape, but whatever it was, it wrapped around her and dragged her through her brackish home. She hit a barrier. Fell through it. The water was suddenly cold all around her.

“You encountered the newest Paragon, Magda of the Sliding Surge, did you not? You will tell me everything you learned from that meeting, and then, when you are finished, you will tell your paramour the exact same thing.” The Placid King’s voice echoed in Magda’s head. “This may be an opportunity. For your sake, you had better hope it is.”

Magda shivered. She still couldn’t see. Still couldn’t move. All she could do was talk—and talk she did.
