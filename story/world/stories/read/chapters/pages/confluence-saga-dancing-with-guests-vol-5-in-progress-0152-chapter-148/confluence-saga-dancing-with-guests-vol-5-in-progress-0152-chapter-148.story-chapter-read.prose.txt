Sarna sighed as she massaged her temples, her elbows rattling the table. The class hadn't even been here for a year and already she was wondering where it would end up. Normally that question didn't come up before they were at least level 18. If she was honest it probably wouldn't come up for most of it, but the fact that there were two outliers, three if Leo ever managed to get his mentee settled, meant plans had to be shifted around.

You couldn't have tournaments to rank the students when anyone paying attention already knew who would be taking the top spots. The solo duels might be interesting, well the top eight might. Duos? They'd have to remove all restrictions and then have every instructor on standby and ready to intervene. Full groups wouldn't be any better.

"Duels?" Leo asked, coming up behind her.

"Tournaments. I just can't see any way to make them-"

"Duels. Just say it. You're losing sleep over duels because you don't want to be seen playing favorites. That's why I keep saying send them Diving-"

"When are you starting that again? After the next family visit?"

"Same as every class. One year of pure training and theory, then off to the Depths they go to put their learning to work, to make progress and not fall behind the curve."

Sarna leaned back, resting her head on Leo's chest as she looked up.

"By Dorm?"

"Well… That was the plan but."

"But your mentee, the quintet we still aren't allowed to openly name and the Syl pair you're not sure where to send the Coordinator Dorm?"

"Damn it Sarna, you went and said it. I was all happy about the problems being yours for once."

"Two or three down?"

"They can start at two, but four forks down," Leo answered.

"Four? Isn't that the end goal for-"

"Don't remind me or I'll ask how you'll make a group fight fair."

"Any of those three will wipe the floor with any other group. Any of the top 8 solo will probably wipe any group they're not part of on their own by graduation if this keeps up."

"Solo against a group? I can see Mithrax managing…"

"You've seen how worn out Syl are?"

"As long as one of them stays awake I say nothing," he said. "I've had to startle them at least once a week though."

Sarna tilted her head forward again, nearly bouncing it off the table before rubbing her temples once more. She felt Leo's hands on her shoulders, helping work away the tension.

"Don't stop," she mumbled. "They're at the point where even in that state they can beat the Hammerswells or Blehmans."

"So with enough time to recover…"

"Exactly."

"And he's not slowing down on their training?"

She groaned.

"The only reason he hasn't kept speeding up is he's only official Seb's mentor."

"Campsite is the second on the left," George said from behind her.

"We can drop our things and set up later," she snapped.

"Of course, there's no rush in making sure we have somewhere to relax and recover."

She wouldn't need recovery, not the sort that was implied. She'd handled herself more than well enough in the Depths for decades, fighting on level would be simple.

She dropped her pack just inside the designated campsite, sparing it only enough of a glance to confirm it was as safe as implied. She didn't wait for George to follow, she could hear the woman rushing to catch up as she stopped in front of her chosen cavern.

"Here."

"Further than I might have, but I'm just the healer."

"Exactly."

"They'd better have pastries waiting…"

"What?"

"Nothing my lady. Just waiting for you to engage."

Stepping into the cave Lys took a good look around. No offshoots, no further tunnels that might indicate reinforcements or additional threats. Less than a dozen vermin, nothing she couldn't handle, especially when none of them showed signs of variance.

It had been too long since she faced something that challenged her, hopefully it wouldn't be long until she could push herself once more.

Morgan said nothing as the argument resumed for the fifteenth time this week, the six hundred and thirty ninth time, unless he'd lost count, since they had come to the school. Well since they had met her. He already knew how it would end. One of them would snarl and storm off, the other would claim victory that would only last until the next discussion.

The twins hadn't gotten along well in years. He vaguely remembered a time when they all were happy to be together, to spend time learning, training, declaring that they would be the next triumphant elites. Which they were by any standard, they'd made it to the hall. They'd done so without limiting their training time or neglecting all other royal pursuits. All three of them were suited to take on the duties of branch line heir, even if only two of them ever might.

Therein lay the issue, or at least a part of it. The twins could not both inherit, could not both be heir, both be recognized as ascendant. There was only one line, one family. There were no others in their cohort, no branches that might claim them as a partner for their child and elevate them that way. Were they a decade younger or older it would be different.

It changed little for Morgan, their arguments changed little. They never forgot he was there, not really, but neither did they bring him into the debate. They didn't ask his opinion and he would have avoided giving it. It didn't matter that Anya was clearly a relative, that the Geas proved it. It didn't matter that her existence would be an affront to those who believed the royal line must remain intact. To state either would be to take a side, and that was not where he wished to be.

Keldren forgot him. Keldren didn't care for much, didn't focus on much beyond the moments of battle. Still he was a threat that no one would truly wish to face alone. The fact that he was sworn to Bern was something that Morgan had never wondered about, not openly. As a child he had no reason to, as an adult he knew better than to ask how it had been permitted.

Cara never forgot about him, never relaxed around him. She watched him the way she watched everyone in their group except Gwen. She didn't distrust him, she simply didn't trust him. It was not a contradiction, it was a fact. He had been in her mind, in all their minds, often enough to know she disliked the invasion, disliked the need for him to be coordinating them. Not because he did so, but because anyone did.

"-will do as I say Gwennae," Bern snapped, the words rippling as he used her name against her. "She is not family, he is not some future myth. He is a human playing with forces he should not."

"You. Do. Not!" Gwen snarled, each word shorter than the last before she seemed to sag. "You may be the elder but this is not your choice. I will fight them in earnest when they are rested and not before, but know that you will face the consequences that may follow."

"I'm sure you'll be glad to know they have been given a reprieve today and tomorrow," Bern all but gloated. "Our next class will have them at their peak. Apparently even Lord Percivus will take pity when there's to be a celebration of a year of classes coming up."

Morgan kept his face schooled as Gwen stormed off, the door nearly catching Cara as she caught up. Another fight, another supposed victory for Bern. That put him at nine for the week and four hundred total. Yet none of it mattered until an Elder gave their opinion and made it fact.
