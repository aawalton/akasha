It was the sort of silence that settled when everyone in the room understood exactly what was about to happen and had privately decided that speaking would only make it more real.

Amy was the first to move. She pushed herself off the wall silently, adjusted her satchel, and looked around the hall once; her eyes scanned the defensive structures they'd built over the past several hours.

Lain's ice barriers and spikes were layered across the two main corridors; the areas further away from their main position were replete with even more spikes in addition to traps. Meanwhile, a collection of improvised barricades made from stacked furniture rested against the far wall.

It looked like shit to be honest, but it was as ready as it was going to get.

"We should get into positions," Amy said, breaking the silence.

They had further spoken about their defense plan during these past hours, as well as explained it to Crow. The gist of it was to gradually retreat towards the hall with the seven doors while slowing down the chaos creature and Abaddon as much as possible, all while making sure not to get surrounded.

Upon hearing Amy's words, the others, with the exception of maybe Crow, looked at her as if it was her fault—or at least that's how Amy sensed their gazes. How unfair…she liked this far less than they did, but the more they waited, the more Abaddon would tire their forces. Someone had to make the call, and seeing how nobody—not even Crow—moved, she regretfully had to.

For a moment, everyone was still, no one really wanting to move first. Amy considered speaking up once again, but then Lain suddenly stood up while brushing something from her sleeve, and with one last glance and nod towards Amy, she walked toward the left corridor without a word.

Seeing her, both Ash and Iris gave awkward smiles before cracking their knuckles and patting each other on the shoulders, then followed. Each went into a separate hall silently; Iris left with Lain while Ash went right.

Lyra lingered for exactly two seconds longer, gaze drifting from Crow to Amy and back again. Then, nervously, she turned and moved toward the right corridor, following Ash with slow, trembling footsteps.

Zayd rose from his position by the wall in that unhurried way of his, collected his teacup, and glanced at Amy. "Please be careful, Miss Stake. Remember that you are, without a doubt, one of his primary targets; make sure to prioritize your life."

Amy sighed, then nodded. Why was it always her…?

Zayd stayed watching her silently, something small and unreadable in his expression, and then he was gone too, toward the left corridor.

And with Zayd gone, that left only Crow and Amy.

Amy was standing a few feet away while Crow, with his missing arm and downcast gaze, was still seated against the door with the blood ritual lines pulsing steadily across his skin.

Amy looked at him, took a deep breath, and then asked. "Do you need a minute?" She said, knowing full well that every second counted.

Crow's eyes met hers. "No," he said. Then, after a pause, "Yeah. Maybe."

Amy nodded and didn't move. Honestly, her question felt more directed towards herself than anything, so she really didn't mind that much.

Crow breathed in slowly through his nose. He stared at the opposite wall—not at anything specific on it, just somewhere neutral.

[The young soldier prepares themselves in their reinforced castle against impossible odds.] Bloodedge suddenly spoke in Amy's head, making her instantly frown. [How poignant. Such valor! In all my nineteen hundred years, I have seldom— OW! Girl, what are you— Ow, stop that!]

Amy squeezed the hilt harder. Bloodedge gave up talking and began humming to itself instead. Huh. She could shut it up like that? She'd have to remember it.

Crow let out a breath after about fifteen seconds. When he looked at her again, he seemed ready to get this over with.

"This sucks."

"Yeah," Amy said.

"It really, really—"

"Crow."

He looked at her.

"I know."

She wanted to complain too. Preferably for a good long while, somewhere that wasn't here. But they were here, and Abaddon wasn't going to wait for them to feel better about it.

Crow gave her a tired little smile, then leaned forward to get up.

It took him a while. Amy kept watching the space where his arm should have been, then made herself look at his face. He was pale, but once he was standing, he straightened and turned toward the way to the trials.

He glanced at her, closed his hand into a fist, and stepped away from the door.

The blood of the ritual stopped glowing.

Crow looked down once. That was all the time he gave it.

He ran for the left corridor. They'd agreed on that route earlier; with Amy going right, there was less reason for Abaddon to concentrate his forces on the left. Besides, Lain, Iris and Zayd were defending it. Crow would have the strongest protection they could give him.

He was fast, even like this. Amy watched him avoid the traps they'd warned him about without once losing his footing on the ice.

Then she headed right at a much more careful jog. She'd barely reached the corridor when she felt the magic around her weaken.

And that was it. No noise, no flash. The barrier was gone. Only then did she notice that the hum she'd been hearing for hours had stopped too.

She picked her way past the traps at the entrance. Somewhere ahead, something growled.

Another answered it.

Within seconds there were too many to count, and the sound was getting closer.

Amy's hand tightened on Bloodedge's hilt, and her expression gradually shifted into one of deep concentration.

The growling grew gradually louder with every step she took.

[They come. As they always have. As they always will.] Bloodedge's sudden voice cracked Amy's focused expression into a frown. [The tide of chaos is breaking against the fragile shore of mortals. The soundless shrieks of the enemy army were yet so loud in the allies' minds. They could almost hear the millions of monsters that are in this very moment wanting nothing but to devour them.]

"I can hear them loud and clear," Amy muttered under her breath. "There is no 'almost'."

[I was being poetic...]

Amy ignored the sword and kept jogging. She rounded the second intersection and finally spotted them.

Ash waited where the corridor widened into a roughly circular space before narrowing farther back. He'd planted himself just behind the first row of spike traps, ready to catch whatever got through them. His arms were crossed, but Amy noticed how firmly he kept both feet on the ground.

Lyra stood to his left, her trembling hands already glowing faintly in a green light which Amy recognized from classes as her way of keeping herself primed without expending unnecessary mana.

Both of them looked up as Amy approached.

"Took you long enough," Ash said.

"Crow just went in," Amy replied while slowing to a stop beside Ash. "Barrier's down."

"Yeah, we figured," Ash said. "They aren't trying to be subtle about it."

Amy looked at Lyra and nodded with a gaze as if asking her if she was ready.

Lyra's expression tightened, and she seemed very hesitant, but she nodded anyway.

The growling got louder.

Then something hit the far end of the corridor—it wasn't a creature, at least not yet. Just the sound of something massive impacting something, traveling through the obsidian walls until it reached them as a damn loud thud.

Amy could see how Lyra at her side started muttering what looked like a prayer with her trembling hands. Amy considered for a second telling her that praying to that Goddess would only bring bad luck if anything, but decided against it; for all she knew it might actually work, considering that they were talking about that egocentric bitch.

Amy was suddenly pulled away from her thoughts when another loud thud rang out. Then another. Then a third, accompanied by a crack.

Amy's eyes started shining as she began using her ability, aiming to the future where the first part of their plan had them reuniting with the others as they gradually retreated. Any more than that and it would leave her without a single ounce of strength to carry it out in the first place. Moreover, she needed to save some for her actual objective.

A fourth thud shook dust from the obsidian ceiling.

Amy stared at the far end of the corridor where the darkness looked like it had begun to move, followed by shrieks and a constant, grinding sound.

"How many?" Ash asked while breathing rapidly.

Amy only gave him a side-eye before refocusing on the hallway.

"Is there a point in asking?" she said.

Ash turned to look at her. "That many?"

"Again, is there a point?" she repeated.

He turned his head back toward the corridor, then let out a series of shaky breaths. "No, I guess not."

Besides Amy, Lyra had stopped muttering her prayer and instead was holding a series of mostly protective amulets and a small knife.

Amy looked at her for a few more seconds, then turned her gaze once again towards the hallway. "Just follow the plan," she said quietly. "Hold position. If they get too overwhelming, fall back. Never go on the attack; make them come to us. Do not break formation. And please be careful with the traps."

"You've already said that three times, Stake," Ash said.

"I'll say it a fourth if it means you both remember it."

"I remember it," Lyra whispered, or at least that's how it felt; hearing anything over the amount of noise here was beyond hard.

Loud…maybe she had just jinxed it, because right at that next moment, there was an even louder thud, and then, it wasn't loud anymore. She couldn't even hear herself breathing.

What the—

Blood was running from her nose. Everything around her had slowed down.

Then she was moving, before she'd even worked out what was happening.

Her ability had never pulled at her this hard. She stepped half a pace ahead of Ash and lowered herself, both hands on Bloodedge, the blade raised in front of her. Who the hell had taught her to hold a sword like that? Certainly nobody she remembered.

She'd barely braced herself when something shot along the corridor. Even with everything slowed down, she could hardly follow it.

It was shaped almost like a person, small enough to get through a gap they'd left between the traps and barricades. Nobody had thought to close that one. Ash was only beginning to shout when it reached her. She couldn't see any eyes on it.

She felt Bloodedge pierce it.

The collision jolted both her arms and snapped her teeth together. For a moment the blade wouldn't go any farther. Then it did. The thing screamed, and Amy nearly lost her grip before it stopped moving, its weight dragging at the sword.

Black liquid ran off it onto the floor.

She couldn't stop staring. That thing had been going straight for her. She'd put a sword through it, and now she could barely hold the sword because her arms wouldn't stop shaking. She was breathing far too quickly too.

Ash hadn't moved.

[Oh, MAGNIFICENT! Such skill, and from—]

"Get ready!" Amy pulled the blade free. The creature fell, already coming apart into black smoke.

"Holy fuck," Ash said.

"Get READY!" She looked back at them. Ash finally raised his fists and took a stance. Lyra lifted her hands, light gathering between her fingers.

She tried to tell Amy something, but the shrieking drowned her out.

The first things through looked like wolves, if wolves had that many legs. Some didn't have heads. Behind them came dogs, lions, things that might've been human, all with too many limbs and black fluid dripping from them as they ran.

They couldn't fit through all at once. The ice walls forced them together, and within ten paces the first ones were on the spikes. Amy heard them hit even over the rest of the noise. Two died there. A third tripped over a body and disappeared beneath the creatures behind it.

They were using the dead to climb over the traps.

"Ash!" He brought his fists down on the ice support that ran up to the ceiling.

It broke, dropping a row of stone spikes across the hallway. A few of the spikes managed to miss the entire swarm. Of course. The rest slowed it enough for Ash to move up.

A wolf thing got past the stones and he punched it straight back, knocking two others aside with it. Amy was still following where it went when he hit the next one.

"Get back!" he shouted, still punching.

Amy went left instead. Her ability was pulling her toward something trying to slip past him, another of those small eyeless things. Slower than the first, thankfully, but Ash hadn't seen it.

She raised Bloodedge and let the pull correct her aim. The creature ran onto the blade before it could reach him.

[Ohhh~ Yes, like that! A little crude, perhaps, but with some practice—]

Amy gritted her teeth, dragged the sword free, and backed away with the other two.

Something much bigger followed them. It looked like a bear, except for the spider legs. It hit the second row of spikes mindlessly and slowed, but didn't stop, pulling itself forward with the legs that hadn't been impaled.

Amy threw up a barrier between it and Ash's exposed flank.

She was ready to cast a second barrier as soon as the first broke. The creature hit the golden light hard enough that she expected it to come straight through. It didn't.

Wait. Really?

It struck again. Cracks spread across the barrier, and the light flickered, but it was still there.

Last time, while she was running from the mini-chaos creatures, her barrier hadn't broken either—she had thought it was simply due to the lack of strength of the insects…yet…

Not now! Amy! She made herself look at Ash again.

She kept the barrier up while he finished the two creatures on his right. When he turned toward the bear, she let it drop. One punch from Ash killed it.

Ash was bleeding from somewhere above his eyebrow. He didn't seem to care. He probably hadn't noticed either.

"Lyra, Ash—" Amy started.

"I see it!" Lyra said, and the healing light went towards him.

Ash nodded to Lyra without stopping. He'd probably meant it as a thank-you.

They gave up more ground. Twenty seconds passed, or fifty, but at last there seemed to be fewer creatures reaching them at once. Could they stop here?

Then Amy heard something to their left.

That hadn't been another impact. Something was cracking inside the wall.

She turned to look.

Lain had sealed the side passage with ice so nothing could come at them from there. The ice still blocked it, but a large crack ran through it now.

[I would be concerned about that, personally.]

"Yeah." Finally, something useful out of the sword.

She caught Ash by the back of his collar and pulled.

He stumbled in the middle of a punch. After a quick look back at her, he nodded and started moving again, still fighting off whatever came within reach.

Amy followed her ability as best she could. Ash kept backing up in front of them, punching anything that got too close. She took the flanks, using Bloodedge when she could and barriers when she couldn't. Lyra stayed between them, healing one and then the other. Somehow, they were keeping together.

Amy looked back at the blocked passage. The crack had widened, and another was opening beside it.

"Faster!"

"I'm trying to punch things here!" Ash shouted.

"Punch them faster!"

"That's not—"

A section of the ice gave way. About two feet of it, thankfully, instead of the whole wall. Broken pieces slid out across the floor.

Creatures poured through the opening, and suddenly Ash had more in front of him than he could stop. They had to retreat another thirty meters before the hallway narrowed enough to slow the swarm again.

"Here! Hold here!"

Ash was already digging his heels in, trying to drive them back.

Amy kept casting barriers and stabbing through the gaps. Between attacks she checked their position. They'd lost more ground than she'd wanted, but at least they were no longer being pushed back with every creature that reached them.

More kept coming.

Now it was just a battle of attrition.
