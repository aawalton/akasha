“They’ll be back soon,” I soothe Trouble who’s pacing like a caged beast off to the side, his head twisting to eye the forest every couple of seconds. “Spot and Blaze aren’t nearly as bothered as you seem to be.”

Trouble stops and levels me with the dirtiest glare I've ever seen from him, and that's saying something considering that I lived through his teenage era. Though he's still not quite on the point of sending his thoughts verbally, he sends me the distinct impression that I don't have a clue what I'm talking about because this is his sister, damn it!

"Well, if you're so concerned," I tell him with a touch of pique, "then hurry up and Evolve. Then you’ll be able to be part of the party." That, after all, is the only reason he wasn’t chosen to be part of the group minding the hatchlings – he would have just offered another distraction for the Tier twos.

Trouble’s glare, if possible, grows even more disgusted. He continues pacing angrily, his tail twitching and his head snapping towards the forest.

I have to admit that I’m not as calm as I’m trying to appear. This is the first time the samuran hatchlings have gone out into the forest, and while the beasts have become far more reticent to attack those under Bastet’s protection, that doesn’t mean they won’t – especially when the samurans are so weak.

If it had been up to me, I wouldn’t have sent them out at all until they were adults. But I was overruled by my samuran companions, who emphasised that learning how to behave in the forest within the first year is essential to a hatchling’s development.

Since they’re the experts, I didn’t argue. But that doesn’t stop me from fretting.

We’ve managed to keep these samurans – these babies – alive through storms, and sea-beast attacks. I don’t want to lose any of them now. Not within the first couple of months of reaching the Lost Continent.

Movement catches my eye, and I snap my head towards the forest just as Trouble does. Blaze and Spot – though still less settled into the pack – are also watching intently.

Nerves claw at my belly as I fear finding out that we’ve lost someone on this first outing.

Then the little samurans start popping through the undergrowth.

I recognise Chews-a-board, who earned his name on the ship when he almost chewed a hole through the wooden boards between their living space and the next compartment. He was also one of those Ninja had to chase off the deck when a group of them decided that heading into an active battle zone during the fight with the roc was the best possible idea. He hasn’t lost that urge to explore – or his boldness – but at least it’s been tempered with a little more caution as he’s grown and matured.

The next few are samurans who haven’t yet earned their names. I track the wave of samurans as they emerge.

One. Two. Three… Twenty-five….

Where’s the last one? There should be twenty-six.

I recount the samuran hatchlings in case I’ve made a mistake, fear rising me as I confirm that my initial count was right.

And then there’s a rustle of leaves and a hatchling sidles out of a bush, glancing one way and then the other as if to check if anyone saw him arrive.

There he is. Twenty-six.

My heart slows from its frantic pace, and relief floods through me.

Relief seems to be the predominant emotion among the minders as well. I don’t think I’ve ever seen Pride look quite so harried as he does now. He’s corralling three of the samurans who seem to have decided they don’t actually want to leave the forest and are trying to return to it, making their adorable little clicking noises of protest as he blocks their path.

Catch looks exhausted too as he comes up to me.

“Hard morning?” I ask, with no small amount of amusement.

He gives me a dirty look – almost as dirty as Trouble’s was earlier.

You have no idea, he grouses. Two of the hatchlings have earned new names.

“Oh?” I ask, curious, because his tone promises dire consequences for these hatchlings.

Yes. Pulls-a-Tail and Fails-to-Have-Any-Self-Preservation.

I can’t help but laugh, despite the betrayed look he gives me. It’s laced with humour, though, so I know he’s not truly offended.

“I’m not sure you can saddle the poor samurans with names like that,” I point out. “You might actually be dictating their futures.”

If these two samurans make it to adulthood, I will consider my job very well done indeed, he replies darkly. Seriously, Markus, they are…. We were pointing out the traces of an Evolved beast we did not want to enrage, and what does one of them do? Climbs into the tree where it was sleeping and pulls on its tail. The other one, clearly in cahoots, levelled his spear and charged at it as soon as it dropped down from the branch, its claws and teeth bared, spikes flaring.

“I take it the Tier two didn’t take that kindly,” I muse.

Catch makes a grunting sound – their equivalent of a snort.

That’s putting it mildly, he replies. Not only did it take exception, but it called over five others who we hadn’t yet spotted to hunt us for the temerity.

I give him a once-over. Sure enough, there’s not just mud covering him, but dried blood as well.

“Do you need healing?” I ask seriously.

Catch shakes his head and I don’t sense that he’s lying.

We wouldn’t all have made it without the potions you insisted we take, he admits. And that wasn’t the only incident, though it was the worst. He clicks his teeth together in their version of a sigh. I had somehow managed to forget exactly how reckless samuran hatchlings can be. I don’t know how, considering they didn’t exactly show much more self-preservation on the ship…but I swear I wasn’t as bad as them at their age.

Don’t fool yourself, mate, Iandee interjects with amusement as he walks up behind Catch. I remember your hatching, and you were just as bad as anyone else. Worse – half the time you didn’t even seem aware of what you’d stumbled into until it was over. Some of us had a bet that your broodmother had accidentally fed you litha fungus. He sighs, his light tone shifting to something heavy with regret. Your vision problems started young, I guess.

Iandee is just as muddy and bloodied, clearly having put in as much effort as Catch to keep the hatchlings alive. He’s also looking more contemplative than usual.

I wonder what our village could have been, he muses, if we’d thought to protect our hatchlings before you came along, Tamer. Now we see them as resources to be protected, not just a waste of them who needed to prove their right to exist by supporting the village. After last year and this one…I find that I have a different view point than I used to.

There’s real regret in his voice, and it brings home something I don’t always consider – that he’s several years older than Catch. He often behaves more immaturely, but the truth is that he was already a Warrior before I ever arrived at the village. That makes him…what, at least ten years old? Catch is barely three. And it’s moments like this where that difference really shows.

“We all make mistakes,” I tell him quietly, “and we’re all products of our own culture.”

It’s not like I’m innocent of making mistakes myself – bad ones too. Even ignoring the mistakes I made back on Earth and when I first arrived in the samurans’ world, I made plenty when I first took over the samurans’ village and tried to impose my own concepts of morality and what’s right on them. I don’t exactly regret the changes I made, but I could definitely have been more sensitive in how I went about them. If I hadn’t had a Bond with all of the Tier twos, I have a strong suspicion that I would have ended up dead one way or another before too long.

I’ve tried to be more aware of that since coming to Moriax and once more encountering a culture so very different from my own. Though, of course, there is a limit to what I’m prepared to tolerate, even for the sake of respecting someone else’s culture.

Iandee and Catch soon excuse themselves to deal with a group of hatchlings who have managed to get into a fight over a bone – a blood-stained thing that one of them has clearly claimed as a trophy from today’s hunt and that the others apparently now want.

I remind myself to ask Catch whether he managed to fill the butcher’s storage bracelet I gave him. We don’t have many of them, but the hunters always take one out, because unlike my Inventory, they’re designed to hold meat without stripping the Energy from it and making it less nutritious.

I head over to where Ninja is aggressively grooming one of the dirtiest hatchlings. I see with amusement that she’s managed to rope her brother and the other two raptorcats into the task. They’re cleaning some of the slightly less dirty samurans with less fervour, but with a more-than-satisfying attention to detail.

I hope that either Bastet or Ninja will eventually choose a mate, perhaps of Blaze or Spot, because both of them clearly enjoy interacting with the samuran babies and treat them like their own cubs. The thought of having little raptorcat cubs in the pack again makes me unexpectedly wistful.

Then I remember how much trouble the last lot caused and reconsider. Perhaps they should wait until the samurans have grown up. That way, we might have enough minders to keep the future babies out of trouble.

“How did it go?” I ask Ninja, curious whether her report will differ from Catch’s.

Well, for a first visit, she replies, her mental voice calm even as she continues washing the hatchling without pause. The cubs don’t yet use their claws and teeth to their best advantage, but they are starting to use your metal claws well. I think we need more lessons in the big den before taking them out again.

I nod.

“Hopefully now they’ve seen why they’re learning to use weapons, they’ll be more interested.”

The ‘big den’ she’s referring to is a small school I’ve set up – for the samuran hatchlings, but also for the younger children who came with us. Setting it up has been a challenge, particularly since the teaching staff consists of both samurans and humans. Lizel’s communication tattoos really have been a godsend. But I feel that there is more value in combining the classes than separating them.

Hopefully having mixed species teachers as well as students will help reduce prejudice or fear towards the lizard-folk who I anticipate having an increasingly large role in our new society here given how quickly they become adults. The samurans teach forest survivability or their own specialities in the case of the Pathwalkers. The human volunteers teach reading, writing, and basic mathematics.

The other lords were rather surprised when I proposed a general school that any child could attend, and even more so when I proposed an additional night school that any adult could attend. They considered teaching everyone to read and write pointless.

I pointed out that, with as few people as we have in the settlement, having a larger pool to draw from for positions of responsibility could only be a good thing. Loran, for example, was incapable of reading or writing before we set off on the journey but, after months of lessons on the ship, has become perfectly competent in both. As my manservant, he has been given a great deal of responsibility and, once his confidence grew and he learned a few tricks from Sarran, he took to it with almost surprising ease.

Ultimately, they agreed to promote the school and the night school among their own people, and I left it at that.

So far, we haven’t had quite the numbers I was initially hoping for. At the start, many were, like Alyna was, concerned about what they might owe in return for this free education. When that was brought to my attention, I decided to instead start charging a small fee. When people can see the cost upfront, they seem more willing to trust that there won’t be some hidden price later. It increased our numbers by about double – mostly among the adults – but the school still only welcomes less than fifty adults and, if the samurans are taken out of the equation, only about thirty children.

In any case, I’m not too disappointed. After seeing all the teething problems involved in establishing the school, I’m actually rather glad we didn’t have too many people at the start. Now, I feel we have a more solid structure and can handle larger numbers.

One of the samuran hatchlings trips over my foot. I put out my hand to catch him and push him back to his feet. They’re getting big, I comment to myself, feeling the strength and weight of the hatchling’s muscles. By this point they’re more than halfway to adulthood.

Once the samurans begin taking a greater role in hunting and aren’t spending so much time in the classroom, I’ll push the school more actively among our people. For now, managing the language barriers and adapting the lessons to suit the hyperactive samuran hatchlings is taking up a fair amount of our teachers’ time. Which is another reason we decided to send them out in the forest today – we thought it might do them some good to use up some of their seemingly limitless energy.

As for the language barrier, that continues to be an issue. Lizel has informed me the hatchlings are too young for tattoos. Apparently, magical tattoos should only be given to adult beasts, or they risk damaging the beast’s capacity to Evolve. I agreed with her that it’s better to wait. Temporary complications caused by limited communication aren’t worth risking the samurans’ future potential. At least they are pretty sensitive to body language at this point, even if they only understand limited amounts of Moriaxar.

I spend some more time with the hunting party, enjoying the simple joy of listening to the baby samurans’ enthusiasm about the new things they’ve seen and done – and, of course, the inevitable bragging from some of them about kills I’m fairly sure they didn’t actually make.

But, unfortunately, my responsibilities intrude far too soon.

A messenger stands at the edge of the clearing, hovering just beyond the group. As soon as I spot him, I know my time here is over.

Sighing, I bid farewell to my companions, giving each of the raptorcats a pat on the head or a scratch behind the ears, depending on their preference, before making my way over to the heavily armoured messenger.

“My lord,” he greets me with a bow. “I bear a message from Lord Elarion.”

“What is it?” I ask, my stomach sinking.

The messenger hesitates.

“Lord Elarion would prefer to speak to you personally on the matter.”

I sigh, casting one last wistful glance back at the group – Trouble and the other two raptorcats have been pulled into one of the samuran hatchlings’ games – before turning back to him.

“Alright. I’m coming. Please lead the way.”
