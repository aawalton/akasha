“Something weird’s happening up ahead,” Aurelia announced, slowing down. She’d taken the lead in case of more insect swarm attacks, which seemed common throughout the outer region. Her flames were the most mana-efficient at clearing paths through large groups of weaker creatures. Noah’s abilities were stronger, but burned through his reserves at a much faster rate and were less sustainable over long periods.

They’d been descending for three hours. The path from the outer depths to the middle had been gradual at first, a steady slope downward, the canyon walls growing taller and more oppressive with each mile. Now they were more or less on the bottom, thankfully free of whatever shadowy monsters lurked back toward the entrance. Free of anything living, for that matter.

“What kind of weird?” Noah asked, coming up alongside her.

“Some kind of power field.” She pointed ahead to a section of the canyon floor where the air shimmered like heat rising from summer asphalt, except instead of heat rising, it was concentrated mana compressing. It was easily the size of a full city block, filling the gap between both cliffs and leaving no way to slip past it.

“How far up does it go?” Even Noah’s Dragon’s Eyes couldn’t see where the shimmer ended and sky began. “Can we get over it?” Though from the collection of bird and pterodactyl-esque bones flattened against the stone under the shimmer—which was itself several inches lower than the area ahead or behind it—he expected it would be a very dangerous place to try to fly through.

“There is no above,” Zax said. “There is only through. The gravity lensing is even more intense near the top of the cliffs and spreads out over the valley in both directions, which is the main reason for the upper approach existing at all.”

Now he was looking for it, Noah did see a faint spread to the shimmer, like a vast mushroom funneling from the sky down to this one section of compressed stone. “How does it work?”

“The mana density of the outer reaches and the middle depths intersect here, causing a semi-stable gravitational distortion.” Zax tore a chunk of stone free from the wall and hurled it upward, back the direction they’d come at a diagonal. It shot up and away in a straight line, sparking with the lightning Zax used to accelerate it.

For a moment, Noah thought it would leave the valley entirely, maybe burst out of the clouds and emerge into space. But even Zax’s throwing strength didn’t allow it to escape.

It slowed and started to visibly warp back toward them, gradually at first while still rising, then in an abrupt arc as Zax stopped pushing the stone. By now, it was so far away that Noah could barely track it, if he hadn’t been so intensely focused on observing. Probably about three days’ walk away, back toward the very beginning of the valley descent.

The rock reversed course, streaking across the sky like a meteor on a string, arcing directly toward them.

Aurelia yelped and instinctively threw up her golden bubble shield to protect herself, but no piece of it escaped the gravity that had drawn it down. Instead it only compressed, flattened into dust before it even hit the ground.

“Ah,” Noah said, somewhat uneasily. “I see.”

“This is not the only such region, but it is one of the strongest.  This is why the majority of those who try to enter the Valley do not even survive long enough to be eaten.”

“Is that why the monsters are so weak?” Noah asked, a lot of different pieces suddenly falling into place. “For being level five or six hundred, they seem pretty pathetic. The ones back at the arena were harder.”

Zax smiled and nodded. “Exactly right. The monsters in the outer reaches are born already high level and grow higher through accumulation of raw mana, but they have no proper enemies to test themselves against and no reason to learn or create abilities such as we are familiar with. The Death Valley ecosystem is isolated into regions and the one we’ve just traversed is where only those with no ambition linger.”

“And because the monsters respawn instead of being harvestable, they don’t even provide valuable materials,” Aurelia added. “Even if more people knew how to get down safely, there’s zero incentive to doing so.”

“This is not a place for the sane,” Zax agreed. “Any reward that may be gained here is more easily claimed elsewhere. All but the struggle against death itself.”

Noah nodded. “So how do we get through?”

“If you wish to cross as you are, you may do so.”

Noah approached the edge of the gravity pocket, extending one foot tentatively over the boundary. The moment his boot crossed the threshold, the dense wall of energy slammed into him. His leg buckled, and he threw himself back, barely escaping.

“What was it like?” Aurelia asked curiously. “Can we sprint through it?”

Noah could only shake his head. The feeling of two different gravities pulling him differently had been unsettling, but not so distracting that he couldn’t tell it would be a bad idea to enter the area as he was.

Zax watched them from where he stood. “At that gravity, a B-Rank would be moving at a fraction of their normal speed. You’d be exposed for several minutes, but it shouldn’t be enough to kill you.”

Noah stared at the shimmering zone, his mind racing. They hadn’t come this far to be stopped by heavy air. “What about Arcane Step? A space-based teleport should bypass the gravitational effect entirely.”

“In theory. We’ve never witnessed anyone attempt it. Death Valley is rather unforgiving when it comes to experimentation.”

Noah considered his options. His mana reserves were at about seventy percent after having finally purged the last of the lingering venom earlier in the morning and the handful of shorter encounters they’d faced since beginning the descent. “I can do it,” he said. “Probably.”

“Probably is far from reassuring,” Aurelia muttered.

“It’s the best we’ve got, unless you’d prefer walking?”

Aurelia glanced at the shimmering air. She made no move to step into it.

“Alright.” Noah centered himself. He focused on his destination—a point of stable ground just beyond the gravity zone. The mana within him stirred, eager to be unleashed. “If I make it, I’ll come back for each of you one at a time.”

Zax made a soft amused sound. “Don’t worry about us. This isn’t our first time visiting. Such a field is no obstacle to someone of our strength.”

Noah activated [Arcane Step]. The entry was completely standard, like any other time he used the ability. Emerging from the other side, though, was akin to walking through a wall of mud. Noah stumbled and thought at first he’d miscalculated the far side of the gravity zone. But, no, the gravity field ended several meters away from where he stood.

The pressure against his skin felt oppressive. Ambient mana visibly swirled in lazy currents of luminescent purple and blue fog that drifted through the air. Breathing now felt distinctly uncomfortable, excruciatingly hard.

His lungs ached with every breath he took. Even if his body was strong enough to survive it, that didn’t make it easier. And that’s when he understood. This was what it meant to stand in an A-Rank region.

Tony can help, the symbiote reported, already hard at work reinforcing Noah’s heart and lungs, with a note of concern. It will require full attention.

Do it.

He triggered Arcane Step again to return to where his companions stood, the relief of pressure making his ears pop and his breathing suddenly loud and effortless.

Aurelia grabbed his arm. “You survived.”

Zax simply nodded, as if he’d expected nothing less.

“No need to sound so surprised.” Noah grimaced and glanced at Zax. “How does anyone without a Tony survive that?”

Tony did good? the symbiote asked hopefully.

Tony did great, Noah confirmed. And Tony will be very busy for the next couple weeks.

“Most people do not attempt to traverse Death Valley below A-Rank,” Zax said. “If you are to grow, it will not be comfortable. You know this.”

“I’m fine with discomfort.” Noah glanced at Aurelia. “You ready?”

She looked back at him with a determined nod, but he saw a glimmer of fear behind her eyes.

He took her hand and carried them both to the other side with another Arcane Step. It was easier this time, braced for the pressure, he pushed through it without stumbling again.

Aurelia’s breaths came with quiet grunts of effort, her flames flickering unsteadily as the surrounding mana pressure smothered them, and she clung to Noah’s arm like it was the only thing keeping her upright. She didn’t look any more comfortable than him. Without Tony to help reinforce her body, the environment seemed to be impacting her even more strongly.

“Is it supposed to feel this way?” Aurelia’s flames surrounded her, pushing back against the oppressive mana. Even with her rank, sweat beaded on her forehead.

“As if your soul is being slowly compressed into a diamond?” Noah supplied. “I assume so. Welcome to Death Valley proper.”

“Yay,” Aurelia mumbled as Noah glanced at his overlay. The mana bar was already fluctuating, trying to stabilize against the environment’s pressure.

Bun Bun clung to Noah’s back with claws that dug into his shoulders too. The familiar’s mental voice was uncharacteristically quiet.

Zax walked through the gravity pocket at a steady pace. “Well done,” he said, but he seemed distracted.

“Everything okay?” Aurelia asked.

“We’re not alone. We should hurry.”

—

“Why are we waiting here, again?” Tahira Voidbinder asked. She sprawled back against the silver-streaked rock and stared up at the distant distorted sky with half-closed eyes. “Are we really expecting them to make it this far?”

“If they don’t, then we wouldn’t be needed, would we?” Geron, the Crimson Cataclysm, retorted in a low rumble. The man’s crystalline armor made everything he said echo oddly and Tahira had never even seen his face. Maybe it was his skin, rather than armor? She didn’t really care, the Crimson Cultists were weird enough she wouldn’t be surprised to hear he’d grown the stuff himself.

“They’ll come,” Eileth Silverbloom said with cold confidence. She stood looking away to the west, a stiff and motionless silhouette against the evening sky. “They have no other choice.”

“It’s a couple C-Rankers,” Tahira argued, spinning her tiara around one finger lazily. “If you think they’re coming here, won’t they be dying here long before reaching us?”

“It’s more than a couple C-Rankers, or there’d be no reason for us all to be here,” Geron said. He somehow managed to sound both bland and disdainful, which Tahira took issue with.

“I have seen no evidence of anyone in this group being any threat to us. The only thing strange about them is that they evade our seer.” She glanced over at Eileth with a smirk.

“I can see the echoes of their passage even if their steps are shadowed. They will come to us. Along with their guardian.” There was an edge to her voice, Tahira noticed. This was personal to her in a way it wasn’t to the others.

“If you’re so confident, you can wait here for them.”

“She cannot fight them,” Geron argued with that damnable neutral logic of his. “And I cannot kill them.”

Tahira huffed, spinning her tiara faster, then gripped it in one fist. “I’m not the only assassin on the planet, you know.”

Geron turned to look at her with those eerie red-on-yellow eyes of his, a crystalline mockery of humanity. “You’re the only Ascendant Voidbinder.”

“Because you couldn’t lock them down well enough for a mere A-Ranker to take out a pair of C-Ranks?”

“We are all needed,” Eileth said without turning from her perusal of the western sky. “None of us can succeed without the others.”

“How much longer are we going to wait, though? I have other obligations within the Cult. My master has many plans, of which this coalition is not the most essential.”

At that, finally, Eileth turned to face her. Tahira shivered involuntarily at the madness glinting from those glowing moon-white eyes. “Not long.” The Lunar Seeress raised one hand to the sky.

A meteor streaked across the sky toward the central Gravity Well.

Eileth’s mad grin grew wider still. “See? They are almost here.”

Tahira settled her crown back atop her head and manifested her fourth-favourite bow. In a fraction of a breath she’d drawn it back and released, the arrow unerringly intercepting the falling stone an instant before it disappeared out of sight behind the stone walls of the lower valley. The rock lost all integrity, collapsing into dust as it fell beyond reach. “Good. The sooner we get this over with, the better.”

Someone laughed from behind her.

Tahira spun around, then relaxed with an irritated, “Oh, it’s you.”

“You’re impatient, for an immortal.” Severence, the Lunar Judge, Unwatched, Negator. Also known as Sev, their fourth member was the most uninteresting person Tahira had ever met.

She’d forgotten he was even here. “Some of us do have lives outside our jobs, you know.”

“You are young,” Sev said serenely. “You’ll learn. What you are is what you are, and the more you try to fight that the unhappier you’ll be.”

Tahira turned her back on him and went back to forgetting he existed. He annoyed her almost as much as Mister Crimson Calamity over there. But for now, she would pretend not to hate her allies, and hold off on trying to assassinate them until after their job was done.

Unkillable monsters and informants who thought too highly of themselves did have their uses. For now.
