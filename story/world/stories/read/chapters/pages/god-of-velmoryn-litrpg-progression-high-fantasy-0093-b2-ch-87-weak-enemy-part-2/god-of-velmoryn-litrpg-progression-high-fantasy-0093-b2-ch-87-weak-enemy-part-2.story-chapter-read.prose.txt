Did this divinity belong to the God of Beggars, or were there even more gods on Noah’s side?

Using Guidance directly on divine energy was impossible, at least if I wanted to learn who it belonged to. So instead, I tried using it on the priest.

Nothing.

No feedback at all.

Then what about the artifact he had used?

Guidance.

[Mythic-Tier Item]

Capable of storing a minuscule amount of divine energy.

The almost useless information further soured my mood. To be honest, I had not expected anything better, but for some reason, it still stung.

Still, I was not going to sit and watch as the odds turned against me. If Noah intended to rely on divine power, if that was his true plan, then I would counter it.

I still had my ultimate weapon.

The bane of deities.

“Aurelion,” I called to my angel.

A crimson shimmer immediately appeared before me, and Aurelion’s figure formed within my realm. He knelt on one knee, his right hand resting on the glaive planted in the smoke-like floor between the pulsing roots of the Crimson Guardian’s projection.

“My Liege, You summoned me,” he said.

“This army might be more dangerous than I thought. If they can use divinity, the velmoryns may struggle against them, and I do not wish to watch my children die. Go and bring Aria and Avenor back.”

“As You command, my King.”

He offered no opinion of his own. The moment his words echoed through my realm, his figure faded, dissolving into a blur of crimson and gold.

This was not the best way to use Aurelion, but I had little choice. While Avenor’s Hollow Core was my strongest weapon against divine energy, it also meant I could not pull him into my realm and teleport him directly to Eriavel.

My only option was to have Aurelion fly to him and bring him back.

I pressed against my connection with Aria and instructed her to find an isolated place, warning her that Aurelion would soon come for them.

Then I shifted the Window back to Eriavel.

Mirion had already assembled the army. More than a thousand Velmoryns, all trained for battle and prepared for the war against Noah, stood before the Crimson Guardian.

They were not lined up yet. Each warrior was surrounded by family members or friends, exchanging hopes of a safe return and promises of victory. Their expressions were somewhat strained, but confidence still remained in their eyes and hearts.

“Would it not be better to march with our full force?” Lily asked.

Mirion stood beside her, still staring at a rough map of the forest. It mostly showed the territories where beasts roamed, the location of the velthwyn, and the places where the tribes had lived before coming to Eriavel.

The map was outdated, but Mirion still found it useful because of the red line recently added to it, marking Eriavel and the wall the Crimson Guardian had raised around the settlement.

“We will not shed more blood than necessary,” Mirion said. “The Priestess told me the enemy has no great force. If that is true, then our strongest warriors will be sufficient. If it is not, and we find ourselves walking into a trap, only the most experienced among us will be able to maintain order and retreat to Eriavel. Here, the rest will prepare our defensive lines.”

“By High Father’s will, we will not need them,” Lily murmured, studying Mirion’s expression. “But would it not be more effective to meet them near the divine tree? It would…”

“No,” Mirion cut her off flatly. “Our Lord warned us that this is not the war we have been preparing for, and that our enemy’s true strength will reveal itself later. I cannot show them everything we possess. That is also why I have not summoned Karla back. We must keep our losses to a minimum, but we must also keep our true strength close to our hearts.”

Lily sighed as she watched Mirion mark a location on the map, not far from the wall. Despite what he claimed, he had chosen a place that would allow them to retreat quickly if the velmoryns lost.

What he did not know was that the location still fell within Orrvyn’s range.

The wall I had ordered the Crimson Guardian to create had not been built at the limit of its reach, and that had been before Orrvyn grew stronger. Its influence now covered almost twice the area and continued to expand steadily.

Apparently, Orrvyn’s range did not depend solely on rank. As the tree aged and grew larger, its roots spread farther as well.

“Sometimes…” Lily murmured, “Sometimes I fear that by accepting His grace, we invited threats far beyond our understanding upon ourselves.”

Mirion lifted his head, and a small smile appeared on his face as he studied the velmoryns gathered before the Crimson Guardian.

“I have never trusted gods,” he said. “Whether it was the Goddess or… any other deity, I always believed none of them truly cared about us. Gods only need mortals to remain their loyal pets, puppets filled with enough zeal to throw themselves into death merely to please them.”

His smile widened as he watched Dariel lift his daughter into his arms while embracing Freya.

“I do not know whether our Lord is truly different. I do not know whether He genuinely sees us as His children. But tell me, Lily, have you ever seen our people as they are now? United as one, free of hatred toward each other, behaving like a single family?”

He paused, his gaze passing over the gathered warriors and their loved ones.

“I do not serve our Lord because of His power, nor do I serve Him merely for Tekla’s sake. I serve Him because of this. He kept His promise. The velmoryns are united beneath His name, and I would gladly sacrifice my life to keep them that way. If my death could help our children live without hatred or fear, it would be a small price to pay, would it not?”

“Why are you suddenly speaking of death?” Lily asked coldly. “Is there something you are not telling me?”

Mirion chuckled and placed his large hand on her shoulder.

“No, my friend. There are no lies between us,” he said, still grinning, though his smile gradually began to fade. “But we all die someday, and if this war becomes as great as I fear…”

He did not finish as a young man ran up to them.

“Lucas has returned,” the scout reported.

“Where is he?”

“In the temple.”

Mirion grunted, pressing his lips together in disapproval, but said nothing.

“Dariel,” he called to the man, who was still speaking with his family. “Take over for me. I need to go see Lucas.”

Lucas entered the temple with quick steps but stopped near the wooden gate. He cleansed his hands in the small basin of water beside it, then wiped the sweat from his face and arms before continuing inside.

The temple was dimly lit. Dark clouds had already covered the sun, and the candles Gundir had begun producing after Amon taught him the method had not yet been lit.

He found Tekla kneeling before my only statue, her eyes closed in prayer.

Lucas hesitated. He did not dare interrupt her, but the matter at hand was far too important for him to stand aside and wait until she finished. In the end, he coughed softly, pretending to clear his throat.

Tekla traced my symbol across her chest and turned around, looking at Lucas eagerly. But when she saw that he was alone, the light in her eyes faltered.

“The others?”

“They are well. I hurried back to bring the news,” Lucas explained.

“Thank High Father,” Tekla murmured. Then, as if remembering something, she added, “Wait. I will send someone for…”

“I already did,” Lucas said. “He needs to hear this as well.”

His attention shifted when the white griffin emerged from behind the Tree of Life with a rat hanging from its beak. With a single effortless motion, it tossed the creature into the air and opened its mouth, waiting until the rat dropped inside. The tail disappeared last, slurped down like a strand of pasta.

“Lucas.”

Mirion’s deep voice boomed through the temple.

He was fully equipped, his giant axe strapped across his back. This time, Tekla did not reprimand him for bringing a weapon inside.

“What news did you bring?” Mirion asked.

“I led the Crimson Root in an ambush against the human army. They have roughly twice our numbers, but their equipment, magic, and ability to respond to sudden attacks are all lacking.”

“Heh.” Excitement flashed in Mirion’s eyes. “Thank the Lord. Then this battle shall be…”

“I am not done,” Lucas cut him off. “Our goal was to frighten them and weaken their morale. We targeted their mages and healers, and I believe we killed at least two dozen of them. But…”

Lucas paused and glanced at Tekla.

“I know you ordered me not to engage them directly, but the man I assumed to be their leader appeared defenseless, so I took the opportunity.”

“And?” Mirion asked impatiently, clearly annoyed that Lucas was dragging out the report.

“I failed,” Lucas said flatly, though his knuckles whitened at his sides. “He used god’s mana to protect himself with a barrier.”
