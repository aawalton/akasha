Ezra fought to keep a look of disgust from his face. A fat boy? While children were starving in the slums. And he didn’t even have the decency to put in the hard work to turn that fat into muscle.

“But I can see that you’ll fit in among the rest of the soldiers, like I can’t,” Yoran put on a smile that was different from the other smiles he’d had before. “I’ll make sure you’re ready to begin practicing with the rest tomorrow. Suddenly, the Madam’s command to bring all of his things today, giving him the nice pack and sending him on his way with his money, made sense. She’d known this would happen. A part of him was angry at the thought that he wouldn’t be allowed his goodbyes, but another part of him smiled at the faith she’d had in him. She’d had every confidence that he wouldn’t return.

“Alright then,” Ezra nodded, wanting to stand up but thinking better of it as his legs starting shaking again. “Where will I buy the boots?”

“You can purchase higher quality ones from the army,” Yoran said, “but I wouldn’t. There’s a good cobbler near the barracks, they’ve got a couple different levels of quality so that you can choose what best suits you. They’ll fit you better and break in faster while lasting longer. If you’d like, I can give you directions when we all return to the barracks.”

“Yeah, sure,” Ezra replied, already rethinking his original opinion of Yoran. “Thanks.”

“Sure,” came the reply with a beaming smile. “For now, warm up, I’ll see if I can get you something warm to eat. After all, you’re one of us, and you deserve your three meals a day.”

At those words, Ezra was suddenly ravenously hungry, and he remembered the small loaf the Madam’d provided for his breakfast. Another reminder of the trust she had in him. As his stomach growled its protest, Yoran nodded and walked out of the tent, leaving Ezra alone.
