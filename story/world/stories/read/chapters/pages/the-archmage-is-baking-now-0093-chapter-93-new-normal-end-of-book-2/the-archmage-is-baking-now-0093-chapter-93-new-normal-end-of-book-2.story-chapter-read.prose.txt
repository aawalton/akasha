Jane’s bathwater water turned gray almost immediately as days of road dust and sweat sloughed off into it. She scrubbed, washed her hair, then drew another bath just to rinse. She could hear Cecelia moving around in the shop below, setting up her cot. Jane had offered her the bed, but Cecelia had claimed to have slept worse places than a warm shop in an adequate cot. Jane believed her.

Jane dressed in clean clothes and climbed into bed. With all the things she was worried about, she might have thought that sleep would be difficult to get. As usual, it wasn’t. She fell asleep and woke up to the sun, much too late to restart her normal baking activities for the day. She supposed she had earned a bit of a respite, though, and decided to spend it catching up with her people.

She left her aunt to sleep, locked up the shop, and headed for Bella's. Jane was relieved to see that the crowd had thinned a bit compared to the last time she had tried to visit. There were still more customers than Bella typically served, but it was no longer the madhouse it had been in the days following the showcase.

"Jane!" Bella waved from behind her counter. "You're back."

"I am."

"I’m glad.” Bella slapped down several wrapped breakfast packages, more or less clearing the line in front of her shop in one fell swoop. “Hungry?"

"Very."

Bella assembled a plate and handed it over. Jane found a stool at the edge of the stall and settled in to eat while Bella worked on prepping food for her next wave of customers. Once things were sizzling and cooking on the griddle, she turned back.

"Looks like things are going back to normal," Jane said. “I was worried you’d be stuck working double-time forever.”

"It does look that way. I suppose I won't have to build a second stall after all."

“Is that good or bad?”

“On balance? I’d say pretty good.” Bella moved the sizzling ingredients to a less-hot part of the griddle to stay warm, then crossed over to sit by Jane. "It turns out I like my old normal better than my new one. It was nice to be exciting for a while, but I’m glad it’s over. I suppose things aren't going normally for you just yet."

Jane shook her head. "No. I suppose not. But I don’t want to talk about that right now, if it’s all right. What's been happening here while I was gone?"

"Some rumors about the prince, especially when you disappeared. Your shop is a form of town news now. Whenever it's closed, people assume something is going on. Nobody ever knew exactly what was happening, though, and now you are back. That’s over."

"How has everyone else been?"

"Normal, mostly. Allen is right back to doing normal tinker things. Emily has been spending the normal amount of time in the library again. The town is calming down, too. You’d think the royals had left already."

"That's because they stayed longer than was planned," Jane said. "They could only be novelties for so long. They should have been gone days ago."

Bella nodded, then glanced over Jane's shoulder. Another customer had appeared, politely waiting for his turn at breakfast. Jane didn’t have to be told that the shop came first, in these situations. Bella stood and pulled Jane into a hug.

"It's good you're back," she said. “Now just hold on one second before you go.”

She rushed back to the grill, did some quick work, and produced another wrapped package that she handed to Jane.

"For your aunt. Tell her I said hello."

Jane took the package and left Bella to her customer.

Cecelia was up and about when Jane returned. She brightened when she saw the wrapped package in Jane's hands, crossing the room in just a few tall-person steps while looking hopeful.

"Food?"

"From Bella. She says hello."

"Wonderful. I really do approve of that girl." Cecelia took the package and handed Jane a cup of tea she had brewed while Jane was out. "Come. Let's sit outside."

They went to the back yard and set up on the bench that most directly faced the water. Jane had always liked this view, even if she didn't take advantage of it as often as she should. Cecelia settled onto the bench with her and unwrapped her food, digging in right away. Jane sat beside her and sipped her tea, watching a small boat move across the water in the distance and feeling a bit jealous of its passengers.

"It's been a long time since I was out on the water," Jane said. "I should have Frank take us out. Or Allen, if he knows how to use a boat."

"Does he?"

Jane considered. “I don't know. I bet he could learn, though.”

They talked for a bit then about whether or not it was fair to mention Jane’s desire for Allen to sail a boat, considering the fact that he would almost assuredly learn if he knew she wanted it. They settled on her perhaps learning to do it herself, and taking him out. Cecelia thought it would be a nice surprise.

Dave showed up a bit later, poking his head around the corner of the building. He lit up when he saw Jane, but hesitated to interrupt. Jane waved for him to sit, and he did, settling onto a second bench a short distance away.

“Good to see you looking rested,” Dave said. “I was sorry when I saw how ragged you had run yourself.”

"Don’t be. I'm sorry for introducing you to Brand," Jane said, then paused to reconsider a bit. "A little sorry, I think. It all got so complex for you."

"No, no, it's fine." Dave shook his head. "Brand is a good guy. I'm glad I made a friend, even if he's leaving soon. It let me feel like I knew more about what was going on than someone else, for once."

"What about your wagon?" Jane asked. “I was worried about your horse.”

"A guard told me everything would be back in a few days. Just as soon as they can send word." Dave shifted, looking uncomfortable. "He also gave me a purse. Filled with coins. Way more than I should have made on the delivery job.”

He tossed it to Jane. She weighed it in her hand and found, yes, that it was far more weighty than she would expect from a simple delivery.

“I don't feel right taking it," Dave said. “It feels like too much.”

"You absolutely should take it," Cecelia said. "It's to pay you for your inconvenience, and it's a sign of the king's thanks. It would be much less comfortable to try and refuse it." She took another bite of her breakfast. "Besides, it's money. More money is always nice."

Dave seemed to agree with that logic, then jolted.

"I sorry, I’m being rude. I haven't introduced myself. Hello. I'm Dave."

"Jane's aunt," Cecelia said simply. Dave didn't seem to make the connection between that and the legend sitting in front of him. Jane was glad about that. “Nice to meet you.”

They shook hands, and Dave stood again, pushing the purse back into one of his pockets.

"I have another job lined up once my wagon is back, so you might not see me for a while. I'll send someone else to pick up the buns for the Underbridge Market while I'm gone."

"Thank you, Dave."

He nodded one last time and left, disappearing around the corner of the building. Jane watched him go, then laughed.

“He’s nice,” Jane said. “I’m glad they sent him here. And that he has work he likes. I think deliveries are about right for him.”

“Agreed. I wonder if he’ll take me on the next leg of my trip, if I go. He seems like he’d be a laugh to have along for a while.”

Jane and Cecelia sat around for a few hours after that, just wasting time. They talked about nothing in particular, moving from some of the more interesting bits of Cecelia’s trip north to Jane’s ever-changing opinions on the best kinds of shoes. None of it mattered much, which Jane felt was somewhat the point. The part that mattered, she felt, was that they loved each other, and loved being together.

She hadn't been expecting a visit, but sitting here with her aunt reminded Jane how much she missed Cecelia whenever she was gone. Her aunt was one of her very favorite people, and always would be in the specific way they related to each other. Cecelia made everything feel more manageable just by being nearby, like there was no problem so big they couldn’t tackle it together.

Even so, there was only so much time two healthy people could sit before they got bored. They were just about to go find something else to do when Brand showed up, poking his head around the corner of the building just as Dave had done earlier. He looked a bit tired, but not unhappy.

"Brand!" Jane waved toward the bench Dave had vacated. "Sit with us.”

"No, I'm sorry." He shook his head. "I have to leave. We’re going back to the capital. My parents insist. Considering how long we’ve been away from the royal court, I’m not surprised. I barely got enough time to come here and say goodbye."

Heading back to the capital didn’t sound ideal. Jane winced. "So things didn't go well?"

"No, absolutely they did." Brand's smile widened. "My father and mother listened. More than they ever have before, I think thanks to you."

"So what's next, then?" Jane asked. “After the capital, I mean.”

"I don't know for sure. But my father said they'll start to work out a way for me to disconnect from being royalty. Just to be a person. I think I might try to be a courier. That way, I can come and go before people figure out who I am.”

Jane laughed at that. "Well, if you need a place to call home, Glenfall is always here."

Brand shook his head.

"No, probably not. I'm a little too famous here, at the moment. Maybe one day."

Jane stood and pulled him into a hug. He stiffened for just a moment, then relaxed into it.

"Well," she said, stepping back, "I'm glad for you. Sorry we didn’t get married."

"Ha! I'm glad it worked out the way it did." Brand straightened his coat and peeked around the corner. It seemed his time with them was running short. “I'm not the king, Jane. But I absolutely owe you a favor.”

Then he was gone, disappearing around the corner of the building the same way he had come.

Jane sat back down on the bench beside her aunt, exhaling long and slow.

"I think I like this favor better," Jane said. "Somehow."

Cecelia nodded. "Yes. I would too."

Epilogue

The shop was very busy, and Jane was hustling to keep up. This was the last batch of bread for the day, and she pulled several very plain loaves out of the oven before drawing a quick circle on the counter and pushing magic through it. Cold air washed over the bread, cooling it in seconds.

"This still seems wasteful," she said.

Bella, Brit, and Allen all looked up at once, their expressions suggesting she had lost her mind.

"Don't be crazy," Bella said. "This is duck day. The ducks are back. They'll probably stop here for the next several days. It would be absolutely insane not to have bread for them."

It had been months since the king and queen had left, and the ducks were the next big significant visitors since then. Even now, Jane could hear them quacking away on the water outside, milling around and looking for food. They seemed to have identified her house as a source of bread, and she wondered if she would get any peace that night while she tried to sleep. Even if she didn't, this was but a single stop on the ducks' migration. They had flocks of them for a few days, she'd been told. Then they would be gone.

"Are you really upset about them?" Bella asked. "For disrupting things?"

"No," Jane said. It was weird to her in a way she was sure would eventually wear off, but it was far from bad. "This is the kind of disruption I can handle. Even if it wasn't, I can’t complain. My shop has been open all but some rest days for the last few months. That's some kind of new record, I think."

"You like normal that much?" Allen asked.

Jane stepped across the room and sat in his lap. He looked surprised, just like he did every time she did it.

Her ‘normal’ these days, she thought, was these friends. It was pretty good.

"Yes," she said. "I do."
