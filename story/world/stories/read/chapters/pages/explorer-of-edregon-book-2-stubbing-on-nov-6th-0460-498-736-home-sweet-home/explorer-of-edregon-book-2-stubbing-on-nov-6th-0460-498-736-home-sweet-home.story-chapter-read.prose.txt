Vin, Shia, Scule, and Reginald spent the next two days within the Beacon. Shia spent most of her time simply being with Tabetha and helping reinforce the fact that the divigel wasn’t alone anymore. Scule and Reginald scoured the structure from top to bottom, no doubt finding all sorts of interesting trinkets that were quickly hidden away within the Rogue’s cape. For his part, Vin took advantage of the wait to work on his latest spell.

Well, that and play around with his new belt.

“Woo!” he shouted, laughing as he flew from the bottom of the Beacon to the top, taking advantage of its hollow core to practice his flying. The sensation of actually flying rather than flipping gravity around and falling through the sky was an amazing one, and he didn’t think he’d ever get tired of it.

Vin had done the right thing and revealed the belt to Tabetha along with the magic key and other contents of the safe, but she had been more than happy to let him keep the belt as thanks for retrieving the key for her. With that in her possession, she was finally able to leave the Beacon without worry of not being able to get back in should the door close behind her for any reason. In fact, she was currently flying outside with Shia, taking a look at how the fragment borders worked on Edregon.

Carefully touching down on one of the middle layers of the Beacon, Vin smiled as the sensation of weightlessness left him. While convenient and much easier to maneuver around in the air, the belt wasn’t without its own drawbacks. As much fun as flying was, it wasn’t nearly as fast as Redirect Gravity, which was really just free falling in a direction of his choice. On top of which, he could tell that the belt wasn’t able to keep him afloat forever. The mana contained within would definitely last a good while, but eventually, he’d have to give it some time to replenish. As far as he could tell, it seemed about fifteen minutes was the limit.

Unfortunately, he didn’t seem to be able to push mana into artifacts like he could people to replenish their mana stores, which was one of the first things he’d tried. It seemed for an artifact to be capable of absorbing mana directly from a mage, it had to be designed in such a way, such as his enchanted waterskin. And that wasn’t refilling its mana as much as just forcing it to make extra water.

And speaking of water…

“Have you finished playing around with the artifact?” Ovord asked, raising a judgmental eyebrow at him once Vin slipped back into Introspection. “For one who harps on a lack of time so much, you certainly enjoy spending it frivolously.”

“I was flying. Come on, the whole point of learning magic in the first place is to do fun stuff like that,” Vin grumbled, whipping up the runic formation he’d been working on the past few days. Despite his newest spell being only a tier-2, it was proving to be a bit trickier than he’d originally expected. Though as Ovord continued to oh so helpfully point out, the block was almost entirely his own way of thinking.

“It is practically the same runic formation with tweaks here, here, and here,” the former Arch Mage repeated, shaking his head as he walked around the spell in progress and pointed out the troublesome areas. “You should not be struggling so much.”

“Air is already surrounding everything! Trying to get magic to do something I know is already happening is tricky, alright?” Vin muttered, knowing arguing with the Arch Mage was pointless as he went back at it. “Not to mention I need to be able to actively shift it around, or it’s largely pointless.”

“Then it sounds like you need to shift the spell to be a tier-3,” Ovord pointed out. “The additional mobility isn’t something you can cram into the tier-2 formation as it is.”

Vin grumbled, but got to work doing just that, realizing he could borrow from Magic Lock to help keep the spell in place around his target. The addition meant the spell was going to take even longer to figure out, but it would hopefully be well worth it in the end. A nonlethal method of taking down pulmons was just what he needed, and he felt confident this was the safest way to go about doing so.

Now that he had a better idea of what he wanted, Vin managed to make some more progress, and he eventually came out of Introspection to find Shia waiting patiently for him to return to the land of the living.

“She’s made up her mind,” Shia told him, giving him a big smile. “Tabetha wants to come join Terra.”

“Should be interesting,” Vin yawned. Cracking his back, he looked around. “Where is she?”

“Putting together her belongings. Where’s Scule? He’s hiding from my Magical Bond, probably just because he can.”

“Not a clue,” Vin admitted. Throwing together Sense Soul, he pointed down below them. “Roughly twenty floors in that direction, doing who knows what.”

“If Reginald wasn’t with him, I’d be a bit more worried,” Shia admitted. “Anyway, I’m going to go with Tabetha back to Terra and help her get situated. Maybe introduce her to the elves in the Sacred Forest as well. I’ll probably be tapping out of the remainder of your journey through the third ring. Are you going to be okay with just the three of you?”

“Your Magical Bond wouldn’t happen to know if Alka’s back in Terra yet, would it?” Vin asked hopefully. “Ideally I’d like to have a little more stopping power for venturing into unknown fragments. Scule’s poisons are certainly deadly, but all it takes is going up against something poison doesn’t work against and I’m functionally on my own.”

“Yeah, I’m pretty certain she’s back,” Shia said, scrunching up her face as she focused on her Capstone. “It’s a little tricky to read exact distances, especially at this range.”

“Cool. In that case, we can all hit Terra together. Just come poke me once Tabetha is ready,” Vin said, slipping back into Introspection to get a little last-minute practice in.

It took another few hours for Tabetha to finish saying her goodbyes to the Beacon, and then they were off. Vin used Slow Fall to bring them all carefully to the ground, and then they set off for the new dungeon.

“You know, if it weren’t for the fact that the door was located half a mile into the sky, I’d sort of expect Spur to try and convince Terra to go live out of the Beacon instead, what with how well defended it is,” Scule pointed out.

“Not a chance,” Vin said. “Remember, he originally wanted to make Terra into a military base rather than a town, and people didn’t like that idea one bit. Nobody wants to live out the remainder of their lives in a stone bunker, never able to feel the sun on their faces. No offence Tabetha.”

“None taken,” she smiled, practically glowing just from being outside with them. “The Beacon wasn’t anyone’s dream, it was a last-ditch plan to try and save people from the monsters. While I don’t think I can forgive the Gods for what they did to my people, I can at least be grateful they managed to solve the rising monster problem. Being able to wander around like this is amazing!”

“Just be careful. The monsters may have grown less in number, but they still exist,” Shia said, gripping her staff as the ground started shaking again. “Speaking of… you should take to the sky.”

Tabetha did just that, flapping her wings and shooting up without waiting for an explanation. No sooner had she done so that another one of the giant snakes burst out of the ground in the center of their group, hissing as it turned and tried to sink its teeth into Vin’s head.

It was almost cute in a way.

“Stone Wall,” Vin called out, easily blocking the monster with a dense pillar of rock. At the same instance, three darts landed in quick succession along the snake’s neck, magically punching straight through its dense scales thanks to Scule’s latest Capstone. Before the poison could even take effect, a dense, pointed branch exploded out of Shia’s staff, piercing through its flesh and out the other side.

Less than three seconds after emerging from the ground, the monster collapsed, dead before it even hit the ground.

“Show off,” Scule snorted as he tucked his blowgun back into his cape. “My poison had it.”

“Guess I’m just used to you not really doing much during these fights,” Shia teased, decaying the branch back into her staff.

“You guys are amazing!” Tabetha said, her eyes wide as she landed beside the dead monster and stared at them in awe. “You just took that thing down like that! You didn’t even communicate, it was like you all knew exactly what the other one would do before they even did it!”

“Guess it comes from spending the better part of a year fighting monsters alongside one another,” Vin shrugged, blushing slightly at the unexpected praise. “Terra would be happy to help train you to fight as well if you want. I bet Phil is practically drooling over the idea of getting a genuine flier to act as eyes in the sky.”

“Oh, I don’t know about that,” Tabetha said, suddenly looking far less excited about their monster fight. “I’ve never really fought anything before.”

“It’s not something you need to worry about right now,” Shia said, taking her hand and continuing toward the dungeon once more. “Let’s just get you to Terra in one piece.”

The dungeon in Tabetha’s fragment was a tunnel style, leading straight down into the ground. After fighting their way through a few more subterranean monsters, they entered the Underside, which got the usual look of awe from the divigel while she was led through it. Once they’d cleared the Earther forest, Vin smiled as Terra’s walls came into view.

“We’ve only got a week before our first festival,” Vin explained as Tabetha’s wide eyes swept around town, taking in the bustling people running every which way. Everyone seemed too focused on what they were doing, few even stopped to stare at the winged woman walking into town, much less Vin and his friends. “Most are probably trying to put finishing touches on their current projects or get that last bit of experience needed to show off a new ability. It’s not normally quite so hectic.”

“Yo, Vin,” Scule muttered, pointing off to the side. “Theodore at four-o-clock.”

Sure enough, what looked like an old, western cowboy complete with giant hat and twin six-shooters secured on his belt was standing at the end of the path, his fingers twitching as he glanced up at Vin from beneath the brim of his hat. A tumbleweed of all things slowly bounced along the path, as the exact tune that sounded in every western film known to man rang through the air to signal the duel that was about to happen.

“Seriously?” Vin asked, throwing an exasperated look at the man hiding behind a stack of barrels with an instrument in hand. He recognized him as one of the Bards who often played music in Terra’s restaurants for people to enjoy. “How did Theodore get you to go along with this?”

“He promised me one of his ‘leave me alone’ tickets,” the Bard said, grinning sheepishly.

“How did he do the tumbleweed?” Scule muttered, his brow furrowed in thought as he watched it continue on down the path.

“Theodore, this is only a quick visit, so unless you’ve got something important, I need to run!” Vin called out. “Also, we have a new member of Terra here, and I don’t want you freaking her out!”

Instantly, the cowboy shimmered and was replaced by Theodore, who smiled as he walked over to them. “My apologies, miss, I didn’t mean to startle you. Might I say, I love your wings!”

“Oh!” Tabetha said, blinking and returning Theodore’s gleaming smile. “Thank you!”

“Trust me, this one is more trouble than he’s worth,” Shia muttered, taking Tabetha’s shoulders and steering her around the Spy. “I’ll get her situated with Linda. Good luck with the rest of the third ring, Vin!”

“I think I’ll take my leave as well. I want to show those tainted water samples to Bill,” Scule said, giving Theodore a lazy wave before hopping onto Reginald and taking off.

“So, is this the usual greeting, or do you need me for something?” Vin asked once his friends were gone.

“A bit of both, I suppose,” Theodore grinned, before turning serious. “It’s about the neilans. Tom and Vanessa have been working them ever since what happened with the seventh wave, and they finally got them to crack. The neilans have agreed to lend us their ritual expertise to help us figure out a way to prevent what happened with the seventh wave from happening with the remaining waves.”
