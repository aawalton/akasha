Chapter V.XXXII (5.32) - Combat Rank

Hours of meditation passed. Kizu’s knees felt like they’d been mashed into paste by a mortar and pestle. Still, he refused to complain and instead ignored the pain as he sorted through his thoughts and memories and cleared his mind.

Roba sat at her desk, completely engrossed in her work. The only noises in the room were the scratching of her quill and the occasional fish bumping into the glass wall. A part of him suspected these meditation techniques were just a means in which to silence him so she could work on her paperwork without interruption.

“Congratulations to those remaining!” a booming voice announced.

The sudden noise knocked Kizu over. He groaned and rolled over on the floormat. He tried to bend his legs and the movement sent spikes of pain through his leg. So he just remained staring up at Roba’s office ceiling. Mort, who’d dozed off hours ago while staring at a star fish sealed on the other side of the glass wall, jerked awake and scampered over to Kizu’s side.

“We have four champions of this challenge. Basil Ballerfulur, Faible Joon Artagnan, Tip Green, and Kaga Kizu. The rest of you are now granted movement once again. Hopefully your nap wasn’t too uncomfortable! You have one hour to reassemble in the courtyard. Failure to reconvene in this timeframe will result in complete disqualification.”

To Kizu’s shock, he recognized each of the names listed. In hindsight, it made sense that Basil and Faible would be well suited for this sort of challenge. They were both clever and magically equipped for deception. Tip Green, the only champion who wasn’t one of Kizu’s friends, was a pal of Harvey’s. Tip was also the only one of Harvey’s new friends that Kizu had any positive interactions with. While Kizu didn’t know the fifth-year’s abilities, he knew enough about that clique to not be surprised that one of them would be equipped to survive this challenge.

Kizu attempted to stand and toppled over as his legs gave out under his weight. He closed his eyes and stifled a groan. After counting to five, he righted himself and stumbled up to his feet.

Roba, not even bothering to look up from her paperwork, healed Kizu’s knees with a flick of her fingers. “Report back to me in two weeks on your progress with the exercises I gave you.”

“Thank you,” Kizu said. He took a step in the direction of the exit. Then he paused. “Is it actually safe now? Or is this an attempt to lure us out?”

Roba chortled. “It’s safe. Clever thinking though. Stay suspicious. It will serve you well in life.”

Kizu left the office and retraced his footsteps. He passed a moaning Gregor, sitting under the pendulum and massaging his leg where a glittering blue handprint was imprinted.

As he walked he heard someone balling. Against his better judgement, he peeked into the Art Club room. A pile of painted wood scrapes, deformed statues, and easels were scattered around the door, like something had burst through a makeshift barricade. Curled up in a ball amongst the whirlwind of colors that coated the room's interior, was the student who’d slammed the door as he’d approached during the challenge.

He slowly started to close the door in an effort to creep away.

“Kaga Kizu?”

He stopped, the door nearly closed. He hadn’t seen her in the corner, but a second student stepped into the light. She was wearing a rainbow smock which blended in with the chaotically colorful room, which had likely contributed to his lack of detection of her. Her face was smeared with clay and paint but Kizu still recognized her.

As she strolled up to him, as she passed the crying student on the floor, the girl looked up. The defeated girl’s face was red and swollen from tears and glared at the other girl’s back.

“Ah, Choko, right?” Kizu asked the approaching girl. “You’re in my Illusions A class and tried to recruit me to the Art Club at the start of the semester.”

Choko sighed dramatically. “And failed. Just like I failed this challenge. Walk with me?”

“Depends. Are you going to keep trying to recruit me?”

The girl burped out a laugh. She blushed and spoke quickly as if to cover up the outburst. “Nah. Too late now. Maybe next year.”

Rather than enter the underground, they strolled through the academy corridors. All around them, students with glittering blue markings were converging into the hallway from their hiding places.

“I almost had it too,” Choko said with a sigh. “I managed to disguise my scent with an illusion and was hiding in the club’s room. I figured I had the home field advantage there. Then that other student barged in and drew attention to my spot.”

“The builder of the broken barricade?” Kizu guessed. The girl who’d been crying on the floor was the obvious suspect.

Choko’s face fell into her hands. “So. Much. Unnecessary. Noise.”

“Still, you must have made a decent rank,” Kizu said. “It looked like nearly half the student body got knocked out in the first fifteen minutes.”

“We’ll see.” The girl didn’t sound confident. “How’d you survive until the end?”

“I fought my way past a few professors and then hid in one of their offices.”

“You… you fought multiple professors?” Choko took a step away from him as they walked and eyed him head to toe.

Mort, perched on his shoulder, stuck his tongue out at her when her gaze lingered on him.

“Just lucky. It’s not like I actually beat any of them. I just ran away.”

“Which professors?”

“Arclight chased me into Krimpit’s area, Knoff ambushed Mort, and Oasaji’s dogs were hunting everyone in the hallways.”

“Four Professors?” Choko choked on her exclamation.

“I never actually saw Arclight or Oasaji close up. And Mort escaped Knoff. Really, all I managed to do was slip away from Krimpit, who teaches history and politics. Not exactly an impressive showing.”

“Not impressive.” Choko shook her head. “I got taken down just by Oasaji. His wolf pinned me down and he trod over my body while I squirmed on the ground.” She raised her forearm, revealing a turkey’s footprint branded in sparkling blue.

Choko continued to vent about how the other student in the club room had broken the leg off of one of her clay statue projects while piling up the ineffectual barricade. That led into a conversation about clay golems. While Kizu knew very little about the process of golem making, he found it interesting to listen to her. He mentioned the invisible golem he’d fought in Tross and her eyes lit up in excitement. Before he realized what he was doing, Kizu had offered some of the ichor he’d brewed for the golem body he’d given to Chiame. He passed her a small vial of the liquid that he stored in his ring.

“You have no idea how valuable that is,” Choko said, a bit more pep in her step as she held the glass bottle to her chest. “Thank you so much!”

Kizu shrugged. “I really don’t have any use for it anymore now that I gave the golem away.” As a witch, Kizu had no doubt Chiame had already recreated the ichor for herself using ingredients down in the World Dungeon. “If you do find a use for it, just let me know and I’ll consider it an even trade.”

They approached the courtyard and Choko slowed down a bit. Kizu glanced at his classmate. She tugged on a lock of her brown hair and then fiddled with her smock’s collar.

“I… don’t suppose you’d be interested in getting lunch together sometime?” Her eyes nervously flitted over to him then darted off to the side.

“Ah.” Kizu winced. “Sorry. I’m seeing someone.”

“Exclusively?” Choko tried, a note of desperate hope in her voice.

“Exclusively,” Kizu confirmed. He felt awful as he watched her hopes shatter before his eyes. It wouldn’t be so bad if the girl wasn’t so expressive, showing every emotion on her face.

“Best ones are always taken,” Choko bemoaned quietly.

“You know, my pal Faible—”

“Sorry. But I really don’t want to be set up by someone who just rejected me.” Choko laughed. “Maybe suggest it again in like a month?”

“Sure. Sorry about that.” He felt it was a bit unfair that he now felt guilty. It wasn’t like he’d rejected her for any reason other than circumstance. But better to just step aside.

They split up as they entered the courtyard and Kizu found Sene, Basil, Aoi, and Ione in a clump off to the side.

“Well done, Basil,” Kizu said as a greeting.

Basil preened at the praise, head held high. “Well done, yourself!”

“I saw your guys’ battle with Arclight,” Kizu said to Ione and Sene. “You two nearly had her.”

“Hardly,” Sene replied dryly. She barely acknowledged him, instead her gaze was fixated on the list of students at the front of the courtyard. It had yet to update with the results.

Ione gripped her shirt over her heart, eyes wide. “You watched your girlfriend getting beaten to a pulp and stood to the side as a spectator? You brute! Have you no heart?”

Kizu rolled his eyes. “You didn’t get beaten to a pulp. Arclight just pried you out of the dirt. And what was I supposed to do? Wakino was actively disrupting spatial jumps.”

“Heartless!” Ione cried out.

Basil nodded stoically in agreement.

“How did you two end up together?” Kizu asked them.

Ione shrugged, her dramatics fading in an instant. “I donno. We just popped up together over there.”

“Likely due to their connection via their souls,” Aoi said. “Nothing else makes sense. For example, I assume your brother didn’t appear with you?”

“No. I have no idea where Finn ended up.”

“Likewise, I never saw Akira during the challenge either. So, I doubt it has to do with simple blood relations.”

Before they could continue their conversation, up on the roof, Arclight clapped her hands together. The impact’s shockwave silenced every person in the courtyard as dust blasted backwards. Kizu had to pop his ears from the sudden change in air pressure.

“Look at all of these delightful faces!” she boomed. “I see a few of you acquired a little keepsake from me and my colleagues. They look very nice!”

The expressions in the crowd were a mixed bag. Embarrassment, humor, anger, frustration. Kizu believed he was probably one of the only ones here who felt pride. After all, he’d avoided the dreaded handprint that marred nearly every other student.

“First, let us view your current placement in the tournament!” A massive scroll unfurled with glowing names.

Kizu didn’t need to look far to spot his name. #1. The very summit of all the contenders. He was, after all, the only student to ace both the first and second tournament challenges. And he wasn’t the only one who took notice. All around him, his peers were giving him sidelong glances and he heard his name muttered by several people in the crowd.

“383,” Sene said the number with absolute disgust. “The first challenge’s hiccup was not anything to be concerned about. But this is truly unacceptable.”

Sene standing in the middle of the pack was surprising. But Ione had stolen her token at the very start of the first challenge. While, as far as Kizu knew, Sene had stolen her own target’s token just as early on, it still greatly damaged her score to be robbed so early. And for her and Ione to both go down to Arclight in the first twenty minutes of the second challenge…well, there were plenty of people with worse results. But it hurt her standing a great deal.

“I know many of you are likely feeling a bit put out!” Arclight said. “I know, I promise that two stealth and information gathering challenges in a row irks me as much as it does you! Nevertheless, these are vital parts of combat and we felt it essential to help the students who cannot compete in the standard weekend matches. Combat is far more than punching!

“And yet, that’s not to say we’re forgoing punching either! As many of you know from previous years, our final challenge will take place during the final two weeks of the semester. A bracketed tournament. While in previous years we’ve revealed the bracket immediately, we felt that since you’ve already completed an information gathering challenge that it might be prudent to test your skills against unknown opponents. And so, no bracket will be showcased until the day of the challenge. That means you should train every aspect of your power in preparation!”

Kizu reflected that, while annoying, in some ways this would actually be helpful. It meant more time dedicated to practicing his magic and less time spent studying his potential opponents.

“Finally, how about we take a look at how these last two challenges have altered all of your rankings?” Arclight raised both arms, one of flesh and the other a prosthetic artifact that gleamed in the sunlight.

A second massive scroll unfurled.

This time it took Kizu a minute to find his name on the list. Sene, despite her recent blunders, still claimed that first place position. Weston remained number two on the list. It probably helped her that he’d also been taken out quickly in the second challenge. But no shift in the top two didn’t mean their positions were just as stable as they had been before the tournament began. There just wasn’t a visual marker of how far they’d fallen.

Kizu’s vision traced down the list. Then it snagged on a name.

Harvey was now rank 99. And, immediately below, Kizu sat at rank 100.
