Daevon and I flew for hours.

Once we passed the borders of our faction, we started being notified by the system each time we entered a foreign territory.

You have entered a territory currently claimed by another faction.

I read the name of the current owner.

Tremordia

It didn’t sound particularly human.

We kept moving, and more faction names came and went without one that caught my attention.

As stunning as the view was, after about an hour or so, I began to feel restless.

Our mission to find other human factions was important to me. And although I was taking it very seriously, it was hard not to enjoy the fact that I was flying. With a thought, I urged my spirit wings to swoop low just above the treetops.

The magical fabric flapped at my ankles and wrists pleasantly. I conjured my helmet and raid pattered against the spirit steel. The wings responded precisely to what my thoughts were directing.

I imagined a barrel roll and felt my stomach turn as the wings wrapped my body while I twisted above the branches. I dropped slightly before the wings opened again using the relative wind to drive me higher again.

The experience was everything I’d imagined and more.

I thought of a few more manuevers and as before my robe responded and I looped and twisted through the open air.

Rain drenched me but it wasn’t cold and somehow the moisture never seemed to reach my skin.

We passed into a new Territory.

You have entered a territory currently claimed by another faction.

Again, like the others, I read the name of the faction.

Ricardos Place

Now that seemed to be the name of a human faction.

I craned my neck to look up at Daevon. He was already looking my way, likely because he’d seen the notification also. I gave him a thumbs up, and he nodded and began coasting downwards toward me. My wings pumped as I rose to meet him.

“We just entered a territory that’s owned by humans.” I told him, as I pulled out my map. I’d been rewarded with the map after defeating the dark elf bandits, and it was quietly becoming one of my favorite rewards.

Like in a video game, the places I traveled were automatically mapped driving away the fog of war.

His eyes scanned the tops of the trees.

“The forest is thick. Will we be able to see them from the sky?”

“Probably.” I said, shrugging. “I bet they have a settlement somewhere that should be easy enough to spot. Let’s circle the area and see what we find.”

He bowed his head in agreement, and we took off in search of signs of life. A few minutes of gliding around later Daevon waved, directing my attention to a thin puff of smoke that barely survived long enough in the rain to rise above the trees.

I gave him a thumbs up.

“We should probably land, and walk in. It just feels weird to fly in unannounced.”

Daevon didn’t seem to care either way, so we glided back down to earth and jogged the rest of the way towards where we’d seen the smoke.

It wasn’t hard to find, and at first glance, it looked promising.

The buildings were all made of logs, but they were built with care and obvious intent. Judging by the number of structures, I estimated it was likely a population of a few hundred people at most.

Daevon and I looked from a distance, hidden behind a wall of orange and yellow leaves, as human people strolled between buildings.

“They seem normal, right?”

I looked at Daevon and a small spark jumped between his antlers. He peered through the leaves and scratched the fur on his chin.

After a second, I decided that maybe I was playing a little fast and loose with the term normal. But at the very least, they looked human. And more importantly, they were passing the vibe check.

There was something that bothered me though. We were less than a hundred yards outside of their settlement, and they seemed to have no idea we were there.

Jared had purchased several security upgrades to our faction. He would’ve been warned the second an intruder’s foot passed across the invisible boundaries.

It seemed as though this particular faction hadn’t invested in the same thing. Actually, now that I thought about it, I didn’t think any of the other factions had whatever Jared bought. I thought that was weird, especially with how helpful it had been having it for our faction.

Oh well.

“Rock, paper, scissors on who does the talking?” I asked Daevon sarcastically.

He cocked an eyebrow. “Rock, paper, scissors?”

“Don’t worry about it, I’ll show you later.” I consoled him, pushing aside some of the leafy branches.

“Alright then, Let’s see how this goes.”

Daevon, who wasn’t much for words, followed me through the wet leaves and together we trekked our way towards the small settlement.

After only a few steps it became pretty apparent that we were spotted. What tipped me off was the blaring horn that sounded from a tower towards the center of the town. My face scrunched up at the obnoxious noise and then it scrunched up even more when I saw the people in the town go from calm to frantic.

The horn was obviously some kind of an alarm, and as frantic as the people seemed, what was also obvious was this was not their first time it had gone off.

Everyone dropped whatever they were doing and sprinted full speed in one direction. From where I was standing, it was impossible to see where they were all running too. So, I chased after them.

Even with the nearly hundred-yard head start I caught up to the rear villager in seconds, a teenage girl in overalls and blonde pigtails.

The young woman spared a glance over her shoulder and yelped when she saw I was right behind her. I considered reaching out to stop her but figured it might give the wrong impression.

I opted instead to just follow along and jog after her. At this leisurely pace I was able to really take in the sights of the small village. It was a quaint little mountain town with attention to aesthetics. Many of the cabin homes had patios with wood crafted furniture and pleasant gardens.

Whoever these people were, they were building as though they intended to stay. So why were they all running away? How had this little village survived so long if they turned tail and ran at the first glimpse of a threat?

After a short chase I arrived at what the villagers were retreating towards.

People filed in two lines into what was essentially a cube of logs. If I wanted to, I could’ve easily followed the girl into the log building. I didn’t though.

These people were not fighters. They were too slow.

I heard Daevon arrive behind me. He didn’t chase them either following my lead of watching them. At the entrance of the building, a man with dark brown skin and straw hat ushered people in one by one. His dark brown eyes followed me and Daevon closely.

The creases in the forehead indicated his concern, but also maybe curiosity. It’s not like the man couldn’t tell that I was a human. They all could. So, were they just running because I was with Daevon?

I raised my hand and with a half grin waved politely at the man. His concerned creases deepened.

Once the last person passed by him, he stepped backwards into the building to shove the heavy wooden door from inside, slamming it closed. There was a heavy thump a moment later as they barricaded the other side.

I crossed my arms and Daevon stepped up beside me, scratching his chin again.

“You scared them away.”

I raised an eyebrow. Humor? From Daevon?

“That would be a first.” I muttered while walking over to the log structure. If anyone was the scary one here, it was Daevon.

I rested my hand on one of the trunks.  It wasn’t a very pretty building, but it looked sturdy.

Was this really supposed to keep someone like me or Daevon out if we really applied ourselves?

I pulled out Tranquility and felt the soft leather grip resting on my palm as I placed the tip of the blue spirit sword against one of the logs and leaned against it. The tip might as well have been resting on concrete for how much the blade bit into the wood.

“Huh...”

I let more of my body weight rest against the pommel of Tranquility. As a spirit weapon, I knew the blade was as sharp as a razor, but even with the bulk of my mass resting against it, the wood stubbornly refused it.

Jared once explained to me how crafters applied their skills. So, I was aware that a skilled crafter could enhance the durability of their creations.

I rested Tranquility on my shoulder to scowl at the log. The air started to fuzz. I backed up a few steps.

BOOOM!

A crisp bolt of lightning sizzled past me and stuck like a semi truck against the log wall. I pursed my lips and looked back at Daevon and together we approached where the shot landed.

There was a quarter-sized scorch mark that was still smoking. Daevon licked his finger and pressed it to the hole, causing it to hiss.

The two of us shared a look.

“Pretty tough.” Daevon noted.

“Pretty tough.” I agreed.

We were both still examining the scorch mark when a whistling noise caused my ears to perk up. I was just about to turn around when something struck my barrier and rebounded into the dirt at my feet. I looked down to see an arrow planted in the ground between my feet.

Both of us whipped around to face at least a hundred, or more, armed warriors sprinting full speed towards us.

I summoned my helmet, and Tranquilities shadows.

“Try not to kill if them if you can help it. If we can’t get them to surrender, finish them quickly.”

Daevon’s eyes became a raging storm, clouds roiling from under his robe lifting him into the air, a staff held aloft at his side.

“If they are wise, they will surrender.” His voice crackled with energy. A shiver ran down my spine.

No kidding. I thought to myself.

Several arrows whistled toward Daevon.

He didn’t even need my barrier.

Thin bolts of lightning flicked out like dark whips vaporizing the projectiles.

Not wanting to feel outdone, I planted my feet and kicked off in a burst of motion using the skill dash to propel myself into the oncoming attackers like a rocket.

They weren’t prepared for my speed, and I had to jump slightly to plant an open palm into the chest plate of the first enemy in my path. They were wearing armor that was high-quality polished steel and carrying a mean looking halberd that was taller than I was. My strike wasn’t enough to dent the armor, but it was enough to send the person inside reeling backwards with a heavy grunt.

My opening attack was enough to put the other warriors on notice.

Whoever these people were, they looked competent. Most if not all had full gear suited for their respective classes, some in suits of armor, others in lighter leather padded protection and even some in robes.

The one I’d struck was completely covered in armor from head to toe, but there were plenty of others that weren’t, and the ones who weren’t immediately looked familiar. They were the same lizard looking people who we’d freed Sadie’s faction from.

I felt like I was beginning to see what was going on here.

More human slaves.

It wasn’t a surprise. Apparently, it would be a common theme for my new existence. Still, enough felt off that I wasn’t ready to just go genocidal quite yet.

CRACK!

A bolt of lightning rained from above and one of the archers in front of me dropped in a heap. Daevon’s attack was far from his true power, and I doubted the archer was dead. But it was more than enough to stun the lizard man.

Light from skills and spells flashed as Tranquility’s shadows intercepted them with uncanny precision. Some of the spells couldn’t be blocked with the phantom swords though and several washed against my barrier.

I didn’t waste using dome of protection since I was already moving before the barrage of attacks was even over.

It was clear from my next targets’ face, that it hadn’t expected me to survive the attacks, so when I emerged unharmed, trailing smoke, the tall lizard nearly tripped over its own feet trying to run.

I punched the taller lizard man in its face and felt pieces of bones shift underneath my hand.

I’d expected the lightly armored lizard man to have higher constitution or strength. But it didn’t, and so the body folded behind me while I open hand slapped a mage conjuring a fireball. It surprised me when I felt the scaly skin against my hand.

In my experience, most magic based classes used some kind of barrier since they didn’t wear armor.

The rainy evening air was filled with the crack of lighting and slaps as I wound my way through the lizards, dropping them one open hand at a time. I would’ve stowed Tranquility, but I didn’t like the thought of not having the extra layer of protection her shadows offered.

I was probably being overly cautious.

In my mind I could almost feel Tranquilities indignation. She wanted to join the fight. There was no point though.

Another bulky lizard man fell to a well-placed back hand strike. I felt like I was fighting children.

Not that I’d ever back hand slapped a child across the face, but if I did, it would probably go about the same.

I started to feel a little guilty. Maybe it would be better if I did just kill them, instead of embarrassing them like this?

As if in response to his thoughts a female voice shouted over the chaos.

“OK! You’ve made your point! We surrender!”
