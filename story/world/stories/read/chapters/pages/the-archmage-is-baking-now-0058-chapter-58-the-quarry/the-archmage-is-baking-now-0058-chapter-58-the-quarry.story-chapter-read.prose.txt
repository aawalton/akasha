"I can show you everything we do here,” Eustice said, leading Jane and Bella towards the quarry. “And if you need to see the proper mine afterward, I'll guide you through that, too."

He paused, looking slightly apologetic. "Though I should warn you, that'll take a lot longer than a single morning. The tunnels go on for quite a ways. Sorry for the work that's coming."

Jane was about to respond when a sound rang out around them, a deep, percussive boom that she felt in her chest as much as heard. Her magical senses flared instinctively, and she caught the tail end of something powerful moving through the air. Fiery magic, concentrated and violent, there and gone in an instant.

"What was that?" she asked.

"Blasting." Eustice spoke casually, the way someone might mention rain. "Alchemists bring the bombs for us. We score the rock first, drill holes in it, then set the charges. Breaks the stone apart so we can move it."

"Bombs.” Bella looked toward the quarry with new wariness. “You use bombs. Should we really go down there?"

Eustice held up his hands. "They are controlled, I promise. Very controlled. Trying to cut this rock without them would take forever. We do lose some stone along the way, just because a few fragments are too broken up to use for anything but gravel. But this method makes the extraction much easier overall."

Jane filed that information away. Alchemical explosives were not uncommon in mining operations. The magical signature she had felt was new in her experience, but it matched up with her understanding of how explosives worked.

"Shall we?" Eustice led them toward a wooden platform at the quarry's edge, a lift of sorts operated by a system of ropes and pulleys. "This'll take us down to the bottom. Watch your step."

The descent was slow and steady. The platform creaked slightly as it lowered them into the pit. Jane tried to ignore her fear of vehicles of this type and used the time to probe the magic in the area, letting her senses extend outward into the surrounding rock. She felt mostly what she expected to feel. Stone had its own signature, and there was nothing obviously wrong with it here.

When they reached the bottom, she stepped off the platform and looked around. The quarry floor was a chaos of activity. Some men worked at the rock faces with picks and chisels, while others loaded cut blocks onto carts. Near the center of the quarry floor, she spotted a group of men working a pump. Water had accumulated in a shallow pool, and they were laboriously forcing it up through a pipe that presumably led somewhere outside the pit.

"Why isn't that water usable?" Jane asked. "You're pumping it out anyway. Couldn't you collect it?"

Eustice shook his head. "Water that leeches through the rock is poison. Don't know why, exactly, but we do know that anyone who drinks it gets sick. We pump it out and let it drain away into a catch basin where it can't do any harm."

Jane walked toward the pool, curious now. Something about Eustice’s casual acceptance of this mystery made her want to investigate it further. Anything unusual, so easily explained away as ‘routine’, seemed like an opportunity to find the insights she needed.

She knelt at the edge of the pool and dipped her fingertip into the water.

The sensation was immediate. Something was wrong with this water, something she could feel. There was a sort of earthy signature mixed into it that shouldn't be there. It was natural, but not normal. It felt like stone and water had gotten tangled up together somehow, the essence of one bleeding into the other.

It wasn't the worst thing she had ever felt. She had dealt with true, insidious contamination before. This was subtler, more like a wrongness than a danger. But she wouldn't drink it. Nobody should.

She was still crouching there, still feeling the water, when another blast went off.

The boom rolled through the quarry. Jane felt the magic surge again, that same burst of fiery transformation energy. But this time, she was connected to the water, and she felt something else as well.

The magic didn't just move through the air. It moved through everything. The water vibrated with it, carrying the signature down into the earth below. And as it passed, she felt something subtle happen. A settling. A compression. As if the ground itself had compacted downward, just slightly, pressed tighter by the force of the blast.

Jane stood up so fast, she nearly lost her balance.

"Jane?" Bella was at her side immediately. "What is it? What's wrong?"

Jane was already moving, heading for what looked like the lowest point of the quarry floor. "Nothing's wrong. Give me a minute."

She soon found the spot she was looking for: a depression where water naturally collected before being pumped away. She knelt again, placing both palms flat against the wet stone, and pushed her magical senses downward as hard as she could.

It was difficult work. Sensing through stone was always harder than sensing through air or water. She was also trying to go deep, far deeper than she had gone at any of the pumps. Her head began to ache almost immediately, but she pushed through it, forcing her awareness down through layer after layer of compressed earth and rock.

And then she felt it.

The stone was tight. Not just solid, the way stone should be, but compressed. Squeezed. It felt like someone had taken the earth and pressed it in a giant fist.

She brought her senses back up, running them along the quarry walls instead of downward. The same tightness was everywhere, a pervasive compression that extended as far as she could reach. It wasn't natural. Rock didn't do this on its own.

And the rock itself was aware of it. She felt like the stone knew it was in a wrong form, though it could do nothing about it.

Jane stood up and brushed the dirt from her knees. Her head was pounding now, her body paying the price of pushing her magic so hard, but she barely noticed. She was too excited.

"We might not need to go into the mine proper. I think I've found it."

"Found what?" Eustice had followed her across the quarry floor, looking concerned. "What did you see?"

"The problem. Or at least the shape of it." Jane took a deep breath, trying to organize her thoughts into something that would make sense to someone who didn't feel magic the way she did. "The rock down there is compressed. Squeezed tight, like someone's pressing on it from all directions.”

Eustice frowned. "Compressed? How does rock get compressed?"

"That's what I need to figure out. But I have a theory." Jane looked toward the rock face where the blasting had occurred. "Those bombs you use. What exactly are they?"

"Alchemical charges. We buy them from a supplier in the city. They've been using the same formula for years."

"And how long have you been using them here?"

Eustice thought about it. "Maybe three years? Before that, we did everything by hand. The bombs made things faster, so we bought more. Expanded operations."

Three years. The water problems started two years ago, and have gotten worse lately. The timeline fits.

"I need to do some tests," she said. "But I really don't think we'll need to go down into the mines. I think the problem is right here."

"Here?" Eustice looked around the quarry as if seeing it for the first time. "The quarry is causing the water shortage?"

"The blasting, specifically." Jane held up a hand to forestall his objections. "I'm not certain yet. I need to examine one of those charges, and I need to do some more sense-work to map out how far the compression extends. But the magic I felt when that bomb went off did something to the stone. Something that's been building up over time."

Bella had been quiet through all of this, watching Jane work with the patience of long familiarity. Now she spoke up.

"Is it fixable?"

That was the question, wasn't it? Jane could identify problems all day long, but identifying them meant nothing if she couldn't solve them.

"I don't know yet." She said it honestly. Bella deserved honesty, and so did Eustice. "Compression like this, if I'm right about what's causing it, is not the kind of damage I can just undo. You can't un-squeeze rock. But there might be ways to release the pressure, or to stop it from getting worse. I need to understand it better first."

"What do you need from us?" Eustice asked.

"I need you to drill a hole as far down as you can go. Using magic through stone is hard, so every foot counts.."

Eustice nodded slowly. "I can arrange that. The morning shift ends at noon. I can have the hole ready by then and give you as much time as you need."

"That would be perfect."

They made their way back to the lift, with Jane thinking all the way. The compression she had felt was extensive, but it wasn't uniform. There had been variations in it. If she could map those variations, she might be able to find a place to release the pressure without causing more damage.

It was the kind of problem she had trained for, in a way. Her aunt always said that the hardest part of magic wasn't power or technique. It was understanding. You had to know what you were dealing with before you could hope to change it. And now, finally, Jane felt like she was starting to understand.

Bella squinted at Jane as the lift carried them back up to the surface. "You look happy. Happier than you've been since we got here."

"I'm not happy, exactly." Jane watched the quarry walls rise around them. "But I'm hopeful. That's something."

"It's a lot." Bella smiled. "Yesterday you were worried you wouldn't be able to help at all. Now you've found the problem in less than half a day. That's pretty good."

"Finding it isn't fixing it, but at least it’s a start.”

When they reached the top, Dave was waiting with the carriage. He took one look at Jane's expression and raised his eyebrows.

"Good news?"

"Maybe.” Jane climbed into the carriage, her mind already jumping ahead to the tests she would need to run. “Take us back to town, please.”

“Are we done at the quarry for the day?” Dave asked. “Already?”

“No. We’ll need to come back this afternoon. Right after lunchtime, I’d say.” Jane settled back into her seat and closed her eyes. “For now, I have some thinking to do.”

—

The common room was nearly empty when they returned. A few townspeople sat at the far end, nursing drinks and speaking quietly, but the table nearest the window was free. Jane claimed it, setting her bag on the bench beside her and pulling out her notebook.

"What are you doing?" Bella asked.

"Thinking. On paper." Jane flipped to a fresh page and pulled out a small stick of charcoal. "I need to try to visualize what I felt."

She drew the quarry as a rough bowl shape, then added layers beneath it, trying to capture the way the compression had felt to her magical senses. The resulting picture was nonsense, even to her, but it helped her flesh out the impression of what she had sensed from the rock.

Bella peered over her shoulder. "That looks like nothing. Is it just scribbles?

"Basically. But it helps me think."

The visualization had taken Jane about as far as it could. She now had the most complete idea she could reasonably expect to get of what the stone was like. The question was what to do about it.

Stone was stone. It wasn’t malicious, but not liking to move was part of its fundamental nature. This made it resistant to almost any typical approach Jane could think of. Under most circumstances, stone would prefer to sit still and not change rather than cooperate with her efforts.

Jane started writing down ideas, each crazier than the last, but every one carrying a bit of possible success wrapped up in the madness. Slowly, she began to isolate some core ideas that seemed like they might work, if she combined them right.

"Can't you just blow the blockage apart?" Bella made an explosive gesture with her hands. "You know. Magic it open?"

Jane shook her head. "It was explosions that got them into this situation in the first place. If I tried to force the stone open with raw power, I'd probably just make things worse."

"So what then?"

"Something subtler." Jane stared at her sketch. “I have some ideas that might just work.”

"Might?"

"Might. I have to try something, even if it’s uncertain. These people are counting on me."

They sat in silence for a while after that. Jane continued to stare at her sketch, occasionally adding a note or crossing something out. Bella watched the few other patrons come and go, patient and silently supportive.

When lunchtime came, the innkeeper was nowhere to be found. Bella unwrapped the leftover buns from the night before. Jane bit into one and felt some of her tension ease, like the food was bringing back memories of her easier yesterday.

Dave arrived shortly after they finished eating. He pointed through the window, where his carriage waited outside.

"Ready when you are, Miss Jane."

"I’m as ready as I’ll ever be.” Jane gathered her things and headed for the door, Bella close behind. “Let’s go.”
