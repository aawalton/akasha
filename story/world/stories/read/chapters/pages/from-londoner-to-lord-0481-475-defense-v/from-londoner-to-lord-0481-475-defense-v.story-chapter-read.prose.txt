"Where is the other adzee?" Hyola asked without looking away.

"Still near the tree line," the younger guard said.

The archer added, "If it moves, I’ll tell ya."

Hyola kept the second beast centered in her sights.

It stood near the fire and looked toward the wall again. Its head started to lift up.

It was going to howl.

Hyola knew what was coming before the sound came, and got ready.

The neck stretched. The head tilted. The beast opened its mouth.

Now!

She aimed for the side of the neck and pulled the trigger. The adzee began to howl.

The scorpion shot with a hard snap.

The bolt struck exactly where she wanted.

The howl cut off halfway.

For one heartbeat, no one on the tower spoke. Then Hyola let out a breath through her teeth and grinned.

The iron-tipped bolt had sunk deep into the side of the adzee's neck. Not all the way through. The beast was too thick for that. But deep enough. Its head jerked sideways, and it stumbled one step. Blood showed dark in the firelight.

A normal arrow would never have done that. A crossbow bolt might not even have reached with enough power. But this thing had pierced into an adzee from the watchtower like the beast was only another target board.

Scorpions were wonderful!

"Another bolt!” Hyola said quickly, already grabbing the gear handle to reload.

Below, Hudan shouted, "What happened? Is it coming? Do we go out?"

"Wait!" Hyola shouted back, knowing the guards couldn't see the beast from the palisade gaps, since it was standing on the other side of the large fire. "Not yet!"

The older guard handed her another iron-tipped bolt as soon as the string was pulled back and set. Hyola placed it into the groove quickly and leaned back into the sights.

The beast was still standing.

She had expected that. Something that big would not die from one shot unless the Goddess herself guided the bolt. But it was hurt. Its head was raised and its mouth open, as if it was trying to howl again, but no sound came out.

Good.

"Other one?" Hyola asked.

"Still at the treeline," the younger guard said. "It hasn’t come closer."

Hyola decided to take the second shot.

This time she aimed for the upper body. The head was too small a target, and at that distance, its skull might be too hard to break anyway. A sure hit to the chest or stomach was better than a risky miss now.

She pulled the trigger.

The adzee moved at the last moment.

The bolt struck lower than she wanted, driving into the hind thigh instead of the upper body. The beast jerked, stumbled, and collapsed sideways near the fire.

Hyola whooped.

"Hyola, one! Adzee pack, zero!"

The older guard cheered. The younger woman let out a sound that was half laugh, half gasp. Even the archer grinned.

Below them, the guards heard enough to understand. A cheer rose from the men near the gate, short but loud.

Hudan shouted from below, “Status!"

“The first adzee has fallen.” The archer leaned over the parapet, looking left. “Second is still near the tree line, Captain! But it may be coming closer. I can’t say for sure yet. It probably heard the howl cut off."

Hyola grabbed the handle again. “Reload!"

The older guard was already reaching for the handle.

Hyola kept grinning as the scorpion was loaded again.

One beast was down.

And if the second one was foolish enough to come closer, she would be ready.

The older guard finished setting the string, and Hyola pushed the holding lever into place.

"Next bolt," she said.

The woman grabbed another iron-tipped bolt from the storage compartment and passed it over. Her hands were steadier now. Those two hits had helped. Fear was still there, but it had something else beside it now.

Hope.

Hyola set the bolt into the groove and looked through the sights again.

The first adzee was still down near the fire. It had not gotten back up, though it was trying. Its front legs moved once, digging at the dirt, but the wounded hind leg folded under it whenever it tried to rise. Blood ran dark against the firelit ground.

Good.

That one wasn’t getting up again tonight.

The archer shifted on the left side of the platform. "The second one is moving!”

Hyola turned the scorpion left, then saw the shape in the distance. The other adzee had come out farther from the tree line now. It did not run yet. It moved slowly, head low, probably watching the fallen beast near the fire.

"Come on," Hyola muttered. "Come see what happened to your friend."

The younger woman gave her a quick look, as if Hyola was even worse than the adzees.

Hyola ignored her.

The second beast kept moving.

It reached the edge of the firelight and slowed. The flames showed more of its body now. It was just as large as the first one, maybe larger, with heavy shoulders and a thick neck. Its head moved toward the fallen adzee, sniffing.

Hyola brought the sights to its neck.

It was facing the watchtower now, so the angle was not perfect, but good enough for her.

She took a breath, held it for a moment, and pulled the trigger.

The scorpion shot with a hard snap.

The bolt did not hit the neck.

The adzee shifted just before the bolt reached it, and the iron tip struck the middle of its body instead. It sank in deep, enough to make the beast jerk and snarl, but not enough to drop it.

"Still a hit," the older guard said.

“Reload!" Hyola snapped.

The wounded beast threw its head up and howled.

The sound was loud, angry, and close enough to make the platform under Hyola's feet seem too small. The younger guard flinched again, but stayed at her post. The archer lifted his bow and shot.

The arrow struck the adzee in the side.

The beast barely reacted.

The archer cursed under his breath and drew again. "Arrows won’t do much at that distance. It will only annoy it, at best.”

"Then keep annoying it," Hyola said. "Maybe it will look at you instead of me. If it turns, I'll get a better angle.”

The archer shot again.

This arrow hit closer to the shoulder. It stuck, but shallowly. Against a man, that would have been a good shot. Likely even a killing shot. Against an adzee, it was just another small wound.

Then the beast turned fully toward the wall.

Hyola's grin vanished.

It started running.

“It’s fast!" the younger guard cried.

"I can see that!"

Hyola grabbed the handle and began reloading as quickly as she dared. The older guard reached for a new bolt, fumbled, and dropped it onto the platform. The heavy bolt struck the boards with a dull thud and rolled toward the edge.

"Leave it!" Hyola barked. "Next one!"

The woman grabbed another.

The adzee was coming straight toward the wall now, crossing the firelit ground in long, uneven strides. The first bolt in its side had slowed it, but not nearly enough. It was still too fast. Too big. Too close.

Below, Hudan shouted, "Hold! Spears ready!”

The guards must have a clear view of the beast now that it had come ahead of the fire.

The archer shot again. The arrow struck the front of the beast and bounced away without going deep.

“It’s useless," he muttered, already reaching for another arrow.

The string reached the marked point.

"Now," Hyola said.

The older guard shoved the holding lever into place. Hyola took the bolt and set it. There was no time to think carefully. No time to wait for the perfect angle.

The beast was coming head-on. Its chest was broad, but the head moved with every stride. The body would be the safer target, but she knew a body shot might not stop it before it reached the gate.

So she aimed for the head.

"Come on," she whispered as she struggled to bring the head in front of the sights.

The adzee kept running.

She pulled the trigger the moment she found the target.

For half a heartbeat, she thought she had missed completely. The beast did not fall. It kept moving, crossing another stretch of ground as if she hadn't even shot the bolt.

Shit! She should not have been greedy and targeted the head. She should have gone for a more certain shot.

Dammit!

Then she saw it.

The bolt had indeed struck the head. She had no idea how she had made that shot, or whether it was just luck, but she already knew the Goddess was helping the village tonight.

The bolt had gone in near the front of the snout, not cleanly through, but deep enough that the beast's run slowly broke apart. Its front legs stumbled. It shook its head once, blood dark across its face, and somehow kept going.

Hyola stared at it with wonder. "How are you still moving?"

The beast took three more uneven steps.

Then it finally fell.

It hit the ground hard, rolled once, and tried to push itself up again.

"Reload!" Hyola shouted, finally remembering what she was meant to be doing.

They moved faster this time. The older guard did not drop the bolt. The archer shot once more, this time into the beast's flank while it struggled close to the wall. The arrow stuck, but again did little.

The adzee got one front leg under itself.

The scorpion was ready.

Hyola dragged the weapon down as far as the mount allowed. The beast was almost too close now. The scorpion could turn side to side easily enough, but it could not bend much downward like a bow in a hand. The target sat at the very edge of what she could aim at.

"Hold there," she muttered, knowing the beast could not hear or understand her.

It tried to rise again.

Hyola aimed at the point between the neck and upper body and shot.

The bolt struck deep. From this close, the scorpion’s hitting power was immense, and barely any part of the bolt was left outside the body.

The adzee collapsed again. Its legs twitched, then kicked weakly at the dirt. After a few moments, even that slowed.

Hyola let out a shout.

"Hyola, two! Adzee pack, zero!"

The older guard cheered with her. The younger guard made a strangled sound, then cheered too, as if she had just remembered she was allowed. The archer grinned and slapped the parapet.

Below, the guards took up the cheer once they found out what had happened. Spears lifted. A few men shouted Hyola's name. Someone yelled something about the scorpion, and the others answered with more noise.

Hyola looked at the weapon in front of her and laughed.

She loved scorpions.

She might even love them as much as she did Calubo.

Maybe more.

But she would never tell him that.

"Status!" Hudan shouted from below. "Are they moving?"

"One near the gate is not moving," Hyola called back. "The one near the fire is down too, but I don't know if it is dead."

Hudan looked toward the gate, then back up. "Any others?"

Hyola turned quickly. "Look everywhere. Now."

The younger guard looked left. The archer scanned the same direction. The older guard checked the northeast and the darker ground beyond the fire.

"I don't see anything," the archer said.

"Nothing moving," the younger guard added, though her voice still shook.

Hyola leaned over the parapet. "Two beasts down! One near the gate and one near the fire. Go out and finish them. We will keep watch from here. If you hear the horn, run back inside immediately."

Hudan lifted one arm in reply, showing that he had understood.

Then he turned and shouted, "Gate!"

The heavy bars were lifted.

The gate opened just wide enough, and the guards streamed out. Around fifteen of them moved together, shields forward and spears ready at the side. Hudan led them, of course. He looked almost too large to move quietly, but he still went fast.

Hyola reloaded the scorpion at once.

"Do not stop looking," she told the others. "If you see anything, even if you are not sure, say it. Their lives depend on us."

No one argued.

For the first time that night, Hyola found herself hoping she did not see another adzee. Not until the guards were back inside.

The captain reached the beast near the gate first. It looked dead already, but the guards did not trust that. They surrounded it at spear length and stabbed it again and again until even Hyola felt sure it would not rise.

Hudan turned back and looked up at the watchtower.

Hyola pointed with her open palm toward the fire.

Safe. Go.

The captain waved his men forward and ran toward the first adzee she had shot.

Hyola's mouth went dry.

The space between the gate and the fire suddenly looked much wider than before. The guards were outside the wall now. If an adzee came from the tree line at a run, it could reach them before they made it back, unless she saw it early enough.

"Keep looking," she said again. "Left side too."

"I am," the archer said.

The guards reached the fallen adzee near the fire.

This one was still alive.

It could not stand, but its front legs scraped at the dirt and its head jerked weakly when the first spear struck it. The guards closed around it, stabbing from different angles using the long spears while Hudan shouted for them to keep clear of the jaws.

Then the younger woman next to her pointed toward the left.

"Wait. I think... I think something is moving there."

Hyola turned sharply.

For a moment she saw nothing. She had been staring too long at the fire, and the darkness beyond it looked flat and empty. She blinked hard, then followed the line of the girl's finger.

There.

Near the left side, right at the treeline, another dark shape moved.

"Damn," Hyola whispered.

Another one on this side?

That was half the usual pack size in the north already.

Did they all want to die by her hand tonight?

Fine by her.

She grabbed the horn and blew hard.

The sound cut through the grunts and shouts ahead.

Hudan reacted immediately. "Back! Back inside!"

The guards left the wounded beast near the fire and ran for the gate. There was no time to finish it off. Not if they wanted to remain alive.

A few kept their shields turned toward the trees as they retreated. Hyola swung the loaded scorpion toward the new beast, but it was still near the treeline, well beyond a clean shot in the dark.

For now, the guards were safe.

The third adzee stood there, watching.

At least until it decided to come closer.

Hyola kept it in the sights as best she could. The beast did not move for several breaths. Behind it, the forest swallowed most of its shape, but she could still see enough.

The guards reached the gate. One by one they rushed back inside. The gate was pulled shut and barred again.

Only then did Hyola breathe properly.

The third adzee lowered its head. Then it began running toward the fire.

Hyola grinned. The Goddess really was on Tiranat’s side tonight!

"Load another bolt as soon as I shoot," she said.

The older guard nodded.

The archer lifted his bow. "If it comes close enough, I’ll help."

"Good," Hyola said.

The younger guard stared at the charging beast with wide eyes.

Hyola leaned into the sights.

By the end of tonight, the score would at least be Hyola, three. Adzee pack, zero!

If more beasts came after that, she would put them down too.

No one from Tiranat was dying to these beasts—not with the scorpion under her hands.

She only hoped Darora had mounted the third one on the southeast tower before the pack had reached that side. After seeing what this weapon could do, she knew exactly how difficult it would be to fight these beasts without one.

The adzee ran closer to the fire.

Hyola tracked it through the sights, waiting for the right moment. The scorpion was loaded, the guards were safely back behind the gate, and the beast was coming straight into her range.

Good.

Let it come.

Tonight, no adzee was getting through this gate. Not while she was standing on this tower.
