Before the cheers could fully subside, a ripple of golden light spread across the sky, drawing the attention of every divine warrior in the Grand Colosseum. For me, it was the unmistakable pressure of divinity descending upon us.

A radiant column of light speared down from the heavens, striking the center of the arena where Boris stood. From within it, white feathers drifted slowly toward the ground, dissolving into motes of light before they could touch the stone. At the same time, a small, winged figure emerged—Raphael.

She hovered above the arena, looking stern and solemn, wings unfurled as her presence washed over the colosseum. The crowd instinctively fell quiet, many dropping to one knee without even realizing they had done so.

“Hear the decree of Lady Istellia,” Raphael declared, her voice echoing far beyond what her size should have allowed.

Boris looked up, unflinching, though even he straightened his posture slightly.

“By the judgment of combat, witnessed by Divine Will,” Raphael continued, “Boris has proven himself the strongest among Fantasia’s divine warriors. Thus, by Lady Istellia’s will, he is hereby appointed commander of Istellia’s divine warrior army.”

A golden circle appeared beneath Boris’s feet, light washing upward and enveloping his body.

“Receive the blessing of command,” Raphael said.

Golden runes briefly traced themselves across Boris’s arms and chest before sinking into his skin. The blessing granted by Istellia through Raphael carried the authority to command Fantasia’s divine warriors during the Battle of Divine Will, while simultaneously enhancing command-based effects and reinforcing his presence as a leader on the battlefield.

Boris clenched his fist once, then slowly released it. “Heh. Guess it’s official.”

Raphael gave him a brief nod. “Your commands will be recognized within Istellia’s domain during the Battle of Divine Will. Lead well.”

Her gaze swept across the colosseum one last time—lingering for the briefest instant in my direction—before she added, “Do not squander what has been entrusted to you.”

With that, her form dissolved into a cascade of golden light, the radiant column fading as the sky returned to normal.

As the meaning of what had just occurred finally sank in, the cheers erupted once more, louder than before. Boris stood alone at the center of it all as our new commander.

The tournament concluded not long after that, and the Grand Colosseum gradually began to empty as spectators filtered out, still buzzing with excitement. Down on the arena floor, Boris finally made his way back to us.

Michelle was the first to step forward, greeting him with a warm smile. “Congratulations, Commander.”

Tuilë let out a laugh. “Wow. We’ve really got the leader of thousands of divine warriors in our party, huh? That’s one hell of a promotion—from brawling barehanded to commanding an entire army.”

“Heh.” Boris scratched the back of his head, clearly unaccustomed to the title. “Still feels strange hearing it out loud. Back in the military, they used to call me Lieutenant Colonel.”

Lucian gave a small nod of approval, while I returned Boris’s gaze with a sincere smile.

Before we could say much more, others began approaching. Leaders of various factions, independent divine warriors, and even a few contestants I recognized from the tournament stepped forward to offer their congratulations with genuine admiration. One by one, they acknowledged Boris as their superior.

Boris accepted them all with the same straightforward composure, returning nods and brief words without letting it go to his head.

“Looking forward to fighting under you,” Ronan said as he appeared among the crowd, the very finalist Boris had just defeated. He extended a hand toward Boris.

“Likewise. Happy to have you,” Boris replied, gripping Ronan’s hand firmly.

As the crowd continued to thin, I glanced upward. The sky beyond the colosseum had darkened, and it was well past afternoon. Only then did I realize how exhausted everyone was—myself included. Between the length of the tournament and the intensity of the final matches, the entire day had slipped away.

“It’s already evening,” I said. “Today’s been… a long one.”

Michelle let out a tired sigh. “Yeah, no kidding.”

“Hmm… I’m still pissed that I lost before the semifinals,” Lucian muttered.

I looked around at the group. “Let’s call it a day. We can talk about plans tomorrow.”

Boris chuckled. “Sounds good to me.”

“Call it a day?!” Tuilë chimed in immediately. “We should celebrate this! Let’s go to the most expensive restaurant in town!”

Come to think of it, I’m so famished… And this is indeed something worth celebrating, I thought. As such, I agreed with her suggestion.

Everyone else was also in the same opinion, and so, we left the colosseum behind, heading back toward the main plaza. The most expensive restaurant we knew was located there, and even though each dish cost around a hundred soul coins, we planned to eat until we couldn’t move anymore tonight.

When I returned to my room, I didn’t immediately go to sleep. Instead, I summoned Raphael and asked her how many days remained before the start of the Battle of Divine Will. She wasn’t as irritated as usual when she appeared, seemingly satisfied with the outcome of the tournament.

“I’m not supposed to tell you this, but since you’re working directly under Lady Istellia, I guess I should. The Battle of Divine Will should begin in about three days,” she said. “Prepare accordingly.”

With that, she dispersed into motes of golden light.

“In three days, huh,” I muttered.

It wasn’t much time. Clearly, I could forget about clearing the remaining prerequisites for my class advancement and would have to attempt the trial only after the first stage of the Battle of Divine Will. After all, there would be a short grace period between stages.

I might still be a Gimmel-rank divine warrior, but my base stats were already comparable to those of an average, newly advanced Daleth-rank divine warrior. I should be able to hold my ground against the divine warriors of other gods, who were likely stronger than those on Istellia’s side. It couldn’t be helped that things had turned out this way, despite my best efforts, thanks in no small part to Graham’s actions sabotaging everyone’s growth.

There was also something I wanted to ask Tuilë to help with. I still had the materials from the three-headed serpent, and I planned to use them to upgrade my equipment. They should be more than sufficient to craft epic-grade weapons and armor, and when combined with Tuilë’s expertise, the results would almost certainly surpass standard equipment.

I hadn’t brought it up after the tournament because I didn’t want to spoil the celebratory mood, and it probably wouldn’t have made much difference anyway, since she wouldn’t start working on it right away… Wait, she might!

Still, matters for tomorrow could wait until tomorrow. All I needed to do now was climb into bed and close my eyes, letting myself sink into a well-earned night’s sleep.

As a tribute to the Goddess of Imagination, Istellia, 100 Soul Coins have been deducted from your balance.

The next morning passed more quietly than the day before. After a simple breakfast, I brought up something I had been meaning to mention since last night—namely, the exceptional materials I’d gathered while the others had been busy with their class advancements and tournament preparations.

Naturally, that immediately caught Boris and Michelle’s attention. When I showed them the materials from my inventory and explained that there was enough for everyone, they were shocked.

Within the hour, we had gathered everyone at Tuilë’s workshop. The familiar metallic scent of oil, charred ash, and half-finished constructs filled the air the moment we stepped inside. Tools lined the walls in meticulous order, enchanted worktables were active, and several inactive golems—resembling Bernardo—stood neatly to one side like obedient sentries awaiting commands.

Once everyone was inside, I opened my inventory and released the materials. I had kept the most important parts from all three heads, each possessing its own unique properties. There were thick scales boasting exceptional physical and magical resistance, dense bones infused with lingering vitality, and sections of hide that looked tough and sturdy yet were surprisingly pleasant to the touch.

“Whoa! What’s this?!” Tuilë exclaimed. “Are you taking all this out just to make me fall in love with you?” As she spoke, she grabbed a handful of materials and pressed them against her cheek.

I rolled my eyes. “Enough joking. Just make us epic-grade equipment from them.”

Ideally, she’d be able to finish everything before the Battle of Divine Will began in two days, but I knew that was wishful thinking. Still, both my sword and armor were only at unique grade—and worse, they were already on the verge of falling apart. If she could manage even a partial upgrade or replace them entirely, it would already be a huge improvement.

Lucian studied the scales closely and remarked, “The mana density is incredible. These would indeed qualify for epic-grade crafting without question.”

Boris crossed his arms and nodded slowly. “Not bad.”

Tuilë, however, brushed her fingers across one of the scales with reverent focus. Her playful grin faded, replaced by the sharp, hungry look of a master craftsman staring at a rare treasure.

“Yep. This is good. Really good,” she muttered. “Only that…”

“Only what?” I asked.

“Only that I’ll need some complementary materials, and I’m currently short on those since I’ve been using them on my babies.”

Babies? Oh, her golems.

“Don’t worry about that,” I said, taking out a large pouch filled with soul coins and placing it on the table. “There’s around fifty thousand soul coins in here. Since you’ve completed your class advancement, you can purchase higher-quality materials in District C. That should be more than enough to cover the expenses.”

“My, oh my!” Tuilë’s eyes widened as she weighed the pouch in her hands. “Maxim, I think I might be developing a crush on you.”

“Really now…” I was genuinely at a loss for words.

“Maxim, lad, how did you get your hands on this much money?” Boris asked, brows lifting slightly.

“Uh… let’s just say I did some betting during the tournament and won big,” I replied. It was the truth—except that it wasn’t the whole truth.

“As expected of our true leader,” Lucian remarked calmly.

Michelle hesitated before speaking. “Maxim, I don’t really feel right using your money…” She met my gaze. “How about I reimburse you once the equipment is finished? I still have a decent amount of soul coins.”

The others seemed to share her sentiment. Even though we had grown close after facing countless scenarios together, they still understood boundaries—and their own pride, for that matter.

In response, I simply shook my head and explained, “No need. I have more than you can imagine.”

After convincing them, Tuilë began pacing around the pile, already muttering to herself. “Serpent scales for layered armor plating… bones for reinforcement cores… and if I process the hide properly, I can retain its natural resistance without sacrificing flexibility…”

“Go ahead and plan it out,” I said. “In the meantime, I’ll be leaving my old equipment here.”

I removed my armor—the Predator’s Stalker Hide—as well as my sword, the Howling Edge. I still had a few other pieces of equipment at unique grade or below, but prioritizing these two felt like the wiser choice.

The others followed suit, leaving behind equipment they wanted improved or, in some cases, requesting a brand new one.

Tuilë cracked her knuckles. “Then I’ll get to work! If I’m doing this, I’m doing it properly!”

Since we didn’t want to disturb her, we quietly left the workshop, leaving Tuilë alone with her creations.
