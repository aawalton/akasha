All three of the Summoned appeared nearly two feet in the air after they teleported, falling onto a large, blue mattress. Adabelle and Lora landed gracefully while Trevor stumbled slightly, though his wife’s tail kept him from planting his face in the ground. The air was oddly warm and the room rocked gently in a familiar motion.

“Ah, I see,” the noblewoman said as she looked around. “This ship isn’t a stationary location, so it’s harder to visualize.”

“Bingo!” Lora confirmed with a smile as she scratched her head. “It is stationary, though. Well, the landing zone is. I can’t teleport here all willy-nilly unless the boat’s in position otherwise I’ll fall into the water. The Matador is currently docked and awaiting its final load so that customs can come and check things through. So, Trevor, if you’ll please drop off everything for locations three and four.”

Nodding, the courier moved off of the mattress. He helped the women down and retrieved Clay’s husk before he started dropping off crates and boxes. They had come from Paladona, Garend, and Aletander so far, though he wasn’t sure why he was dropping off two sets here.

“The Matador, huh?” Trevor asked with a chuckle. “This has to be one of Klar’s ships.”

“That’s right! We’re docked at what should have been our next destination,” Lora said with an apologetic smile.

“Should have been?” Adabelle asked inquisitively, looking at the teleporter.

The other woman immediately jumped a step back and bowed so deep that she was at a ninety degree angle. Her hand furiously rubbed against the green ribbon on her belt as if it were a good luck charm.

“I’m so, so, so sorry,” Lora said. “The boss man was expecting your passports to be ready so there wouldn’t be any trouble. They weren’t rejected, but one of them was put on hold at the last minute, which put the whole thing under review. You’re allowed to be on this ship because it’s considered merchant prince property, Lady Adabelle, but you can’t go on land and I’m really, really, really sorry.”

The newlyweds shared a glance. It took them a moment to realize what was going on. There was only one place they knew about where Klar had people who would actively try to undermine him.

“Lora, it’s okay,” the demonkin said, walking up and gently guiding the woman back into a standing position. “This is the Isle of Horns, isn’t it?”

She nodded, looking like she was about to cry. “The boss really wanted you to see his homeland. Not just because he thought you’d like it, but because he knew that if he could show you two off then he’d be able to help the citizens see that Summoned aren’t something to be scared of. Trevor’s passport was cleared yesterday after it was already put on hold by one of the boss man’s competitors, which was in line with what he expected, but then they put another one on yours, Lady Adabelle.”

Taking a shallow breath, she continued. “I’m really, really sorry. They said that your Power was too dangerous no matter how polite you might seem and they used a lot of underhanded tactics to sway the officials and spread fear which is a load of bull hockey and I know you’re, like, one of the most even people I know but—”

“Shh, shh,” Adabelle soothed, pulling Lora into a hug. She patted the teleporter on the back. “It’s okay. You didn’t do this, and neither did Klarkartar. I’m no stranger to political games and the truth of the matter is that I could be dangerous. It might be disappointing, because I’ve wanted to visit for a while, but it’s in no way your fault, okay?”

“I know, but—”

“It’s not your fault or Klarkartar’s, okay?” she repeated, gentle but firm. “Take some breaths.”

“O-okay, Lady Adabelle, thank you,” Lora replied before inhaling deeply and letting it all out.

“Are you feeling better?”

“Yes, ma’am, thank you.”

“Good. I’m glad,” Adabelle said as she let the woman go, smiling. “We can still see the Isle of Horns from the deck, right?”

Lora nodded. “Yeah. Trevor needs to go on the mainland, though, because he has things to pick up. That, too, will be subject to customs,” she said, turning to the courier. He had stopped taking out the inventory for this location to watch the two, and he got back to work. “Sorry, Trevor. You’ll probably have to remove everything from your inventory. Or lie about it, I guess.”

“No need to apologize, and I’ve got nothing I’ll have to lie about,” he replied with a small smile. At least he knew what made her cringe when they were back at the hotel. Her usual peppy self was probably worried about breaking the news. “The passport is just on hold, right?”

“Yeah.”

“Then we’ll come eventually,” he said, shrugging. “Now would have been nice, but there will be plenty of chances. Klar will just have to bring us personally next time.”

“I’m sure he’ll like that a lot,” Lora agreed. Taking another deep breath, she slapped herself on the cheeks before putting on a more determined face. “Okay! I don’t want to bring this honeymoon down, so I’m going to get over it. I’m getting over it. I am now over it. We’re all alive, awake, alert, and enthusiastic, because it means we get to go somewhere else where there won’t be any old fogeys trying to stop us!”

“I’m glad that you’re feeling better,” Adabelle giggled, patting the woman on the back one more time before checking on her husband. “How’s it coming along?”

“Just about done,” Trevor said as he started his last stack. The items marked three were in one pile, and he was finishing up with the fours.

“All set. Now, there was something about customs?” Trevor asked as he returned to his wife’s side and took her hand.

The teleporter nodded before running to one of the doorways. She opened it and disappeared, coming back a few seconds later holding up two coats, one green and one blue. They had a thick fur trim that made Trevor feel warm just looking at it.

“Adabelle can at least go on deck, so you should take in as much as you can while you’re here,” Lora said, back to her usual self as she handed them off to the newlyweds. The moment they were done pulling the jackets on, she took their hands and started leading them out of the cargo room. “Come on, let’s go!”

They followed after the excited teleporter with smiles on their faces, her enthusiasm infectious. The crew was almost all minotaurs, though there were a few dwarves and humans amongst them, and each one greeted the four of them warmly as they walked through the ship. It was much larger than either of them expected, but they eventually emerged onto the deck.

Lora ran ahead and posed at the railing. “Welcome to Klar’s homeland, the Isle of Horns, where things are majestically cold!”

Even with the fluffy jackets, the cold was noticeable on their face and hands. Snow fell gently from the sky, leaving a thin layer on the deck. Trevor had always liked weather like this so long as it wasn’t freezing, and loved the sound of crunching footsteps in winter. He stretched, taking in the atmosphere. Adabelle was quick to cling to his side, her tail snaking up his sleeve to wrap around his arm. It was already cold.

With a smile, he gently brushed his fingers against the base of his wife’s tail and retrieved a scrunched up blue sleeve from his inventory. It appeared perfectly around her, and he smoothed it out to cover the length that hadn’t gone into his jacket. As a prehensile type tail, he knew that the extra appendage was prone to getting uncomfortably cold. Adabelle could have always wrapped it around her waist or put it in her coat, but she preferred to seek out his warmth instead.

Trevor couldn’t say he minded it, either.

With his demonkin taken care of, they stood at the railing and took in the sights.

The village was built on a hill that had been carved into what looked like steps. Businesses were down near the docks and along the main road in the center, and houses were higher up. The brick structures were built against small cliffs, and each one appeared to have a mailbox and garden, though they were under a thin blanket of snow.

Even though everything was covered in a dreary gray thanks to the weather, it still managed to be colorful. The minotauran people preferred bright, vibrant clothing and that translated well to their buildings. Even though Adabelle wasn’t allowed off of the ship, she could still appreciate the picturesque scene before her.

“This is the village of Rastarore,” the teleporter explained, adopting a tour guide persona. “It’s the smaller of two ports on the Isle of Horns, and thus less busy, which makes it one of the gateways into the minotauran homeland. Their main exports are platinum, jewelry, livestock, pelts, and horns. Not their own, of course, but there are several animals that fit the bill here.”

“Truly, the Isle of Horns,” Trevor joked.

“Exactly right,” Lora confirmed with a grin. “We can wait here for a bit so you can take in the sights, or we can go through customs so you can get the goods. Your call.”

The courier looked at his wife, and she patted his arm as she removed herself from his jacket. “No time like the present,” Adabelle said. She was quick to fix the tail sleeve so it covered all of her, then tucked it into her own clothing.

“Do you want to stay up here? Or visit the lounge?” the teleporter asked.

“I’m fine here, thank you,” she responded. Trevor pulled out a steaming mug of hot chocolate from his inventory and handed it to her. Her face lit up with delight. “Yes, this is exactly what I need to make it through this tribulation. You know me so well.”

“I aim to please, my lady,” he replied with a smile. There was a hopeful look on Lora’s face, and he laughed. “Alright, one for you, too.”

“Yay!” the teleporter said as she received a mug of her own. She took a sip and almost seemed to melt in place. It took her a moment to get moving again. “Okay, let’s get this over with.”

They descended back into the ship and came out of a hatch big enough for minotaurs and the cargo they would be carrying. It was an easy walk down the gangplank from there. They were greeted by two minotaurs in royal blue garb, one of which was holding a clipboard while the other checked the crates as they were taken by.

“Yo!” she called, raising her hand. “Asalorana with Trevor Anderson, the man of much inventory here, and his familiar, Clay. You should be expecting us?”

The minotaur with the clipboard looked through the papers he had before nodding. He eyed the stoat on the courier’s shoulders for a moment before moving on. “I am customs clerk Carrarl. Do you have your passport with you, Trevor Anderson?”

Before he could even turn to Lora, she had already pulled two of them out of her pocket. “Right here. Mine, too.”

He took both of them and brought them close to his face so he could squint at them. First one, then the other, and he handed them back. “Please follow me.”

“You got it,” Lora replied as he turned on his heel and started walking down the pier. They followed after him.

There was a staging area that had been cleared of snow not far from the dock. That was where Carrarl led them. Trevor turned to look at the ship and saw his wife watching him from the deck. They shared a wave before he started paying attention again.

“We don’t have a lot of protocols for Summoned, but you’re the first we’ve heard of with the Power of Inventory so we’re treating it the same as any extradimensional space. You’re to remove everything for me to inspect before you’re allowed to go further into Rastarore. Understand?”

“I do,” Trevor said before frowning. He turned to Lora. “But how come we didn’t just bring things onto the ship for me to take?”

“Customs. Extra cost if it’s on the ship,” she answered.

“But I’m…” the courier trailed off, his frown deepening.

“New rules, so there’s a lot of new hiccups. Sorry,” Lora replied with a shrug.

If Carrarl was bothered by this exchange, he didn’t show it. Trevor just sighed, shrugged, and turned to the minotaur. “I’ve got some stuff that’s heated or cold sensitive like food and alchemical potions and medicine. Is it okay if I pull those out last?”

“That shouldn’t be a problem,” the minotaur replied.

“Alright. Here goes,” he said, holding out his hand.

It had been a long time since he had taken everything out of his inventory, but it was easy enough. With practiced ease, he started with the biggest and heaviest things on the bottom and worked his way up. Everything he pulled out was level with the ground or the item below it, not dropping a single inch.

His familiar’s husk, coins stacked high, precious metals and gemstones of various colors and qualities all came in one excessively large pile.

“Hold on,” Carrarl said, but it was only the beginning.

Bulwarks, complete with Floatstone bases to keep them airborne, armor, and a plethora of clothes were next, all neatly folded up. Everything except for those too intimate to show.

Boxes with Klar’s symbol on it, piles of adventuring gear from ropes, to ladders, to rope ladders. Sacks of flour mingled with compasses and pitons. His mace leaned against tables and chairs for both comfort indoors and ease of transportation outdoors.

“Why is there so much?” Carrarl muttered.

Lora jumped into his recliner as she grinned, enjoying the look on the custom clerk’s face. If anything, it seemed as though she was enjoying this.

As was his familiar. Clay perked up when they saw the diamond they had gifted Adabelle back in the wildlands. His familiar took the gemstone and the rod of Sammie Steel they received as a quest reward, which was a shimmering silver color, into Trevor’s backpack before closing it up. He could hear them working inside.

“Don’t forget that we’ll need to teleport soon, so don’t get too engrossed,” the courier warned, and he felt his familiar’s enthusiasm dampen just a little.

Stacks on stacks of paper, pencils, crayons, and more. A few candles of Rashie’s favorite scents. The stun gun Wayne had given him once upon a time. The souvenirs from Paladona and Aletander that they were going to bring home to everyone. A small trophy in the shape of Sammie the Gamer, which was a prized possession now.

Then came the perishables.

“There’s more?” Carrarl asked incredulously, looking at the stuff already dropped.

“Yeah. My Power is Inventory. I’m not even full yet,” Trevor replied as he filled the tables he had set down. “This is the important stuff, by the way.”

Food and drink, most warmed or chilled as needed. Medicine and potions in vials of all shapes and sizes, each clearly labeled. He grimaced as they started to get cold.

The staging area was almost completely full. Dock workers had stopped to stare. The courier couldn’t even see the customs clerk on the other side of the piles, and he had to move to get line of sight on the minotaur. At the very least, visually seeing what had to be ninety-nine percent of his inventory out in the open was an impressive sight.

Carrarl needed a moment, but Trevor was insistent. “Alright, all you, man,” he urged.

“Uh, yeah, one moment,” the minotaur said dumbly before flourishing his pen against the clipboard.

The courier couldn’t help but notice the look on Lora’s face. It seemed as though this might have been a little bit of revenge against the system that prevented her friend’s perfect honeymoon.

Trevor found that he was okay with that, and started pulling things back into his inventory as the clerk went through them.
