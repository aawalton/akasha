Watching the followers and Paragons squirm on the ground was vaguely entertaining for perhaps a dozen seconds before it became shameful. I barked at them to get back on their feet.

When that failed, and all I received in response were the grunts of unsuccessful attempts to obey, I sighed.

Their inability to continue was more my fault than theirs. My lack of understanding of my own Domains had left them quite literally drained of energy, so the fault was mostly mine. I needed more practice controlling my Domains, especially now that I had so many variations of them.

“Alright, alright,” I conceded, waving a dismissive hand at the sorry sight. “Rest. The lesson is over. I expect everyone back to training in an hour.”

The shocked silence that followed felt vaguely insulting, but I said nothing and stalked out of the central field. Behind me, the exhausted moaning turned to relieved chatter and laughter.

“That was surprisingly kind of you,” Dralos observed, his large, scaled frame bobbing slightly in a casual jog as he caught up to me.

I shot him an irritated look. “Broken soldiers cannot fight.”

He only grinned, a conspiratorial expression that rankled me for no reason I could place.

“Where is Ethan?” I asked.

“With that fire lady,” he answered, a long black nail scratching the bridge of his scaled nose.

“I’ve told him not to spend so much time with her,” I said with an irritated grumble. She was, after all, someone who had tried to kill me.

“She has no memory.” Dralos shrugged. “My lady, we’ve run every possible test to determine the truth of her words.”

“Except a mind energy user.”

“Who are exceedingly rare, and the only one in Elyndor is currently working on your brother’s mind,” Dralos pointed out, keeping pace just slightly behind and to my right.

I frowned and dismissed the topic with a wave. I’d already warned Ethan multiple times. The consequences of ignoring those warnings would be his own to bear. “Is there anything on my schedule today?”

He snorted. “My lady, Duchess you may be, but I too am nobility. Have I fallen so low as to appear a secretary?”

I rolled my eyes. “Oh, spare me the drama, draconian. If you don’t know, just say so.”

“Ah, but I do know,” he said, stepping even with me now that we were out of the followers’ sight. “I simply do not enjoy the expectation that I would.”

I turned to glare at him. Had I been giving the draconian too much leeway lately? He’d done a commendable job managing the city’s affairs from the background, but perhaps he could use a reminder of his place.

As if he’d read my thoughts in my eyes, Dralos raised his hands in mock surrender. “No offense meant, of course, Duchess Lilliana.”

I groaned. “Just tell me. I’ll find out in a moment anyway.” The familiar pulse of energy drawing ever nearer told me as much.

“More ambassadors from the Holy Kingdom arrived early this morning, and this time not one, but two of their Cardinals. The entire Spire is in a panic. I’m surprised no one told you.”

“What do they want?”

“Your Grace, how would I know?”

“You did not ask?”

“I attempted to, but they demanded respite first, and Your Grace was already asleep.”

Some sleep I’d had.

We reached the entrance to the Duke’s Spire just as Hannah and Roxanne bolted through the doors, barely stopping when they spotted me. Well, Roxanne managed to halt her momentum. Hannah collided with Roxanne’s back, though the Paragon showed no sign she even felt it.

“My lady,” Roxanne said, her voice quavering slightly despite her clear effort to control it. “You have visi—”

“There are Holy Cardinals here to see you,” Hannah blurted, visibly shaking with excitement. “The Holy Kingdom never sent even a single Cardinal to visit the old Duke! Why do you think they’re here?”

I raised an eyebrow at Dralos as if to say, See? He grunted and rolled his eyes.

“I imagine they’re here for Ethan’s new friend,” I said. “Given that she used to be a Cardinal.”

Hannah’s eyes widened in either disbelief or surprise, I couldn’t tell which. “Lady Mirabelle is a Cardinal?” Her voice was nearly a whisper, as if I were sharing some deeply held secret.

It might have been Mirabelle’s wish to keep it a secret, but it wasn’t mine. I nodded. “She was a Cardinal. Not anymore, I believe.”

Hannah’s eyes doubled in size, making her look rather comical. “You can quit the Faith?”

At that, I shrugged and gestured for Dralos to answer. How would I know?

Dralos scowled, not particularly thrilled at being volunteered. Still, he cleared his throat and spoke with a deliberate dignity I suspected stemmed from his draconian roots. “In theory,” he said carefully, golden eyes narrowing slightly, “one may leave a religious order. The official religions recognized by the Holy Kingdom cannot outwardly refuse anyone who wishes to leave. But…” He paused, running a black nail absently over his scaled cheek. “Whether the Holy Kingdom views a Cardinal’s status as a position one can resign, or a title one must carry to the grave, is, I suspect, a matter of considerable internal debate, regardless of which order one wishes to separate from.”

Hannah nodded fervently, as if receiving a revelation from the Gods themselves. “So she might still be one, then?”

“She might still be considered one,” Dralos corrected. “Whether she considers herself one is another matter entirely.”

The discussion carried on, but I stopped listening. Now that I’d reached the Spire, the cores I’d sensed earlier resolved into distinct signatures I could easily separate from the others in the building. Both were wholly unfamiliar and deliberately contained, radiating the particular controlled stiffness that came only from long practice. If nothing else, I could respect that discipline.

I reached into my pocket and bit into the lotus cake I’d stored away.

I wonder who trained them.

“Where are the Cardinals now?” I asked, already moving in their direction.

“The eastern receiving hall,” Roxanne said, falling into perfect step behind me. Dralos and Hannah lagged behind, not out of decorum or deference, but likely because Hannah kept pestering Dralos with questions about the Holy Kingdom and the draconian did not want to bother us with them. “We had refreshments brought to them.” She hesitated, then added, “They’ve apparently been waiting since before dawn.”

“Why did they not notify anyone?”

Roxanne let out a long sigh. “They did. But the servant they told felt he could not notify you directly. So he told his superior, who told their superior… and so on. It took some time to reach me.”

“Hmm,” I mused, filing the information away for later. Arriving before dawn implied either great urgency or disrespect. Their reactions upon meeting me would likely reveal which, so I didn’t belabor the thought. “I assume they asked for me specifically? Not any of the Alistar nobility?”

“Of course,” Roxanne said. “They requested a meeting with Duchess Lilliana Silverwater of the Alistar Duchy.” She stepped slightly closer and dropped her voice. “They called you ‘Lady Silverwater’ at first, before one of their aides corrected them. It didn’t seem rude… perhaps simply uninformed?”

Interesting.

I didn’t respond outwardly, so the Paragon fell back to her previous position.

The eastern receiving hall was one of the few rooms in the Duke’s Spire I had deliberately changed since becoming Duchess. Where much of the Spire still carried the old Alistar aesthetic of comfort and bright luxury, the receiving hall was now paneled in dark wood that seemed to swallow the light. The posh, high-backed chairs had been exchanged for plain wooden ones that discouraged long visits. Half a dozen of them surrounded a table of the same dark wood, with a single chair at its head. That seat loomed over the rest, high-backed and dyed a deep crimson that seemed to shower the room in unspoken bloodlust.

The room was designed to make guests uncomfortable, but perhaps I had pushed it too far. I’d wanted it to be intimidating, not threatening.

The two Cardinals rose when I entered. They had been sitting side by side, to the left of my seat.

They were not what I had expected, though I’d been careful not to expect much of anything. The first was a woman, older, with close-cropped silver hair and the unhurried bearing of someone who had long since stopped bothering to prove herself. Her robes were white, with three gold circles around each cuff. I guessed they marked significant rank within whatever order she belonged to.

The second was a man of perhaps forty, with sharp, bird-like features. His eyes darted constantly around the room, betraying either caution or fear. His hands were folded at his midsection, though that did little to hide how tightly they were clenched. His gaze had locked on Deathbringer the moment I walked in. Even now, as I looked at him, he struggled to hold my gaze, his eyes flickering back to the sword again and again.

“Duchess Silverwater,” the woman said with a short bow. Her accent was smooth, her vowels rounded in the way of the Holy Kingdom’s southern dialect. “Thank you for receiving us. I am Cardinal Snow of the Church of Balance. This is Cardinal Tundra, also of the Church of Balance.” She paused, if only briefly. “We apologize for the early hour.”

Cardinal Tundra offered me a slight bow as well, though the corners of his lips twitched downward throughout.
