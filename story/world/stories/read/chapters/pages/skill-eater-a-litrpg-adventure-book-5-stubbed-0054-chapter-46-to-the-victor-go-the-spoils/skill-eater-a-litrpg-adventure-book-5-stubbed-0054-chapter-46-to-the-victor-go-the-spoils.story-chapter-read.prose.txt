The mortally wounded gladiator straddled the precipice of annihilation, but Edge wasn’t ready for him to meet oblivion’s embrace.

Before the man succumbed to his grievous injury, Edge activated Rejuvenation’s aura mode, extending its effect to his would-be assassin while retrieving his shorn limb, aligning it with the stump, and holding the ragged ends together.

His assailant had been bisected above the waist, losing several vital organs in the process. However, the healing skill kept his heart beating while its magic took hold, giving Edge’s sundered tissue time to mend. When the process was complete, he wiggled his fingers, raised his arms, and called upon the twin chains of oblivion. Extraction.

He had never stolen a skill from an innocent person, although he’d been sorely tempted on several notable occasions. He wasn’t sure what he would do if his life was on the line, since the code he followed didn’t permit such predatory behavior.

However, he felt no ethical conflict whatsoever when claiming powers from people who wanted to kill him or harm the people he loved. Edge wasn’t going to leave someone who’d tried to murder him in cold blood alive regardless, and this was a rare opportunity to pilfer powers from a warrior whose weapon of choice and combat style were similar to his own.

While he mulled the matter over, the twin chains crossed the open air in a clinking flash before burying themselves in the gladiator’s chest and diving into his core. Edge had several minutes before the aura cut out and followed them inside—plenty of time to take a good look around, ponder his options, and claim the best skills of the bunch.

The warrior’s central chamber was a sunny swath of shoreline with a small dock extending into a freshwater ocean. A pole and a bucket of bait were sitting beside a wicker chair, and the water was teeming with fish. He could see the free city in the distance, but only the music of lapping waves and the mournful cries of shorebirds reached his ears.

It was a soothing scene, and it seemed the warrior enjoyed a bit of peace and privacy when he wasn’t fighting in the ring. That didn’t change the fact that man had tried to assassinate Edge, so he shrugged while examining the avatars scattered across the area.

While he was busy, the chains found something they wanted to devour. It was an uncommon occurrence, and advancing Manifest Chain was one of his top priorities. He gave them permission to pursue their quarry while he considered his options.

The moment he laid eyes on an avatar shaped like a war scythe, Edge began to drool. A hunger that had nothing to do with his dark passenger sent the thrill of avarice coursing through his veins. The power was a perfect addition to round out his skillset—some manner of learning skill that would help him grow more proficient with his polearm.

“That’s our first catch, boys,” he addressed the animate loops. “Run it down and reel it in.” The living links had finished their meal and didn’t need to be told twice. Foebinder and Magebane were mirror opposites in many ways, but one trait they had in common was their love of the hunt.

The black chains took off like they were spring-loaded, chasing the weapon skill down and wrapping it tight. The war scythe tried to slash, but Foebinder slid past the blade before winding around as Magebane arrived to deliver its antimagic touch. When the polearm was subdued, they yanked it out of the core, which meant it was time for Edge to finish the job.

A weapon-shaped avatar could wreak havoc on his mouth, since he was literally going to chew up a blade. That was why he’d already Hardened his gums, tongue, palate, and teeth. Even so, the vicious bastard gave him a scare. In a trick he hadn’t seen before, the scythe dove down his throat before it was subdued, and he sensed it would enter his stomach rather than be drawn into his core.

He wasn’t sure what would happen past that point and had no intention of finding out. He shifted into shadows, let the four-inch polearm pass through his throat, then rematerialized and chomped it from the side—not letting go until the shaft shattered in two places and dissolved into goo on his tongue.

Rejuvenation healed the damage while he popped into his inner world to discover the details of his hard-won prize.

You have extracted the skill: Polearm Mastery (Rare, Rank 4).

Digestion is complete.

Would you like to slot in Polearm Mastery now?

“I would indeed.”

When Edge confirmed the prompt, the avatar bonded with an empty socket and transformed, shifting from a war scythe to a perfect replica of Opulence. Mastery came with enough experience that a single Extraction would take it to rank two, although that wasn’t going to happen until tomorrow, since he didn’t want to pick an upgrade until he had a better sense of what it could do.

While he was here, he pulled up his Guide to supplement the vision that had accompanied slotting the skill.

Polearm Mastery <passive, learning> (Rare): Rank 1.

This skill will assist in mastering the mechanics of wielding polearms. It provides corrective feedback that makes performing efficient maneuvers feel pleasant and unwieldy attempts uncomfortable, guiding its recipient toward mastery.

He hadn’t realized the skill was Rare, since he no longer had to sacrifice Mortium to Extract anything below Epic, which made Polearm Mastery even better. Edge knew a bit about learning skills already. They were superior to most AR-training programs, and some provided visual and auditory feedback too.

This variant wouldn’t teach him techniques directly, but it would help him master any moves he wanted to perfect, making well-executed attacks and efficient blocks feel pleasurable to a degree that corresponded with the quality of his performance. He’d heard the corrective feedback could be distinctly unpleasant, providing a powerful incentive to fix his mistakes.

He was excited to find out how Mastery would improve when it hit rank two, but he had no doubts that it would help him become proficient with his weapon of choice far faster than would otherwise be possible, compressing what would normally take years into a span of months.

All in all, it was a timely acquisition. Edge was advancing at a rate most people could only dream of, thanks to Skill-Eater’s Unique magic and to being thrust into situations where he took on an absurd number of high-stage opponents. However, his weapon technique wasn’t improving nearly as quickly, leaving him at a severe disadvantage against enemies with more combat experience and formal training. Polearm Mastery would help close that gap and would be an invaluable tool over the treacherous days ahead.

He introduced himself and welcomed the avatar to his collection, promising to celebrate its arrival as soon as he finished stealing two more skills. That was when something tapped his leg. Edge had been so focused on his newest skill that he hadn’t realized the twin chains had emerged from beyond the black door. They were waiting at his feet, shining like the sun—ready to rank up then and there.

“Oh, hell yes.” While he praised the Unique avatar, his Guide appeared of its own accord, displaying a list of options. As always, the living links picked their own upgrade before he had time to read. They both tapped the screen and began to change shape. While the process was underway, he pulled up his profile to learn what they’d chosen.

His eyes went wide when he realized they had picked two upgrades this time around. That shouldn’t be possible, but I’m not complaining.

Manifest Chain <soulbound> (Unique): Rank 4. Paths: Restraint, Dual Chains, Adjustable Anchorage. Mana Enhancement.

You may summon the twin chains of oblivion and guide them by your will. The chains may also be freed to act at their own discretion. If a manifestation is destroyed, it cannot be summoned again for 24 hours.

Foebinder can carry considerable weight and features considerable retractive power. It is resilient to physical attacks and weak against magic. Magebane is resistant to magic and vulnerable to physical attacks. The chain can conduct your Disruption field, suppressing the skills of lesser opponents and weakening the magic of powerful foes.

After the chains emerge from your wrists, you may adjust their anchorage to appear anywhere on your body or within six inches of its boundary. Channeling mana into the chains will increase their attributes.

Awesome. Until now, Edge had been forced to fight with the chains attached to his wrists if he wanted to use them in battle. They’d managed to make it work, but the arrangement had always been awkward, interfering with the motion of his arms while he fought. Now, he could reposition the portal-like anchorage beyond the boundary of his skin.

The upgrade would let him fight at his full potential even when both chains were out. He no longer had to worry about being dragged by a powerful enemy or being stricken by elemental energy conducting through their metal.

The second upgrade was just as exciting. It let him boost Foebinder and Magebane by channeling mana into them, similar to how he could flare his other skills when they reached rank three. He had the sense that the cost was considerably higher, but anything that made the chains stronger was an invaluable asset.

“Looking good. I don’t know how you squeezed in two upgrades, but I am deeply impressed.” Alive with the visceral thrill that always accompanied taking a big step forward on the long path to power, Edge hopped back into his body and got ready to make his next choice.

The convict was still breathing thanks to Rejuvenation, although mercifully, the shock had knocked him out. Edge realized this technique would let him steal skills from critically injured foes in the future, which was both highly ironic, considering he’d chosen the aura upgrade to protect his friends, and incredibly useful at the same time.

He had no reason to draw this out, so after making sure nothing nasty was headed his way, he used the second charge of his ultimate ability. The gladiator’s inner world had been ravaged by the rough Extraction, not to mention whatever happened to a core when the chains dined on one of its skills. He didn’t let himself dwell on this odd and distinctly unpleasant aspect of his magic, since the warrior was unconscious and it would all be over soon.

The next skill Edge wanted specialized in evasion, which made stealing it unusually tricky.

The elusive avatar moved so quickly that his eyes could barely track it. It raced the chains as they chased it around the core—dodging, ducking, twisting, and leaping in a flurry of silky-smooth movements that only made him desire it more. In the end, he had to ask Chibime and See to provide backup while pitching in himself. Together, they cornered the slippery skill long enough for the living links to grab hold.

He braced himself as the chains pulled the power out of the warrior’s core and flung it between his jaws. He couldn’t even come close to chomping the evasive avatar, but this wasn’t his first rodeo. Edge used the technique he’d developed while subduing Shadow Step, sucking the air from his mouth and tightening his cheeks—leaving no space for it to hide. Once it was trapped, he crushed the skill between his tongue and his gum, stunning it long enough to take a bite.

Its essence washed over his palate, a delectable flavor reminiscent of salted watermelon with a pinch of chili powder. The flavor was paired with an impression of dodging so precisely that he could dance between the raindrops, although he was pretty sure that was a metaphor and not a literal interpretation of what its magic could do.

One way or another, I’m about to find out. When the pureed avatar slid into his core, a message from the System appeared.

You have extracted the skill: Kinetic Evasion (Rare, Rank 5).

Digestion is complete.

Would you like to slot in Kinetic Evasion now?

Once again, the Rare skill came with enough experience that he could rank it up with a single Extraction, which meant he had time to run some tests on both powers and advance them before his meeting with King. When he confirmed the prompt, the avatar zoomed over to an empty pillar and bonded to the socket. It didn’t change shape this time around, remaining a blur that flitted about so swiftly that he couldn’t bring it into focus.

The vision gave him a good sense of how Kinetic Evasion worked, and he read the description to fill in the gaps.

Kinetic Evasion <passive> (Rare): Rank 1.

This skill allows you to feel incoming attacks before they land. The specific sensation corresponds with the nature of the threat, the intensity reveals its distance, and the location indicates where the strike will land.

Kinetic Evasion was one of the most unusual powers Edge had come across yet. It was a defensive skill that specialized in avoidance but didn’t offer direct protection. Instead, it created an entirely new sense, which would let him feel when and where something was about to hit him. He had a hunch that after he grew accustomed to using Evasion and ranked it up a time or two, he would be able to discern a great deal about their nature and potency too.

After battling the gladiator, he already knew how potent this skill would become. While it couldn’t protect him against attacks that were so fast that he didn’t have time to react, this was going to be a real game-changer, letting him evade killing blows as naturally as breathing. Better still, it worked on strikes he didn’t see coming, although he was sure the skill could be fooled by various countermeasures. For example, I bet Sakura’s Deception could create a false positive. Beelo should be able to help me figure out the details.

Regardless of the specifics, being able to feel when he was about to be wounded would dramatically reduce the damage he took, teaching him how to move more effectively on the battlefield in the process. Evasion would work hand in hand with Gaze, helping him sort through the sensory data to dodge pressing threats and let Harden and Wind Ward handle the rest.

Edge put himself on the frontline whenever possible, since his friends didn’t share his myriad protections. He had done his best to suppress his awareness of his own mortality while grappling with one ordeal after the next, but he was still afraid of dying. Even with Rejuvenation to repair the damage, being struck by weapons, claws, and spells was terrifying, and he still felt the pain of his wounds. If something penetrated his diamond scales and overwhelmed his Organ-Guard, he could be killed just like anyone else.

Shaking his head to clear his mind of such weighty matters, he returned his attention to the task at hand and used his final charge of Extraction.

There weren’t any more Rare powers inside the man’s core, or anything that seemed like it would synergize well with his collection. Before he claimed his final skill, Edge did something that hadn’t occurred to him before. He took a long, thoughtful look at the gladiator’s skillset, pondering their properties while considering every upgrade the man had chosen—how the powers came together to form a whole that was more than the sum of its parts.

It wasn’t easy for most people to obtain, remove, and successfully merge skills. It took diligent daily practice and operating in threatening conditions to rank up powers the usual way. Thus, each choice the warrior made had been a literal gamble with his life. It gave Edge a renewed appreciation for just how special Skill-Eater was, and he resolved to make the most of her gifts during the days to come.

By the time he reached Extraction’s limit, he had analyzed the complex arrangement, unpacking the logic that led to the skillset’s present form. He claimed a high-rank Uncommon, left the wreckage of the warrior’s inner world, and put the murderous bastard out of his misery. Edge added the experience to Enhance Senses, which was next on his list for advancement.

With that out of the way, he went through the distasteful process of looting the body. The armor was a bloody mess, and its materials were nothing special, so he took the man’s shorn weapon, backpack, and everything attached to his kit to sort through later, including a potion bandolier and belt pouches full of consumables.

Next, he called up his Guide to check out his updates.

You have accumulated sufficient experience to cycle up your core. Your new cycle is: 3.

You have accumulated sufficient experience to cycle up your core. Your new cycle is: 4.

You have 14 attribute points awaiting allocation.

For defeating a cored opponent Stage 2 or above possessing additional Mortium, you have earned 17 Mortium.

Whatever guilt Edge might have felt was assuaged by that information. He supposed the gladiator could have been hunting monsters to earn so many lifecoins, but under the circumstances, it was far more likely he’d been preying on hunters operating in the Lakelands.

“Good riddance. It’s better for everyone that you disappeared today. It’s only a matter of time before the monsters invade in full force. Every life you snuffed out brought the Ancients one step closer to devouring us all.”

As he’d planned, Edge put two points into Amplification and Disruption and ten into Speed, taking it into a range he was comfortable with for the time being. The potentia worked its magic, enhancing his muscles to make them quicker than ever.

When it was over, he left the body for the scavengers and stepped into his central chamber—ready to welcome the new members of the team and celebrate Manifest Chain’s major milestone. As he joined his avatars for an impromptu party, he praised Rue for staying inside his den while the fight was underway, laughing when the beast teleported into his arms and started licking his face.

“That’s right. I had everything under control, and a stage-three warrior is too dangerous for you to tackle until you’re older. I’m glad that you can trust me and I can trust you. I’m pretty sure our Companion bond is narrowing the gap between us over time, since a portion of my experience is flowing into you. Keep working hard and trust the process. It won’t be long until we’re fighting side by side.”

When he reached his daily limit, Edge bid his team farewell and popped back into his body, reflecting on his recent experiences as he hiked.

While some aspects had been distinctly unpleasant, his short solo hunt was a resounding success. He had picked up some awesome new skills, gathered a decent pile of loot, and spread his wings along the way. He had finally found a good use for his triple token and had three more slots to fill in the days ahead, which he planned to save for Rares or Epics unless he came across something particularly promising.

He devoured the miles with a spring in his step—eager to return to the free city and share his accomplishments with his friends.
