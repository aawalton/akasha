Everything around me was dark. I felt nothing, except for the faint sound of water flowing around me. It felt as if I were diving deep inside a river that flowed steadily.

"Do you think it would work?"

"Probably."

The voice of gods rang in my ears—it was calming. Surprisingly, the gods were actually girls! I could tell by the way their voices sounded.

And then, all of a sudden, the invisible water around me hurled itself into my mouth.

My eyes shot open as I gagged on the feeling of being drowned. A weird aftertaste lingered in my mouth; my lips were wet. Has someone fed me something? Or had my saliva’s texture changed? I asked myself.

Though my brain didn’t think much about it, my eyes shifted. I blinked, fixing my gaze on the familiar study table I recognized—it was the same table I’d sat and studied at for the past two months. And the room… It was mine. I was back in my dorm room.

"Are you alright?" someone spoke up.

My eyes turned to the right, to the source of the voice. I paused, staring at two faces as my gaze darted from the blue-haired girl to the black-haired one… I finally recognized them. The people beside me were none other than Mabel Weeble and Noelle Armise.

"Did you two feed me something dirty?" I asked in a barely audible voice, the bitter aftertaste still lingering.

"I told you we should’ve fed it to him with a spoon!" Noelle scolded Mabel, ignoring my words, or maybe she didn’t even hear me?

"What did ya—" my voice rose, but I paused, noticing the half-filled mana potion placed on the floor. "Ahh…" I murmured.

After that, silence stretched. I turned my head away from the two girls and stared at the study table for a minute or so, trying to push the drowsiness away. Mabel and Noelle didn’t interrupt me, which was surprising since both of them were the talkative type.

"Wait!" My voice rose. "What happened in the dungeon!?" I asked the moment I remembered everythi— well, a few things.

There was a blast. I was certain of it, though not sure why or how, but Mabel must’ve protected me. Why would I be alive otherwise?

"Your Fireball stole all my kills," Mabel pouted.

"My… Feral Ball?" I repeated, blinking owlishly.

"Yeah." Mabel blinked, then glanced at Noelle. "What are the chances he’s lost his memories?" she asked.

"Hmm…" Noelle murmured. "That depends on when exactly his mana level depleted completely, was it a few moments after he cast the spell, or was it instant?" She paused, pressing the back of her palm to my forehead. "Not feverish."

"Not feverish," I repeated after Noelle, nodding like a young child.

Listening to the faint clues Noelle and Mabel gave me, my tired mind tried to make sense of the situation.

It was easy, actually. Really easy. There was a blast; I most probably protected Mabel from it using my body as a shield. Afterwards, my mana got drained. Maybe Mabel learned a method to harness other mana?

"Yeah, that might be true." I nodded.

"What is true?" Noelle questioned.

Ignoring the question, I continued thinking. She must’ve taken all my mana and dragged my body back to use my mana for another day. "Haha," I laughed at the idiotic plot I’d made in my mind. Even my barely conscious self knew Mabel wasn’t like that.

"You know what, Arnos," Mabel spoke up.

I glanced at her. "Wha’t?" I questioned.

"You should be thankful to me. I brought you out of the dungeon, gave you my shoulder for support, then placed you on your bed and put the blanket over you." She paused, her eyes narrowing slightly as she thought harder. "I also used my mana potion on you!"

"Wait… so you really did harness my mana!?" My eyes widened. I couldn’t believe it. How could she!

"Harness your mana? Hmm… yeah, I did use it indirectly."

"What! How could you!" My eyes teared up.

"We made the plan—" Mabel began, but before she could continue, Noelle placed her hand over Mabel’s mouth, muffling her speech.

"He’s still in a mana-less state, I think. Let him rest," Noelle murmured.

"Why? So that you could suck my mana again and again? What am I, an energy source to you…"

"Hmm… should we leave him be today then? Pretty sure he’d pick a fight with someone if we forced him to go to school."

"I’d go to swool! You can’t stop me, idiot Mabel… and uhh, Noelle."

"Why am I ‘idiot Mabel’ and she’s just Noelle!?"

"Idiot Mabel," I smiled.

Irritating her was something even my mana-less state enjoyed.

"Calm down, Mabel. He’s basically drunk… he doesn’t mean it. Pfft… and I mean, he didn’t say anything wron—"

"Quiet down, woman!" Mabel shouted, half-serious, as she crouched to pick up the mana potion. She twirled the bottle between her fingers, swirling the contents with a lazy motion.

"Oi, Arnos," she called out.

"Yeah."

"You aren’t going to ‘swool’ today. Drink the potion and sleep tight. When you wake up, you’ll thank me."

"I refuse."

"Instructor Seras has put a bounty over your head for five copper coins. If you go to class, you’ll be killed for copper coins. You don’t want that, right?" Mabel asked.

"You…" I murmured, "sorry." How could I have done that? Why did I call this generous, angelic lady an idiot? Oh, how mistaken I was. My eyes turned to Noelle—she was blinking with a smile, enjoying herself at the thought of me being killed for copper?

"Idiot Noelle, and Jus’t Mabel!" I exclaimed, correcting my past mistakes.

"Good, good… now, you want to get better soon, right?" Mabel cooed.

I nodded. Being a burden on her was certainly not something I desired. "Fine, give et to me!" I exclaimed, securing the bottle in my hand.

I looked at the bottle—the yellow liquid glowing faintly. The bitter taste my mind remembered made me hesitate; I didn’t want to drink it. But that wasn’t an option. Being killed for copper coins and causing Mabel and idiot Noelle trouble was something I definitely couldn’t do.

I gulped, then brought the glass bottle to my lips, chugging it down in a few moments. The bitter liquid flowed through my mouth. The taste wasn’t too bad—until it was too bad. The last swallow left my mouth filled with the aftertaste.

I grimaced. "Bleh."

"Good, good." Mabel clapped her hands, smiling. "Now, go back to sleep. Make sure the door is locked and you’ll be fine."

"Yes!" I nodded, stumbling up from the bed. Mabel and Noelle gave each other a quick glance before turning to the door. I followed them and locked the gate from the inside, I had no interest in being assassinated, certainly not for a few coppers.

With the door secured, I turned my attention to Mabel’s next instruction. She’d said I should sleep to recuperate, and that’s exactly what I did—silently moving back to the bed, lying down on the soft mattress, and tugging the blanket over my body as I went back to sleep.

It was around nine at night when my eyes fluttered open, due to a loud sound… Maybe someone snoared loudly or fell to the floor headfirst, but whatever the case, my eyes were now wide open.

It didn’t take long for the pain to settle in. My muscles ached badly; the mana-less state had taken its toll on me, and that was certainly not pleasant. Letting out a long, drawn-out yawn, I shifted on the bed and glanced at the clock—it read 9:40, meaning the mess had already started forty minutes ago.

Not that it mattered. I was pretty confident I’d still be able to secure dinner even if I reached as late as 10:20… probably. Most certainly.

Slipping my right leg from under the blanket, I placed it over the top when a memory from the morning flooded my mind… or was it yesterday? I wasn’t too sure.

"Take the day’s rest," Mabel had said before asking me to chug down a golden, glowish liquid. I supposed I hadn’t gone to school today.

"I mean, taking a day’s break isn’t that bad, right?" I murmured… and that’s when the gravity of the situation settled in.

"Fuck!" I yelled, remembering how I was supposed to increase my mana range to learn Dark-Pulse. Not much time was left before the Cellera Battle Fest, and I certainly had no time to waste.

Pushing the blanket off my feet, I sat up on the bed. I took a moment to breathe, inhaling and exhaling slowly as I let my eyes adjust to the darkness.

In moments I stood up from the bed, my legs were unsteady and my body imbalanced, as if I had no center of mass. I did, though. That was weird. My eyes turned to the floor—it was too dim to see. "Luminous," I muttered, and the chandelier of my room lit up.

Sliding my feet into my slippers, I stretched my arms like a cat, letting out another long yawn.

Afterwards, I did the same with my neck and legs, and with my body free from pain, I moved on to the next task.

Not having access to a staff, I decided to practice spreading my mana with the wand instead. Since it was smaller than a staff, I held it by the end and took my seat on the bed, placing a pillow between my hand and my lap.

With my eyes closed and breathing steadied, I calmed myself into a meditative state as I began picturing the mana flowing into the wand.
