57 - Chapter 51

While her team were busy making the preparations she had asked for, Sa Bina went through her mental checklist of preparations for the launch. She had split up the list into pre-event, during the event and post event lists.

The pre-event list was the longest. She had tasked Lou Sie and Be Net to finalise the invitation lists, create the invites, and send them out. Then there was the completion of the venue to her satisfaction and booking of entertainment options.

During the event, there was the order of events, the menu of both food and drinks, any presentation, and a speech. Finishing with entertainment.

Post event, she wanted to send away everyone with small gift baskets, along with a menu detailing the range of items available.

As she was thinking about the kind of menu she wanted to create, she had a thought. The people around here had a somewhat similar look to east asians from Earth. Did that mean they shared issues like lactose intolerance? What about allergies and other dietary issues? Thankfully she now had her healers on hand who could potentially answer questions around these sorts of issues. She went to see Shao Jia. Sa Bina could have just as easily called for her as the head of house and her employer, but felt that she should show her the respect of both her profession and age.

Even accounting for Sa Bina’s past life, Shoa Jia was several times older. Come to think of it that was true of most of her cultivator retainers. The realisation actually made her feel young for the first time. She had been unconsciously carrying a chip on her shoulder regarding her age and experience. And while that view was true of the mortals and body cultivators, it was decidedly not for the cultivators. In terms of age, she would not even be considered a child in the eyes of the elders. She could not say why the thought had come to her just then, but it also gave her tacit permission to be a little childish from time to time.

She made it to Shoa Jia’s office and knocked. Shao Jia was surprised to see her.

“Lady Sa Bina,” she said. “Do come in. You could have sent for me. How can I help.”

“It’s no trouble,” said Sa Bina. After their regular sessions, she was more open about her past. That made it easier to frame the questions. “I was just working on the menu for the opening of our new venue for the harvest festival, and I was hit by a realisation. I thought it would be best to check with you.”

“I assume this is something medical,” replied Shao Jia. “What is the issue.”

“Well,” said Sa Bina. “In my world there are several chronic conditions that impact people. The two which are germane to our venue opening are in relation to foods. More specifically, do people have issues with intolerance towards certain foods?”

“What do you mean?” asked Shao Jia, clearly interested. Sa Bina had found the healer's attention always sharpened when anything medical related was discussed.

“In my old world,” said Sa Bina. “There were people who would react badly to some foods. This was not an issue of not liking the taste. Some people would come out in a rash, or find their face, hands and throat swelling from certain foods. To the point, it could be life threatening. We called these allergies. The general treatment was to identify the allergens, then keep away from them. The other option was to use medicines to reduce the effect of the immune system. Generally allergies are caused when the immune system is hyperactive, and attacks things it should not.”

“This immune system,” said Shoa Jia. “It is the system that deals with threats at a very small level. You called them bacteria and viruses I believe?” She still found it hard to accept that someone who was not even trained in the healing arts would have so much in depth knowledge about the functioning of the body. The quality of schooling in Sa Bina’s old world must have been leagues beyond their own.

“Yes,” said Sa Bina. “It also deals with non-organic threats like poisons, as well as works with the body to expedite healing. The other issue we see with food is to do with intolerances. So in those cases, the impact is not life threatening, but the people are unable to digest certain food groups, like milk or grains. If they accidentally consume these things, then it leads to them spending time having to purge their bowels, usually quite painfully over a prolonged period. Have you heard of anything similar here?”

“So,” replied Shao Jia, after some thought. “I have heard of things that could be similar to what you describe, though it is normally seen in children and mortals. Generally once you start to cultivate, a lot of these defects are fixed for you. Some of these issues may persist in the early stages of body enhancement, but after the qi gathering stage it is impossible to impact a cultivator by such mundane means. Even to poison us requires rare and specific ingredients containing high quantities of qi.”

“Does that mean I should prepare a separate menu for non-cultivators?” asked Sa Bina. “How widespread is the issue?”

“I don’t think you need to go so far,” said the healer. “The cases are rare, and again more prevalent in those with poor quality spirit roots. If I were to hazard a guess it would not impact more than one person in a thousand, it may even be as rare as one person in ten thousand.”

“Qi really changes everything,” mused Sa Bina. “I just wanted to check. Talking about not being able to poison cultivators, does this also apply to the effects of alcohol? Does that mean I would need to brew alcohol with ingredients with qi to get them drunk?”

“Brewing spirit wine is an art form,” said Shao Jia, laughing. “As is procuring spirit herbs in any significant amount. It is not something you can farm just anywhere. You would not see the production of spirit wine outside of the sect. Certainly, no one would expect you to be serving it in your venue. Though if you start getting enough high tier cultivators, then you may want to procure some to sell.”

That thought frustrated Sa Bina, and her plans. It would mean her alcohol would not be targeted to powerful cultivators, but to the lower end of the spectrum. It explained why people were not spending time creating and perfecting high quality mundane alcohol. Still, if they enjoyed the taste, they may find it an amusing curiosity, maybe even enough to serve it at their functions.

She thanked the healer for her advice and went back to her planning. At least she would not have to worry about the menu choices causing issues. She was thinking of going with a variety of canapes, both savoury and sweet, to showcase a range of both local and new dishes. She had already been experimenting with some recipes, but would like to increase the range to truly show off for the event. It would also be a way to gauge feedback to her creations from a wide range of locals.

She knew that she would upset Be Net and Lou Sie, but she had to return to the kitchen to experiment with options for the evening. Time was short and she wanted everything to be perfect. To that end she had to utilise all her free time, outside of training and school to best effect.

***

Be Net looked at the finalised invitations. They looked brilliant in gold. It had taken time, but finally all the retainers and the army were fully dressed in the house colours and carried the house coat of arms. He had not realised how much it had grated on him to not see the household appropriately attired.

Lou Sie was working on scribing and sending out the invitations to all the worthies of the city. Even with the short notice he hoped that most would choose to attend. It would be embarrassing if the household was snubbed on their first outing.

He was still of the opinion that the young miss was rushing ahead, and should have waited a little longer. Yet her mind was set. He would do what he could to support her ambitions. To that end he had a lot of things to finalise himself.

He had already sent off the orders for provisions that Lady Sa Bina had asked him to procure. What he had left to do, he could not start immediately. Only when the venue was finalised, could he conduct the final training on the wait staff for the event. There was simply too much to do.

As he was working through this extensive list of tasks, there was a knock on his door.

“I wonder if you have a few minutes Be Net,” asked the cook.

“Sure, what can I help you with,” he replied.

“We need to send for some more ingredients,” she replied. “The young lady is in the kitchen again, trying out new recipes.”

Be Net struggled not to let out a long suffering sigh. He knew that her upbringing and therefore values were different. But it really was not helping her reputation amongst the retainers.

“I will send someone,” he replied. “Do be sure to remind the kitchen hands not to gossip. I would be most upset if the rumours about our lady were to become any louder.”

“Why do you think she insists on doing these things herself?” asked the cook. “Do you think it is because she does not trust us?”

“No,” said Be Net. “I haven't found her to look down on anyone. I think she does not know what will work, and so is learning the ingredients.”

“So she really is not from this kingdom,” said the cook, a note of wonder in her voice. “To think she has been travelling at her age.”

“Of course she is not,” said Be Net. “I am just surprised that her kingdom puts so much pressure on one so young. Surely they could have sent out an adult to represent them. Then the lady could have enjoyed time with her friends and socialising instead of working all the time. There will be time enough for that once she comes of age in a few years.”

“One of my girls heard from one of the guards,” confided the cook. “That our lady has already started training. Not only that, Cultivator Sun Li has been pushing her hard, so I hear. On top of that she has been working hard on her studies. I do wish she would take a break now and then. Surely it can’t be good for her to be so busy all the time.”

“I must admit,” he replied. “That I would love to see her relax for a few days. Alas, I fear it is not meant to be. She pushes herself harder than anyone, no matter what she says.”

“Did you hear she wants us to cook for everyone to try out the menu before the event? I think I will get back and see what she is working on,” said the cook. “See if I can help her any. Besides, I will have to learn all the recipes anyway, as I will be running the kitchen on opening night.”

“Yes,” he replied. “I am not sure I will be able to properly appreciate the strange new dishes she is planning. I am too old and set in my tastes I fear.”

“Oh don’t get so wound up,” said the cook. “You like her bread and butter well enough. I dare say her other dishes will be even better. It's not the eating that worries me, it is the cooking of it. She has it in her mind to create bite sized portions. All the fine work is going to be hard to get right. Not only that, but she expects them to all look the same!”

“Her standards are indeed high,” he replied. “Yet she does not ask of others any more than she demands of herself. Hopefully she will be happy with how everything plays out.”
