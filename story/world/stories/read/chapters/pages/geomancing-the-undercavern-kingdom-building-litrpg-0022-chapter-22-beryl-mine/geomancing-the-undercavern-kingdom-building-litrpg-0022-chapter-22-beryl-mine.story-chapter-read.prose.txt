Spears of stone shot through the air, crashing into a couple of yaddik guards near the tunnel entrance. Surprisingly, none of those who were hit died, despite taking injuries and staggering to the ground. But that quickly changed as Carmack’s vanguard squad rushed forward and stabbed their swords through weak spots in the insectoid’s natural armor.

He hadn’t needed to finish them with a single attack. The men were fast and decisive, not giving the injured yaddiks a chance to recover.

However, Carmack wasn’t about to let the vanguard have all the fun. His assault didn’t slow for a second, and he raised a hand toward the closest yaddiks and cast “Liquid Rock,” sinking them into the ground and immbolizing them for his troops to deal with.

Within seconds of launching his attack, Aphexia and Commander Gorn led their own forces into the fray.

Through the corner of his eye he watched as Aphexia spun into two yaddiks with a whirlwind of magical color. It wasn’t just her blade that was powerful, her skill clearly improved the effectiveness of her swirling steel, and the armored carapace of her enemies stood no chance—limbs went flying.

This was the difference between Bronze and Gold rank, he realized. His vast array of spells and the natural advantage of being below ground gave Carmack a clear edge on anybody at his own rank, but Aphexia’s abilities were clearly beyond that.

Carmack had to use wit and patience to outsmart and defeat the enemies before him. Had he launched a straightfoward attack, he likely would have quickly ran through his mana as his attacks ricocheted off the yaddik’s formidable carapace. Aphexia, on the other hand, needed no tricks. Her attacks could cut straight through their hard shells as if it were no stronger than human flesh.

Cutting short his thoughts, alarms sounded. Carmack’s gaze rose up to where he heard the booming horns and spotted larger, meaner looking yaddiks with spiked carapaces exiting the huts. The menacing yaddiks ran across to their vivek beetles and mounted the massive, flying insects.

Within seconds, they were buzzing through the cavern sky above, and gathering as they prepared to attack.

Around twenty of the mounted elite yaddiks formed a line and aimed for Carmack and the troops as they dispatched the last remenants of the yaddik guards.

“Commander!” Carmack shouted. “Defensive formations!”

Gorn looked up and nodded, realizing what Carmack had spotted and immediately began barking orders at his men.

The three main attack forces had all charged the high ground, while the remaining squad had rushed to the aid of the slaves once they realized that the battle was won. Carmack glanced between the yaddiks and their forces. They appeared to be preparing their charge for their forces on high ground platform.

Perfect. We’ll face them here.

Gathering together the vanguard troops pointed their spears and shields up toward the enemy. They were brave in the face of danger, but Carmack knew these men were still far too green to take on an enemy of this level. Even with a considerable numerical advantage and good tactics on their side, this would be a hard fought battle. He could tell just by looking at their enemy.

Carmack gathered his mana and watched the insectoids prepare their assault. Then, at once, they descended. A flying cavalry charge swooping down at great speed.

Holding back his attack, Carmack waited then slammed his fist against the ground, casting “Grave of Stone.”

Spikes shot out from the ground between gaps in guardsmen’s formation. He had waited for the last second, and the bugs couldn’t slow or turn back now. The speeding, mounted warriors crashed into stone spikes and pointed spears of the guardsmen alike.

In that instant, Aphexia leaped up and dove down upon the yaddiks from above, releasing a furious combination of attacks and mana infused skills that shredded carapace and flesh alike.

Attacked from all angles, the yaddiks screeched and turned their gazes all about as they struggled to pick a direction to aim their attacks. Spears already impaled into their enemies, the guardsmen then drew their swords and enter the fray.

At teh battle’s rear Carmack staggered. He had completely drained his mana after casting the 4th circle spell. But it had done its job. The spikes had broken the enemy charge, and whilst they were still formidable, they now found themselves pressed up against his soldiers with nowhere to run.

Despite this, the yaddik elites were no pushovers. The struck back with cascading slicing attacks from their scimitars, sending the attacking guardsmen back into retreat. Their attacks were just too fast.

However, the wounded yaddiks couldn’t break free from the melee to attempt another charge, and even though they sent some guardsmen fleeing, more took their place.

In the end, the melee was short and decisive. The enemy was too greatly outnumbered, and their attacks countered too precisely. Still, despite an impossible situation, they had still managed to kill three vanguard warriors.

Carmack surveyed the battlefield with panted breaths. At least a dozen men had been wounded to some degree, and even Commander Gorn and Aphexia looked tired from the encounter.

We won, but it’s clear that we have holes that need patching, Carmack thought with an internal sigh.

“Bastard insects” Aphexia spat.

“Those were certainly not goblins,” Commander Gorn muttered over the corpse of one of his men. “Had we been equally numbered, we likely would have fallen here today.”

Carmack couldn’t refute him. He had exhausted his mana breaking their charge and yet they had still taken casualties. With equal numbers he was sure his men would have broken and would have been destroyed.

But he wasn’t able to linger on the thought. A buzz of activity redirected his gaze up to the yaddik camps and he spotted a couple of large yaddiks take to their bug mounts and fly away, buzzing up into tunnels above them and disappearing into the shadows.

Okay, now that’s less than ideal.

As the notifications rolled past Carmack’s vision, he was reminded of their gains. Sure, they had taken some losses, but they had gained experience and freed the slaves. This was a win in anybody’s book, and there was little point moping about a less than perfect outcome.

Until now, he hadn’t put much thought into when he would hit Iron rank, but figured it wasn’t too surprising that he had finally hit it. From what he understood of this world, Iron was rather achievable for anyone who had a decent bit of combat experience under their belt.

Most people would take far longer to reach such an achievement, since they wouldn’t have access to an entire skill tree, and likely would be forced to spread their stats. Not only that, but cheating the System was no easy task. To become a strong warrior you had to fight—in real battles.

Reflexively, Carmack placed his points into Mental and sighed. Today could have gone better but he couldn’t complain with results.

Scanning over his status page, Carmack shrugged. He couldn’t discern and real change besides his stage. Perhaps there were other changes he hadn’t noticed yet, but he could ask someone later.

He also had a skill point to spend again, but he needed to think on the matter, and decided to leave it for now.

“At least I got a level,” Gorn chuckled as he stretched out.

“Man, I got three whole levels. That’s insane. More progress than years of training in the guard,” one of the guardsmen nearby shook his head.

“Yeah, ya kill thieve. I had that one guy on the ropes. What the hell was that about?”

“Hey, what do you mean, kill thief? You wanted me to just leave him? I was helping out, damn it!”

“Looks like the men grabbed a few levels as well,” Carmack said, turning to Gorn as they eavesdropped on the conversational.

“No doubt a bunch of them did. Still, feels a little hollow with our losses. Even if the spirits are high.”

“It’s hard, I know,” Carmack sighed. “But if we’re to survive, we’re going to need to get stronger. This was just a small camp of thugs and we’re supposed to be the vanguard and strongest warriors of an entire city. This should have been nothing for us.”

“For once I’m actually agreeing with the hero,” Aphexia said. “If we lose men against such an enemy even when he have the element of surprise, we got problems. Big problems. And there’s only one way to fix them.”

“Yeah, I know,” Commander Gorn slumped. “It just bums me out a little.”

“It’s fine. The good thing is we learned of our limits now. It’ll give us time to prepare. The last thing we want is to charge into a battle we can’t win,” Carmack said.

“Yeah,” Aphexia nodded. “Especially with those rats around. Who knows what they’re up to.”

She’s right about that. We can’t waste anymore time. Lydrus needs a real army.

“Hey, Commander.”

The three of them turned to a couple of guardsmen.

“Yes?”

“It’s the beetles. We–ah–caught a couple.”

“Show me,” Carmack interjected.

The guardsmen led the trio across the cavern to where a dozen guardsmen had five beetles surrounded and were trying to lasso them.

Casting “Liquid Rock,” Carmack ensarled the beasts and the guardsmen were able to capture them.

“Looks like they’ve hurt their wings,” Commander Gorn said, eyeing the awkwardly flickering wings.

“Secure them and bring them back to the settlement,” Carmack ordered.

“Got plans?” Aphexia said.

“We’ll see.”

Interrupting the conversation, the sergeant of the fourth squad marched up to them with a couple of his men.

“Hero, Commander Gorn, Aphexia,” he bowed. “The slaves have been freed, but they’re a wary bunch. It’s clear they don’t trust me or my men just yet.”

“Understandable,” Carmack said and strolled up to the stone ledge to look down into the mine where the freed slaves were rubbing at their tender wrists and ankles.

The ragged slaves look up with tears welling in their eyes. The fourth squad of guardsmen had rushed out to protect the slaves when the battle started, though they'd only faced off against a couple completely outnumbered yaddiks.

The men were now sharing their water skins and handing out health potions. Looking down, Carmack could see the concern etched clearly on their faces. They were no doubt grateful for being saved, but had been through too much to just throw their trust at a stranger.

“Hear me,” Carmack said. “You’re all free now. We'll be heading back to our settlement once we're done looting the insectoid camp and we plan to occupy this mine. Though we cannot currently provide passage to the surface, we can keep you safe. Whether you choose to leave for the surface on your own, or not, is your decision. But we would be happy to welcome you to our settlement with open arms.”

Response was muted. The former slaves shared scared glances with one another, but dared not speak.

“Do not worry, I will not ask any to return to this mine. Commander Gorn,” Carmack said, turning back to the guardsman. “Prioritize getting these people back to the settlement quickly and safely.”

“Will do, hero. And what are your plans?”

“I'll make sure this cavern is secure and close off any unnecessary tunnels, working my way back to the spire.”

“Won't that take a while?”

“Hours probably,” Carmack shrugged. “Small price for our security.”

Blankets, bowls of food and healing potions were handed out to the former slaves who gathered in the eating hall. They had entered with broken spirits and deep set frowns, but when the potions touched their lips and the food filled their bellies, their expressions brightened.

The former slaves had looked as if they were ready to collapse, their hands so shaky they could barely bring their bowls to their mouths. Their legs trembled with every step. But as they ate, smiles crept across their faces, and color returned to the palor of their skin.

The settlement’s stocks of health potions were running low.The settlement only had what was brought with them from Lydrus, but they couldn’t refuse people in need. As useful as the mushrooms and moss were, they weren’t ingredients that could be used to make healing potions.

Once Carmack was finished closing off the beryl mine from the rest of the Undercavern, he trekked back through the zombie tunnels.

A couple of guardsmen had been left behind with him in the mine, and he had them carry one of the yaddik corpses back.

The moment they were within the mist, Carmack could feel the deathly mana seeping into the corpse. The effects were not immediate. He had the guardsmen place the corpse in one of the deadend tunnels and then closed it up, leaving a hole to peep through.

The entire process took about an hour. The corpse jerked to life and staggered to its feet, moaning. It’s head lurched from side to side before turning to the peep hole and dragging itself toward Carmack.

The carapace would no doubt make the yaddik zombie stronger than the human ones, but there was little it could do against a stone wall.

“Do you mind putting it to rest?” Carmack asked one of the guardsmen.

“The zombie? Of course, ready as always!”

The guardsmen stepped up to the hole and Carmack enlarged it. Taking a soul always provided experience, assuming they were high enough level to assist the one doing the killing. However, restraints effected how much experience was provided. An unconscious or sleeping foe provided far less experience than one able to put up a fight. And having his men attack it from behind the wall would have the same effect.

“Do you think you can take it?”

The guardsman produced another waterskin and poured it over his spear. “Holy water. I should be fine.”

“Good,” Carmack said and lowered the wall.

The zombie crept forward, but thanks to the holy water and the reach of the spear, the guardsman easily stabbed sizzling holes into the monstrosity until it fell down dead.

“How was the experience?”

“Yaddik zombie…” the guardsman murmured. “Says it’s above my level. Pushed me up to freaking level 10, would you believe?”

It was a pity they didn’t have many yaddik corpses to train with. Once the zombies had suffered a true death, the deathly magic couldn’t resurrect them again. Still, this was great opportunity, Carmack knew.

“Good job, guardsmen. Come, let’s head back to town.”

“Yes, hero!” The guardsmen echoed.
