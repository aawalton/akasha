“What do you think, Celeste?” Viktor murmured. “Why does Rennald put so much trust in me? Why does he think that I know the dark spirit personally? And why, of all things, does he believe that the dark spirit is the one who saved Daelin eight years ago?”

[I don’t know, Master. The monster attack happened long before you woke up, back when I was still alone in that dark place, so there is simply no way for either of us to know what actually occurred during that time. Still, I believe Rennald was sincere when he offered his support, so I think you should just take it at face value for the time being. You can always ask him the reason later, when it is more appropriate.]

Viktor nodded. That was more or less the course of action he had settled on himself. He glanced over at the woman lying motionless on the bed next to him, her body little more than a brittle outline under the blankets. Tomorrow, he was going to avenge her. That was all that mattered at the moment. That was the only thing that deserved his focus.

“Yes, the Overseer’s secrets can wait,” he said. “Come to think of it, the ink drop Yvonne planted inside his office all those months ago is still working, right?”

That had happened on the very same day he and Claire went to the caravan station to meet Rennald. While they were intercepting him in the courtyard, Alycia had caused a commotion inside the building by sealing the front doors shut, and Yvonne, taking full advantage of that distraction, had slipped up to the top floor unnoticed and placed an ink drop somewhere.

In hindsight, the whole scheme turned out to be completely unnecessary, since Rennald had become an ally anyway. Also, while the act of planting that ink drop might have been difficult to pull off at the time, Viktor now had a lot more tools at his disposal should he ever wish to spy on the Overseer again. He could simply pass through the wall to do the planting himself, or he could order Galatea to sit invisible in a corner and listen to every word spoken in the office.

[Yes, the quill still writes down everything the ink drop overhears in that room. In fact, I have a full record of the conversation you just had with him, right here at hand.]

“Is that so?” Viktor said, amused. “What else?”

[I do check it from time to time, but most of the conversations are simply business talk related to the running of the caravan station. There is nothing about our dungeon that we didn’t already know. Well, a week ago, after the parley had concluded, Isadora came there to speak with the Overseer. I do have a record of their conversation during that visit, and... let’s just say that she had some very strong feelings about you.]

Viktor chuckled. “Yes, yes, I’m well aware. Mommy is mad.”

[Is there anything else you need of me, Master?]

Actually, yes.

There was indeed one more matter he wanted to ask her opinion on. But he stopped himself at the last moment, swallowing the words back down before they could leave his mouth.

“No. Go back to whatever you were doing.”

[Understood, Master. Just call me if you need anything.]

The voice faded, and his gaze drifted back to Claire. He let out a weary sigh. This was a burden he had to carry alone. There was no reason to trouble anyone else with this, least of all Celeste, who had already been put through enough because of the way he behaved during that one week.

Still, what choice should he make?

He stood there for a long while, unmoving, turning the question over and over without arriving at any answer. Then, he shook his head. He didn’t have to decide tonight. Tomorrow, he would kill Xander. Everything else could wait until after the bastard had received his long-overdue, well-deserved death.

Let’s have a walk, he thought.

The west side sat under curfew still, but the Sanctuary’s courtyard was spacious enough for him to stretch his legs. So, take a stroll around the yard, breathe in some fresh night air, and then go to bed. He needed to be in his best shape possible for tomorrow’s fight, after all.

He made his way out of the room, down the corridor, and through the main hall of the building. Pushing the heavy wooden doors open, he stepped outside, and there he found—

Tobias was kneeling in front of him, eyes closed, head inclined.

Huh?

He was startled for a second, caught off guard by the unexpected sight, before quickly realizing that the old man was simply praying to the Matriarch’s statue perched above the entrance.

“Sorry to disturb you,” he said as Tobias slowly rose to his feet.

“It’s fine. I was nearly done anyway.”

“I’m curious. Why do you keep the statue out here, of all places? If it’s meant for prayer, why not place it inside, in the hall proper? Isn’t it inconvenient, being disrupted every time someone happens to walk through?”

“The Mother is here so that she can see everyone who comes to her house. She casts her merciful gaze upon those in need who pass through this entrance. Anyone who wishes to speak to her could do so immediately, the very moment they set foot on this sacred ground, without having to venture any further inside. As for disruption, what problem would be there in that? What we say in prayer is short and to the point, brief enough that it hardly matters if it’s interrupted partway through, while she herself does not mind in the slightest.”

“What do people pray to her, anyway?”

“I don’t know what the others pray for. Everyone carries their own wishes and burdens. As for myself, I pray for her protection over the souls who seek shelter within these walls, so that no harm may come to them. I ask her to grant me the strength to help those who still can be helped, and to grant peace to those whose fate is beyond my power to alter.”

Right. Prayer tended to work out rather well when what you asked for was vague enough to survive any outcome. Especially when you had already made room for the possibility of failure within the request, as though disappointment had been given a seat at the table before it even came knocking.

“Have you ever prayed for one specific patient’s recovery? Someone in critical condition?”

“I pray for strength, so that I might bring them salvation through my own hands. If I fail to do so in the end, then it means I’m simply not good enough. The fault belongs to me alone, not to her.”

I take that as a no, Viktor thought.

Hardly a surprise, really. A specific, difficult-to-fulfill prayer tended to let you down. It forced you to confront the uncomfortable truth that prayer did nothing at all. So instead, you opted for a “safe” one, the kind that would be granted no matter what happened, immune to disproof by design. But if that was the way of it, what was even the point of praying?

“You don’t believe any of it, do you?” Tobias asked.

Viktor gave a shrug. “I’ll start believing the moment Claire wakes up and walks out here herself, standing before me as though nothing has ever happened to her.”

The old man sighed. “The Mother is neither omniscient nor omnipotent. She cannot tend to every suffering soul in the world by herself, which is precisely why she needs the rest of us to act in her stead, lending our hands where hers cannot reach. Still, she is listening, and she offers what help she can.” He gazed at Viktor. “You can try talking to her. Tell her about your sister. Tell her how desperately you want your sister to get better.”

Me? Praying?

The Dark Emperor himself, bowing his head and begging for help from some carved figure of stone?

Ridiculous!

And yet...

If there was even a sliver of a chance it helped Claire somehow, then he could stand to swallow a mouthful of pride and call it a fair trade.

“How am I even supposed to pray, anyway?”

“There’s no single correct way to go about it,” Tobias said. “Just tell her your sincere feelings, and that will be enough.”

And then, without another word, the old man turned and disappeared back into the building, leaving Viktor standing alone in the empty courtyard, before the statue of the holy woman whose merciful gaze fell upon him and only him.

This is fucking stupid.

But... fine.

Words were cheap, after all. He could certainly spare a few for a statue. This wasn’t going to accomplish a damn thing, but it was not like he had anything to lose by trying either.

Of course, kneeling was out of the question. So he stood instead, upright and unbending, gazing up at the stone woman’s serene face, and murmured his prayer into the quiet night air.

“Matriarch of the Emerald Order, if you can truly hear the prayers spoken before your likeness, then hear mine as well. My name is Quinn, and I stand before you today with a single request. My sister, Claire, now lies unconscious within these walls. She has suffered because of something that was never her fault to begin with, a cruelty imposed by circumstances beyond her control. If there is mercy in this world, if your compassion truly reaches those who suffer, then I ask you to watch over her. Ease her pain, guide her back to me, and grant her the strength to open her eyes, so that she can see the world as she once did. I seek no blessing for myself. If there is any grace you can offer, then let it be hers, all of it.”

Done. Happy?

He was about to turn away. But then, he stopped himself.

Sincere, huh?

He took a deep breath. He let it out slow.

Fine, let’s do it.

And so, he gazed at that serene face once more.

“My name is... Viktor.”

He wasn’t entirely sure whether this was a good idea.

“...And Claire is not my sister. We aren’t bound by blood, and until less than a year ago, we were complete strangers.”

This was awkward.

“Tobias told me to tell you my sincere feelings, but to be honest, I’m not even sure what they are. I... I used to hate Claire. Yes, I did. She reminded me of my real sister, the one I lost a long time ago, and I resented her for that. Now... I don’t hate her anymore. Probably.”

“...I still couldn’t tell you exactly what she is to me. Family, or not. What I know is that I’m upset when bad things happen to her, and I do want her to wake up and live the life she had been living before all this.”

“You know what? I don’t believe that you are listening. And even if you are, why should you care what I have to say? I’ve always thought begging was pointless. Especially begging a god. If they help you, it’s because they want to. If they don’t, then no amount of pleading could change their minds. People turn to prayer not because they know it works, but because every other option has already run out. So they pray. Maybe it gives them hope. Maybe it makes them feel a little less powerless.”

“Still...”

“If you’re there... if there’s even the slightest chance that Tobias is right, that behind that stone face there’s indeed someone who listens...”

“Then please...”

“...let her wake up.”
