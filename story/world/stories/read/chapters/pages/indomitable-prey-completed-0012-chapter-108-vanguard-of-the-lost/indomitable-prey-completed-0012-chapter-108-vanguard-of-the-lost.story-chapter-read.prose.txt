The fighting seemed to be over, and I took the chance to shake blood from my spear. It was not entirely that of my enemies though, for I'd received a nasty gash along my chest that wept crimson tears down my torso.

I'd gone to ground with a soldier of immense strength. He was clearly in the 2nd tier, and highly placed within it if I had to guess, based on his strength compared to my own. We’d wrestled in the mud like a pair of pigs in shit, squealing and goring at each other with knives instead of tusks. Our shared blood soaked the earth and painted each other's faces and weapons, and while I had come out on top, it had been close. Far closer than I would like.

I stumbled over to where Nathlan stood, concern etching my face, but he waved me off and I realised quickly that the blood that coated his robe was not his own. Sadrianna was nowhere to be found, and I whirled around looking for the others.

Jacyntha was leaning against a tree, holding her side. Blood sheeted down her stomach. I moved over, trying to ask if she was badly injured, but my lips were swollen from a nasty punch to the face, and the words didn't come out properly. She seemed to get the gist of it though and shook her head as she spat to one side.

“I’ll live,” she said. “The others?”

I sniffed, before realising that simply looking might not be the best way to do this. Bodies were strewn around the small enclave of our camp. Broken weapons, broken armour, broken men and women littering the ground and turning a once peaceful forest glade into a scene of slaughter. There were two dozen at least just within eyesight, and I knew more had been killed further away, hidden behind trees and beneath rotting logs.

If this ambush had managed to kill Vera or Jorge, then we would die soon ourselves too, whether or not I drew attention with my shouting. So, I heaved in a breath and bellowed to the dark sky.

“Vera? Jorge?”

It took less than a minute for the old man to appear, and while Jacyntha and myself looked like we'd been through a battle­—which I suppose we had—and Nathlan looked, if not injured then at least spattered by gore, Jorge was clean as a whistle.

He bore not a mark on him, or his gear, and I was reminded of when we had hacked through the Wandering States in monsoon season; that strange shield he'd managed to work around himself to keep off the rain. I wondered if he had some sort of incredibly fine-tuned personal aura, or armour skill, but the thoughts were kicked from my head as he replied.

“Peace, Lamb. Sadrianna and Vera are culling any stragglers that tried to run, and they’re uninjured too, thank the gods.” I scowled and spat to one side, and he continued, “I know you don't like it lad, but we can't let them live. If they report what happened here, we’ll be hounded all the way through this cursed country.”

I ran a swollen tongue along teeth that felt slightly too loose, and grimaced. “It's not that. Fuck ‘em— they attacked us first. My face just hurts, that’s all.”

He seemed taken aback for a moment before nodding. “You came around quicker than I thought, little Lamb,” he muttered. “Right,” he said louder, clapping his hands to get our attention, “how are we all looking?”

He looked to Nathlan who waved him off. “No injuries. I am fine,” he said.

Jacyntha nodded next, lifting her arm to show the deep gash across her upper stomach, just below her chest binding, which was marred by crimson mud and no longer its previous pristine white. Her blood had sheeted down her stomach, obscuring the ridges of her abdominals, and the cut looked deep. A several inch long flap of skin and flesh hung away from her body, as if a butcher had taken a flensing knife and carved her like a turkey. It would need stitches, I knew.

“Anything else?” Jorge asked calmly as he approached.

She gestured vaguely to one side of her head as she replied, a little blearily. “Took a blow above my ear. Balance is all crooked.”

Only then did I notice the blood matting her hair, and I wasn’t sure if her speech was simply a product of the intense fatigue and adrenaline rush common after a battle, or whether the head-wound was having an effect.

And then it was my turn. With a grin I pointed at my mouth; bloodied lips, one ragged where it had split upon my teeth, the other swollen comically. “Teeth are a bit loose,” I said. “Took a gauntlet right in the face. Luckily it missed my nose though, so I’m still the handsome one in our little group, don’t you worry.”

I said it with an overdramatic wink, but my lips were so swollen that the words came out all screwed up, and it took a moment for the others to decipher my joke.

“Well, nice to know your spirits are still up, lad,” Jorge said with a smile, and I grinned a crimson smirk in return. “How about that chest of yours?” he asked.

“Ah,” I grimaced. “Lost a lot of strength in my left arm so I think it's as deep as the muscle,” I said, shrugging.

Jorge simply nodded. “Good. Good.”

Even Jacyntha looked a little put out at that, and Jorge hurriedly amended. “Coulda’ been a lot worse than that, mark my words. Good job on the warning, Lamb. Other than you two,” he said gesturing at me and Jacyntha, “we're unharmed, so this won't slow us too much. But we need to get out of here. This is...”

He blew air out between his cheeks as he indicated the mess around us, and I nodded.

“A fucking catastrophe?” I offered.

He chuffed a laugh. “Aye, lad, you can say that. You see the armour? These aren't bandits, they're soldiers.”

“What do you think this means? They recognise us?” I asked as I set down my weapons and looked for a clean bit of clothing that I could use to wipe down myself and my blades.

Jorge shook his head. “It could be targeted, but I think it's more likely they took us for bandits. The raid at Darrow's Edge was not the only one, I'd wager. And with that being true, there’ll be a lot of frustrated, angry soldiers looking for vengeance, someone to strike back against. Shit, their commanders may even have realised we were travellers, but simply needed a win or else risk mutiny. We were just in the wrong place at the wrong time.”

“Typical,” Jacyntha muttered, and I grinned over weakly.

“We were in the right place and time for you,” I retorted.

She inclined her head to acknowledge the point and then looked down at her gruesome wound. “Starting to regret that a little,” she said, to laughter from Jorge and myself. Even Nathlan smirked at the joke.

“Right,” Jorge said, clapping to get our attention. “We need to get cleaned up, salvage anything we can from this mess, and then get you both stitched up before we're on the move. Jacyntha, I know you don't want to hear this, but we might have to carry you. I'm not sure we have time for you to recover your balance. We're going to be moving at speed for a while.”

Jacyntha just sighed warily. “Well, to whatever unlucky bastard I bleed all over: thank you.”

Jorge inclined his head and turned to head towards the nearby stream but was interrupted by Nathlan.

“It gets worse,” the tall man said, and we all looked over to him. Seeing he had our attention, he gestured at the body below him; a man with a shaved head, dressed in fine robes, a thick bracelet of some sort of red metal encircling one wrist. Only once he pointed the man out did I realised that he didn’t match the others.

“He does not share their uniform. This man is from the Leviathan Coast,” Nathlan said. “See the bangle? That denotes him as a member of the Wavebreakers. Minor, from an unimportant branch of the family most likely, but he has some skill with ward-craft.”

At my startled look, he explained further in that clipped and direct way of his, his usual meanderings thankfully not present given the time constraints. “It is not entirely unusual. After all, the Wavebreakers foster and encourage ward classes in all of their children, but not all can work on the storm-wards themselves. Many like him—most even—see nothing more than an interesting class. There is a reason that my perimeter ward wasn't activated. We cannot rely on it any longer.”

Jorge nodded again, seemed to process things. “Shit,” he said simply. “Well, good find, lad. Better we know than not, but this does complicate things. We've got both the Desolate Empire and the Leviathan Coast meddling in things here. This smells to me like civil war.”

“Why would they send a Wavebreaker here?” I asked, confused and missing what the others seemed to grasp.

“Because they want the Riverlands strong, or at least stable. They know that there is trouble brewing and they’re hoping to lend support to the armies to tamp things down. Given that they have some of the most competent ward-crafters on the continent, I'm not surprised to see them here. It's also a common practice to send their young to experience combat and leadership in new areas. After all, there’s not much strife within the harbour cities themselves.”

“Shit, okay,” I replied. “So, we just need to cross a country on the verge of civil war, avoiding not just bandits but soldiers, too.”

“That's about the long and short of it, aye,” Jorge said, and I laughed bitterly.

“No more fires then, I take it?” I asked somewhat plaintively.

The old man just shook his head.

I saw out of the corner of my eye Jacyntha pout at that proclamation, and that at least brought a smile to my face. Her Cat-Bear companion had started to take great delight in cooked food, and I knew she wouldn't relish going back to feeding him jerky.

“Right then,” Jorge said once more, and that was all we needed to hear. Weapons were cleaned and sheathed, and valuables collected from the dead. It didn't take long to strike camp and head to a nearby stream.

Weeks spent together in the wilderness removed much of the modesty we may have felt before, and so we stripped off quickly without care and set to scrubbing. Jacyntha and I traded a few glances, though I tried my best to pretend I hadn’t caught her looking my way. I just wanted to see what her stomach injury looked like without all the blood, of course. Simple curiosity for my companion’s war-wounds—no ulterior motives at all.

Vera and Sadrianna had appeared after we had finished rinsing our wounds in the achingly cold water and donning new clothes courtesy of Jorge’s storage ring. Our wise old mentor had ensured we all had basic knowledge of wound-care, but he and Sadrianna were the ones with the steadiest hands, so Jacyntha and I each had someone to stitch our wounds for us.

Once we were all patched up—alchemical salves and bandages applied to particularly bad wounds—we were on our way; running through the night and leaving the scene of slaughter far behind. Jacyntha had regained her balance by then and didn't need to be carried, but we still kept a close eye on her as we flitted over the countryside like starlings in flight.

It was in some ways ironic. As we travelled, we cursed bitterly the strife and war brought to a previously peaceful country, even as we aimed to reach the Sunsets and bring blade and fire to a stable polity ourselves. That irony wasn’t lost on me, and I stewed over the contradiction for long hours as we travelled.

Eventually, unable to come to a satisfying conclusion, my thoughts strayed to other areas. The face of the man I’d killed, as my spear took him in the heart. The gurgling scream that the man I’d wrestled in the mud had let out when my dagger had punctured his lungs. He’d known it was over by then, but still he grasped at the weapon, still he tried to speak. I wondered if I would be so unprepared when death came for me.

It nearly had, this night. I’d survived by a matter of inches. Sure, my reactions were heightened far beyond mortal means by over 50 points in cognition and perception, and with agility in the triple figures, I’d been able to spin aside with the grace of a pinpoint acrobat. But without that warning—without that itching instinct that told me something was wrong, that unconscious acknowledgement of missing sounds in the forest—I would have died.

I’d written no books or scrolls, and my name was recorded in no deed of land or marriage license. There was no village census, and unless my companions wrote of me long after my death, there would be no trace of me on the face of Tsanderos had I died tonight. It was a sobering thought. I’d not dreamt of my name echoing down the generations or anything of the sort, but the mere fact that nobody would even know if I was gone, except the five people I now journeyed with, was strange to think on.

Just like the Hasta. I had a skill named Vanguard of the Lost for a reason. There was a similarity there between us—me and that ancient people—and the System itself had acknowledged it. While journeying through the Dragonspines, I’d discovered something about that antique skill; something tickling at the edge of my mind whenever I’d trained with it, like a memory that wouldn’t quite come back to consciousness, but the feelings lingered.

I’d spoken to Jorge, wondering if it was like the hints that Improvised Weapons had dropped, conflicting with The Forgotten Spear and spurring me to create my own weapon with Sally the Tinkerer’s help. But no, it had been more profound than that. It had taken hours of discussion over the course of several weeks, and plenty of experimentation—by myself in the quiet hours of the morning, or with Jorge while we sparred in the evenings—but I’d eventually unlocked the secrets the skill was trying to impart.

‘Wield the weight of history’ had sounded fanciful—an overwrought way to say that I would carry their ancient technique into modern times—but when paired with the feelings I was exploring, it took on another meaning. The same was true with another section of the skill’s description; ‘your actions bear the marks of their memories’.

I’d initially taken both as metaphorical statements, but I was beginning to suspect they hinted at something altogether more real. Like ghostly hands pushing me forwards, I found new momentum imparted by each strike as I chained combinations of attacks and parries together. It was slight, and perhaps would have been missed by someone with my level of perception under normal circumstances, but I was intimately familiar with how my momentum shifted and changed as I moved, based on Break-Step and the practice with that skill to safely carry out the Ram’s Descent gambit.

With every new strike in combination, something gathered behind me. Once I’d noticed it, I began to focus more on the sensations, and further study had led to further revelation. I’d begun to hear a faint chanting with my strikes, a subtle glow sheathing my limbs and my weapons when practicing under the stars. Never in the dawn or the light of day, and not on a cloudy night either. But when I danced with my weapons under those same stars that would have watched the Hasta live their lives, I saw the ghostly light guiding my steps and pushing me onwards to the next.

The effect had grown with each new level, and Jorge had truly started to get excited. I was curious and a little intrigued of course, but it was just another skill for me at that point. It wasn’t until I’d hit level 4 that things had changed.

One night, while restless and unable to sleep, I’d left the safety of our cave and trudged into a snowfield beyond the light of the dying fire. There, with a crown of mountains surrounding me and the twinkling stars watching from above, I’d moved through the now familiar motions, focusing on that enigmatic feeling that approached and fled seemingly at random as I trained. I lost myself to the movements, and age passed under the stars as I made a dance of violent steps.

And then I’d felt the first flicker of memory stir. Just a flash, but it had jolted me from my rhythm. It had been so brief I’d almost missed it, but for a fleeting moment I’d remembered nursing my child at my breast in a yurt on the open plains of the Great Emerald Ocean. Grasses had waved with the wind, the moonlight trickling down to kiss the forehead of my baby swaddled to my chest, and I’d looked out into the night with hope in my heart, waiting for my love to return from the war in the north.

It had hit me so hard, that phantom memory. Not one of my own, certainly. Not Lamb’s, and not even one belonging to the man I’d once been—the one buried deeper, long thought gone until the Subakir had exposed that for a lie. Just buried by the system, is all. For now.

I’d danced once more, spear and shield in hand, and as the snow fell and my footsteps were obscured, I relived the life of that phantom woman in snatches of memory and flashes of insight. A short life, and a troubled one. But a life, nonetheless, deserving of remembrance.

When I had mentioned it later that morning to Jorge, he’d urged the others on ahead, assuring them we’d catch up later, and grilled me for hours. We’d discussed at length, formulated theories and experimented with different techniques until I could reliably gain flashes of memory by following the katas that Vanguard of the Lost provided.

This was no longer just a weapons skill to Jorge and I. Yes, it seemed like it could further enforce my strikes—and possibly my body itself—with extra speed and power when I chained subsequent strikes together in strange combinations. But more than that, this was a way to remember a lost people.

Jorge had unrolled a scroll, dipping quill to ink and recording feverishly all that I could recount from my new memories. The rituals of daily life, the taste and fragrance of their food, their words of worship and their language of love. Everything that made a people a people, unique and mundane all at once.

Of course, they weren’t my memories. I never felt like I was being subsumed by them; simply stories I got to experience in flashes of insight. But privately, in the sanctity of my own mind where Jorge couldn’t counsel me against it, I dreamed. If Vanguard of the Lost could grant me access to the memories of those long dead, memories that no longer existed in any material being on Tsanderos, why couldn’t it give me access to my own long-buried memories of a past life?

I knew they were still there —the Subakir had confirmed as much—and so all I needed was a key to unlock the way. This weapons skill was that key, and I had promised myself that I’d open the way. My recent brush with death had only hardened that view.

I’d get my memories back, system and gods be damned.
