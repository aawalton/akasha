While mana cores serve as reservoirs for a mage’s mana, mana rings allow that mana to be drawn out more efficiently. When they turn and spin, they draw out and refine the mana stored within a mage’s core. The more rings a mage had—and the faster they spun—the more mana that could be used and invoked for spells.

At any point, a mage could either reforge their core to ascend to the next tier or form that tier’s mana ring.

Most mages put off forming their mana rings since it was a tedious process. Meanwhile, ascending by reforging one’s core depended more on comprehension and ability and was generally easier, despite taking longer.
