Ezra felt his mouth go dry as Jormun asked the question. The thought of becoming an Adept was something he’d hoped for and now considered a necessity, but until now, he’d thought of it as something to do later.

“Do you think that I can?” Ezra asked in a voice just barely more than a whisper.

I just asked you, Jormun grunted. And you know I’m not going to answer that. Do you think and feel like you’re ready to expand your inner world? To continue and take a substantial step towards Sainthood?

“I feel like yes and no,” Ezra shrugged, unsure. “I know that I can’t fill up any more. That it hurts a little, and like the aura’s demanding that I use it. But I feel like an Adept knows a lot more than I do. It’s only been like two and a half months since I started to even feel aura, much less use it. I’m constantly looking to learn more, because everything I hear is new. You’ve said that the fundamentals are the most important thing, and I feel like I haven’t had enough time to develop them. Isn’t it just asking for trouble to push like that when I’m not ready?”

What do you feel like you need to learn to become an Adept?

As Jormun asked it, his voice was casual, but Ezra felt the weight to the words. Instead of just complaining that he didn’t think he was ready, he thought about it. He’d seen two Adepts in action, hadn’t he? Maybe Helen and Artemis weren’t outstandingly powerful Adepts, but they were competent enough to be trusted by the Duke to escort a valuable Pseudo-Knight. So, he could estimate that they were, at least, average people in that tier he was going to ascend to. What did they have that he didn’t?

They were trained, much better trained than he was. The clever use of water and fire affinity aura was something that the two times Ezra himself had used aura couldn’t be compared to. That said, he wasn’t really supposed to be using it at all until he’d ascended a tier, so that couldn’t be a requirement for someone to be qualified. More than just with aura, though, both women were more competent in fighting, their wielding of weapons more comfortable and confident than him, and by far. But so far as he could tell, actually becoming an Adept had nothing to do with weapons. Only at the Master tier did someone need to focus on some skill like that.

There were the dozens and hundreds of things that he just didn’t know, though. What was happening in the Dragon Wilds, military maneuvering, what kinds of aural affinities there were, and dozens more things about aura and meditation. Ezra just didn’t know those things, only how to pull aura into his inner world and, when desperate, maybe how to use that aura.

Their equipment, of course, didn’t have anything to do with their competence, and everything else he could think of didn’t seem to be at all impactful, so did he just need to learn more to be considered ready to be an Adept? The thought seemed stupid, with what he knew about the “path to Sainthood”. Then again, he knew next to nothing about that path, so…

Finally, after thinking in circles for a while, Ezra shook his head and met Jormun’s eyes. “I think I’m ready.”

Then this is how you can attempt to ascend a tier, Jormun explained without hesitation, only a quick nod. The fuller you can get your inner world the better. In the end, you’re forcefully expanding the vessel for the aura to be in, and having more of the material that’s stretching your inner world is good. Once you get to the point that it’s so full that you can’t take the pain any more, then you need to take control of the aura in your inner world and push it out without removing it from your inner world itself. Think of it like being trapped in a barrel—you’re pushing the inside out until the barrel breaks and spills everything inside out. Then, you gather the aura back and recompress it to where it was before, or even more compressed to make the coherent form it initially was before ascension.

“Seems pretty simple,” Ezra answered, “but what happens if I fail?”

It is simple, Jormun agreed. It’s the simplest real ascension from any tier. All it is is the expansion of your capacity and then regathering what you’ve already got. It’s still hard, even if it’s damn simple.

“And if I fail?” Ezra repeated.

If you fail drastically, you can kill yourself, though it’s only idiots who manage to do that to themselves. Jormun’s voice was supremely casual as he said it. More likely, either you just waste the aura you’ve collected or you hurt yourself and have to take it easy for a while to give your inner world to recover.

With that answer, he nodded and carefully evaluated if he felt he was truly as full as he could manage to be. It was certainly uncomfortable, but was there no way to get more in? After just a few seconds of consideration, Ezra knew that there was still plenty more he could stuff in. As such, he started to pull a less-commonly used affinity towards himself: water. With less experience, he struggled to manipulate and convert it, but he had time. Maybe at night he’d be confident that he’d refilled and stretched his inner world to its limit.

***

The day passed with nothing noteworthy to speak of, only every minute growing more uncomfortable as Ezra carefully strained to convert whatever affinity of aura he settled on that moment into wind and stuffed it into his inner world. Now that he was more aware of the limits of his inner world, he could feel just how much more limited he was in trying to meditate while walking as opposed to when he was sitting and focused. With this difference, it was no surprise that he struggled to meditate at all while in the middle of a fight. In fact, he wondered if it would always be impossible to do so.

Cord and Leah asked constant questions about sensing aura, and Ezra figured that he wasn’t really doing anything worthwhile with his mind wandering as he tried to meditate. Whatever questions they had about sensing aura in general were answered to the best of his ability, but it wasn’t long before he was giving them the same answers that Jormun and Chloe had begun relying on most of all: “It’s your path, you need to figure it out yourself.”

Around lunchtime, Ezra found Adam and exchanged a few quick words, no time for a real conversation as Adam seemed to be largely outcast from his squad of levies and had been forced to focus instead on carrying some materials for the rest of the squad that they’d neglected to carry themselves. Beyond that, the levies were being allowed to march much slower than the soldiers, their jobs done and now they only needed to get back home to be paid. There was enough food for them to keep from starving for at least three weeks, so they were to take their time. Even so, when Ezra demanded to help Adam with his burden, his friend turned him down.

“It’s jealousy,” the older barkeeper shrugged as he settled his pack better onto his shoulders. “You know it. But we’ll be home soon, and then I’ll be back at The Toenail and they won’t be able to do anything. After all, I’ll be in a respected establishment with my weapon and armor while they’ll be outside after having been forced to return the nicest pieces of equipment that they used, the wages paid too valuable to spend on some weapons that won’t be worth much to anyone other than Knifefingers or the rest of the Blades in the slums.”

“Just keep everyone safe for me,” Ezra asked as he was pulled away by other responsibilities.

“I’ll keep Tilly safe,” Adam answered with a smirk.

“I said everyone,” Ezra replied with a groan.

“But you meant her specifically. She was missing you, the last time I saw her,” Adam said, the teasing smile fading from his face. “If you can, it’d mean a whole lot to her if you came by and said something, something more than just my words that you’re still alive. The Madam too. She was worried about you.”

The thought of the severe woman pacing, worrying at her lip, or even saying that she was worried about someone drew a smile to Ezra’s face. He knew she was human, that she had feelings, but she was always so controlled that he couldn’t imagine it. Even so, he felt a faint glow of happiness in his chest at the thought that the people back home were still thinking of him.

“I’ll try,” Ezra said as Adam’s squad leader started shouting the levies into position. “I don’t know how long I’ll be in Villy, though, and might have to send a letter.”

“I hope it’s legible, then,” Adam laughed, clasping Ezra’s right hand in a tight grip. “I’ll be seeing you, I’m sure.”

“At some point, yeah,” Ezra answered as he stepped back.

Then, he was back with the soldiers, who set out to make time about twice as fast as the levies. The materials from the Dragonkin, supposedly, would spoil before too long, and those that had been meted out to the Counts Villy and Svana were being hauled to their lords as fast as the soldiers could manage. The Duke’s men had done much of the same, marching off as quick as they could manage towards the Duke’s lands. They were to the north, so Chloe, Basil, and Artemis were long gone.

Walking through the lands that they’d only just been marching through a few days before was a mindless task, and Ezra found himself getting lost in conversation much more than he’d expected. The discomfort in his inner world pushed him away from the mental effort of stuffing it further, instead spending the day listening to Boyd’s stupid stories about things he’d done before enlisting. Fortunately, the march passed quickly by, with sunset coming as they set up camp.

“—The unlucky part is that I ended up coming out just as her mother was tossing the chamber pot out the window,” Boyd laughed. “Luckily, it was summer, so I didn’t have to freeze as I washed the piss and shit out of my clothes.”

“I’d say it wasn’t unlucky,” Leah said, her tone drier than the jerky they’d been chewing on, “but that she knew what you were doing there.”

“Well, if that’s the case, then I suppose she got her revenge,” Boyd replied, not at all worried about it. “But Cassandra was trouble for me many more reasons than that.”

“I’m done hearing about her,” Cord shook his head. “She’s in all your favorite stories, and that ‘divinely fated lovers’ idea you’ve got is going to fail to go anywhere. I’m sure she’s already found someone new.”

“Not for long, not if I have anything to say about it,” Boyd grunted. “Now that I’m a soldier, a member of the Count’s elite squad, I could walk up to whoever dares to try to take my woman and force him to give up.”

“That kinda shit is the reason why you two are always on and off again,” Cord said, raising his voice. At that, the brothers devolved into an argument as Ezra and Leah arrived at the campsite, leaving  them behind.

“They’re morons,” Leah said as they set about preparing their tent.

“Boyd is,” Ezra half agreed, “but Cord’s just dealing with his idiot brother.”

Leah shrugged as they continued working, the brothers appearing several minutes later. The minutes passed quickly, and it wasn’t long before the tent was ready and the recruits were eating their dinner. After scarfing his own portion down as quickly as he could manage, Ezra could finally fill his inner world to bursting. In truth, it didn’t take him long, and when he asked Georg to give him a quiet place alone and away from any observers, he got it without any questions.

“Inside this tent’ll be out of view,” the wagonbound lieutenant said. “I can’t give you anything outside of the perimeter for security reasons, but I’m happy to do whatever you want.”

After thanking his superior for the help, Ezra found his way into the tent and, though the pressure from his inner world was pressing on him, demanding he do something, he had to ask something entirely unrelated.

“Why is he treating me like that?” he looked at the floating ghost as he asked it. “It’s weird.”

You’re his savior. He’s always going to treat you this way, and it’s a matter of weeks at most before you reach a peak he’s never been able to, Jormun shrugged. You appeared in his life, showed what genius was, and will shortly disappear, whether to serve the Duke or otherwise. Villy doesn’t contain enough to hold you for long, now that you’ve found your wings. You’ll fly free, wherever you decide your path takes you.

“I don’t like it,” Ezra grumbled. “It’s not right.”

Maybe not, but it will happen more and more. When you become a Pseudo-Knight, people will look at you the way you looked at Chloe when you met her. If you become a Knight, Master, or Saint? You’ll be worshipped. Get used to it, because that’s an inevitable side effect of the path you’ve chosen to walk.

With a deep breath, Ezra shoved that thought to the side, and instead focused on his inner world, pulling in as much more aura as he could manage, and then beginning the trial of trying to overcome the boundary to becoming an Adept.
