“Alrighty. Fractal, Ten,” I said, switching the gloss from text to voice. My thumb hovered a moment over the interface before committing—I preferred voice when I needed clarity, and this definitely qualified. “I know you’re both still scouting a bit ahead, but how’s the path looking for our carts?”

There was a brief crackle of static, like the forest itself exhaled into the channel, before Ten’s voice cut through with a dramatic groan.

“Terrible. Honestly terrible. Completely, profoundly terrible.” She punctuated it with an audible sigh loud enough that even Cordelia snorted. “The forest gets really, really thick and it doesn’t look like it’s letting up anytime soon. Also, everything here looks like it wants to either sting me, bite me, or propose marriage. What are we even carrying that needs a cart? Why not just backpack it in?”

“Classified.” I answered without hesitation.

The immediate chorus of laughter from my team was almost insulting. V made a little “ooooooh, spooky” noise under his breath. Cordelia hid her smirk behind her sleeve, but very poorly. Fallias outright giggled, her tail flicking in amusement.

Was it actually classified? No. Not at all. It was in the briefing packet. The packet Ten was supposed to read. The packet that, if she weren’t Ten, would have been skimmed at the bare minimum. Instead, she had probably used it as a fan, a coaster, or perhaps a tool to scrape jam off a table.

Could I force her to read it via the seal mark? Yes. Absolutely. One mental flick and she’d memorize the documents front to back.

Did I want to force her?

…Gods no.

The day I started using my seal to bully one of my own into doing basic homework was the day I deserved to be punted into a volcano by Dominus Demeterra herself.

Still. The laughter stung.

“We’re carrying a special shipment,” I said more diplomatically. “A kind of honey that promotes meridian growth. The plan is to deliver it to the nearest settlement we can reach after mapping the interior, then establish trade circuits.”

“Ah.” Ten paused. “So definitely classified.”

“It’s not—” I pinched the bridge of my nose. “Read. The. Packet.”

“No can do, chief. I used it to swat away a moth the size of a melon. I think it achieved sentience halfway through the struggle.”

Fallias choked on a laugh. Even Cordelia cracked a smile. “Gods,” she muttered, “why are we like this?”

“We’re like this because he,” V said, pointing at me, “invited Two Hitchhikers and a Menace to be part of this journey.”

“Hey!” I snapped. “Fractal is not a menace.”

“I meant Ten,” he said.

“Oh. Yeah. Fair.”

Ten’s voice returned, cheerfully unaware that she had been the subject of an entire character assassination. “Anyway, thick forest, no breaks, no paths. We’re hacking through brush at this point. I’m making a path mark every ten paces, but the plants keep… moving?”

“Moving?” I repeated.

“Like politely shifting back in place whenever we try to cut them,” Fractal chimed in quietly over the line. “Not aggressive. Just… stubborn.”

Great. A sentient forest. Or semi-sentient. Or magically reactive. Or fate-twisted. Or something else that would inevitably complicate our lives.

Cordelia’s expression tightened the way it did when she was mentally rearranging her danger list from “manageable” to “annoying.”

“It sounds like the realm itself has an instinct to preserve its growth,” she murmured. “Mana signatures that strong, in a realm with an active miasma cycle… it’s not impossible the flora is partially animated.”

“Or fully,” V added helpfully. “Maybe the trees can sing. Or tap dance.”

“Please stop helping,” I said.

Fallias nudged me with her shoulder. “Are you stressed?”

“Yes,” I said immediately, then tried to salvage my dignity. “No. Maybe. It’s fine. It’s normal mission stuff.”

“You are sweating.”

“It’s humid.”

“You always sweat when you’re trying to hide stress.”

“That’s a bold accusation.”

“And accurate,” Cordelia said.

I inhaled, trying to keep both my lungs and ego steady. “Look. The mission is simple. Step one: find non-hostile inhabitants. Step two: establish a supply line. Step three: protect said supply line. Step four: if everything in the realm is hostile—”

“Claim and subjugate,” V finished, tone flat. “You know, the fun part.”

“It’s not fun,” I corrected.

“It’s a little fun,” he countered.

Cordelia shook her head. “Violence is only fun for you because you cope by being dysfunctional.”

“I cope by being delightful.”

Fallias rolled her eyes affectionately. “You cope by talking nonstop.”

“Correct. I talk so you don’t die.”

I held up a hand. “Everyone focus. Ten, Fractal—how far out are you?”

“About three-fifths of a mile,” Fractal answered. “We’re approaching a ridge. Ten thinks she sees a light source ahead.”

“Not a natural one,” Ten added. “It’s too uniform. Might be a settlement. Or a trap. Or a really, really polite forest spirit offering us tea.”

“If the forest offers tea,” V said, “don’t drink it.”

“If the forest offers tea,” Cordelia said, “I’m drinking it.”

“Please,” I begged them all, “don’t drink anything the forest offers you.”

Fallias placed a gentle hand on my arm, grounding me. “We’ll be fine,” she said in a low voice. “We’re prepared. And we trust you.”

That last part always hit harder than I wanted it to.

“The feeling of being watched hasn’t gone away,” Cordelia added, scanning the shimmering foliage around us. “But it doesn’t feel hostile. Just observant.”

“Curious,” Fractal said.

“Hungry?” V suggested.

“STOP,” I said immediately.

“I mean emotionally hungry,” V clarified. “Like craving attention.”

“That’s not better.”

Ten cut in again, her tone suddenly sharp. “Alexander. We see movement.”

Not hostile.

Not friendly.

Not anything I could categorize yet.

But definitely watching us.

My hand drifted to Lumivis’s resting crystal.

“Group,” I said, voice turning steady, “we move on my mark.”

It didn’t take long for the humor to die off.

A silence settled over us—not the comfortable kind, not the wary kind either, but something hollow. Something wrong.

I noticed it first as a faint itch at the back of my mind, like a missing puzzle piece I hadn’t realized was gone.

No birds. No chittering. No rustling of underbrush.

Just the soft hum of bioluminescent life and the constant slow throb of this forest’s unnatural pulse.

I slowed my steps. The others followed suit.

“…Hey,” V murmured, tail flicking once behind him. “Has anyone else noticed that we haven’t seen any fauna? None. Zero. Not even insects.”

Cordelia stopped walking. Her brows furrowed, eyes tracking slowly across the glowing foliage as if trying to force it to reveal its secrets.

“Now that you say it…” she whispered. “You’re right. No fauna. Not even the sound of wings. Even the glowing insects aren’t moving—just floating.”

I turned toward one of the little motes drifting lazily beside a giant moss-covered root. It was bright, a soft warm amber glow, pulsing gently like a lantern firefly. But it didn’t move. Not of its own accord, anyway. It simply hovered, as though suspended by invisible strings.

Fallias clicked his tongue. “Hell, the slimes we fought at the other Gate were more lively than this. What kind of realm doesn’t even have—anything?”

“Mmm.” I crouched, brushing aside a cluster of luminescent fern fronds. The ground was perfectly still. Not even disturbed soil. No tunnels. No tracks. Not even droppings.

“This isn’t natural,” I muttered. “Not by any stretch.”

“Tch.” V tapped the side of his Gloss. “Fractal? Ten? You see anything alive up there? Anything moving?”

Static for a moment. Then Fractal’s soft, chiming voice:

“There is… movement. But not fauna.”

A cold line traced down my spine.

“Define ‘not fauna.’” I asked.

“Structures that appear to breathe,” Fractal answered. “And vines that move without wind. The trees shift their branches as if adjusting position. The light sources drift… as if aware.”

Cordelia stiffened. “So the environment is alive. Or responsive.”

“Could be sentient,” Fallias added. “Some Otherrealms do that—everything acting under a single Dominus. But still. No fauna? Not even predators? No scavengers?”

V grimaced. “Yeah, even a Dominus-run realm should have something that walks, flies, or crawls.”

I exhaled slowly. The air felt thicker now, clinging to my skin. The humidity had been oppressive before, but now it felt like the forest itself was pressing close, listening.

“It’s watching us,” I murmured.

All three pairs of eyes snapped to me.

“That’s what it feels like.” I continued. “Not just that we’re being observed—more like the entire damn forest has its attention on us. Like a thousand eyes we can’t see.”

Cordelia rubbed her arms, as if warding off a chill despite the sweltering heat.

“V, Fallias,” she said quietly. “Have either of you sensed anything? Even subtle movement? Echoed mana? Traces? Anything?”

Fallias shook his head. “Nothing. No hostility either… which might be worse.”

“It’s like everything is hiding.” V muttered. “Or everything that would normally count as fauna has already been dealt with.”

“Dealt with by what?” Cordelia asked.

He gestured around us with both hands. “I’unno, Tea-Pot. You just said it! Maybe the damn trees!”

I stood again, brushing dirt from my gloves.

“Fractal,” I said, “Can you fly upward again and try the canopy? Ten, stick with her. Don’t push deeper—just vertical movement only.”

“Affirmed.” Fractal chimed.

“Right.” Ten groaned. “Let’s see if the sun exists in this place…”

Their connection dimmed again.

Fallias shifted his grip on his mace, eyes sharp. “Even if the environment here is alive, why no animals? Usually worlds that can support this kind of growth density have huge ecosystems.”

“And apex predators.” V added. “Big ones.”

Cordelia nodded. “Right now it feels like… everything that should be here is missing. As if purposefully removed.”

A thought took root in my mind.

Purposely removed. Missing. A realm of only flora.

“…What if this realm has no fauna at all?” I asked.

“Impossible,” Fallias countered. “No natural realm survives without some cycle of consumption and growth.”

Cordelia’s eyes narrowed. “Unless the flora consumes.”

V grimaced. “Yeah, that tracks. Maybe the insects glow because they’re lures. Or… worse.”

I lifted one of the floating motes gently between two fingers. It didn’t react. It didn’t fight. It didn’t skitter.

It wasn’t even an insect. It was… a bulb. A tiny floating seed pod, glowing softly, almost warm to the touch.

When I released it, it drifted right back into its previous place. As if attached by a magnetic tether.

“Yeah,” I muttered, “This isn’t a bug. And it sure as hell isn’t alive like fauna.”

Cordelia crouched beside me, picking up another. “These are… spores. Decorative ones. Designed to mimic fireflies.”

“For what purpose?” Fallias asked.

“To lure fauna,” she answered. “But if there’s no fauna…”

V swallowed. “Maybe there was.”

A chill settled over our group.

I didn’t voice what we were all thinking, but the thought was obvious:

Something ate them. All of them.

Before I could speak again, Fractal’s voice came through—tight, uneasy.

“Alex… we reached the upper canopy.”

“What do you see?” I asked, already bracing myself.

Another pause.

Then Ten’s voice, strained:

“There is no sky.”

Fallias jerked. “What?”

Cordelia stood so fast the spores scattered from her hands. “Explain.”

“The canopy isn’t blocking the sky,” Ten said. “The ‘sky’ is the canopy. It keeps going. Up and up. Like the forest never ends. No atmosphere. No horizon. Just… layers.”

Fractal added softly, “This place is not a surface realm. It may not even be three-dimensional in the way we understand.”

I exchanged a slow look with Cordelia.

“An infinite growth construct,” she whispered. “A realm built only for expansion of flora. No fauna needed. No sky needed. No ecosystem cycles. Just… growth.”

V groaned. “So what—you’re saying we walked into a plant’s dream?”

Fallias exhaled sharply. “Or a prison.”

I squared my shoulders.

“Alright.” I said. “We need to establish a path for the carts anyway. But if this entire realm is built to grow and only grow, then we’re inside an active, consuming system. And we need to treat it like we’re in the belly of something that doesn’t know we’re here yet.”

Cordelia nodded firmly. “Then we move together. No splitting up further than we already have.”

V snorted. “Absolutely fine by me. I don’t want to get eaten by a fern.”

Fallias tapped his mace on his shoulder. “Still haven’t seen anything move besides the plants. That’s the bad part. Because I’d rather fight ten wolves than one smart vine.”

“Let’s regroup with Fractal and Ten,” I said. “Then we figure out the safest route we can force for the carts.”

I turned, giving the forest one last slow look. Everything glowed softly. Everything breathed softly. Everything waited.

And still— still—

Not a single creature stirred.
