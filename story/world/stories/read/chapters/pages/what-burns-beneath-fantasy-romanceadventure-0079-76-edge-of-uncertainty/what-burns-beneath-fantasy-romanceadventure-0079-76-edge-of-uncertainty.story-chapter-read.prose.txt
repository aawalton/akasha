I was at Royce’s side in a heartbeat. “Are you alright?”

He spent a moment flexing and stretching his… everything. Moving his jaw, as though making sure it still worked. Curling and uncurling his fingers. Tentatively bracing both feet on the floor before standing with a heavy breath.

“Yeah.” He sounded unsure, then cleared his throat. “Salt. No, but… yeah. Are you?”

“Yes.” I looked between my blood-spattered gown and the stained bed, then offered a tremulous smile. “Well. Same as you. No, but… yes.”

Royce stared at my gown, too. The cascade of red down my bodice. “That wasn’t…” his throat bobbed as the question caught. He looked too frightened to fully voice it. Or… perhaps too frightened to bear the answer.

I shook my head. “That wasn’t him.”

“Right. That’s… good.” Blue eyes tore away from the crimson, fixing on me. “Do you think… was it… he… telling the truth? About the city being dangerous?”

I spent a long while searching the floorboards, tongue trapped between my teeth, before spreading my hands wide. “I have no idea. I don’t know why he’d lie.”

“To get you to go there alone? Maybe? Without friends, without protection? Make it harder for you to find help?” Sweat-slicked hands dragged through sandy hair. “Saints, just to scare you even more?”

‘I’ll return it to you buried in the skull of whoever had it.’ The blood drained from my face. Scare me, indeed.

And I didn’t think for a moment that he’d been lying. Nor worried about whatever human bastion of strength I’d planned to give the shard to.

A bitter laugh choked its way up my throat. “Royce, when I woke up he said that if I called for help he’d kill everyone in the inn. I… I don’t think he’s worried about me finding protection. At least, not more than it would just… inconvenience him.” I chewed my lower lip, eyes trailing to the sword fragment.

It lay on the floorboards, right where Velidan had stood before he disappeared. Just like the first night, he’d been holding it when he left… but it hadn’t gone with him. And now, lying on the wood, it looked… oddly dull. Lackluster, as though the color had been drained, and a tentative touch revealed that it felt cool.

That happened before. It was dull and cold in the dream, when I gave it back to him. But… not the first night. Not when he tried to take it from Fellbrook. Why?

I shook my head. That question, along with the hundred other ones bubbling up, would have to wait. “Royce, I know I keep asking you this, but… does this change anything? His warning, I mean. Saying you and Renner and Teela all need to run? Saying the city’s dangerous? Do you want to leave?”

My friend looked at me as though I’d grown a second head, and that was answer enough.

“Right. Let’s… I need to change. And clean up some of this mess.”

Royce winced. “Or just leave some extra coin before we go. I don’t there’s any saving that pillow. Saints, Brin, that’s a lot of blood.”

“At least most of it’s on me, and not the bed,” I muttered, twisting at my ruined gown.

He just paled. “Brin…”

“Will you go downstairs and get the others?” I thought about that for a moment, then amended, “Er, no. There’s probably other people down there. The innkeeper, at least. Don’t tell them anything just yet. Just… have them get ready and I’ll come meet you all outside.”

He nodded, giving one more ashen-faced glance towards the bed, before disappearing out the door.

I stripped off my gown, used its cleaner parts to dab the bed- while thanking my good fortune that the quilt was already a deep shade of burgundy- and repacked my belongings with poised efficiency. Just like before, the sight of Royce being so frightened was instilling me with a curious calm. As if, because he’d been so rattled, it was somehow my job to stay composed.

When my things were packed and the room was somewhat in order, I dressed in my storm-gray trousers and embroidered sapphire-blue tunic. Once my boots and cloak were reaffixed, only one thing remained.

My eyes trailed to the sword fragment. More questions brimmed; why had my rune worked? Had he known that’s what would happen? Had my mom known? If another piece of the sword was present, could I do the same thing again? If given enough pieces, could I reforge the entire blade?

And, more concerningly… had I tried the rune without Velidan present, what would have happened? Would I have fallen face-first onto the bed, helplessly choking on a throat-full of blood? Would it have killed me?

It didn’t, though. It didn’t kill me. It left me with an even bigger weapon. Big enough to go right in his heart.

Part of me hesitated at the thought. I wanted Velidan dead; for his cruelty, for his callous dismissal of human life, and for my freedom. But…

What if he’s actually telling the truth? What if other Fae are coming, and he truly wants to stop them?

And of course, the more frustrating thought on the heels of all that; why should I believe anything he says?

Saints, what do I do?

I was in no position to answer any of those questions. Not yet. But I closed my fingers around the shard and drew it close.

“Everything can die, right?” I murmured. My thumb brushed along one razor-sharp edge. Then I gasped, nearly dropping it.

For a moment, I could’ve sworn the metal grew hot.

It was, quite ironically, truly breathtaking scenery that served as the backdrop of my harrowing tale. Leafless maples and fragrant pines towered along our descent, the sun was unfettered by clouds, and the blue, sparkling expanse of Senna’s Gleam grew ever closer. Our path was lined in brittle ferns, curling strips of moss, and the dappling white of bold snowbells.

I relayed every word Velidan had spoken before producing the sunlit steel. It was beginning to feel warm again. The metal was hued like honey.

“One thing’s for sure,” I held the sword fragment aloft, letting it gleam beneath the sunlight. “I’m not worried about him killing me anymore. Well. Not for a few days.”

“Sure.” Renner’s voice dripped malice. “Waltzing into your bedroom while we’re all asleep down the hall is much better.”

Teela’s face was drawn. “Or coming to take the shard away and go kill stuff with it.” She thought for a moment. “Bad stuff, maybe. Other monsters. Ashes, I don’t know what to think about all this.”

I sighed. “That makes two of us.”

“Three,” Royce muttered from beside me.

“Four.” Dappled sunlight lit Renner’s skin in warm hues as he paused and turned, though the effect didn’t quite lesson his scowl. “But either he was lying, or he wasn’t. Either way, we’re still going to the Thalessians in the city.”

I stopped, too. “I’m not giving them the shard anymore.”

Copper eyes narrowed at me. “Because you believe he’s going to use it to kill monsters, or because you’re worried about them dying for it? They’re trained to kill Fae, ladyship. They live for it, or they die trying.”

“Didn’t you hear her story? Ashes, that… thing kept me trapped with one hand, while he was burning, and crawled inside my head. Made me do everything he wanted.” Royce’s tone was thick with anger, but his gaze was fixed on a nearby patch of moss. He looked sick. “How can someone be trained to fight that?”

The smaller man didn’t bat an eyelash. “You’d be surprised.”

I tucked the shard away and moved towards my shivering friend. I wrapped both arms around him in a quick embrace, but at his sudden stiffness I pulled back. “I don’t know if I believe him about planning to kill Fae, Renner. But I do believe him about the other part. About killing anybody I give the shard to.”

“Trying to kill them. What if they win? What if this is the solution?” He drew close, expression hard. That flat, brutal cold that made me want to turn away. “Salt, we have bait. We hand the shard over, tell them there’s a Fae coming for it, and maybe that solves the whole damn problem.”

“Or maybe he kills all of them,” I whispered.

“Maybe not. If there’s a whole troop in the city, that’s… ten paladins, give or take. Standing at the ready with weapons designed to kill him.

I was shaking my head. “I don’t think it’s that simple! Durst shot him right through the heart, and he was fine! I used one of your bolts, too. I took it to bed, into a dream, and I stabbed him with it.”

He hesitated. “And?”

“And he handed it back, then said I could keep stabbing him if I wanted! Saints, he said he’d hold still for me!”

He thought for a moment, grimacing, then threw both hands skyward and resumed stomping down the trail. “Well, maybe one of them can shove the shard in his throat. Or maybe a few swords at the same time will do a little more. This might be the answer.”

“Or it might end with even more death.” I said glumly, trailing after. “I’m sorry, but I’m not doing this. I won’t use human beings as bait.”

His shoulders stiffened. For a long while, the only sounds were the pleasant lapping of water against the lakeshore and boots tromping on dirt. Finally he snapped, “Fine. But we’re still going to tell them what he said about a ritual.”

Teela cocked her head to one side. Beneath the felt hat’s brim, everything but her mouth and pointy chin were cast in shadow. “And, uh, how are we gonna do that safely? Without getting Brin… y’know.” She winced and made a slicing motion across her neck.

Renner sighed. “We’ll just tell them he said it in Fellbrook. That he showed up, attacked the town, and gloated about how Sedgewick Vale was next. It’ll put people on their guard. Maybe the Thalessians can even root out what’s going on. If he was telling the truth.”

I nodded, in full agreement. “We can tell the Watch, too. Ashes, maybe we should tell everyone.”

“Not everyone,” the Fae-touched woman winced again. “I mean, the authorities, sure, but if you just start running down the streets shouting warnings there’ll be chaos. People will panic. Or,” she added after a moment’s thought, “You’ll be taken to a cleric. They might think your head’s messed up.”

“We’ll find out where the Order’s being housed when we reach the gates.” Renner nodded ahead of us, where the city walls were visible deeper in the valley. Then he stopped midstep, eyes fixed on the lake and hand lifting to shield from the sun. Several breaths passed, and he didn’t move.

The rest of us stopped, too. Barley gave a little whicker and stooped for a mouthful of brittle grass.

“Are you alright?” I stepped up beside Renner, following his gaze. There were several figures, most of them dressed in dark clothing and holding long rods or nets, clustered around the nearest bank. They all seemed to be gathered around a woman, though their voices weren’t legible from our distance.

The woman was, by far, the most notable figure. She stood tall and broad-shouldered, wearing a breastplate that gleamed beneath the morning sun. A faded white cloak fluttered behind her, and the sight of it made my breath catch. The cloth was emblazoned with an image I would know anywhere; a silver-threaded, black-leaved tree. Her blonde hair was tucked into a neat bun.

“She’s a paladin!” I gasped, turning to face Renner fully. “Do you know her?”

He was shaking his head, but not in denial. It seemed more like disbelief. One gloved hand lifted to rub his jaw. “I’ll be damned.”

I opened my mouth to press for more… and froze as the familiar sound of hissing metal came from close behind. Someone had drawn a sword.

“Yep,” came a smooth, masculine voice. “Sooner than you think.”
