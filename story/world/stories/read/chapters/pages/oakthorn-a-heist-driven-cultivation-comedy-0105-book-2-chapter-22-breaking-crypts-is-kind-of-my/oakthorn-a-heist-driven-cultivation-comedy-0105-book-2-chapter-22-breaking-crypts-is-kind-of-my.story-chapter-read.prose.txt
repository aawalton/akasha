“This can’t possibly be what the architect had in mind,” Cade muttered, his knuckles white as he clung to a crumbling ledge while noxious green vapor swirled six inches below his dangling feet.

Across the chamber, Nora balanced on a narrow stone outcropping, her muscles taut with strain as she braced herself against the ancient wall. The distance between them—barely fifteen feet—might as well have been a mile given the bubbling sea of caustic fog separating them.

“Well?” he called out, trying to ignore how the stone beneath his fingers crumbled a little more with each passing second. “Any brilliant paladin insights about deadly gas chambers with no apparent exit?”

“I missed that day of training,” Nora replied, shifting her weight as her foothold threatened to give way. “I was too busy learning how to look intimidating while carrying an unreasonably large sword.”

Despite their predicament, Cade barked out a surprised laugh. “So the Dreadcaller does have a sense of humor. Who knew it would take imminent dissolution to bring it out?”

“Imminent death has a way of clarifying priorities,” she replied, a rare smile ghosting across her lips before her expression tightened again as more stone crumbled beneath her boot. “Shit.”

The chamber had sealed itself behind them moments after they’d entered, the seemingly solid floor dissolving into the roiling toxic fog below. Only their battle-honed reflexes had saved them, both leaping for the minimal handholds that lined the otherwise smooth walls.

“I’m open to suggestions,” Nora called, her voice tight with controlled strain. “Preferably ones that don’t end with us as puddles.”

Cade’s gaze darted across the chamber, cataloging details with the practiced efficiency of someone used to finding escape routes in seemingly inescapable situations. His attention fixed on a recessed alcove across the room, just visible through breaks in the vapor. Inside sat what appeared to be a crystalline mechanism connected to the piping system.

The thief nodded toward what had caught his attention. “That looks like ventilation control, but it’s on your side of this delightful death bath.”

Nora twisted to look, her movement causing more stone to crumble. “Even if I could reach it, my arms aren’t quite ten feet long.” Her grimace spoke volumes. “Though right now I’d gladly trade an inch of height for each extra foot of reach.”

“Feeling inadequate, Dreadcaller?” Cade teased, then immediately regretted it as his own handhold creaked ominously. “Okay, levity and near-death experiences apparently don’t mix well.”

“Says the man who can’t pass a guard without making a joke about his sword size,” Nora countered, surprising him again.

“I have standards. I never mock a man’s sword unless it’s compensating for something.” The stone beneath his fingers cracked further. “Ah, poor timing. Got any magic that works on toxic mist?”

“Fear doesn’t exactly work on inanimate objects.” She paused, reconsidering. “Usually.”

The stone beneath Cade’s fingers cracked ominously. Time was running out, and with it, his patience for playing by the crypt’s rules.

“You know what?” he grunted, shifting his weight to a slightly more stable position. “I’m done letting this place dictate the terms.”

“What are you planning?” Nora’s tone carried equal parts concern and curiosity.

“Something stupid,” he admitted. “But it’s got a solid thirty percent chance of not killing us immediately.”

“Those are the best odds we’ve had all day,” she replied dryly.

Cade channeled his silver-gold energy in a concentrated burst toward a series of decorative sconces lining the far wall. Instead of his usual explosive force, he focused on precision—surgical strikes that sent the metal fixtures crashing into the roiling vapor below.

The reaction was immediate and violent. Where metal met mist, the fog swirled into vortices, creating momentary vacuums in the toxic cloud.

“Ha! Science!” Cade crowed triumphantly.

“That’s not science, that’s dumb luck,” Nora replied, though her eyes gleamed with new hope.

“I prefer to call it ‘educated desperation,’“ he shot back, targeting another sconce. “The vapor reacts to the metal—disrupts it somehow.”

Nora’s expression shifted from skepticism to calculation. “If I jump for that pillar, I might reach enough sconces to create a path.”

“And risk falling if you miss? Not happening.” Cade shook his head. “Let me clear more of them first.”

“Since when are you the cautious one?”

“Since I realized having you splattered across this chamber would seriously dampen my victory celebration later.”

Something flickered in her eyes—surprise, perhaps, or something warmer. “Assuming a lot there, Stormhollow.”

“Just providing motivation for our mutual survival,” he replied with a wink that belied the strain in his arms.

Working systematically, Cade destroyed every metal fixture within reach, each destruction creating another temporary clear spot in the deadly fog. As the last sconce fell, Nora suddenly closed her eyes, her expression shifting to intense concentration.

“What are you—” he began.

Before he could finish, tendrils of black energy erupted from her fingertips, lashing out not at any visible target but at the vapor itself. To Cade’s astonishment, the mist actually recoiled from her magic, curling away like a living thing confronted by a predator.

“You can scare gas?!” he asked incredulously, watching as her magic carved channels through the fog. It wouldn’t have been enough without his aid, but what he had started, she was finishing.

“Apparently,” she replied, sounding equally surprised. “Didn’t expect that to work.”

“And here I thought I had all the unpredictable magical talent in this relationship,” Cade remarked, then froze as he realized what he’d said. “Partnership. Alliance. Two-person escape team.”

A flicker of amusement crossed Nora’s face. “You know what they say about assumptions, Stormhollow?”

“I don’t know what you’re talking about,” he replied with exaggerated dignity. “I’m entirely focused on not becoming vapor food. Besides, I have no idea who ‘umption’ is anyway.”

“Umption?”

“Yeah,” Cade answered with a wave of his hand. “Make an ass out of of “u” and “umption.”

Despite the mist that could turn them into soup, Nora leveled a flat look in his direction.

“What?!” Cade demanded, feeling a little bit of heat enter his cheeks. “That was funny. I’m funny.”

“Keep telling yourself that.” She intensified her magic, carving a wider path through the death fog. “Now, if we time this right, we might just make it across between your explosions and my... whatever this is.”

“Mist intimidation?”

“Vapor coercion?”

“Gaseous manipulation?” Cade suggested, then winced. “No, that sounds wrong.”

“Jump on three?” she asked, ignoring his verbal flailing.

“Skip straight to three,” he countered. “One and two are overrated.”

Neither hesitated. They pushed off from their respective handholds simultaneously, leaping toward the temporary gap in the mist. Cade landed awkwardly, immediately firing another burst of energy to widen their safe zone. Nora touched down with frustrating grace beside him, her fear magic maintaining a protective bubble around them.

“That’s new,” she observed, studying the transformed vapor beneath their feet.

“Still figuring out the instruction manual,” Cade replied, flexing his cramped fingers. “Let’s move before my improvised walkway remembers it’s supposed to be deadly gas.”

They crossed quickly to the alcove Cade had spotted, where Nora examined the crystalline mechanism with practiced efficiency.

“It’s a puzzle lock,” she determined, tracing the intricate patterns etched into its surface. “These symbols need to be aligned in a specific sequence to deactivate the vapor system.”

Cade leaned closer, recognizing elements of the patterns. “These match star charts.”

“And they correspond to the constellations carved into those friezes we passed,” Nora added, her mind clearly racing through the implications. “It’s testing whether we were paying attention to the details throughout our journey.”

“Lovely. A deadly pop quiz,” Cade muttered, already working to recall the specific arrangements they’d encountered. “The pattern in the maze chamber had the constellation Draco at the center, with—”

“The Hunter’s Bow to the east and the Twin Serpents below,” Nora finished, her fingers already moving the crystal components to match the configuration.

For several tense moments, they worked in synchronized silence, each recalling different fragments of the patterns they’d observed throughout the crypt. Nora’s paladin training gave her a methodical precision, while Cade’s thief’s eye for detail filled in crucial gaps.

As the final piece slid into place, the mechanism hummed with awakening energy. The pipes overhead shuddered, reversing their flow. The toxic vapor began to recede, draining through newly revealed grates in the floor until the chamber stood empty and clear.

“We did it,” Cade said, surprised despite himself.

“Don’t sound so shocked,” Nora replied, the ghost of a smile touching her lips. “Some of us can solve problems without melting them first.”

“Where’s the fun in that?”

The chamber shuddered again, more violently this time. A section of wall slid upward, revealing a new passage leading deeper into the crypt’s bowels.

“Onward to certain doom?” Cade suggested, gesturing toward the opening with mock formality.

“After you. You’ve already demonstrated your talent for creating improvised bridges when necessary.” Nora tucked a stray strand of her black hair behind an ear and nodded with her head for Cade to lead. “Go on.”

He did.

They proceeded deeper, the architecture growing increasingly alien as they descended. The walls no longer resembled constructed surfaces but rather living tissue—pulsing subtly with each resonant beat of the crypt’s persistent heart-rhythm, which grew louder with every step downward.

“They’ve gotta be down here somewhere,” Cade said suddenly, voicing the concern that had been gnawing at him since their separation. “I mean, how big can an ancient crypt be? We’re bound to run into whatever this horrible place throws at them, right?”

“They’re capable,” Nora reminded him, though her own worry was evident in the tension around her eyes. “More than capable.”

“I know. But that doesn’t mean—” He broke off as they rounded a corner, coming face-to-face with their next challenge.

The passage terminated in a vast circular chamber dominated by a massive contraption that defied easy classification. The whole structure was part mechanical, part organic, and it resembled nothing so much as a colossal ribcage fashioned from bleached bone and ancient metal, each segment larger than a man. Suspended in the air within the skeletal structure pulsed a core of amber energy, radiating waves of heat that distorted the air around it.

And blocking their path to the only visible exit on the far side stood a behemoth that made the fire elementals they’d fled earlier seem like children’s toys. Twelve feet tall, its body constructed of interlocking bones fused into an impossible framework, topped with a cluster of skulls arranged in a sphere to provide omnidirectional awareness.

“Skeleton golem,” Nora identified grimly, her hand instinctively reaching for the massive broadsword. “Imbued with necromantic energy.”

“Of course,” Cade sighed. “Because we haven’t faced enough animated bones today.”

The golem hadn’t moved yet, its hollow eye sockets fixed on them with unnerving stillness. It stood perfectly centered in their path, positioned as a guardian rather than an aggressor.

“It’s waiting for something,” Nora observed quietly. “This isn’t an immediate attack response.”

Cade studied the chamber more carefully, noting details beyond the obvious threat. The floor featured concentric rings of inscribed symbols that surrounded both the golem and the pulsing core-structure behind it. The patterns matched those they’d seen throughout the crypt, but arranged differently—interlocking rather than sequential.

“It’s not just guarding the path,” he realized. “It’s part of the mechanism. Look at how those bone segments align with the floor patterns.”

Nora nodded slowly, her tactical mind clearly processing the implications. “So we need to... what? Defeat it? Redirect it? Disable it?”

“Maybe we need to complete the circuit,” he suggested.“These star charts form part of a larger whole.”

The moment he spoke, the golem stirred, bone joints creaking like ancient doors as it took a single thunderous step forward. All skull-faces swiveled in perfect unison to focus on Cade.

“I think you just got its attention,” Nora said dryly, shifting her stance to have her greatsword set protectively in front of them. The obsidian blade shimmered in the amber glow of the overhead orb.

“Good. Better than being ignored.” Cade stepped a little closer. “Hey, bone-brain! Mind telling us why your master was obsessed with stars?”

The golem advanced with shocking speed for something of its size, the massive frame moving with the fluid grace of a predator despite its seemingly ungainly construction. Cade barely had time to dive aside as it lunged, bone fingers sweeping through the space he’d occupied a heartbeat earlier.

“Split up!” he called to Nora, rolling to his feet on the opposite side of the chamber. “Keep it confused!”

They moved in perfect coordination, years of separate combat experience evident in how they maintained optimal spacing while presenting two targets for the golem to track. It pivoted between them, skull-cluster rotating constantly to monitor both threats simultaneously.

“The core!” Nora shouted, indicating the pulsing amber structure floating above them. “That’s what controls it!”

Cade saw what she meant—each time the golem moved, energy surged from the core in corresponding patterns, flowing through the floor inscriptions just like any other ward or array he’d studied.

“I think I know a way to disrupt the connection,” he called back, formulating a desperate plan. “Get it to face away from the core!”

While Nora darted forward to draw the golem’s attention with a feint toward its legs, Cade circled wide, positioning himself on the far side of the chamber. The moment the construct fully committed to pursuing Nora, he made his move.

“Over here, you oversized marionette!” he shouted, hurling a piece of debris at the golem’s back with perfect accuracy, using a dash of his wind magic to guide its path.

It spun with that same unnatural grace, immediately charging toward this new threat. Exactly as Cade had hoped—and feared. He stood his ground until the last possible moment, pressure building in his right hand.

Just as the golem reached him, bone hands extending to crush, Cade dropped to one knee and slammed his fist into the depression in the floor he’d spotted during his circling maneuver. He didn’t recognize this style of runework in the slightest, but certain patterns were fundamental to all enchantments.

Everything—everything—had an on-and-off switch.

The effect was immediate and dramatic. Energy surged through the floor patterns, flowing into the disc and then rebounding toward the golem in a blinding flash of amber light. The construct froze mid-lunge, its entire framework vibrating as conflicting commands coursed through its necromantic animation.

“Nora!” Cade shouted, the array’s magic burning against his skin as he forced the magic within to a halt. “Whatever you’re going to do, do it now!”

She didn’t hesitate.

With a fluid motion that defied her usual reliance on brute strength, Nora unleashed a concentrated wave of fear magic directly at the golem’s skull-cluster. Black tendrils of energy wrapped around the bone surfaces, seeping into eye sockets and sutures.

The massive construct actually recoiled, skull-faces emitting a high-pitched keening that set teeth on edge.

“You can scare bones?” Cade called incredulously, struggling to maintain his grip on the increasingly hot disc. “What’s next? Terrifying inanimate objects into submission? Making rocks cry?”

“Jealous?” Nora shot back, pressing her advantage with another wave of black energy. “Not everyone needs to blow things up to feel accomplished.”

“I have a sophisticated relationship with destruction!” Cade protested, wincing as the disc scorched his fingers. “It’s just that sometimes the most elegant solution is making something not exist anymore.”

“Like your survival instinct?”

“Says the woman shooting black fear-tendrils at a walking bone collection!” He released another burst of silver-gold energy at the golem’s knee joint, causing it to buckle momentarily. “Also, can we discuss later how terrifying it is that you can literally scare the non-existent pants off something without a nervous system?”

“Maybe it’s not the bones I’m scaring,” Nora replied, her eyes gleaming with exertion and something like triumph. “Maybe it’s whatever’s animating them.”

For just an instant, Cade spotted a flash of crimson in his own silver-gold magic—there and gone so quickly he might have imagined it. But the effect was noticeable—his destruction strike hit with significantly more force than usual, shattering the golem’s entire left leg.

“Okay, that was new,” he muttered to himself, then louder: “Going for the big finish! Aim for its ugly faces!”

In perfect synchronization—as if they’d been fighting together for years rather than days—they launched a combined assault. Cade channeled everything he had into a concentrated blast aimed directly at the skull-cluster, while Nora wrapped the entire construct in a cocoon of fear energy that seemed to constrict like a living thing.

With a sound like a thousand bones snapping simultaneously, the golem collapsed, its animating energy dispersed by their combined assault. The amber core flickered and dimmed, the conflict between their magic and its power apparently too much for its ancient systems to handle.

Silence fell across the chamber, broken only by their ragged breathing and the omnipresent heartbeat rhythm of the crypt itself—now noticeably faster, more urgent.

“Well,” Cade managed, removing his fist from the indent and checking over his blistered fingers. “That was invigorating. Like a spa day, if the spa specialized in fear therapy and bone arrangements.”

“Are your hands okay?” Nora asked, surprising him with her concern as she approached.

“They’ll heal,” he replied with forced nonchalance, though the burns throbbed painfully. “Just another scar to impress gullible tavern patrons with.”

She took his hands without asking, examining the burns with a critical eye. “These need attention.”

“I’m touched by your concern, truly, but unless you’ve been hiding healing magic along with your bone-terrifying abilities—”

“I’m not concerned,” she interrupted, tearing a strip of cloth from her undershirt. “I just don’t want to have to carry you when your hands are too damaged to climb whatever nightmare this place throws at us next.”

As she wrapped his burns with surprising gentleness, Cade found himself studying her face—the focused intensity in her eyes, the slight furrow between her brows, the way she tucked a loose strand of hair behind her ear with an impatient gesture.

“What?” she asked without looking up, somehow sensing his scrutiny.

“Nothing,” he replied, too quickly. “Just wondering if this crypt has a gift shop. I’d like a souvenir to remember all the quality near-death experiences.”

Her eyes flicked up to his, unreadable for a moment before returning to her task. “There. Try not to get these wet or, ideally, burned again.”

“No promises. Occupational hazard.”

“Being burned is an occupational hazard for thieves?”

“Being reckless is,” he admitted with a half-smile. “It’s in the guild handbook, right after ‘how to look innocent when caught red-handed’ and before ‘witty one-liners for dramatic escapes.’“

The ghost of a smile touched her lips. “You wrote that handbook, didn’t you?”

“Wrote it? I lived it, paladin.”

They stepped into the chosen passage side by side, shoulders nearly touching in the narrow space. The moment both crossed the threshold, the obsidian walls rippled like disturbed water—and dissolved completely, revealing that both paths had been illusions.

Before them stretched a single corridor constructed of the same flowing stone they’d encountered when first entering the crypt’s depths. The walls pulsed visibly now, the structure’s heartbeat rhythm growing steadily faster as they ventured deeper.

“We’re getting closer to something,” Nora observed, her senses clearly alerting her to changes beyond the visible. “The energy concentration is increasing exponentially.”

“Let’s hope it’s something pleasant, like an exit,” Cade replied without much optimism. “Or at least our missing friends. Gods, I hope they haven’t strangled each other.”
