After the meeting was summarily ended and everyone left, Samantha was surprised by a quest completion.

The quest “Pantheon: A New Era” is complete. +500 FE added to the Pantheon. 2 exp (flat) gained in [God/Goddess/Demigod] for all Pantheon members (retroactive for new joiners). Pantheon Level 5 gained. Domain #3 available. [Holy Proclamation] gained for the Pantheon consensus.

Experience cannot be gained in [Goddess]. Allocate 2 exp, fixed without modifiers, to a sub-class of choice.

New Quest unlocked: “Pantheon: Championing Priorities” — Establish 777 Devouts, have 12 members gain Advanced Classes of at least 3 varieties, and have 7 Pantheon-related quests completed, giving at least 7 base exp.

● Rewards: +2000 FE (Pantheon), +2 exp in [God/Goddess/Demigod] for all Pantheon members, Pantheon Level 6 (Champion’s Blessing).

When Samantha checked what did it, apparently the now Fanatical [Exorcist], Galynth, had hunted down some elusive, murderous spirit terrorizing their churches specifically, which satisfied the final part of the quest for a Pantheon-related big quest completion. That was great! She hadn’t been sure it would ever happen, because so many things had failed to qualify.

Her realm was so big now that these sorts of things were simply inevitable with time. Unfortunately, she didn’t have it — very little time at all — so the next quest was dubious to be completed. It was a long, long quest line, and she hadn’t even unlocked half of what else a Pantheon could do. It was built on timeframe, she was sure. The minotaurs had maxed theirs out over perhaps thousands of years.

While she might’ve become as personally powerful as someone spending centuries, perhaps aeons, becoming stronger, but in a far shorter timeframe, some things were just not feasible.

In any case, she personally thanked and congratulated Galynth, now practically frozen solid, for receiving direct attention from her, so the conversation was not lengthy.

Actually, come to think, he’s always been like that!

Samantha actually felt a bit bad, because she probably could’ve prevented that by being closer to him over their time. But so life went — she accrued her regrets.

On the bright side, she had a few more gains. The measly couple of experience points, she dumped into [Magineer], annoyed about the fact she couldn’t multiply her first exp gain since coming back. [Holy Proclamation] she already knew about and had gained for her core religion, so no mysteries there. The biggest thing was the new Pantheon Domain, which would govern access for divine magic.

The options were [Nature], [War], [Life], [Light], [Trickery], [Peace], [Destruction], [Earth], [Water], or [Purity].

While [Earth] was a strong consideration going forward with a potential siege, they’d already determined the Dominion had gone all-out to neutralize this vector again. Collectively, at least — it was unlikely they could stop Six Moons Falling from doing what she wanted, whatever their wards. At best, they’d probably slow her down. Meanwhile, if there was something to break or blow up, Samantha was not going to be denied. That included walls, if they were stupid enough to rely on them.

Ultimately, she went with [Purity] to help out the numerous healer types that filled out the Pantheon ranks compared to her primary religion. For her own people, it would probably combine well with [Light], [Pneuma], and [Magic], too.

After selecting it, she was thrilled to notice the huge gain of a lower-level access to basic [Entropy] cleansing in both the pure Pantheon and an even stronger combination access to her priests. This was only for the lower-level varieties, but the Dominion would likely be heavy on them as a collective whole. She’d just heavily mitigated that advantage. Healers were overall already rejoicing from the news of these new options.

As the cherry on top, she’d kind of rewarded Galynth with a relevant Domain for his diligence, which had allowed the selection in the first place.

Just as Samantha was moving on to the next thing, Ash was suddenly in front of her, handing her a thin booklet or manuscript. With a hesitant smile, the Daughter said, “While you were away, and some aspects even before then, I took the liberty of looking into a certain puzzling matter. There are ways, even without direct knowledge.”

Brows furrowed curiously, Samantha began flipping through the booklet, and saw schematics of a tower, as well as illustrations, and a plethora of notes. It took a moment for Samantha to realize what it must be. “The auxiliary tower?”

“Yes! I brought in architects, engineers, geometers, investigators, and others of unusual specialties, to attempt to physically map what the auxiliary tower must’ve looked like. After all, it was once here. We have the vastness of the examples that are the surrounding building structures, most prominently the main tower itself. Furthermore, we have foundations! We knew exactly where it was, and so we could excavate the area to understand further what it might look like. The depth of detail gained was surprising and almost staggering. We made numerous determinations that resulted in the designs you see, and we’re confident we got it, as you would say, well within the ballpark!”

“Holy shit!” Samantha exclaimed, laughing briefly in appreciation. “This is incredible, Ash! I’m about ready to slap my forehead that I didn’t think of doing that myself. I’m the Earthgirl, after all.”

Ash beamed in pleasure, rocking up on her toes in nerdy excitement. If she had glasses, she would’ve pushed them up her nose. “I’m glad you think so, Mother. It was quite a project. And I should say, you were the inspiration. Only your memories and tales of Earth, and all that was accomplished there, without any magic or System, motivated me to find alternative solutions. Naturally, it also helps that you have been wooing and drawing these people to Geirkos and Itha Ranon as it is, particularly with all the funding into grants for projects.”

“The stores of wealth have to go elsewhere than just war, after all,” Samantha muttered absently, as she was still flipping through and looking at the drawings. They definitely showed something that fit seamlessly in with the main tower, like a little copy of it. The skeleton and dimensions were likely spot on. She didn’t voice it, but despite this, none of it was quite right. She couldn’t put her finger on why. “Thank you for this, though, Ash. I can use this. I will.”

Ash, still beaming, bowed her head. “Of course, Mother. If you need anything further, let me know. Many of these specialists are still on retainer. I promise they’ll be kept busy, but they can be diverted if you need them.”

“Fortunately, I can build myself now.” As effectively as anyone on the planet, actually, only differing in the style used.

“Ah! The new past life — of course! That changes things, doesn’t it? You may be able to complete it sooner than later.”

“Indeed. I believe I should, yes.”

“I look forward to seeing it! Oh, I should go, though. I have to prepare to help with a ritual. I might be late at this point. Bye, Mother!” She quickly ran off.

Samantha muttered a response, still lost in the visuals dancing in her head, of what the auxiliary tower should look like. It was like chasing down a dream, but the chase was in her blood, and she had every reason to get the damn thing built as soon as possible.

The auxiliary tower was made to store a ritual spell for spontaneous use later. With the new abilities of her D.I.V.A. crown, that spell could be unleashed at her location once she triggered the once-a-day function. That was a powerful resource to add to the battle. They had many layers to unleash for it, as usual, but there could never be too many. Extra backup power was always welcome and could end up being the difference between winning and losing. Even if not so extreme, it could save lives, and that was sacrosanct.

In puzzling over it, a realization dawned: she had someone to consult, too.

It’s high time to do it, too.

Samantha made for a window and flew outside the tower, wrapping around to get to the ruined foundations where the auxiliary tower once stood. Indeed, she saw signs of cleaning, excavation, and measurements made, markings all over the surface of the rough stone. Nonetheless, there weren’t any toolboxes lying around or anything like that. The project had been finished or paused.

Landing softly on the stone, Samantha activated {Mirroring Duplicates: Strange Phantasm -1], summoning some facsimile of the heroic and admirable woman she’d vicariously lived through the life of.

A giant over ten feet tall appeared, built of rippling muscles, wearing golden bronze-age style armor, and wielding a club vaguely like a giant metal baseball bat. Her skin was blue-gray and vaguely ethereal, like a sky teasing at a storm, and her hair was like wisps of clouds flowing upward as if to escape. Her eyes were glowing blue as she turned to Samantha and smiled, leaning on her club with two hands.

“Successor,” she said in greeting, her voice a rumble with a sharper note wedged inside, distinctly feminine. “You have more than battle on your mind today, I gather.”
