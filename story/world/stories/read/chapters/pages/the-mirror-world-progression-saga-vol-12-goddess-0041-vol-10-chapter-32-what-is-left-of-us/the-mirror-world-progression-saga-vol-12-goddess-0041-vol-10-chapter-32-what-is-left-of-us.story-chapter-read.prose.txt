The rehearsals of the rune-marring ensued over the course of hours. Just syncing up in the strange, spiritual way necessary was quite the challenge. It was work, that was for sure. At least the frustration quickly eliminated the lecherous frames of mind of Samantha’s companions for this endeavor.

Samantha had to ‘touch’ the specific flexible contours on the Bridge of Edges repeatedly for Azure to gradually feel what she was doing. Back and forth, back and forth, helping Azure to reach out and take hold of the hand she lent on the other side. Meanwhile, Bast had to get a sense for his own ‘muscle’ within the organ, like swishing liquid around held in the mouth. Only it was destruction pneuma, not liquid, and the whole analogy was thin at best. So, figuring out how to make that kind of ‘pressure’ was an experiment in the extreme. To this end, every time Samantha did her pokey-pokey, she did a squeeze-squeeze, too, hoping Bast would feel it.

Real technical stuff, in other words.

No spell could help them, here. Everything about the subject was utilizing the intrinsic fabric of reality — or something like that. Foundational pillars using an unclear interface. Samantha could avoid breaking anything by nature of the malleability of what Bast’s entity had conspired. It was designed to respond to shaping; they just had to learn how to work with new hands.

This sort of power was certainly, in some way, quite illegal to the System and its ‘fair game design.’ But the operation was beyond it, or underneath it, in some way. Back door access. Wherever and whenever it branched into actual reality, it then had to take on a legal effect. This is essentially what they’d do: unleash something that was designed from within the System, then taken away, transmuted, and enhanced, right back into it, along existing lines of access.

It would thus be far easier to arrange along the axis of the Transition Points system, which had apparently existed in some form for perhaps tens of thousands of years, depending on exactly when the Consolidator was around.

Interestingly, the weapon might not be very usable in the Capital, even if they wanted to. In addition to the Transition Point soft limitation, Samantha was a bit worried that the pneuma was getting more unstable. That there was a runaway reaction going on, and thus they had to dump the shit soon or be in serious trouble.

Over time, copious theory-crafting, and extensive explanation and coaching by Samantha, the other two started to tune in and hone into their sensitivity. The first little blips of information feedback got them pretty excited and kept the motivation going for quite a while. Proceeding along those baby steps, they then could twitch and shudder their relative angles; flex the muscles.

Painstakingly, they progressed, Azure able to stretch one border of the containment ‘bubble’ toward the Transition Point (Samantha firmly insisted on parting from the reference of it being a ‘sac,’ for reasons of her allies’ immaturity). Meanwhile, Bast’s seemingly much simpler task saw progress but would need more work. He could make the pneuma swirl and push things slightly, but confessed he wasn’t yet sure how to open the maw, and even a little scared to try moving those particular muscles.

Samantha didn’t know what else to tell him but that he’d have to seek out inner insight, just as she’d tried to tell him on this subject immediately after discovering it.

Bast’s face said it all about how much he relished this, but he grimaced and nodded agreement. Samantha made him say he would, on top of this, which got a quick affirmative, if with some serious sass included.

What ensued with them could only be described as getting ‘spiritually tired.’ They’d flexed muscles for the first time that were now worn the hell out, and their souls couldn’t handle more. It took Samantha quite a few rounds of pushing them to no avail, and responses of frustration, to understand this, because it was unbelievable to her. They were both totally insatiable in every other way! Meanwhile, when it came to this sort of stuff, she was the insatiable one.

With little choice left, she had to call it, but insisted they’d need to do it again. If possible, they should do it regularly on their own, to boot.

Because her lover was also frustrated, sassy, and even downright crabby after everything, Samantha took her home to give her a quick ‘reward’ for being such a good student for her teacher. All was well after that. Not much of a sacrifice on Samantha’s part, but she patted herself on the back all the same.

There was plenty more work waiting for her on the itinerary from Jeeves. Soon, the army would be marching again. The grind to Matrazere was on.

Palthennius brooded into his ale cup as his men, all around, hustled and bustled in merriment, forgetting their circumstances with ease and drinking, within the oddly quaint confines of the Blackbean Inn in ‘The Proper,’ the otherwise dour town in view of the Unbroken Fortress of Matrazere. The inn was full of pleasant music and soldiers, as Palthennius had deliberately conspired. It wore away the dark mood and countenance of nigh every citizen of the area.

Anytime that Palthennius caught sight of one of the sunken-eyed poor saps, whether a passing citizen on the streets, an innkeeper, or an otherwise pretty serving wench, he just felt awful. Their smiles were always especially painted-on, especially obsequious and full of fear. That was just the way of things close to the fortress.

And that was easy on the eyes compared to those former criminals that had their souls removed, which Matrazere, in contrast to nearly every other place within the argutes of the Dominion, seemed to especially utilize. They purchased the life sentences of those men here like a valuable commodity, and pretended it wasn’t slavery. They were used like status symbols: the more, the better off you were, among the Wise and Enlightened.

Here, more than anywhere, a peasant had to watch their behavior, as punishment lost you your soul all too swiftly. Rumors even went that charges were often made up. The righteous Palthennius of a few years ago would never have believed such ‘nonsense,’ but his faith was not the same.

The awful town, to the eyes of his present faithlessness, seemed less like one of contained and suppressed sin governed by honorable judges of sacrifice — per the official stance — and more like one of abused victims driven by evil-minded masters.

Why his vision had been so transmuted… he didn’t know. The dirge of failure, perhaps.

There’s definitely a curse in this soil. In the dust of the air. What lies merely hidden and contained below the City of Heavenly Order, always looming under our feet, is instead exposed here. They don’t even try to contain it. They’ve let it loose, to soak in men’s bones…

Palthennius grimaced and took another swallow of bitter drink. One good thing about The Proper was the dark ale. It suited his mood recently.

He had been fighting with Unobus and anyone else who had an ear he could bend, to be sent back south to harry the enemy on their inevitable approach. It only made sense. He was well practiced at skirmishing and hit-and-run tactics by largely all means of mobility. He didn’t need his best mount for that. The enemy would hardly commit their best in response, and if they did, he was disciplined enough to retreat again. They’d just have to eat casualties, again and again and again.

But they refused to budge. He was to stay in Matrazere. The orders were absurd. He could only think them worried over morale in the event he was slain. No matter how much he assured them he would not put himself in that position, his superiors just listened calmly and nodded along until he was done. Inevitably, he was told his position would be considered carefully, and then he was dismissed. Anytime he pressed, he was told his orders hadn’t changed.

So here he was. Doing drills copiously with a new mount during the day, and drinking until his brain turned off at night.

The last gulp was apparently the straw that broke the camel’s back. Palthennius felt the bite of pain go too deep. He stood.

Some of his men paused and looked at him. He met their eyes ominously at first, but then held up his hands, trying a grin. “Relax. I just need to piss.”

One of his captains laughed and raised his cup. “Hear, hear, General! Piss for me, too!”

“That’s Commander to you, pig,” Palthennius quipped sarcastically. It was technically true while he was under the higher authority of the Sage and Garrison General, Unobus.

Laughter followed him out of the inn as he found a convenient spot to take a delightfully long, long, satisfying piss.

As he was headed back in, a feel of something in his coat pocket made him freeze. He reached in and pulled out a coin that shouldn’t have been there. He looked around, but the evening streets were empty of movement.

Huh. When did this get here? It’s-

His eyes widened as he inspected the coin. Ancient, worn, invalid; of another long-obsolete time and place. The place his homeland had once been.

Only a handful of people could’ve sent this.

Despite the alarm surging in his blood, he played it cool, pocketing the coin and going back in at an even pace. He pushed through the crowd of soldiers and took a sip of ale, then told the men he had letters to read in his room and he wanted to do so before his eyes got blurry.

Once in his room, Palthennius locked the door and did, in fact, open a letter, though it was one he’d already read. He pinched the coin in his pocket as he read, and the words completely changed in his head, replacing the text as his eyes burned through the new, damning words.

Words that sobered the blood pumping faster and faster through his veins. It was a warning. It unveiled the worst horror he could imagine. Such a horror, he couldn’t believe it, even as it awoke a sinking dread within him.

But at the bottom were two familiar letters like a brand, forcing the reality hot and burning into his very soul. Two letters and an undeniable explanation.

‘G.N. — I owed your father my life. Damned, the lot of us, it seems. Let the damnation of this betrayal take me, so it still the lesser to betraying his son.’

As he felt the coin disintegrate between his fingers, Palthennius collapsed into a nearby wooden chair. He felt sick and weak, a stew of existential terror and alcohol making an abominable concoction in his gut. A cold sweat broke out over his forehead. He stared, panting, shaking, at the paper in his hand, which had switched back to a far more mundane scouting report.

He stared through it, feeling caught between some mad mirage painted by the hidden warning — which destroyed evidence of its existence so easily and readily — and the unraveling world he knew. A world some part of him wanted desperately to hold on to, demanding that he dismiss the absurdity of those words.

So much had been false, but this was the dagger in the back of everything. He’d been slain in his soul, already, as he could not deny the truth, whatever the demand in his soul. It fit. It fit too well.

How could they? What would even be left of us after this?
