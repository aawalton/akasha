Clark really liked his boots, and having made them himself made them just that much cooler.

They were still a little tight, and Clark had trouble getting ⟨Retouch⟩ to get just the right level of tightness, but he preferred it being a bit too snug to a bit too loose, because when it was tight he did not need to worry about his feet slipping inside his shoes, just the shoes themselves slipping on whatever he was walking over. He had made a couple of buttons for the side, and each was laced up with a single leather shoelace, and he felt that they really completed his look.

He had been able to make them white, but it looked wrong with his mostly-blue clothing and the super pristine look that [Unblemish] kept them at made them look out of place with all of the red dust and mud and rock everywhere, and it was a bad look to seem so absolutely above everything. That was what Tyrants would do, to make themselves seem above the 'petty mortals,' and so not only was it his duty as a citizen of the First Empire of Humanity to not copy them, it was his duty as a scion of House Haleford to remain approachable and friendly, that any who suffered might be able to find comfort with him.

That the only ones who might seek relief from suffering were his four friends who already knew him was not important, but appearances always were.

So instead, he had made them a very light brown, giving them a blue trim that matched his robes.

Clark wiggled his toes in happiness, watching the tips of his boots shift as he did so, and finished hanging up the last of the meats to dry. Alyssa needed a lot of food for her multi-day excursions, and thin-sliced meat hung in a moderately hot clay oven for hours to days had proven to be the best way to provide it. She returned often enough that her full nutrition could be supplemented with fresh fruits and cooked vegetables, which made things a lot easier. Clark might not have had the Skills needed to detect nutritional health, but it stayed a concern even when he was unable to see it.

He hummed a little to himself, [Unblemish]ed his hands and his knives clean, put them away in the little knife block he had made to hold all of his culinary implements - each had their own little slot made just for it, it was so fun - and skipped over to the Commander to help her with the new Charcoal Furnaces.

He might not have minded the old ones, as he'd found ways to use his magic such that it only took a couple of hours out of each day to correct what the inklings in charge of gathering wood were doing, plaster a bunch of mud over the top, place the runestones that helped all the magic happen, and break apart any furnaces ready for collection, but it was still going to be quite nice to not have to work with them at all.

The new Charcoal Furnace design was entirely reusable. It was going to be like, thirty feet long and taller than Clark was, so it could make a bunch of charcoal all in one go. he would say it would make a ton of charcoal, but he was uncertain whether it would be an actual ton! But the best part, something about these new furnaces would make the charcoal they made even better, which the Commander was going to use in some of her high-temperature alchemy to improve the Blast furnace, maybe even making steel with it!

It was, however, only half-made, with a lot of iron beams sticking out of the ground, bending over in a slightly irregular arch - Clark had helped to bend them, but his magic had done a lot less than the giant tree which they had found and wrapped white-hot iron halfway around.

There had been a lot of smoke, and he was unsure how much his magic had helped keep the tree from entirely burning down, as opposed to what would have happened normally, but the result was one that they were happy with, so he was happy. Either way, they had these enormous curved-around iron bars, and Commander Inq and Warrior Veeran were in the midst of now driving them into the ground. Specifically, the Commander was holding them steady, and Jacob was standing on a reed platform, swinging a massive sledgehammer with a head as big as the Warrior's torso as though it were all but weightless.

Each impact into the iron pillar drove it further into the ground with a tremendous CLANG, and Clark waited for a moment to intercede. In the meantime, he looked around the Ironroad area. It was cool, how he could see the magic flowing around, changing things as they expanded their industry past their prior walls. Fortunately, the giant passageway from the Junglewaste also funneled most of the dragons through it, and as such it had been a lot easier to defend against the attacks with concentrated inkling guards. Sometimes Jacob still needed to run out and kill something big and ferocious, but it was hardly a common occurrence. Having all of their enemies in one place was really nice, because it allowed them to expand much more easily!

Also, the sands conveniently turned things tossed into them into sand over time, so they had way fewer dragon bodies piling up or that needed to be thrown into the river to keep from rotting all over the place. Most of them tasted bad, and they really just did not have anything else to do with hundreds of dead animal bodies, so... disposal it was. That they took care of themselves now was kind of cool.

If he really looked, he could almost see where First Tower ended and The Jungle started, but it was fuzzy and kept shifting and neither Nature nor Technology nor any of the other ones that seemed to really change between inside and outside were ones he could see that well. Something similar happened with The Jungle and the Junglewaste, and it was just... neat, seeing the collision between ecosystems like that. Even if it was just kind of like an occasional really subtle haze in the air.

Eventually, Jacob pounded the arch level with the others already driven into the ground, and Henrietta signaled for a stop. Jacob swung the hammer down to his side, rattling the platform he stood on, and panted for breath. Henrietta let him be, and walked up to Clark, "Food's set?"

Clark nodded and gave a thumbs-up.

"Excellent. I want you doing your earth-softening trick for now. We have two more of these, and then we'll move on to putting the iron plates on. Sound good?"

It did, and everything worked excellently. ⟨Remake⟩ and ⟨Retouch⟩ helped everything go into the ground really smoothly, to the point that Jacob could almost just muscle them into the ground directly, without any hammering. He might have been able to if Clark could do work on both ends of the arch at the same time, but his arms were unable to reach that far, and he still needed to touch whatever he was working with.

Once the structural frame was in place, they then installed a few cross-braces, which Clark's magic made really easy, and then they started putting in the iron plates and bolting them into the bars. Once that was done, and Clark fused the iron together like it was being welded, they would put a ton of clay on everything for insulation, make a big pair of iron doors for each end, and set up something like how the brick kiln had a bunch of fire beneath the main area, which would be transferred through the iron floor into the top when it was filled with basically a full tree, and then they could just let it bake for a week, and pull out lots of charcoal at the end of it all.

Making it was, of course, a lot of work! The less air-gaps that got in, and the more insulation the furnace had, the better it would all work. Because making charcoal out of wood gave you more charcoal if they kept the wood from burning up in the process! It was the same idea that had gotten the sniffer inklings trained to know when to starve the fire of the old furnace out or when to let it burn, but this time there should ideally be no fire at all!

It was a lot of work up front, though.

A bit of noise drew Clark's attention, and he looked over to see Oliver's familiar-drone bobbing around the gates of where First Tower really started. Clark skipped over to it, reveling in the feeling of his boots, and tried to poke it. It cleanly ducked out of the way, and made a few motions in the direction of Henrietta and Jacob.

Then, after bobbing back and forth a couple of times, the drone took off into the air and towards the Spire Elevator. It then paused and looked back at them, before making a very obvious 'follow me' motion. Then it flew back to the gates and kept flying back and forth.

Clark blinked for a moment, a bit tired from all of the labor he'd been doing... oh shoot, dinner would be late, there were some things that he had needed to get started that... well, he could make something in a bit of a faster method tonight?

Still, he had a message to deliver. "I think Oliver wants us," he mentioned to the duo, and they looked up, noticed the drone, and then headed off to see what the wonderful wizard Oliver had made.

"I figured it out!" Oliver happily shouted, the moment the three of them - the Commander had tagged along as well - got close to where he was, by the Metalpress. "It's an Attunement failure!"

"Attunement?" Henrietta asked, with some surprise. "I... was going to say I can understand, but I don't think I actually do." She rubbed her forehead. "Attunement shouldn't matter until after creation. Transmutation without Attuning is a classic way to create temporary objects, so even if your inbuilt Attunement mechanism failed, it should work just fine, right?"

Oliver pointed at her dramatically, "So yes, but no. So, Attunement isn't just for enchanting. It's also really important for hardening against ambient magic. If the object itself knows what it's supposed to be, then it can more readily weather brief attempts to change its nature, or... extreme environments."

Clark had no idea why Oliver said the last part like it explained everything, but it looked like Warrior Veeran and Commander Inq did not understand it either, so there was no need for him to feel bad.

Oliver did not offer any additional explanation, instead standing with his hands spread, until Clark finally decided to ask, "But the weather here is so mild? It is wonderful, really."

"That's... not what I mean," Oliver shook his head. "Exposure to extremely high amounts of mana is going to be bad for... basically anything. It's the basic principle that all magic works on, that sufficient amounts of an element in a location will cause the physical world to mirror the Tapestry. So any time there's a lot of magical power in an area, if something doesn't know what it is well enough, it's likely to break. So..."

"I think you will need to explain, Smith," Henrietta said this time. "It's the end of the day, and none of us are familiar enough with the forces at play to follow what you're saying."

"It's not that hard," Oliver half-whined. "But fine. The Metalpress, because it doesn't have any particular precision involved in it and it has a ton of power draw, has the entire build area experiencing the magical equivalent of a kiln hot enough to melt iron. Then, because what it's making isn't being Attuned enough, that environment is breaking the things it would be making before it's even done."

Recognition sparked in Henrietta's eyes. "So that's why you were having so much trouble diagnosing the error."

Oliver nodded. "Everything is working, it's just also breaking before I can get my hands on it. Adding in enchantments from the instant-firing kiln made it worse, just made all of it disintegrate instead of just portions, and it got me thinking."

"What's your solution, then?"

"Well, the first thing I want to try is just making the Attunement stronger, and for that..." he pointed to Jacob. "I want to try incorporating [Frostblade] and ⟨Rimeforge⟩ into the enchantment."

"That sounds like an excellent idea, Smith. But it is nearly dinner, and therefore that sounds to be a good tactic to explore tomorrow," Henrietta insisted. "I'm not going to let you overwork yourself again."

"But I was so close..." he looked back at the Metalpress with a heartbroken expression.

"It is entirely alright, Oliver!" Clark decided to pull the Artificer's attention away from his work so that he could get the rest their Commander knew he needed. "I have been meaning to ask you whether I have some way of changing the names of my subskills."

"No, not really," Oliver said carefully, "Their names are a part of them, and you can’t change how elemental Name interacts with them. Your only real chance would be changing the meanings of the words themselves, so that the translation resolves to something else. But what's wrong with them right now?"

"Well," Clark said, "My skill is called [Unblemish], yet most of my subskills are 're'-something. It is a most dreadful conundrum, and I do not know how I can manage to sustain it long-term. Can you assist?"

Precisely as Clark had planned, Oliver did not say one more word about wishing to work on Metalpress for the rest of the day!

Schemes were such fun.

"That was so much harder than it should have been," Oliver slumped into his preferred chair, his head leaning back. "But... it is done."

"You got Metalpress working?" Clark asked, scooping up some extra broth from the soup for a hot drink for the Artificer. He carefully maneuvered it into a mug, tossed a small scrap of meat he'd knocked out of the pot to Tully, and passed the hot drink to Oliver, who eagerly took a deep whiff.

"Oh, that smells so good. And... not complaining, but are we out of the teas?"

"Alyssa has been doing other things recently," Clark apologized. "And the last of the herbs I usually make teas from went into this soup today."

"Right," Oliver took a sip of his broth, and his eyes began to drift closed. Unfortunately, Clark did not yet have any way he could use [Unblemish] to restore energy, so he waited for the Artificer to collect his thoughts. "Because the inklings were bad at collecting herbs and berries."

"They just shredded anything too small," Clark confirmed.

"Mmm," Oliver mused. "Oh, my brain hurts. But I got the Attunement working, got rid of that stupid sharp-edge tendency, probably fixed the material-flow blocker... that one was annoying, it could just be pretending to work though. I added the anti-interference buffering, and while I couldn't get the enameling or high-detail bits working, those I think make more sense to be separate tools anyway... or maybe that's just my brain lying to me about what makes the most sense."

He paused for a moment, taking another sip of his drink. "I even fixed that one bug where it would randomly send shrapnel flying everywhere on a failed press. That one was because the bronze gear controlling the motion-fixing was slightly out of balance, because I guess I hit it too hard with my hammer when hand-carving the glyphs into it."

"Avoiding shrapnel is good," Clark encouraged. "So it is good you fixed that."

"Ughhhhh..." Oliver whined. "Do you have any idea how hard it is to debug something when the problem could be the enchantment design, sure. But it could also be because the glyphs were miscarved, because I had to do it all by hand. Or it could be what the glyphs are carved on, because that was also handmade. Or it could be because some of the materials had some hidden elemental contamination that wasn't gotten rid of during processing, because we're doing all of that by hand. Oh, or maybe it's just environmental conditions screwing with things, because we can't control those at all..."

"I do not," Clark admitted. "But it does sound a bit like diagnosing trickier illnesses with no Skills, which I always thought was quite hard."

"When the mechanical diagnosis is as hard as a medical one, something's wrong." he murmured. "I had a job offer for Kortola too, you know? I could have been building out a System with a team, and we would have had machines, that were already made, and which just work, and..."

"And it is most excellent that you are here!" Clark encouraged him. "Imagine if you were not!"

"That... that's fair."

"And now you have made Metalpress, so you can make so much more! Shall you make the clockwork hearts upon it?"

"Pieces, sure," Oliver nodded. "Most of the pieces, honestly. Maybe even all at once, if we're lucky. I wish I had figured out some kind of support structure... that would be nice. Just press the entire heart in one go..."

"Why can you not?"

"Too complex," Oliver shrugged. "It can do individual parts, but it would always come out as one solid piece. If it needs to be separate, because of movement or enchantments or whatever, they can't be put together on the press."

"Still, it sounds as though it shall be most wonderful to use!"

"Yeah... yeah. It will. Mm, this stuff is good. Could I get some more soup?"

"It would be my utmost delight."
