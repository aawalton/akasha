I floated along in one of the gently flowing currents of the ocean of souls. The experience was how I imagined it must feel to fly, pulling me along like a cloud in an airstream, not a care in the world. Beneath me in the depths, something massive swam, outpacing the stream I found myself in. In its wake, my own current buckled for a moment, but I wasn’t afraid in the slightest. Something that big in the ocean of souls was undoubtedly a Guardian, patrolling for anything that shouldn’t be here, which basically meant anything that wasn’t a soul waiting for rebirth.

I couldn’t say how long I had been dead. The ocean had that effect on you. Like the feeling of a warm shower, with your eyes closed as the water cascaded down over your skin, forming rivers and paths, shutting out the world. A moment of respite, between lives, meant to lull you before what came next.

I may not have known how long I had been dead for, but I still remembered my old life. I’d died somewhat young, just before setting off for college. The son and only child of a pair of kind, loving parents. I had wanted for little and been doted upon all my life – my past life, I reminded myself. I didn’t miss my parents, but that was because they’d been in the car with me when I died. Small reliefs, but we had passed on together. We’d even had time in the ocean of souls together. Time to say goodbye. Few people got that opportunity and I was incredibly grateful for it.

Both of my deceased parents had already been reborn. Where and how, well, those weren’t things that we souls got to know. The Ocean of Souls imparted only enough knowledge about itself to help calm its denizens. All I knew was that when the time came, you would rise to the surface, and after passing beyond the ocean, you found a new home.

Passing beyond the surface was no small matter. To be taken from the ocean of souls was to be scrubbed clean and prepared anew. All memories taken, all personality wiped, like bleach for the soul. When it was my turn, the same would happen to me. On one hand, I might have feared it; on the other, after it happened I wouldn’t even know, so why worry about it?

Without my parents to keep me company I had been forced to find other souls to talk to. Plenty died in gruesome battles on worlds I had never even conceived of, though most died of disease or old age. A few even claimed that their worlds were like the roleplaying games I remembered from Earth, with classes and everything. I kind of hoped I would be reborn in just such a world. But the deceased did not get to choose.

At least, as far as I knew.

So I continued to drift, carefree and relaxed. The wake of the Guardian now passed as it continued its patrol. One moment I was surrounded by clear blue water, enjoying my time between lives, and the next, a new shadow was passing over me that didn’t leave a disturbance in its wake. That shouldn’t be possible. I frantically tried to move out of the current, uncertain what was above me, when I found the darkness closing in from all sides. I panicked, then, trying to swim away. But there was nowhere to go.

The void surrounded me, blacker than the darkest night. Clawed fingers ripped into my soul and I screamed. I thrashed back and forth wordlessly stricken with fear that whatever had surrounded me was killing me. Throwing myself at the darkness, rising panic filled me as my formless attacks did nothing.

My screams for help went unanswered as I desperately sought a way to survive whatever this was. My hands continued to scrape at the darkness, their meagre might seemingly amusing the beast as its claws once more raked through my form. When my attacks continued to fail, I pleaded, begging for my life. My appeals fell on deaf ears, its teeth casually gnawing away at me, taking its time like a cat playing with its food.

I knew what it was then, even before I heard the snicker of its voice in my head.

A demon. I was being devoured by a demon. One who dared to sneak into the ocean of souls. In that moment it became obvious that I had no chance of winning. Where was a Guardian when I needed one? I continued to thrash and struggle, unwilling to cease to exist without a fight. Yet, all my effort came to naught.

A sense of movement jolted us, and I was given a moment's reprieve as I felt us rising. Then came the surface of the ocean, and for the first time I heard more from my attacker than a cruel chuckle of amusement. The demon shrieked, and a ray of light opened up in the darkness. With the light burning me I could see that the demon had enveloped me with its body, cutting off all avenues of escape. That realisation was followed by pain as the little bit of light leaking through the demon scoured me as well, burning away memories and thoughts of my past life. But not all of them, as only a fraction of the light touched me: the demon bore most of the assault.

Then the light was gone, and the gentle thump-thump, thump-thump, thump-thump of something nearby was all I could hear. I tried to look around, but I could see nothing and feel even less. A whimper stirred my attention, and I realised wherever I was, whatever I was, I was not alone. The tone had changed, but I could still recognise my attacker.

That memory remained firm.

I spread my soul out and found that whatever we were in was humanoid. The demon spread through half of it and I the other. I prepared to defend myself, expecting to be attacked again, but the wordless mewling drew me to one simple conclusion. The demon was weakened. Whatever the surface of the ocean of souls did to cleanse those that left its waters, the demon had suffered the worst of it.

In a fury I lashed out. Where it had toyed with me, enjoying my suffering, my response was that of a wild beast, ripping apart its soul in an animalistic rage. I had no claws but we weren’t physical beings and with my metaphysical teeth, I tore its spiritual flesh apart. The demon bellowed an unholy scream echoing around us as it desperately tried to hide. But there was nowhere for it to go. Nowhere for it to escape my retribution. Torn pieces of its spirit floated around me like macabre confetti, but I refused to stop till there was nothing left but the tatters of its existence.

With each shred of itself the demon lost to my anger, I settled deeper and deeper into the humanoid form we were in. As my soul slowly melded into the lifeform so too did the torn fragments of the demon blend into my soul. By the time I was done, not a single piece of my tormentor remained. As though an equilibrium had been reached, I felt my soul retreat into the form, merging perfectly with the body I found myself in.
