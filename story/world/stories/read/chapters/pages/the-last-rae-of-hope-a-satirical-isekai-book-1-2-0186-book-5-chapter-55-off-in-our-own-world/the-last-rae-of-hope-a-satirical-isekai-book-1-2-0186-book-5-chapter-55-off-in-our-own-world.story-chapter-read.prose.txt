I caught Nora as she left Sparkles’s room. The priests who had helped her were also heading down the hallway, their heads bowed.

Pastel pink, whew.

“Yes—thankfully so. At this point I’d say Euphridia deleting them from Naught plays well in our favor. But I’m the only healer you can have, going into Paradise tomorrow.”

The lords have their subjects on alert for any changes or attacks, but it’s only a matter of time.”

“So… It’s tomorrow then…” I trailed off as a hollow sound filled my ears.

“Yes. Tomorrow.”

My heartbeat started to thud in my ears. “Tomorrow, tomorrow.”

“Just tomorrow?” Nora arched an eyebrow.

“Right. Tomorrow.” I took a deep breath, hoping that would clear away whatever was distracting me.

She stared at me expectantly for a bit, but I couldn’t quite figure out what I was supposed to do.

“Why don’t you go to sleep, Rae? It’s pretty late.”

“Sleep… Then tomorrow.” My head started pounding. “Tomorrow? Tomorrow—”

I felt her grab my arm. “This way, Rae.”

“Bed?” I questioned, stumbling after her. “Bed is… for sleep?”

A familiar buzz came from ahead of us, and Olethros appeared, looking rather concerned. Nora pulled me forward and put my hand in his, just as something passed between them that I was too foggy to parse.

“I’ll see you out, Rachel,” he said, his fingers wrapping around mine.

Nora stepped aside. “She needs actual sleep,” she called. “Not a long-winded discussion on why.”

“Noted,” he said over his shoulder, already opening a portal.

After stepping through, I was even more dumbfounded.

“Bed?” I questioned, squinting into the darkness.

He shook his head with a small sigh as he gripped my hand a little tighter. “Why don’t we look around first?”

My eyes were already adjusting. We were on a tall mountain somewhere, near a solitary pine tree with an excellent view of the smaller ranges around us. The night sky was filled with clear stars, and no Paradise was visible anywhere.

“Where… Where is this?” I asked, finding myself more grounded than before.

“A little place of mine that’s off most maps. We can’t quite physically reach it now, but I remember it well enough to reconstruct it.”

I looked up at the stars. They were pleasant enough, though I didn’t recognize any particular arrangement. Not necessarily Speranza’s or Earth’s, but arranged just enough to complete the skyline.

“It’s beautiful,” I admitted, everything starting to make sense again as my heartbeat slowed. “But I need to talk to you. About after tomorrow. About what happens after—”

“I know.” He was quiet for a moment. “But, just for tonight, let’s pretend there are no other worlds, let alone anyone else in this world, shall we?”

He really does know… doesn’t he?

Of course he does. He’s not an idiot.

“Have it your way,” I said with a small sigh. “But we’ll talk about it later, yeah? Otherwise… Nora…”

“Nora? Who’s that?” He pulled off his cape and gestured toward the tree.

“Never mind.” I sat on his cape and leaned back against the trunk.

He settled in next to me, and I plonked my head on his shoulder. “So, what’s our perfect world’s name, anyway?”
