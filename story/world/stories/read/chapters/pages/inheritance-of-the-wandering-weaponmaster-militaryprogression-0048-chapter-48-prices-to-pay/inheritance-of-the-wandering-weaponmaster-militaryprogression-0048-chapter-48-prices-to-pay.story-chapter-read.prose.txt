The rest of the evening passed in a blur. Ezra was told that half of the fee would be covered by his squad leader, due to his feeling culpability for Ezra’s willingness to lash out violently at the constabulary. Georg had the good sense to look embarrassed at that, with Aaron shooting a glare at his younger brother that seemed to communicate to Ezra that the Count didn’t know his brother was leveraging the Standage name for such a reason. Regardless, it was obvious that the Count was distinctly unhappy with Ezra.

Despite any mercy shown by Georg, that left Ezra with a fine of fifteen large silver and a week to spend in the stockade. Considering his recent relationship with the constabulary, he wasn’t looking forward to what might happen to him when he was locked up and in their control. Before he could mention those fears, the conversation continued.

Within a few minutes, Ezra, his fellow soldiers, and the guards were escorted from the Count’s manor. The man had given hard glares at both Georg and Brom, and they made their promises regarding their owed visits. Then, once they were away from the gates of the property, Georg turned and looked at Brom.

“Sorry for all this.”

“I would say it’s all to be expected,” Brom said, “but you know it’s not. I’m just glad you didn’t tell me to do anything in there with the Wainwrights.”

“Yeah,” the Lieutenant nodded, smiling weakly. “I know she’s technically your superior.”

“Not just technically, literally. You’ve gotten me into trouble, and I’d go so far as to say that’s a pretty fucking rude thing to do to your best friend. Especially since we never see each other.”

“But you know why—”

“I know what reasons you’re going to give, and I think that they’re stupid and you know I do,” Brom cut Georg off. “I wouldn’t mind having to go through this trouble twice as often as I already do if you’d just spend more time around me and, you know, be a friend. Instead, you say you don’t want to impose or to make my job harder, and then you stay away and end up making my job harder just without us actually having fun together.”

Georg hung his head in what seemed to be a genuine apology, and nodded. Then, he looked at Ezra, and once they made eye contact, he commanded, “Listen to him. He’ll take care of you, keep you safe. I’ll see you in a week. Don’t cause trouble.”

“Yes, sir.”

“Good man. Now hand over to me anything that you don’t want to lose in the next week.”

It was obvious what exactly that meant, so Ezra pulled his purse out from the inside of his jacket, handing it over, as well as the knife that the Madam had given to him the day he left to enlist. At that, Georg said his goodbyes, and Petr, Troy, and Amy all stepped forward to do the same before they retreated at a jog towards headquarters.

And just like that, Ezra was left alone with the guards. And that was when the night truly started passing quickly.

As the commander of this squad, Brom gave commands and sent off his men at a run in two different directions while he, another guard, and Ezra went in a third direction. When Ezra tried to ask a question about where they were going, he received no answer, and he understood what his duty was right now: to stay silent and to follow.

Maybe five minutes’ walk later, they arrived at the stockade where Ezra was going to be kept. He’d never been to the jail before, as for slum rats, it was effectively a death sentence. Maybe you’d get out if the people who put you there remembered you existed. Maybe you’d get killed there, maybe you’d get shipped off to a labor camp. You’d never know, but most people didn’t ever return if they were actually sentenced to go there.

“We’re gonna pat you down,” Brom grunted. “Don’t fight back, don’t do anything, just let us make sure you’re not carrying anything dangerous. We’re gonna try to get whatever we take back to you, but frankly, there’s a detective and a noble who hate you and will probably do their best to make sure that anything valuable you turn over to us gets lost. I’m sorry, but Georg has respected you enough to tell you the truth, so I won’t hold back.”

“If they’re gonna try to steal my stuff,” Ezra asked, the anxiety that had stalked him with every step to get here spiking, “what’s going to keep me safe while I’m locked up?”

“Mostly anonymity and the presence of other people who can serve as witnesses in the event that you are found. Now hold your hands out in front of your body. I’m gonna pat you down.”

Brom gave the instructions and warning with no hesitation, and Ezra found himself falling in line with the other man’s commands. The pat-down was thorough, but not especially invasive or rough. That turned up nothing and so Brom then ushered Ezra into a nearby building.

From the outside, it looked much like any other building, except for the fact that its walls didn’t touch any of its neighbors. Instead, it had a small courtyard with a wall, but it didn’t look like a jail. Ezra followed the commands and found himself guided into the building, which on the inside certainly did look like a jail. Unadorned stone walls surrounded him, leading into a small, narrow hallway that then led to another hallway, which had four trapdoors in it.

“You’re the only prisoner who’s going to be in here,” Brom explained. “We’ll have at least two guards in here at all times. If anything’s happening in the cell, you can call. Otherwise, you’ll be delivered three meals a day, allowed to go out to the yard twice a day, once in the morning and again in the afternoon. If, during your free time, you try to escape, that privilege will be revoked. Please, do not cause trouble. Got it?”

Ezra nodded, surprised at how reasonable this imprisonment seemed to be shaping up to be.

Seeing his agreement, the detective continued, “If your fire goes out, let us know. You’ll be delivered enough coal for the day in the evening. That’s to make sure that at least through the night you’ll be warm enough. If you’re cold during the day and you’ve used all of your coal, tough shit. Plan better. You’ll have enough space to move around in the cell, so even if you run out of coal, you can keep yourself warm.

“You should have two blankets in the cell. If you don’t have two when you look, call up, we’ll make sure you get a second one. Otherwise, like I said, three meals a day. There’s a bucket in the corner, you know why. If you want to try to hold it in to relieve yourself during your two breaks during the day, you’re free to do so. Any questions?”

“No, sir.”

“Then hop on down. Don’t cause trouble, and this will go fast.”

And just like that, Ezra found himself in the dark of the night with a small barred window and a cell that was maybe four meters by three meters. Looking at the window, which was just above head height for him, Ezra could tell that there was glass over it, to keep most of the heat from escaping. Then, looking over at the tiny stove, Ezra could see that there was a good stock of coal ready to be burned, but it wasn’t lit.

“Can I have flint and steel? Just to get this started?”

Nobody answered for five seconds. Then ten seconds. Then fifteen. And Ezra cursed himself for trusting the damn guards. Of course they were doing this. Put all the things to keep him warm in here, but then not let him burn the coal. Of course Georg thought they were trustworthy and it turned out that they were fucking assholes.

There was no use continuing to beg, so instead he looked into the corner, expecting to find maybe a threadbare sheet if they were feeling generous. He’d made it through plenty of cold nights, and with his uniform coat, he wouldn’t freeze to death, but it would certainly be pretty miserable. But with a good coat and aura, with it being early spring? He’d survive. First, he had to find the blankets, though, if they existed.

The trapdoor overhead creaked open, and a face poked down, illuminated by glowing coals.

“I’ve got a load of coals to get in your stove. It’s a lot easier to get it going with this than with flint and steel. Go ahead and stay in the corner while I get it loaded up, okay? For your information, I’ve got two guys up here, each one holding a crossbow, so please don’t do something stupid and get yourself killed.”

Ezra’s hatred of the guards was wiped away by that, and he looked up at the guard, whose name he didn’t know, and felt pretty stupid.

“Is it all right if I just stand over in this corner?”

“I really don’t care which corner it is, so long as it isn’t the one where the stove is,” the guard said.

Ezra nodded and leaned over in the corner. Now that he wasn’t trying so hard to convince himself he was fine, he admitted that it was a damn cold cellar he was in. Sure, he’d survive without the fire, but it’d be bad. The damp cold would almost certainly get him sick staying down here for a week.

He stood in the corner while the guard carried the small load of smoldering coals into the stove, poked them in, and then fed the fire with two other pieces of coal.

“That should get you going. It’s cold enough that you’ll probably wake up before the fire is out.”

“Thanks,” Ezra nodded. “What was your name?”

“I’m Sam. The two guys upstairs on duty with me tonight, Fred and Vash.” The guard gave a smile as he said it, nodding his head. “I would shake your hand, but we’re not supposed to, just in case you’re feeling violent. I prefer to get along with you, if that’s an option, so let’s not choose violence.”

“Yeah, I’d prefer that too,” Ezra said, once again totally on the back foot.

While he reeled, the guard climbed up the ladder and out, before the trapdoor creaked and then slammed closed. Everything that he’d heard about the guards from people who had gotten in trouble inside the inner city, as well as all of his own interactions with guards and detectives, was entirely different from what he was getting now.

Even so, he wasn’t going to argue with it, and simply accepted the goodwill for what it was. Once Sam was out, Ezra set about figuring out his sleeping situation.

I was at least half sure you were going to get put into a pillory. Jormun’s voice cut through Ezra’s search of the dark room.

“Me too,” Ezra mumbled, just barely loud enough to be heard as he found the two blankets, each of which was rough but woolen, more than enough to keep him warm, what with being sheltered from the elements and having a burning stove besides. Before long, he was huddled under the blankets and near the stove, which was putting off a pleasant heat. It wasn’t nearly enough to keep him cozy, but he was comfortable enough, and then, with nothing better to do, Ezra threw himself into meditation.

Every opportunity to meditate that he’d had until now since he ascended to Adept had been rushed, on the march, and then one night in The New Dragon’s Toenail as he was recovering from a fight. Now, however, he didn’t have anywhere that required his attention. As a matter of fact, he didn’t have anywhere he could go.

Leaning on freezing stone and beside a fire, a meditative Ezra pulled various types of aura into himself, stripping each of their attributes, and then transforming them into wind and maybe something a little different while it was inside his inner world. The whirling tempest inside grew a little stronger, a little denser, a little more than before under his ministrations, and it wasn’t long before he lost himself in the pleasant calm of meditation. And thus began his incarceration.
