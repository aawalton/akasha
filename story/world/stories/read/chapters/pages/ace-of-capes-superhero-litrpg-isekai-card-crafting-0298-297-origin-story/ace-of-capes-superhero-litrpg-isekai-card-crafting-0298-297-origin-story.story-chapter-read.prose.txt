Lexie held up her hand before they could react to that piece of information. “Hold on. Before you tell me how ridiculous that sounds, let me get it all out first.”

“Yeah, we weren’t going to tell you that," Tate said.

“You weren’t?”

“No. Frankly, this whole thing has been ridiculous from start to finish. Everything that has happened so far has been pretty hard to believe, making anything else you tell us from this point forward sound plausible in comparison.”

“Oh.” Lexie supposed that made sense, although what she was about to tell them was probably crazier than what they'd learned prior. “Okay, here it is. I think that all the humans involved in creating the original system are still alive. The Engineer, the First Mage, the Architect, the Developers, they’re all still here in various forms.”

"What do you mean by various forms?” Torin asked.

Lexie pointed at him. “I mean, they're around but may not necessarily be around..." She pursed her lips. "It's hard to explain. I think their souls live on, but not necessarily in their original bodies. So their soul can live in the system or in an item, and it gives them a form of life. Or they can live in an in-between space that I don't even know what it is."

“So they’re alive but as souls?” Torin ruffled his eyebrows.

“No. They have bodies too. But it’s more like…” She didn’t know how to describe it, how she observed the Engineer. He was strange because he clearly had a body, but there was no way he'd lived that long and not aged, and he lived in a spaceship far away that pulsed like a living entity itself. “The Alchemist and his golem, right? It’s kind of like that, except the soul possessing the golem doesn't need its original body and doesn't need the golem either. Something like that. I think that existence and that soul state is what gave Mikhail the idea to create Saint-0, a soul that can just exist without the need for a body and can possess things at will.”

“I see,” Aiden said and brushed his chin. “It’s a similar system the Alchemist uses to remain alive forever. He simply makes younger golems for his soul to possess and then lets the old golems die. It’s probably why he’s lived for so long.”

“So the Alchemist was one of the original creators of the system and signatories of the oath?” Torin asked.

“I don’t know that yet, but I do suspect he was around during that time, and he might have been involved in some capacity. Probably whatever he saw or experienced was what led him to contribute to creating the ISTS."

Lexie wasn’t sure how that timeline worked with the idea of him being an experiment of Neqal, but she put a pin in that thought for now.

“Okay, so here’s how I think the story goes for Mikhail. He’s the First Mage, the man who pioneered the research of magic and its infusion in our society. He probably had some experience with soul magic already enough for him to test human souls and figure out that they don't incorporate well with magic."

“Either that or the Fae told him," Aiden said.

“Yes, but I don't think the Fae did. I don't think they knew much about humans before they came here. I think they might have actually come to make a deal with the Guardians initially, and they found humans by coincidence. But I don’t have any evidence for that, I’m just spitballing because truly there was nothing special about humans to be had." She took a breath and swallowed. "District 8 was the region that served the Guardian of the Underworld and had people who were well-versed in soul magic. So it would make sense for Mikhail to be from there. I believe the First Mage was a powerful necromancer, and he was recruited for his understanding of human souls and how they transform with magic. Through testing, he found that humans could not carry magic in their souls unless said souls had already been transformed by a Guardian's test. Therefore, as the story goes, he crafts cards, then runes, then other things to help facilitate magic, but at some point, he learns that people from a lineage whose souls had already been transformed by the Guardians would be able to hold some magic. And that's how the Elementals came to be." She gestured to Torin briefly, then continued. "After he was done, he worked with the System Developers, the Engineer, and the Architect to set up Earth’s system, and they signed a contract with the Fae and all the world’s governments that would ratify the system and assign all its rules and regulations. I think that perhaps their immortality might have been a condition of this contract, or their existence might be the reason that the contract is still in place. I don’t know, but that’s just a guess.”

“It’s a good one,” Aiden assured her.

“Okay, but here comes the next part. After the system came into place and time passed, I don’t think that the original team was happy with the way things unfolded. In fact, I think that they realized pretty soon how nerfed they were in terms of power, at least compared to the Fae. They might have suspected the Fae had an ulterior motive in all this. So, they began to search for a way to advance magic outside of the Fae, or perhaps even discover a new way of magic. That’s how the ISTS came to be.” Lexie stared at them. “The Engineer, Alchemist, and the First Mage got together and figured that they could explore alternate dimensions to look for alternate possibilities where humans found magic one way or another. Then they could figure out a way to work that into our world."

Lexie thought the Chosen program was also a subset of that, but she hadn’t yet pieced it together precisely, and she didn’t want to talk about it in front of Torin anyway.

But bringing people from other worlds here had to be another way of trying to discover magic. But how? Who decided who came? And what was the selection criteria? She would discuss that with Tate later.

“It always seemed interesting to me that the ISTS was created to send researchers to other worlds, but they would then hide that research from us, so we don't even know what the return on investment is. We just assume that it's going into legitimate system improvements, but I mean, that's a lot of resources they're using for, as far as we know, nothing to show for it."

"So you're saying that the ISTS doesn't actually conduct the research it says it does?" Tate asked.

"Maybe now it does, but that might be a cover, or it might be thanks to interference from the Fae. But it is definitely not what it was created to do. It was created to explore dimensions and search for magic. I think the Fae found out about the ISTS and what the original three were trying to do. They probably figured it would never come to fruition, but just in case, they sabotaged it and orchestrated the theft of the ISTS from Mikhail.”

“How?”

“The Greater District War. I'm sure they planned that whole thing and somehow made it into a war about territory, when it was really about the ISTS and ownership of the project. I'm not really sure how it happened, but something caused a rift between the three, or perhaps in their ideology. The Engineer sided with the Fae and betrayed the other two. That's why he's in charge of the ISTS now. Using the war and the Heroes, the Fae seized Indigo and the Time Space Research Institute and ousted the necromancers and researchers from the area. Some of the research was destroyed in the process. It derailed the entire purpose of the ISTS, and it was rebranded as something to make the system 'better'. The original ethos was shut down."

However, oddly enough, they hadn't shut down the Chosen program. That bugged Lexie. What could they hope to gain from leaving it open?

Another pin on another thought.

"At some point, the Alchemist began working for the Association to continue his research," Lexie said. "But then they kept interfering and slowing him down with bureaucracy, frustrating him in the process and preventing him from achieving his goal. The Engineer continued to run the ISTS, but with some kind of Fae supervision, and Mikhail was left with nothing. He went back to District 8 and continued his soul research there. His descendant was in charge, and somehow, he came into control of the lab. I don’t know if the Emperor knows that Mikhail is his ancestor or not, but I suspect he does, which could also explain how the Emperor knows that immortality was possible. Perhaps Mikhail also promised the Emperor an immortal soul." She sighed. "Part of researching that immortality went into the creation of Saint-0. Saint-0 was really created out of a need for revenge, but not the Emperor's. Mikhail's. Not only did he want stronger magic for humans, but he also wanted soldiers with undying souls to beat the Fae and free us from their tyranny.”

Lexie paused there, waiting for them to digest everything she'd said so far.

“Wow," Torin said first. "That’s…”

“Yeah, I know it's a lot. Again, I don't know everything for sure. I'll tell you about the clues I got later, but some of it is just me filling in blanks, which I could end up being wrong about. But so far it makes sense."

"It does make sense," Aiden said thoughtfully.

"Also, I'm really not sure when or how the Alchemist came into this. He's not mentioned amongst the original creators of the system, so he probably joined them later. Perhaps, he wasn't even there for the initial stage of the ISTS. Maybe he was only used to replace Mikhail after he left. The important thing to note, though, is that while the Alchemist and Mikhail appear to have the same goal, their methods and missions are entirely different." Lexie put both index fingers together and then spread them apart.

"Mikhail’s main goal, I think, is turning humans into undying souls who can use magic. That's what Saint-0 is. They're soldiers, yes, but the main aim isn't just about their VOID-resistant souls. It's also about a soul that can be transformed without chaos and can not be destroyed by the Fae. Think about it, the Fae live a lot longer than we do. Even if we have magic, they could simply introduce a disease and kill us. They've done it before to others. Or they could target the strongest and leave only the ones without any knowledge of magic. The Fae would outlive us, and that was the major problem Mikhail saw. So he wanted to transform our souls so that no matter what body we took, we would live on.”

“Kind of romantic in a creepy way,” Tate said, and when everyone looked at him, he merely shrugged.

"The Alchemist, however, wants to destroy the system and go back into a time before the proliferation of fae magic. He wants to do it a different way. Perhaps, he thinks that he can use his golem-making skill to siphon magic directly from the Guardians without harming humans. Because essentially, the human body would be put to sleep, and the magic would be used by our golems.”

"The two sides sound pretty similar," Tate said. "One side working on the body and the other working on the soul."

"Here's the major difference. One of them wants to work with the Engineer, and one of them doesn't. One of them simply wants vindication, and one of them wants revenge." Lexie took another deep breath. "Oh, also, I think Irina knows some of what I just said. That’s why she didn’t go to the heroes, and also why she didn’t tell us the full story. She has probably met Mikhail and been convinced by him of the cause. In fact, I'm sure she knew Mikhai. She fully believed that the Time Space Research Institute had been stolen, even though it was widely thought to be propaganda from the Emperor. Why would she hold onto that belief so strongly, even though she hates the Emperor and everything he stands for? Except she knows it's not just propaganda. She heard it from a more credible source."

“So if she knows everything," Aiden piped in. "Why would she want you to look for Saint?”

Lexie shrugged. "Maybe she figured out the details of exactly what Mikhail was planning to do, and it scared her. I mean, making everyone immortal sounds great in theory, but the way he's going about it is an incredibly unhinged thing to do. When she discovered it, she realized that she'd made a mistake helping him. So she came to us for help, but she didn't want to incriminate herself and reveal that she'd been more involved than she let on."

"And she didn't tell us about Mikhail because..."

"Because she doesn't want him found for some reason." Lexie shook her head. "That part's a little wonky. We can figure it out later. But I think I have the meat and potatoes here."

"Another thing is," Aiden spoke up. "If they're technically working together, why would the Emperor imprison Irina?"

"I don't think they're working together. I just think they have a common associate. It's important to also understand that the Emperor's goal isn't the same as Mikhail's. The Emperor simply wants to live forever and be powerful, and destroy District 9. Mikhail is more concerned with perfecting and propagating Saint-0 to fight the Fae. Somehow, their plans align for now. I don't know exactly what Mikhail needs the Emperor for, but maybe he's using the Emperor's destruction to test Saint's capabilities. There must be something he wants for himself. But I think that with Irina and Mikhail, it might be more personal. I think at some point, the Emperor was also trying to play the Alchemist and Mikhail against each other, using both of them to achieve what he wanted." Lexie cocked her head. "The more I think about it, the more I realize that the Emperor really must have something they both want, which is why he could play those games. Whoever grants him immortality first wins."

"Zakhar told me about Irina's detainment," Torin said. "While you were gone, Aiden told me she visited you, and we ran some checks and realized that when Irina was captured, there was no correspondence between Mikhail and the Emperor for months."

"Exactly," Lexie said. "I think Mikhail might have started pulling away. Without the Alchemist, the Emperor is more dependent on Mikhail than vice versa. Mikhail does need him for something, I'm not sure what yet, but he has other options that the Emperor doesn't. So when Mikhail found out the Emperor was messing around, he might have considered going to someone else. Like Vacek, who might have actually heard him out."

"Vacek?" Torin raised an eyebrow. "Are you sure?"

"Vacek knows how dangerous the Fae are, and the idea of having undying soldiers would have been appealing to him. He might have cut a deal with Mikhail, or at least Mikhail thought he might, which the Emperor absolutely didn't want. So he kidnapped Irina to find Saint-0 and blackmail Mikhail, and when that didn't work, he probably went to beg for Mikhail back. But when Vacek died, the Emperor was sure that Mikhail would need him because there’s no way the current Hero’s Association without Vacek would agree to side with someone like him who has done rampant human experimentation. They will choose the Fae over him.”

“Even if he threatens them with Saint-0?”

“Especially if he does. I don’t think Saint 0 is actually finished yet. I think he’s close to finished, but they haven't yet stuck the landing, and that’s why he hasn’t revealed it to the world. There’s still one final piece.”

They were all silent, musing for a few seconds. This was a lot to take in. So much of what she'd said was suppositions and lucky guesses, but Lexie had her reasons for thinking so.

"So what do we do now?" Torin asked, making it clear that he thought Lexie had a solution.

She was happy for the vote of confidence, even though it didn't necessarily make her any more confident in her abilities.

However, she did have an idea.

"Mikhail's method is different from the Alchemist's, which is also different from ours. But we all want the same thing. Magic for humans and freedom from the Fae. Maybe if we can talk to him, we can come to a compromise."

Aiden brushed his chin, nodding slowly. "I can go talk to the Alchemist tonight. Try to ask him probing questions to figure out how much of what you said is fact."

"Okay, but he might try to mislead you. I don't think he wants us to work with Mikhail."

"I know. I'll watch for that." He got up and stretched. "Is that all, Lexie?"

No. That wasn't all. She still hadn't talked about the stuff with the Chosen program and how it made all the other pieces interlink. She hadn't gone into detail about the Engineer and the role he played in orchestrating the delicate balance of fate and chaos. There was also, of course, Janus. But that was all stuff that she would discuss with Tate when they were gone.

"Yes," she said. "That's all."

“Great, now let’s get you something to eat. You look awful.”

"Okay," she said, happy for a soothing meal after the heavy afternoon.

Then, after Torin had gone back to bed and Aiden had gone to talk to the Alchemist, Lexie went into Tate's room.

The lights were off, but she called out, "Hey, are you awake?"

He reached over to click the bedside lamp, casting the orange glow on the room. His hair was all over the place, and his voice was groggy when he said, "I am now. What’s up?”

She went over to sit on the bed. "I think I went into the brain of the ISTS today and met one of the people running the chosen program."

"Yeah?"

"Yeah. He's the Engineer. I think I also did something wrong, or at least something I wasn't supposed to do. Oh, and I found these..." She opened her storage and began to pull out the comic books, CDs, and everything she'd collected.

"What the heck is this stuff?"

"It's stuff I took from his office. Or spaceship. Or whatever it was." She put it on the bed. "Doesn't this remind you of something?"

"Yeah." He picked up one of the CDs. "It reminds me of the game I was supposed to beta test."

"The one that ended up being the world we're living in right now?"

"Yeah," he sighed.

"Is it strange that my brother knew about it, too? He sent me the character sheets and everything?"

He didn't look surprised. He simply nodded, rubbing his eyes.

"How did you get Chosen?" Lexie asked. "Do you know?"

"No clue," he yawned.

That felt like a cue, and he seemed pretty tired, so Lexie left him to it, figuring they could continue their conversation after their big day tomorrow. Depending on what Aiden came back with, they would be paying Mikhail a visit tomorrow. Lexie might as well rest and heal her body with sleep, too.

But there was no rest to be had.

The second she drifted off into the dreamworld, it turned into a haze of burning black and heavy fog, through which she saw a familiar figure.

"Naem?"

"You're in danger," he said without preamble. "Come with me now."
