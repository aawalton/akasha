A pulse of the behemoth's domain snaps out again, freezing us in place. I only have time for one brief thought – not again – before I’m hurled into the deck below.

Although I’m lucky enough to come out relatively unscathed – bruised but nothing worse – I flinch at the crack of bone as a white mass crumples to the deck right next to me.

Ivor, I cry through the Bond and am answered by a pained acknowledgement. The behemoth’s domain is still weighing heavily upon me, but I force my tired body to move nonetheless. I have no magic to heal with right now, so I reach into my Inventory and pull out a healing potion.

Ripping out the cork, I shove the bottle into Ivor’s mouth and upend it, uncaring of the way I accidentally slice my forearm on two of his sharp teeth.

Even as the potion pours into his mouth, I sense the domain shift around us. Though no longer weighing so heavily, it is still no weaker, and the intention I almost hear as words makes the hairs on my arms stand upright: the behemoth has lost patience with its meal taking so long to enter its mouth; now, it’s going to do something about that – actively.

What happens next is inevitable – the rough structure we created cannot hope to withstand the focused domain of a Tier Five beast, even without any additional physical actions. Loud gunshot cracks rend the air and the whole bulwark fractures.

The roc folds almost double and whips towards the behemoth’s gullet as its wings are caught more easily by the current than the rest of its body. As they are slammed into the rocky solidity of the behemoth’s mouth, the rocky spurs break apart and most sections sink deep into the water. All the while, the icebergs we took such pains to form are crushed into slush as they slam against each other.

Worse for us, the current has caught us once more – and it’s stronger than ever. The ship judders and shakes beneath me and the stern heels sideways as it’s dragged.

The tip of our prow slips back into the behemoth's mouth. We're already losing ground despite the lifeline of the anchor chain attached to Elarion’s vessel.

With even less resistance to the current than the main ship, the rowboats tethered to us with lines of rope are quickly whipped past where I’ve managed to stagger to the railings. My gaze follows one of the boats helplessly. The people inside are pale, several shouting or screaming in fear, and several more scrambling towards where their own lifeline is tied. Classers, slaves, and volunteers, all made equal in bone-deep terror.

They don’t even need Sarran’s shouted orders to pull themselves in – those with their heads screwed on are already trying.

But the ropes are horribly tenuous, and my stomach sinks as I see one already starting to fray near where it’s tied to the main ship. There must be a fault in the rope itself because it’s nowhere near anything that could cut it. A fault and simple force from the current putting it under too much strain.

It’s like I’m moving in slow-motion – I run towards where the rope is rapidly thinning, but I’m still several steps away when the final strand snaps. It’s silent, but my thoughts are not.

No!

I don’t have the time to ask permission; I don’t have the time to respond to the frantic questions coming from those on the other ships. I reach out into the Bond network and drag. I might not have any of my own mana right now, but my companions do.

It’s enough to spin a cord of air in a fraction of a second. That’s almost too long – I only just manage to catch the frayed end of the ship’s rope before it hits the current. Even in that little time, the gap has widened considerably between us and the rowboat.

The boat jerks to a halt as my grasping air-magic cord stops its forward moment, saving all but two – not sufficiently braced, they topple overboard at the abrupt halt. Guilt rends my insides as I realise there’s nothing I can do to save them. Their screams echo oddly, and then abruptly end as their forms disappear beneath the current.

I swallow hard. Channelling this magic is more than painful, but I can’t let go.

My focus narrows until all I can see is the frayed end of the rope; all I can feel is the searing, needle-like pain of forcing my channels to support magic despite all the abuse I’ve given them today. Air magic isn’t great for this task and I need every fraction of Willpower I have to make it act like a rope and not like a breeze.

I’m so focused that it’s actually a surprise when I realise that the frayed end has suddenly got close enough for those on deck to grab it and haul it in.

I blink. Then blink again as I process what I can now observe.

“What…?”

You were making so much effort to keep them alive; it only seemed right that we help you successfully do so, Kalanthia explains even as she grips the rope with her mouth and lends her strength to the task.

She’s not the only one of my companions helping. Ivor is still recovering from the injuries he took when he landed awkwardly, but Noir seems to have come off better because he’s offering his tail as a means for people to climb onto the ship from the boat. A rope ladder has also been dropped and Ninja is working with people on deck to pull the fighters onto the ship while Lathani is boosting them up from below. The other rowboat soon manages to haul itself in and that one is evacuated in the same fashion.

Worryingly, Bastet is absent, but, as much as I want to go and check on her, I need to see how things are going at the front of the ship. We’ve lost ground but we haven’t been consumed yet. That’s a good sign, at least.

Pausing just to order that the rowboats should be pulled onto the ship if at all possible, but not if, in doing so, people are put at risk, I hurry off to the prow of the ship.

There, I find a great deal of activity around the front anchor port. After seeing what happened to the rowboat rope, I’m doubly glad that we decided to use the forward anchor to attach us to Elarion’s ship – the chain is strong enough that even the power of a behemoth’s domain isn’t sufficient to break it.

There, I find the strongest Classers we have sweating as they struggle to push the levers attached to the capstan. Even Sarran is helping, beads of either fear or exertion rolling down his forehead as he encourages everyone on.

It only takes me a few moments to understand what’s happening – and why. If the current is strong enough that Elarion is unable to pull us forward, we’ll need to pull ourselves to him – and hope that we don’t accidentally do the reverse. But that means putting our own muscular strength against the power of the domain. Stepping forwards, I join two men on one of the levers.

If anyone strong is available to help, we need all hands on deck right now, I send out to the Bond network. I’m vaguely aware of Loran updating River and Hunter on what’s happened so they can update the other ships, but my focus right now is on getting us out of here. For a moment, I curse sending Artio to Elarion’s ship – he’d be very useful right now.

Still, I’m grateful when Kalanthia once more willingly steps in to help – though this one is less surprising since she would die along with the rest of us if this doesn’t work. She’s not the only one – in the end, our limit becomes the length of the levers. But we can’t lengthen those because of the space in which we have to work.

The current offers an enormous resistance, but the strength we have mustered is considerable. We move forwards, at first slowly, and then picking up the pace. Every moment that passes makes me worried that the behemoth will close its mouth or pull itself below the waves once more, but it doesn’t happen.

Something must be looking out for us – though the roc’s carcass was swept towards the monster’s gullet, a small piece of the rock spur we created has snagged on something and the carcass is stuck just ahead of the deep blackness.

The tip of our prow emerges into the light once more, then I feel sun shine on my head as the line advances further. The current is starting to relax its grip on us. But we’re not out of danger yet – we need to turn the corner around the edge of the behemoth’s mountainous mouth to get out of the current. Elarion’s ship isn’t yet in view.

Still, a shudder of relief goes through everyone as the whole ship emerges into sunlight once more. We’re finally a little further ahead of where we were when the bulwark was destroyed. Victory is almost ours – we can practically taste it.

Suddenly, we all stagger forwards, several of us falling to the deck below, others managing to catch ourselves at the last moment as the tension we’ve been bracing against suddenly disappears.

For a moment, I blink at the lever in my hands, expecting it to have broken. But it’s intact.

Then my gaze goes further and I see that the chain we’ve been winding in has gone slack.

My stomach sinks as my mind quickly makes the connection: somehow, the anchor chain has broken. Our life line is…gone.

Our forward momentum disappears; the current, still present even if weaker, catches on us once more. Understanding ripples along the Bond network.

Above us, Aingeal presses hard against the envelope of the hot air balloon, his heat threatening to destroy it as he works desperately to save us.

Ivor, recovered as much as he can without a healer’s help, leaps into the air, his back claws gripping a rope. Noir follows his brother into the sky. Their help slows down our backsliding, but they’re not strong enough to pull us out of this.

My own wings lie limp on my back. I know that I don’t have enough mana to make a difference, just as much as I know that if I hadn’t emptied myself so thoroughly against the bulwark that helped everyone else to escape, I would probably be able to tip the balance now.

If not for this accident…. A dark thought occurs. Accident…or sabotage?

My breath comes faster as I struggle to see a way out of it for all of us. In that moment, the coward in me whispers an incredibly tempting possibility. If all of my Bonded shelter in my tattoo, I’m confident I could fly with them to Elarion’s ship. My mana pool is all but empty, but I have my wings, and my domain is controlled more with willpower than magic.

And, ultimately, I am the leader of the expedition, the Great House heir. This one ship and everyone aboard are, by Moriax’s standards, less important than my single life.

But I can almost hear the screams of anger and betrayal that would follow me through the sky – and forever afterwards. I couldn’t live with myself if I abandoned ship and left everyone to die. And no one would ever be able to forget that, when it came to me or everyone else, I'd chosen myself.

So, pushing that cowardly part of me away as far as I can, I focus back on the situation, frantically wracking my brains for something, anything we can do.

My gaze lands on the carcass of the roc, almost in the Tier five’s gullet, but not quite consumed. The seeds of an idea sprout inside me.

Time is of the essence so I explain my plan to those in the Bond network as I run towards the back of the ship, pulling out my bow as I do so.

The behemoth wants the roc – we know that. Well, what if it gets the carcass finally? We’re out of its mouth – for this short moment. The timing will be awfully tight, but if we can get it to shut its mouth before the current pulls us back in, we’ll survive this.

I hear shouts and the drum of feet behind me – Loran has obviously passed on my words and others are joining in. The more the merrier.

I’m just approaching the quarterdeck when a massive shadow wreathed in red flame passes over my head with a loud crackling noise.

I lose a second of time as I stare at the eye-catching form. Flickering with flame as she is, it takes me a moment to see the more-than familiar form of Bastet inside the inferno as she soars overhead. She Evolved?

Given how much distraction there’s been, I suppose it’s not terribly surprising that I didn’t even notice when it happened, though I feel incredibly disappointed about missing it. I’d have loved to see what happened in her body during the process. I’ll just have to examine the differences. Later, that is, when we’re no longer in mortal danger.

Shaking myself, I run up to the quarterdeck, only sparing another glance behind me that notes how Bastet swoops down to grab a rope in her jaws, then soars upwards and…blasts out fire from her wings like she’s a living rocket engine?

I blink hard, then nock an arrow to my bow, one of the special ones. Using a whisper of air magic – that hurts like I’ve tipped acid into my veins – I ensure that when I fire it, it flies unerringly for the roc. Thanks to its aerodynamic shape and the fact that it’s heading towards the behemoth’s gullet, not away, its flight is unimpeded.

I shoot another one while the first is still in the air, and then my projectiles are joined by those of several others. When my arrows land, there are small explosions as the explosive potion held within their heads reacts to the impact.

The first arrow doesn’t work, and nor does the second, but eventually, one of them must hit the right spot. The roc’s carcass shifts, then slowly, but with increasing speed, it heads for the blackness at the back of the Tier five’s mouth.

We watch anxiously, though I note that the distance between us and the Tier five is slowly increasing – Bastet’s literal firepower has tipped the balance.

The roc’s carcass disappears and the whole world seems to hold its breath.
