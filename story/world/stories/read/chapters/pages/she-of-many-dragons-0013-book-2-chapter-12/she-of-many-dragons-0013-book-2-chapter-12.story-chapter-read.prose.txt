That night was harrowing, to say the least.

Prim was utterly spent from her long overuse of her illusion skill and lay draped over Alice’s shoulder, with her head and tail hanging loosely.

Alice held her steady as she walked through the forest. The ashen ground illuminated everything like snowfall, which made it easier to pick their way through but also easy to see.

Numi got the bright idea to walk behind and sweep Alice and Natura’s tracks away with her tail. Everyone else was on the wing.

Still, the gray searchers had not stopped, and Alice feared they were worryingly easy to spot from above.

At one point, Bella was certain she heard wing beats coming in their direction. The dark, starry night showed nothing. Alice was forced to shake Prim awake and ask her again to hide them.

Though exhausted, Prim was ever loyal and brave and went on to cloak them in another illusion. Sure enough, Alice and the others soon heard the whistling wing beats. They stayed in place until it passed overhead.

Iggy growled. “Alice, let me go up and ensure they learn to stay away from us.”

“No,” Alice said. “Not unless we’re directly threatened.”

“But I can chase them away.”

“You will not,” Bella piped up. “Look how determined they are—they're searching through the night. You won’t be able to stop them.”

“What are they searching for?” Numi asked, cocking her head to the side. She had posed this question several times during the night and offered solutions... but most had to do with a theory about a secret cache of treasure.

Bella shrugged her wings. “How am I supposed to know?”

They fell silent as the whistling wings passed overhead and then fell away as the dragon moved on.

Prim dropped the illusion and instantly fell back asleep. This happened twice more in the night.

Natura’s contribution was to point the way and ensure they didn’t accidentally walk in circles. She could sense the growth of healthy forests and kept them on the right path.

Ignoring her increasingly aching legs and exhaustion, Alice walked on. But her mind went back to the dragon’s discussion. She wished she knew what the gray searchers wanted. However, she wished more that she could ask without putting her other dragons at risk.

Somehow, she felt that the gray searchers were completely opaque to her. She had not been consciously aware of trying to sense them with her She of Many Dragons class, but now that she thought of it, it was as if the dragons were behind a wall of stone. They were completely cut off from her.

That in itself was unnerving.

Eventually, they spotted a dark line in the distance. The fire had not continued in this direction, leaving the trees at the end of the ashen zone undamaged.

Natura led them on without any error or hesitation, effortlessly winding them around unseen obstacles that lay beneath the ash. Looking back, Alice was certain that without her, they would never have made it.

Finally, they were under the cover of the trees, and Alice heard the quiet singing of crickets and the lonely call of a night bird. Oh, how she had missed those noises without even realizing it.

“Should we be on the lookout for tree wraiths?” Iggy demanded, staring around as if he suspected the trees of conspiring against them.

Natura shook her head. “No, the trees are healthy, and the water is good. I sense none of the sickness the wraiths bring here.” She sighed and flopped down where she stood, the ash that had been clinging to her puffing out on the green grass.

Alice did the same, leaning against Natura’s bulk. She was the largest of her dragons—still much too small to ride in the air, but she made a good backrest. And she was warm against the night’s chill.

Prim crawled down from her shoulder and settled on Alice’s lap while Bella curled by the crook of her neck. Even Iggy ceased his watch at the perimeter and came to drape himself as a warm band across Alice’s ankles.

Alice fell asleep almost instantly.

When she woke at dawn, she was both hungry and thirsty. Thirsty, though, she could deal with.

There was a babbling stream not too far away that had not been badly affected by the ash. As Natura promised, the water was sweet, and from the presence of tadpoles, there were none of those blood eels, either.

But the water did nothing for her hunger. Even worse, poor Bella was drooping. She’d had nothing to eat since hatching.

“Iggy,” Alice said, “could you hunt us a deer?”

Her red dragon perked up. “It will be my pleasure!” And he and Spark zipped off.

“Will it be long?” Bella asked wistfully.

Alice could only pat her. The little hatchling had not complained once, even though she must surely be hungrier than all the rest, just having come out of the egg.

Soon, Spark returned. Though he didn’t speak, he did rapid figure eights in the air from his sheer excitement.

Alice and everyone else rose and followed him through the trees. Not too far away, she found that Iggy had indeed downed a fine buck.

Bella spread her wings. “I must have some of that immediately!”

Alice nodded. “Do you think a hind leg will be enough?” she asked, almost laughing because the dragon was the size of a small kitten, and this buck was by no means small.

“Yes, a hind leg to start,” Bella replied, practically purring.

Alice took her belt knife and sawed off the leg at the top joint before she went about gutting and cleaning the rest of the carcass.

She fully expected Bella to ravenously dive in, but instead the dragon started to tug—rather ineffectually—at the leg as if to drag it away.

Wondering if she wanted to eat by herself, Alice said, “Dear, no one will steal your food.”

“It is not about that. I must have room for the ritual!”

“Ritual?” Alice repeated.

“Yes, my Burnt Sacrifice skill!”

She had forgotten that Bella had one more skill to explore. Curiously, she helped to drag the leg several feet away.

With one claw, Bella started to draw a circle around it. In fact, as Alice stood back to watch, she saw it was a perfect circle. The moment it connected, the leg burst into unnatural black and green flame that immediately consumed the fur, meat, and even bone. The ashes left behind flowed straight to Bella. She inhaled it greedily, and in a flash, she was no longer the size of a runty kitten but a half-grown cat.

“That is much better,” Bella sighed, sitting back. Her stomach was notably full.

Everyone stared at her. Prim was the first to speak. “What... What did you do?”

“I consumed its essence, of course,” Bella said. “Through my powerful skill.”

“Oh!” Numi said. “Much like my coins!” With that, she yanked a silver coin out of her stomach storage space and then re-popped it back in her mouth, chewing this time.

Bella looked mildly scandalized. “It is not like eating coins! Well—” She paused. “I suppose it is, in basics, but my way has fire and a fancy ritual. And now I need a nap.”

With that, she flitted to the middle of her ritual circle, which still glowed faintly from the heat of the unnatural flames, and curled up.

Alice and Prim exchanged a look.

Well, Alice thought, I did find her egg in a graveyard. She was always bound to be a little different from the others.

She went back to the carcass, finished gutting it, and tried not to watch as her very best friends descended on the entrails. Especially Prim who was absolutely famished thanks to her illusion work last night.

Alice preferred her meat cooked, so Iggy offered magnanimously, “I will roast it with my fire claws!”

Numi, who was happy with her coins, offered to keep watch and scuttled up one of the tall trees. She took a good look around and then soon returned.

“Huh. The gray searchers are still flying in that pattern of theirs.”

“That’s odd,” Alice said. “I wonder if they’ve rested at all. Is there any sign they are beginning to look elsewhere?”

“No, they’re still only over the burned area, but I’d imagine with the fire expanding, their search is as well.”

“Well, I’m glad they don’t seem interested in us,” Alice said and then continued butchering and portioning out the carcass. It was hard, bloody work but worth it for the taste of venison.

After their meal, everyone had seconds, and in some cases thirds. Alice was looking at the remains of the deer and trying to figure out how to best save the meat that was left when Numi cried out from her tree perch.

“One of the searchers has fallen!”

“What?” Alice asked, standing.

The other dragons at once flew upwards to look for themselves, even Bella, who had been startled awake. Alice, who had no wings and didn’t trust the thin branches enough to climb, had to wait for them to come back down for their report.

“It was near the border of the burned area, but south of us by a couple of miles,” Numi said. “So, I wasn’t worried about it since it had already gone back and forth a few times in searching lines. But then it staggered and fell from the air.”

“Did something hit it?” Alice asked.

“No, I don’t think so. Its wingbeats were slow. I think it was very tired.”

“The others do not seem to have noticed,” Iggy said. “They have continued flying without turning in this direction.”

“Or they do not care one has fallen,” Prim added, looking grim.

Bella’s eyes flashed in excitement. “Delightful! It is injured and all alone. We should go interrogate it.”

That thought hadn’t even crossed Alice’s mind, but... this would be an opportunity to get the questions she wanted.

She looked at the others.

Iggy, of course, was all for it and nodded so vigorously it made a wave effect down the length of his long body.

Numi shrugged, clearly not expecting any treasure coming out of this venture.

Prim said, “It did fall within easy walking distance.”

That settled it.

After another moment’s thought, Alice nodded. “I suppose we shall see what answers we can get from it.”
