That evening, after another day of labour, I was beginning to feel the effects of not sleeping. It wasn’t total exhaustion, but it felt like, back on Earth, when I’d get up for an early morning shift, then by the evening, I’d be pretty tired.

Pretty tired about summed it up. Deathly tired would be a stretch. About to fall asleep while standing would be a lie.

But Cuthward insisted that I should sleep, and I couldn’t keep working forever. Reluctantly, I agreed.

I returned to the great hall, where I’d left my equipment in a back room, and brought it to a tent outside. It was empty, with the soldier who put it up out on patrol, so I could finally relax without worrying about anyone finding me.

First, after eating, I opened my bag and retrieved my reading slate. I hadn’t checked it in a week, and now was as good of a time as any:

Name: Levi Gordon (ID#: DD-333)

Class: Monarch

Rank: Sergeant (SAP)

Tier: Gold

Vitality: 163(+30)

Agility: 188(+20)

Strength: 185(+35)

Perception: 119(+15)

Focus: 204(+45)

Presence: 205

Native Skills: Soulstealer’s Will (Advanced), Unseen Bolt (Advanced), Art: Mass Hammer (Expert), Will to Power (Expert), Memory Modification (Apprentice), Memory Palace Creation (Apprentice), Memory Palace Sorting (Apprentice), Perception (Apprentice)

I’d gained a few attributes, but no new skills since I’d last checked. It was to be expected.

Before I slept, I picked up my armour and rifled through my kit bag, until I retrieved a pouch of Labyrinth keepers and a pouch of topaz dust from the dragon.

I’d used my gravity Art a few times against the convoy, and though I was used to the sting of the Art overwhelming my leys, I did need to use the Labyrinth Keepers to sort them out sooner than later.

As I tapped out a few of the Keepers onto the palm of my hand, I dipped a thin metal needle into the pouch of topaz dust. The keepers were already trying to get to the dust, and I let them.

Targeting each of my resonance nodes, I injected a helping of dust into my body six times. I painted each node as a target for repairs, and the keepers scrambled to work, crawling through the holes and repairing the damage I’d done to the nodes. Any cracks, any chipping marble, they put back together, restoring the nodes to be as good as new.

It wasn’t instant, and the feeling of arcane ants crawling around beneath my skin made me squirm, but it wasn’t painful beyond the initial prick.

The bigger problem was my resources. I held open the pouch. I still had about three quarters of my crystal dust left, but that wouldn’t last forever. Soon, I’d have to either find another keeper topaz dragon, or I’d have to find another solution.

Or, I had to hope I won before then.

That probably wasn’t an option. I was going to need to use my Art almost constantly in the coming days.

I laid down on a heap of furs, ready to let myself sleep the concern away—what could I do about it right now?—but before I could, wings rustled outside, and a raven squawked.

I bolted upright. “Croak…”

Pulling myself up to my feet, I leaned outside the tent’s flap. Sure enough, the flash-raven stood outside, his beady eyes trained on me.

“I don’t have any snacks…” I whispered, holding my hands up—I’d set his expectations sooner than later.

Whether he actually understood what I was saying, or he just saw my empty hands, his head drooped. Still, when I held the tent’s flap open for him, he hopped in. A tiny scroll of parchment hung from his leg.

I opened it. As usual, it was a letter from Alis:

Dear Levi,

I’m pleased to hear about your successes in gathering new recruits, and I’m glad to hear you’re gathering more weapons. I appreciate you sharing with me, though I won’t bore you making you rehash every mission. Besides, I know you don’t have endless reams of paper.

As soon as I had received her letter telling me that I had, technically, been registered as an Ealdorman (and that, since my body hadn’t been burnt in the official ceremony, I was still technically the only one with a legitimate claim to the throne of this Domain), I had replied to tell her I was still alive.

We’d exchanged a few letters since then, but the gaps between them were growing longer.

The letter continued:

I apologize for making you wait. However, I must admit that there were complications. I have been moved to a room closer to the ground. There are more eyes on me now, and it is difficult to get Croak in and out of my room without attracting attention from my guards. This is practically a dungeon on the first floor of the palace.

My father has returned to the countryside, hoping to live somewhere more dry, where it will aid his cough, and most of the protections I would have received from him are gone. My suitors have narrowed to five candidates, and the Warlord is putting them through ceremonial hoops.

It must be done to give them a sense of legitimacy, because these men have not seen battle a day in their lives. I reminded him of this.

Alas, I cannot hold them off for much longer. Your continued survival warms my heart, but I long to see you again.

I am aware of how short a time it was that I truly spent with you, but I now know what I felt.

I travelled the nation looking for a friend, Levi, and for an escape from the duties thrust upon me. But when I found you, I hope I found more than a friend.

If you feel at all similarly, I beg of you: please give me hope, like you’ve given to your men.

Swear to me that you will come to Eraen-Kalora within the year. If you are true to your word, I would accept you as a suitor above any of the others who are attempting to court me. Though you’ve given me no gold or spices, these letters are more than they could ever do for me.

Longingly,

Alis

I read the letter over a few times, chest swimming with joy. My flowers had worked.

Take that, Brawl and Anchor. You guys thought I couldn’t do it.

Of course, I hadn’t successfully swayed the princess to my side yet. My ‘wooing’ wasn’t yet complete.

But damn it, the flowers must have helped.

I paced back and forth in the tent, trying to formulate a reply, then finally, reached into my kit bag and retrieved the scraps of parchment and plank of wood that I’d been using to write her letters while out in the field.

After a few more minutes of trying to come up with the best reply I could, I wrote:

Dear Alis,

I have been thinking long about how to reply, and I realize that there is no reason to dance around the point.

I would help you in a heartbeat. Our time together, and our letters, have only made me grow more fond of you.

Were I to travel to Eraen-Kalora right now, the Warlord would strike me down. When I reveal myself, I must be strong enough to defeat him. I will have to become an Emerald before then.

Given how long it can take to travel—remember the cars that I told you about? I’m afraid I’m still used to them—I’m struggling to put together a proper timeline. With the limit that you’ve given me, is it possible to reach Emerald in a year?

I can only think of one way to do it, but I have, so far only heard rumours. Once, you spoke to me of the Eye of Welkinmere, and how it faces the same point on the surface, always. Am I correct, now, saying that there is a tower there? And if one were to scale it, he would receive rewards beyond measure, which could, in theory, help him grow faster than normal?

Tell me this is true, and I would ascend the tower for you. I would reach its peak in your name, advance to Emerald, and return with the power to help you. Should I need guidance in ascending it…I would appreciate anything that you could give.

If it isn’t true, then I will still do everything in my power to help. Hope is hope, and there is still a chance of success, no matter how slim.

Love,

Levi.

I figured that was an appropriate ending to the letter. It was the first time I’d tried that, but Alis had made her intentions clear.

I rolled up the letter and gave it to Croak. Once it was secured to his leg, I sent him off. It was a risk to send letters, considering Alis’ current state, but I had to take it.

As soon as Croak had zipped away, turning into a speck against the night sky, I opened the flap of my tent, about to duck back inside.

But that was when a bell began tolling.
