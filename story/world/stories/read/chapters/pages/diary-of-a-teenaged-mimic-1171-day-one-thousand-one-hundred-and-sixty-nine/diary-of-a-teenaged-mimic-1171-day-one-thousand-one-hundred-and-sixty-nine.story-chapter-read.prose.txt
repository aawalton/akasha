Dear Diary,

Y'know, there's a trope that I saw over and over again back in the day, one I always had a real conflicted relationship with as a consumer of media. It's got a lot of names. 'It's always darkest before dawn' is how I heard it first, but I think the official Trope name is 'Darkest Hour'.

It's all about how the heroes are getting close to their limits, but also close to their goals, everything goes fuckin' sideways. The villains get reinforced. The big bad evil guy says 'this isn't even my final form'. The gun clicks empty. The sword breaks. The long, slow, painful slog to the finish line is cut short by something out of the blue.

The idea, of course, is to ramp up the tension right before the heroes win, so that their victory is that much sweeter. Unfortunately, shitty writers have done shitty things then pointed to that shitty trope as if that absolved them of anything.

The most common thing they fuck up, of course, is the ass pull. Fancy name is 'Deus Ex Machina'. God from a Machine. It's named after the device old Greek play directors would use to lower some dude dressed as a Deity onto the stage to fix all the shit the shitty playwright had fucked up. Usually, but not always, by painting their protagonist into a corner. Less frequently, but still often enough, by having the play clearly and accurately describe some way in which Ancient Greek culture was fucked, was fucking people over, but because that wasn't allowed, since the Gods made society the way it was and Gods couldn't possibly be wrong, the play had to end with the Deity confirming that yes, everything was set back to rights, the upstart usurper was no longer on the throne, the women were back in the kitchen and denied shoes, the slaves had been properly beaten, chained, and returned to their kennels, and all was right with the world.

But people who write that Darkest Hour shit and get it wrong love the Deus Ex Machina, because it makes them feel clever.

Some older writers really did do that kind of thing all the time. Especially some of them that were considered 'great writers', which always bugged me.

See, the thing is, the Darkest Hour trope really is kinda powerful. It really does ramp up that old tension, and maybe even makes a viewer think that the heroes have lost. They're just standing there, beat to shit, outnumbered beyond any reasonable chance of success, and the villains are winding up that last shot that will erase all hope for the future.

Shit, sometimes they even land it.

And then Hope shows up.

Thing is, when it's done right, it's not 'Deux Ex Machina' it's 'Surprising yet Inevitable'. Like everybody in the audience goes 'oh, shit, I forgot about that'. Whatever form Hope comes in, it's clearly something that's been stated to be there. It's not an ass pull so much as a callback. Sometimes it's not even that, it's just something that everybody knows can or even absolutely will happen, but in the heat of the moment everybody forgot about. The sun rises. Somebody quietly says, 'on your left'. Sudden Iowa Class Battleship.

Okay, yeah, I kinda pulled that directly out of my ass, but that is kinda my thing. Metal objects entering or exiting my ass, that is. Usually in ways I don't want them to.

Thing is, there are things people forget about Darkest Hours, especially when they are in any way literal.

Almost the flip side of the times I'd tried to disperse myself to my tentacles and wound up almost losing myself. Except this time it wasn't me tryna feed my Mind into incompatible tentacles, it was my ladies using my Mind, or maybe my brain meats, or whatever part of my Blended self passed for them, as a kind of repeater tower.

The dissociation didn't let me speak. I felt a little like I did in Mimic Dreams. Only my kids had never been in those dreams. Had never rushed over en masse and hugged me in those dreams. Had never been there making me feel all at once needed, and loved, and Worshipped, all while Menace looked me in the eyes and said, "we gad dis, Mama. Go do wad you godda do."

As noted, I was dissociating pretty hard, and one of the ways that takes me is not knowing shit. Not being able to articulate things, or track down the why of feelings. So while I had the impulse to be elsewhere as well, I wasn't.

Funny, I think I've always been an active part of my Revels before. Object, subject, participant, whatever, I was never really just an observer.

My ladies didn't take me there on my Altars, they just curled up behind me, lay against me, and though I felt them still working to arrange our fleet, I watched the goings on in my Temples as much or more than I watched what else each of them was doing.

Which, one by one, fell off to nothing but holding me. Holding me as I watched what my Revels were when they didn't focus on me.

Eating. Again, people bringing plates or platters of food from the bar or the restaurant, or folks moving tables to the overlook to share food and drink and conversation with the folks in the sanctuary.

Dancing, both in and around the seats, along the gentle part of the slope up to the Altar, in the clear spaces in the restaurant made by folks moving tables, and most especially in the bar, since that had plenty of floor space just for that.

Singing, both individuals singing to groups as much as to me, and small groups joining together to sing songs of camaraderie, community, and care. Some ballads, some love songs, some songs of solidarity.
