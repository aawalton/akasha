While Asher hadn’t had any sort of formal training with his weapon of choice, he still considered himself pretty decent when it came to fighting with his daggers. Stick the pointy end in your opponent repeatedly, or use the sharp edge to cut them somewhere they really didn't want to be cut. How much harder could it get?

As it turned out, the answer to that was very.

Asher started off the bout with a quick thrust of his wooden dagger through a tunnel of compressed space, deciding to see just how good his instructor truly was. The slayer guild had told him the man was one of their best, and seeing as few slayers actually managed to make it to retirement with all their limbs intact, Asher had been inclined to believe them. That said, few people were prepared for their opponent to stab them from ten feet away as if it were nothing.

Apparently, Mornamol was one of those few.

Mornamol’s own dagger somehow ended up directly in the path of Asher’s own, flicking it aside in an effortless parry and causing the strike to miss the man’s chest by mere inches. Undeterred, Asher used Distortion to lengthen the dagger into a sword, turning his wrist to try and slash at the man’s back with the blunted edge. Mornamol blocked the strike from behind with his other arm without even looking, revealing he was also dual wielding the wooden daggers.

“Curious skills you’ve got there,” the instructor laughed, watching Asher carefully as he waited for his next move. “Any other tricks I need to see?”

Pulling his arm back, Asher decided not to hold anything back. The world already knew about The Reaper and his Spatial element, so there really wasn’t any point in handicapping himself. In answer to Mornamol's challenge, Asher slipped into the astral realm, vanishing from sight.

To his credit, his instructor’s eyes only widened a hair before he dropped into a more serious stance and held his daggers at the ready. Asher calmly walked over and behind the man, thrusting forward with his dagger and exiting the astral at the last possible second. But to his shock, Mornmol’s hand shot out almost before he’d even started his attack, moving into the perfect position to parry Asher’s strike from behind his head.

Asher was so surprised he almost didn’t feel the poke in his gut, and he looked down, blinking at the hard tip of Mornamol’s second dagger lodged against his stomach. Somehow, without even turning around, the man had deflected Asher’s attack and even struck back in the same instant, as if he’d known exactly where Asher was going to appear and what he was going to do.

“Not bad, not bad at all,” Mornamol laughed, still having yet to turn around. “You know I only accepted this request from the guild because I thought it might be you? Few people have the shards to burn on dagger lessons, after all.”

Frowning, Asher decided the time for tentatively feeling out his instructor was over. He launched a wild barrage of attacks, leaning on Distortion to compress the space between him and his opponent and give Mornamol less time to react, or slipping into the astral in between strikes to dart through the man and strike from different angles. He was even wearing his Belt of Strength, shamelessly relying on the tripling of his strength to try and strike past the man’s seemingly effortless deflections that he made again and again.

In an actual fight, with Sever the Thread as an option, he’d call it a victory even if he just managed to land a single attack on his opponent, seeing as the vast majority of people would simply bleed out after the fact when they discovered the wounds he inflicted were eternal. All he needed to do was strike Mornamol one time in order to be satisfied with his performance.

He didn’t even come close.

Panting, Asher eventually stepped back, wiping the sweat from his brow as he stared at his opponent. Mornamol didn’t look the least bit tired, which wasn’t too surprising, seeing as he hadn’t tried to land a single attack beyond that first gentle stab into Asher’s stomach. While Asher certainly wasn’t going all out, or even using every trick in his arsenal, it didn’t seem to matter. No matter how hard Asher tried to hit him, or where he tried to strike, it was almost as if the man always knew precisely where the attack was going to land, even before Asher did. Finally, Asher called out.

“You have some sort of predictive skill, don’t you?”

“Indeed I do,” Mornamol laughed, tapping a wooden dagger against his cheek. “You’ll find that anyone crazy enough to specialize in daggers doesn’t tend to survive very long unless they are incredibly good at either dodging or deflecting. You’ve got your weird Spatial skill to help with dodging. I’ve got my own skill to help with both.”

“So you do know who I am?” Asher asked, having pretty much figured that out already from the man’s earlier comment. “If you knew who I was, why did you think I needed instruction with the dagger? Last I heard all the rumors were about how I’d killed off multiple master assassins and even a greater demon.”

“There’s a big difference between being good at killing things and being good at your weapon of choice,” Mornamol snorted. “Sure, I’d heard the rumors. But I also knew as a new Spatial element holder, you must have only been on our world for a few months now. I figured that meant you might have a rather low level of skill with weapons themselves. Just from our quick bout there I can tell you’ve got impressive skills and are good at moving your body, but you’re just using your daggers as a means of hurting your opponents.”

“Isn’t that what they’re for?” Asher asked, looking down at his wooden blades in confusion. “Isn’t that what all weapons are for?”

Rolling his eyes, Mornamol’s arm suddenly lashed out, throwing his dagger directly at Asher’s face. Instinctively, Asher flickered through the astral, allowing the wooden dagger to soar straight through him.

“Well, that’s not what I was hoping you’d do,” Mornamol admitted with a laugh. “Mind not doing that fancy vanishing trick this time?”

Not waiting for an answer, Mornamol threw his remaining dagger at Asher’s face once again. This time, Asher brought up his own and intercepted the attack, knocking the wooden dagger aside with relative ease, thanks to his enhanced strength. Nodding, Mornamol pointed at the dagger in Asher’s hand.

“Was that using the dagger to hurt me?” Mornamol asked, raising an eyebrow. “A dagger, like any weapon, is a tool. Its primary purpose may be to inflict damage upon our opponents, but like any good tool there are plenty of other things you can do with it beyond attacking. And this is where daggers tend to shine.”

Mornamol held up a wooden dagger, and Asher blinked, turning to double check that the two he’d thrown were still laying on the ground where they’d been thrown. They were, which meant he had no idea where the slayer was getting these daggers from. Seeing as they weren’t dissipating, they didn’t seem to be skill-based creations, but Asher didn’t have time to ponder how he was hiding them because Mornamol kept going.

“You said it yourself, daggers are small and lightweight,” Mornamol repeated.

“And you told me that was a bad thing,” Asher reminded him.

“It is. Except when it’s not,” Mornamol grinned. “Yes, there are plenty of downsides to that, but there are a few perks as well. Unlike those who wield heavy swords or more cumbersome hammers, we have the benefit of being able to adjust the direction and angle of our weapon with the slightest twitch of our wrists. That’s why learning how to properly thrust and hold the blade are so important. Controlling the angle of your attack or deflection is something no other weapon can pull off as well as a dagger can. You master the basics, and you combine that with your skills, and you can become a horrifying foe that few people would ever want to go up against.”

“I think I might have already hit that point,” Asher admitted. He wasn’t really one to toot his own horn, but in this case, he knew it to be true. The Reaper’s name was already spreading rather fast and wide after all he’d accomplished, and once he took down the King of Death, his reputation would only grow even further, whether he wanted it to or not.

“Perhaps. But I’ll tell you one thing. Not once did I ever hear anyone discussing how terrifying The Reaper’s skills with his weapons were,” Mornamol pointed out. “Sure, you’re as quiet as a ghost and as scary as a demon. But your skill with the blade? Nope, not a peep. I’m going to go out on a limb here and guess that at least one or two of your more famous fights ended without your opponent dying to your blade at all, didn't they?”

“Maybe…” Asher admitted, his mind flashing back to his encounter with Talon, the Finger in charge of Vildelph. After she’d broken both his arms in a surprise attack, he’d only managed to defeat her by burning one of the few vials of magadrine powder he’d collected from Eight’s workshop after killing him. He certainly hadn’t relied on his daggers to win that particular fight, but that was just one of many. “Still, that wasn’t the case for most of them!”

“I’m sure. And like I said, I have no doubt you’re a terrifying opponent to go up against when you’re truly attempting to kill someone,” Mornamol said, giving him a reassuring smile. “I’m just trying to point out that you have much to learn, if you’re willing to listen.”

“I mean, I already paid the shards,” Asher pointed out, growing more confused as Mornamol burst out laughing. “What’s so funny?”

“I know who you are, which also means I know how many shards you collected from selling that greater demon corpse the other day,” Mornamol said. “Sorry. It’s just that hearing you talk about shards as if paying a thousand of them would make you inclined to stick with something when you’ve got millions nestled away within your soul… I just thought it was amusing. You’re not the first amateur I’ve decided to try training, for the record. More often than not, they get frustrated when I tell them everything from the way they grip their daggers to the angle of their wrist when thrusting is wrong. I can teach you, but I can’t force you to correct your bad habits.”

“Trust me, I’m more than happy to have someone correcting whatever bad habits I may have picked up,” Asher admitted. “Like you said yourself, I’ve really only been at this for a few months. It’ll probably be easier than if I’d been wrongly fighting with daggers my whole life.”

“I doubt this will deter you, but we will be training with actual blades going forward as well,” Mornamol said, holding up a gleaming steel dagger that definitely hadn’t been in his hand a moment ago. “You’ll be responsible for supplying your own methods of healing.”

“That’s fine,” Asher nodded, making one of his own steel daggers fall into his hand with a quick flex of Personal Rift, mirroring his instructor. “Who’s responsible for your methods of healing?”

Mornamol simply grinned at the challenge, chuckling as he lowered his center of gravity and summoned another dagger into his off hand.

“We’ll get to the boring stuff soon enough, like correcting your stance and the abysmal way you hold your blades,” Mornamol said, a flicker of annoyance evident on his face as he narrowed his eyes at Asher’s hands. “But before that… I think it’s important I show you what a real master of daggers is capable of.”
