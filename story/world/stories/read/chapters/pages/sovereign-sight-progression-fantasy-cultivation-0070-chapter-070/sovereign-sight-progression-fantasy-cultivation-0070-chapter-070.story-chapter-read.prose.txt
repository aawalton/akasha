Salt and stone

All alone

Sink beneath the waves.

Stuffing his coin into his coat pocket, Harker turned to face the ball of white light. It hung above a section of heavy moss on a nearby hut, casting harsh shadows across the little village. The doors to the huts remained closed however.

“Jeremiah, if that’s you again, I’ll be reporting this to—oh.”

A twenty-foot swath of moss and vines was ripped violently from the side of the hut. It landed in a half-folded pile at the foot of a giant.

“A new arrival?” At least seven feet tall and wrapped in blue scholar’s robes, he towered above the waist-high wall and Harker alike. The light hovered just above his head, sharpening the shadows across his eyes and cheekbones. “Are you…lost, traveler?”

“I just might be.” Harker glanced at the nearest hut, noticing for the first time that the doors were bolted. From the outside. “I’m looking for the Lighthouse.”

“You’ve found it.” The big man’s blond, bearded face split into a soft, relieved smile. “For a moment, I was worried you were dead.”

Harker stilled. “Excuse me?”

“Nevermind. Tending the dry houses is a bit much sometimes.” He rubbed his hands on his blue robes, leaving smears of dirt and green juice behind. He offered his hand across the wall. "Well met and welcome, traveler. I’m Oswick Hammerhold, first rank scholar of Pellus’ Lighthouse."

Harker clasped hands. The giant's grip fully encompassed his forearm. “Dry houses? What is this village?”

“You must be from far off.” Bright blue eyes regarded him from behind round, copper wire spectacles. Oswick gestured and the harsh light winked out. “These are tombs for the unclaimed.”

Tombs. Harker swallowed. The green huts looked extra sinister without the illumination. What exactly had he witnessed? Were they really Drowned?

The Drowned were Talents that failed their Descents, only to return as something else. They were stories. Like the Eidhrin.

“I’m sorry if I worried you.” The big man waved his hand, chuckling uncomfortably. “Being down here for so long… It plays tricks on the mind.”

“Tricks. Right.” Harker sheathed his weapons. “Why are the tombs locked from the outside?”

“Grave robbers. Few are unwise enough to steal from the Ancestors’ hands, but it happens.”

Had they seen what Harker had, they would be far more than unwise. “Is that why you’re here? To scare off thieves?”

“Do I look intimidating?” The big man laughed. It was a kind sound. “No, I’m here to clean up.” He gestured back to the pile of moss and vines.

“I thought you were a scholar.”

"Even the Ancestors’ houses need tending.”

Harker supposed that was fair. He didn’t know the first thing about Keepers or their scholarly underlings. Still, he couldn’t keep his eyes from wandering back to the green stone huts. They stood eerily still in the half-light of the deep cavern.

“I found them unnerving too when I first arrived.” Oswick adjusted the wide belt around his waist. A number of metal items wrapped in rope were secured along with what looked like two full books. “But the unclaimed don’t have family to perform their funerary rites. The dead deserve their rest.”

Harker frowned. “Salt and stone beneath the tongue is easy enough to administer.”

Oswick scratched his jaw. “The Horizon’s awful close in the city—but don’t worry. The dry houses will take care of themselves. They’re well mazed with labyrinth seals.”

“I see.” Labyrinth seals were something he’d heard of, but hadn’t seen before. Something to do with refined salt and infusers.

The big man reached up for another sheet of the clinging moss. “The Lighthouse is up that cliff. Are you seeking Pellus’ light?”

"I'm just passing through.”

“Many are these days." A great heave tore another strip of moss down. It was oddly quiet, save for the rustle crash of it hitting the ground. "I didn't hear the river barge. Is the pilot still at the end of the dock? I'm certain she's hungry after the trip, I can send—"

"I wouldn't bother. I didn't take the barge."

"Oh." The big man looked into the mist, then back. His eyes swept across Harker before widening. "Pressure of the deep. You've met the Thrysalid."

Harker frowned. "You know him?"

"We've crossed paths." Behind his beard, the man's skin paled. "He does not usually travel this close to the city. Why—?”

“My business is my own.” Harker wasn't about to explain anything to random strangers in his path, no matter how kind they seemed. Duncan only wanted to help too.

"That is fair. I didn't mean to pry."

The big scholar was so earnest that Harker almost felt bad, like kicking a puppy. Before he could say more, his stomach growled.

"You sound like you could use a proper meal, at least. If you don't mind waiting, I can take you up to the Lighthouse for a late supper."

It was the first piece of good news Harker had heard that day. "That'd be fine with me."

The giant smiled and returned to the moss and vines. He piled them all into a conical stove made of green stone. He folded it all in, shoving with considerable strength until it fit.

The big man leaned closer, bent before the hatch. “Incandescence.”

Water flowed outward into an orb before Oswick’s outstretched hand. Blue-green flared into a brilliant white before condensing down into a scintillating spark. Almost immediately, a fire ignited within the stove, and he closed the hatch. The narrow top belched a thin line of pale smoke.

"Every two months, we have to pull the growth from these buildings," the scholar said, straightening up. "The moss eats away at the stone.”

Harker lifted his eyebrows. "I wasn't aware of a species that did that." He picked up a piece of moss from the ground. It wriggled slowly in his grip, like an insect. "Fascinating.”

“Isn't it just? It's not exactly alive, not like humans or animals. It just tries to latch onto whatever it can, then leaches out what makes it stand strong. Look."

The scholar took the scrap from Harker and squeezed it. When he opened his fingers, the moss was unharmed. Its fronds weren't even displaced. "This small and it's already absorbed some of the stone's resilience. Only fire will deal with it now." He tossed it absently into the cone's chimney. "Shall we get going?"

Harker watched the smoke drift out over the river before it vanished. "Lead on."

The big man picked up a walking stick and swung it forward, planting it ahead of himself with a deep thud. He stepped around the wall and onto the path toward the cliffs, and Harker saw his staff's bottom. It was a wide stone hammerhead.

Find the Hammer, Ironwine had said. Mission accomplished, I suppose.

The scholar led them down the path, footsteps crunching on the white stones that separated the green huts. More moss stretched across their backsides, some so much that the ground was almost indistinguishable from the roofs. Harker held his Sovereign Sight at max range, careful to watch for any of the doors opening. It took ten minutes to traverse the outbuildings, but he sensed nothing at all.

What was interesting, however, was that Oswick seemed ill at ease too. The big scholar gripped the haft of his hammer tight enough to whiten his knuckles and his shoulders rode high. Harker couldn’t help but wonder what the man had seen in his time among the tombs.

I doubt that hammer is for show.

It was a big weapon, easily as high as the man’s chest and set with a broken stone ring, much like the blue dagger. Scratches marred the wooden length of it, and the massive hammerhead was pitted and heavily carved with looping designs. It was a weapon made for war. Harker made sure he stuck close, well within the big man’s guard. The giant scholar seemed calm enough, but one swing of that hammer would crumple him.

The path inclined slightly, and the scholar planted the massive hammerhead into the mud step by step. Harker couldn’t help but marvel at his sheer physical strength. "I never caught your name, traveler.”

"...Harker Shoalborn."

Oswick nodded, but that was it. There was no reaction to hearing the surname of a foundling. Harker couldn’t help but think better of him for it. Even if he was lying, most people didn't bother to be so polite.

"Have you ever been in one of Pellus' Lighthouses?" the scholar asked.

"I haven't."

"Then you're in for a treat." Oswick stepped off the path, walking past the start of the switchback and headed toward the green rocks. Harker frowned, hands at his hands beside his knives. Hammer or not, he wasn't going to be caught unawares.

The big man stopped at an unremarkable stretch of stone. Silence stretched between the three of them—scholar, traveler, and cliff. It was followed by the soft sound of clicking, audible only through Harker’s Talent. He stepped closer, letting his range penetrate the cliff. The feel of metal slid across his skin, and the sound of it clinked against itself inside the rock face.

He fell back, just as the cliffside opened up, smooth as a whisper, revealing an iron gate that stretched across a rectangle of amber light. It spilled out over them both, a warm illumination that was still harsh against Harker's exposed Talent.

Nothing emerged, and he released his knives. It was just an empty metal box.

Oswick unlatched the iron gate, and the diamond pattern of it folded open smoothly before he climbed in, gesturing for Harker to follow. "It's called a lift. It will save us some time."

Harker stepped in carefully. His first assessment was correct. It was indeed a metal box, just far fancier than expected. The floor was laid with a tile mosaic, depicting an open book atop a lighthouse tower surrounded by a stormy sea. The walls were also decorated, though the metal was formed into an ornate lattice that sat a few inches in. The holes of it were cut into overlapping circles that resembled fish scales, or perhaps waves. Those overlapping circles converged at the ceiling, meeting at a pitched angle from which dangled a small lantern—the source of the amber glow.

The stone doors closed on greased hinges as Oswick secured the iron gate. He reached out, pulling a lever on a flat panel to the side of the door. A gurgling rush echoed through the lift, but it faded swiftly. The thing lurched upward, and Harker nearly fell over.

"That happens to everyone, the first time." Oswick had braced himself against his hammer. "The lift moves fast, but it's safe."

Harker stared around. It was a curious creation. A marvel, in fact, taken from a dream. What other wonders had been created out there in the wide world?

The lift was smooth, but there was the faintest shudder as it moved. Oswick said it moved fast, but Harker didn’t feel like he was moving much at all. He stepped closer to the wall, allowing the radius of his Talent to penetrate the metal outside of the enclosure. Immediately, his senses scraped across green stone walls that whipped by faster than an empty wagon down a hill.

Harker pulled back, narrowing his attention onto the lift itself. Inside the walls were a series of angular lines, similar to his dagger and Adhira’s axe. Water rushed through them, collecting into symbols that bent through one another like knotwork. At the center of each knot were small tidal gems, each one no bigger than his thumbnail. The lines spread outward toward the four corners of the lift, where a series of brackets, wheels, and narrow metal tracks were set into the stone. Other, far larger tidal gems were set into the walls, ensconced in steel and bronze. They were ten inches square and carved into incredibly complicated facets. One was even set beneath their feet, right under the center of the mosaic.

How much would something like this cost? Not only were the tidal gems huge, but they were clearly carrying a great deal of Water. In order to run a contraption like the lift, they would need it. Harker hadn't a clue what sort of forces they were using, but moving all of this metal and the pair of them would cost.

"I can feel you staring."

Harker looked up from the floor. Had the scholar sensed his Talent?

“Yes, it's a warhammer."

Harker blinked. What?

Oswick rested one hand against the broken circle at the end and pushed it, lifting the head up at an angle. "You're wondering why I have it.”

Harker collected himself. “Seems fairly obvious.”

“Does it?" Oswick gave a half smile, but his attention was elsewhere as if he weren't seeing Harker at all. "Most people don't think so."

Harker shrugged. "You use it like a walking stick. It's a bit overbuilt for that, but that’s not my business."

The big man let the hammer drop back down. He seemed surprised. “Yes. Exactly.”

Oswick adjusted his glasses and coughed. "You're a bit of an enigma, Harker. Is that why the Eidhrin are interested in you?"

Harker laughed softly. "I wouldn't say that.”

“Not just anyone attracts their attention. I was here for years before I ever saw the Thrysalid.”

“Perhaps I’m simply unlucky.”

Oswick chuckled. “I suppose. The Thrysalid doesn't ever bring people to our docks. Not alive ones, anyhow."

Harker's skin prickled. "He's brought you corpses? For the tombs?”

“Yes. Those found in the river or on the banks. It isn't often. We try to find out if they have any family, and if not we provide a proper burial."

"I see.”

“I only mention that because the Thrysalid is known to some of us. Those that handle the outbuildings and the lower docks, we respect him." Oswick cleared his throat. "Others do not."

Message received. Don't mention Ironwine to anyone else. He hadn’t planned on it. Talking about the Eidhrin outside of tall tales would attract attention.

"Almost immediately, you assumed he brought me here." Harker fiddled with the ring of his blue dagger. "How did you know I knew him?"

"Lucky guess."

Harker glanced up at the big man. "That can't be all."

Oswick sighed, but it didn't relieve the tightness across his shoulders. "There's a feeling when he's around. It's heavy. The others say I'm imagining it, but it clings to things the Thrysalid has given us. I can feel it on you a bit."

Harker frowned.

"It fades eventually. The pressure of the deep only lasts so long." Oswick smiled softly. “Something my father used to say.”

The lift slowed, and Harker's stomach lurched lightly.

"Ah, we're here," Oswick said.

Harker shifted his attention, looking at the tidal gem beneath his feet. The flow slowed, then altered. New lines lit up, intersecting with a strange, knot-like shape near the top corners. The lift stopped.

A pair of wooden external doors slid open, and Oswick folded the iron gate out of the way. "Welcome to the Lighthouse."
