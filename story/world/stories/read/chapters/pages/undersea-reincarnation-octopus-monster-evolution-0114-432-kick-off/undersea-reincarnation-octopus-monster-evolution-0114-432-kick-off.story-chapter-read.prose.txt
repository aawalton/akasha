“[You should probably use this time to take a breather,]” Henry said to Velistraine, glancing down at the two swords still tightly held in her grasp. She jumped, then gave him a tight nod before looking about. Sensing what she was thinking of, Henry summoned a small stream of water out of his Maw, which she used to wash the blood out of her eyes and ears.“[We’re not going back out there while they’re this aggravated,]” he continued over the voice of Maurice oohing and aahing over the images Henry was projecting. It had been a couple minutes since Henry had brought them back within one of his inflated and camouflaged Lifeblood Avatars. This one he’d filled with air, which he had pressurized within bone-wrought canisters before releasing here. Like his other three habitats, it was a few miles away from the road, but connected well enough to the network that moving about was still seamless to him.

“How about the ship near my spot? Is it still there?” Velistraine asked after a moment. She’d relaxed her grip, and even went back to holding a single sword in hand, the one still bloodied by the Stalker she’d decapitated. The fire was back in her eyes, and she clearly was itching to get back out there. Unfortunately, that would not work either. “No. That ship took off to join those,” he said, nodding his head at the illusion he was maintaining.

Unsurprisingly, the swarm was still giving chase, and every few seconds, a new ship would join the rest. It was unnerving how single-minded the creatures seemed to be. He was grateful that they weren’t that great at detecting his casting, or the entourage of Avatars he was using to support this push would have been made already. Though they could sense him if he got close enough. Henry was guessing it was his own mental signature they could pick up on. That would be the only thing that’d explain why nearly fifty Void Lords heads had suddenly snapped upward when his Arcane Avatar had flown too close.

Unwilling to spend too much attention on them, Henry converted the accidental discovery into an ambush, steering the Avatar right into their midst before blowing it up.

“Woah,” breathed Maurice as the whole habitat lit up at the massive, blue explosion, making Henry blink for a second. Spiteful Detonation was much more powerful when used by an Arcane Avatar when compared to a Lifeblood one. Then again, it made sense. Skills were supposed to be stronger when used by such clones.

More tools in the toolbox, he thought, most of his attention still on the accelerating pulses in his soul. Slowly, Henry lowered them all while the cores of nearly a hundred vaporized Void Lords settled into his spirit, bringing the upgrade of his Aspect ever closer. A small bone platform rose up, which he connected to the narrower walls. “Short break. I gotta focus on my upgrade,” he muttered, most of his attention on the Aspect that was pulsing more and more within his spirit. Even before he’d riled up the swarm, Henry knew his Aspect was on the brink of reaching its next step, all thanks to that first ship and its Stalker, but now, more and more power was trickling into his soul, and all of it was increasing the pressure within. At first, Henry thought it couldn’t hurt and well, it would be more fuel for the transformation. A reserve to make sure everything went well and whatever remained, he could use it to infuse his soul and body for a stat point or two.

He hadn’t expected Unfettered Echoes to draw everything into itself and keep asking for more. Henry knew for a fact that there was plenty to go around, but the Aspect kept inhaling everything he gave it, and with every drop that fell within that bucket, the Aspect blazed in his mind’s eye more and more. If he didn’t trigger the process himself, it would start without him.

With one last glance through the sentries he’d stuck to this specific habitat, Henry made sure nothing was creeping up on them and turned his focus inward, releasing his grip on the Aspect.

The process kicked off immediately.

Power bloomed in his soul and lit up the space around them, as if they were suddenly back in the lights of the Current and for a brief moment, Henry was glad he’d moved them all away from the road. Like a broken dam, the wave of power flooded out of wherever it had been tucked and down the greedy, dry soil that was the Aspect. It came in waves, each inhaled almost instantaneously by his spiritual organ. Henry didn’t know if every ounce of it was being consumed, but some of it might be escaping, because the lights around Henry were brightening every so often, as if he had a hidden fire within him somewhere. Still, most of it was going into his soul, where it saturated the Aspect itself and every one of the cornerstone skills that propped it up. Between Avatars of the All-Seeing Legion and the rest of the fertile ground that was the Aspect, Henry couldn’t tell which was consuming more.

But that was less interesting than what was happening outside.

Already, Henry could sense the change propagating out into the real world. Around him and around all of his Avatars, reality seemed to fuzz and double, as if seen through a fun-house mirror as the aura of Unfettered Echoes pulsed out. His connection to all his Avatars suddenly shone in his mind, the sights and sensations much clearer to him than they ever were. The network he’d established, which he had thought had been wide and large, now felt unnecessarily constricted. Like a coiled predator, he could feel that none of his limbs were as stretched as they could be, so Henry subconsciously stretched them out, sending his Avatars further and further out while the upgrade of the Aspect continued. Though already, the consequences of these phenomena were making themselves known, which, thanks to his yet again improved Octominds, was easy enough to keep an eye on without letting himself be distracted from what was taking place within his soul.

Quite a few of the creatures that had been giving him chase had suddenly come to a stop, sensing the spike of aura from the nearby Avatars, which was unfortunate, but at the end of the day, not that big of a deal. Not with the amount of mana the lead Avatar was drawing out of the hundreds of instances of Veilwatch’s Mark. That specific Avatar was already creating Arcane Avatars one after the other, so Henry didn’t mind throwing the ones that’d been detected into the fray, detonating them in the midst of the swarm.

That would make sure he had plenty more fuel for the process, and as the cores trickled in from the dead Void Lords, Henry focused back on the upgrade while the road was lit up with a couple dozen blue detonations of dark ichor and wild magic.

Less than a minute later, the upgrade was done. The Aspect and its cornerstone skills had drunk in more and more power, changing and evolving as whatever spiritual tissues made them soaked in the cores of hundreds of slain invaders and now… it quieted down. The light that’d been swimming around Henry had receded into a gentle, soft halo that was fizzling out by the second, and finally, Henry had his first A-rank Aspect, which felt…exhilarating. Sure, none of the description seemed to have changed, but he didn’t need a description to sense how powerful all of his Avatars had gotten. He could tell. As for the Aspect itself and the essence it was generating, well, he hadn’t tested it yet, but he could already sense the difference. If at B-rank the conceptual essence was like watery, airy substance, now it was like honey, thick and potent, containing limitless potential.

It felt amazing, and the first thing he did with this fresh upgrade was sending his senses toward the Avatars he’d left in the Reach.

For a few seconds, Henry sensed nothing, which… while a little disappointing, he knew he could eventually change. But then he caught it. The smallest, weakest flicker of something way past the edge of his reach. It was like trying to see a faraway speck that you could only see from a certain angle and under a certain light, but it was there, and as the seconds ticked by, Henry caught those flickers of presence a few more times, confirming that he wasn’t imagining it, and a chuckle escaped his throat, which had his two companions turn to him with questioning looks.

He could see their way back home. It was out of his reach for now, but it was right there. He just needed plenty of cores and blood to grow the Aspect and give it enough power to re-establish the link, and well, there were plenty of enemies around. A sea of them that only kept swelling as more and more ships approached his little team of vanguard Avatars.

Henry was looking forward to testing his other capabilities, especially now that in both the sight of his scout and the image he was still projecting, a giant, massive door had come into view.

This ought to be an interesting couple of days.
