Erin, the demoness at the Delver’s guild stared at Damien with a mix of surprise, apprehension and sadness that widened her eyes and twisted her mouth.

Risha looked between the demons, wondering why she was so shocked.

“Say that again.” Erin said, her voice hard.

“Take us downstairs.” Damien repeated, all joking gone from his voice.

Erin looked away from him in confusion, she looked at Risha for a moment, then continued to Siyah, where her gaze stopped.

Erin’s eyes glowed as she leaned forward, studying Siyah. After a tense minute, she leaned back, her eyes going back to normal. “Very well, but the Goblin stays.”

Damien nodded, and looked at Risha, “We’ll be gone for a while.” He walked past Erin towards one of the doors in the back, motioning for Siyah to follow without looking at her.

Siyah gave a worried look to Risha, and Risha moved her head, motioning for the Demoness to follow the confident Demon.

The three Demons disappeared behind the door, and Risha turned to where Klu and Klaz’zks were talking with several Delvers at one of the tables.

She walked over to them, and sat down with them, pleasantly surprised to see a game of tiles being played similar to the ones she’d seen the other Grand Mother’s playing, except these ones were made from some kind of plant fibers.

And there were shinies being bet.

Risha smiled wide.

“So all three of you are joining?” One of the Delvers asked.

““No.”” Both Klu and Klaz’zks said at the same time. Klaz’zks glanced at Risha, then quickly looked away.

Risha pulled some shinies out of her pocket. “Risha is playing!

The Delvers shared a look, then smiled at each other. The one who’d first spoken dealt out the cards, and Risha took them, her wide smile growing sharper.

As they played, the Delver’s smiles slowly disappeared, and Risha’s pile of shinies grew larger and larger. A crowd slowly gathered around their table, watching the game, and Risha hummed a small tune to herself as she pulled yet another pile of shinies to herself.

“We never should have taught you this game.”

Risha paused, only now noticing the person who stood directly behind her chair.

Klaz’zks stood up, and Klu reached for his sword as they noticed her as well. Risha shook her head at the two, then she looked up into the beautiful black skin, and pointed ears of a Dark Elf. She wore a hood to hide her features, but Risha could see through it, unlike when she’d first met Damien.

“You should play.” Risha said.

The woman smiled as she walked over to one of the occupied chairs. The Delver, another Dark Elf, stood up, giving her the seat.

She sat down languidly, crossing her legs as she leaned back as if the chair was the most comfortable of thrones. “Deal me in.”

Risha noticed the odd amount of armed and armored Dark Elves in the room now that she was paying attention. Most Delvers were armed, but the difference in quality and spirit was noticeable for Grand Mother Maya’s personal guard.

The Delver who’d started the game looked at the pile of shinies Maya had commandeered from the other Dark Elf, but said nothing as he dealt the cards.

Risha couldn’t help her happiness at being physically with the other Mother. Unlike when she’d met Cala, Maya’s presence was undetectable until you looked at her, and then…

It was like the greatest of predators hiding in the shadows, the fangs of a spider at your neck, already caught in her web without realizing. Risha could only see the smallest glimpses of her spirit, hidden behind a shroud that Risha couldn’t pierce.

Kliks moved in Risha’s hair, peaking out to stare at the other Mother.

“Risha thought you were too busy to see her.” Risha said as she pushed some shinies forward.

“I made some time.” Maya smiled, “How are you enjoying the City Of Freerala?”

“I don’t like it.” Risha said, her gaze meeting the other Mother’s across the table.

“Oh?”

“So many live without food, or homes.”

“So many demons, you mean.”

Risha narrowed her eyes.

Maya spread her hands, “I speak the truth. I have done my best, but I do not rule this city, and my people are not as accepting as yours.”

Risha looked at her tiles, her gaze going past them. “How did they end up here?”

“They were devastated by the wars, their highest level warriors slaughtered after the death of their mother… after we lost. They sought refuge where they could. Erin is one of the few with the power to protect them, but she does not leave her guild, and the dwarves strip her political power where they can.”

Risha looked up, “You know Erin?”

“Yes. I couldn’t forget her if I wanted to.” Maya laughed as another card was dealt. “And it seems I’ve lost this round.”

Risha took the shinies, staring at the many sparkles that filled her vision.

“So, are you going to do something about them, the demons?”

Risha looked up, and tilted her head, “In a way.”

“Oh?” Maya gave a small smile, “What do you mean by that?”

Risha looked away, searching for another topic. She trusted Maya, but it wasn’t time to tell her until Siyah had fully inherited Freera’s power. “Why can’t you forget Erin?”

Maya’s smile disappeared, “She gave me a… lesson, when I was younger.” She looked at the Delver who was incredibly confused by the conversation, “Deal.”

He straightened and immediately started another hand.

Risha leaned forward, wondering what kind of lesson would affect the other Grand Mother like that.

“The Dwarf Lord has noticed you.” Maya looked over the top of her cards. “You weren’t exactly being subtle.”

Risha blinked in surprise at the change in conversation. “Risha won’t bother him.”

“Oh, but you already have.” Maya played one of her cards, and drew another.

Risha frowned as she answered with her own card.  “I haven’t even talked to a dwarf.”

“No, but that little gathering of demons you had yesterday didn’t go unnoticed.”

Risha frowned, not sure what she was talking about. She’d been gone with Damien the entire day until… she came home to Siyah in front of a large crowd of demons.

Hm.

Risha wasn’t sure why that would bother the dwarf, Siyah had just been singing and telling stories, and Klaz’zks had told her that Risha’s people had helped feed the gathering. She began to suspect why the dwarf didn’t like the gathering, but she wanted to be wrong. “Why would that bother the dwarf lord?”

Maya watched her for a long second before answering, “Power. A people divided are no threat, but even the highest leveled Dark Elf is vulnerable if enough of her people turn against her. As I said, the dwarves have been limiting Erin’s political power at every opportunity, if her people rally under her, then they are a threat to the ruling lords.”

Risha clenched her hands, her short claws digging into the cards she held. The look on the face of the Delver who owned the deck stopped her from completely destroying the cards.

She set them down, and met the eyes of the Dark Elf who watched her reaction impassively.

“You’ve already led one revolution, Risha, what’s to stop you from leading another, in their minds?”

“Risha just wants people to be free.” Risha felt tears gather to her eyes as the mixture of anger and sadness swirled in her stomach. “To be fed. To not have to worry that their brothers and sisters will be killed in front of them. Why does such hope come with so much weight? Why can’t this idiotic dwarf realize that I am no threat to him, as long as he does what is right?”

Maya set down her own cards, a tightness to her lips. “Because, yours is a rare gift. People with power lose sight of everything but that power. We live in a world where death follows in our footsteps, no matter which path we walk. Soon enough, it is easier to see death and evil without care, than to fight for what is right.”

“No, Maya. It isn’t power that blinds.” Risha said, her teeth showing as he claws dug into her palm, “It is weakness. It is fear. If this dwarf fears me to the point of blindness, then Risha welcomes him. Risha welcomes the change that terrifies him.”

Maya held up her hand, shaking her head, “Calm your anger. We aren’t there yet, Mother Risha. He is still watching and waiting. Don’t make an enemy where you don’t need one.”

Risha glared at the other Mother for a long moment, but slowly her anger filtered away, leaving a hollow ache in her that made her want to cry. She slumped into her chair.

“It has been a long year.” Risha said quietly.

Maya stood, and walked over to Risha. Klu stood, so that she could take his spot, and Grand Mother Maya sat next to Risha, her scent washing over the tired goblin.

She smelled softly sweet, a scent that Risha didn’t recognize, but still brought her comfort.

Maya then gently pulled Risha to her and held her. “I have wanted to hold you for a long time, little one. You have suffered, and it is okay to face that.”

Risha turned into her shoulder, and let out silent tears, hidden by the woman’s clothing.

After a few minutes, she pulled away, a soft “thank you.” escaping her.

“Of course.”

The sound of a door opening brought their attention to the back, where three demons emerged. Around Siyah’s neck, was a simple necklace with a circular pendant. At her side, was a new sword wrapped in cloth, and her face held a contemplative look.

Mother Maya stood up, her gaze going from the pendant to Brother Damien, who watched the Dark Elf Mother with a wary gaze.

Maya let out a laugh. “Oh, Risha. I see why you’ve been so secretive.” She gave Risha a conspiratorial look, “Shyanne will have a fit when she finds out. Please do invite me when you tell her.”

The Dark Elf Mother then bowed her head to Damien and Siyah. “Good luck to the three of you. It seems that it is time for me to take my leave. I’ll need to prepare more than I thought.”

With that said, she walked towards the exit, several Dark Elves in the room slipping into formation behind and in front of her.

Risha noticed the dealer slipping away from the table, but said nothing as he followed the Mother out.

Damien watched the door for a second, then looked over to Risha.

Risha gave a guilty smile.
