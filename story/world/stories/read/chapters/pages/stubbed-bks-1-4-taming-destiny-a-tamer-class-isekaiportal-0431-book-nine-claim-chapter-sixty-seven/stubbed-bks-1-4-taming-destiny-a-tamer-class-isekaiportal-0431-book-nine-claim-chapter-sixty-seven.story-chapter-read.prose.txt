When we wake in the morning, we all go together to the market to get breakfast. I want to make sure that River eats something, and I'm not blind enough to ignore that Thessalanor's words had some truth to them as well.

In the District One marketplace around the well, we go to one of the stands that the kitchens have set up, joining the queue just like everyone else. The first couple of days, the kitchen staff were visibly uncomfortable at serving me with everyone else, and the others in the queue tried to give way to me. But I insisted they just treat me like everyone else – I wanted my people to see that no one was getting any special treatment.

The rest of the lords grumbled about it, of course, especially when I also insisted that I be offered no better fare than the rest of the settlement – with my example they could hardly do anything different, though Elarion clearly was considering it. But they came around, and by this point, six days in, everyone is more worried about other matters than standing on rank.

I reach the front of the queue.

“Elite Classer,” I tell the server quietly. Her hand jerks a little, pulling back a moment later from the ladle she was going to use. Instead, it goes to a different ladle and she fills my bowl with a greenish-looking slop that is more vibrant than the other similar soups she’s been serving.

I thank the server who bows deeply, murmuring ‘my lord,’ and then I step to the side and let River get her share – from the pot with less vibrant green soup. We then go and join the rest of our companions who are sitting on or near a bench that has become ‘our’ section of the communal tables. That’s the only concession to rank I’ve agreed to – having enough space for everyone to eat together is important for me. The other lords often join me, though I don’t see them present today.

Eating fills a hole that I’d been doing my best to ignore – just because I don’t necessarily need to eat every day doesn’t mean it feels good to forgo meals – and chatting with my loved ones helps me relax in other ways. Thessalanor was right, I can’t help but think. And looking at River, I see the meal is doing her good too.

The difference between our food isn’t special treatment, at least not in the way some might expect. Instead, it’s different because we’ve noticed over the last tenday or so that the lords are significantly less affected than the Common Class soldiers even when consuming the same amount of Energy-imbued plant matter. And Classers are less affected than non-Classers. The food booth we went to is one of those for Classers specifically as the food contains some untreated local plant matter. The more vibrant green soup I ate just has more of the untreated vegetation in than even the usual Classer pot.

I theorise that it’s linked to the amount of Energy we have to gather to level-up – it seems the higher our level or the rarer our Class, the more resistant we are to Energy poisoning. Perhaps our bodies adapt to more Energy moving through them or something like that. We haven’t done enough testing to be sure, but it has become increasingly evident that those with rarer Classes or higher levels have more tolerance to the local plants.

Unfortunately, eating Energy-imbued food doesn't seem to have significantly increased my rate of progress towards my next level. I have made progress, but it’s not like when I was consuming fragments of an Energy heart. I suspect it's to do with the Energy in the plants being less accessible than that in an Energy Heart or a Core. It makes me wonder whether herbivores have a better way of processing it, in the same way that eating grass raw would be technically possible for me without poisoning me, but I wouldn't absorb nearly as much nutrition from it as a herbivore would. Perhaps something similar applies to Energy.

Once we’re finished eating, I walk with River towards the healer's compound, but as we leave the canteen I notice something that makes me perk up.

"Can you hear that?" I ask River.

She tilts her head to one side.

"No," she answers out loud, with a hint of confusion.

"Precisely," I tell her with an excited grin. “The sound of lightning has been near-constant over the last seven days, yet now I can't hear it at all.”

She turns to look at me, excitement rippling over the Bond between us.

"Do you think the storm has finished?"

"I think it's worth checking out. Would you like to come with me?"

She hesitates, glances towards the healer's compound, then reluctantly shakes her head.

"I'd better get back to my work. I've been away too long as it is."

I feel a slight pang of disappointment, but I paste a smile on. As much as I enjoy her company, she has other responsibilities now too. And it’s not like I can complain about that – so do I.

"Alright. I'll see you later. If you're not out of there by dinner, I'm coming to find you," I half-threaten, half-promise.

She tilts her chin up slightly.

"I'll do my best to remember," she replies, then brushes her cheek against mine affectionately and disappears into the healer's compound.

I stride off towards the gate. Halfway there, I hear footsteps pattering against the stone behind me. I turn to see Tiria approaching at a run, her face flushed with exertion and excitement in her eyes.

"Do you think the storm is over?" she pants, drawing level with me.

"Breathe, Tiria," I suggest with some amusement. "Even if it has, I'm sure the world won’t disappear just because you take a few more flames to get there." I slow my pace obligingly so she can join me if she wants to without needing to hurry to keep up.

She nods and takes a few forced breaths. She's clearly added at least some points to Constitution, as she recovers significantly faster than most people would unless they were very fit, which, given her slight build, she most definitely isn't. She has plenty of curves, but they’re not really muscular ones.

She catches me looking and blushes. I quickly glance away, realising I'd been letting my eyes linger longer than I intended, and clear my throat.

"So…did you hear from other mages that the manastorm had stopped, or did you notice the silence, as I did?"

"Neither," she replies cheerfully, grabbing onto the new subject with enthusiasm. "I was running an experiment. I woke up to find that the instrument I use to measure ambient Energy had dropped significantly, indicating that the storm may have passed, or at least be in its later stages. I want to take readings from just outside the cavern, since those will be more precise than ones taken inside."

"I haven't noticed much change in the ambient Energy myself," I respond with curiosity.

Tiria shrugs briskly.

"You mean through your status screen?" She clarifies, and I nod. "That is a blunt instrument," she tells me, a little disdainfully. "My instruments are far more delicate and capable of reading even slight changes in ambient energy – and they don't take marks to start showing results, either."

I have to admit I hadn't noticed that status screens took a long time to show results, but possibly that's just because I had nothing to compare them to. She's the magical researcher. If she says her instruments are more accurate, I'll take her word for it. Speaking of readings….

"How are your investigations going with Loran? Have you been able to determine what effect the Energy-imbued food is having on him – is it causing him any difficulties?" I ask, deciding to take advantage of the opportunity to catch up on how she's been approaching the food situation.

I probably should have set some time aside to examine Loran's internal matrix myself, but with everything that's been happening, the most we've managed is brief catch-ups on the affairs of the settlement. Fortunately, Loran can now see his own internal matrix, and I've pointed out things he should watch for that might signal problems.

"We've seen increased growth," Tiria reports. "And it seems like eating Energy-imbued food has a different effect on him from eating Cores, though we haven’t seen any signs of Energy-poisoning yet."

"How have you been keeping track of it?" I ask curiously. "You can't see his internal matrix directly, I presume? Or have you made some progress on projecting it so that others can see it?" I know that was something she and Keshaka had been working on – an alternative to needing a Dominate Bond that I whole-heartedly support.

A disgruntled expression crosses her face.

"Unfortunately not. It hasn’t been helped that we’ve needed to focus more on this whole food-supply matter, and that Keshaka has been working with the farmers a great deal – they’re trying to investigate whether one of her machine designs might be repurposed. We’ve been reduced to mundane methods – Loran has been drawing his internal matrix for me and showing how it's been changing from day to day. Ever since he stopped consuming Cores and switched to the Energy-imbued food exclusively, his Core has only grown in a single area."

"I'll have to take a look myself," I decide with some concern, "and make sure it's not having an adverse effect on his matrix or his body." Given how balanced Loran’s internal matrix was the last time I saw it, one area growing drastically differently from others sounds potentially risky to me.

Tiria nods.

"That would be ideal. More data points would be valuable for our research."

"And you?" I ask. "Have you made any progress accessing your own internal matrix?" I finally caved and gave her the Meditation Skill in exchange for a written agreement that she will never use it to work actively against myself or my House. I felt a bit bad about how quickly she agreed to that – she’d clearly not had any thoughts of doing so, making it an easy choice.

Her thoughtful expression shifts back to dissatisfied.

"No," she admits reluctantly. "I haven't had much time to test," she adds quickly. "I'm sure I'll get there eventually."

I nod knowingly. It's quite possible she will, eventually, but I suspect her difficulty with using Meditation to find her internal Core space is the same as Nicholas's was, only more pronounced.

Nicholas's struggles stemmed from years of thinking and doing things in a particular way. Tiria hasn't had as many years to entrench her thinking, but she has a scientist's mind that constantly demands how and why – and both of those make it difficult to simply relax and sink into one's inner being.

I don't say any of this, though. I've already offered once to use a Dominate Bond to help her find her Core space. To offer again might feel like pressure, and that's the last thing I want.

Tiria looks mildly relieved when we reach the gates a moment later, which spares her from having to say more about her struggles with seeing her internal matrix. We climb up onto the top of the wall and find the peephole that was left open when we sealed off the rest of the cavern. A group of guards is already clustered around it, but they quickly part as we approach.

"My lord," I hear murmured from several directions, accompanied by bows.

The sergeant currently in charge of the guard steps forward.

"Have you come to see the storm, my lord?" he asks, with a hint of excitement in his voice.

"Has it stopped?" I ask.

"It seems so, my lord. We haven't seen any lightning for the last few marks, and the dawn is brighter today than it's been for a while."

Given that clouds have blocked out the light for the entire duration of the storm, that's a promising sign. Still, I want to see for myself. The soldiers willingly make space as I step up to the peephole, Tiria following close behind, at least as eager as I am.

We peer through. The overhang of rock beyond the wall makes it hard to see much of the sky, but, sure enough, there are rays of light falling on the forest that spreads out from the base of the hill in which we are embedded.

At first glance the world seems unchanged, but on closer inspection I can see sections of forest that have shifted colour, disappeared entirely, or grown significantly larger than the trees around them. I can also hear noises from the forest that sound more agitated than before. I remember what Josiah said about how animals are often fractious in the wake of a manastorm – he warned that the fishermen had to take care whenever they took their boats out afterwards, never knowing what creatures might lurk in its wake.

Given our food situation, though, I fear we can't be as cautious as I'd like. We need fresh meat and fish, and we'd better hope neither has been too badly mutated by the energies flying around during the storm.

I draw back from the peephole and find that Tiria is still enraptured.

"Are we opening the gates, my lord?" the sergeant asks eagerly. I don’t blame him for being restless, but we need to be careful about it.

I glance back through the peephole.

"We'll send a small party first," I decide. "See what's changed, see what new dangers might have appeared. We’ll decide whether to open the gates properly after they return."

"Aye, my lord," the sergeant agrees readily. "Shall I choose a party from the men here, or do you have others in mind?"

I look around and see all the guards itching to get out of here. I weigh up calling for someone else and then dismiss it – honestly, I want to know as soon as possible too and these soldiers are as good as any others.

"All right, Sergeant. Choose five of your best, at least three of them Classers. They can go into the forest on a scouting trip. Only a little into the forest, mind – and take no risks. Our biggest need is to send hunters into the forest again. Whether we can or not would be the most useful information the scouts can bring back."

"Aye, my lord," the sergeant agrees, and turns to his men, several of whom are displaying barely-hidden excitement and relief.

I tune out his selection process and reach out to several of my Bonded. The soldiers won't be the only ones doing some scouting today.
