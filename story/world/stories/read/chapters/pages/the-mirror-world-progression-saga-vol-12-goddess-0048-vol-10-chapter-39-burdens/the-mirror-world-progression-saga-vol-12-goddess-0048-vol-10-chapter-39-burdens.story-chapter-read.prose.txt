The man called Odysseus, formerly Palthennius, in his mind still ‘Palth,’ walked with his common horse. He was separated and given a wide berth, yet nonetheless journeyed with and amongst soldiers and officers of uncommon variety as far as the eye could see. He was just another member, a thus almost invisible warrior of a great horde bound north, headed to bring war and destruction to his former masters. Headed to Matrazere.

He hoped he’d merely face his former countrymen by then. It might be more difficult than putting down undead, but a portion would survive, a portion would merely be taken captive. Or the Pantheon forces would fail — much more likely — and perhaps even fewer common folk would die. Eventually, they could all go home, and the enemy would retreat, perhaps to make a treaty and new borders somewhere between Rivermount and Matrazere.

Wishful thinking. I will slaughter men regardless. Me or former men. That is simply what I do.

It was a bitter thought. Once, he’d thought much differently. A more mercenary perspective had crept up on him year after year as he lost his false righteousness.

Unfortunately, he couldn’t dare expect to fall in battle. He was particularly good at surviving them. Perhaps he’d have some legendary moment to hold off the ‘enemy’ with his life, but he’d done this before and still lived. On top of it, he had every reason to go in with [True Clone] to begin with, so that would need to die first. He planned to save the revelation of this ability until the time came, but it was foolhardy not to reveal it at some point. His new allies could protect his body.

If he had revealed the ability to the Dominion, they’d likely have him locked away somewhere like an Inquisitor, running a puppet from afar for the rest of his career. This group? He wondered.

The contract he’d signed, and the oaths he’d made, even to a man like him… they’d been so strangely even and fair. A man like him, who had not a single excuse to complain about literal slavery compared to what he deserved, from their perspective — and maybe even his.

Maybe they’ll let me decide my own fate. Time will tell, after all the fallout. Odds are, I’ll be there to suffer it.

The day was waning, and the pace was temporarily slower for the armies. They’d made good time under enchantments through most of the day, and were heading for a planned, defensible camp for the night.

Though he kept alert and his eyes peeled, he didn’t think there was even a passing chance of an ambush. For one, he was stuck relatively in the middle, near the command structure itself, together with a loose band of mercenary elites, mostly Southlanders. Men who were fine quietly soaking up significant coin for very little work, as glorified bodyguards. The coin was extensive because of their vetted loyalty, reliability, and discretion.

And… the Dominion would still be reeling from what he’d done, focused on sucking everything inward to contain, even as they perhaps still held a last sliver of hope of finding him. Would they expect him there, riding with the enemy? He found it unlikely. They likely wouldn’t think a Heaven Knight capable of such betrayal, nor foolhardiness.

He had considered fading away north, attempting rebellion elsewhere. But the issue was that he’d been on the other side of that far too many times. He’d crushed hopes and dreams under his boot in their name, and seen how thorough the Inquisitors were at rooting such notions out, just in the extent of intel he’d sometimes been privy to, to do his job serving them.

He knew, even before he tried in Matrazere, of all places. He’d had to try anyway. That didn’t follow for trying such pointlessness again, elsewhere. And so he’d gone for the only prayer he had of stopping their unthinkably heinous act.

And here he was. His prayer wasn’t exactly answered, but the ‘genie’ was out of the bottle and trying her hand at it. Meanwhile, he rode to do what he did best, regardless. Fight. He’d throw and break himself anew on the walls of the Unbroken Fortress. He’d at least let them know he was there, know he defied what it stood for. He hadn’t truly understood what that was until now. He’d been mistaken.

It stands in ultimate cowardice.

He became aware of someone trying to sneak up on him. He let his senses hone in discreetly, giving no tells, pretending he was still brooding — as he had to admit, he generally was always doing.

They were invisible and silent, which was generally very good for not being noticed. Despite this, Palth tracked them with ease and even recognized them by their definitive shape. It was then that he established there was no threat. None immediate, anyway. In fact, he felt strangely glad for the visit. The sneaking was somehow… cute.

As the figure snuck up, right as their throat showed signs of creating speech, Palth called out softly, “Hello again, Azure.”

There was a near stumble suddenly, and a strangled sound, then a furiously whispered, “Holy hells, man, how do you do that?!”

With a faint grin, Palth replied, “Trade secret.”

“Ach. I bet.” The invisible figure moved more easily to walk beside him. “At first, I was just going to come disguised, but then I realized I could test how well I could assassinate you, if needed.” A conversational, cheery tone for such a statement, and one he decided was teasing. “This result is a real shame.”

“No doubt. If you like, I’ll lie down for you and close my eyes for a coup de grace, once Matrazere is over with.”

Azure made a sound in her throat. “Mmn. Tempting.” The word was sultriness itself trying to restrain itself. “Ahem! I'd better watch it. My betrothed wouldn’t approve of flirting.”

Palth blinked. He hadn’t intended any innuendo. On point, his mind involuntarily flashed to that momentary flash of her nudity during the aerial battle. He felt a flush. Just like a succubus from cautionary tales, isn’t she? “I certainly don’t want to get on her bad side, either.”

“I hope you realize the irony in you saying that.”

“I don’t know what you mean. I am but a humble mercenary. You can call me Oddy, by the way.”

There was a brief silence as they divided to walk around a big pile of horse shit — a silence relative to them anyway, as the environment constantly announced the sounds of an army on the move. Horses, the shouts of men, the creaks of wagons.

“I just wanted to clear the air, I suppose,” Azure said. “We were trying to kill each other once. Oddy.”

“Water under the bridge,” Palth said easily. “I suppose I seem a man prone to grudges at this point, but there is none between us. I serve the Goddess and the Pantheon, and I serve you.” The words remained easy, even as he intrinsically felt a wrongness in admitted service to ‘devils.’ He could nonetheless accept it because he accepted he was damned. Damnation for one man had to be worth what he aimed to stop. “I will fight beside you, and I will protect you with my life if it comes down to it. So I’ve sworn.”

Not that an oathbreaker’s word means much, but that’s what the contract is for, after all.

“Likewise,” Azure replied, then sighed. She seemed to be struggling for words. “And I wanted to say… sorry. Sorry for your men. I really am. I carry the regret, the guilt, after the fact, for what we’ve done, what we’re doing, even as I find hate in the moment. Just… hells, we’re not doing this to cause suffering. It’s the opposite.”

Despite himself, Palth found himself looking over. Naturally, he could see nothing, so his eyes shifted back forward. He knew what she meant: the men killed in the aerial battle. His elite companions. “The blame is pointless. It was their duty to die. No different than mine — I’m just longer in doing it. I had little time to mourn them, but I did in my way. It’s not the first time I survived alone among the pack. As for hate and regret, that’s normal for a successful soldier. You have to find the drive to be superior, to hate your enemy enough to kill them and deny them killing you. If you cannot, you will simply be among the casualties… you will not rise above the common line of the burdenless.”

“Burdenless?”

“Corpses. The rest of us bear what they leave as they fall.”

“Mmn. Poetic. So we do. You’re… different though, aren’t you? You don’t fight with any hatred, or at least, very rarely. You have a strong grip on it.”

Palth didn’t reply immediately. Truthfully, it was mostly something to do with fighting her. He wasn’t going to try and unpack that, however. He didn’t know how, other than to say inappropriate, crude things that he realized didn’t contain the whole truth. “Perhaps I’d fought on through it to the other side. I’m finding it again, though.”“A scary thought!”

“Let’s hope our enemy comes to fear it.”

“I definitely hope it, Oddy.”

An awkward silence persisted, one that Palth felt they both had things to say to disrupt, but didn’t have the words to, as if the language they had was broken and inefficient. Though he’d never quite felt it so strongly as with Azure, he’d felt something similar at times, with rivals. With people he’d fought and respected. He’d killed a few like that, and no deaths haunted him more.

Finally, Azure said, “Welp, I’ll leave you to your brooding, I suppose. See you on the battlefield, hmm?”

“Yes. I’m glad I’m not facing across from you this time.”

He could feel her smile as she said, “Likewise.”

As Azure slowed, separated, and then simply vanished, Palth pondered the woman and her lover. ‘Betrothed’ apparently. He felt different things between them. Respect and awe for the Goddess, fascination and admiration for Azure. Tempered desire. Such easy idols, both of them. Convincing divinity.

I suppose men have raised their swords for less. True or not, it’s enough to believe in, isn’t it? Beauty that shouldn’t be possible, cheering you on…

Though he didn’t detect it, Palth walked with a little less burden on his shoulders.
