I meet The Chinese diplomatic convoy at the front gate, and wave them in. A man in his mid-fifties exits the car, and I lead him towards a guest house, far from the other buildings where we might conduct some more energetic experiments.

“Machina. It is good to meet you. I’m Ambassador Chen. Thank you for receiving me, especially unannounced,” he says.

The ambassador gives me a shallow bow, and I show him inside. This building was built with my unique nature in mind, so it can handle my weight. A rarity. I gesture him to take a seat, and I sit down in something closer to a vehicle jack than furniture. It’s a heavy-duty steel monolith of a chair.

“How can I help you, Ambassador?”

“Firstly, I have a message for you from the President of the People’s Republic of China.”

He pulls a letter out of a pocket in his suit jacket and hands it over. The hand-written message handed to me by the Chinese ambassador is short.

Machina.

The People’s Republic of China would like to extend our gratitude for your actions in the interest of nuclear non-proliferation. We are sure you received many tempting offers for the warhead. We are grateful that you instead chose the path of peace for all, especially in such a chaotic world. China salutes your efforts, and should you come across other weapons that need disposing of, we humbly offer our assistance. For the good of all humankind, weapons of mass destruction must never be allowed to spread. Especially so in such tumultuous times. We are in your debt, and if you require assistance with humanitarian matters, please call on us.

President Zheng

“Please convey my thanks to President Zheng, Ambassador Chen. But I imagine you’re here for more than just mail delivery?”

He chuckles.

“Astute, as expected. Yes. China would respectfully ask for your assistance, Machina. We’ve recently had a Fracture open up that we cannot clear without causalities. Causalities that in this day and age are unacceptable.”

“What can you tell me?”

“Ah, I would hope you would keep this information close to your chest, yes?”

I guess it’s an embarrassment, not being able to clear a Fracture.

“Of course, Ambassador. I’m here to help however I can.”

He smiles.

“Good, good! I’m happy to hear that. The Fracture is in a nuclear power plant. Category-3.”

Oh. That’s not good.

“Ah. I can see why you’d be concerned.”

“It gets worse. The Fracture is so incredibly radioactive inside that we aren’t sure there’s anyone who could survive it. Beyond yourself and Estrella, of course.”

Estrella, the Brazilian Ascended, is a literal living star. I doubt any amount of radiation would bother her for obvious reasons.

“I’m sure you’re extremely busy, and I hate to press, but it is a situation that may become a greater emergency if not resolved soon. We’ve had to shutdown the reactors, and the nearby districts are now suffering from rolling blackouts. We’ve kept critical infrastructure up, but fuel for the powerplants isn’t infinite. Especially not with the tanker ships disappearing.”

Cargo ships have been disappearing in the deep Pacific and Indian oceans. Even hugging the coast lines hasn’t saved them. While there’s nothing confirmed, most people are assuming sea monster attacks or magical rogue storms. The slow rolling collapse of global shipping is a problem of truly cataclysmic proportions. Unfortunately, a failing logistical system is not something I can stab.

China is a massive importer of fuel products, actually the largest oil importer in the world. Fuel rationing is already in effect, and every hour the nuclear power plant is offline is an hour closer to a complete grid collapse. Every tanker that vanishes is a blow to a crumbling grid.

“While we do hope that you may find materials and items of interest that would assist in staving off a complete grid collapse,” he continues. “At the very least we would like the power plant to be safe enough to be brought back to full operation. A grid collapse would be catastrophic. Countless people would be at risk.”

“Of course, I would be happy to help. As for salvage rights, well, we can worry about that later. Once everyone is safe.”

Fracture salvage rights are the most important legal topic in today’s day and age. Given how important the materials and artifacts inside are for defending against magically durable monsters, it’s not hard to see why everyone would clamor over them. That doesn’t even count the nuclear device I recovered, though it’s been dismantled for research.

But I’m not truly concerned with them. If China desperately needs something from the Fracture to help keep people safe, well, there’s always more Fractures. Hopefully it won’t be too much of an ask to take a few small samples for Morgan’s research.

Ambassador Chen bows his head for a moment.

“Thank you, Machina. You are a true hero in these trying times. One desperately needed, might I add. As for transport, we’d like to offer you to portal from our Embassy in DC. As you can imagine, most of the airports are overloaded and I imagine even you would have trouble flying out on such short notice,” he says wryly. “As for getting to DC, I have a flight capable of carrying you scheduled in three days, if that would be acceptable.”

With ground travel riskier than ever, airports are overloaded. Normally I don’t have trouble flying even internationally at the drop of a hat, but I doubt the US government would be willing to clear the flight schedule to assist a nation they consider a rival at best.

“No, that’s too late. Like you said, a grid collapse would be catastrophic. An international flight is out of the question, but I can manage a regional flight well enough.”

Ambassador Chen’s eyebrows raise.

“I would hate to be further in your debt, Machina. The Fracture is only seven days old. We have time. And I’m afraid I would not be at the Embassy to greet you,” he adds sheepishly. “My colleagues are more than capable of taking care of everything of course. If you wish to leave early, by all means, do so!”

“Just come with me,” I say, shrugging. “There’s enough room to fit your car in the cargo bay.”

He blinks for a moment.

“If you’re willing to share the space, I would of course take you up on that offer, but are you sure? I have no doubt that space is at a premium on your aircraft, given all the support equipment you certainly require.”

“It might get a little cramped, but it’ll be fine,” I reply, shrugging. “Let’s go, Ambassador. No time to waste.”

Just after we take off, Director Holden calls me. Despite her monotone and cold composure, I can tell she’s annoyed.

“Machina, I’m not aware of any Fractures you’re currently needed for. Why are you disrupting the flight schedules for two airports?”

“Maybe not in the United States, Director. China’s Ambassador has requested my assistance regarding a Fracture. They’ve asked me not to disclose specifics beyond that.”

“You’re taking their side in this?” Holden asks.

“I’m not taking anyone’s side, Director. They asked for help, so I said yes. Nothing more than that.”

“You need to look at the bigger picture, Machina. They have more Ascended than any other nation on Earth. They don’t actually need you. They want something else from you, and they’re manipulating you into getting you there.”

“Everyone wants something from me, Director.” I say tiredly. The implied even you goes unsaid. “The only thing I want is to help save lives, Director. I don’t really care about much else. Whether they need me or not doesn’t matter all that much to me. They asked, and I intend to answer.”

“You can’t help everyone, Machina. You can’t be everywhere. At some point, you’ll have to say no.”

“Director, I say no ten thousand times a day! Countless people beg me for help every hour, every day, and I have to tell them ‘I can’t help you.’ All day, every day, I am forced to watch mass slaughter play out on a global scale! I am so, so tired of not being able to help. I can help. So I will.”

“Fine,” she says after a brief pause. “Just be careful. If something goes sideways, feel free to call me. I’ll do what I can.”

It’s a fairly short drive to Embassy Row where China’s white bricked embassy resides. While most of DC is overcrowded with both civilians and military alike, this road is sparse in comparison. It’s still flooded with personnel, foreign and domestic. Reporters and journalists from countless news organizations roam the street in packs. Already an important location when it comes to geopolitics, the active and ongoing apocalypse has only made the busy area more so.

The Ambassador and myself draw a crowd, and reporters swarm like hungry birds. I can hear the click of cameras and as they sing out over radio to live television stations or upload pictures to websites. The gate opens with a whirr, servos and motors humming their electromechanical work song. Ambassador Chen waves me in.

“Please, Machina. It would be an honor to host you.”

“Thank you, Ambassador.”

He follows me in, and guides me deeper into the compound. The chattering of cameras explodes, so many that it becomes a waterfall of noise.

“This way, please. We flew a portal sorcerer in late last night. She’s been working around the clock to set the ritual up.” He chuckles. “We expected you a few days from now, but we wanted it done early. Both your and her time is valuable, and we wanted to make sure we took as little as possible. I guess we made the right decision, hm?”

We make our way to a helicopter pad. A huge tarp has been strung over it to keep the early morning rain off, though it was probably unnecessary. The ritualist isn’t using chalk, unlike Hale was when she drew her portal ritual to send me to Australia.

Instead, she’s using a can of spray paint. It’s a faint gold color, and sparkles in the sun. She shakes the can, and it emits the usual clack-clack-clack of a normal can of spray paint. The Ambassador leans in.

“It’s an electrum-based quick-dry paint. No need for fragile or messy chalk. All weather, and all terrain. A new innovation from our labs. We’ve been moving quick with everything we’ve pulled from the Fractures.”

He motions to a soldier, and he tosses us a can from a case.

“Here,” he says, handing it to me. “Take a look.”

The canister itself isn’t magic, but I can see that the paint inside is ever so slightly magical.

“Truly impressive, especially considering the difficulty to make ritual chalk or paints. It’s a lot more than just add magical materials and off you go.” I muse.

“Well, here, why don’t you keep it? I’m sure you’ll find some use for it later down the line.”

I store it away with a flash of light. I’m sure Morgan would love to take a look.

“Thank you.”

“Of course. Well, it looks like Miss Wu is done.”

The short woman stands up with a spine cracking stretch before approaching us.

“Mr. Machina, are you ready? The ritual circle is done. It’ll send you directly to the command center just outside of the Daya Bay Nuclear Power Plant. You’ll be receiving a more in-depth briefing there.”

“I’m ready.”

She nods and kneels down before placing her hands on the ritual. With a flash of intent, Potentia pours out from her. Her previously hidden 5-Star Aura, a sign of impressive training, pulses out. With a crack of thunder, space rips apart. A jagged gash hovering the air crackles with purple lightning for a moment.

I step from the morning of Denver into the late evening light of China’s coast. A huge military camp is bustling, with thousands of personnel scrambling around in organized chaos.

Comfortably familiar. That’s probably not a good thing.
