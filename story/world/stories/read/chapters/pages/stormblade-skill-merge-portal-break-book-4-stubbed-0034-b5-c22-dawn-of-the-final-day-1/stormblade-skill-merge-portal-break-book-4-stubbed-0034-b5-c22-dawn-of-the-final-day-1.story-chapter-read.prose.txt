Ellen and I woke up before the sun the next morning.

More accurately, she woke me up, shaking my shoulder. “Kade. We’ve got a lot to do today. Let’s get moving.”

She was right. We did have a lot to do. The Placid King hadn’t made a move yet, but I’d stayed up all night anyway, waiting for him. I’d only drifted off maybe an hour before Ellen’s hands on my shoulder. And today was the last day before the God of Thunder’s grace period ended. The SS-Plus-Rankers were on their way.

And against them, we had the two of us, whatever Tanat had managed to accomplish in the few hours I’d tried to sleep, and a bunch of Traynor-Overholz Cannons that may or may not get delivered on time. The reality was starting to sink in that no matter what I did, it wouldn’t be enough to be ready for them. I shut my eyes.

A second later, something frigid pressed against the small of my back. “Okay, I’m going!” I yelped.

Ellen pulled her feet away as I rolled over. She smiled smugly. “That’s what I thought. I need to test out my new abilities.”

“So…test them?” I said.

“I need to test them on a target, and you’re the only one who can take it.”

“Oh.”

“Besides, you promised last night.”

I nodded, slowly remembering. Then I pulled myself out of bed, stretched—popping some joints as I did—and pulled on a fresh set of clothes. Ellen was already in her battle robes, and as much as I hated to give her any credit after getting me out of bed the way she had, she had let me sleep while she got ready. It wasn’t much of a victory, but even an hour or so had let me refresh a little. In just a few minutes, I was ready, and we headed down to Deimos.

The car took off. We arrived at the GC tower, headed to the basement, and traveled through the long, underground tunnel until we arrived at the S-Rank sparring room. I’d been there before—just three days ago, in fact. The mission to Carlsbad had started there. It was built to handle S-Rankers going all-out, and that was perfect for what Ellen needed.

“Ready?” I called.

”Ready!”

I moved, shifting into Thunderlord stance. If I wanted to beat Ellen, I’d have to get in close and hit hard. As I rushed her, Pepperoni surged out, lightning crackling around her. She beat her wings and took off. Cheddar erupted from my dimensional space to meet her.

Ellen ignored the two winged serpents. She only had eyes for me—and those eyes swirled with an almost crystallized darkness. The gap closed quickly, and Ellen made no move to escape.

The Storm’s Blade whipped out. Its tip rocketed toward her chest.

It touched scales made of golden armor and that same crystallized darkness that even now poured from Ellen’s eyes. Voidwalker. She’d cast a spell, and it had been strong enough to withstand my attack.

“Good job!” I started to say.

I didn’t get the chance to say anything else.

The world seemed to collapse into a single pinprick as she cast. I couldn’t move away from that point of infinite darkness—not even when she started to channel Abyssal Storm. The Black Hole held me in place, and Abyssal Storm’s shadow-infused wind and lightning tore at me. I braced myself for it to be over; she’d won that round, but I was done taking it easy on her.

For the next half-hour, we fought. Ellen shifted in and out of Voidwalker, filled the room with puppets made from my shadows, and even cloned my spells in darkness when I tried to cast against her. I got enough blows in that, in a real fight, I’d have killed her. But in a real fight, she’d have killed me, too. We weren’t evenly matched. I was built better for one-on-one fighting.

Still, she was every bit the S-Ranker I’d hoped she’d be.

We walked, covered in sweat and chests pumping like a pair of bellows, back down the long tunnel toward Deimos. “Happy with your build?” I asked.

She shrugged. “It’s alright, I guess.” But her smile betrayed her. She couldn’t stop beaming—and I didn’t blame her. I could bring the single-target damage, and had just enough area abilities to hold my own. She was the opposite. Together, we probably didn’t even need more damage-dealers to clear portals; a pair of tanks, a healer, and a support would let us steamroll anything.

It was shocking how much power S-Rankers had.

Ellen was still beaming and still talking about her build, when we slid into Deimos’s front seats. I reached for my phone instinctively.

I had thirty-eight new messages.

Crap.

Jessie Gerald sat on the floor in the Desert Wind’s training room. Thudding and banging echoed from outside as a massively powerful monster tried to break in.

The door was shut and locked. Alarms blared all around the building. She’d gotten here in record time—less than a minute from the alarms to the moment she slid inside, and that was while navigating the stairs with no cane—and she’d still gotten beat by every single delver in the building.

With two exceptions.

She’d texted them both—her brother more than Ellen.

That was all she could do. It was all anyone in the training room—Jeff, Yasmin, or the assorted lower-rankers—could do.

The golden portal had opened in the garage. Two monsters had emerged from it, along with a deluge of water that had flooded the parking structure and poured from its open-air windows. Jessie didn’t know what they were. She couldn’t see their system information.

But Jeff could.

“Those were A-Rank and S-Rank,” he muttered, half to himself and half to the assembled, terrified delvers around him. “An S-Rank portal just broke, right on top of us.”

“I’ve…I’ve notified the Governing Council,” Jessie said, gritting her teeth. Stairs were hard under the best of circumstances. “In the meantime, help me out. I need a role call. Who’s here? Are we missing anyone?”

“You already did that,” Yasmin said. “And I did it a second time. We’re only missing Ellen and Kade.”

“Great,” Jessie said under her breath. “We’re only missing the idiot responsible for this mess.”

It was his fault. She was sure of it. Whatever he’d done, it had pissed off the gigantic eel currently wrecking the gym around them and the thin, tentacle-fingered swordsman watching the door. He wore armor covered with shells and coral, and his ‘hair,’ if you could call it that, was made of even thinner octopus tentacles.

According to Jeff and Yasmin, he was at least S-Rank.

Jessie reached for her phone. She’d already sent like thirty texts, but just in case Kade hadn’t seen them, she sent one more.

Jessie: Kade hurry they’re in the gym we need your help!

Ellen took the wheel as Deimos pulled off Bell Road. She floored it, weaving around cars close enough to knock both mirrors off her sports car. The speedometer passed a hundred twenty. A hundred thirty. Faster.

I buckled my seatbelt as the car’s tires squealed and she aimed it straight at the Desert Wind Building. Steam rose from all around the parking garage—that had to be where the portal had opened. I readied myself. The second she hit the brakes, I’d open the door and roll out. Then it’d be time to take the fight to the monsters.

The Placid King had made his move—and he’d timed it for the moment we were gone. I didn’t know what his game was, but I knew we were playing right into it. I didn’t care, though. I’d made promises. To keep Jessie safe. And now, she was in danger. I couldn’t let that stand. No. No, I wouldn’t let that stand.

Deimos picked up speed.

“What are you doing?” I yelled.

“We can’t waste time! Hang on!”

We hit the plastic trash can sitting on the curb. Garbage bags exploded. Dried pancake batter covered the windshield, along with slices of bread, half-eaten apples and bananas, and a half-dozen take-out bags. The mess erupted around us as I reached for the panic bar. Ellen’s fingers tightened until her knuckles turned white.

Then we crashed through the glass front windows. Deimos flipped. It slid on its roof and slammed into the elevator shaft hard enough to cave the entire driver’s side in.

Ellen was fine, of course. It’d take more than that to take out an S-Ranker.

I unbuckled and pulled myself out. Ellen was only a second behind me; she came out of the door at a sprint. Glass crunched under her boots, and smoke swirled around her; the car was totaled. More than totaled—smoke poured from every opening, and the radio wouldn’t stop playing.

Drum and bass. Really, really loud drum and bass.

The first floor was supposed to be rented out as shops, but we’d never gotten around to it. I’d regretted it; the Surprise district was mostly empty houses, and it’d be good to start revitalizing it—and to support local businesses.

But now, as alarms blared all around us, I didn’t regret it anymore.

“Let’s move. S-Rank portal; it’s in the garage, but we’re more concerned with the monsters than the portal,” I said.

Ellen nodded, and we headed for the stairs.

I took them two at a time. They were in the gym. That was the plan if evacuating to the garage didn’t work: go to the gym, get in the sparring room, turn the damage reduction to max, and seal the doors. It’d work against B-Rank monsters.

It wouldn’t work against S-Rank ones. Not for long.

The Storm’s Blade erupted in my hand, lightning crackling along its edge and clouds swirling around its tip. Ellen’s body hardened, growing more angular as Voidwalker’s crystallized shadow formed a thick coat of armor around her.

“Hello, Stormblade.”

There were two of them, just like Jessie had said.

The first was familiar. Slimy-looking tentacles. A sharp blade wrapped in them. Armor covered in barnacles and shells. Had Quellat had a partner? Or was this a successor? I couldn’t be sure, but on paper, he was the bigger threat. His eyes locked onto mine, and his long, heavy sword rose into a guard.

Kalorwan, Paragon of the Stilled Breath: S-Rank

On paper, he was the bigger threat, yes—but only on paper.

The other monster, though less powerful, was much more dangerous.

One of her fins was still missing, and lightning burns crisscrossed her body in every direction. Her purple-and-green, oil-slick skin was marred and ridged; hundreds of circles of raised flesh the color of an angry sun spiraled around her length, over and over. Her body trailed behind her, but it bent at a forty-five-degree angle near her tail.

And her eyes.

Magda hadn’t been hateful. She’d been, if anything, seductive. Sensual. She hadn’t been only a Paragon of the Sliding Surge. She’d been a monster who was close to two SS-Plus-Ranked Paragons, and who navigated that world with skill and confidence.

But now, those massive eyes flickered between hatred and…nothing.

Magda, Paragon of the Sliding Surge: A-Rank

“You cost me everything, Stormblade. These wounds won’t ever heal—not properly. The Thunder God won’t take me back like this! And the Placid King will kill me if I fail here!” Her voice flickered just like her eyes. Something was wrong with her.

But I didn’t care. She was threatening my friends.

“I’ll kill you long before he does,” I said.

The battle trance overwhelmed me, and I pushed my aura out. The Stormkin Avatar formed. Dad’s sword and shield lashed out at Magda, and she summoned a wall of water to take the blow. It held. Barely.

I was already moving, sword out, to engage Kalorwan. His own sword came down as I surged toward him. Torrentborne stance. Parry the blow. The long, thick blade deflected into a weight rack, and hundred-pound plates flew everywhere, shattering drywall and breaking wooden framing. One Rainfall Charge.

It was a start. The next blow came from an unexpected angle; the swordsman’s free hand erupted as a massive tentacle slammed into my stomach. I got the Storm’s Blade between it and me, just in time, but the impact still sent me flying. Kalorwan was every bit the same monster Quellat had been, but with a much more aggressive fighting style.

But I had a new trick of my own.

Ellen’s Voidwalker empowered her spells, and the Black Hole that landed just behind Kalorwan jerked him off his feet. It also picked up a pair of treadmills and a weight bench. They all slammed together, and as they did, Ellen added Abyssal Storm to the combo.

I rushed in just as the Black Hole ended, ignoring her green-black lightning. It wasn’t a threat to me. Thunderlord into Laminar Blade. A trio of tentacles fell free from the monster’s body. I pressed the attack, forcing him to fight defensively as I pushed him back to the gym’s door and out into the open atrium. Behind me, the Stormkin Avatar pressed his own assault on Magda. Thunder cracked, and steam poured out the door behind me.

“Now!” I yelled.

“On it!” Ellen shouted back.

Shadow energy swirled around her. It rippled the air, and the room, already dark from the smoke and steam in the air, went black. Bars of void appeared all around it, sealing the space, and Ellen turned her attention to the A-Rank Paragon.

Our friends weren’t trapped inside the sparring room anymore. Now, Magda was trapped inside the Shadow Cell with Ellen and the Stormkin Avatar. We’d talked through the strategy. Isolate the attacking monsters. Get a mismatch against the weaker one. Then exploit that weakness while they were trapped.

And the Shadow Cell wasn’t only a prison. Shadow Cell wasn’t a new skill, after all. It was an altered, Path-specific, S-Rank version of Shadow Box.

Even as I launched another attack on Kalorwan, the screaming started. I turned; I couldn’t help myself. Ellen was ripping apart everything in the gym. Chunks of cement and padding disappeared in cube-shaped sections all around her as the Shadow Cell did its job. Voidwalker flickered, but held against Magda’s attempt to break free. The Stormkin Avatar wasn’t immune to the Cell’s effect, either. Only Ellen was—Ellen, and the people in the protected sparring room.

I watched as Magda died.

But before she could, a golden portal flickered open. I howled in frustration as lightning poured through it, surrounding the dying eel.

This couldn’t be happening. It couldn’t be. The God of Thunder was intervening now, of all times? He’d promised! The Storm’s Blade came up, and I tried—and failed—to push past Ellen’s S-Rank skill. The Stormkin Avatar took four quick steps, closing the gap between him and the portal. Thunder clapped. A gaping hole appeared in Dad’s chest, and he flickered out of existence as the stink of ozone filled the air.

“I’ll take that, Kade Noelstra,” the God of Thunder said. “Consider it a favor, and good luck with the Placid King.”

The portal snapped shut behind Magda, and I turned my frustration and fury on the only possible target. “Ellen, start evacuating them just like we planned.”

Then the Storm’s Blade lashed out, and Kalorwan’s met it mid-air as static surged around me.
