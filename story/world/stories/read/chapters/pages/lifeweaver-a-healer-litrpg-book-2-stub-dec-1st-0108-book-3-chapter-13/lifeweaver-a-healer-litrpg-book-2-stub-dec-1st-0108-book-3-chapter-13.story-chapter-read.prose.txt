On the way back to Curtis's apartment, which turned out to be a real shithole of a building on the brink of collapse after taking repeated blows from some roaming monster a few days earlier, the group spotted two portals by mere sight. Luke sensed several more not far off, with even more strewn around the city.

"What is going on here?" he asked Curtis, who would feel the portals as well, due to his profession as a Seeker.

Curtis looked thoughtful for a moment, then shrugged. "The Intruders."

"Not this again," Hannah complained.

"What about them?"

"They're up to something here," Curtis said, gesturing out the window. "I think that's what's making all the portals appear."

"I wonder if that's why Alan wanted me in this city," Luke said, looking around the apartment. For some reason, he would have guessed that Curtis was a neat freak, or at least somewhat organized. Not so. There was stuff everywhere, and all of it was covered by a thin layer of dust. "Not to be an asshole or anything, but your place is a mess."

"You didn't ask him before giving him a karate chop to the neck?" Ray asked, leaning forward to inspect a sizeable DVD-collection.

Luke had given his friends a more thorough explanation of what had happened on the other side of the portal, how he managed to return, and the events that transpired after that.

"Didn't really have time for questions," Luke said, peeking behind a door to find the bathroom. "Didn't have time for much of anything."

"I think I'll find a hotel or something until I can find my own place," Hannah said.

"Hotels are closed," Ray said, pulling out a DVD. He held it in the air. "Hey! Scrubs! You have good taste, my man."

"Closed?" Hannah asked.

Ray chuckled, reading the back of the DVD. "This place is a ghost town now. Only ones left are Integrated, and most of those are overwhelmed, to say the least. That's what my contact is saying, at least."

"It's not exactly my apartment," Curtis said, still peering out the window, like he was expecting an attack to come at any time.

"What do you mean?" Luke called back from the bathroom.

Hannah added her own question. "Is it your brother's?"

"It was empty, so I kind of just took it."

Luke turned around and stood in the doorway to the bathroom. "What do you mean you 'took it'? Curtis, are you squatting here? Are you a squatter?"

Curtis huffed and turned to gesture toward Ray. "Like he said, this place is dead. What's the alternative? I'm not living in some damn tent."

"Heh," Ray chuckled. "Urban outdoorsman."

"There are shelters in the Integrated Shop," Hannah suggested. "Haven't looked around much in there, but you can pick them back up and bring them with you every morning."

Luke shook his head, feeling like an intruder in someone's private space. "I'll have to check those shelters out. Not staying here, I'll tell you that much. First, though, shower!"

He closed the door behind him and locked it. Before he had a chance to run the water in the shower, he heard Hannah on the other side of the door ask Ray, "What contact? You DIA or something?"

The warm water running over his spent, blood-covered body was perhaps the greatest thing he'd ever experienced in his life. It took a good forty-five minutes until the water stopped running red with blood and another fifteen until Luke stepped out of the shower as a new, clean man.

"Aw yeah," he muttered to himself as he got dressed in new clothes from his inventory before disposing of the old ones in a plastic bag. It was with more than a little apprehension that he brought out the robe, figuring it would be nothing more than a mess of dried blood. To Luke's surprise, there wasn't a speck of dirt on the soft fabric, and the runic lettering in gold and purple shone and glittered like the thing was brand new. Such an amazing item.

Unwavering Robe of The Fiend (Of Self-Repair): Your presence is obscured from all but the most perceptive. This garment instills fear in the undead (This enchanted piece of armor will repair itself as long as a single scrap remains).

Wanting to feel its comfortable embrace, he pulled it over his head as he walked out of the bathroom to join the others. "What are you doing?" he asked.

Ray was on the couch now, a remote in hand, looking through the extras on the Scrubs DVD he'd found. "Curtis won't shut up about Intruders. I think we should hit up a dungeon. Get those sweet levels in. Do our part to help out in this pearl of the East Coast city."

"Didn't think I'd ever say it, but I agree with the Assassin," Hannah said. She stood in the middle of the room, looking uncomfortable. "I'm not staying in someone else's apartment, anyway. Got myself a shelter from the Integrated Shop."

"Oh?" Luke asked, opening the shop to see what sort of options were available.

Hannah nodded and pointed toward the door. "Nothing fancy. A room, a bed. Bathroom included. I'm going outside to test it now. If you want to come?"

"Sure," Luke said, waving to catch Curtis's attention. "We need more people. I've got a Jumper who might want to join, but that means we still need two more for a full party."

"We should start a guild," Ray said, his eyes still glued to the TV.

Hannah stopped at the front door and turned around. "Why?"

"Bonuses, access to cool stuff, more people to tackle the dungeons. There are some theories floating around that when things really start to get moving in the world, it'll be guilds, not countries, that will be the real power-houses. At the rate we're moving, we'll have war and collapse real soon."

"This is something your source shared with you?" Hannah asked, doing air-quotes at 'source'.

Ray leaned over on the couch to look at her. "Which part?"

"The war part, duh."

"Some of it, sure," Ray said with a sigh, before pointing at Luke. "The nuke our boy set off was the last one we'll ever see, probably. People are getting antsy, and by people, I mean governments."

"What do you mean?" Luke asked.

"Sorry, my boy, but you won't be able to nuke your way out of any more dungeons in the foreseeable future. They're all disabled."

Curtis turned around from peering out the window to face the room. "What?"

"Our nukes are fucked."

Luke cleared his throat. "I read something about Iran's nukes. Ours as well?"

"All of 'em."

"What's the problem?" Hannah said. "That's a good thing, isn't it?"

That made Ray bark out a laugh. "How about no?"

She raised an eyebrow. "No?"

"Nukes are badass, to start," Ray said, shaking his head. "But with them gone, and I'm pretty sure I've got the right of that particular point, do you think our usually so logical and measured government will respond well? What about Russia? China? Countries are spooked. Could be a Cold War situation all over again, but with super-powered Integrated in the middle. While they act like assholes, New Belgium is growing. More guilds are popping up."

"And you think what we should do is start a guild?" Luke asked.

Ray shrugged. "Why not? We could beat those Phoenix Phalanx assholes and become super-duper strong or whatever. No offense, Curtis."

"None taken."

Luke considered for a moment, but found no real reason to say no. "Sure. As long as I don't have to be the guildmaster."

"Dibs," Ray said.

Hannah shook his head. "Not you, Ray."

"What's wrong with me?" Ray asked, the hurt plain on his face, before it turned into a grin. "Just kidding. I'd suck as a guildmaster. Why don't you do it?"

"No," Hannah said. "Not my kind of thing."

"Curtis?" Ray asked.

"Can't."

Luke turned to face the grump. "Why not?"

"I'm in another guild."

All three of them turned to look at him. "What?"

Curtis sighed and rubbed at his eyes, as if a headache was coming on. "My brother convinced me to join and come here with him and the others after one of their group didn't make it out of a dungeon. They promised to help with the Intruders if I find portals for them."

"That sucks," Ray said, looking a little crestfallen.

"Wait," Luke said. "Why do they need you to find portals? They're everywhere here."

"There's a lot of value in getting to a portal first," Hannah explained, before Curtis had a chance to.

Luke looked from one to the other. "Why?"

"The dungeon is chosen from whoever enters first, or whichever party enters first," Hannah explained. "It sets the dungeon's difficulty, quest, everything."

Luke stood there, dumbfounded. "It does? How long have you known this?"

"Couple of days?" Hannah said, looking to the other two.

Ray nodded. "A few days, yeah."

"A week," Curtis said, looking a little satisfied he'd known before the others.

It made sense, Luke supposed, despite sounding like a far too simple explanation. He'd been first to enter the dungeon with the dragon, and he'd been alone. That place had been almost impossible to beat, and a lot of the Integrated joining in had been forced to retreat, and that was before he got all those levels. That would also mean Luke and the rest of his party were responsible for the dungeon on the other side of the pink portal, with all that followed. Well, perhaps not responsible, but was it because of Luke that they'd come face-to-face with Relian?

"Stop," Ray said.

Luke blinked a couple of times, drawn out of his thoughts. "What?"

"Don't get lost in your own head. You always overthink things."

"No, I don't."

Ray gave Hannah, then Curtis, meaningful looks.

"You kind of do," Hannah said.

"Yep," Curtis agreed.

"Let's just say with you in our group, the dungeons will be tough as hell, which means we'll get a metric fuck-ton of experience, levels, and loot? Curtis will be missing out," Ray said.

Curtis just scoffed, but didn't reply.

"Fine, I won't overthink things," Luke said. "Let's create a guild and get some members, and grow stronger. We can't do anything about the rest of the world, so let's focus on us."

"And the Intruders," Curtis said.

"And the Intruders," Luke allowed. "Hopefully, Dot will come to her senses and join us."

The others exchanged glances, but said nothing.

After a moment, Hannah cleared her throat. "Soooo, let's go check out my shelter?"

"Let's do it," Luke said. "I've got some messages to get out, too. I think I've got just the person for the guildmaster position."
