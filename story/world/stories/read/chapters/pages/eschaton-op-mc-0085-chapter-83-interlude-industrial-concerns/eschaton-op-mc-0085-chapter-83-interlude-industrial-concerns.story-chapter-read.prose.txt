President Drayson throws a report from Morgan le Fay onto his desk with a sigh. He’s joined by four others in the Oval Office, three of which are a part of his Cabinet. Secretary of Defense Security Johnathan Cook is sitting in a chair in front of Drayson’s desk next to Secretary of the Interior Brandi Miller. Archer Rutherford, the Secretary of Homeland Security, is leaning up against one wall. In the middle of the room is Elizabeth Holden, Director of the National Security Agency.

Director Holden is someone Drayson has come to rely on more and more. To the point where he’s considering giving her a full Cabinet position rather than an informal one. Her experience in handling Machina and generally keeping tabs on the Empowered has been critical in managing the continual crisis.

“Well, Brandi,” Drayson says. “These arcologies could be game changing if they live to expectations. Just one problem.”

“The magical materials needed to build them,” Miller says with a nod. “We don’t have enough titanium for the mithril. Even the small amount of gold and silver needed for the electrum in the design is well out of reach. And that doesn’t even count the Fractal Shards needed to convert the metals into their truly magical forms. Even before the Apotheosis we only produced about a thousand tons of silver and two hundred tons of gold a year. As for titanium? Well, we’ve always been reliant on imports, and we all know what’s happened with those.”

“The mines we had for gold and silver are effectively lost,” Cook says flatly. “They’re too isolated to defend, and the supply lines we’d have to set up and defend as well would be impossible with the current state of affairs.”

“Southern Mining Industries has offered a solution,” Miller suggests. “They’re partnering with Palisade to secure Fractures off the coast of California. It’s been recently discovered that some of the Fractures have vast mineral deposits. We’ve also found some sources of titanium in Fractures off the Georgia coast, though they are relatively poor. It’s the best we’ve got. Palisade and Southern Mining have offered a bid for those as well.”

“There are political implications with that move,” Holden says coolly. “Giving Palisade more influence is a dangerous proposition.”

“It’s not Palisade who’s doing the mining,” Miller says flatly.

“Correct. But the mining will not take place without Palisade’s agreement. Palisade can always find another mining company and provide the muscle to them instead.”

“Southern’s mouth, but Palisade’s words,” Drayson agrees. “Unfortunately, I see little alternative. The aquaculture project is showing signs of promise, and while it’ll support the coastal cities, the rest of the country is still on borrowed time.”

“Damn it all,” Drayson sighs after a brief moment. He turns to Miller. “Brandi, tell Southern they can go ahead. But,” he holds up a finger. “they’ll pay for the mining rights with a tithe of refined metals on top of the cash they’ll fork over for it. Get as much from them as you can.”

She nods. “From what Machina mentioned, I’m sure we’ll both get a lot. The mineral fields off the coast of California are the richest seabed ore veins ever seen.”

Cook and Miller leave, but Drayson motions for both Rutherford and Holden to stay.

“Okay, I want to know exactly how bad it’s going to be, giving Palisade this deal.”

“Not great,” Rutherford says.

“It offers Palisade even more leverage over us,” Holden says, sliding into the recently vacated chair. “Now they’ll have near complete control over the materials needed to effectively prevent large scale starvation. We have no one else to turn to.”

“Even foreign alternatives are limited,” Rutherford cuts in. “And those have their own drawbacks. Not the least of which is how we would actually ship industrial quantities of refined metal without, well, ships.”

“Palisade’s relationship with Titan Defense can’t be overlooked either,” Holden says. “Palisade is currently solidifying their grasp on the entire military industrial complex, everything from resource production to final manufacturing.”

“They wouldn’t possibly turn on us, advantage in Empowered firepower or no.” Drayson says. “And I know our relationship with Machina and Morgan is… fraught at times. But I’m sure they would help us if we asked.”

“Machina certainly would,” Holden offers. “I have nothing confirmed, but I have suspicions that the Conglomerate’s near-complete collapse and internal divisions was at the hands of Machina.”

“What.” Drayson and Rutherford ask flatly.

“The hack occurred at the same time a foreign national, namely one Captain Hwang of the Republic of Korea, met with Machina. While I am not sure what exactly she asked of him, I am absolutely sure she was asking him for help against the Conglomerate.”

“You weren’t kidding when you said Machina was unstoppable in the cyberspace domain,” Rutherford says with a whistle. “Does the Conglomerate know?”

“So far, apparently not. They do have a 3-Star Empowered with technology and cyberspace related powers. They go by”Xeroday”, and we know nothing else about them. They’ve scrubbed their entire history from the internet as a whole. Xeroday has suspicions that the ROK has employed an outside contractor but is unaware of who it is.”

“A little outside your wheelhouse, Director,” Rutherford lightly reprimands.

“Part of my new responsibilities includes surveillance on Empowered. I am merely being proactive about possible national security threats, Secretary,” Holden says coolly.

“Okay,” Drayson says, pulling his advisors’ attention back to the matter at hand. “So, Machina has shown himself to be a stabilizing influence, at least when it comes to corporate power grabs. As long as he’s on our side, or least seems to be, Palisade probably won’t make any overt moves.”

“I agree with that assessment, sir,” Rutherford says.

“He’s a stabilizing influence for the time being, sir,” Holden disagrees. “He’s shown himself to be willing to break from us. No response from us regarding the hearing, despite how justified many people view his rebuke to be, does set a precedent for him. Not necessarily when it comes to telling us no, but as a general pattern of behavior. If he finds certain things to be repugnant, he will ignore or combat them. No matter how legal or necessary they are. We cannot forget that he chose to divulge information we gave him in confidence to government officials of another nation.”

“If we can’t control him, then others will surely have trouble as well,” Drayson points out. “He already told Palisade no, and it was a deal I don’t think anyone else in the entire world would have turned down.”

“A loose cannon is still a loose cannon, sir. Even when pointed at our enemies.”

“One we’ll just have to learn to live with, Director. The world will have to, whether they like it or not. Let’s do our best to stay on Machina’s good side. We’re not the only ones gunning for that position, and it’s probably one of the most important places to be.”
