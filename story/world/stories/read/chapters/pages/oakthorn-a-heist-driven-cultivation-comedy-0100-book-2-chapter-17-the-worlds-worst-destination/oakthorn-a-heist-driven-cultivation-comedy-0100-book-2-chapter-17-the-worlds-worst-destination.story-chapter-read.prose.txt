Marsh puddles weren’t supposed to follow you.

Cade paused mid-step, watching as the iridescent green pool he’d just skirted rippled, stretched, and impossibly oozed after him like a liquid predator. Its surface tensed, forming an unnatural bulge that tracked his movement.

“Hold up,” he whispered, stopping his crew with a raised hand. “Don’t look directly at it, but our flank has developed... ambitions.”

Behind them, the sentient puddle pulsed in frustration when they stopped moving, its edges creeping forward with predatory intent.

Jer glanced back, brows rising over his intricate mask. “Well, that’s new and horrifying. Add ‘homicidal puddle’ to the ever-growing list of shit trying to kill us today.”

“At least it’s not raining teeth anymore,” Elena muttered, absently picking one of the razor-sharp molars from where it had embedded in her leather pauldron during the last bizarre downpour. “I still don’t understand whose teeth those were. Or why they were falling from the sky.”

“Do you really want to know?” Gav asked, shifting Chubbs higher on his shoulders. The toddler was gleefully arranging her collection of fallen teeth into patterns on the top of his head.

“Pretty gnashers make crown!” she announced proudly.

Above them, the storm continued its relentless assault on the world. Silver-threaded lightning turned the sky into a spider’s web of raw power, striking with equal ferocity across the entire marsh. Each flash illuminated their surroundings in stark, unnatural contrast—twisted trees dripping with phosphorescent fungi, floating globes of gas that pulsed with bioluminescence, flowers that closed instantly when light touched them.

The marsh was a nightmarish wonderland of evolutionary mistakes and magical corruption. The twisted landscape seemed alive in the most literal sense—breathing, shifting, hunting. Perfectly mundane-looking reeds suddenly sprouted barbed tentacles when approached. Harmless-looking moss patches concealed bottomless sinkholes. Even the air itself tasted wrong, though their masks filtered the worst of it.

In short, Cade concluded it was the single worst vacation locale in the world, and he’d once been in a lich’s hut for tea.

“Bunny, you’re up,” Cade called softly to his dragonling. Despite his exhaustion from the gate explosion, he knew they had to keep moving. “Find us a path that won’t, you know, dissolve our bones or turn us inside out.”

The dragonling chirped in acknowledgment, his iridescent scales glimmering with newfound intensity since Chubbs’ divine light show back at camp. He scampered forward, sniffing cautiously at the ground, ears twitching at frequencies no human could detect.

Behind them, the pursuing puddle made a sound like disappointment—a wet, gurgling sigh—before sliding reluctantly back into a larger pool.

“Looking at this place, I’m starting to understand why the Arbiters keep it quarantined,” Rayka said, eyeing a cluster of what appeared to be ordinary mushrooms until they rotated in unison to track her movement. “Though clearly they visit more often than they admit.”

She pointed at faint troughs in the mud that only carriages made.

“Yeah, but I’m sure it has more to do with tracking down and slaying the worst this marsh has to offer. And given how horrible the flora is, I really am not looking forward to how the fauna turns out to be.” Cade stepped over another rug of moss, determined not to fall for that mistake again.

The Son of Ruin pulled out Jameson’s scrap of parchment, squinting at the nonsensical lines that crisscrossed the surface. Rain spattered the ink, making the squiggles even more indecipherable.

“What kind of useless garbage is this?” Elena leaned over his shoulder, her mask gleaming with condensation. “It’s just a bunch of squiggly lines!”

“All maps are just squiggly lines!” Jer threw his hands skyward dramatically. “Do BETTER with your criticism!”

“You do better!” Elena shoved her twin. “Since when are you a cartography expert?”

Cade’s attention snapped away from the bickering twins as something caught his eye—a massive, gnarled cypress tree with bark the color of dried blood. Impossibly ancient, its twisted branches reaching skyward like arthritic fingers.

“Wait,” he frowned, turning in a slow circle. “Didn’t we pass that red cypress ten minutes ago?”

Orro’s eyes narrowed behind his mask. “We’ve been moving in a straight line.”

“I know,” Cade replied, unease prickling along his spine. “But that’s definitely the same tree. Look. There’s the gnarled root that reminds me of Cici’s scowl.”

Everyone stared at the offending root. And for those who knew Cici, they merely nodded in agreement.

“Great,” Jer groaned. “We’re walking in circles. In a place where the ground tries to eat you. Spectacular navigation, Stormhollow.”

Nora studied the crimson cypress, her hand resting on her massive blade’s hilt. “Something’s not right. The positioning is different. If we are walking in circles, then it should be to our right. Now it’s ahead of us.”

As they watched, the ground beneath the massive tree visibly shifted—not quickly, but with the inexorable patience of glaciers. The entire section of marsh containing the cypress was moving, rotating slowly clockwise like a massive, independent island.

“The land,” Cade breathed, revelation dawning as he stared at the map again. “It’s not static. The marsh itself is moving. These lines don’t show paths—they show the migration patterns of the terrain.”

“You’re saying the actual ground is... drifting?” Elena asked, staring in horrified fascination as the cypress continued its lazy rotation.

“Like continents made of malice and poison,” Cade confirmed, tracing the flowing lines on the parchment. “The Clover Clan didn’t map locations but movement.”

“So I was wrong,” Jer concluded with mock brightness. “We’re not walking in circles—we’re standing still while the world’s most miserable carousel spins around us. Much better!”

“Actually, it is,” Rayka observed, studying the map over Cade’s shoulder. “If we can predict how the terrain shifts, we can use it to reach places that might be impossible to get to on foot.”

“I wouldn’t mind letting the very earth shift us to where we need to go. Beats trudging through all this unholy mud,” Gav muttered, adjusting Chubbs on his shoulders as the toddler squealed in delight at the moving landscape.

“Pretty tree dance!” she exclaimed, clapping her hands.

Lightning ripped across the sky, illuminating the crimson cypress in stark relief. In that momentary clarity, Cade noticed something else—carved into the trunk, half-hidden by fungal growth, were symbols. Not random scratches, but deliberate markings.

“There’s writing on that tree,” he said quietly. “Someone’s been mapping these movements for a long time.”

A massive bubble rose from a nearby pool as they contemplated this discovery. Its surface shimmered with rainbow iridescence, swelling to the size of a wagon wheel. The crew watched warily as it grew, tensing as it reached what seemed an impossible size.

Then—WHUMP

The bubble burst, its membrane collapsing into a gelatinous creature that oozed across the ground with alarming speed, absorbing small vegetation in its path. Not responding to their conversation, just another of the marsh’s countless dangers going about its horrific business.

“I vote we don’t stand around to see what else this place has planned for dinner,” Cade said, studying the map with newfound understanding. “According to these flow patterns, we need to head... that way.” He pointed toward a misty section where twisted trees formed an almost-tunnel.

“Of course it’s the creepiest possible direction,” Jer sighed. “Why is it never toward, I don’t know, a nice dry inn with comfortable beds and pretty serving maids?”

“You’d find a way to get us banned from that too,” Elena retorted.

“Move,” Cade urged, finding strength returning to his legs despite the lingering exhaustion from the gate explosion. The bubble-thing was between them and their intended direction, which meant a detour. “Step where it’s solid, single file, stay close.”

“Not where Cade steps,” Chubbs announced with complete confidence from her perch on Gav’s shoulders. “Where Bunny steps!”

The dragonling chirped in what sounded suspiciously like agreement. He straightened his spine, tail held high as he glanced back at Cade with an almost smug expression.

“Smart kid,” Cade acknowledged, nodding to his pet. “Lead on, Bunny.”

Lightning struck less than twenty paces away, the concussion knocking them all sideways. Cade felt the energy resonating with his core—not targeting him specifically, but recognizing something kindred. Like standing beneath a waterfall while holding a cup—he was catching droplets of something vast and indifferent.

“Fun fact,” Jer called out loudly as they regained their footing, “if you count the moments between lightning and thunder, you can tell how screwed we are. One heartbeat means pretty screwed. No separation means completely—”

A simultaneous flash and boom cut him off, the sound so powerful it momentarily deafened them all.

“—screwed,” he finished when their hearing returned. “Like that.”

The first raindrops hit like tiny daggers, raising angry welts on any exposed skin. Cade hissed in pain, yanking his sleeve down to cover his wrist.

“Cover everything!” he barked, pulling his hood tighter. “This rain’s acidic!”

Everyone scrambled to protect themselves as the toxic deluge intensified. The drops sizzled against marsh vegetation, releasing noxious vapor that swirled around their ankles. Even normal puddles began to smoke as the caustic rain mixed with them.

As they navigated through the poisonous haze, visibility shrinking to arm’s length, something massive moved in the periphery of Cade’s vision. A shape easily twenty feet tall, with too many limbs arranged in impossible configurations. It lumbered through the storm, unconcerned with the rain that would have melted human flesh.

“Don’t look,” Cade warned, though he couldn’t tear his own gaze away. “Keep following Bunny.”

The marsh dweller paused, as if sensing their presence, then continued its ponderous journey. Its footsteps left pools of bioluminescent liquid that pulsed in time with the thunder.

Bunny suddenly froze, ears flat against his skull. His growl was barely audible beneath the storm’s fury, but the message was clear: danger ahead.

“What is it, boy?” Cade whispered, drawing his daggers instinctively.

Lightning struck again, illuminating their surroundings in harsh silver-white. The flash revealed what had alarmed Bunny—stretched between the twisted trees ahead was an intricate web that glistened with unnatural moisture. The silken strands spanned at least a hundred feet in every direction, previously invisible in the gloom.

And at the center, waited their architect.

Eight articulated legs supported a bulbous body the size of a farm wagon. Not covered in chitin, but in coarse black and white striped fur like some nightmare fusion of spider and skunk. Its head featured both mandibles and mammalian eyes, along with what appeared to be scent glands positioned to spray whatever unholy chemicals it used to subdue prey.

“What in the Fallen Gods’ name is that?” Elena breathed, horror and fascination mingling in her voice.

“FLUFFY KITTY SPIDER!” Chubbs squealed with delight, struggling against Gav’s restraining grip.

The monster shifted at the sound, multiple eyes swiveling toward them.

“Nobody move,” Cade hissed. “Maybe it hunts by—”

“That’s wyrms, you idiot,” Gav snapped, his eyes beginning to glow amber as his partial transformation triggered. “And it definitely sees us.”

The skunk-spider—because Cade’s brain refused to categorize this abomination any other way—emerged fully from hiding. Its movements were liquid grace despite its impossible size, each leg placing with surgical precision as it navigated its own web.

“Plan?” Orro inquired, already palming multiple throwing knives.

Cade assessed their limited options. Going around meant backtracking and losing their bearing in the shifting marsh. Going through meant confronting a nightmare. Neither appealed.

“Distract and flank,” he decided. “Jer, Elena—circle opposite sides. Nora, Orro—with me for frontal engagement. Gav—get Chubbs and Evie to safety, then join.”

Everyone nodded, understanding without further explanation. They’d faced worse odds. Probably. Maybe.

“On my mark,” Cade whispered, gathering what meager magic had replenished. Silver-gold energy flickered weakly around his fingertips. Not ideal, but it would have to do.

He raised three fingers, counting down silently. Three... two...

Chubbs chose that moment to wriggle free from Gav’s grasp, golden eyes wide with fascination. “FLUFFY KITTY SPIDER!” she shouted again, tiny arms extended toward the monster.

Bunny, evidently interpreting this as their battle signal, launched himself forward with a surprisingly fierce chirp. He bounded straight toward the skunk-spider, his diminutive claws extended.

“So much for the element of surprise,” Cade groaned. “NEW PLAN! DON’T DIE!”

The marsh erupted into a theatre of limbs, mud, and curses.

The skunk-spider reacted with terrifying speed, rearing up to reveal a hidden row of spinneret glands. Ropey strands of web shot toward them, forcing everyone to dive in separate directions.

Orro was first to counterattack, sending four throwing knives into the creature’s eyes. Three struck true, drawing a nightmarish screech that sent marsh birds exploding from cover. The fourth sliced through a large silken strand holding the creature up. The spiderweb buckled under the imbalanced weight.

Nora charged next, her massive blade carving through two of the creature’s legs at the joints. Black ichor sprayed across vegetation, sizzling where it landed.

Bunny somehow climbed onto the monster’s striped back, clawing and biting with surprising effectiveness for his size. The creature bucked wildly, trying to dislodge him.

“Get Chubbs back!” Cade shouted to Gav, who lunged for the toddler. She had somehow evaded his grasp again and was cheerfully waddling toward the battle, clapping her tiny hands at the spectacle.

Cade threw himself into the fray, daggers slicing through web strands that writhed like living things. One wrapped around his wrist, burning through his leather bracer instantly.

“Damn!” he hissed, slicing it free. “The silk burns! Keep it off your skin!”

The twins executed a perfect flanking maneuver despite their chaotic start. Elena’s whip-blade sang through multiple strands, while Jer performed impossible acrobatics to avoid retaliatory strikes.

The skunk-spider, now thoroughly enraged, tensed its abdominal muscles. A noxious spray erupted from its scent glands, expanding outward in a cloud of foul-smelling mist.

Their masks—Chubbs’ divine craftsmanship—filtered the worst of it, but visibility dropped to nothing. Perfect conditions for a predator that hunted by vibration and touch.

“Form up!” Nora commanded, her voice cutting through panic. “Back to back!”

They scrambled to comply, forming a defensive circle as web strands shot randomly into the cloud. Several nearly trapped Cade’s feet.

“I hate spiders,” Jer announced, slicing through a strand that narrowly missed Elena. “I especially hate skunk-spiders the size of a farmhouse. Why couldn’t we fight something nice? Like wobblewumps? Or Fertility? I’d fight her—”

“Less talking, more stabbing!” Cade shouted, his torrent of flames finding unseen flesh, judging by the shriek that followed.

The monster circled them, betrayed only by the squelching sounds of its remaining legs pressing into marsh soil. It hunted methodically now, probing for weakness.

Bunny had disappeared, but occasional flashes of iridescent scales suggested he was executing guerrilla attacks from multiple angles.

“We need to clear this mist,” Cade said, searching for options. His destructive magic could potentially disperse it, but in his weakened state, he risked losing control. “Nora?”

“On it!” she yelled, taking up that water-centric technique she’d shown him when they first started training magic together.

She hissed in a sharp breath through her teeth, and her shoulders relaxed.

“Any day now, paladin!” Orro yelled, but Cade raised a hand to silence him.

Nora let out the breath.

“Whirlpool Guard,” the Dreadcaller whispered, and the world turned into a blur of black and white.

She moved her massive black blade at impossible speeds, practically floating around their tight cluster in her effortless movements. The mist flowed with her steps, following her sword’s directions.

As visibility improved, Cade spotted their opponent—and his blood froze. The skunk-spider had positioned itself directly above Gav and Chubbs, mandibles dripping as it prepared to strike.

Gav had seen it too, his body beginning partial transformation. Claws extended from his fingertips, muscles bulging beneath his clothing, but the change wouldn’t complete in time.

Cade’s body moved before conscious thought. Silver-gold energy surged unexpectedly through depleted reserves—a second wind from somewhere beyond normal capacity. He launched himself forward, dagger extended, directly into the creature’s path.

His blade pierced deep into its underbelly, destruction magic flowing through the metal and into its organs. The monster shrieked, thrashing violently enough to dislodge him and send him tumbling into stagnant water. His dagger remained lodged inside the beast, a sizzling sludge of steel.

The thief cursed.

Cade surfaced sputtering just in time to see Orro execute a perfect leaping strike that buried his longest blade between the creature’s main eyes. Nora followed with a devastating sweep that severed another leg and opened a massive gash in its side.

The skunk-spider, finally recognizing defeat, attempted retreat.

“Oh, no you don’t,” Cade yelled, getting to his feet. “That was my favorite dagger!”

Elena’s whip-blade lashed out, wrapping around one remaining leg. Jer appeared from nowhere, driving both swords through the trapped limb, pinning it to the marshy ground.

“Finish it!” Orro called, his normally impassive face flushed with battle-fury.

Cade summoned his remaining magical reserves, directing a concentrated beam at the creature’s exposed underbelly where his daggers had already penetrated. Silver-gold energy burned through organs and internal structures, unmaking the monster from the inside out.

With a final gurgling shriek, the skunk-spider collapsed, legs curling inward in death.

The marsh fell eerily silent.

“Everyone intact?” Cade called, dragging himself from the fetid pool. His clothes clung to him, soaked in gods-knew-what, but his skin remained unburned beneath protective layers.

“Kitty sleeping now,” Chubbs announced solemnly from Gav’s protective embrace.

“Very sleeping,” Jer agreed, wiping ichor from his blades. “The permanent kind.”

Rayka approached the carcass cautiously, her thief’s eyes analyzing details everyone else missed. “Look here.” She pointed to scarring on the creature’s head. “Old wounds—made by blades. Think Bernard and his friends stopped by?”

“No, these wounds are too clean and scarred over. Arbiter weapons,” Nora answered, cleaning her massive sword on the monster’s furry hide. “Honor’s people venture here despite the quarantine narrative.”

“But why risk it?” Evie wondered, her musical voice troubled.

Cade turned toward the distant spire, briefly illuminated again by another flash of lightning. The silver needle against the sky seemed to call to them—or perhaps to whatever they carried.

The storm intensified, clouds unrolling across the sky like the battle standards of an invading army. Thunder shook the ground beneath their feet, and lightning struck everywhere at once—trees, pools, distant hills, their own island of solid ground—all with terrible equality.

Bunny chirped victoriously, strutting around the fallen monster as if he’d personally slain it. The dragonling’s scales gleamed with iridescence Cade had never seen before, almost as if the battle had awakened something in him. He paused to nip at the creature’s fur, then scampered back to Cade with a broken fang the size of a dagger clutched proudly in his mouth.

“Good boy,” Cade praised, scratching behind his horn nubs. “Though maybe no monster souvenirs next time?”

Another violent lightning bolt tore through the clouds, illuminating the marsh for several heartbeats longer than any previous flash. The momentary daylight transformed the distant spire from mystery to revelation.

Rising from the marsh’s heart stood the unmistakable silhouette of a cathedral. Ancient and massive, its Gothic architecture an impossible anomaly in this primordial wasteland. Flying buttresses and pointed arches supported a central tower that pierced the storm clouds themselves. Partially sunken in the marsh’s grip, yet defiant against time and nature.

“Gods above,” Jer whispered, eyes wide. “How did we miss that?”

“The marsh moves,” his twin sister said while wiggling her fingers spookily.

“Our destination,” Cade said with sudden certainty, recognizing the landmark from Jameson’s cryptic map.

When darkness reclaimed the marsh, the cathedral’s image remained seared into Cade’s vision. The silhouette promised answers—and dangers—beyond anything they’d faced.
