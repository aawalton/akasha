Chapter 83

The settlement was quiet. Not peacefully quiet. It was the quiet of something missing.

Nikola heard the horses first. He had been at the council table by torchlight, going through the latest HUD messages, thinking through timer decisions he already knew because strategy planning was easier to sit with than the casualty numbers that had been there all night. The torch had burned down since he last looked at it and that gave him the excuse to move, which was another welcome distraction. Outside, somewhere past the walls, an insect clicked steadily in the grass and then stopped. The night was quiet enough that sound carried without trying. As he opened the door he stopped, listening for whatever didn't belong.

He was at the gate before the first soldier came through leading his horse, its cargo morbidly precious. Landryn was already there, standing a pace back from the entrance with his arms at his sides. He had his coat on over his work clothes, which meant he had not been to bed either. Zelgra arrived a few steps behind Nikola, her war hammer strapped across her back. There were only a handful of soldiers left in the settlement and she had put it on because someone had to. She stopped when she saw what was coming through the gate and her hand found the haft and stayed there.

Each soldier came in slowly, leading their horses by the reins. The animals moved with their heads down, not pulling, not shying, just moving at the pace they were led. The shapes draped across the saddles swayed with each step. Torchlight caught on wrapped cloaks, on boots, on hands bound at the wrist. One of the horses had a smear of blood dried on its flank that nobody had bothered to wipe off. That detail settled into Nikola's chest and stayed there longer than it should have. Nobody spoke. They just stood there until a single boot slipped from one of the dead and hit the ground. Zelgra snapped out of it first. She crossed to it and picked it up, holding it carefully, as though she intended to return it to its owner. The column turned toward the hospital and Nikola followed it. Landryn fell into step beside him without a word between them.

Inside, the hospital beds ran in two rows along the length of the room. The place still smelled like fresh-cut pine and the sour edge of old blood from the last time it had been used. Each bed was a wide wooden frame strung with rope laced tight across the span, topped with a straw mat wrapped in rough canvas and a single dark wool blanket. All the beds were fastened to the floor, a reminder of what the hospital was and what it was not yet. A small shelf sat above each headboard with a hook for a lantern and nothing else. The lanterns along the walls burned low, their light amber and unsteady, leaving the corners in shadow and making the room feel smaller than it was.

The soldiers began lifting the fallen from the saddles with a care that did not come naturally to them. These were men who would drop freshly cut timber into piles with a crash and think nothing of it, but not on this night. Nikola took hold without being asked. He gripped a pair of boots. Landryn took the other end. They carried the first soldier in and laid him in the nearest bed with the same deliberateness the carrying soldiers had used, and then they went back out for the next.

Zelgra stood in the doorway. She watched the first two bodies carried past her and did not move. Her face had gone still. One of the soldiers coming through misjudged the width of the door and bumped her arm hard enough that she dropped the boot. She stepped aside and collected it, stood there for a moment turning it in her hands, then scanned the room until she found the one who had lost it. She crossed to the bed and carefully put it back where it belonged. After that she did not look at anyone. Just the floor.

Nikola kept his eyes on boots and blankets. Landryn worked like a man trying not to think at all. The beds filled. At some point Nikola stopped counting. He stopped registering faces too. It became the work and only the work, the weight of a body, the distance to the bed, the arrangement of limbs, the blanket pulled to the chest. The lanterns flickered each time the door opened and closed. Once, two soldiers came through at the same time and one of them misjudged it and the door swung back and knocked against the frame with a sound that was too loud for the room and too loud for the hour, but the disruption was left unanswered.

Then the last horse came through. Two soldiers held the reins and two more walked alongside with their hands braced against the figure across the saddle. There was something in the way they positioned themselves, not just handling a body but attending to a person, that made the difference plain before anyone could see the face. They carried him in together and set him in the one of the remaining open beds. They straightened his arms. They pulled the blanket up to his chest with a care that had nothing to do with the task and everything to do with who it was. Then they filed out and the door swung shut behind them and the room was quiet again.

Landryn pulled a stool to the bedside and sat. He looked at Valrik's face for a long time. He did not shake his head. He did not sigh. He just sat there with his forearms on his knees, thumbs rubbing slowly against each other like he was trying to keep his hands busy while everything else in him went still. Nikola stayed near the door. He had meant to cross the room and had not. Zelgra walked to the far wall, pressed her back against the logs, and stared up at the ceiling beams like they owed her money. Nobody spoke for a while.

A flicker moved at the edge of Nikola's vision. He turned his head and found the HUD glowing at the periphery, calm and unhurried. Riley watching from the compound. Already acting, because she was always already acting, because the system did not stop and so she did not either, even now, even out there alone with whatever she was carrying in the dark beyond the tree line. He opened it.

❖ Healing initiated: All units

❖ Timer: 8 hours

He let the display close. She was out there alone with it. That fact sat in his chest.

The glow came quietly, without announcement. It moved through the room in no hurry, spreading from bed to bed like warmth finding its way through cold stone. Most of the soldiers it touched did not move. A hand shifted. Someone's breathing changed cadence and then steadied. Another man turned his head to the side as though settling into sleep that had been waiting for him a long time. The room smelled briefly of something clean and faintly electric that had no name Nikola knew. Landryn noticed the glow when it reached Valrik and leaned forward on the stool.

Nothing happened for a moment. Then another. Landryn did not sit back down. Valrik's face changed, not dramatically, the muscles around his jaw pulling tight, holding for a beat, then releasing. His eyes moved beneath their lids like he was tracking something in a dark room. Landryn got a hand out toward him, not quite touching, hovering an inch above his shoulder. Valrik's lips parted. The sound that came out first was not a word. It was low and rough and effortful; the sound of a voice being used again after it had stopped. Then something gathered behind it and the shape of a name began to form and Landryn was already on his feet when Valrik's eyes flew open and it came out of him.

"Riley…"

He sat upright. "NO!"

The shout filled the room and hit the walls and the low ceiling and sent one of the lanterns guttering hard on its hook. Valrik's hand shot out and seized the bed frame. He stayed there with his chest working and his eyes moving across the room, across the other beds, across the lanterns, across Landryn standing over him with one hand still raised and Nikola by the door and Zelgra against the far wall. His breathing slowed. He looked down at his own hand gripping the bed frame, looked at it for a moment like he was deciding something about it, and then let go. Then his eyes rolled up and the lids closed as he fell back onto the bed, and his chest stopped moving for the longest moment before it slowly started to rise and fall.

He did not explain. Nobody asked him to. Nikola did not move from the door. Zelgra hugged the wall. Landryn lowered his hand and sat back down on the stool. The ghost of Riley’s name hung in the air.
