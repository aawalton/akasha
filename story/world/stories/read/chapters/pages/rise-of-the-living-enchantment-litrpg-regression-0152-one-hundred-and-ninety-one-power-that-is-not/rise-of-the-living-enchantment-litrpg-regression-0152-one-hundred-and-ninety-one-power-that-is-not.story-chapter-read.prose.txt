She must’ve been looking a little too much because her uncle spoke.

“Power that is not benevolent.”

Her uncle gave her a kind smile. “Power is meant to be benevolent. But not the way people think. Power of the people is meant to bend.”

“Shouldn’t that apply to the church?” Elaswit asked.

Her uncle chuckled. “Why?”

“Because what worked two generations ago won’t necessarily work today.”

“But the gods aren’t people. They aren't changing, and their rules aren’t situationally created. Killing a million people years ago does not suddenly become good just because the world has become a harsher place to live in. Stealing, too. Religion, Elly, is not the laws of men.”

“But shouldn’t the church be more forgiving if times are harsher and a person is caught stealing?” she asked. “Doesn’t the church teach mercy?”

“It teaches Wrath and judgement, too,” he pointed out. “It also teaches punishment. If you change the punishment to something more lenient because you want to be understanding, then the world will sin more, because they do not fear sin. Not anymore. The church is not of man that it should bend.”

Elaswit blinked. She had never known her uncle to be religious.

“I know that the church has been accused of abusing their powers on multiple occasions,” he continued. “But that is just the sad reality of life. Like most perfect things, religion has been ruined by the insurmountable imperfection that is human flaw. And it is in the nature of human flaw to corrupt the beauty of all things, even the gods and religion.”

Her uncle turned to her, looked into her eyes with all seriousness in his face as a maid came down the stairs, undoubtedly approaching them.

“When a priest shows you injustice, Elaswit,” he said very carefully. “Hate the priest, not the church.”

“She did leave a message should you come,” the maid added quickly.

Uncle Tradis’ brows furrowed at that. “What is the message?”

“She said not to worry, and that while Valsin is not where it is supposed to be, it is safe.”

Elaswit had never seen her uncle go pale before. And she had never seen anyone pale as fast as her uncle did.

“The [Spear of Valsin],” he grabbed at his hair, thinking, worried, “is the strongest known heroes’ relics.”

“Oh.” It was all she had to say.

Her uncle ran a calming hand through his head and controlled his breathing. He stood up straight and let out a calming breath.

Everywhere was silent once more.
