“What do you mean we are not welcome here?” Levi asked, confused as to why they were being denied help.

A look of hatred and disgust crossed the guard's features as he pointed his spear at Levi's chest. “Your kind, who have decided to be controlled and to worship so-called Cerpital as a god, are nothing but a shame to humanity.”

Levi's face turned blank as he was taken aback by the guard's words.

Then again, he did not have time to go back and forth with the guard, so he was left with no other option but to change his course and tactics. He looked up to the guard. “Please help her, she is dying as you can see, she doesn't have long, I just need a healer and we will be out of your way.“

The angry guard turned and looked at him, his eyes flashing with vehemence. ”I have told you, your kind are not welcome here. The unsynced are nothing more than puppets being controlled by Cerpital; you have no free will, and if you both should die of your injuries, then that is the greatest gift we can give you.”

“And unless you want to join your partner who is already dying, I suggest you leave and find a place to bury her, or take your chances here, of which I guarantee you that my people will not hesitate to kill you and leave your carcasses for the beasts.”

How exactly am I supposed to respond to that? These people seem to think that I am cursed just because I am synced. How am I supposed to appeal to their sense of empathy? How am I supposed to get to them?

Levi stared at the guard in silence. Even though he wanted to end the man’s life, he couldn’t, as the rogue was already worn out from the long journey that it took to get here.

His constant use of mana had sapped him of more than he had expected, making him weaker, and he wasn't even sure he could take on a single guard, let alone more of those who were hidden up in the trees.

So he did the only other thing he could do. “Please help, I have money, I can get you whatever you want, just save her life.”

The guard poked his spear towards the chest of the rogue again. This time, the steel part of the weapon bit into his skin and drew blood. It caused Levi to wince in annoyance more than pain because the spear itself hadn't gone deep, but it had sent the message that the guard was unmoved by their plight.

They were not welcomed here, and it was almost going to be impossible for him to get a healer for Xara elsewhere, with the short window of opportunity the mage had.

Levi considered his options.

He could decide to stay here and risk getting killed, or maybe if they chose not to kill him, he could stay long enough to gather enough mana for a void step that he could use to get into the village. If he were lucky enough, he would find the healer and force them to take care of Xara, but that didn't seem like a good plan, especially since he would be entering into enemy territory with an almost dead mage weighing him down.

It would not be easy enough for him, and so he was left with no other choice but to barter or beg for healing, and at the moment, it seemed like they were uninterested in anything that he had to offer.

The second guard spoke up, and she was a female, as she seemed to be dressed in the local attire of the villagers. Her voice was soft, but at the same time cold. “Slow down, Gazun. Let's hear the synced puppet out.”

Levi didn't know whether to feel offended or relieved by the second guard's words.

But from what he'd heard, there might be a chance for him to save Xara's life and maybe even his own.

The female guard turned to him.

“I can clearly see that you are both tired and injured,  but it is not in our habits to allow any sync, injured or not, within our village. That is a practice that has been held for years. We have been allowed to do as we wish, and we would rather not deal with your kind. However, just for the sake of the girl on the verge of death, I would bring you to the village chief, and he will be the one to decide if your friend is worth saving or not. But I warn you, if he should decide that neither of you is worth saving, you will be killed.”

“If you refuse to go with us now, we will let you go, both of you. Hobble and stumble your way out of our territory. And not a single person would lay a hand on you. But if our chief should sense that you are a threat to us, you both will be killed on the spot.”

A lump formed in his throat at the words of the female guard; he couldn't help but stare at the mage, who was barely as alive as he was. In fact, she seemed to be drifting in and out of consciousness, and he was certain that she hadn't heard the conversation that was going on.

The rogue wondered if it was worth risking his life for the mage.

Just a few hours ago, he had chosen to pick the portalist subclass over the healer class, and in essence, he had picked himself over her. This was the same two options being presented to him again. Would he pick his life over hers for a second time? Would he abandon her only chance at surviving just to keep his own head intact?

There’d be no other outcome for her if he decided to decline the female guard's offer to be introduced to the village chief. There was no way that the mage would survive the next two hours without some sort of help from a healer.

He was once again at a crossroads.

While it was true that the mage had gotten him good gear, he wasn't sure that he would want to sacrifice his life for her.

At the moment, he would willingly return all her gear to her if he meant that he didn't have to deal with all these problems. But at the same time, he had to accept the fact that she was as much of his problem as he was hers, considering the fact that she'd agreed to go on this journey with him, even though she had known that he was the obsidian rogue.

She hadn't even flinched when they returned to his room and found it ransacked.

In fact, she had joined to try to fight when they discovered the break-in, and she hadn't called it quits after that encounter, which was the logical thing to do.

He might have a chance to very well save her life, probably her last chance. And if she did die this time, her blood would definitely be on his hands because he had been given two chances to try to save her.

And if he declined this one, then it would make him a murderer of a partner, no different from Ghost using and discarding people just to get whatever he wanted.

“Well, what is your answer? Speak now or get out.” The male guard shouted at Levi.

A flicker of annoyance flashed through his features at the way the guard had spoken to him.

But he didn't make any hostile movements as he knew his and Xara's lives were hanging in the balance. Therefore, there was nothing more he could do. “I accept your offer.”

I hope I don't get killed because of this.

But if I do, what better way is there to die than trying to save someone's life?

The guards shared a silent look and then nodded to each other. Then the female guard spoke.

“Very well. Follow us.”

She opened the wooden gates to the sight of the village. The village was filled with numerous dirt paths and houses. He saw adults and children milling about, dressed in the same attire as the guards.

The only difference was that most of the adults and children were unarmed, although a few of them carried bone daggers and similar weapons. A few of them shot both  Levi and the mage curious looks.

“Move.” The male guards ordered as he pressed the Tip of his spear against his spine.

The feel of the cold steel on his skin was all it took to bring him back to attention. Levi’s anger had begun to rise, but instead of doing anything rash, he chose to follow the guard's command, hauling Xara. Along with him as he walked behind the female guard who led the way.

She led them down the dirt path, going through twists and turns. The way was lined with various buildings, which these villagers had obviously constructed based on their composition. They walked silently until they ended up in front of a building that was slightly bigger than the rest. Levi did not have to be a genius to guess that it belonged to the village head.

The female guard turned to him, “We're here,” she said.

The rogue’s sole response to her words was a grunt, but that seemed to be enough for the guard as she nodded to Gazun, who was behind them, and walked into the building.

Levi was left to wait with the mage’s body still pressed against his and the male guard behind him. The point of his spear pressed against the rogue’s spine.

For what it was worth. Levi didn't doubt that if he were at full strength, he would be able to take on this guard easily, in fact. He believed he would be able to kill him within a breath. But unfortunately, he was not at full strength. Furrowing his brows in frustration, he turned his attention to checking out His current vicinity.

He scanned the other buildings, and while he could attest to the fact that they weren't of the same quality as the ones he had seen back in the city, they were very close to the standard of the ones he had grown up seeing in Gantus: the structural work and architectural look of the place looked solid, and he couldn't help but internally applaud them for achieving such a feat.

The buildings had obviously been made without the help of any synced. This meant that these people had relatively good builders within their ranks and it was rather impressive to see the work of those who were able to construct such buildings, even without the help of skills.

Done with his assessment of their buildings, his eyes drifted to the kids playing around. Many of them had stopped to stare at the pair from a safe distance. The arrow sticking out of her shoulder was unsurprisingly the main focus of their stares. Evidenced by how most of the kids pointed towards it.

The absence of laughs, cheers, or jeers from the kids and other adults around confused him. Gazun had made it clear that synced were not welcome in this place, and yet the regular adults and children in the village weren’t hostile to them. If anything, they seemed intrigued.

Rather than try to wrap his head around why that was, Levi returned his gaze to the entrance of the village chiefs' building. His timing was impeccable as he managed to catch sight of the female guard exiting alongside a figure that defied his expectation of what a chief was supposed to look like.

He had expected a portly middle-aged man with a beard or maybe even an old man with gray hair, walking with a cane, but the man who’d walked out of the building alongside the female guard was nothing like he’d imagined.

In fact, he waited for the female guard to tell him that it wasn't true, that this was someone else.

But when she spoke, she only confirmed his thoughts. “Chief Meron, these are the two synced puppets that I informed you about.”

The Chief, who was bare-chested, stared down at the pair, his frame easily towering over them both, and his muscles were intimidating to say the least. His hardened gaze caused a chill to creep up the rogue's spine. The chief might have been an unsynced, but Levi innately knew that he was dangerous.

When the chief finally spoke, his voice sounded like crushed gravel. “Normally, you both would be on your knees,  but I can tell that neither of you is capable of doing that without going unconscious.”

“From where I’m standing, I think it's a waste of time to invite either of you into our homes if I'm just going to kill you anyway. But there may be a chance for you both to walk out of here alive. In fact, I am here to offer you that chance.”

Gazun had stepped out of the way to allow the chief to move freely. An action in which Levi’s spine appreciated as he no longer had the steel part of the guard's weapon biting into his skin.

“I am Chief Meron, Leader of the Aseroq Tribe.” He said as he circled the pair like a predator.

The chief radiated the authority of someone who had been uncontested for a while. Confidence and power oozed from every word. His tone brooked no arguments. “I know that you both have synced to the puppet master. You have sold your souls in exchange for power, and if I am to be honest, my heart bleeds for you both, and  I would prefer to end your suffering here and now by killing both of you, but I sense that neither of you wants that. So I've decided to offer you what you think you want, an opportunity to heal your friend… but I’d require a service in exchange.”

“What do you want?” Levi asked.

That was the only question that could be asked at this point. It was easy to tell from the chief's demeanor that if he had said anything that wasn’t in line with what the chief wanted to hear, they would have lost their lives on the spot.

“I am sure that you are well aware of the multiple monster leaks of your master dungeons. Of course, we are still a far way away from the dungeon itself, but most of the monsters who have escaped often attack us, and one troublesome beast in particular Has decided to make its dwelling close to ours. It's called the krupi, and while I have sent multiple of my men to kill it, None of them have returned.” The chief said.

“This beast of the puppet master has even tried to take the village once, but we managed to thwart it, at a not-so-insignificant cost to our numbers. We are not equipped to deal with the creations of your master, and so it managed to escape. As you are well aware, we are not like the big cities that have soiled themselves with multiple synced like you to fight their battles, and if you want me to heal your friend, I will gladly do so, but in exchange. You will have to bring me the head of the krupi by noon tomorrow. If not, I'll kill your friend.”

Levi hesitated to respond.

The rogue has no idea what kind of monster these people had fought. To be honest, he wasn't even sure he wanted to fight any monster that managed to escape a dungeon by himself.

There was also the question of whether he could trust these people to let them go if he succeeded in his quest to slay the Krupi and bring back its head. He feared that they’d betray him, but that was the only offer he was going to get. So he was going to have to decide very fast because the chief's patience was obviously waning.

“If I may, my chief,” Gazun spoke, his voice tinged with tightly controlled rage, ”I don't think we should allow these outsiders, these synced, to meddle in the affairs of our village. They should be killed where they stand and their—”

The guard trailed off as the chief fixed him with a hard stare.

“I understand your worries and concerns, Gazun, but I think it is better for us to fight fire with fire.

We should let the puppets fight themselves rather than waste the lives of good people

of our tribe,” he said, before turning to face Levi. “Let the puppets fight and kill themselves. Whoever wins does not concern us. If he should die while fighting the krupi, that does not concern us, and if so happens that he fails in his quest, then you are free to kill the girl.”

“However, if you are able to kill the krupi and return with its head, then you and your friend will be allowed to leave, I swear it on the honor of my ancestors.” The chief said. “You are also free to decline this offer, and it will be my pleasure and that of my kinsfolk to relieve you both of your lives and cut you off from the strings of the puppet master.”

Levi stared at the chief, his mind whirring. The Chief had certainly made it clear that they did not mind if he died fighting the krupi, and they seemed to think that Cerpital is a puppet master.

The people of the village also seemed to believe that the synced and the synced monsters were

all the same, and he could not fault their logic, as they were all linked to Cerpital. At the same time, he was human, just like them. The only difference was that he had synced, and they were unsynced.

The chief's casual indifference to the possibility he’d die made his skin itch, but he pushed the feeling to the side—It wouldn’t be the first time that he’d be taking a job that he wasn't particularly comfortable with.

Moreover, this was his opportunity to save both their lives, and he was the one who’d made the decision to see the chief even at the risk of death. Now, his only real response could be yes; anything other than that, and he’d be food for the worms.

Steeling his resolve, he locked eyes with the chief.  “I’ll do it.”

“Good.” The chief grunted, then turned his attention to the guards. “You both should take the girl to see the healer, then return to your posts. I would like to have a few words alone with him before he departs.”

The guards nodded silently and relieved Levi of the weight of Xara, leaving the rogue alone with the chief.
