“In Blooded etiquette, courtesy is the velvet sheath over the poisoned blade.” — Veyl aphorismVelca vanished into the dark, her silver eyes reflecting light as she trailed off into the night.

Red stared after her a second longer. Then turned to John. Not angry—just tired. That kind of quiet, disappointed tired only reserved for people you love who’ve done something deeply dumb.

John shifted. “I probably deserve that look.”

Red didn’t reply. Just nodded at the gun still in John’s hand.

“You pull that on her?”

John holstered the PD11. Slow. “Yeah. And I punched her.”

Red grunted. Walked forward—bandaged and bruised but still moving like a freight truck built for wartime logistics. He stopped right in front of John. Met his eyes.

John sighed.

[Skill Activated: Hardbody Lv. 2.][Skill-Energy Remaining: 3.]

Red slugged him.

John hit a knee. Pain went nova through his jaw. Blood traced the taste of copper on his tongue.

“Fair,” he muttered, rubbing his chin. “We even now?”

Red sniffed. Turned toward the Boilpoint. “Yeah. I got Onikara ramen going. Made enough for two.”

John got up with a wince and followed.

Athena flickered into view behind them. “I do not understand either of you.”

John gave her half a smile. The other half of his face hurt too much to use.

Inside, the Boilpoint was dim. Quiet. Closed.

Red moved behind the bar, pulled two bowls from the warmer, and set one in front of John.

They sat in steam and silence.

“So,” Red said.

John lifted noodles. “She tried to hit me first.”

Red raised a brow. “Looked like she was gonna finish the job.”

John snorted. “Didn’t get me the first time. And I wasn’t done.”

Red poured sake into two cheap cups. The kind he served to humans and customers, not friends. Weak brew.

John took his. “Glad you’re alright.”

Red downed his, poured again.

“Should you be drinking?” John muttered. “You got shot.”

“I’m fine.”

John eyed him. Wrapped in blood-stained bandages. Moving like none of it mattered. “I noticed.”

Red didn’t meet his gaze. “You here about what happened, or the other job you talked me into?”

“Told Velca I came for you. Her too, I guess.”

Red frowned. “What do you mean?”

“I mean stop bullshitting me, Kai. You don’t eat a skill-infused trench-gun to the chest and walk it off. Not even an oni does that.”

Red exhaled. “Armor stopped most of it.”

“No, it didn’t,” John said flat. “Ghaz shot you in the gap.”

“You think I design my stuff that sloppy?” Red replied. “Had synth-Kev laced through the padding. Only a little got through.”

John nodded toward the crimson soaking his wrappings. “Doesn't look like a little gunshot wound.’”

Red stared at the counter. “You’re not my NCO anymore, John. And I’m tired.”

John sipped his sake. “Was it her blood?”

Red flinched.

“What do you think you know?”

“I know she had her claws to my throat tonight. And I know how fast you bounced back. I have assumptions. And some other info to back them up.”

Red’s grip on the bar went tight. “I love her.”

John nodded. “And that’s fine. You stood up to her and to me—makes me think she hasn't turned you into a blood bag. Or fried your brain. Her hating me? Doesn’t matter. You still being you? That’s what counts in my book of shits to give."

Red relaxed. Just a hair. “Thanks.”

“You’ve earned my trust a dozen times over.”

Red glanced down. “More times than that.”

John sipped. “Sure.”

“I was gonna come after you. If Kaito hadn’t let you walk.”

“I figured.” He paused. “But why’d he call Velca? Said something about ‘courtesy.’ Sounded like some conspiracy bullshit.”

“I wasn’t very awake when she came to get me,” Red said. “Didn’t ask.”

John huffed. “I was out cold after my vampire blood sampler too.”

Red tried pivoting. “He gave you blood?”

John squinted. “She’s been with you all day. Unless she left you alone—which I doubt—you had time to ask what her deal with Kaito is.”

He paused. Red didn't respond.

“Oh, you motherfuckers,” John said, realization dawning. “You were gonna come get me after you two were done.”

Red gave a tiny grin. Caught. “You blame me... or her? I almost died. Ghaz hit me hard.”

John rolled his eyes. “Yeah, well… isn’t she like a corp—”

Red raised a hand. “Finish that sentence and I hit you again.”

John shrugged. “Fair. Old habits--but I'd hit you back this time."

Red drank. “Velca told me Kaito doesn’t screw over people who handle business. Figured you were breathing and not being drained. That was enough not to go full breach-and-clear.”

“Reasonable,” John admitted, slurping broth. “His place is a fortress.”

Red leaned in slightly. “How much blood he give you?”

“Just a drop. I think.”

“You feel different?”

“Why? What happens if it’s too much?”

Red’s voice flattened. “Depends. And I don’t know all the rules.”

John narrowed his eyes. “Guess Velca's still got secrets.”

Red didn’t argue.

“I feel fine. He did try to mind-whammy me, though.”

Red blinked. “Did it work?”

“Not for long. My magic got in the way.”

A lie. But not one he liked. Athena wasn’t something he could explain right now—not without derailing the current topic.

Red leaned back. “The way you fought back in the undercity... and now this. You’re really a mage now.”

“Sort of,” John muttered. “Can’t use much.”

“Why not?”

John exhaled. “Something called an Incarnate. Basically a magic soul-ghost. Mine’s pissed off. Locked my spells.”

Red grimaced. “That’s rough.”

John raised his glass. “Tell me about it. I'm special now—but the special part of me hates me.”

“What’d you do to piss it off?”

“If you find out, let me know.”

Red poured again. “Mana and no way to use it? Waste.” He paused. “I could teach you ki.”

Athena appeared on a nearby stool, arms folded. “I have also considered this.”

John didn’t look at her. Just raised an eyebrow at Red. “What’s it take?”

“Not hard enough not to learn. Just takes focus. You breathe right, learn to feed mana and skill-energy together. Push them into each other. Make ki. Channel it into your Skills.”

“No angry ghost involved?”

“None. But if you want to fight with it, I’m gonna need to hit you a few more times.”

John smirked. “Think I can manage. Maybe with some painkillers."

Red nodded. “Before or after we meet with the Retainers?”

“Tonight work? I’ve got another job. Day and a half from now.”

Red raised a brow. “What job?”

“Kaito wants me to break into the Grin’s data-fortress.”

“Residential tower. Full of said zombie-puppets.”

She tilted her head. “You may be right. If he can explain the internal mechanics, I should be able to replicate them. Your body’s skill-energy and mana pathways are already within my realm of control.”

John shrugged. "You got anything better to do than prove me wrong? I already scared off your company.”
