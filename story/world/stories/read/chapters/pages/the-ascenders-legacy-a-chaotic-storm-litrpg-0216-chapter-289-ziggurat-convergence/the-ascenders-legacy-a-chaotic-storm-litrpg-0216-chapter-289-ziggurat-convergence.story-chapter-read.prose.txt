Back in the continent of Lutia, news of the cultist location spread like wildfire despite being strictly on a need-to-know basis. It had only been a few minutes since Aldric found the spatial graft, but already, the armies of each kingdom had begun assembling—from champions to lowly soldiers. Even mercenaries and private guards joined the assembling force, not out of coercion, but because the cultists had offended so many people, and no one wanted to miss out on the chance to pay them back.

The Ziggurat itself had been swarmed with a host of eager soldiers. Thousands of them filled the basin, armed to the teeth and radiating a thick haze of bloodlust.

Floating above them was a host of mythic-class awakened, waiting for the go-ahead to join the attack, each one radiating an aura of power and barely leashed violence.

Already the might of those gathered around the ziggurat was enough to conquer a city in one fell swoop, and unfortunately for the cultists, their only exit out of the order was through the ziggurat.

They would either have to defend themselves from within or brave the growing army of champions, elites, and mythics waiting outside.

The cultists chose the former, but that only bought the alliance more time to prepare.

Already, they had numbers on their side, but without the mythics cleared to engage, General Lucas and the other members of the alliance refused to move. Doing so would mean underestimating the cultists, and after all they had seen in the ziggurat, no one was willing to take that risk.

And so, they waited while the councils of lords deliberated.

Bringing mythics to any battlefield posed a significant danger to the terrain. Their power was beyond what the natural reinforcement of ambient essence allowed. But not bringing mythics to this battle spelled destruction for the armies of the alliance because the cultists certainly wouldn't be considering the terrain.

And so, in only half an hour—the shortest duration for a decision since the formation of the council—the lords came to a decision.

Every single mythic who was once a soldier or champion was to be recalled into battle. They would be more careful than the average mythic but also more skilled.

This decision spread through the four kingdoms like wildfire, and in minutes, the number of mythics floating above the ziggurat quadrupled. Former champions and soldiers all over the realm rushed to the scene without hesitation, eager to flex their power and engage themselves in a real battle after what, for some, had been decades of inaction and bone-deep boredom.

But the mythics weren't the only ones eager to shed cultist blood. The cultists had destroyed the lives of so many people that this was the perfect chance for many to avenge the deaths of their loved ones.

And as soon as they heard the news, these people began making their way to the military sectors of the kingdom, eagerly signing up and conscripting themselves as soldiers. The kingdoms accepted them eagerly, vetting their identities and making them swear oaths to certify their allegiances.

Despite that, people came out in droves to take the oath without hesitation, their gazes determined and eyes burning with an unquenchable need for vengeance.

One of such people was Baron Shrapanelly and his trusted assistant, Enrique LifeHeart, who had heard the news from one of their contacts in the military camp of Conquestia.

Ever since the whole piracy and smuggling trial with Aldric Brystion, both men had tried their very best to keep a low profile. Baron Shrapanelly had had to pay a hefty sum of money to bail both of them out of jail, and with the loss of several properties, contacts, and businesses, his hatred for the cultists had only increased.

So, the moment he had heard the news that the alliance was gathering soldiers, he hadn't hesitated. The cultists had killed his entire family—wife and children—to foster their abominable rituals.

For years, he had wondered just how he could avenge them, and the moment Jeffery had fallen into his lap, he had thought that was his opportunity. But this was another opportunity, and he would rather die than miss it.

"Do you think they'll take us?" Enrique asked as they made their way to the teleportation hub. "We are not soldiers, after all."

"They'll take us," Baron Shrapanelly replied without doubt in his heart. "They have to take us. I don't care about the oath. I'll do anything they ask if it means I can put a spear through a fucking cultist, and I'll be damned if I let this opportunity pass me by."

Enrique nodded, his own determination rising with his master's words. Eyes hard, he glanced at the servant working the hub and ordered, "Activate the portal. The baron and I wish to go to the seventh sector."

The servant complied immediately, and Baron Shrapanelly watched as the portal slowly hummed to life, anticipation burning in his veins.

It had been fifteen years now since he lost his family to the cultists. It was high time he avenged them and put their spirits to rest.

It was high time he sated his bloodlust.

Matharantha sat cross-legged in the silence of her meditation chamber, crystalline essence floating in an orbit around her like translucent moons. The moons caught the light from the mirrors around her and bent them into streams of colorful energies that wrapped around her form, weaving patterns that faded as quickly as they appeared.

She had kept herself relatively busy ever since her ascension into the mythic class, occupying herself majorly with the creation of a shield of perfect reflection. The project was the main reason she had been forced to ascend, after all, and for the first few days after her ascension, the project had been her life.

Despite not providing the thrill of battle, it was still a fascinating project—creating a shield that was virtually impossible to destroy, as it simply reflected every attack on it.

However, after a full week of near-obsessive crafting and testing, Matharantha had begun feeling the creeping sense of boredom in her bones. The kidnap of Aodhán Brystion and his comrades had spiced things up a bit, but after the initial excitement, things had eventually settled back down into boredom.

No longer a champion, Matharantha had been cut off from the champion chat group, placing her completely in the dark concerning the steps the alliance would be taking.

It was hell, not being actively involved in every single problem the kingdom was facing, and Matharantha hated it.

To combat the growing sense of uselessness and boredom, Matharantha began occupying herself with anything at all that she found exciting. She had found many, but with her current power and speed, they had inevitably lost their spark after a few hours.

And with every activity she found unchallenging, the sense of boredom crept closer, eating deeper into her bones, until finally, only a few hours ago, she had finally gained something thrilling enough to excite her indefinitely.

Congratulations! You have gained the Aspect of Duality.

Every reflection contains two sides—the real and the reflected. Manipulate the dual nature of existence by pulling things into their opposite states.

It was an understatement to say Matharantha was surprised when she had gained the aspect. She hadn't even been working toward it, giving herself a break from cultivation to let her core and spirit settle into her new class first before doing anything.

But even if she had been working toward it, Duality wasn't the affinity she would have expected to gain. With an affinity like reflection, she would have expected an aspect like glass, refraction, or even infinity. But duality?

It wasn't an affinity she was familiar with at all, but from the little explanation the system had given, she could already imagine the potential uses.

With duality, she had seemingly gained the ability to warp existence itself like a reflection, pulling health into injury, courage into fear, life into death, and truth into lie.

Most likely, she would have to refine the aspect to the max before she could pull life into death or anything so astronomical, but the fact that the possibility was there…

Matharantha shook her head. The aspect was mind-blowingly powerful, and she couldn't help but wish she could test it out on the cultists, or at the very least test it out in battle.

Duality.

The aspect whispered to her through every reflection, tugging at her mind, humming within her spirit, practically begging to be used. The itch soared, and without conscious thought, Matharantha stretched a hand out, hundreds of reflections around her copying the motion.

For a fleeting moment, nothing changed, and then one reflection lowered its hand, revealing a crimson slash that ran through it from wrist to shoulder. Blood dripped to the ground in a pool.

Matharantha stared at the reflection for a moment, mesmerized, and then she spoke. "You are real."

Immediately, she felt pain lance through her arm, and when she glanced at it, she saw the exact same scar she had seen in the reflection on her skin, blood dripping.

Health into injury.

A thrill stirred in her chest, sharp as a blade.

"If only the cultists stood before me now," she whispered, bone-white hair peeking beneath her cowl as her body shuddered in silent laughter. "I would show them what it means to be divided against oneself."

As if fate itself had been waiting for the words, a ping resounded in her mind, and Matharantha frowned as she opened the message.

To Matharantha, (The Mirror)

By order of the crown, you have been summoned to battle against the cultists…

She had barely read the message completely before her lips split into a wide predatory grin, blood-red lips glistening against her marble-white skin.

"I accept," she whispered as she rose, reflections trembling all around her as mirrors began rippling like liquid glass, warping and folding until her presence vanished.

An instant later, the chamber was empty—save for the reflections, which still stared in mute confusion, their gazes darting as though searching for the true Matharantha.

But Matharantha was long gone.

This might be her one and only chance to fight in a battle as a mythic, and she didn't intend to miss it. At the very least, she could test out her new aspect without restraint.

In the eastern province of the Calodan kingdom, hidden behind the marvelous dunes of Kruzuvel, lay a small temple, its white-gold exterior blending so perfectly with the sand that from a distance it seemed part of the desert itself.

Despite its remoteness—the nearest clan nearly a hundred miles away—the occupants of this temple still heard the news of the cultists and of the army the alliance was raising.

They needed no messengers. In truth, it was very likely that the temple heard the news long before the royal family themselves.

It was, after all, a sanctum of telepaths.

Of the four kingdoms of Lutia, Calodan awakened the most telepaths, psychics, and mind-affinitied awakeneds. One might assume that, surrounded by so many, the people of Calodan would have grown accustomed to the absence of mental privacy. But the reverse was the case.

Telepaths were respected, even celebrated once a year in a festival made for them—but loved was another matter entirely. Their abilities were a thorn in the sides of proud chieftains and a blade at the throats of ambitious nobles.

And so, for the good of all, the temple had been built—to house every telepath. As soon as anyone, be it male or female, awakened the affinity of telepathy, they were taken to the temple to refine their abilities and train themselves in peace.

To the world, it was a place of safety. But to the telepaths, it was a prison.

However, years of peace and seclusion had worn down that resentment. The temple, far from the endless press of civilization's thoughts, had become home. Here, with only a few hundred of their own, the telepaths refined their craft and found peace.

Some had even begun to believe the world had forgotten them—until one day, one of their brightest members had suddenly vanished during a routine excursion to the nearest town to barter for supplies the temple did not make for itself.

Four had gone, but only three returned. And with them came no blood, no body—only a stark confusion as to how Mirith Vaelys, the brightest telepath the temple had seen in centuries, had simply vanished, kidnapped from among them without a trace.

Mirith's strength and cunning had already caught the eye of the temple's highest echelons—her power so vast and methods so unpredictable that whispers had begun to circulate that, for the first time in centuries, a true telepathic champion might rise among them.

So, when she failed to return, the temple had immediately dispatched search parties. It hadn't taken long to learn of the involvement of cultists in the matter, and they had quickly taken the matter up to the royal chieftain, where they discovered that the kidnapping wasn't an isolated event.

Mirith was not the only one. Prodigies across Lutia had been stolen away.

But when the temple offered their aid to the alliance, the royal chieftain refused them, citing a series of excuses that would make it awkward for everyone. Bitterly disappointed yet bound by oath to obey the crown, the temple withdrew, forced to wait in silence—and hope.

And then, at last, the news came. The cultists had been found.

This time, however, the temple would not remain behind, whispering prayers into the void and hoping their lost jewel might be returned. No—they would go themselves, and they would bring her back.

And if, along the way, the minds of a few thousand cultists were shattered into silence, then so be it.

For the first time since the Attilan incursion, an army of telepaths stirred. For the first time in centuries, the temple would not wait nor watch the world from the shadows of thought. They would march.

And the cultists would learn what it truly meant to wage war against the mind.
