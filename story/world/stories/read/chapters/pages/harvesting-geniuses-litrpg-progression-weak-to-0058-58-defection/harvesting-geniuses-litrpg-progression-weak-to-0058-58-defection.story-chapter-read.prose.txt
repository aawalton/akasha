“What…? How does it know our names?” Nephrin’s chest tightened with dread. Encountering a monster with intelligence was stressful enough on its own. But it also knew her name—and everyone else’s. The feeling of being read through completely was deeply unsettling.

“A long life, you say. That does sound like a generous offer for an ordinary human.” James chuckled softly through his throat.

“What happens to the other three you didn’t name?” Esther addressed the creature directly. Both Nephrin and James each stepped back one pace the moment he finished speaking.

“Luciria Halfrin was originally scheduled to die on October 4th. But due to someone’s interference, a person who deserved to die is still alive.” The moment the Lord of the Mountains finished speaking, Luciria’s face drained of color and she began shaking uncontrollably. The memory of facing the Locust Demon crashed into her. She was afraid—the truth that someone wanted her dead made her shake so hard she couldn’t stop.

“Jinyo Southerncross and Gretel Erindel were originally meant to live. But His Lordship has changed his mind. These two names will no longer exist in the future. Only three will have the chance to return alive. Should you submit, whatever you desire will become reality. Riches beyond spending, freedom from disease, a lifespan of hundreds of years—these are merely the basic benefits.” The Lord of the Mountains delivered its offer in plain words alone, yet the words carried an irresistible pull that compelled belief. It was strange—there was no evidence to trust, nothing to justify it—yet those who heard were drawn toward those hollow words as if by gravity.

Endless wealth. Freedom from sickness. A hundred years of life as a bare minimum benefit. Any way you looked at it, these were things every human being desired. But they were mere basic amenities—the floor, not the ceiling. Which meant if one wanted something else entirely, they could have that too.

‘No. Get a grip. It’s a monster!’ Nephrin shook her head and slapped her own cheek to snap herself back. Never trust a monster’s words—that was the most basic principle of being a Hunter.

“It’s an impressive offer. But if you want Miss Luciria dead, I don’t see myself looking for a new employer right now. For me, a long life is just a longer stay in hell. Please don’t make my existence any more painful than it already is.” James gave his answer without a moment’s hesitation. Luciria found herself looking at the butler she wasn’t particularly close with. If not for Sven asking him to accompany her during this of all times, the two of them wouldn’t have interacted much at all. Why would he be willing to give his life for her?

“So if I submit, I get riches and a long life.” The words slipped out from the mouth of the person no one wanted to hear speak.

“Hey—don’t trust a monster.” Nephrin whipped around with a glare.

“Correct, Mister Esther. Monsters love to use beautiful words to lure humans in. What they mean and what we understand are never the same thing. Don’t let it deceive you.” James went so far as to deliver a lengthy lecture. What no one had anticipated was Esther’s age. Esther was the strongest among them—but he was young. A hot-blooded, impulsive young man.

The Lord of the Mountains glanced sideways at the young man with the world-weary face. Its eyes shifted just slightly.

“Of course. Riches and jewelry. A long lifespan. Strength beyond your current limits. And above all, you will become part of the architects of a new Heaven. You will be freed from your fate without having to stand as Heaven’s enemy.”

“Enemy of Heaven. What an arrogant way to put it. Sounds like you’re claiming you actually come from Heaven.”

“When you follow His Lordship, you will learn the truth of the cosmos. As for all those who are unnecessary—they will be eliminated. All of it for a perfect Heaven.”

“Interesting. Then I have a proposal of my own.” Esther raised his red Ether Blade and pointed it at the Lord of the Mountains.

“My Divine Punishment—the Heretic Inquisitor—exalts the righteous and despises the wicked. If you are truly a representative of a good and righteous Heaven, the Divine Punishment will not respond. But if you carry even a trace of guilt, I will fight you to the death.”

“Do as you please.”

“Then—Release Divine Punishment.” The Ether Blade drank in its wielder’s Planar and shifted its form. The Divine Punishment revealed its true shape and seized its hold over the Lord of the Mountains. Esther’s ruby eyes shifted to gold.

The Golden Eyes was a skill that saw through a target’s transgressions—even minor ones, rendering them as case files. Since Esther had obtained his Golden Eyes, the skill had never once malfunctioned. It sorted the righteous from the wicked with perfect accuracy.

Esther had lived until today by those Golden Eyes and by what the Golden Eyes and the Heretic Inquisitor showed him.

What they showed him now was emptiness.

“The Divine Punishment isn’t responding.” No wrongdoing. Only purity.

“I am a Servant of Heaven. Do you think that before the sacred heavens, a blade built to judge sinners would dare turn its edge against the Heaven it stands beneath?”

“…Fine. That’s all I need to hear. My Divine Punishment never lies. I will follow the Heretic Inquisitor’s guidance.” Esther reversed his blade and slid it back into its scabbard. The young man stepped forward and came to stand beside the monster. When he turned to look back, he found a boy staring at him with eyes gone blood-red.

“Why?! Why betray us?! Don’t you have any shame at all?!” Gretel screamed at the top of his voice. He lunged toward Esther, but Nephrin snatched his arm and held him back in time.

“Stay still, Gretel.”

“He’s a coward! He betrayed us—I hate traitors!!”

“Hmph.” Esther just made a noise in his throat.

“My life was wretched from the moment I was born. Scraping by day to day. What could be better than having money that never runs out—and a long life to spend it on? That’s the highest point a human life can reach. A life that wants for nothing. Gretel—you’re too young to understand how hard a human life is. You have a mother. A father. But I have nothing. And most importantly—” Esther lifted his shirt to reveal his stomach. The bruise that had been petal-shaped before was now spreading—larger than before, its color shifted from green to black.

“Internal hemorrhage. Organ damage. Without a hospital, he doesn’t have long.” James assessed the severity from visual inspection alone.

“It’s because of me…”

“Esther.” Jinyo rose to his feet and looked at the man he’d known for less than a full day. He couldn’t quite call him close. They were just strangers who’d had overlapping interests.

“Don’t hold a grudge, demon. My Golden Eyes never lie. Compared to a disgusting creature like you, coming over to this side practically guarantees survival odds.”

“I understand. I don’t hold a single shred of grudge against you.” Jinyo drew the Sonneteer in a single stroke. Sword Spirit surged through the blade—sharper than any he had ever produced in his life—without a ripple on his face, as though the betrayal affected his mind not in the slightest.

“A very wise decision. Let me show you just how right you were to enter Heaven’s service.” The Lord of the Mountains pressed its hand to Esther’s back. A dense surge of vital energy poured in. Esther closed his eyes and guided it in a circulation toward his stomach. The pooled blood was drained and repaired. What had been a critical wound was reduced until nothing remained but a small bruise.

“A miracle?! Even a medical mage couldn’t do that in an instant.” Nephrin lost her composure. Having studied magic her entire life, she had never witnessed a wound healed in a single breath. If this wasn’t a miracle, what else would you call it?

“As for you two—I take it you have your answer.”

“!!” James raised the spear and flooded it with Planar.

“Isn’t this obvious enough? Find me anyone who looks like they want to be someone’s underling and circle their face in marker.” Nephrin drew a black pen. Violent Planar erupted in a wave of heat. Her rain-soaked clothes went from drenched to damp in seconds. A fireball launched from the pen’s tip.

The fireball was swatted aside by the Lord of the Mountains’ arm like brushing away a fly. Nephrin raised the pen to aim again—but what she saw made everyone take an involuntary step back. The Lord of the Mountains tossed the leather ball into the air. It rotated slowly as it fell—but that wasn’t what made them flinch. It was the creature’s pose.

The Lord of the Mountains bent the opposite arm into a striking extension, jumped to the perfect height to survey every square meter in a hundred-meter radius—

Its posture looked exactly like a volleyball player.

Bang!

The Lord of the Mountains served the leather ball with a heavy topspin. The energy wave hidden within it was powerful enough to see with the naked eye. Nephrin stood frozen. Her reaction speed was too slow to do anything but wait for death.

‘This is bad.’

A shadow lunged in and intercepted. The elderly butler lowered his stance, arms stretched wide, and received the leather ball with both. He took the catch perfectly—but the concealed energy wave slammed through the center of his core, and he coughed up a single mouthful of blood. The leather ball bounced back into the air, leaving nothing but two snapped arms like broken chopsticks.

“James!” Nephrin went pale watching the elderly butler crumple because of her.

“Miss Nephrin… please take Miss Luciria and the Young Master… and run…” James coughed up another mouthful. Everything had gone numb—bone and nerve alike. Who could withstand an attack that powerful?

‘The ball itself isn’t the danger. What’s terrifying is the power concealed within it—it passes through physical and magical defenses completely. Is it a one-hundred-percent unavoidable attack?’

“Nephrin—take everyone and run! I’ll handle this alone!”

“Don’t be ridiculous. You can’t do this.”

“I can! Entrust my son and my student to me—I’ll discipline Esther myself.” Jinyo looked up at the sky. The leather ball was descending toward the optimal striking height. The Lord of the Mountains bent its knees and jumped. It pulled back its arm and swung with a thunderous crack. The violent spin—invisible to the naked eye—shifted its trajectory along the wind’s current.

A spike with no readable trajectory. Jinyo couldn’t tell where it was aimed.

Eye of the Sage!

He crammed an Explosive Potato into his mouth and entered Moonlight Status. Jinyo’s body radiated a bright luminescence. His eyes opened wide, reading the movement pattern, seeing through the technique hidden behind the enemy’s skill.

Jinyo channeled power into his palm. Instead of cutting with the blade, he reached out and made direct contact with the leather ball with his bare hand.

“Don’t receive it barehanded!” James screamed at the top of his lungs. He had received with both arms and they had snapped like matchsticks—what would Jinyo have left?

Wilting Blossom Palm.

Jinyo struck with his palm, imparting a counter-rotation. The high-spin leather ball froze—slow-motion—and was sent back. The pale hand that had done it was in perfect condition, unmarked except for a red welt from the slap.

“What?!” James’s jaw dropped. How had he done that?

“Two looks were enough to read the pattern, I see. Heaven’s Demon.”

“…” Jinyo narrowed his eyes, watching Esther circulating his recovered energy, then shifted his gaze to the Lord of the Mountains guiding the leather ball back into its hand. It tossed the ball again, the same as before.

“!” The leather ball was flicked off his wrist before it even had time to rise. It simply floated in the air and was struck on contact. Jinyo wanted to dodge, but behind him lay Sven’s unconscious body. Jinyo focused, tracking the ball’s rotation, channeling fine Planar control into his wrist to generate a counter-spin wave.

Wilting Blossom Palm!

Bang! Jinyo sent the ball back at the cost of a wrist turned scarlet.

“So it wasn’t a fluke.”

“Nephrin! Take him away—he’s in the way.” Jinyo snapped at her. Nephrin startled. Her heart slammed so hard blood rushed to her face. She reached out and pulled Gretel into her arms, then with her other arm hoisted Sven’s unconscious body onto her right shoulder. Just like that, the slender young woman went from delicate to fierce, running with a grown man over her shoulder.

“Bro Yo!! Bro Yo!” Gretel called for his father without stopping. But Jinyo didn’t spare him a single glance.

“Nephrin, the Professor is two against one—he can’t take that. Why are we leaving him and running? We can fight with them. Survive together or die together.”

“Don’t you understand yet, Miss Luciria? In Doctor Jinyo’s eyes, you’re dead weight. Staying only makes it harder for him to fight—just like a minute ago.”

“But you can fight. Let us run on our own—you go help the Professor.”

“You’d entrust the safety of his beloved son to a man with broken arms and a girl who can’t hold a sword steady? Quite the comedian. And besides—it’s my own selfishness.”

“…” Luciria’s lips trembled. She ran with tears streaming down her face, unsure of what destination she was even running toward. Gretel, cradled in Nephrin’s arms, watched his father grow smaller and smaller in the distance. It was humiliating. He swore he would carry this shame for the rest of his life.

The Lord of the Mountains continued spiking the leather ball at Jinyo without stop. Jinyo was beginning to adapt to the high-speed spikes. He shifted his body in the last possible fraction of a second before impact—twisting his weight to send a Punishment Wave spinning through his wrist, his palm neutralizing and dissipating the power before the leather ball could destroy his pulse points.

The secret of the high-speed spike was a specialized rotation that amplified its destructive power. The technique was called [Perfect Rotation], and its result was [Punishment Wave]—the same thing James had received in full. The Punishment Wave was powerful enough to penetrate any defense. Regardless of physical durability, nothing was exempt.

Jinyo had combat experience against the Locust Demon. When two Punishment Waves of equal strength collided, they cancelled each other out. That was the technique he was using to neutralize the high-speed spike’s rotation. To do it, Jinyo had to read the spin correctly and strike at the right moment to generate a Punishment Wave of matching force.

The disadvantage of this method:

Jinyo had to produce the Punishment Wave himself.

Bang!

The leather ball slammed squarely into Jinyo’s body. Jinyo was launched backward, his pulse points taking damage directly and completely. He had no blood left to spit. He simply endured it and forced himself upright as fast as possible. He crammed a Happy Carrot into his mouth to reset his mental state and resupply nutrients at top speed.

‘This is bad. What do I do? I can’t think of a plan.’ Jinyo just wanted his son and student to get clear of this place. He hadn’t even considered how to win against the Lord of the Mountains. Looking at Esther, who had betrayed them, made it feel even more hopeless.

‘If there’s any way to turn this around, there’s only one.’ Jinyo gripped the pink Ether Blade tightly in his hand.

“Please—Sonneteer. Just hear me out.”
