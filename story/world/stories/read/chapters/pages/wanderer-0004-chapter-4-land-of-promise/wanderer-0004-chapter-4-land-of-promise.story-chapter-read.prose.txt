“Here Lass, yeh shou’d eat somethin',” Torsten says as he hands me some salted meat and hard tack. Scattered amongst our resting point in the copse of trees, the others are eating the same and drinking from water skins. After Huginn the raven departed, Froðe walked off to do Jarl-related things, I suppose.

“Thank you,” I say to Torsten as I take the provisions. Torsten winks at me, then takes off to a group of warriors talking by the creek. One of them audibly slaps him on the back as he joins them, and Torsten’s booming laughter is heard not long after. My stomach growls, hunger suddenly remembered after all the shock of what’s happened starts to wear away. I tear an aggressive bite off the salted meat and chew, noting that it’s pork. Honestly, it’s not bad.

The sun has gotten lower in the sky, starting to paint softer colors across the horizon, telling me it will be another hour or so before sunset. With it comes another breeze, sending chills through my beat up body and causing me to shiver. For the umpteenth time, I wish I had better clothing for this climate. It appears to be early spring in this world, based on the young lambs and blooming wild flowers I've seen. In addition to the buttercups and bluebells I’ve sighted, there are plenty of thorny looking bushes with equally vibrant yellow blooms. I wouldn’t want to get my hand stuck in one of those.

I look around the camp, noting that although the warriors are resting, eating and laughing, there seems to be an edge of excitement in their eyes. They must be waiting until dark for their next raid, and will likely keep the horses out of sight to raid on foot in the night to gain the element of surprise. I think back to a viking movie I saw once that featured Berserker vikings who wore the pelts of wolves and howled at the moon, dancing around a bonfire before unleashing utter devastation in their raids of villages and fighting in trancelike states. The Berserkers were especially known to be more brutal than other viking clans, and were smaller more, independent groups of elite warriors often hired by other clans. I shudder at the memory, but these vikings don't seem to be Berserkers, if that's a thing here. I tear off another bite of meat, thinking. Maybe I can try to escape in the commotion of the raid. It will be dangerous, but it’s probably my best chance. The thing is, I need a map. I still have no idea where I am at and where I can go from here. I also really need different clothes. It doesn’t help me much if I stand out like a sore thumb wearing office clothes from another world.

“Planning a futile escape?” Froðe says from behind me, causing me to jump and nearly drop the salted meat and hard tack. I whirl to find him leaning against a nearby tree, arms crossed and watching me. There is a black fur draped over one of his arms.

“What makes you say that? Maybe I’m just enjoying my little meat stick here.” I gnaw off another bite of the pork pointedly.

“With the way you are chewing it, it’s as if you wish to slay the pig again,” he says as the ghost of a smirk returns, but there’s curiosity in his eyes.

“I haven’t eaten in a while. Honestly, I don’t even know how long it’s been anymore,” I say, looking down at the food.

Froðe simply nods, and walks over with the fur, then drapes it over my shoulders. The unexpected chivalry from a viking Jarl completely throws me, and I’m even more shocked when he pulls out my hair from underneath the fur so that it’s not tucked anymore. I look up at him, bewildered, then suddenly I realize how close he has gotten. He lowers his head so that his face is only inches away, eyes locked on mine. He traces my jaw with a finger.

“I will find out who you really are, sooner or later,” he says in a tone that brings chills to my skin despite the fur draped around me.

Shit. “I…I already told you my name,” I stutter.

That menacing grin returns. “I talk not of your name, fiery one.”

Well...here goes nothing.

“I’m…not from here,” I hesitate, trying to find the right words. “I’m…a traveler.” Not exactly a lie.

“And where are you traveling from?”

“A faraway land, much different from here.” Also not a lie. Can't exactly tell him I'm from another world, he'd think I'm crazy. "But I'm also lost," I add, and his eyebrow raises.

"Lost..."

Froðe picks up the golden pendant on my neck, I honestly forgot I was wearing it, his fingers brushing my skin and sending tingles through me. That’s right, I put the pendant on this morning before work, which feels like forever ago. It’s a knot work pendant from my trip to Iceland a few years back, a silver Valknut (three interlocked triangles) that's set around an emerald gem. I’m very fond of it.

Come to think of it...isn't the Valknut of Norse origin?

I remember learning that the Vikings settled in Iceland during the 9th and 10th centuries during my trip. Something about the pendant called to me in a shop in Reykjavík, and I had to get it. I've worn it almost every day ever since.

“A wealthy land, it seems. Do you know what this symbol means?”

I nod. “The cycle of life, death and rebirth.”

Froðe smirks, satisfied with my answer. The deeper meaning of the Valknut in my current situation is not lost on me, and I frown as my eyes go distant for a moment.

But Froðe speaks again, pulling me from my thoughts.

“My people also use knot work in our metalwork and textiles, but we often incorporate animals in the designs. They each have their own specific meaning.” He drops the pendant, and it makes a soft thump onto my chest.

“What meaning does a raven have?” I ask, curiosity getting the better of me. Froðe tilts his head.

“They mean many things to us. Wisdom, memory, symbols of war and the transition between worlds. They are sacred.”

“I’ve never seen one before today," I admit. "But I’ve heard about them. I’ve heard that they are very intelligent and loyal to those who are kind to them,” I say. We don’t really get ravens in the Pacific Northwest, but plenty of crows.

Froðe’s eyes slightly widen with interest. “Ah, and what did you think of my Huginn?”

“I’ll admit he started me at first, but he is quite magnificent. Does he…” God I feel silly asking this, “does he talk to you?”

To my surprise Froðe nods. “We speak to each other mentally. As does his twin, Muninn. They are my advisors, and I use them to speak with my Seeress,” Froðe says, lifting his head and looking to the sky, as if he could see them now. "They can fly over great distances, seeing and hearing everything around them. Then they share that knowledge with me."

So...magical ravens that share knowledge. Is this why Froðe is so intelligent for a Viking?

And there are two of them. Huginn means “thought,” and Muninn means “memory,” my knowledge of this language tells me. Appropriate names for raven advisors, I would think. Although Froðe is Jarl, his name doesn’t mean leader, but “wise” or “learned,” which traits would make a better leader anyway in my opinion. Not that I plan to see him as my leader, of course.

“What do they advise you on?” I ask.

With his head still raised to the sky, Froðe laughs dryly. “That’s not something I would tell you, fiery one.”

Fine. Small talk is over. “What do you plan do do with me?”

Froðe finally looks down at me, considering his answer. “In Tir Tairngire you will meet my Seeress, and she will determine your path.” So, they plan to keep me alive at least until she gets to Tir Tairngire.

I nod. “When do we head out again?”

“YOU will stay here with Torsten and the horses while we complete the raid,” Froðe says. Well....there goes that plan. Fuck. Froðes eyes twinkle and he flashes a feral grin like he knows.

“Great,” I look down at my feet, not masking my defeat well. Escape is going to be harder than I thought.

Froðe’s rough hand grasps my chin once again and tilts my gaze back up to his. “I trust you won’t cause me any trouble while I’m gone.” It’s not a question, and his eyes take on that commanding edge that unsettles me. “Beatrix.” It's the first time he's said my name without hyphenating it. Something deep inside me stirs at hearing my name on his lips, but I stuff that  back down to where it belongs.

“I’ll behave,” I grind out.

“That’s what I like to hear.” Froðe lets go of my chin and walks towards the others, who have started to gather in the setting sun. The feeling of excitement is palpable in the air. A giant warrior with a long brown beard and curly brown hair examines his extremely large axe with rapt fascination, looking for dullness. It doesn’t look like he finds any before hooking it over his back with a look of pure glee. Looks like they leave for the raid soon. Tir Tairngire has no idea what is about to hit them, and it’s going to be bad.

"Let us claim what is rightfully ours!" Froðe shouts as he stalks toward them. This time I raise an eyebrow. Theirs? But the group gathered eats it all up and shouts in agreement. Froðe begins to pace as he talks. "Glory and riches await those who fight without fear, and we do not show fear." He turns and addresses the others."Fear is for the weak," Froðe says, and glances back at me. My eyes narrow. "And should death claim us by battle, we shall greet it as an old friend, as a glorious afterlife in Valhalla will await us. But today, my warriors, Odin smiles upon us." More sounds of agreement. "For Odin and Valhalla!" he roars.

"For Odin and Valhalla!" the rest shout in unison, banging their blades on their shields in a drum-like beat. They repeat this a few times, with a shout in between each double beat on the shields, before disbursing.

Short and sweet. Not bad for a pep talk. "Torsten, I leave the rest to you here," Froðe says, as he turns North without another glance at me, the clan following.

"Aye, Jarl. Ye ken count on meh. Fight fer the glory of Valhalla!" Torsten yells. I wonder if he's bummed he's going to miss out on all the fun because of me. But I guess someone has to stay behind for the horses too.

As night falls, Torsten starts a fire and we sit beside it. A skinned rabbit is roasting on a spit above the flames. My eyes stare at the dancing blaze, just imagining what carnage is taking place a few miles away and I shudder.

"Sometin' on yer mind Lass," Torsten asks.

"Why did you join them?" I ask instead, because I've been wondering.

Torsten seems surprised by the question, as if he was expecting something else, and he's not wrong.

"I w's very young when th' clan came to meh village, bak when Bjørn was Jarl," Torsten said. "Bjørn was Froðe's father," he added when I frowned at the name. "I had eh choice. Be killed, or prove meh worth," he says as he takes a swig from another skin, and I detect the nutty scent of ale coming from it. "I was good wi' a blade even at eh young age, so, I prove'd meh worth and they took meh in. Joined th' clan."

"How so? How did you prove your worth?" I almost dread the answer, but can't stop myself from asking.

"I killed one o' meh own people, at th' ripe age o' twelve."

I stare at him in shock. So this is what it takes to join a viking clan. Utter ruthlessness. Killing your own countrymen. At such a young age.

"I'm so sorry, Torsten." I honestly don't even know if that was the right thing to say or not, but he simply shrugs and takes another swig.

Eager to change the subject, I ask, "So when do we go to Tir Tairngire?"

"We ride ou' in th' mornin.' Will bring o' few horses wi' us as well for others to return with for th' rest."

I nod without saying anything, and we are quiet. The wood in the fire crackles, sending small floating embers towards the clear, unpolluted sky filled with stars and the milky way in its full, beautiful glory. The stunning beauty of this new world has its way of taking my breath away. And this new life...what will it become for me? Will I abandon everything I know in order to survive? Will I become as ruthless as Torsten if it means I have a chance of surviving at all? Only time will tell, I guess.

Torsten keeps me bound but lies down another fur for me to sleep on near the fire. Despite my desire to plan another way out of this mess, the exhaustion and trauma of my first day in a new, dangerous world completely overtakes me and I am lost to the blissful nothingness of deep sleep.
