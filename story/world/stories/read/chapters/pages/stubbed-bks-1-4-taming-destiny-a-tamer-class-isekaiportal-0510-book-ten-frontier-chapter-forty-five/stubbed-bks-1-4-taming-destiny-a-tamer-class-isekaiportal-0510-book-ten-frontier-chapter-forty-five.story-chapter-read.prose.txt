Romeo looks at me in some surprise and a dawning sense of delight, but before he can answer, he’s interrupted.

You’re asking the cub to teach you lightning when he’s barely able to control it himself?

The rumble that emerges from the forest makes both of us jump.

We scramble to our feet as a large, white form suddenly emerges from the deep dusk shadows. The sun has disappeared below the horizon with only its last rays still lighting the sky, but Tilathis’ ability to hide in the forest given his white fur is still deeply impressive.

I eye the Tier four warily. I’m mostly sure that he won’t attack me – for Kalanthia’s sake more than my own. Still, since he isn’t one of my Bonded, I have no way of sensing his emotions or knowing what he’s likely to do. His body language, at least, looks relaxed.

Romeo bristles, not taking the slight against his abilities lightly.

Barely? I can obliterate my targets with it! Can you?

Tilathis huffs with incredulous amusement.

Little cub, you don’t even understand what it is. You play with it like a toy when it is a force of nature. One day, you will lose control and be burned.

I am not a cub! Romeo snarls, sparks of lightning leaping off him in his anger.

Tilathis sends me a weary glance as if to say ‘this is what I was talking about’.

To calm the situation down, and also because I’m not so happy with the high-handed approach Tilathis has taken, I make a quick decision of how to respond.

“As a Beast King, Tilathis surely has a great deal of wisdom to share,” I say, ostensibly to remind Romeo of who he’s talking to – a Tier four who might very well attack and kill him if he is too insulting. However, I have to suppress my smile as I see Tilathis puff up slightly in my peripheral vision. “You should take advantage of the teaching that he’s so clearly willing to offer you.”

What, teaching? Tilathis asks, blinking and taken-aback. Romeo, in his turn, loses the last of his aggravation and instead eyes Tilathis with speculation.

“Yes,” I smile, though I keep my teeth covered. “After all, you are clearly so concerned about his techniques – and the danger of poor control over them. And how better to demonstrate just how far he has to go than teaching him the basics?”

Tilathis hesitates.

Well…I….

I sense that Romeo’s speculation has turned into enthusiasm as he fully grasps my point. A Tier three learning directly from a Tier four is probably practically unheard of – outside of family bonds, at least. However, he shows impressive acting skills as he turns away from the massive leopard and lifts his head up high.

Perhaps Elder is afraid that we will see his abilities as lacking if we examine them.

Tilathis snarls, piqued.

Examine this.

In response to his words, the sky splits apart with a fork of lightning, the boom of thunder reaching us almost as soon as the light appears. The lightning illuminates the clearing around the hill almost as well as the sun for the brief moment it’s there, and then it’s gone.

I exchange a conspiratorial glance with Romeo. Somehow, in that moment, we don’t even need the Bond to communicate.

“I mean, that was powerful,” I muse, earning a suspicious glance from the massive leopard. “But I was expecting something…more.”

Yes, Romeo agrees, looking distinctly unimpressed. After Elder's words, I expected something I could not do myself.

For a moment, I fear that Romeo might have gone too far – he seems to have an impressive ability to rile others up and the snarl that Tilathis lets out is truly fearsome. Romeo’s eyes blow wide in sudden fear and he whirls around, summoning an airblade to either attack or defend himself.

Something you could do yourself? Tilathis rumbles threateningly.

I feel a sense of static on my skin a moment before the world suddenly goes white.

A hammer blow of sound crashes through me as hot wind slaps across my face, carrying the acrid scent of ozone and burning sap. Every hair on my body – even those below my armour – stands on end. For a moment, my muscles seize as a crawling sensation races up my legs from the ground. Fragments of the forest floor dance beneath my boots as the earth itself shudders.

I blink the white spots away from my vision, sending healing magic through my body, searching out the damage that had been done by the blast.

When my vision clears, I see that a tree about three metres away from me is charred black, bare of leaves and half of its branches destroyed. And Romeo is staring at it with pure awe spilling down the Bond between us.

After a moment, we turn back to Tilathis who’s looking very smug.

How is that for a strike? he asks us rhetorically.

Romeo and I exchange a wide-eyed glance, then, calming down, I wink at the larnatis.

“Ten out of ten for dramatic effect,” I compliment the nunda. “But hardly something that required any sort of delicate touch. Just how much power did you put into that?”

Maybe Elder doesn’t want to teach me because he’s afraid we will see that his abilities rely only on his strength, and not on his control, Romeo muses. I’m sure I could learn to do that after a few tries.

Tilathis’ ears pin back to his head and his lips lift off his teeth. I decide that we need to step a little bit more lightly or risk him actually getting mad.

How dare you question me, you who cannot even wield it without a whirlwind to create the sparks! Tilathis snarls.You think making something bright and loud means you control it? You are displaying for a female, not using true power.

Romeo actually staggers backwards at that, and a bolt of hurt slams through the Bond between us.

“How about a bet?” I suggest, trying to calm things down a bit. That hurt could so easily turn into fury. Due to my intervention, the Bond between Romeo and me settles down a bit and the larnatis sends me a side-eye, clearly wondering what I’m on about. And maybe recalling the bet I offered to him earlier – that I won.

Tilathis gazes at me with just as much suspicion.

“Work with us both. If we cannot create a lightning bolt by the end of the night, we will admit that we are poor students and never bother you again about it.”

And if I win? He asks. Romeo’s side of the Bond flickers with sudden amusement. I guess it’s fun for him to be on the other side this time.

“Well, what do you want?”

He considers it for a moment.

There is a Beast King in the mountain closest to my own. I want to add its territory to my own, but its powers counter mine. If I win, you will help me defeat it.

I hesitate for a moment – committing myself and my people to fighting another Tier four so soon after the last isn’t ideal. Then again, we don’t have to do it immediately – and perhaps the experience could help more of our people advance.

“OK, fine. Deal.”

Tilathis gives a toothy grin, his anger slipping away from his expression. For the first time, I wonder whether maybe he was the one playing me – if he’d been willing to teach us, but just wanted to get something out of it.

Watch, he instructs.

I close my eyes and engage my magic-sight. I see magic gathering, curiously, in two places – in the sky, certainly, but also in a tree nearby.

I realise that that must be his target in just enough time to leap away from it so I don’t end up getting electrocuted again. The blast of hot air and crash of thunder hit me in mid-air, sending me further back than I’d intended. I catch myself with my wings and hover in midair, staring at the tree with eyes closed, memorising what the magic is doing.

I almost absently extinguish the fire that has caught in the local trees – unlike the previous, this one had several branches woven with its neighbours.

I take a moment to contemplate the way the magic moved – the way it didn’t move downwards in the way I was expecting, but up.

I frown. I need more information.

Opening my eyes, I look at Tilathis with determination.

“Again.”

He gives me a toothy grin and then the sense of static reappears.

*****

By the time the first rays of dawn touch the underside of the darkly threatening clouds that have built overnight, I’m mentally exhausted, and more than a little frustrated.

Lightning is far more complex than I thought it was. No wonder I haven’t been able to earn Lightning-Shaping yet – it’s only because I’ve been able to see the magic Tilathis has been wielding that I can see what’s happening in the lightning at all.

Part of the issue is that it’s just so quick. Even when I asked the nunda to slow the strike down as much as he could, the whole thing happened within a second, minus the build-up time.

Romeo managed to create a lightning bolt from the sky a few hours ago – it was weak, but it struck and blackened a tree, though there were none of the dramatic explosions that accompany Tilathis’ strikes.

He was absolutely ecstatic when he succeeded – so much so that it was adorable, to me, at least – crowing about it to both of us until I thought Tilathis would knock him head over heels in annoyance. Fortunately for us all, he collapsed a moment later from exhaustion.

I checked him over, worried that he’d done himself more damage, but Tilathis just coldly said he was too weak to channel the power of a lightning strike. A quick examination justified Tilathis’ statement – Romeo’s mana pool was practically empty, but he was otherwise fine. Apparently it takes a good deal more effort to create a lightning strike from the sky than to fill a whirlwind with lightning caused by static electricity from his feathers.

I’ve long come to the conclusion that this is no beginner’s technique. No wonder that Tilathis is so confident about winning the bet.

The sun is almost up, Tilathis rumbles, looking self-satisfied. You have lost the bet.

“Not yet,” I tell him, clenching my jaw. “It’s not yet dawn.”

He sends me a pitying look.

And you think you can learn this in the little time before the sun crests the horizon?

“I’ll never know unless I try, will I?” I retort. “Now let me concentrate.”

He offers me a fang-filled grin, but quietens down.

I breathe deeply, trying to clear my head. I consider everything I’ve seen and pieced together.

Tilathis’ magic is the instigator, and works in two places. He designates a starting point up in the sky, and then marks a destination below.

The initial charge comes from his magic up above that reaches down to the magic below, but the magic below is not idle. When the magic above gets within a certain range, the magic below connects to it. The lightning is caused when the charge flashes upwards. And it’s not always a single flash, either – in some of his bolts, the magical charge has flowed between the starting point and the destination multiple times, causing the bolt to flicker.

Then, the destruction of the trees is caused by the charge ripping through the sap and superheating it. The tree cooks from the inside out, often exploding or bursting into flame in the process.

There seem to be two things standing in my way right now. First, creating the starting point so far away from me. Second, that my mana doesn’t seem to be the right kind to create the charge. Using a spot just below my hand as the starting point, and a piece of wood on the forest floor as the destination, I’ve managed to get magic to flow between the two, however it didn’t create any sort of electricity.

That suddenly makes my thoughts pause.

Electricity…it’s caused by a movement of electrons from negative to positive. So far I’ve just been using my normal unaspected mana, but maybe that means there’s no charge….

It feels like I’m grasping at straws – I don’t even know whether electronic charge has anything to do with different mana types. But I’m out of other ideas, so it’s worth trying. But that raises a new question: what mana should I use?

Fire? No, lightning heats, yes, but that’s a byproduct of its passage through space, not its nature.

Earth? I pause on that thought. We talk about ‘earthing’ a charge…. Maybe earth-magic would be the best destination.

But then what for the starting point?

I consider metal – most metals are excellent conductors. But does that mean it’s good for generating charge? I wonder, hesitating. Batteries aren’t made of pure metal.

Water is another good conductor. Though not pure water, I remember. It’s to do with the salts in water, not H2O itself. Anyway, water alone doesn’t create charge either.

Flesh is an option given that bodies create their own charge through movement of salts between cells, but I decide not to start with this one.

Air, however…. It’s not my most powerful Skill, I muse – though I practised a lot with it on the ship, it’s still only at Initiate rank – but there’s plenty of air up in the sky. The good thing is that I’ve already used air and fire together, dual wielding them. I haven’t tried air and earth together, but the principle has to be the same. On the positive side, there aren’t many mana types that are so opposite as the two of them, so if that has anything to do with electronic charge, then I’m onto a winner there.

A glance at the low horizon indicates that the sun is already rising behind the mountains, but Tilathis seems to be willing to give me the time until it emerges completely. I’m almost more worried about the heavy clouds overhead – they look like they’re about to tip down on us at any second.

Channeling the fluid, free mana of air through my left hand, I have to concentrate hard not to lose it as I channel the heavy, slow mana of earth in my right hand.

Tipping the earth mana onto the ground, I concentrate on extending the air mana towards the little pile of glowing brown earth magic, at the same time as extending the earth magic up to the air.

The moment they make contact, it’s all over very fast. A flash of light makes me close my eyes reflexively as there’s a simultaneous bang. I leap away and swear, shaking my hand – it caught the backlash of electricity and now feels numb and tingly.

But…

“I did it!” I exult.

It doesn’t count, Tilathis rumbles. It must flash in the sky.

But he can’t put a dampener on my joy. I send him a gleeful look as I feel the nagging sense of a notification. I don’t read it immediately, but I’m sure I know what it says – and that gives me confidence that this will work.

Lifting my hand, I focus on an area in the sky, imagining a gathering of charged sky mana, then focus on the ground, imagining a gathering of charged earth mana.

And then I connect them.

The stunned look on Tilathis’ face, illuminated by the flash of my lightning bolt as its bang crashes over us is immensely satisfying.

“I win,” I tell him, nodding at the horizon where the first sliver of sun is only just appearing below the cloudline.

He’s silent for a moment, then dips his head, a flicker of respect in his eyes.

You win, he agrees.

Even when the deluge of rain from the skies starts a moment later, it isn’t enough to cool the warm flame of hard-won victory in my breast.
