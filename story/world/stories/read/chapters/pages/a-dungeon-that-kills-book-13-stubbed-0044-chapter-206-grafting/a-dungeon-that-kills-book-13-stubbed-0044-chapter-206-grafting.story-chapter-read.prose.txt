Once again, Viktor had to change his vision. His puppet had been destroyed, so he could no longer see anything through its eyeballs. Thus, he switched back to Celeste’s sight before returning to the scene to check the aftermath of his attack.

Where the walking corpse once stood, there was now a shallow crater gouged into the stone, and scattered across the ruined floor lay fragments of splintered bone mixed with strips of charred meat.

Drake himself did not fare much better. The shockwave had caught him at close range and flung him bodily away, reducing him to a motionless heap at the base of the wall. A dark crimson streak painted the masonry behind his mangled form, feeding a thick pool that steadily spread outward. His spear sat nearby in two pieces, about as much use to him now as he was to himself.

Such a result was, more or less, what Viktor had expected. What he had not was that the once-stoic Rowan was screaming like a gutted pig, clutching at his right hand, or rather, at the ragged stump where it used to be. Because that hand was gone, vanished entirely, leaving behind only an ugly wound from which blood pumped out in rhythmic spurts.

I see, Viktor thought. It took him only a second to piece together what had happened.

Rowan must have lunged forward and grabbed the corpse from behind in a vain attempt to yank it off his companion, and in doing so he had gotten his hand caught where it very much shouldn’t have been. Viktor was simply unaware at the time, since his puppet’s sense of touch wasn’t shared with him. Yes, that had to be the explanation. An unexpected development, no doubt, but far from an unwelcome one.

“Rowan! Rowan!” Valeska shouted once she had recovered from her stunned disbelief. She let her axe fall from her grip as she sprinted to the man’s side.

“It’s gone... it’s gone...” The former soldier managed to choke out between ragged breaths, his words barely coherent.

“Shut up and hold still!”

Valeska tore a long strip of fabric free from the hem of her own tunic and wound it tightly around the ruined stump, cinching the bandage down hard enough to draw a sharp hiss from between Rowan’s clenched teeth.

Cynthia, meanwhile, did nothing but stare. Her gaze was fixed on what remained of Drake, her already pale face draining of what little color it had left. The bloodied cloth she had been using to dab her nose slipped from her fingers without her seeming to notice. Her lips parted and trembled, but no words came out.

Xander, on the other hand, still wore the same irritated frown. Two members of his team were down—one dead, one maimed—but the bastard didn’t bother pretending to care in the slightest. If anything, the way his impatient glance shot toward Rowan suggested the wounded man’s moaning was what actually got on his nerves.

“He needs help,” Valeska said. “We need to send him back.”

“What?” Xander’s frown now sharpened into an outright scowl.

“I said we need to get him back to the outside.”

“Are you fucking serious? We push forward, no matter what happens.”

“He can’t fight anymore. What’s the point of forcing him to go any further?”

Xander’s jaw tightened, but no answer came out of it.

Well, the bastard had received an order from Khalid: to use the other adventurers as meat shields all the way to the Core Room, then tidy up the loose ends once they got there. But if he pushed the issue now, with Rowan short a hand and the corridor still smelling of Drake, he would risk a mutiny before he had even cleared the first floor.

He could leave Rowan behind, Viktor thought. The guy hadn’t known anything about the real plan, so there was no need to silence him just yet. Thinking about it, losing a hand might have been the luckiest thing to happen to Rowan today. He got to walk out of the dungeon alive. Compared to the fate awaiting the others further down, a limb seemed like a small price to pay.

But then—

“He can still fight,” came a smooth voice.

Every head turned at once, and they found it belonged to the one member of the group who had said nothing at all this entire time.

The Apostle.

“What do you mean?” Valeska asked, frowning. “Does he look like he’s in any shape to do so?”

Instead of giving an answer, the hooded man simply lowered himself down the debris-littered floor and picked something up from among the wreckage.

A hand.

It can’t be Rowan’s, right? Viktor thought.

No, that would be impossible. His hand should have been blown to pieces by the blast. But if that was the case, then there was only one other possibility: it came from the very corpse Viktor had been possessing.

The Apostle walked toward Rowan, before slowing to a stop directly over him. He looked down at the wounded man, his expression hidden entirely under the shadow of his hood.

“What... do you want?” the former soldier asked, his voice thin and shaking, his eyes flicking upward with visible wariness toward the figure now looming before him.

Without a word, the Apostle gripped the bandage that Valeska had just finished wrapping so carefully around the stump, and in one swift, merciless motion he ripped it away. The blood-soaked cloth tore free, unleashing a violent spray of fresh crimson, and Rowan screamed in agony, a guttural sound bursting raw from somewhere deep in his chest.

“You bastard!” Valeska shouted as she strode toward the hooded man. But before she could reach him, the Apostle had already stamped the severed hand down into the open, weeping stump.

The warrior woman’s hand shot out regardless, clamping onto the Apostle’s shoulder with a grip fierce enough to crush bone on a lesser man.

“Get away from him, you—”

The hooded man didn’t budge. At all. Valeska’s eyes widened in shock as she realized, slowly and with mounting disbelief, that she couldn’t pull him away no matter how hard she tried. She, someone who was even taller than Xander, corded with muscle built through years of intense training, found herself utterly unable to move this small, unassuming man even a single step from where he was.

The Apostle, for his part, stood as if none of this concerned him, all the while holding the dead hand firmly against Rowan’s wound. The former soldier’s scream climbed higher and higher, now rising to some new and horrifying pitch.

“What are you doing?” Cynthia shrieked.

And, before her horrified eyes, the stump began to move.

Tendrils of red tissue squirmed from the raw edge of the wound, writhing and twisting like maggots surfacing from rotten meat. They latched onto the severed hand, burrowing into the dead flesh, knitting bone to bone and muscle to muscle. The corpse-hand, pale and lifeless, began to darken as Rowan’s warm blood flooded into it, filling vessels that had long gone dry and cold.

Rowan screamed through the whole thing. He thrashed, he jerked, he fought to yank his arm free, but the Apostle held him with an unyielding grip, so he had no choice but to suffer through it.

Before long, the screaming stopped. The Apostle straightened and stepped back, calm as a man who had just finished a chore. Rowan stared down at his own arm. Where his hand used to be, a new and different one now sat grafted in its place. It was pale, almost gray, with short, chipped nails and skin mottled by decay. It looked wrong attached to the end of his living arm, a grotesque patchwork stitched together from life and death.

But it moved.

Rowan flexed the fingers, slowly at first, then with growing urgency. They curled into a fist. They spread open wide. They trembled, but they obeyed him all the same.

“What have you done to me?” Rowan murmured.

“I give you back the ability to fight,” the Apostle said, before turning to Valeska. “This is what you asked for, isn’t it?”

“That’s not—” The warrior woman swallowed. “What are you?”

The Apostle gave no answer. Instead, he glanced at Cynthia. “Want me to fix your nose?”

The aeromancer, understandably, flinched as though she had been struck. She stumbled back a step, then two.

“No. Stay away from me, you freak!”

Viktor could swear the guy was smirking under the shadow of his hood. Quite a personality this one had, didn’t he?

A sadistic bastard, huh?

Funny, wasn’t it? The ordinary members of the Children of the Abyss operated like drones, hollow vessels that couldn’t do anything without an explicit order from their superiors, but this one, who was supposed to be soulless, could somehow act with his own will, could amuse himself at the expense of terrified adventurers, and carried himself as though he were no different from any other living man.

Who, then, was actually controlling this body? The original soul that had once inhabited it was long gone, and now what occupied the space left behind was the essence, the very thing that held the power of the artifact. So was this the soul that had been transformed into the Reliquary? If that were the case, didn’t that mean a living Reliquary was actually sentient, unlike normal ones that existed only as inanimate objects?

Also, where exactly did this twisted personality come from? Was this simply how that soul had always been, even before its transformation? Or had something in the process of becoming a Reliquary warped it instead, corrupting whatever it once was into... this?

Well, it doesn’t really matter, Viktor thought.

Understanding the Apostle’s true nature was not his immediate concern. What he wanted to know above all else was how to defeat this enemy of his.

And by that measure, the first engagement had been a resounding success. He had managed to kill one member of the Iskora team outright and, at the same time, catch a valuable glimpse into the abilities of the most dangerous threat present.

First of all, the way the Apostle “restored” Rowan’s hand. Viktor knew that high-level mages within the Emerald Order were capable of reattaching severed limbs or even making entirely new limbs to regrow over time, but to take someone else’s hand, a hand from a corpse no less, and graft it seamlessly onto a man’s living stump, that was certainly something he had never heard before. Whatever it was, it couldn’t be something as simple as healing. More likely, it was body manipulation of some sort.

Also, there was the matter of the man’s strength. He had pinned Rowan’s arm still and effortlessly shrugged off both the latter’s thrashing and Valeska’s attempt to pull him away. Which meant he must have modified his own body in some fashion, reinforcing it, augmenting it far beyond the baseline power of a normal human.

If that were all he could do, then it wouldn’t really pose much of a problem. Viktor could handle something like that without too much trouble. But he seriously doubted that was the case, that he had seen the full extent of what the Apostle was capable of.

But it was fine. This was only the first skirmish, after all, the opening exchange in what would surely prove to be a long and bloody battle. There would be many more confrontations still to come, plenty of opportunities ahead for him to probe, to press, to force his enemies to reveal their hands, one card at a time.
