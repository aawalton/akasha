Despite having taken down yet another member of the Council of Death, and earning another fortune in shards for doing so, neither Asher nor his team really felt like celebrating. The rest of the day was spent recovering from their back-to-back near-death experiences as they waited for any sort of news from the other team currently off on their mission. Asher probably would have Recalled over to them and tried to help them out, only as it turned out, Lanra wasn’t the only one who had sustained soul damage from that little maneuver of hers. Asher figured out something was wrong quickly enough when he tried experimenting with Distortion’s new tier-three effect and realized his everything hurt when he tried to activate the skill.

“Your soul is strained as well,” Richard confirmed as he pulled his hands away from Asher’s chest and gave him a curious look. “I can’t say I’m surprised. Having such a vast quantity of tritessence shoved into it so suddenly couldn’t have been pleasant, even if Lanra did her best to remove her personal attribute from her tritessence and make it pure. What’s strange is the fact that I could view your soul healing entirely on its own in real time. Souls don’t heal without outside assistance, that’s just a fact of life. The fact that yours is must be due to your Eternal Regeneration.”

“I am eternal, after all,” Asher shrugged, rubbing at his chest as he sat up from Richard’s dining table. “Does that mean I don’t have to worry about soul damage like I thought?”

“Hardly. Even if your soul is healing, it’s doing so far slower than any part of your physical body. The current damage is akin to a lightly sprained ankle. It’s nothing serious, and it’s still probably going to take you at least a few days to heal up completely. If you took any meaningful damage to your soul? I imagine you’d be comatose for at least a couple of months at a minimum as your soul repaired itself. And of course, if something destroyed your soul entirely… well, not even you can regenerate from that.”

“Okay… soul damage is still bad, got it,” Asher said, throwing a worried glance down at Lanra. They’d returned from their mission in the late morning, and now the moon was well up over their heads. Just as Asher had feared, Lanra looked far worse than she had earlier, the woman’s skin deathly pale and pulled taut against her trembling frame. “How’s she doing?”

“As expected,” Richard sighed. “I will be honest with you, Asher. I don’t expect her to pull through. She went deeper into tritessence debt than anyone I’ve ever seen. I’m doing everything I can to save her because having another wizard in my debt would be huge for my research, but it doesn’t look good. Right now I’m taking a break for her sake. It’s a tricky balancing act attempting to insert slivers of my own tritessence into her soul to partially pay back her debt without straining her soul further than it already has been. Push too hard, and the soul suffers. Don’t push hard enough, and the debt continues to destroy her body. One wrong move and I’ll do more harm than good.”

“Makes sense,” Asher muttered, his heart sinking as he took in Lanra’s trembling form. Strangely, he wasn’t even upset to hear Richard admit his main motivation for saving Lanra was for his own personal gain. In fact, it was almost comforting to hear it confirmed. The wizard would probably give up and throw in the towel after giving it the old college try if he were just paying off one of their favors that was owed, but he knew Richard would move heaven and earth if he thought doing so would benefit him. Feeling a sudden need to change the subject, Asher looked around. “Where’s Mutae? I haven’t seen him.”

“Currently, he is in a cage in one of the other rooms that is enchanted to keep him silent,” Richard said, frowning at the reminder. “My efforts to find a way to keep his form stable are still underway. In the meantime, he has assumed the form of a small bird that has the fascinating ability to mimic sound. I was intrigued. For all of five minutes.”

“I get that,” Asher chuckled. He’d never really interacted with parrots much on his own, but he figured someone who didn’t want one suddenly having their pet morph into one would probably be a rather rough experience. Unsure what else to talk about but not yet willing to leave Lanra’s side, Asher asked the first thing that popped into his head. Or at least, he tried to. Just before he could ask Richard if the man knew about this mysterious, continent-wide pact he’d recently learned about, his sending stone began vibrating. Knowing who it was going to be before he even answered, Asher sighed as he noted Donvath’s glowing rune. Rolling his eyes, he brought it to his ear.

“Damn it, Asher, this is why I was mad at Gran for telling you as much as she did in the first place!” Donvath hissed. “Stop trying to spill state secrets and making me use Timeline! The last thing I need right now is Richard storming into my office and demanding I explain everything to him. Yes, he’s in the Stage of Power, but he’s also Richard. I doubt I have to explain why we didn’t recruit him into the pact.”

“Alright, sorry,” Asher drawled, cutting off whatever else Donvath was going to say as he hung up.

“Was that Donvath?” Richard asked curiously. “What did he want?”

“Oh, you know. Something stupid,” Asher said, hopping off the table and tucking his sending stone into his pocket. “Thanks for checking out my soul, Richard. I’ll try to take it easy on my skills for the next day or so.”

“See that you do. A few uses here and there will still hurt, but they won’t do any real damage. But if you get into another fight, you’re just going to make things worse.”

Waving his understanding, Asher left the wizard to his patient. There were at least a couple of things he could take care of that didn’t require him to use his skills too much, and he got started on the first one by pulling his sending stone right back out and tapping one of the few other runes he had other than Samantha’s or Donvath’s.

“Asher?” Bellod asked, clearly surprised to hear from him. “How are you?! It has been far too long!”

“Hey, Bellod,” Asher said, smiling at the clear excitement in the auction master’s voice. “I suppose it has been a while, hasn’t it? Sorry again about pulling that greater demon corpse from the auction at the last minute. I’m sure that probably made some people a little upset.”

“You have nothing to apologize for, that was entirely within your right! And if anyone was annoyed by such an action, they certainly aren’t going to admit it, lest they anger The Reaper and get on his bad side,” Bellod chuckled. “What can I do for you?”

“In the most professional way possible, I’ve got a stupid number of gems on me that I want to sell,” Asher said. “And I suppose a couple of dismantled golems that probably contain enchanted handiwork more impressive than anything anyone in this day and age is capable of. Think you can help?”

Unfortunately for the Eight Crests’ realm wandering team, they hadn’t gotten the chance to loot any of the golems before the deadman's switch was triggered. Asher, on the other hand, had the pieces of the four golems he and Moxy had taken out. He would have had a lot more if he’d shoved the pieces of the defeated golems into his rift as they went through the castle, but he hadn’t been willing to put anything that Flunx’ magic was a part of into his Personal Rift while she still lived. He’d already had one enemy trash his rift without his permission, and he hadn’t wanted a repeat of the Watcher incident.

“When you say a stupid number,” Bellod said slowly. “How stupid?”

“It would probably be better for me to just show you,” Asher laughed. “Can we meet up?”

Less than an hour later, Asher stood in Bellod’s office, watching the man positively drool as Samantha pulled bag after bag of precious gems out of his rift. In order to try and limit his skill usage, Asher was just holding a portal open for her to head in and out. Doing so still hurt his everything a little bit, but he figured it was still better than the alternative. Eventually, she ran out of gleaming stones, before heading back in and grabbing the sacks of gemstone dust.

“And these,” she finished, dropping the dense sacks at the foot of the man’s desk. “Will those sell?”

“Are you kidding? If that dust is as pure as the few gemstones I’ve examined thus far, buyers will be clamoring to get their hands on them,” Bellod said, rubbing his hands together in excitement. “This is going to be an auction for the ages. It’s far too early to say for certain, but I’m all but certain you’ll actually come away with more shards than you got for the greater demon corpse.”

“Really?” Asher asked, blinking down at the sacks of gems. “Just for a bunch of gemstones?”

“The quality and size of these gemstones is unlike anything I’ve ever seen. Every rich noble wishes for gems like these to use in the construction of their artifacts. The only downside is the fact that they have all been cut already, as if someone already knew precisely what they were going to be used for. They will have to be cut again to match their new purpose, but that is a minor thing in my opinion. Yes, this is an incredible find indeed! It will take me some time to go through every individual gem, but I will inform you when they are ready to go up for auction.”

“Thanks, Bellod, we appreciate the help,” Asher said. After Samantha shared a few quick words with the auction master, the two of them left his office, strolling through the capital as they walked back to the palace. Shivering slightly in the cold evening air, Samantha took his arm and pressed up against him.

“Bah. I hate winter,” she grumbled, frowning up at the sky as if just daring it to start snowing. “I know we still have a few weeks before it hits in earnest, but I miss the warm, sunny days of summer already.”

“I never really paid much attention to the changing of seasons back on Earth beyond the holidays,” Asher admitted as they walked. “Other than having to dig my car out of the snow and making sure I had presents for people, winter was pretty much the same as summer for me. Does the Noala Kingdom have any sort of gift-giving holiday?”

“Yes, but it’s at the end of winter. To mark the start of the new year, when spring arrives and life returns to the forests. It’s customary to give people a gift to welcome in the new year.”

“Interesting… our new year was in the middle of winter. And we didn’t give people gifts, we just made a ton of noise and watched a giant ball drop in the middle of one of our larger cities.”

“…Why?”

“I have no idea,” Asher admitted with a laugh. “It was just something we always did. I don’t know the holiday’s history, and it’s not like I have a way of finding out now. What other holidays do you guys have? I know there was the harvest celebration you told me about, and birthdays are still a thing, but do you—”

A faint whistling sound was all the warning Asher received before something hit him in the side of the head, punching straight through his skull and into his brain. He had the briefest glimpse of shock and horror on Samantha’s face as she summoned her emerald wings and he began to fall, before everything went dark.
