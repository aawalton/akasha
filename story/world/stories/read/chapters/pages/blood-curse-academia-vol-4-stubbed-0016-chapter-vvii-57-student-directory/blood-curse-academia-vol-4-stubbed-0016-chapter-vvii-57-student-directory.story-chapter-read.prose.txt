Chapter V.VII (5.7) - Student Directory

“This is not nearly as helpful as I thought it would be,” Kizu complained as he flipped through a tome. He sat at the Student Council table alongside a sulking Sene, a sleepy Ione, and a grinning Faible. It had been a battle to get Kizu access to this detailed student directory. A battle he had won by bringing it to a vote. Naturally with Faible and Ione’s assistance, Kizu was able to bat away Sene’s protests. Sadly, the directory was a disorganized mess without an appendix and Sene refused to help him navigate it. There were snippets about the different students scattered throughout, but not in any discernable structure. It was as if the entire book had been designed to thwart any outsiders attempting to pull anything useful out of it.

“I want a peak when you’re finished,” Faible chimed in from the side. “My target is a second year student that I’ve never heard of before.”

“This is an abuse of our authority and privileges!” Sene declared, not for the first time. “You should all be ashamed of yourselves.”

“Who did you pull?” Kizu asked Sene. “If you want, I can keep an eye for them while I search for Yugo.”

“That is a private question,” Sene snapped back. “I will handle my own information gathering and refuse to engage or be associated with your corrupt endeavors.”

Kizu and Ione exchanged a look, the latter obviously barely biting back her laughter.

“Is it me?” Kizu asked.

Ione’s laugh finally broke. “You know, I bet it’s Faible. That’s why she’s being so cagey about it. I bet she pulled him and was ecstatic.”

“It could just as easily be you,” Faible pointed out. “It would certainly be you if this was a story book. Two rival sisters battling it out.”

“It’s none of you,” Sene snapped. “I won’t reveal who, no matter how much pressure you apply. Drop me in a boiling vat of acid and I’ll remain mum on the topic.”

Faible continued to poke at Sene, clearly enjoying watching her frustration and irritation build. This was getting nowhere. Kizu wasn’t going to be able to focus on his research with all of them bickering.

“Fine, Sene,” Kizu said. He pushed the tome to the side and sat forward in his seat. “I’ll do this research after the Student Council meeting. What do you have on the menu for today?”

“Finally. At least one of you understands the value of our time in this room.” Sene straightened and spared a glare for the other two before continuing. “Today we need to discuss the use of public drinking fountains in the academy.”

“We have public drinking fountains?” Kizu asked. “I always just get my water from the cafeteria or back in the dorm.”

“That’s the point. We need to educate everyone in the academy. I believe we as the Student Council should be prioritizing the good health of our students. Nearly every hallway has a drinking fountain, and yet nearly nobody uses them. Last week I spoke to Professor Taroe and he mentioned that nearly ten students last quarter were found throughout the academy passed out due to dehydration. Absolutely unacceptable when such things are entirely preventable with proper education.” “Where are these fountains?” Kizu asked, genuinely baffled by this revelation.

“Usually they’re behind the wall. You need to tap six times on the upper side of the wall and eight times on the lower half and the wall will open to reveal the drinking fountain. Except for in the west side of the academy where it’s two down, four up, and one down again. And of course on the left side of the hallway with the music classroom and side practice rooms you need to use your foot to kick the wall twelve times instead. That’s in place so that the students with instruments that utilize their mouths can use the fountains to empty the spit from their instruments while still carrying them in their hands. Quite a clever and thoughtful decision by the architect.”

They all stared at Sene for a minute. Ione once again appeared near bursting with laughter.

“That is the most convoluted method of hydrating the student population that I’ve ever heard of,” Kizu said. “Small wonder students are passing out.”

“How do you propose we educate our peers?” Faible asked. “Do you wish us to set up signs to signify the fresh water’s locations?” For once, he sounded serious as he discussed with Sene. There was no subtle jab or irony laced into his words.

Sene paused and her eyes narrowed, clearly also catching onto that. But she continued on, also not snapping at the gnome. “I believe we should have an emergency schoolwide assembly.”

“Are you serious?” Kizu asked.

“Why would I make a jest about drinking water? Where is the humor in that?”

Kizu shook his head. Of course she was serious. Sene didn’t have this kind of sense of humor. If she had any at all.

“Sene. Think about the timing of all this.”

“It’s the start of the quarter. An excellent time to bring together everyone so they are educated for the remaining semester.”

“What were we just discussing before we started talking about drinking fountains?”

“You wanted to use Student Council equipment entrusted to us to your own advantage to better your position in the…combat tournament.” It took until the last few words of her sentence, but Sene looked like she finally understood his point. She scowled and tapped a finger on the wooden table in front of her. “You think that the students will use this as an opportunity to steal tokens from one another.”

“Or, they just won’t show up. I know I wouldn’t,” Kizu said bluntly. “Not unless the headmaster himself demanded it was mandatory. If you have some means in which to enforce that everyone attends, then maybe we can pull it off. But without that you’ll be lucky to even get half of the student body there. And, like you said, those who do show up will probably be too busy hunting each other to pay attention to anything you have to say.”

Sene nodded along to his words, though her facial expression didn’t cheer up. “Understood. Excellent analysis, Kizu. This is exactly why we needed you as the vice president of the Student Council. You alerted me to a blindspot and helped us avoid a potential disaster. Now, what do you propose in place of the assembly?”

Kizu shrugged. “We wait until after this challenge is over.”

“No. That’s unacceptable. We need to avoid any more occurrences of dehydration related heat stroke events. These summer months are especially hot.”

“Not to be callous, but why is this our problem?” Faible asked. “I think it’s a problem worth solving, don’t misunderstand. But if this is serious, shouldn’t it be taken care of by the faculty? We’re just a random assortment of students. Nobody even voted us in.”

“I wouldn’t expect you to understand.” Sene’s tone was icy as she addressed the gnome. “True members of the Student Council understand how to take responsibility for and rectify a plight. Regardless of where it fell through the cracks in the paperwork.”

“I’m with him,” Ione chimed in. “Unless you’re going to start paying us.”

Sene rocked back, as if struck by her sister. “You’re…siding with the interloper on this issue? He doesn’t even deserve to attend these meetings!”

A familiar dialogue played out as Sene insisted that Faible must have cheated to achieve his rank as the best history student on campus.

Kizu stared out the Student Council’s room’s window. The academy was sprawled out below him. Now evening, students slowly wandered around the campus grounds with long shadows reaching out beside them. Everyone was either starting to make their way down to town or over to the cafeteria for dinner. Some might even be taking an early night and heading to bed. He knew he certainly wouldn’t mind a bit of extra sleep. This whole conversation was exhausting.

Finally he had enough of their arguing and he raised a hand in front of himself, grabbing their attention. It looked like it fell on him to smooth this over. “I actually agree with Sene on this. Helping students know where they can easily fetch fresh water is a good use of our time as the Student Council.” Though silently he wished it was literally anyone else’s time being spent. He had so much to do and never enough time at his disposal. “I also think that Faible’s immediate solution isn’t a bad one either.”

“Signage?” Sene folded her arms, but continued to listen.

“Yes. We did this once before last semester for the ball. Let’s just create some fliers and post them around campus.”

Faible smiled smugly, as if he’d won the argument. And, to his credit, Sene certainly deflated as if defeated by him.

“Fine,” she muttered. “We’ll do the fliers. But since it’s the gnome’s idea, we should put him in charge of it.”

“I also said I didn’t think it was our place to get involved,” Faible protested.

“Faible, relax,” Kizu said, cutting in before they could return to bickering. “Ione and I will help you with it. Sene, I need you to mark down the exact locations on a map of the academy for us so we can mark them properly with the fliers.”

The rest of the meeting went surprisingly well. Kizu only had to mediate a few more small disputes before they finally broke away for dinner.

Sene and Faible left together, the two of them bickering and verbally prodigy at one another as they departed. Ione stayed behind to keep Kizu company as he resumed his studies of the directory tome.

“Who did you get?” Kizu asked as he flipped through pages that described the professions of all the students’ parents.

“Oh, Sene,” she replied simply.

Kizu’s attention snapped away from the tome. “Are you serious? You actually got your sister?”

“Yeah. And I snagged her token during that conversation just now. When she was pouting about me siding with Faible.” Ione raised an open palm revealing a slug creature undulating on top of a green wooden token. “I sketched a summoning circle into the bottom of the table at the start of our meeting when I was pretending to sleep and had this guy crawl up on her. I got super lucky with this pull. It was the last one available too. Don’t know why nobody took it before me. She’s a really easy mark.”

“Probably because she’s the highest rated combat student in the academy,” Kizu said dryly.

Ione shrugged. “Doesn’t matter if she can toss someone into the sun with a blast of wind. That’s not the challenge we were given. Sometimes I wonder if Krimpit is right and everyone else at the academy is brain damaged.”
