It was hard for Tenor to accept what his people had been reduced to.

From the outside looking in, someone might confuse the carefully constructed walls reinforced with thick timber as a thriving burgeoning faction. But Tenor knew better of the rotten core festering within.

Stacy took what was once good and beautiful and twisted into something vile. It wasn’t that long ago that the streets they now traveled were full of people excited at the prospects of a new start.

This wasn’t the new start Tenor was hoping for.

“Where is everyone?” Layton asked.

“They are gathered at the temple.” Tenor inclined his head toward the center of the town. “In anticipation of our return.”

“Were they planning to be taken as slaves?”

“Yes. And they would’ve taken on that title gladly.” Tenor reassured Layton.

“Then they should be extra excited that we are helping, and they don’t need to be slaves, right?”

Tenor thought he sensed a certain tone in Layton’s question.

“Of course! Your generosity will be met with celebration.”

“You hear that?” Layton smirked. He prodded the armored man, Martin, in the ribs. “I’ll be met with celebration.”

Tenor felt a twinge of guilt. He tried his best to ignore it.

The temples' wooden spires emerged, and Tenor was filled with a rush of adrenaline.

How was this going to play out?

He wondered, to the chorus of boots shuffling against the dirt streets. Perhaps the others could sense his nervous energy as a resounding hush engulfed everyone standing in the shade of the temple steeples.

Tenor waited at the doors expectantly, the silence looming menacingly. The air smelled sweet with fresh rainfall pattering against hardwood buildings.

His breath puffed in the chill air. He didn’t feel cold though.

The earth’s climate was mild compared to his home planet.

“Soooo...are they coming out?”

Layton’s voice cut through the silence like a blade, causing Tenor to jump. The man in armor noticed and Tenor saw his hand twitch.

Tenor cleared his throat. This was already not going the way it was supposed to.

“Yes. Yes, of course.”

Tenor gulped.

He wasn’t sure what to do.

He took a few steps towards the temple doors. He eyed the sturdy eight-foot, carved wood, bound tight by dark polished iron.

“Tenor?”

This wasn’t right.

He had one job.

Bring Layton here.

That was his part.

Feet crunched against damp packed earth.

“What’s behind those doors Tenor?”

Tenor had no answer for Layton. He’d done his part. She would see that, wouldn’t she?

“It’s not your people, is it?”

Layton wouldn’t understand.

It was his people in the temple. People he’d grown up with. Friends.

At least they used to be.

What a mess.

Was the witch waiting on him? Did she want him to make the first move? Tenor tilted his head back and let the mist of rain fall into his face and tried not to think of the lighting mages stormy eyes watching his every move.

Tenor's finger twitched.

“Tenor, you don't have to do this.”

Layton’s voice was calm. Controlled.

Why was the universe so unfair?

Tenor didn’t want to be here. This wasn’t even his fight. He’d just been pulled into the orbit of two competing giants and used. Like bait on a hook.

He tried his best to accept his fate, but his hand wouldn’t move. Why should he die because some crazy witch wanted this healer?

A mournful howl carried through the rain from somewhere nearby, followed by several more. It was enough to give Tenor pause. Although if he was honest, he was already looking for any reason at all not to fight Layton’s faction.

The howls drew closer, until three of Layton’s wolf-like comrades prowled into view.

And when they did, Tenor’s heart sank into his stomach.

They had the witch.

Stacy bobbed up and down from underneath the wolf man's arm who was comfortably four times her size.

She was bound, with her face covered in what looked like a black fabric sack.

The wolf man walked right up to Layton where he let the woman down on her feet.

“We found her watching you from the ridge just there.” He said, pointing. “She wasn’t alone.”

A second wolf man stepped forward that Tenor hadn’t noticed and tossed something large at Layton’s feet.

It was a head.

It was Badlan’s head or at least the hairless wrinkled distortion of Baldan’s head. His tongue pink tongue lolled from his lips.

Tenor recoiled.

Layton ignored the head, his own face stone. Tenor felt something stir within the pit of his stomach he couldn’t define.

“You must be Stacy.”

Things had gone pretty much gone exactly the way I thought they were going to.

I started being suspicious the second that I saw Tenor waiting peacefully outside our territory.

Since when did that ever happen? After over half a year, there hadn’t been a single non-human faction that I’d come across that wasn’t at least a little hostile.

The second strike was when they offered themselves up as slaves. Who did that? Even if things were as bad as they said they were, which it appeared really was that bad, it made more sense to have engaged in it at least some level of diplomacy.

It just felt like he was trying too hard.

Of course, I wasn’t one hundred percent sure Tenor was working with Stacy.

But it made sense.

It was the whole reason why I left when I did. If Stacy did happen to be there, I didn’t want to get cursed again.

So, we left and retreated into our territory to make our plan.

There was a part of me that wanted to take Tenor and his people with us. His story felt authentic.

I had no doubt that Stacy was using him. She must have had some powerful leverage to force him into serving her.

But the more I thought about it, the more I realized this would be a too good of an opportunity to catch the witch.

The only problem was we didn’t know where the ambush was going to happen. Which is exactly why I sent Nick and the wolves to run recon while we followed along.

And since we knew that the two things Stacy needed to use her spells were visibility and time, there were only two logical places where she might be.

The first was right outside our territory.

I doubted that would be her choice though. It was too risky.

And I seriously doubted that she was lying in wait in transit. Which left what I considered the most likely scenario. She was using Tenor to take us to his village.

So, we gave them exactly that.

I thought about bringing more of our army along, but I didn’t think we needed it, and I was worried that if we did it might tip our hand.

The last thing I wanted was to too spook Stacy. If she ran, who knew what kind of mess she could make.

She needed to go.

And following along seemed like my best chance to make that happen.

I trusted Samson, the other wolf men, and Nick completely. I really did, but at the same time, I also really didn’t want to get turned into whatever happened to Tenor’s faction leader Badlan.

The entire time following Tenor I was on high alert. Every slight gust or twig that snagged against my robes made my skin crawl. My mind played tricks on me, trying to convince me that the spell was already taking hold. I knew it couldn’t be true of course, at least not the curse used to block mana.

Just to be sure though, I repeatedly refreshed the barriers of our soldiers as we went, each time exhaling in relief when I wasn’t met with pain.

I knew it didn’t need to keep casting spells. If I was cursed it would shut any active spells off anyway, but it helped give me something to occupy my thoughts.

And it really did help calm my nerves all the way up until we were standing in front of the temple.

“It’s not your people is it?” I asked, but I already knew the answer.

Even from outside the thick doors, I could sense the wrongness creeping beneath the undercut.

Tenor’s tortured face scrambled for an explanation.

His finger twitched at his side.

“Tenor, you don’t have to do this.”

I could see his pain.

I recognized his pain.

Then the howls came and any chance Tenor had of revealing the truth before everything came crashing down melted away.

The woman Samson placed just a few paces away was mostly how I remembered her, aside from being caked in filth and a sack covering her face.

“You must be Stacy.” I said, looking her over. It was still so hard to believe the woman in front of me was responsible for so much pain.

If a stranger happened to stumble into this situation and saw the bloody, filthy middle-aged woman lying bound, covered and surrounded by a bunch of dangerous looking men and monsters they’d no doubt feel they’d have to come to her rescue.

But we knew better.

“We’ve met before though, haven’t we?”

I stepped closer, close enough to smell her sour body odor. “You can’t see me now...but maybe you remember trying to kill me? You cursed me while I was fighting an army of cyclops.”

My fingers itched to yank the sack from her face, I wanted to see her face, wanted to see if she could still smile now that she couldn’t hide anymore. That would be stupid though, and I resisted the urge.

The bag shuttered in and out keeping pace with Stacy’s breathing.

“I can’t believe you're really here.” Stacy’s words were barely more than a whisper, and if mana didn’t enhance my senses, I wouldn’t have been able to hear her through the sack cloth covering her head.

“So, you do remember me.” My attention was glued on the woman.

Now that she was here, standing in front of me, it was hard to remember why she’d been haunting my thoughts. My gut told me I should just kill her where she stood and be done with it.

I also knew this wasn’t the time for an interrogation, but my curiosity nagged at me.

“Where did you come from Stacy?”

Those were only two other human factions that we knew about so far. Jordan’s and Cassie’s.

I already knew she wasn’t part of Jared’s group. It was a question that had been bothering me for a while. I’d done some digging after the incident. I thought maybe if someone knew her, it could help me understand why she might want me dead.

The only problem was she was a complete unknown. To be fair, I had little to offer in terms of a description. It’s just that, if she wasn’t part of those factions, then where the hell was she from?

The rain hadn’t let up completely. The wetness made the cloth over Stacy’s head turn a darker shade of black.

Her head tilted causing droplets to fall from the top corner.

“Portland.” Her response didn’t sound snarky.

I frowned.

“Right. But what faction are you a part of?”

“No faction.”

“You’ve never been a part of another faction?” I pressed.

She shook her head, flinging water.

“Why did you stay alone? There were human factions all around you, why not join them? Why try and kill me?”

I’m not sure why it bothered me so much. Why face this world on your own if you didn’t have to? Why isolate yourself? There were so many things I wanted to understand about Stacy, but I knew I wouldn’t get the time. I could already feel the people around starting to grow uneasy.

“But I wasn’t alone.”

“You weren’t?” That’s when I remembered Tenor had already mentioned someone else.

“Zortana?”

Stacy didn’t confirm whether that’s who she was referring to or not.

“He told me you would join me. That we just needed to be strong enough.”

The way Stacy spoke didn’t sound natural. It was like how a mother talked to a child, all high and nasally but like AI generated it. Almost like Stacy was a simulation.

Her response didn’t make any sense though. Join her? Was she just thinking in terms of experience points from killing me?

“I really hoped you would join me.”

Stacy’s shoulders started to bob up and down.

“I’m...” She sniffled. “I’m...so alone..”

I raised an eyebrow in the direction of my dad, who, of course, had no context into this conversation at all.

His reaction was to just shrug his shoulders.

The truth was, this was not at all how I’d imagined things would go.

I thought there would be more fighting. Maybe that she would be angry, or feral. In all the scenarios that had played out in my head, crying was not one of them.

The most confusing part was that it seemed genuine.

I sort of figured that once she was captured, I’d ask a few questions, confirm what I already knew, which was that she is evil and had to die, and then we’d be done with it.

Only, the whimpering woman in front of me now seemed anything but evil. I wish that she did.

That would have been easier. But no, this woman didn’t seem evil at all.

She seemed pathetic.

I wanted to hate her for trying to kill me, instead, all I felt was pity.

“If you were so alone, why isolate yourself? Why not instead of trying to kill me, asking to join our faction? Also, why torment this faction?” I asked, gesturing at Tenor who was watching the exchange closely.

The sniffling intensified.

“I...never...wanted...” Stacy was clearly in a losing battle to control her emotions.

“Zortana...he...he...I...didn’t want...he said you would...save me...”

Was this really the soccer mom? The witch? It had to be an act.

“Stacy, you can't cry your way out of this.”

I told her sternly.

“What you’ve done, to these people. Can it be undone?”

Thin mist curled and twisted around the pointed wooden spires of temple. I was certain corrupted villagers waited inside.

If they attacked, sadly we’d have no choice but to kill them.

Stacy sniffed hard, pulling the fabric against her nose.

“No.” She shook her head. “I can’t fix them.”

I frowned.

“What about your friend, Zortana? If we found your friend, could he fix them?”

It was a long shot, but I’d regret not asking.

Stacy stomped down onto the packed dirt splashing water onto my robe.

“Don’t laugh!” She growled.

“Who’s laughing?” I asked, feeling a knot growing in the pit of my stomach. “Stacy...?”

“Nooo...Urgghhhh.” Stacy groaned, pitching forward.

My hands moved reflexively, catching her by the arm before she faceplanted into the mud. Beneath my grip I felt her skin ripple, the small muscles squirming like worms.

I fought the urge to let go and pulled her back up to her feet. I took a few steps back but watched her carefully in case she fell forward again.

She seemed to be more stable though.

The pit in my stomach got bigger.

The sniffling stopped, and for several long heartbeats the world felt still.

“Stacy?”

A bead of water dripped from the tip of my nose.

And then things just sort of clicked.

“No, that’s not right. Is it? Zortana.”
