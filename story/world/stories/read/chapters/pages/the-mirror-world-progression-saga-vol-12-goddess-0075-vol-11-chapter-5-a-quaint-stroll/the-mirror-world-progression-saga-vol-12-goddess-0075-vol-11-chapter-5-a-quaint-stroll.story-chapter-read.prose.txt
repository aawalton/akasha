Within the extensive catacombs beneath Matrazere, Azure had wandered and wandered, seeking some ‘spot’ that was calling her. The gravitas could be felt even from a distance… Something very important happened here, long, long ago. She was tracking the deepest connection she felt to it.

“This was the sarcophagus of one of those liches,” Six commented. “The runes read: ‘Governor of the Western Blight.’ Cute, isn’t it?”

Azure turned her head to frown over at the bombshell gorgon, who was wearing a minimalist white Geirkos-style robe, wrapped around her tightly and leaving mostly bare, scaled flesh rippling with muscles.

Six turned to meet her gaze in the faint light and smiled warmly.

Gah! So distracting. And she knows it!

Azure had come with a strong party as an escort, at the insistence of Samantha. It was hard to argue considering what ancient threats might lurk in the place, but they’d found it as tame as could be. The sages had scoured all the useful entities out already. It was still and silent, as such places should be.

Azure had utilized the party to range them ahead of her as she felt things out. They could find routes or secret passageways. Logically, the strongest was sufficient to guard her immediate person, and that was most certainly the new addition, Six Moons Falling, formerly Endless Templar.

“Never mind that,” Azure commanded, deliberately peeling her eyes away from that predatory gaze. “Follow me. We’re getting closer, but… I think we need to go down one more level.”

“I’m more than happy to go down,” Six replied in cheery, innocent tones.

Azure almost missed a step despite the tone. That was definitely false innocence!

Checking the mental map of the route, she sent her planned movement to the others, instructing all but two Servitors to go down a level. With that, she made for the staircase Athos had found.

As they walked, Six kept close, her many eyes of snakes keeping watch in all directions at once. She asked idly, “What do you think of Odysseus?”

Azure sighed. “What do you mean? He’s obviously one of our best fighters, along with you.”

“Yes, and thank you for mentioning it, but I mean personally.”

“There is no meaning personally. I fought him, now we’re allies, and that’s about it.” Not exactly true, but she had no intention of articulating it to Six.

“I see.” Six was quiet for a few moments as they passed through narrow, decaying brick corridors. “My observation is that he cares deeply for you, in particular. Is perhaps even infatuated.”

Azure scoffed and glanced over at the gorgon incredulously. “How the hell do you figure that?! Are you a mind-reader?”

Six shook her head. “Not exactly. I see things most do not, however.”

“A lot of those things, you should probably keep to yourself.”

“Where’s the fun in that?” Azure just scoffed again and shook her head, so Six continued more soberly, “I did that for… too long. Suffice to say, aspects of me that were turned to dust and cobwebs are revitalized in this new light. I prefer stirring the pot I’m in while I have the chance.”

“Okay… You know, actually, I was meaning to ask: what the hell were you, anyway? Six Moons Falling. Just how accurate is that?”

“Extremely accurate. I orbited Calrenazzod in distributed pieces. Tiny, comparatively. Fragments, heads, angles, moons, the word isn’t important from my perspective. I am aspected to ancestral stone and to the greatest of the water serpents, my father. I have mastered the control of mass to keep myself from crashing too soon, mastered supreme contortion and endurance against Calrenazzod’s continuous pull, and mastered supreme will and energy to survive the fires of entry.”

“Huh. But why? Why were you in fragments?”

Six smiled faintly. “Deliberate artifice and design. It’s simple, really: to not be a denizen of Calrenazzod is to avoid her curses. Only by those means could I be a true daughter to my mother and retain memory where others degenerate. She needed me, as someone more… flexible in Fate. More connectable. In some things, she could work through me much more effectively, and, in my relative autonomy, I could manipulate the world on a steady beat unavailable and unfeasible for her. And, as the cherry on top, well, the destruction above us attests to it. The power of mass and impact.”

Azure frowned as she listened. They found the stairs and went down. She paused at the bottom and turned to Six. “What do you know about my predecessor? If you remember the ancient world.”

Six met her gaze and then turned to nod her head down at the stairs. “Your path will give you better answers than I, Azure.”

“But you know. Without me even needing to explain, you know what I mean.”

“A servant of Fate had better know the major players of the world, wouldn’t you say?”

“Do you know how Goldy is involved? The naga in general, perhaps?”

Six shrugged. “I only know the primary beats. I saw events from afar, not intimately — with but a few exceptions.”

“What do you think this place will tell me?”

“How she departed.”

Azure took a deep breath and nodded, finally looking around. More corridors of decayed brick walls. But somewhere in the twists and turns was what she sought. “Yeah. I guess so. Let’s keep going…”

Not long after she started walking again, Six said in lighter tones, “So! You don’t have mnnn… dibs, or anything, called on Odysseus?”

Azure facepalmed and sighed. “Is that what you were on about!? Good heavens, no! I don’t have dibs on anyone. Ride him all night long tonight for all I care!”

Six actually giggled at that. “That may be a bit premature!”

“Pfft. Trust me, you’d seduce him easily.”

A strong feeling of Six wanting to say something and hesitating could be felt. Azure side-eyed her balefully, willing her to just come out with it.

With a bold smile, Six asked, “Is there anyone… else I could seduce easily?”

The innuendo was much clearer here. Azure laughed and looked away as she scratched her cheek. Clearing her throat, she said, “Look, my betrothed just isn’t into that sort of thing, sorry.”

Short and sweet; cut and dry.

Six nodded calmly. “A shame, though.”“Right…”

“Normally, I’d consider pushing the envelope anyway, on the assumption of mere nerves in the way.”

“Don’t. Consider it an order.”

“As I said, I see things others don’t. I understand the undercurrents, now. Personally, I’d prefer to sample a large variety of partners.”

Azure found herself laughing again. “The world is full of different desserts to partake of, huh?”

“We are all one, sprung from the same source, growing in the same bed. I know this more than anyone.”

“Yes, well, sapience makes for lots of complications that interfere with ideals like that. Emotions in particular are not always logical or cut-and-dry.”

“I’ve seen it all, of course. But I don’t have that sort of fog over my mind. I don’t think you do, either, at least not in respect to sex.”

Azure shook her head. “It doesn’t matter. The key is that I’m flexible, she isn’t, and the bed we’ve made is exclusive. Nothing is more important to me than Samantha. Everyone else can burn, and I don’t care if that sounds evil.” After a significant pause, she added, “Let me focus, now, please.”

Through the winding corridors and turns of the catacombs, they came through an open arch into a wide area that was… ripped apart and partially collapsed. A huge hole was in the center, going down maybe two more levels into a bunch of irregular and jagged stone and brick destruction. Multiple sarcophagi were broken up, though there appeared to be no remains left.

In staring at it, Azure knew this was it — possibly the final piece of the puzzle was lying here. The gravitas had intensified, as had the baleful aura she’d felt throughout her walk in the catacombs.

“Here we go…” Azure said as she cast a Flight spell.

“It’s very lovely, really,” Six commented as she began hopping down through the rocks, many eyes on the lookout for threats.

Just as Azure was lowering herself down, she stopped short, blinking. Right over the area before the descent, in midair, was the focal point. It wasn’t actually below, in the stone.

“What the hell?” Azure muttered, puzzled. Slowly, she floated closer, moving her hand outward, feeling the intensity grow. She paused before fully connecting, dropping her hand, swallowing in sudden hesitation, and looking down at Six. “I’ve… got to do it here in midair. Not sure the spell is going to keep me floating, honestly…”

Six’s eyes widened, and she smiled. “Allow me, my gorgeous horned one!” In a sudden blur of motion, Six picked up first one big chunk of brick and the remains of a stone pillar, one in each hand, and smushed them together, as if they were no more than pebbles in her hand. They fused, smoothed, and elongated as Six created the first of a big pillar underneath Azure. In ensuing blurs of rapid movement, she grabbed more and stacked them, shoving reshaped stone blocks underneath the mass, building it up from the bottom.

Azure blinked in bewilderment as, in less than a minute, a stone-brick hybrid pillar gently pressed at the hanging tip of her foot and went up just slightly, a bit more until she was standing firm on top of it.

From below, Six could be seen with a big stone being loudly bounced on a palm, as she quirked an eyebrow questioningly. Each time the rock bounced, a cascade of smaller rocks and dust exploded everywhere.

Chuckling, Azure sat down cross-legged and called, “A little more, then.” Once it rose to the right height, Azure grunted favorably to halt the growth and added, “Thanks.”

Six hopped up on top of the pillar and made a bow. “You’re exceptionally welcome!”

Azure took out Deathsting and tossed him over the lip of the pillar, and he promptly transformed into his wyvern form, no words needing to be spoken that he should help keep an eye out, below.

With that done, Azure took a deep breath and began reaching out her hand. “Here goes nothing. It’s poking into the past time!”

Once her hand touched the center of the aura resonance, a vision took her.
