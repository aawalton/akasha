The clock was ticking.  Every magical alarm across the city would be going off, and squads of mages were no doubt forming up to teleport in and make my day miserable.  There was so much gold, though.  I blurred down the long rows of loot, snatching up everything as I passed.

Maybe there was a quicker option… I tried to load the shelving and its contents into my pocket dimension and was pleased to discover that it worked.  All I needed to do was get my hand within a foot or so of a row, and that section would vanish into storage.  What I would do with the no doubt expensive shelving was a different matter.  Would it count as loot, and I’d need to split the value with the thieves?

Muscles pumped as I blurred around the room.  Prenderghast the Expander had done good work on the vault.  It covered an area roughly the size of a football field, in a building that was far too small to allow for it.

Muffled voices came from outside the vault.

“Can’t portal straight in!  We aren’t allowed to!”

“Fuck what’s allowed!  We’re being robbed!”

I recognised the second voice.  It belonged to Angtirm, my first true foe in this world.  My grin became positively demonic as I continued to sprint around, stealing everything in sight.

“Get in there!”

A man leapt across the portal I’d left open on the floor by the door.  He hit the ground and rolled, then hurled a flurry of daggers at me.  They somehow crossed the distance in the blink of an eye and whizzed past me.  One landed in my arm, and I snarled in pain.  I consoled myself by stealing another set of racks and leaping behind the next.

With an effort of will that could only be described as gargantuan and masochistic, I did not immediately steal my new cover.  I heard more thuds as people entered the vault proper, barking orders and “clear!” at each other like good little soldiers.

I couldn’t stop cackling.  Knowing Angtirm was out there made this even sweeter.  I had been forced by circumstance not to eat that bastard when I took over the Mill.  Now I was under no such constraints; all I needed was an opportunity to finally take my revenge on the man.

“Fall back and use the gas first!” Angtirm snapped.  There was panic in his voice, and part of me shivered in delight.

That wasn’t going to be fun.  Vents opened above me, but nothing happened.  I’d been expecting green fumes to come spilling down; what I got was a faint hissing sound.  Deadly or knockout?  Either way wasn’t good for me.   I had the headspace to shift back into my true form; the vault was arched and could accommodate my draconic majesty.

Shifting would give me much better reach.  Eight tails, each a dozen metres long, and my armoured scales would be in play as well.  I’d also have lungs the size of cars.

As I transformed, I kept my snout close to the floor and snatched one massive breath of hopefully clean air before surging along the floor.  My snout also worked to get me in range to stash the loot, and between my nose and my multilimbed ass, I was clearing treasure at a rapid pace.

The clatter of the troopers moving into the room was lost in the sound of them opening fire at me.  Arrows spanged off my armoured hide, and spells washed over me.  A blue blot struck one of my tails and expanded into a block of ice that must have weighed nearly a ton.  I swivelled my snout back and doused it in orange-green fire, bashed the damn thing against the floor till I shook it loose and then continued my looting spree.

“There’s no escape for you!” yelled Angtirm.

No point replying.  Waste of breath, quite literally in my current situation.  I couldn’t risk checking the contents of my storage space; I’d be entranced and lose focus, so with iron willpower, I focused on what I needed to do: steal everything that wasn’t nailed down.  Then steal the nails and get the rest.

I rounded on the last section and charged, gold flying as I barreled into the shelves faster than I could store them away.  My tails were just as intent on maximising my gains as my brain, so they swept out and caught the strays on suckers for long enough for me to store them away.

Something stabbed through the scales on my side, and I hissed in pain, losing some precious oxygen in the process.  Rather than fret about the details of my response, I rolled over, leaning into whatever had hit me.

Noble Ninja level 89 slain!

Gold earned… Nothing!

489.4kg of biomass added to storage.

The corpse became visible as he died, smeared up my side and impaled on the blade he’s snuck between the overlap between my scales.

There are times in life when everything crystallises, and the things that matter resolve into perfect clarity.  I needed the rest of the loot, and then to get the hell out of dodge.  The mammalian part of my brain shut down, something cold and reptilian taking over.  Wrath, Greed, all of my mental menagerie changed forms, becoming reptilian and vicious.

I poked my head up above the shelves I hadn’t stolen yet and sent a blast of fire towards the vault door.  Shields sprang up as mages barked spells.  They were too far away for me to spot the sigils and steal their magic as well.

“Scutum Iris!  Scutum Iaculis!  Scutum Iris!  Scutum Iaculis!  Conjurare Barnaculum!”

With a thought, my rainbow shields swept in from where they’d appeared, scooping the mages and ranged fighters back towards the portal I’d left open on the ground.  Most of them jumped over it, but a few were bundled through to join my allies in the dungeon.  The melee specialists had spread out, too far apart to be cleaned up with the shields, but they got barnacles on the eyes, ears and noses, throwing their coordinated movements into confusion as they clawed at their faces.

Nearly done.  The last few sets of shelves disappeared into storage, and I rushed back towards the entrance to the vault.  Blades bounced off me, which I ignored.  My tails smashed bodies or tossed them away as I went, and within seconds, I was pouring point-blank dragon into the entrance, driving back the mages who had managed to dispel my shields.  Kill notifications popped up, but I ignored them.  I was starting to feel uncomfortably full again.

Adding counter-magic to the list of things I would have to steal, I was briefly grateful for the oddity of my breath attack mechanic.  If I’d needed to breathe to use it, I’d have been in trouble.

Two more warriors died, and I was forced to relieve the pressure.

1200kg of biomass evacuated.

Gods that felt better, and it was a satisfyingly appropriate calling card to leave behind.  I leapt, my scales brushing the ceiling before I began to shift back.  Sending a blast of fire towards the men and women gathered on the other side of the entrance, I gave them the middle finger as I plummeted through the gap and snapped the portal closed above me.

“That went well, I think?”  I said cheerfully before taking in the scene in front of me.

Pocket lay on her side, unmoving.  Burglar was engaged in close combat with a pair of warriors while Lughole and Particlics threw metal objects at the feet of the handful of mages from the security team who had been knocked down.

“Help her!” Burglar snapped, pointing at Pocket.

“Sanara Vulnera!”  Green light swept out, and the woman moaned softly as her flesh began to knit back together.  Where the hell is my security? Look, you lot, knock it off!  You’re trapped here; even if you win, the unbunnies will get you.  Surrender or become rabbit food!”

“Rabbits don’t eat people,” muttered one of the warriors.  They fell back slightly, trying to come together to face the threat I represented.

“These ones do.  You don’t want to get on the wrong side of Bun Bun the third or whichever of the OG monsters spawn is in charge at the moment,” I replied.

A blast of fire smacked me in the face, sending me reeling.  The sounds of fighting broke out again as I blinked my eyes clear.

“Missa Somnambulis!”  To my surprise, it didn’t work.

“We’ve got counterspells active,” sneered one of the mages as he jumped back to avoid a glowing trinket thrown by Particlics.

“Does it work on dragon fire?”  I asked sweetly.

“Why are you wearing purple underwear?” asked Pocket, her voice woozy as she pulled herself to her feet.  “Nice thighs.  You ever fancy a roll in the hay…”

“I’m married!”  I looked back at the Lucratis people.  “You don’t need to die here.  Just surrender!”

“If we don’t, we’re dead anyway, you bastard!”

This day just kept getting better.  From behind the knot of mages stepped an old, familiar face.

“Angtirm!  Nice of you to join us!  Do you like what I’ve done with the place?”

“I’ve never been here before, rebel.  I shouldn’t have agreed to withdraw from the Mill.  I could have nipped this all in the bud,” he said bitterly.

“You’d lost.  I’d have killed you, and the Foreverknot troops had already run away.  I should have killed you and not bothered about the title.  Titles aren’t shiny, but now I’ve got all your boss's gold, and you’re in the shit.”  Glee, exuberant happiness, it is hard to describe the emotions running through me in that moment.  My mental menagerie was rapidly escalating from back pats and high fives to something disturbing as Lust got more and more worked up.

“Kill them,” Angtirm ordered.

“Erm, sir, I’m not sure that’s even possible,” muttered one of his warriors.

The fighters had taken the opportunity to regroup, the Banks people falling back together around their superior.

“I mean, the thieves we can probably manage, but that prick with the exploding figurines is a problem.”  The soldier waved a hand at Particlics who menaced with them with what I now realised was a tiny carving of a fox.

“I’m a thief too!” snapped Particlics.

“It’s not something to be proud of,” I muttered.  I’d fallen in with thieves, and as much as I liked them, mostly due to the vast wealth I currently had stashed in my pocket dimension, they were still the vilest of creatures.  As a professional hoarder, they represented a threat to everything I held dear.  And here they were inside my dungeon.  If they all died… no, Philpott would still be there to collect the Guild's share.

“Neither is being a rebel and a usurper,” sneered Angtirm.  “I hear you got married?”

I blinked at the non-sequitur.  “And became a father.”

“The woman and the child are marked.  They’ll never be safe.”

“Morris is his own dragon,” I bluffed.  I would be extremely miffed if anyone went after him on my account.  “But if you threaten Esme again…”

“Oh yes, that was her name.  The slut from the bar, what was it called, the Flinging Sod?”

A wave of anger rolled out from me, manifesting that odd draconic aura that I’d never been able to master.  All the puny mammals were pushed to their knees as I strode forward.  “Don’t talk about my wife that way,” I growled, the words seeming to fill the world.

“She… wasn’t that good in the sack…anyway,” Angritm ground out, a pained smile on his face as I arrived to stand over him.

A portal opened beneath his feet, and he vanished as I took a long, slow breath, and my vision became less pink-tinged.

“Tim, got another zombie worker for you, or an experimental test subject.  You can do whatever you want with this one.”

“Whatever I want?  Cheers, Bob!” Tim called up cheerfully.  “Now, new friend, how do you feel about amalgamation?  We need stronger warriors for the frontlines.”
