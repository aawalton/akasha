After the walk with my dad, we both agreed there really wasn’t much need to bother anyone else with the vision I was shown. It wasn’t like I wanted to hide it from anyone. But like I’d told my dad, it just didn’t change anything.

We still needed to build our faction, we still needed to gain levels, and we still needed to help anyone we possibly could.

And all of that, started with planning.

Just like I’d promised, every assimilated faction had representation within the greater faction of Layton Mischief. When Jared first proposed the idea of building a city hall where we could conduct our councils, I’d considered it overkill.

Now though?

Every space in the meeting chamber was filled with someone. Hundreds of bodies crowded into the main hall and loud chatter echoed through the room bouncing from the rafters above.

As usual, Jared sat at the front along with me and Ellison.

Ellison was of course a fighter class, but he’d become Jared’s vice steward in all but title, and it felt right to have him at the front.

I’d already informed Jared that I had no intention of speaking today, and he agreed to run the meeting. We’d spoken before, and Jared was aware of what I wanted to accomplish. He was also briefed on the situation with the Gabordians, they hadn’t accepted our invitation quite yet, but I was confident they would soon enough.

Jared started the meeting by welcoming the new members of our faction from New Boise, which included my father but not my mother. While Jared covered the agenda of the meetings, which would include breakouts into separate meeting rooms later, I scanned the members of my faction.

Cassie, Nick, and Alex sat in the front row along with several of the Guildians. Behind them was Gorf and the gnomes, followed by Harold and the Azulites, Trasnik and the goblins, humans from New Boise, Enora and the dark elves, Wolf men, and even one of the Red Orcs.

All in all, it was a very diverse group.

Yet somehow, so far, people had managed to co-exist. The question now was, how could we turn that coexistence into thriving?

The scourge trial had already done a lot to push us into the “more money than we knew what to do with territory”. Now it was more about making sure that money was put to good use. Jared was convinced that meant establishing a robust distribution network between cities.

Intercity travel was simple enough, and teleporters were common. The challenge was rough goods, building materials, and even livestock. None of those items could be stored in dimensional storage.

Jared was particularly excited about the schematics for airships. So excited in fact, that he’d created an entire list of objectives related to achieving his goal of entire fleet of airships. A group from New Boise, and another from the Elder Gnomes had already begun dedicating resources.

But that wasn’t why we were here.

We were here to welcome the first official sub-faction representatives.

“Welcome everyone!”

Jared’s voice was louder than his normal speaking voice. The podium he stood behind had been fitted with a mana shard that enhanced his voice, so the entire hall could hear him clearly without him needing to shout.

The side conversations abruptly hushed, and attention shifted to the front of the room.

I could only see the back of Jared’s head, but I knew he was smiling.

“What an exciting day. As Steward of faction Layton Mischief, I am pleased to read the first elected sub-faction representatives.”

Jared cleared his throat and pulled his costmary notebook, thumbing to a section where the page had its corner turned down to mark its spot.

“Those whose names are read today, please take a spot at the head of hall.”

He gestured behind himself at the empty chairs situated near where Ellison and I sat.

“For the Guildian’s, Jamus.”

I raised an eyebrow and glanced at Ellison. He looked at me and smiled reassuringly as Jamus stood and made his way to the stand where he sat next to Ellison. I was a bit surprised by Jamus being the elected Guildian, but then again Ellison seemed to have other aspirations.

Maybe I’d ask him about it later.

“The Dark Elves, Enora.”

No surprise there.

“New Boise, Bob.”

The popular vote had gone handily to my dad, Martin. He’d turned it down. He felt there might be a conflict of interest. I didn’t push the issue. It wouldn’t make much difference to me anyway.

Next was Gorff of the Elder Gnomes, Harold from the Azulites, Trasnik from the High Goblins, Damon from Layton Mischief Township, and Talon from the group of surviving Red Orcs.

Talon rose from the congregation. He was built like Alex, tall and brawny. When I’d first encountered the intimidating Red Orc, he was barely covered in thin straps of leather, his black hair a matted mess of dirt and grime. Now?

His hair was washed and pulled back into a ponytail. His leathers were replaced with a crisp white cotton shirt and clean leather pants that had become popular within our faction.

The upgrade was Jared’s idea. When I told him that I wanted to make sure his group had representation, he hadn’t fought me on it. He just said that we needed to make him more palatable to the other factions.

It was a good call.

As Talon made his way to the stand with the rest, he held his head up high. As he approached, I nodded at him.

His eyes met mine, and he nodded back, taking his seat with the others.

The last name called was one Boretek’s slaves. Carlos, the self-proclaimed magical landscaper.

He chose a seat separated from the rest in the corner of the stand.

There were still plenty of seats left open.

I was sure Tenor would fill a spot soon, followed by many others.

Once all the names were read, Jared explained the different breakouts that would happen and invited any people who were interested to join the individual rooms.

Layton Mischief was getting organized. We needed a professional army. It was actually Harolds suggestion. He pointed out that right now, there was a sense of duty to fight and protect the faction and the people in it. But that wouldn’t always be the case. Great factions needed great armies.

There were rooms dedicated to army configuration, freight logistics, zoning for new residential districts, new agriculture plots,, and my personal favorite, who would get the bid to construct the training facility?

The answer? Still very much unsure.

Building the new training facility was essentially the equivalent of fighting a major boss fight or completing a great dungeon for a non-combatant. The rewards would be immense in experience, titles, and money.

Everyone wanted a piece.

All I wanted was the building.

According to the description, once the training facility was completed it would give our faction access to a whole new section of the system interface that included the ability to purchase new skill books.

Some of the faction members had already earned skill books. Including myself.

Fundamentals of Sword Combat

Just from that one skill book I felt like I’d learned ten years' worth of knowledge. And that was only the fundamentals.

I was very eager to get my hands on more.

But I trusted Jaerd to get it done. It's why he was my steward after all. My specialty was in other areas.

Once people started separating into the breakout meetings, I was heading for the exit.

Mischief was already out exploring somewhere, and I was itching to do the same. There was still so much more to find in our valley that we’d never even seen. Our faction was growing, and I needed to keep growing alongside it.

The foundation was laid down. It was time for expansion.

Even though the room was crowded, people parted when they saw me coming. By now, I’d become accustomed to the eyes constantly watching me. News of my fight with Boretek was making the rounds in LM, mingling with other fights I’d had and twisting together into a cocktail of accomplishments I wasn’t sure I loved.

I couldn’t help but feel like each sideways glance was sizing me up. They were trying to figure out if the man matched the stories. I’m sure there were plenty who wondered if they were looking at the right person. That didn’t bother me though.

It just felt like the more the stories were shared, the more they grew.

Oh well.

I stepped outside.

It was raining again, making the cobble roads shine like polished stones. The twinkling streetlamps powered by mana stones conjured within me somber emotions that drew on the nostalgia of the season.

My feet carried me into the square pavilion. The dreary weather hadn’t dissuaded people from going about their business and it was just as busy as ever. I made my way across the square to stop at a busy shop that was selling warm bread and cheese.

The delicious aroma wafted from the doorway, joined by cheerful conversations. Inside people sat around wooden tables surrounding a cozy looking stone firepit where orange flames crackled.

The line was long, but I wasn’t in a huge hurry and the bread in this shop was worth the wait. I’d become somewhat of an addict since the shops started popping up through the faction.

One of the women working behind the counter noticed me and waved for me to pass to the front of the line. I returned her wave bashfully trying to gesture I didn’t mind waiting, but she shook her head and beckoned even more urgently. Reluctantly, I lowered my head and pushed my way forward.

A few people seemed annoyed until they turned and realized who I was.

“How many times do I have to tell you?” The middle-aged woman with tattoo sleeves of anime characters scolded. “You don’t ever wait in line in my shop!”

The woman reached behind the counter and pulled out a steaming loaf of what I think might be sourdough, although I really didn’t know. I just knew that once I chomped down on the bread, I would be rewarded with a satisfying crispy crust and cloudy soft insides.

“It’s really ok Stella, I don’t mind waiting.” I grumbled, taking the loaf and replacing it with several UBCs. “Can I get a couple to go?”

She swiped the coins and pulled half a dozen more loafs from behind the counter while rolling her eyes. For some bizarre reason, Stella, along with every other person in this faction, found it downright insufferable that I refused to take their products free of charge.

I ignored her obvious display of annoyance and politely thanked her for the loaves, dropping the extras into a storage bag at my hip before slipping back out into the rain where a tall, cloaked figure stood waiting.

“Ready?”

Daevon’s deep voice seemed to buzz ever so slightly as he spoke, like a radio that was just barely out of tune. I ripped off a healthy mouthful of bread savoring the satisfying crackle of the crust.

“Ready.” I said through a mouthful of bread.

Two seven-foot-long wings sprouted from the robes on my back.

It was time for LM to start its rapid expansion project. Besides developing the territories that we already had control over, we wanted to control our valley.

Our factions' fighters had grown to nearly 100,000 with the addition of New Boise. It felt like a staggering number compared to when I’d started, but numbers really didn’t mean much unless they could contribute.

We’d created three different armies to begin sweeping through the valley offering peace whenever possible. I still wasn’t in love with the idea of eliminating other factions, but my fairytale’s idea of being friends with every faction while keeping my own safe was being eroded little by little.

It didn’t help that I was recently threatened, for all intents and purpose, by a god. And all I did was kill his son for trying to kill me. What was I supposed to do? Was that what a millennium of life bought you? Rather than carefully cultivated patience and wisdom. Petulance?

It irked me. Why wasn’t Red Beard better?

But I already knew the answer. Because when you're the strongest, you can be whatever you want.

My body bobbed to the rhythmic beat of my wings carrying me higher with each thrum until I was coasting just close enough to touch the twirling grey of the clouds.

Daevon wasn’t far off.

His lower body was obscured behind a rolling charcoal grey thunder cloud. His method of flight didn’t have quite the same top end speed as my wings did, but his spell came with a boost to his lightning attacks when he was standing in the five-foot cloud, and it wasn’t a huge draw on his mana when he was just flying.

So far, it was just the two of us who could fly, and we were on a mission.

There were hundreds of small territories scattered throughout the valley. In just the twenty-five or so that we’d been through, I’d already met and incorporated four human factions, not including New Boise.

Jared and I were both convinced there had to be more out there somewhere. So, while Ellison and the other armies went about clearing territories around our faction, Daevon and I set out to find other human factions.

The plan was simple.

Daevon and I would fly low and just look for humans. At first, I was worried it would be hard to see anything from so high up. But I was totally wrong.

Increased stats really were incredible.

Green, orange, yellow, brown and blue worked together to create a masterpiece of beauty. Leaves on deciduous trees were starting to fall. My eyes scanned the forest for signs of life. We already knew that there would be other factions, but those would be handled by the armies.

It was easy to get lost in the beauty of my flight. Droplets of rain poured down my hair and over my cheeks. I could tell the air was freezing cold, but it just felt refreshing. A shiver of appreciation crawled down my back at the pure exhilaration.

I spared a glance at Daevon. His dark blue robes flapped in the wind. The Guildian really did look formidable. Or most of the time he did.

Right now, though?

He was smiling.

It was the first time I’d seen the sober Guildian show even a shred of emotion. Apparently, not even the ever-stoic Daevon was immune to the wonders of the system.
