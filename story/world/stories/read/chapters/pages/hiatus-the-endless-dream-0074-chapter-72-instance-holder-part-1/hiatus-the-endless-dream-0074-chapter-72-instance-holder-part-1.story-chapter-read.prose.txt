I hadn’t made it more than ten steps into the Dream when screaming started frying my brain. I hardly recognized what they were; too busy rolling on the floor. It was layered and sounded like the damned souls of hundreds of thousands of people compressed into the span of twenty seconds. Then it was over, and I found myself hurling, throwing up, and bleeding from my eyes on the floor of Joey’s room. Something had killed me. Something had killed me the moment I stepped into the Dream, and there was nothing I could do about it, even with the relatively large amount of Presence I had.

Dorian sat on Joey’s bed where I had left him, a hollow look in his eyes. He looked strange, like a mannequin of himself. Too rigid and stiff. My heart picked up, throat closing as I feared the possibility that I had accidentally stepped into an Instance, yet no notifications came.

“Dorian?” I asked.

He jumped, staring at me with wide eyes as if he’d seen a ghost.

Dark brown eyes met mine, and he said nothing. Time stretched on until my heart settled itself enough for me to get more than one word out.

“Are you alright? Did your meeting with Ananke … go poorly? Did she not accept?”

He looked like I slapped him.

“Ah. Right. Yes, that. It went well. As well as it could have, I guess.”

A chill ran down my spine, and my fingers twitched. Something felt wrong. Very wrong. It should be him, but it didn’t feel like Dorian. It wasn’t an Instance, I knew that, but that didn’t mean something else hadn’t invaded the world directly. It hadn’t been that long. It wasn’t possible. I didn’t know what to do, but I knew I was where I didn’t want to be. I pulled on the feeling of entering the Dream, of taking a step and being somewhere else and commanding it to happen.

“No. Don’t do that. It’s not safe,” Dorian said.

The feeling dissipated, and I stumbled. I reached out through my mental link to Dorian, “Is that you? In front of me?”

A pitiful laugh resounded around the room. “It’s me … Maerin. Sorry, it’s … it’s been a while.”

“We just separated,” my voice came out shaky. I scowled and bit into the side of my cheek, letting the pain diffuse the fear.

“Time dilation. Ananke had a lot to say. We … we had a lot of misconceptions, but it’s fine. I know what to do now. We were still on the right track.”

“Dorian,” I started, “What happened?”

The far-off look in his eyes returned.

“A lot. A lot happened.”

Moonlight - Dorian

[Once More.]

The sliver of Ananke’s existence she had broken off to teach me thundered in my mind, and I clung to it with all my might.

I had thought the worst pain I could possibly ever feel was the process of my body being rebuilt from the barest hints of my consciousness through [No Good Deed]. The shock of my mind begging for existence had been forcibly suppressed as I threw myself into solving a cruel Instance. It was the only thing I could do back then. Somehow, this was worse.

Not worse in terms of actual pain, but having to do it manually without the Boon had been torture. There was no point in tracking how long I had been practicing. Ananke had done something with time. Half of it felt like a dream. She had started by creating a bare room, and in that bare room was a door. I could not say what the door looked like, even while trying to commit any details to memory; they evaded me. It was not because the appearance of the door shifted like my attempts at creating a wall had. The door’s appearance held in static space, but I could not grasp it, and through the door sat everything all at once. All of anything that had and could exist, beyond a single door.

The first month of my time was spent trying to tear my eyes away from the door.

Then she started her lessons.

They were simple in comparison.

Create a box.

And I did it without complaint.

[How big is the box?] she had asked, and my creation had shattered.

[Again.] she had demanded.

I made the box. I made it a perfect meter on every side.

[What material is it made out of?]

The wood was overlaid with cardboard, and the box broke.

[Once More.]

[How heavy is it?]

That box didn’t break until I tried to pick it up.

[Once More.]

[Any scents?]

I started learning at that point. Changing the properties of my simple little box.

[Once More.]

Over and over again.

[I’m tired] I had said.

[Why?]

[Because I’ve been working non-stop for days. I need a break. I need a second to think or breathe or do anything else, fuck.]

[No. You still do not understand, then. You are not tired. You can not get tired. Let me show you.]

And she had. Ananke had allowed more of herself into the sliver, and her Presence pushed on every part of me, and I was no longer tired. Not in body or mind.

[Tired is a concept. You are an Instance Holder. In your domain, you control concepts. You are not tired unless you decide to be.]

I wanted to be disgusted. To have horror fill me and break down at the idea of having the very ideas of life stripped away slowly, but I felt refreshed. I wasn’t tired, not of anything.

Eventually, the lessons became more complex. The box turned into a room with decoration. The decorations then had to hold a function. The Room turned into a house and then a neighborhood. All of that took another month.

After I had created the neighborhood, Ananke would have me walk through it. She’d single out a house and make me repeat everything that existed in it. Then she’d have me expand the neighborhood. The pain of doing so was excruciating. It wasn’t so much the act of creation that pushed at my mind more than the need to keep each of those creations in existence, consistent, and static. Eventually, I ran into a wall. I just couldn’t remember anything more. I could make more neighborhood, but it all became nebulous—even the previously solid portions of space—like a less solid version of the Dream, but not quite the chaos beyond the door.

[You’re obsessed with Presence.]

[You should be too.]

[I just want to know how to keep my world safe and find Rowan.]

[It is one and the same. You need Presence.]

[I can make things now. I’m missing creatures, though. We need them next.]

[No. We have something else before that. You did well in shaping Presence externally, but more important is the ability to do that internally.]

[Pause. It sounded like it, but you just confirmed it. What do you mean by shaping Presence?]

[That’s what you’re doing. What is Presence if not the act of existing? The Presence is a part of you, and you shape it to create. You can only stretch your existence so far before it thins to nothing.]

[Like the things I make are part of my fucking skin or something?]

[What the fuck are you talking about?]

[Things only exist if they are observed. By creating something, you are observing it, and by keeping it in your mind, it’s a type of observation. That observation takes up Presence, but it doesn’t have to be your Presence.]

Context helps shape Visitor perception prior to seeing the impression, but it is not a foolproof thing.]

[So use more solid impressions to build context, and once I’ve anchored my Visitors further into my Instance, I can use looser impressions and have their Presence do the heavy lifting.]

She had then made my leg disappear. It didn’t hurt. No blood either, just a perfect cut revealing what amounted to a colored in CT cross-section of my upper thigh.

[Create a new leg.]

It escalated continuously. She’d make both arms vanish, then all my limbs, then my heart; on occasion, she’d steal a piece of my brain. Despite not having a piece of my thought processes, I could still feel my thoughts in the space around me. Ananke had mentioned it, but I hadn’t understood. Part of me, my Presence, was stuck observing the neighborhood around me. I was in, and simultaneously was, my domain. So if part of my brain was stolen, my consciousness still existed there.

I thought back to the last time I pulled myself together, and the red text box that had screamed at me to use my Boon. Had that just been me? Part of my Presence acting as an instinct? I didn’t know. I didn’t find out either when Ananke escalated to making my entire body vanish into nothingness.

The first time had been the worst. Having no senses, yet still existing, held uncommunicable agony. The best comparison I had would be intense vertigo. My thoughts weren’t quite the same. I was the blue 1.3kg ceramic vase, the marble countertops, the red door on the house, and the asphalt painted yellow in the center of the street. I was there, and I knew I was there and that I was supposed to be making myself a body, but it was split between what felt like thousands of single-celled organisms capable of only instinct, not thought or reason for how or why.

It still worked. I pulled myself together again and again. Just not perfectly. Pieces were in the wrong place. The skull was not intended to rest on the heart nor on the eyes to rest directly on each of my fingernails, which sat atop my knuckles.

[Once More.]

And I was scattered again. Rebuilt again until I got it right.

That one only took three weeks.

Then the process of creating monsters came. I decided to go simple and made a large masked man with an axe, and to my surprise, I found that I could see through the creature's eyes. Think with its brain and move its body as if it were my own, and I could do that in both of my bodies at once.

[There are two ways to create a mind. One: you can shove the body through that door, into the chaos beyond the Dream, and wait. Eventually, something will come to fill the vessel. That way is dangerous, as it will no longer be your creation. You will only have control of its body and nothing else.]

I frowned, not quite understanding how it was any different from anything else. It felt like the equivalent of walking up to someone off the street and shoving them into the Dream. Even that wasn’t quite right, since Ananke made it sound like this process would give a mind to the body, but they’d still have no history to go along with it.

[The other way?] I asked.

[The other way is to take a piece of a copy of someone’s mind. A small part, the parts responsible for motor function and basic thought, are the best. Copying the brains of children or the dying is the best. Place that copy inside the vessel and then shape the world’s context to raise the monster as you want it to behave. If you want it to be violent, raise it in violence. If you want it to be smart, raise it where it has to be cunning rather than strong.]

I nodded, a small bead of excitement welling in my chest. The power of an Instance Holder, of a god, still frightened me, but it was almost completely blocked out by the idea of creation. The axe murderer I had created. What environment could I make for them to be effective? It’d be best to start with the classic Jason archetype. I could turn the neighborhood into a little lakeside summer camp. I could create a number of empty vessels with basic instructions, without a true mind attached to them. Then … then what? Cut them loose on unsuspecting Visitors? Even if I made this monster, it wouldn’t help me against the Instance Holders. Not directly. Ananke’s words resounded in my head. Her reminder that if I wanted to protect anything, I needed Presence, and my monsters could farm that Presence for me.
