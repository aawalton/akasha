Chapter 116

Joint Guild Exercise, Part III (Displays of Power 1)

August 3rd

0 days, 12 hours, and 10 minutes until Phase 2 of the Gods Game commences…

The following morning comes too fast.

That’s the thing about “big breaks.” They don’t arrive with confetti. They arrive with calendar reminders and a tight feeling in your chest that doesn’t seem to want to go away, like you dry swallowed your multivitamin regiment and it didn’t go down as smoothly as you expected.

The AEA has my entire day plotted out to the very last second. Jerome and I are back at the AEA warehouse for early morning joint-Guild practice and training. After that, we’re supposed to spend the rest of the day on tactical prep—intel reports, threat profiles, strategy sessions—then back to the warehouse for more training this evening. All in preparation for tomorrow morning, the day of the expected Gate breach. Of course, a few hours before the breach we have a final briefing. As if by that point in time, there’s anything else we can do to be prepared to take on a Level 130 shark-king and his small army of baddies.

I show up in activewear—a pair of black shorts, a gray compression shirt, and broken in running shoes. I look like I’m about to do a HIIT session that ends in vomiting. Which, honestly, might not be far off.

Jerome shows up in a suit.

Today’s shade is a professional navy, with a clean white shirt dappled in a faint, powder blue pattern. A pair of black, designer loafers with a matching leather belt. I’m pretty sure those two articles of clothing alone cost as much as my apartment’s monthly rent (if Harvest didn’t foot the bill). He looks more like he’s on his way to negotiate a merger, not throw down in a containment arena with elite System Users. Perhaps he’s trying to look good for Stephanie Wolf’s camera golems, who I’m sure will be in watching our training session?

I stare at him as we walk through the security checkpoint.

“Are you… seriously wearing that? No change of clothes?” I ask.

Jerome doesn’t even look offended. He just adjusts his cuff. “Yes. And no.”

“Jerome.”

He finally glances at me, mild amusement in his eyes. “I’ll take off my jacket if I need to.” And then he adds, with the faintest smirk, “Though I don’t believe I will.”

That makes me snort.

We’re escorted into the warehouse space again. Reshma and Matt are already there. Matt is in an all-black bodysuit with the Pegasus Guild emblem blazoned across his chest. A golden, winged stallion in flight. He has no visible weapons. And no flashy gear. He’s also barefoot. I can tell he’s ready to spar.  He stands near the center of the floor, eyes half-lidded, expression flat, looking like he’d rather be anywhere else on Earth. Or, better yet, any Realm other than Earth.

Reshma is beside him in a similar suit, but hers is white. It’s clean and bright, like she’s the “good cop” to Matt’s “silent nightmare.” The Pegasus Guild emblem on her suit is in a bright, red fabric. It almost looks like a bloodstain across her chest. Ironic, for the strongest healer in Pegasus Guild. Her hair is braided into a long ponytail, which is wrapped into a tight bun atop her head. She’s also barefooted. She smiles when she sees us, warm and genuine, like she’s meeting friends at a yoga class and not preparing for an offshore monster breach that threatens to wipe my little Rust Belt city off the map.

“Good morning,” she says, hands clasped lightly in front of her.

“Morning,” I reply.

Matt gives a nod that might be a greeting or might be a tic. His gray eyes flick to Jerome’s suit, then to my activewear, then away. I can’t tell if he’s judging us or just cataloging. Probably both.

Jerome steps forward smoothly, offering Reshma a polite nod. “Ms. Murmu. Mr. Bruck.”

“Jerome,” Reshma says kindly, like she already knows him. “Joseph. Did you two have a good night’s rest?”

“I little hard to sleep,” I admit. “With everything going on.”

Reshma considers this for a second.

“Do you mind if I touch you?” she asks.

“Er… Sure,” I choke out.

She steps forward and gently places her hands on my shoulders. A warmth washes over my shoulders and I feel instantly refreshed. Like I had the best cup of coffee during my morning shower. My focus sharpens and any residual fatigue is scrubbed away as though Reshma to a spiritual sponge to the dirty skillet of my brain. A soft pulse echoes through my mind.

Ping!

SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: You are now [Refreshed]!

“Wow,” I breathe. “Thank you!”

“Of course,” she says, taking a polite step back. “We are teammates, after all.” She turns her head to Jerome. “Jerome, should I help you as well?”

Jerome raises a hand. “I appreciate the offer, but I am fine. Thank you.”

Matt’s gaze settles on me a second longer this time. Like he’s trying to remember something. Then he looks away again.

I roll my shoulders, feeling my muscles fully wake up thanks to whatever Skill Reshma used to give me the [Refreshed] status. I’m thinking about Lake Erie, about the DMG, about this Nereus we’re going to need to fight.

And then I hear the subtle whir of something mechanical behind the viewing glass. Camera golems. Of course, Stephanie Wolf is here. Watching us.

I exhale slowly and mutter under my breath, “Alright.”

Reshma raises her hand, getting our attention. “Before we begin with warmup drills, it would probably be a good idea for each of us to get a sense of what the others can do.”

Matt doesn’t respond. He just stands there like a shadow that learned to breathe. If he does that long enough, I might forget he’s even here.

Reshma continues, unfazed. “To begin, we should switch into our System gear.”

I feel my shoulders tighten. Show and tell time.

“Sure,” I say, trying to sound casual.

Reshma steps forward—and pixelated light blooms around her body like a digital sunrise.

I can’t help but think, Oh my God, is she about to do a full magical girl transformation sequence?

Like any moment now, she’s going to spin, pose, and shout something like MOONLIGHT JUSTICE FORM! while sparkles explode.

She doesn’t.

But the effect is still dramatic enough that my brain wants to clap anyway.

The light fades, and Reshma is no longer donning her figure-tight bodysuit. A white cloak drapes over her shoulders, large hood resting back casually. Under it, sleek armor crafted from something that looks like white gold. Scales of luminous, elegant and shining metal linking together to form a seamless layer of armor. A silver thread now laces through her hair, catching the light like moonlit wire. Rings adorn three fingers on each hand—six total—and each ring hums faintly with contained power when I focus on them. On her feet are a pair of white knitted loafers.

I activate my [Aura Sense], ratcheting it up and take her in. She becomes a body of blinding white light. I wince and suppress my sixth sense, immediately regretting the decision. She’s dipped head-to-toe in some serious enchanted gear. Even those loafers were shining. Reshma, however, continues to stand there, gentle smile on her lips. Even with my [Aura Sense] intentionally dampened, she’s surrounding by a steady, serene pressure, like I’m standing near a cathedral bell that hasn’t rung yet but could.

Matt equips his gear next. Pixelated light wraps him like a shroud and collapses inward.

When it fades, he’s… exactly what I expected, honestly.

All-black armor and leathers cover his body. Matte and minimal, nothing flashy. Black sandals on his feet, because apparently Pegasus Guild gear has an aversion to socks. A headband wraps around his forehead, not dissimilar to some ninja anime I used to watch as a kid where everyone whispered dramatically about friendship and threw knives and fireballs for emotional reasons. Jet black metallic gauntlets cover his hands, catching the warehouse lighting in hard flashes.

He glances at me, expression still flat.

I swallow.

“Okay,” I mutter. “My turn.”

I mentally select all of my equipment. Pixelated light crawls over me, my street clothes dissolving away as my gear snaps into place. First, my boots and my hat, followed by my new cape. Then, my shirt dissolves, just as the fanny pack of dimensional holding forms around my waist. And, last but not least, there’s my now iconic jorts. Always the jorts.

The light fades, and I’m standing there in all my glory.

Reshma takes me in, starting with the Behemoth Cap and slowly tracking south. Her eyes widen—just a little—when she reaches my torso and legs.

“Oh,” she says, voice gentle and kind. “That is… quite something.”

“Thank you,” I say dryly. “It’s cursed—by the way—not something I choose to wear. Mostly.”

“Cursed?” asks Matt, raising an eyebrow. He shifts slightly, as though that may be contagious.

“Ha! Yeah, but it’s fine,” I say, eyeing where I suspect Wolf and her golems are hidden. “I hope Stephanie’s special is PG-13, though.”

Reshma giggles.

Matt’s mouth twitches. It’s not a smile. It’s the closest thing to one I’ve seen from him.

“The shorts are nice,” he says, voice flat but unmistakably playful. “Very intimidating.”

“Right?” I say. “They’re my strongest weapon. Emotional damage. No one expects to lose to a guy who looks like this!” I strike an archer pose as dramatically as I can.

Reshma chuckles softly, like she approves of coping mechanisms.

Finally, it’s Jerome’s turn.

Pixelated light surrounds him too, though it’s a small ripple that washes over his body. Even his transformations are clean and professional. Good for him, I think, nodding.

When the light fades, he looks exactly the same.

I stare. “Jerome… did something go wrong with your Inventory?”

“No.”

“Did you even change?” I ask.

Jerome adjusts his tie slightly, calm as ever. “Yes.”

Reshma tilts her head, intrigued. Matt’s eyes narrow, mildly curious.

Jerome continues, as if explaining something simple. “All of my gear has been magically modified to take on the exact appearance of any suit I may be wearing at that moment.”

He looks at us like this is obvious.

“I find there is benefit to not parading around in public in armor,” Jerome says. “Certain things require subtlety.”

I think about walking into Starbucks in my jorts and wizard cap and realize he’s not wrong.

“That’s… a fair point,” I admit.

“Cosmetic enchantments like that are… very pricey,” Reshma says, admiring Jerome’s suit not-suit. She nods approvingly, silver-thread hair catching the light as she moves.

Matt’s gaze flicks over Jerome again, assessing. Then, he gives a single nod of his head. “That’s cool as hell.”

“Alright,” Reshma says cheerfully. “I’ll start.” She turns slightly, letting her white cloak fall open just enough to show the sleek white-gold armor beneath. The rings on her fingers hum faintly as she gestures. “My Class is titled Bone Cleric,” she says. “The ‘Sainted Blade’ title is… marketing. And not accurate marketing, either. Blade makes me sound like a fighter.” She shakes her head. “No. I could barely hurt a fly. Even with the System.”

Matt makes a tiny sound—could’ve been a laugh, could’ve been a breath. I get a sense that he’s not entirely pleased about his older companion spilling Guild secrets like that. I admit, with a title like Sainted Blade, I definitely thought she’d be wielding a sword of vanquishing light. Guess not.

Reshma continues, still smiling. “It gives people the wrong impression. I’m not a sword fighter. I’m a pure healer.”

I blink, thinking of Liv. “Yeah?”

She nods. “And actually one of the best. At least that I’m aware of. If I can say so, without sounding absolutely full of myself.”

The way she says it isn’t arrogant. I’m inclined to believe her.

“My specialty,” she goes on, “is supplementing my mana pool with my massive Health pool, and other things, to perform feats of healing and restoration.”

That makes my eyebrows climb. “Wait—what do you mean other things?”

Reshma’s smile turns a little more… mischievous. Like she’s about to show a kid something they definitely shouldn’t try at home.

“I can sacrifice bones,” she says gently.

I stare.

“Bones?” asks Jerome.

“Bones,” she repeats, as if clarifying the obvious. “Including my own bones and my bone density, if I need to. I have an Inventory full of other bones, however. They aren’t as potent as using mine, but far less risky to use.”

Jerome’s gaze sharpens. Matt’s eyes narrow slightly, not surprised so much as… respectful.

Reshma lifts a hand, flexes her fingers. “It boosts my healing capacity. And it eliminates casting times. There was this one time where Matt—” Her eyes flick to her companion. “Was incinerated. It was in an instant. A full adult dragon in a Gate we were clearing. I was able to bring him back, at the reasonable price of the bones in my left pinky toe.”

I feel my stomach do a small flip.

“Sheesh,” is all I can say. I eye down Matt, who shifts uncomfortably at the attention. I try to imagine what it’d feel like to be hit with flames so hot my entire Health bar is depleted and my entire body is turned to ash, only to be reconstituted in an instant. Did he remember being incinerated, or just coming back? I think of my own fight against the Headless Harbinger. I can feel the Behemoth Cap on my head purring at these thoughts.

Reshma looks at me expectantly. “Your turn, Joseph.”

I scratch the back of my head, suddenly aware that my class description sounds like something a drunk guy would invent at a gym.

“Uh… I’m a Muscle Mage,” I say.

Reshma’s brows lift. “Muscle Mage.” Then she smiles widely. “A nice counterpart to Bone Cleric.”

Matt’s mouth twitches again—barely there. Jerome remains a suit-shaped statue.

Reshma tilts her head. “And what does that entail?”

Matt chimes in. “Is that legitimately a spellcasting class? Or just a misnomer?”

I nod. “Yeah. I’m a legit spellcaster.”

I take a breath and explain, because if I say it too fast it sounds fake. “It’s… a mage that uses my body’s vitality to cast spells instead of mana,” I say. “So, I don’t have a mana pool. I have… this.” I gesture to my shirtless torso, letting my pecs dance a little for emphasis.

Reshma’s stifles a laugh. I watch her expression as she considers my explanation, and her eyes widen with interest. For the first time since she walked in she looks less like a saint and more like a scientist finding a rare specimen.

“That is…” she breathes, then smiles. “I’m very impressed.”

I shrug. “It’s mostly inconvenient.”

Reshma waves that away. “No. No, Joseph. Think about it.” She steps closer, rings catching the light. “With System improvements, enchanted equipment, high enough Physical Attributes, and healing—” She taps her own chest lightly. “—that means you have an astronomic ceiling for casting. Mages confined to mana as their source would salivate in jealously, I’m sure.”

I blink.

Hearing it phrased that way—astronomic ceiling—makes something in my chest tighten. I guess it’s exactly what Labonte told me when he scouted me. But it was nice hearing it from someone else, too.

“Can you demonstrate a casting?” Reshma asks. “Something I can observe?”

I hesitate for half a second, before nodding.

“Sure.”

Reshma’s expression turns focused. She mutters something under her breath—a spell, I think. Her eyes begin to glow, a frosty white light surrounding them like headlights through a frosted windshield.

She looks at me through that glow, and I get the sense she’s not just seeing me anymore.  She’s seeing something else. Something… more.

“Go ahead,” she says softly.

I flex. The familiar pose hits my muscles like a switch.

I cast [Wizard’s Fist]. Twice, out of habit more than anything.

Silvery mist blooms on either side of me. Righty appears first, massive spectral hand hovering with a faint glow. Lefty follows. The twin fists hang in the air, waiting. Patient, but eager to beat the shit out of something. Anything.

Reshma leans in slightly, eyes glowing brighter. I feel it—her magic brushing against the edges of my existence. It doesn’t feel invasive, not exactly. It’s attentive. Like a doctor listening to a heartbeat.

“Oh,” she whispers, surprised. Her voice is full of wonder. “I can see it,” she says. “The vitality. The… exchange.”

She watches my chest rise and fall, my muscles subtly tightening as my spell sustains itself.

“This is fascinating,” Reshma murmurs, almost to herself. “It’s like you’re burning your own life as fuel… but not dying. Not yet. Not even close. Your body is too strong.”

I swallow, suddenly aware that she’s not just impressed. She’s taking notes. And something about that makes me both proud and deeply uncomfortable. She cuts off her spell and the light around her eyes fade.

Matt goes next, like it’s a chore he’s been assigned and not an introduction.

He rolls his shoulders once, flexes his fingers inside those metallic gauntlets, and speaks in the same flat tone he uses for everything.

“My Class is called Nimble Ghost,” he says.

Reshma listens with that gentle interest she has, like she’s at a PTA meeting, which is impressive as I’m sure she’s intimately familiar with the functions of Matt’s Class features.

Matt continues. “I can turn invisible. I can temporarily phase into surfaces.” His gray eyes flick around the room once, quick and assessing. “High Dexterity.”

I nod slowly. “So… you must not get hit too often.”

Matt’s mouth twitches again—barely. Not a smile, but a concession that the statement is accurate.

“I’m hard to catch,” he says. “But when I am caught…”

He gestures to his neck. That thin scar, ear-to-ear, like a smile carved into the wrong part of his body.

“…things generally don’t go well.”

His scar is a reminder that the System sometimes leaves us with reminders that even Users can die. I think of Veronica’s scar from our fight against the Storm Dragon while in the Bronze Gate together. I swallow and simply nod.

Reshma’s expression softens, like she’s seeing the person underneath the Class for a second. She places a gentle hand on Matt’s shoulder.

Then she straightens, professional again. “Would you like to demonstrate for Harvest, Matt?”

Matt shrugs, effortless. “Sure… Why not.”

Reshma turns to me. “Joseph, would you attack him, please? Just enough to show how he moves.”

I blink. “Attack him?”

Matt stands there like he’s waiting for a bus.

Jerome, of course, says nothing.

I swallow and nod. “Okay.”

Lefty and Righty are still hovering beside me, spectral fists idling like eager dogs. I mentally direct them forward.

“Alright, guys,” I mutter. “Hit him. Lightly. Don’t—”

Lefty and Righty rocket across the floor in twin bursts of silvery mist.

Matt doesn’t move.

For half a second, I think—Wait, is he seriously too slow? Or are my fists just too fast? Is this going to be Chase Guild 2.0?

The fists connect.

Or… they should.

Instead, they pass through him like he’s made of fog.

The “Matt” in front of them disperses into mist, dissolving into a faint, shimmering illusion.

My brain lags.

What?

Then pain detonates in my face.

An arm—Matt’s arm—bursts out of my chest like my body is a doorway and he just kicked it open from the inside.

A gauntleted and very real fist is definitely sprouting from my chest. And it punches me square in the nose.

There’s a wet crunch.

My vision flashes white. A HUD blink pops into the corner of my sight like a smug reminder and my Health bar dips—barely. My Resistances catch mostly all of the blow. But the pain? The pain doesn’t care about my Resistances.

My nose is absolutely broken. Air and blood explode out at the same time. I stagger back, hands flying up, and the arm in my chest is already gone—withdrawn like it never existed, leaving me with the horrifying sensation that I just got hit by a ghost that lives in my ribs.

Before I can even swear properly, my [Perception] finally catches up. Matt reappears, right behind me. He’s just… there. Out of thin air. I spin on my heal, hands to my face to find the guy casually standing there, posture relaxed, like he didn’t just punch me from inside my own torso.

“Too much?” he asks, tone flat.

I stare at him, eyes watering, blood dripping down my upper lip. The System’s natural regeneration already kicked in and healed and reset my nose, thankful. A run my forearm across my face, wiping away the blood.

“Damn,” I manage, voice nasal and ugly, fighting back the feeling that I need to sneeze. “That’s… crazy.”

Reshma chuckles, like she’s amused by the sheer absurdity of it.

“What happens if that Skill is turned off while you’re phasing through organic matter?” asks Jerome.

Matt eyes him. “It’s not pretty.”

Reshma steps toward me, calm as ever, and places a gentle hand on the bridge of my nose. “Your nose didn’t set quite right.”

Her fingers are cool. A soft sensation washes through my face. Cold water over a burn.

The crunch unwinds further. The pressure disappears. Any lingering pain evaporates.

My nose snaps again, and then again. But there’s absolutely no pain.

I blink, stunned, and inhale carefully. Perfect airflow.

Reshma withdraws her hand with a satisfied nod. “There. Better.”

I touch my nose once, incredulous.

“Better,” I echo.

Then, I look at Matt. He’s watching me with that same half-lidded expression, like he’s already bored again.

I didn’t expect to have someone reach through me and punch me in the face. The entire experience was unsettling. I try my best not to think about it any further.

Jerome goes next like he’s presenting quarterly earnings. He steps forward, loosens his tie by half an inch—an act so subtle it feels like watching a man unsheathe a blade under a conference table—and looks at us with calm, flat certainty.

“My Class is Magistrate,” he says. “I create [Rules].”

There’s a pause.

“Rules?” asks Matt.

“Yes. My [Rules] are limited only by my mana pool,” Jerome continues, “and the power of those I’m trying to restrict or control.”

Matt’s eyes narrow. Reshma’s smile fades into careful attention. If Labonte was here, he’d surely be hovering off to the side like a proud gremlin.

Jerome looks at me for a moment, then at Matt.

Then, he speaks.

“You will not hide.”

The air in the room becomes heavy. It’s like gravity deepens for a heartbeat, as if the room itself inhales and holds its breath. A weight settles over my shoulders, not physical, but real enough that my knees almost want to bend in surprise at the sensation. My skin prickles. My vision sharpens around the edges, and my stomach flips as something takes hold.

It all snaps when the familiar pulsing sensation of the System greets my brain.

Ping!

A notification blinks into my HUD.

SYSTEM NOTIFICATION:

You are now subject to a new [Rule].

Rule: [You shall not hide.]

I swallow hard.

That is… horrifying. From the look on Reshma’s face, it looks like she received a similar message. Matt is harder to read.

Jerome turns his head slightly, as calm as ever.

“Try again,” he says. “Mr. Bruck. Mr. Sullivan.”

Matt’s expression doesn’t change, but there’s a flicker in his eyes now. Interest? Maybe even irritation?

Reshma folds her hands, watching like she’s witnessing something truly miraculous.

I mentally tap into Lefty and Righty again. The fists hover, eager as always.

Matt stands in front of us, shoulders loose, like he’s about to perform the same magic trick again.

I point.

“Get him,” I mutter.

Lefty and Righty rocket forward.

Matt moves—or tries to. He flickers, the air around him distorting, the beginning of that vanishing act. And it just… doesn’t take. It’s like the world refuses to let him slip away. Like the shadows he normally swims through have suddenly become concrete barriers.

Lefty slams into his chest.

Righty catches his shoulder.

The impact sends him sprawling across the reinforced floor, skidding hard enough to leave a faint scuff line. He rolls once, catches himself, and pushes up to his feet with a grunt.

He’s okay. But he’s not amused. I mentally call off my spectral fists, dispelling them. They disappear with twin puffs of mist.

Matt’s eyes lift to Jerome, and for the first time there’s something clearly expressed in his face: holy shit. He gets back to his feet, dusts himself off slowly, posture resetting, gaze still locked on Jerome.

Jerome makes a subtle gesture with his left hand.

Ping!

SYSTEM NOTIFICATION:

A [Rule] has been prematurely terminated.

[You shall not hide] is no longer in effect.

Matt opens his mouth, voice flat but carrying genuine weight. “That’s insanely powerful,” he says. “I might be able to resist the effect, but it would do some damage. And there wasn’t a shot of doing that fast enough when I didn’t know what to expect. You’re a monster.”

Jerome gives a polite nod, like he’s just been complimented on the quality of his e-mail signature block.

“I know,” he replies.

And somehow that’s the most terrifying thing Jerome has said.
