“So?” Karva mused. “What’s it going to be? Because if you say no, I’ll put out a similar notice. It’ll say something like… all students who extend their contracts with Junolyx University will be blacklisted from the Crimson Sickle affiliate network.”

Mayle laughed breathlessly. “They’ll be blacklisted from your network? Karva, these kids can’t get into any network without Junolyx, let alone yours. Parents know that, so they’ll go with the side that actually affects their children.”

Karva’s face hardened. “We have a lot more influence than you think.”

“No, you’re underestimating our influence. Yes, it’s less than yours and Belia’s combined, but once I post videos of Vestra’s crimes, you’ll be called a villain for choosing his side. It’s bad for business.”

Karva’s lips curved into another smile. “Oh, I don’t think that’s the case. See, I’m not getting involved upon Belia’s request—and my shareholders know that. I’m here to take what is yours while you’re weak and defenseless—and my shareholders find that prospect wonderful. So if you have half a mind, you’ll call back when you’re ready to negotiate.”

Karva cut the connection, leaving Mayle alone in the silence.

Mayle chuckled after a moment, face numbing from the drink as she closed her eyes. A news site had a new headline: “Breaking: Belia Alicibria Vows Legal Action Against Streaming Sites That Circulate Deepfakes of Son Vestra.”

Mayle leaned back. “It’s only been two weeks…” She touched the back of her ear. “Tell the PR team to release our counterthreat. Then start making examples—viciously and without exception. I want two expulsions by tomorrow.”

A new message went out a moment later.

“To the parents and alumni of Junolyx University,

In response to Belia Alicibria’s malicious attacks, we are releasing a video of her son, Vestra Alicibria—who killed an innocent student just last year—committing multiple felonies during his entrance test. The video includes a threat to maim Junolyx student Hayden Reskco, who Vestra coerced an official into fighting, for a woman’s entertainment.

Junolyx will not stand for such heinous behavior. We will also not be intimidated by threats, and will protect our students—including your children—no matter the cost.

We expect the same of our parents. If any parents publicly attack, or take business or legal action against Hayden Reskco or Junolyx University in response to Ms. Alicibria’s disturbing threat, we will enact the goodwill clause of our contract, and expel their child from Junolyx University. Due to the severity and time-sensitive nature of Ms. Alicibria’s threat, expulsions will begin tomorrow. We ask our parents to exercise patience and prudence when moving forward.

Sincerely, Mayle Haya, Junolyx University”

As Junolyx's patrons received their second warning, Hayden was concentrating on something far less petty. He had just moved into his new flat in the teacher’s quarters and had then escaped surveillance to meet with Kayline in a conference room.

“I can’t talk to you, Hayden,” Kayline said, voice torn and eyes matching.

“That’s fine—because I want to talk to your father.”

Her lips curved into a sardonic smile. “Well, he doesn’t want to talk to you.”

“I think he does.”

“I think… Hayden.” Kayline laughed in frustration. “I don’t think you understand. My father called this morning and said that he’d pull me from Junolyx University if I talked to you. It might not be that way forever, but right now? Hayden. He really doesn’t want to talk to you.”

Hayden leaned against a wall. “Yes, he does. Because I can solve his problems. And…” He leaned in further. “His family’s.”

Kayline blushed furiously. “What do you mean by that?”

“Your sister’s diseased core,” Hayden said. “I can heal it, and tell you what it is. But only if your father agrees to speak to me—and soon. I’m not going to wait even a day to act upon this. Either he chooses Belia, or he chooses his daughter’s future. Those are his options.”

Once Kayline finished her conversation with Hayden, she entered a conference room and called her father. Ritrix Iska materialized across from her, wearing his legal specs—and he was livid.

“If you’re calling about Hayden—no. I’ve been warned that if I so much as talk to him, I’ll lose a trillion in campaign donations. So no matter what you have to say—no.”

“Dad, listen.”

“No.”

“Dad. Listen. He just wants to talk.”

“I said, no. I won’t repeat—”

“Dad!” Kayline screamed.

He paused and gently yet equally sternly said, “What?” Kayline had never yelled at him, so he was clearly suppressing his frustration.

“Sairan Ikol just invited Hayden to the Blind Monks, Jeela Rain made him a VIP at the Nebula Auction House, and Mayle is betting the school on him. With that said—”

“Kay—listen. I’m a trade commissioner, a politician, and a father—I’m not a visionary, a businessman, or a researcher. Even if this boy lays golden eggs and washes his body with the philosopher’s stone, it won’t help our family. But he can hurt our family. Right now, Belia Alicibria has spent countless billions setting up transportation strikes—and all of my investors are panicking. And you know who’s responsible for that? That boy. Is it fair? No. But the bottom line is, he was the catalyst for a meltdown that’s sending our family tumbling down the Six Steps without mercy. The fact that I’m not screaming at you for your immature, destructive behavior this week is proof that I love you. But not now. You understand me?”

Kayline bit her lip. Her cheeks were pulled taut, and she shook in rage.

“Do you understand me?”

“No,” Kayline declared. “You’re a politician—politicians speak to friends and enemies. And right now, you’re tucking tail in a lose-lose situation instead of trying to figure out your options.”

“My options? The options are a multigalactic trade crisis, or risking everything on a mysterious kid.”

“No. It’s not. Hayden’s offering to fix your problem—not cushion the fall. Not ease the pressure. Fix it. He’s also offering to heal Lina’s core.”

Her father’s face sharpened. “First off, I don’t want to hear that anyone could casually fix the situation I’m in. It makes you sound delusional. Second, that’s clearly baiting. Haeta Core Syndrome is incurable.”

“Delusional? You clearly aren’t listening. Titan after titan are throwing away their careers for this man—and you’re still treating him like a kid? His spirit beast ate through a B-rank barrier. He created an illusion so groundbreaking that Sairan Ikol showed up to the school. Hell, he benched a thousand pounds twenty times without aura, spirits, or gates open. I think it’s more fair to say that Hayden is qualified to do whatever the hell he says he can. Or is at least worth talking to.”

Her father stared at her silently for about five seconds. “Mayle just sent me a letter of neutrality—that’s the only reason she’s not expelling you from Junolyx. But if you rock the boat, that will change. So unless you want Lina’s expulsion on your conscience, you’ll act accordingly.”

He cut the call, leaving Kayline in a state of disarray.

To numb the pain, she took the longest shower of her life. Then she got dressed in the silkiest pajama shorts she could find, wishing her diet allowed comfort eating. Twenty minutes later, a bell chimed outside her door. A drone was flying away when she opened it. There was a small box on her porch, and inside was a pint of choco-cherry ice cream. Kayline sat on her bed defiantly, stabbing at it with a spoon and shoveling it into her mouth like a chipmunk.

That didn’t help—so she went on a run, feeling sick, promising never to comfort eat again. Then, she declared she’d never get dressed in pajamas ahead of schedule when she saw a group of women giggling at her. She looked down, saw that she was wearing her shorts, and then ran home, face tomato red, praying that someone didn’t post a video on Juno Licks under the title: “Kayline on her way to Hayden’s booty call.”

She locked the door to her apartment, took another shower, pulled on fresh pajama shorts, and ate the rest of her ice cream, which was now soft and agreeable.

Only then did she say, “Dad’s going to kill me.”

Like many women, Kayline didn't like to disappoint her father. He had been lenient with her, but right now, his life was hanging by fraying stitches. She couldn’t risk his life, and yet… she couldn’t look away from her opportunity. So she called Hayden.

“My father won’t talk to you, but I’ll force him to if you can fix Lina’s core.”

Hayden stared at her on the other end of the line with a lifeless expression. “Can you? Force him.”

“I can,” Kayline said. “Not influence him. Not threaten him. Force him. My dad’s a politician—I know when I have leverage.”

“Okay. Meet me tomorrow at the Coral apartment complex, Room 326, with Lina, at 2 p.m. We’ll finish before the end of third period. Just make sure she’s wearing shorts that expose her thighs. We’re working with pressure points.”

Hayden cut the call, leaving her in a state of suspended animation.

What the hell am I even doing? she thought as she called Lina.

“If it’s about Hayden, kill yourself,” Lina said.

Kayline’s cheeks pulled taut. “It’s about… your core.”
