Chapter 62

Secrets Revealed

After months of field trials across the battlefronts, it was finally time to return, and that moment came near the end of February, now that the year was already 2095.

Mai said goodbye to Vargas’s squadron, which had ended up becoming the team she had spent the longest time with, and crossed the Atlantic to return to the Canary archipelago.

Coming back left her with a strange feeling. In just a few months, the island had changed. Activity pulsed through nearly every corner of it, and security had tightened as well, out of fear that a sudden portal might open somewhere without warning. Fortunately, nothing like that had happened in any of the planet’s Evanescent Lands, which was already a relief. Nor had there been any Fractus incursions in those regions, something that had drawn the interest of certain specialists in magic and spatial physics.

Diplomats, businessmen, construction-android engineers, and people wearing insignias of every possible color and flag now passed through Siren and Nevermore’s board of directors. What had once been a small but powerful organization had changed completely with the arrival of the Fractus. It was becoming something close to a global nerve center thanks to the coordination it was maintaining with hidden-world enclaves in preparation for what was to come. Leon was trying to convince Rein to act as director on the island, but Rein herself was firmly against the idea, insisting that her abilities were far too useful on the battlefield because of her control over storms. In fact, she and Noki were already being referred to globally as Team Tempest, moving from one part of the world to another aboard their own ship.

Whatever the case, Mai felt a measure of relief simply knowing the island was still there.

The warm, humid ocean air wrapped around her like an old, familiar coat, yet she couldn’t fully relax. Images of the battles and losses still flashed through her mind: the Fractus, the cold that cut through the skin, the heat that pressed against her lungs with every breath, the smell of ozone after each weapon discharge. Decisions made in seconds had determined the difference between life and death. And of course, there were also the faces of all those she had only known for brief stretches of time before they were gone.

The faces of those two young soldiers still appeared in her dreams. It didn’t matter that the reports said she had done nothing wrong, or that, given the danger of the experimental missions, casualties were something everyone had to accept. That did nothing to lift the burden from her shoulders—the belief that she should have thought things through better. Soaking her pillow with tears and walking around with a taciturn expression was not going to bring either of them back.

And she wasn’t the only one who had returned with a burden on her shoulders.

Many had died and had not been so fortunate. Mages, espers, feys—the losses had been the same for everyone. But in its own brutal way, it had also been a trial by fire.

The battlefield testing of prototypes, the use of enhancements, exoskeletons, and joint tactical maneuvers had all produced results. Now those weapons could move on to broader use in the war against the new enemy.

The conflicts of the century weighed on everyone’s mind. It had taken a disaster like the Fractus for the opposing sides to finally set aside their internal disputes and focus on the new problem now in their hands.

Mai didn’t like to think about it, but she had seen it with her own eyes: in the span of just a few months, everything seemed to be moving forward in ways that had been stalled for decades, even centuries. Political, religious, and economic grudges still lingered in countless places, but there was no turning back now.

At last, the day arrived.

That morning, Mai woke early and went for a run through the forest with Alexia. At one point they had to dodge a herd of angry stegosauruses, which felt like a fairly rude way to start the day, but after that she took a shower and had breakfast. The day seemed ordinary enough, and for most people, that was exactly how it began.

By the afternoon, the coordinated global announcement had drawn together everyone who happened to be anywhere nearby. Others preferred to watch it alone or in small groups. Mai had originally planned to watch it in the main building with the others, but in the end her nerves got the better of her, and she joined the crowd. She decided to sit down because her legs had started trembling and there was an itchy restlessness crawling under her skin.

That day, the base’s third dining hall was packed, with a little over two hundred people gathered around the holographic screens. The same was true of the other halls and main buildings across the base. Even the hangars and the ship and robot workshops had suspended all activity.

Noki stood with her arms crossed, eyes fixed on the hologram, one leg bouncing restlessly as if she might bolt at any second. Beside her, Rein remained rigid, her posture betraying her nerves. Mimi, though standing still, pursed her lips in thought. Near her stood a fey girl with green hair, a little messy, tied back in a ponytail.

As Mai looked at them, she raised an eyebrow. Who was that fey standing so close to Mimi? She knew the girl was some kind of teacher in the city on the island, but she had no idea why she was there, practically glued to Mimi’s side. Mai tried to remember. She was fairly sure her name was Zoe, and that she was also friends with Oxy. Mai had seen her strolling through the northern city on the island with Mimi a few times, but she had no idea what their relationship was.

She had big boobs, though.

Van and Oxy leaned against the wall on the other side of the room, arms crossed. Neither of them spoke, but Mai noticed Van’s fingers pressing into her own arm in a nearly imperceptible display of tension. Nikolai and Oswald, a werewolf and a gray-skinned giant, exchanged quiet whispers, yet even they, usually so composed, seemed uneasy.

Off to one side of the room, Fermi and Dirac remained silent, wearing their usual poker faces. Near them, two young humans, Carlos and Stefan, representatives from Nevermore Brazil, watched the projection with tense expressions. Mai knew they had fought in the past few months, but this battle would be different.

At the center of the room, the hologram floated like a ghost.

The General Assembly of the United Nations.

The event had been announced a week earlier, but the contents of the message had remained shrouded in mystery for the public. No matter how many times Mai turned it over in her head, the weight of what they were about to witness was overwhelming.

A live broadcast to every corner of the planet.

The day had arrived.

The hologram flickered slightly before stabilizing.

The image of the United Nations General Assembly appeared in absolute clarity at the center of the room, showing the vast conference hall in New York. Delegates from every country were present, some with faces so tense and severe it looked as though their skin might split if they changed expression, others wearing nervous looks as if they’d been marched there at knifepoint.

At the podium stood Nam Soomin, Secretary-General of the UN, radiating solemn authority. His bald head, marked by age and by the burden of guiding the world through one of its greatest crises, made for a strange sight. On one hand, he looked calm. On the other, whenever the cameras caught his left side, Mai could see a vein throbbing so violently that it looked as if it might burst at any moment.

He knew what he was about to say would change the course of history.

The assembly hall gradually quieted, and Nam Soomin let out a breath so subtle it was almost imperceptible.

Huh. The vein’s gone, Mai thought. Where did it go? Did they erase it with live AI makeup?

He cleared his throat, and his voice resonated through the room and through millions of devices across the world.

“Before I begin this announcement, I ask everyone present, and those watching, to observe a minute of silence for those who have lost their lives in recent months. Parents, children, siblings, friends. For all of them, for every person taken by a war they did not seek, let us take a moment of respect.”

On the island, the room fell completely silent. Mai watched as her companions bowed their heads. Some closed their eyes, others simply stared at the floor. Mai herself closed her eyes. Three months on the new front lines had taught them all how fragile life was. They had all witnessed death. Some had even lost close friends.

Less than a minute passed.

Nam Soomin lifted his gaze and continued.

“The world is undergoing an unprecedented change. Just months ago, our world faced a threat it could never have imagined. Creatures from another dimension, which we now call ‘fractus,’ have arrived in our world. We do not know where they come from, nor how extensive their presence is beyond our reality. But what we do know is that they have come to stay. The struggle against the fractus is, and will be, the fight of our lives. Yet this is not the first time the world has been shaken by the unknown.”

Mai narrowed her eyes.

Here it comes.

“Decades ago, the world witnessed inexplicable phenomena. Events that defied logic, science, and human understanding. And those events are still here to this day. We called them ‘Dark Events,’ and over time, humanity came to accept them as part of nature itself. Skies tearing open with lights that could never be fully explained. Shadows moving at the edges of reality. Entire cities waking up scarred by something they never saw coming. For most, these anomalies were little more than terrifying curiosities, but for a few, they were proof that our world was, and still is, far stranger than our wildest imaginings.”

Rein took a deep breath without taking her eyes off the hologram. Mai felt a pressure building in her chest. They had known for years that this moment would come someday, and yet it was still overwhelming.

“The world reached the brink of collapse. Between climate crises, wars over resources, and the fear of global annihilation, we stood one step away from losing everything. But we did not. And it was not due to human and aeon effort alone.”

A murmur rippled through the UN hall. The delegates already knew what was coming next, but for the rest of the world, this was the moment the lie would finally shatter.

“Since the late twentieth century, some countries in Asia and other parts of the world chose to act as refuges for those who could not be easily categorized. Not as a threat, but as an opportunity for coexistence. Those we call feys, those once dismissed as myths, creatures of folktales and superstition, have been among us from the very beginning.”

The impact was immediate.

Mai could imagine how the world was reacting in real time. Billions of people across the planet, watching the broadcast at home, at work, out in the streets, all with the same stunned expressions on their faces. She could easily picture a huge portion of them staring at their screens and wondering whether this was some kind of joke, while another part of the population would already be posting online that, if it was a joke, it was in spectacularly bad taste and the Secretary-General or the entire UN ought to be cancelled on the spot. Others would react with terror. And some, the ones who had always suspected the truth, would smile bitterly.

The conspiracy crowd was probably having the time of their lives right now, all yelling I knew it!

Nam Soomin did not pause.

“These are other peoples who have remained in the shadows and can no longer stay hidden. Not when the world faces a threat that does not distinguish between humans and those who merely appear different on the outside. It is time to leave fear and ignorance behind. It is time to unite.”

No one in the dining hall spoke. Mai was nervous too, but she glanced at Mimi out of the corner of her eye.

Mimi exhaled slowly, her expression hardening. Zoe ran a hand through her hair as if trying to process everything she had just heard. The two exchanged a look, and Zoe gave her a faint, knowing smile. Noki narrowed her eyes and said something to Zoe. Zoe smiled again, and Mimi stepped closer to Rein and Noki, wrapping them both in a hug.

Everyone was having their own reaction to it.

Mai felt a chill run down her spine.

The truth had finally been revealed.

And the world would never be the same again.

“Ten years ago, in 2085, the world changed forever with an unprecedented revelation. At that time, we announced the existence of espers: human beings with abilities that defy our conventional understanding of biology and physics. People with extrasensory, psychokinetic, and cognitive powers beyond any known limits.”

In the room where Mai and the others watched, no one spoke. The memory of that moment was still fresh, even if for some it had already become established history. When espers were revealed, the world had split between awe, disbelief, and fear. Chaos had erupted in many parts of the globe. Religious movements had declared them miracles or heresies, governments had tried to conceal their own experiments, and some societies had simply accepted them as the next step in human evolution.

At the UN, Nam Soomin allowed the revelation a moment to settle over the audience before continuing.

“At that time, we assured the world that espers were not a threat, but an inherent part of our species. Part of our world. That they were not an anomaly, but a natural variation of humanity. And although the process was difficult, and though there were conflicts and resistance, over time the truth was accepted.”

Mai rested her elbow on her knee, her chin against her hand.

Nam Soomin straightened and let out a slow breath.

“Today, at this critical moment in our history, we find ourselves once again at a crossroads. We face an existential threat that has humanity on the ropes. And it is precisely this crisis that compels us to share another great truth. A secret that has remained hidden in the shadows for ages.”

“In centuries past, across every civilization and in every corner of the world, there were stories, myths, and legends about beings different from humans. Some called them gods. Others, spirits. Some saw them as monsters, demons, or angels. Every culture gave them different names, and every society interpreted them in its own way, shaping them through the lens of belief, politics, and religion.”

The hologram shifted again, displaying cave paintings, medieval manuscripts, and ancient texts referencing beings of inexplicable power. From the kami of Shinto to the Tuatha Dé Danann of Celtic mythology, from the djinn of the Middle East to the huldra of Scandinavia.

Mai felt her heart pounding. This was moving a little too fast, honestly, and she couldn’t help wondering what was happening in the rest of the world right now. Plenty of people were probably just waking up, seeing this, and asking themselves whether they were still trapped inside some bizarre dream, and really, she couldn’t blame them. Even for Mai, watching it unfold right there in front of her own eyes felt unreal. She had never truly stopped to think that one day she would actually live to see this moment.

Nam Soomin took a deep breath and spoke.

“These beings were never just myths.”

The silence in the room was absolute. No one moved. No one spoke. In the holographic projection, the United Nations General Assembly had become a stage of palpable tension.

Then the great doors of the hall opened, and a group of people entered.

Mimi let out a sharp breath and wrapped Noki and Rein in a protective embrace. Without saying a word, Zoe stepped closer and hugged Mimi from behind. Mai noticed the slight tremor in Noki’s shoulders, but the girl never took her eyes off the hologram.

The group that entered walked with steady steps, their black suits immaculate beneath the lights of the hall. They wore no insignia, but they didn’t need any. Mai felt her breathing slow as she recognized one of the faces among them.

Leon.

Her old friend, the one who liked to call her “Mothy,” had not changed at all. His hair, now cut short, was perfectly groomed, his posture upright, his expression unreadable. Beside him walked a small woman with elegant features and sharp eyes, moving with the same quiet confidence.

Mari.

Mai had only met her a couple of times. As far as she knew, Mari was something like the president of a community in the Pyrenees.

But it was the rest of the group that truly drew everyone’s attention.

A man with a round face and pale skin walked with perfect calm, almost like a nobleman surveying the room.

Franz Rackhenhell.

The vampire. One of the most influential figures in the secret negotiations between feys and humans. Mai let out a quiet breath and felt a knot tighten in her throat as she remembered Miho. Seeing Franz there brought back memories of that afternoon in Nakano Park. It had been nearly a hundred years since then.

Next to him, a woman with blonde hair and a serene expression scanned the hall, her eyes carrying the look of someone who had already anticipated every possible reaction.

Emmeline, the Claymore of Scotland.

Mai had heard rumors about her, but had never seen her in person.

Finally, a man with long black hair walked with the faintest smile on his lips, as though he found a certain pleasure in the weight of the moment.

Fu-Xiang.

Mai knew him personally, and his reputation more than preceded him. Not a fey, merely an alchemist by classification, but respected by many and feared by others, his presence on that stage meant this was more than a simple revelation. Mai almost smiled at the sight of him and found herself wondering whether Jinat might be somewhere nearby too.

“So this is it,” Nikolai murmured beside her with a half-smile. “They’re not holding back on the theatrics.”

Oswald nodded slowly. “They can’t. If they’re going to reveal the truth, it has to be done in a way the world can’t ignore.”

Van narrowed her eyes. “The question is… how are they going to take it?”

No one answered. Everyone knew that was the great unknown.

In the Assembly, Nam Soomin extended a hand toward the newly arrived group and spoke again, his voice clear and resonant throughout the hall.

“These individuals you see before you are neither actors nor representatives of a fictional story. They are not figures taken from ancient mythologies or fairy tales. They are the living proof of what I am revealing to you at this very moment.”

The silence grew even heavier.

Mai could feel the tension in the air, the expectation charged with possibility.

Nam Soomin’s voice echoed through the Assembly with a weight that seemed capable of shaking the building itself. The holographic image projected him with perfect clarity, capturing every nuance of his expression, every carefully measured pause.

“People of Earth,” he began, his tone firm and solemn, “it is time for you to meet two other societies that have lived hidden among us, avoiding intervention in our affairs for centuries.”

Rein and Noki, still held in Mimi’s embrace, stared at the hologram without blinking.

Nam Soomin continued, his gaze sweeping across the crowd before him.

“We cast them long ago into the realm of mythology and tradition. We believed they were only stories, tales from ancient times… but sometimes, legends have a far more real origin than we imagine.”

The cameras focused on the group standing firm at the center of the room. Leon and Mari showed no emotion on their faces. Franz and Emmeline remained perfectly composed, and Fu-Xiang still wore that faint smile, as if he found the entire situation almost entertaining.

Finally, Nam Soomin spoke the words that would make history.

“On this day, I present to you… the feys and the magical society.”

It was as if the entire world held its breath.

In the room on the island, the tension shattered instantly.

“He said it…” Oxy murmured, eyes wide.

Van let out a slow breath, as though she had been waiting for this moment for a very long time. Nikolai smiled in disbelief.

“Well, it’s done.”

Oswald crossed his arms thoughtfully. “There’s no turning back.”

The first visible crack in the room came from somewhere no one could quite identify. Someone started crying. A second later, two friends threw their arms around each other. Nervous laughter broke out in one corner while, elsewhere, someone simply sank into a chair as if their legs had stopped working.

Then the whole room seemed to come apart at once.

Alexia passed by Mai, hugged her from the side, and said something with a bright smile, but Mai barely heard it. A sharp ringing had started in her ears, drowning out the room beneath a layer of static. Rein and Noki were already clinging to Mimi, and a second later Mimi pulled the green-haired fey into the embrace as well. On the other side of the dining hall, Van had gone straight for one of the machines and was pulling out cans of beer like she had personally won the war. Oxy caught one and downed it in one go. Nikolai did the same, raising his can toward the hologram where Nam Soomin’s speech was still continuing and Fu-Xian and Mari were now talking too. A few soldiers assigned to training simply sat there smiling faintly, glancing around as if they still hadn’t fully processed what they had just witnessed.

Mai said nothing. She only stared at the projection and at the faces around her, at the strange mixture of disbelief, relief, and joy reflected in all of them.

Then Rein walked over and hugged her.“Come on, Mothy! Smile!”

Noki joined in a second later, wrapping her arms around her too. Oxy followed right after, laughing as she nearly crushed Mai in the process with a tight hug. Finally Mimi stepped in front of her and held out a hand.

Mai didn’t know at what point it happened.

One moment she was standing there, and the next tears were already spilling down her face, dripping onto her shirt.

Eh?

Why am I crying?

She stared at the tears as if they belonged to someone else.

This is weird...

Mimi looked at her, smiled softly, and pulled her into a hug, one hand gently rubbing her back.

“It was hard, wasn’t it? You did well.”

That was all it took.

Mai broke down like a child.

She hadn’t cried like that in a very long time. In fact, she hadn’t thought she was going to cry at all. She had been nervous, yes—everyone had—but she never imagined that this was what would come out of her when it was over.

“Eh?! This is weird!” Noki said, blinking in surprise. “I don’t remember ever seeing you cry.”

Mai barely heard her. For the next several minutes, all she knew was warmth: arms around her shoulders, voices overlapping, laughter, someone pressing a drink into somebody else’s hand, and the dizzying realization that the secret they had carried for so long was no longer theirs alone.

Outside, the world would not take long to react. Fear, denial, anger… but also hope, curiosity, and the possibility of a different future. Somewhere out there, perhaps others were reacting just like her—staring in disbelief at first, only to find themselves crying before they even understood why.

The secret had been revealed.

And nothing would ever be the same again.

And while the world was still struggling to recover from its astonishment, Mai opened her inbox the following morning.

Among the many messages waiting for her, one in particular tightened something in her chest the moment she saw the sender’s name: General Briggs.

Inside were the contact details for Ethan Mathesson’s father and for Ivonne Williams’s grandparents.

He had kept his promise.

Mai let out a slow breath.

She spent the rest of the day training, but her thoughts kept drifting back to the same place. That night, she finally sat down and wrote the emails.

She did not want to write them like reports. The army had already sent those, along with the bodies. If she was going to do this, she would do it the only way she knew how.

She had not known Ethan and Ivonne for years. They had not been comrades forged together over months or campaigns. She had only known them for a handful of days.

But those days had been their last.

So she wrote about the little things she remembered. A few awkward conversations. A few stupid comments. Small details that would have meant nothing to anyone else and yet, to Mai, felt far more honest than a list of formal condolences. She left out the uglier details of how they had died, but she wrote enough for their families to know the truth that mattered: that Ethan and Ivonne had died doing their duty, and that they had not been alone when it happened.

When she reread the messages, she felt a little foolish.

But it was the truth.

Foolish or not, it was still the truth.

So she sent them.

She did not expect a reply.

She received one a few days later.

It was brief, but it was enough.

After reading it, Mai made herself a quiet promise.

If she ever passed through the Kingdom of the United States, she would visit them.
