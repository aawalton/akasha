AMBEROSE – ROYAL CASTLE

It had been one of those days devoid of anything worthy of note, and yet had kept stretching toward infinity. King Isadore forced himself to see the last petitioners off with a minimum of decorum, before slumping in his throne as soon as the door closed. He groaned out the overflow of boredom he'd accumulated, much to his chamberlain's amusement, who smirked as she looked at him from the corner of her eye.

The king scowled at her. “I have no idea how you manage to stay upright like that all day and not lock up.”

The wolfkin woman, back straight as when the day started, smugly returned her eyes toward the far end of the room. “Rigorous physical training, my Liege. And also my husband's massages with his exquisite hands.”

Isadore snorted. “You keep saying that; I'm starting to believe it. Would you consider an offer to make him the royal masseur?” He asked, lifting a brow.

“Unfortunately, sire, my husband's hands belong to me, and I'll declare open rebellion if you try anything.” She cautioned with a weightless growl.

The kind flopped a hand. “Don't get your armoured undies in a knot, Agnes. I'm just jealous,” he said before stretching, letting out a tired moan and unfolding himself upright as tense muscles were forcefully limbered back into place.

“Ahhh, we seriously need to limit the ruling hours. I can't keep holding entire days like that. Anyway, what's next?”

The wolfkin turned to face her liege. “Not much, your majesty. A meeting with General Duvoyer, then supper with your family, followed by a short visit by Lord Shuster and his immediate family- who you have been rescheduling for the past full season,” She reminded him as he opened his mouth to protest.

Her ears waggled once. “Really, Sire, they are kind and loyal people, and they have been looking forward to informing you of what they've gathered during their tour of the Parnaran frontier.”

Isadore pouted. “But they are so boring! Urgh, fine. The king of Amberfall will listen to their travel stories,” he begrudged before straightening up. “Agnes, the good general, it's about the final report on the Sangliore fortress, isn't it?”

“I believe so, sire.”

The king perked up. “Ah! Then that should be interesting, at least. So many rumours floating around. If you were to believe some of them, the Founding Gods returned to the living.”

Agnes lifted a brow but remained silent, simply turning to follow after the King as they left the court of rule.

She had heard quite a few rumours herself, but she'd also talked with some of the officers who had been there, so she had a pretty good idea of what the general was going to report, and she couldn't wait to see Isadore's reaction.

Not that she'd show any signs of it, of course. She had the best poker face. Everyone in the castle knew that, which, incidentally, was probably why she didn't get invited to card game nights anymore.

Isadore had sat down for the meeting in high spirits, but it had gradually seeped away as General Duvoyer ran his King through the events at Sangliore.

Now, he was trying to wrap his mind around the nonsensical tale he'd just heard.

“General, to summarize, Lord and Lady Allwarden walked up to the fully-garrisoned and alert fortress and... took it over with two deaths on the enemy side, themselves suffering no injury of any kind.”

Duvoyer nodded grimly. “They flew, rather than walked, and their guardian golem helped create a diversion, but yes, Sire; they took over the fortress without any apparent difficulty.”

The king leaned back in his chair, steepling his fingers over his chest. “That is... concerning.”

Duvoyer remained silent as his king mulled the ramifications over. He himself had the report triple-verified, suspecting that it had been exaggerated, only to find out that everyone along the chain had felt and done the same.

Isadore looked back up at his General. “Have you had an official report prepared?”

“Yes, Sire,” the man confirmed, pushing out a bound file. “The public report will state that the Allwardens were instrumental in launching a diversionary attack which hobbled the fortress' defences, allowing infiltration units armed with take-down tools to assault and subdue the defending forces.”

The king dipped his head. “Good. The Allwardens can receive the honour they deserve without scaring the gentry senseless that two of their numbers are essentially an army upon themselves. Now, what to do about that...”

Duvoyer blinked. “My Liege? Are you insinuating that...”

“What? No! Absolutely no hostile actions are to be taken against those two or their household! Lady Niala is as saintly as they come. We only need to remain in their good graces, and we will have two powerful defenders to call upon. Even more so, with her sister now married to the Wardenfel heir, she has all the more reason to help defend this Kingdom.”

Isadore got up and walked over to the lone window overlooking the evening cityscape of Amberose and sighed. “I do have to ask, Simon; would we be able to stop them?”

The general stared at his hands for a second before looking up. “If they chose to offer battle in the traditional sense, and we were given enough time to assemble an appropriate force, I do believe so. The cost would be terrible, but I'm confident we could at the very least grind them down with numbers.”

“However...?”

Simon Duvoyer nodded. “However, I don't see them doing something like that. They are two individuals, not an army, and from all accounts, extremely mobile. If they ever decide the Royal Family must be removed, they would fly here, undetected, and assault the castle directly. While Castle Amberfall has many defensive capabilities, I doubt they'd be enough to repel a decapitation strike.”

The king looked back out at the city. “Hmm. Then my initial conclusion was the right one; stay on friendly terms. We should also listen to any grievances they might have, and attempt to address them.”

He heard the general rummage in his satchel and retrieve another report, shuffling through a few pages before speaking. “I've had my aides prepare a report for you in that regard, since I thought it likely this would be your preferred response. The Allwardens have heavily invested in social-focused enterprises; schools, orphanages, and outreach programs of all sorts. Those have been funded in large part by the exploitation fees from the Living Vault mine.”

The man stood up and brought the report over to his king. He pointed at a section within it as he handed it over. “They were, from what we could tell, upset about the state of the city-run orphanage in Bellharbour, and have begun investigating other public establishments of the sort in neighbouring cities.”

Isadore narrowed his eyes at the report. “Involved in the money laundering ring... oh, saint's fart. I do hope they'll understand this was due to isolated bad actors, and that not all state-run orphanages are similar.”

As he spoke, he noticed the General fidget. “What is it?”

“Hrrm, well, while it is true that not all orphanages were impacted by the actions of the laundering ring, they were easy targets for such an operation...”

“How many?” His king demanded.

The general winced. “Seven out of ten, overall.”

Isadore groaned. “That is not good.”

“No, my liege.”

The king rolled up the report and tapped it in his hand a few times. “General Duvoyer, your new assignment, pre-empting all other prior commitments; form a task force to review all publicly-funded orphanages in the realm. Rate them against the Royal Amberose Orphanage, that my dear wife patrons, and have them be brought up to similar standards; let's say, 80 percent of the way there. Can't expect the cities to have the same kind of budget, but that should be plenty good already.”

Isadore looked at the general. “Once the task force is up, and you've appointed someone suitable to oversee it, preferably someone from the public sector, you will then go and audit the Library.”

Duvoyer froze. “The... that library, sire?”

His king's eyes bore into him. “Yes, that library. I will make certain you have the appropriate authorization. I want you, as someone outside of the organization, to give me an honest, candid report on the treatment of the weavers within.”

“I- yes, my Liege. Is there anything else?”

Isadore shook his head. “No, that is all. You are dismissed, and thank you for your good work, general.”

The general puffed out his chest, bowed, and departed. As he stepped out, Agnes, Isador's chamberlain, took his place in the room. Closing the door, she turned and smirked at her king. “How bad is it?”

Isadore scoffed. “Don't act like you don't know most of it already. But, not that bad; yes, they are powerful individuals, but from all accounts, neither is overly ambitious. As long as the kingdom develops in ways which align with their ideals, I don't foresee any issues. In fact, as long as Lady Angeline Wardenfel supports this kingdom, we should have easy access to their power.”

The wolfkin waggled her ears in amusement. “Yes... And Lady Angeline certainly isn't that ambitious now, is she?”

The king stiffened. “Please don't make me think of that woman too much. I'm going to have nightmares.”

Agnes laughed out loud, and Isadore's glare made her laugh even harder.

She hadn't had to deal with Lady Angeline directly, but, being the King's secret spy master, she had been sufficiently informed about her.

Yes, the lady was scary, but she was also a dutiful daughter and sister. The King was also either forgetting, or simply unaware, of the father's past and what he was capable of.

Really, the whole All Brew family were their own kind of monsters. It was a balancing act, then; keep each one happy and safe, and the other two would do everything they could to keep it that way.

The trick, then, was to make certain that each one of them knew they had the Kingdom's support and desire for cooperation. She didn't have any doubts that her King would aim himself along those lines; he was a good king, the kind who cared more about the overall health of his lands than for any one individual.

In this case, however, these few individuals definitely needed to be given their fair share of attention.

And she'd make certain they knew.

DAYLIGHT CITY

Maldrin reviewed his report on the performance of the trade skill classes one last time before closing the booklet and bringing it to Overseer Rupert's desk.

The elder bunnykin looked away from his ledger and up at Maldrin. “Finished already?”

The daylighter nodded. “Yes, sir. Report compiled and looked over,” he replied in broken Amberfallian.

Rupert smiled. “Your diction is getting better, Maldrin.”

The man beamed. “Thank you, sir. Have been practising much with my family. Little Emily is giving her grandfather private lessons. She learns much easier than us old people.”

The overseer chuckled. “Ah, yes. The brains of kids are little sponges. Is she still learning Lumiran?”

“Of course, sir! Lumiran is heritage! The Lady of the Light told us so! Our heritage will be preserved!” Maldrin declared.

“You still insist on calling my niece the Lady of the Light, huh?” Rupert remarked with a glint. “You know she hates being put on a pedestal, right?”

The daylighter nodded with fire in his eyes. “All Daylighters know. She wants us the best, not for praise, but because she is kind and holy. That is why she is worthy of it.”

“Yes, she certainly is worthy of some praise. Either way, I do believe your shift has ended a few minutes ago. You are free to go, Maldrin. Enjoy your weekend. I shall see you back in the office on Monday.”

Released of his duties as the overseer returned his attention to the documents on his desk, the daylighter bowed, walked back to his desk, tidied it up, retrieved his jacket and left the town hall.

He walked out in the late afternoon sun, the main road still bustling with activity as workers hurried to finish last-minute deliveries and late-closing establishments got ready for the Friday evening rush.

Just a year ago, the concept of a restaurant or alehouse was as foreign as the sun, and the mere idea of staying out past daytime was akin to seeking death.

But then, they had been liberated from a short, dark life and thrown out into the glorious sun by the Lady of the Light. She had told them to explore this new life, to experiment, to not be afraid to enjoy long, productive lives and to dream of even better times.

The Daylighters, his people, had embraced everything she had said, and were ravenous for more. The Lady would not find her subjects ungrateful or wanting.

Which is why, even though overseer Rupert had told him he was off the clock, Maldrin didn't feel like his work was done. He would take a few bells to walk around, talk to people, feel the pulse of the town.

He'd found, and addressed, quite a few minor issues like that. Some, he had been confident to fix himself. Others, he had brought up to the Council of the Light to discuss. A few had made their way up to Rupert.

Speaking of the Council, he was still amused at the overseer's face when he'd learned that the habitation blocks had spontaneously elected representatives and formed an unofficial assembly to manage social issues.

Rupert had fought the notion for a bit, claiming that such things had to be official and supported by the Town Hall.

Maldrin's argument had been to simply make it official, then. Problem solved. Besides, she had told his people to work together, to talk and discuss issues and to not let things fester unsaid.

Whether Rupert liked it or not, a Council of the Light had been ordained by the Lady, so it was going to happen. So, it was official now.

He smiled at a gaggle of children running past, screaming and screeching with the kind of energy that only innocent lives could. Before, back in the pit-cursed tunnels of the vault, such primordial expression of joy had been muted compared to these little gremlins.

And to think that they would grow up without having to worry about the approaching hunger that would force them to seek death.

Truly, his was a people set free.

The Lady of the Light and her warrior were due decades of their individual lives; the years they would not have had otherwise.

All the adults knew, and they had all pledged to it in an initial, secret assembly; the Daylighters would all devote twenty of their years to the betterment of the Lady's domain. Selfless work, donations, cooperation; anything that followed her ideals, that made life better, just the way she wanted them to have.

After those twenty years, individuals would not be expected to give back to society as much as they had so far. Their burden would be taken over by the next generation.

Maldrin smiled. The Council of Light had the right idea, but as he learned more about the world from Rupert, he also discovered other means to capitalize on this pledge.

Dabbling in economics, which was one of the overseer's obvious interests, he had learned about things such as banking, funds, loans, interest, and investments.

It was a burgeoning idea, still in its infancy, but he could see the possibility; twenty years of a productive life, funnelled into a social fund that would be used to build infrastructure and support fledgling industries, which would all pour money back into the fund.

The future of the Daylighters was bright, and he would make sure it remained that way, long past his eventual death, in many, many years from now.

Years he would dedicate to the Lady of the Light and her wishes.
