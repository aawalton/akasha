"Your spouse?" Sam repeated after Ashton. "But didn't you say before that–"

"Yes, I did. I said that it was complicated, because it was. Because it is. It's..." he let out a long sigh, and slowly put his mask back on. Even without being able to see colour, Ashton could tell that Clementine had gone pale. "I will explain things more on the way. So for now, let's get moving."

Sam nodded, placing a hand on Clementine's back as they continued to walk. Ashton was the one mostly taking care of the monsters in the area. Whenever they were wearing or carrying something useful, he would stop and collect it, and then cut out their magic stones as well as any other useful material from their bodies.

He was running down a permanent checklist in his head, trying to make sure that he could get all the materials he needed to call on more summons. In particular, there were a few more elementals that he wanted to call on, some more constructs he wanted to imbue, and spirits that were going to come in useful especially when the next quest officially started.

There were so many things to prepare beforehand. And so many things to figure out, and talk about. Though, maybe in this moment, Ashton was just using that as an excuse.

He wanted to tell his friends about himself, but the more they realised that there was a lifetime of things they didn't know about him, the less they may want to be around him.

Their relationship was going to change forever.

Though, it already had. Ashton could tell how on edge his friends were around him, as if they were trying to figure out what was going on with him. But there was a moment when Ashton couldn't avoid talking about anymore. He had already promised them that he would, and when he could finally hear the mechanical rattling of the ancient constructs, together with the sound of stone grinding against stone, there was no excuse he could make anymore.

"Are there here?" Ashton asked, facing forward. Sam slowly nodded.

"Yeah, this is about as far as we can go without them attacking us, I guess. But they do take out every other type of monster as well, so they don't approach, either," Sam explained. "There are quite a few of them, too... Are you sure you want to see them?"

Ashton nodded. "Yeah, I am. But you two can stay here."

He moved forward, just a few simple steps for now, and he could feel his heartbeat in his throat.

His friends stayed behind, just watching. They had been patient with him. Ashton said that he would explain everything, and then was mostly quiet on the way here. There were a lot of things that he had to mentally sort through before being able to tell them the story. They had spent years together; there was so much to say, so much to talk about.

And Ashton had to decide what to tell his friends, and what to protect for himself. Decide what memories were going to belong to just him alone.

But for now, there was something he had to see. It was the only thing on his mind. He had only seen bits and pieces, small objects that Oka had brought along with them as they fell into Lumia, or their sketches of the constructs.

As Ashton walked closer, he could hear the heavy, rhythmic footsteps of a construct rushing toward him.

It was a humanoid figure. It was tall, around four metres. Its legs had two knees on each one, and it moved without its torso shifting up and down even slightly. The stone that was the main material of its body was carved into patterns that supposedly signified the exact purpose of the construct. Oka had spoken a lot about them, though it was hard to figure out, since the patterns were often damaged or the constructs were too overgrown to spot it properly.

This one wasn't in such a bad state, luckily. From its overall figure, Ashton could already tell that it was a combat unit, but from the patterns, he could tell that specifically, it was made for protection.

Ashton spun his staff around and built up momentum. He had the wisp split into two, and had each one absorb part of the asphalt. Each half of the wisp was floating right on one end of Ashton's staff, just an inch away from the metal.

As the construct swung its large stone fist at him, Ashton smashed it out of the way using one of the wisps, before immediately pushing the staff into its chest.

As the first wisp hit the construct, the second one slammed against the other end of the staff, basically hammering it into the stone being once again.

It stumbled backward slightly. That was the first attempt. Just now, Ashton wasn't using internal energy to attack. But in the end, internal energy was just another form of mana, so the wisp should be able to accept and use it easily enough. It was able to take in any other sort of magic without issues.

Ashton let his energy flow through his arm into his staff, and then into the two wisps. He felt a closer connection to the wisp, as if they were an extension of his body. Like they were another set of hands.

This time, as he spun his staff around, hitting the construct with the wisps, he used his internal energy to let out a blast. Every impact, although it was already loud and attracting more constructs before, was almost ear-shattering, as if the wisps were exploding slightly with each impact.

The ancient construct tried to fight back, but its body was slowly being torn down. It was pretty unlucky; Ashton wasn't a jargo-maras—a past seeker. He didn't care about keeping the historical integrity of this thing intact.

Right now, as it and the other constructs were in his view, all that he could feel was anger that they were here in the first place. That the council had torn apart Karros.

With each strike, and each impact, the construct in front of him fell apart more, until it wasn't able to function anymore.

But Ashton couldn't relax. More were coming at him. There were a few more that seemed to have a similar purpose to this one, but there was one with a more powerful weapon, even if it was effectively just a stone pillar. None of them had complete control over their functions, or else this would be a lot harder.

Usually, these constructs should have arcane weapons. Shields, axes, and blades made of compressed magic. There were a few sparks of that magic here and there, but nothing that actually took shape.

Beside those basic combat units, there were smaller, somewhat spider-like constructs with a round body and four legs. They were the size of a small dog, or maybe a cat. From what Ashton knew, they weren't meant to engage in combat directly. They tried to climb up onto Ashton and tie him down, but Ashton struck them away.

They broke pretty damn easily.

By the time all the constructs around Ashton had broken down and were nothing but rubble that couldn't keep itself together anymore, Ashton was fuming.

He had hoped that this would help his anger subside, but it was just the opposite.

Ashton clicked his tongue and squatted down. He picked up some pieces of stone. This was an extremely dense material, and now that it wasn't manipulated by the construct anymore, the gnome could control it. Weirdly enough, despite being so dense and heavy, it was easier to manipulate it, both overall and also in more subtle ways like changing its shape. Maybe that was why this specific type of stone was used for the constructs.

"Backpacker, pick up a bunch of the stones, please," Ashton said as he started to dig through the piles of stone to grab the arcane hearts, the main power source of the constructs.

Most of them were damaged, but that was fine. They could still be useful. There were also a number of other pieces spread throughout the body that were useful. They were orbs of particularly strong stone, different to the one that the rest of the construct was made of, at the centre of each major piece.

Ashton wasn't quite sure how exactly these constructs knew what they were supposed to do, but he did figure out that these orbs were how they were being controlled, and figured maybe they could be useful.

And in the end, even when he grabbed everything that seemed useful, Ashton just sat there for a while, squatting in front of the rubble. He didn't even notice that Sam and Clementine had approached him.

"Are you okay?" Sam asked, and Ashton shook his head.

"No," he stood up and sighed, running his hand through his hair. "The very fact that they're here makes me sick to my stomach. Oka spent almost their entire life studying these things. Their history, why they protected the ruins they gathered around, and what they were trying to accomplish by collecting things from the surrounding area."

Sam thought for a moment. "That truth-seeker thing, right?"

"Past seeker; jargo-maras. But yes. They would fight these things as they tried to get deeper into the ruins. Each ruin seemed to have a different purpose, but they all brought a lot of innovation with them for the ganta—the people of Karros. Some were hospitals, others were workshops. Or schools, or museums. There were other ruins, too, but they weren't protected and repaired by the constructs, so they fell victim to time a lot faster," Ashton explained, picking up one of the stones, just feeling the weight in his hand.

"Were they also summoned?" Clementine asked. That was the first thing she had said in a while.

Ashton shook his head. "No. Somehow, they fell into Lumia on their own. They were exploring a ruin, activated some ancient magic, and then found themselves there. They were trying to find a way back for a while, and then around a year into their time in Lumia, we met." Ashton smiled, placing a hand onto his throat. "It was a pretty exciting first meeting."

Ashton slowly opened his eyes. He didn't technically need to sleep, but he enjoyed it anyway. Made existing easier. And now that he was left to be on his own, away from the Summoner, he took full advantage of that tiny bit of 'freedom' he was given.

But this time, when he woke up from his nap, he had the blade of a knife pressed against his throat, as a massive person with pale blue skin stared right at him.
