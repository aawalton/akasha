Tanat loved the Black Tower—or, as it had come to be known, the Lightning Tower and Void Garden.

She sat on the throne, shaping the maze into a dizzyingly complex array of shadow-casting earthwork ramparts and deep trenches filled with an almost black darkness. Brick paths offered only the narrowest of causeways to walk, with Ellen’s magic a constant threat. The entire Void Garden was, in effect, designed to bolster her aura and make Tides of Darkness an incredibly lethal weapon, and the sheer number of shadows the maze cast was proof it was working.

The shadowy earthworks were lined with lightning rods, and those hundreds of metal spikes channeled the constant barrage of electricity through conduits and massive copper wires. Every one of them led directly to the Lightning Tower.

And, high above the tower, the storm raged. The tower’s portal metal frame was almost constantly electrocuted. Sparks arced back and forth mere feet from where Tanat worked, their green-black and blue-white colors leaving afterimages even on her closed eyes. Wind buffeted her thin frame, and rain poured in through the open window.

And Tanat loved every second of it.

This was true power—the power to shape a world, and the power to create a storm like the one overhead. She’d never seen Retan claim even half this power. His world had been built the hard way, with shovels, hammers, and saws. With mortar and stone. But what Tanat had done in Kade Noelstra’s portal world was a thing of beauty.

Then there were the cannons. They were crude, but they’d be effective. Hopefully, surprisingly so. Kade’s sister had delivered almost four dozen of them so far.

They were hideous. Five feet long, a foot wide, with a barrel Tanat could just about have fit her head inside. Their loading structure was simple—a hinge opened it, and a ramp rolled the next shell inside. She had no idea how they’d automate that without someone controlling the Lightning Tower, but that wasn’t entirely her problem. At least not right now.

Right now, her problem was installing the hideous, portal metal weapons into the side of the Lightning Tower, ready to fire in defense of the portal world she’d been gifted.

Yes, gifted.

Officially, it was Kade’s world. Tanat knew that. And she knew that Kade belonged to Ellen Traynor. She would never try to interfere with a core-bonded pair, especially one as far along as the two of them obviously were. Their connection was one of the strongest she’d seen—they’d even developed their builds with each other in mind.

But oh, how she’d have sprung on Kade Noelstra if he hadn’t been claimed!

Still, the world was a good consolation prize. She would gladly serve as his castellan, his seneschal, whatever role she had that allowed her to shape and change this world to her whims. It was almost as good as having a core-bond partner. Maybe, in some ways, it was even better.

For one thing, she was beholden to no one save Kade Noelstra, and he didn’t seem to care what she did as long as it benefited his fight against the God of Thunder. And for another, the world didn’t talk back, save to allow her to—

A portal opened. A glorious, golden portal wreathed in blue-white lightning.

Tanat took stock of the defenses. Inadequate without more S-Rankers to defend them. Maybe inadequate anyway.

Then she drew her sword and started sprinting toward the portal to Kade’s world. He had to be warned. He and his allies had to be rallied.

The God of Thunder had arrived.

Tanat didn’t make it to the portal to Earth.

As she rounded the corner, a being of solid lightning appeared in front of her. One moment, he was a massive dragon the size of a thunderhead, barreling down on the Lightning Tower through the storm clouds as lightning tethered him to them and then broke away. The next, the God of Thunder stood, encased in armor that swirled like a maelstrom and with a body forged from electricity itself. His spear rested in a relaxed stance.

Tanat wasn’t fooled. The God of Thunder was well within striking distance.

“You must be one of the spares that kid recruited. He’s making a mistake,” the Paragon of the Stormsteel Path rumbled.

“Is he?” Tanat managed to say. The SS-Plus-Ranked monster’s aura hadn’t even pushed out yet, and it was still all she could do to stand upright.

“Yes. He’s relying on others to further his own strength.”

“Is that a mistake?”

“You’re stalling, girl. Prepare yourself.”

The spear lunged out, slicing toward Tanat’s face.

Thunderwarp.

Tanat wasn’t the lightning swordsman Kade was, but she had a few tricks. As the spear-tip cut into her cheek inches below her jet-black eyes, she seemed to explode, only to rematerialize a few feet to the left. She was already counterattacking when the spear’s butt flashed across and slammed into her chest.

Her sternum gave with a crunch.

Pain hit her like an avalanche. Like a thunderstrike. Bone was in her lungs. It might be in her heart. She might be dying.

Stamina poured through Tanat’s body, dulling the sharp agony to a dull but insistent roar. She forced her perforated lungs to breathe in and out, all but screaming in agony, and kept fighting.

The God of Thunder was overwhelming, though. “I take it Kade Noelstra’s not home, then? A shame. I’d ask you to give him a message, but I think it’ll be clear what that message is when I’m done here.”

“What…what are you talking…about?” Tanat’s sword flashed. Its tip had only been inches from the lightning monster’s face, but somehow, the spear intercepted it all the same. It was like trying to stab a mountain.

“Girl, I am talking about the inevitable. If it’s not me, it’ll be someone else ascending. That’s the nature of this universe. But it won’t be you, and it won’t be Kade.”

Another blow. Tanat caught this one on her sword’s blade. The impact drove her back a half-dozen feet, and it had only been glancing. Her arm went numb from the sheer force of it.

No, not from the force. From the lightning sparks still arcing up and down her limb.

She gritted her teeth against the pain in her chest even as she tasted blood on her breath. Then she braced herself for the onslaught, dropped into her best defensive position, and prepared to keep fighting. She had to stall until Kade got here. It was the only way to survive—or to warn him what was coming.

The next forty-five seconds were the worst of Tanat’s life.

The God of Thunder continued to be overwhelming. His speed and strength were on an entirely different level from anything she’d seen. His mastery of the storm was such that her best efforts looked like static from a wool blanket compared to him. Every second, another wound opened, or she was driven another few steps away from the portal she desperately needed to reach.

And, worse, it was abundantly clear that the God of Thunder was holding back. Her Thunderwarps and Lightning Ripostes were nothing compared to his casual parries and sweeping blows. The tip of the spear had only drawn blood once—when it cut her cheek in the opening moments of the fight. Other than that, he’d used the butt like a quarterstaff, deliberately holding back.

The meaning was obvious, and after less than a minute, she stood tenderly on a shattered leg, her off-hand hanging uselessly from a destroyed shoulder ball. Pain came from too many places for her Stamina to hold it off.

“Do you admit defeat?” the God of Thunder asked. The spear-tip hovered inches from Tanat’s eye, ready to thrust into it and end her life.

She flicked her sword and pushed the tip away, then began to retreat, step by step, away from her goal.

And the God of Thunder pursued.

Tanat had no hope of winning herself.

Then again, this lonely gate had been her second home. She’d spent years of her life slowly clearing it, methodically eliminating every walking suit of armor and Script-casting robed figure in the city before turning her sights on the Black Tower itself. It had been, quite literally, her life’s work. She’d put every waking moment into preparing for it, sparring with her friends and allies back at the Soaring Saber Sect, or fighting the monsters inside her Lonely Gate.

Then Kade had appeared out of nowhere, inside her Lonely Gate, and done in a handful of sessions what she’d been working on for a decade or more.

So, as Tanat retreated, trading space for time and safety, her mind was almost exclusively on the God of Thunder’s spear. Its shaft snapped into the side of her head, and only a well-timed Thunderwarp saved her life. Then, as she reappeared, the butt whipped around to crush her shattered ribs. She rolled with the impact, letting it throw her up the stairs. Then she found her feet again as the God of Thunder closed in again.

Almost her whole mind was on the fight in front of her.

But a small part focused on the building storm overhead.

Tanat was no fool. She knew from experience that, while she was every bit the artist with the sword that Kade was, she had no hope of holding her own against him in a no-holds-barred fight. He had more tools, and he’d mastered his far more naturally than she had hers.

So, the moment the God of Thunder had arrived in Kade and Ellen’s world, Tanat had been focused on the growing storm overhead. On the ever-increasing barrage of lightning that filled the air—where it wasn’t overwhelmed by the stink of her own blood—with the stench of ozone.

Overhead, the Eye of the Storm was slowly but surely opening.

She’d realized, after the fight against the Placid King, that the Eye’s speed was its biggest weakness. It couldn’t be maintained forever, try as she might to figure that out; in fact, the moment it was fully open, the storm began to cannibalize itself. She estimated it had less than an hour of functionality from the time it started until it was too unstable to exist, and it collapsed. So, she’d done the next best thing.

The storm raging outside had been pushed as far as she could take it. She’d kept it there, at the cusp of opening, the whole time she’d worked. And the moment the God of Thunder had arrived, she’d given it the last vestiges of power it needed.

The God of Thunder stopped mid-blow. He looked up. “Interesting. I suppose you’re thinking it’ll turn the tide. It did for Kade against the Placid King, after all. Well, you’re wrong. This is the end.”

His spear shot out like a snake striking. The impact folded Tanat’s body around the shining tip. Lightning rippled into her, and she screamed in pure agony.

The scream went on and on.

Ellen and I sprinted for the open portal.

I held nothing back.

Stormrunner and Lightning Dance threw me forward through the Portal Tyrant’s stone-and-cement halls. A handful of low-ranked delvers tried to dodge. One wasn’t fast enough; I clipped him and slammed him into a wall hard enough to knock him out. Behind me, Ellen ran as fast as her S-Rank legs could carry her, but her speed was nothing compared to mine.

Hopefully, the C-Ranker would be okay.

But I couldn’t go back to check on him.

The God of Thunder had to be in our portal world. He had to be hunting the one S-Ranker separated from the pack. And if we weren’t fast enough…

Lightning arced along the walls behind me as I pushed Stormrunner to its limits. I rounded a corner; the room with the portals was just down the hall. Lightning Dance covered the distance instantly, and I crashed through the portal like a lightning bolt.

Sound hit me. The howl of hurricane-force winds raging around the tower. The thousand hooves on the roof as a deluge of rain tried to drown the entire world. Thunder, of course—there was always thunder here.

And screaming.

A long, endless scream that sounded like every last bit of air was being ripped from someone’s lungs. It went on and on, high-pitched enough to cut through the storm’s cacophony. It was the sound of someone dying.

Tanat.

I pushed myself up the stairs, Stormrunner arcing even more lightning across the portal metal frame, and slid to a stop in the throne room’s entryway.

Tanat hung in the air, feet far from the ground. Her sword had clattered to the floor, though her hand reached desperately—and futilely—for it. Her eyes welled with agony, and as I arrived, her scream cut off. There was simply no more air in the woman’s lungs. Her stomach was braced on a pair of metal cross-bars, and those cross-bars were attached to a spear tip almost two feet long.

That same spear tip was currently sticking out of her back, just above her armor’s cullets. Blood dripped from the wound and onto the floor.

My grip tightened, and my eyes narrowed as the God of Thunder turned in place. His eyes locked on mine, burning with electricity, but there wasn’t a drop of malice in them. No. They were the eyes of a predator hunting. “Ah, kid. Good to see you. You’ve been busy. I should congratulate you on your victory over—“

“You killed her,” Ellen said as she got up the stairs and stopped behind me.

“It does seem that way, doesn’t it?” The God of Thunder looked at the still-struggling woman impaled on his spear. He reached up, grabbed her by the back of her armor, and pulled her off; she screamed again, and more blood spurted from the twin wounds—and from the dozens of other injuries covering her body. “Alas, not yet. Are you going to do something about it?”

I tensed. The God of Thunder wanted us to attack him. It was obvious. But at the same time, we couldn’t let him get away with murdering Tanat. Every drop of sympathy for the monster I’d seen in the Hall of Thunder faded, and the battle trance started to grow inside me.

But I didn’t make the first move, and neither did the God of Thunder.

Behind me, power welled, and the Shadowstorm Battery began to draw Mana from my core. It flickered briefly as Pepperoni took to the room’s upper reaches, and a moment later, I unleashed Cheddar, too. The serpents flew high overhead, and Ellen shifted to her Voidwalker form and started casting.

At the same time, the Eye of the Storm opened overhead. I let the power pulse over me; this was my world, not the God of Thunder’s, and I was going to show him that. The Storm’s Blade went up in a high guard, the better to handle the monster in front of us.

And the God of Thunder laughed. He shifted Tanat’s weight effortlessly, flinging her over his shoulder and grabbing both her wrists in one armored hand. His spear whirled in his hand, and he nodded. “Let’s see what you’ve learned, my most interesting pupil.”
