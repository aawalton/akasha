Henry stared grimly at the spiky, giant limb as tiny tendrils poked from its underbelly, reaching out to the vibrant and lively coral around. It was hard to suppress the shiver he felt creeping down his back, even though this was clearly some sort of massive starfish. There was no denying the resemblance of this thing to the creature locked in Attal’s basement.

Sending some clones around, Henry carefully examined the spiky limb as it noticeably sucked the life out of the reef from every snaking tendril it was extending. It wasn’t the alien. It likely had nothing to do with it, but the resemblance was disturbing, and while he sensed no madness encroaching on his mind, Henry still felt wary, mostly because of the “vassal” part of the name.

What did that mean? Was it a vassal to some nebulous concept or was it a vassal to a very real and very fleshy being? Because if this A-rank was a vassal, that meant there might be a master nearby. As for the giant starfish whose body he couldn’t even fully see, it was just… eating. Devouring its way through the sea floor. Coral and anemones turned pale then broke down, while the smaller denizens of the reef ran out, scrambling away from whatever the starfish was doing, though some of them didn’t make it, turning pale and desiccated as the massive creature inhaled the life out of them.

And if this was barely the tip of a limb… This thing might be trouble, he thought as he pulled his awareness back to the ship, nearly six hundred feet above. Henry spoke over the sound of crashing waves. “I’ve got eyes on it.”

The party stirred, and even a couple of the bustling sailors slowed at his word. Rafa frowned, but while a couple of the sailors had a distrusting look in their eyes, none contradicted him, and instead, they approached and glanced in the direction he was staring.

“Where?” Rafa asked.

“Around six-hundred feet below. It’s an Unending Acanthaster Vassal. A-rank. Level 9. Seems to be consuming everything it can manage to put its limbs on.”

Henry didn’t voice his other concerns, especially around the vassal bit. He hoped these people had an idea of what that meant, and seeing their reaction, it seemed like they did, because Rafa’s features immediately twisted as he winced, while the girl who’d almost detected Henry’s clones had suddenly gone still, lips parted. But while those two seemed to be taking him seriously, Henry couldn’t say the same for everyone else in the party.

“You’re telling us you’ve got eyes on something that far underwater? Are you serious?” deadpanned Mathias, one of the two sailors Rafa had brought along for the mission. These ones didn’t seem to like working with people they didn’t know. Ever since meeting them at the docks, the two kept shooting him and Maurice distrusting looks, and as for the second, Navar, the man didn’t show his disbelief on his features though it wafted out of him like three-day-old fish. “How?” he asked, keeping his face neutral, though with Henry’s Telepathic Sense, there was no hiding how the two felt about him.

But neither in his work contract nor his agreement with Rafa did it say that Henry had to worry or care about their opinion. As for the latter’s annoying question, Henry didn’t really feel like explaining his abilities. Especially when he didn’t like the tone those two were taking. So instead of engaging with them, Henry just turned to face Rafa. The party leader was the only one who mattered. Now it was just about figuring out what this vassal thing meant, and whether Henry and Maurice could take on this creature by themselves.

“Captain Rafa,” Henry began, suppressing a smile at the flare of outrage he felt coming off of his left. “If you can handle the water pressure, I can show it to you.”

That earned him a beat of quiet while the captain considered him, but a moment later, he heard Mathias whisper loudly enough to be heard. “Who even is this guy?”

“Shut your trap, Mathias,” snapped their final party member, a tall, dark-skinned woman by the name of Ophelia, which drew both the gaze of the other scouts and the sailors alike.

Right… we must have made quite an impression yesterday, Henry thought. Him and Maurice had been on an extermination mission with her the day prior, and apparently, they might have overdone it a little. It had been a swarm of migrating, steely-gray crabs which, once they winnowed them down, had opted to change course and by mid-day, Henry and Maurice had bagged nearly as much carcasses as the other ten hunters combined.

That tidbit might have not made the round just yet, but reputations weren’t built overnight. As for Mathias, Ophelia’s tone coupled with Rafa’s glare were enough to have him raise his hands in surrender, but there was still resentment there which… frankly didn’t matter.

“What do you mean, exactly? A teleporting skill?” asked Rafa, looking away from Mathias and focusing on Henry, while Maurice crouched over the side of the ship, peering down the water with a grin on his lips. A couple of sailors glanced worriedly at the teen-looking crab, but Maurice didn’t even register them. He was only thinking of the A-rank they might be taking home. In the meantime, Henry kept his eye on the limb, and now, another one of his dark and yellow clones was hovering another limb, which gave Henry a hint toward where he would find the starfish’s central disk. With a Dancing Shadows-empowered Invisibility, Henry sent three clones in its direction. If there was a second A-rank nearby, he didn’t want to rile it up just yet. As for Rafa’s question…

“It’s a teleport aimed at a summon of mine,” Henry said, meeting the older man’s gaze. “I can take you down there and back up in less than a minute.”

And that they did. After making sure the clone he was about to switch places with was invisible, he stored the captain in his Maw, then jumped down the water before he teleported them to the creature’s limb. When he pulled him out of his Maw, the captain shrugged the pressure easily enough. He identified it, used a couple more skills Henry couldn’t identify, then signaled him to pull them back up to the surface as Kiara sprang up.

“Rafa… what did you see? What are we dealing with?”

Raking his wet hair back, Rafa wiped at his face then shook his head, lips pressed into a line.

“It’s likely not alone,” Rafa said, digging into his satchel. “Maybe symbiotic. Maybe a parasite. I’d give it… 4 chances out of 5 that it’s not alone, but I couldn’t detect anything, and that would make sense considering how crafty this thing has been. And so if the A-rank is the vassal, we can expect another A-rank a couple dozen levels above it.”

Rafa finally pulled an intricately carved tablet with what seemed to be cores embedded within it and with a raised hand, he signaled the navigator. “We’re pulling back and waiting for reinforcement,” he shouted. “One A-rank might be already beyond us. Two… I gotta report that.”

Stepping to the side, Rafa began speaking down to the tablet he’d dug out, while Henry and Maurice shared a look before they looked away. To keep up their confident but aloof routine.

“[Do you think there’s two of ‘em?]” Maurice’s voice echoed.

Eyeing the other party members, Henry leaned against the side of the ship while he looked through his scouting clones.“[We’re about to find out. My clones are approaching the center. Though with how close this thing is to the island… maybe we should wait?]”

Henry was really tempted to get started on the extermination by themselves, but while the starfish by itself was likely manageable, he really didn’t know what else was hidden. If there was indeed another creature, it could be low A-rank, or it could be in the nineties, which might make it way more dangerous than what they could handle, especially with how steep the difference in power was in the rank. That crab Henry had beat-up had been tough, but he remembered Velistraine’s strength when they’d sparred, and he also remembered how Velistraine herself had been swatted away like an unruly cat by Zerathstra. Every ten levels in A-rank mattered greatly. Henry and Maurice had struggled against the seahorse, and that had been level 17. So anything in its twenties was dangerous, but manageable after the recent soul upgrades Henry had gone through. But anything above level 30 was likely more than what they could manage.

“[Yeah. We should wait,]” Henry decided. He didn’t want to put them at risk, even if he was tempted. “[It’s likely that Attal’s the one who’s about to show up, and we can ask him to let us take a shot at it. If it’s beyond us… then we’ll bring Velistraine along. Hold on. I think I see the main body… and it’s big. Damn.]”

Through the eyes of his clones, Henry watched as every limb of the starfish grew as thick as three or four tree-trunks put together. Though with their length, the arms still looked incredibly eerie with how relatively spindly they were. The disc itself was not that large. Maybe the width of a large building or two, but not as large as any of the island’s street block. The whole thing was bristling with undulating spikes, and from the looks of it, the starfish was nestled on top of a seamount which was bare of any life. Just gray, chalky stone. Whether the creature detected him or not, he couldn’t tell. But he knew this one would be here. What he was looking for was the second.

Over the next couple of minutes, Henry tried to locate it. He tried to get a visual on it, but as time went on, he saw nothing out of the ordinary. Up above, Henry frowned, considering his options. If what was hidden was a parasite, it might not like him outing it. Henry was pretty sure he could confirm its presence with a boosted Find the Path. But maybe he should wait for Attal to be nearby first. He wouldn’t want another seahorse incident—

A shout broke Henry out of his musings. Henry looked up to find sailors pointing behind, and before even the first shout was finished, a shriek took over.

A person’s shriek.

Henry and Maurice reacted the fastest, taking to the air while Rafa and Kiara broke into a run and within the second, Henry saw what he was looking for, through the chaos of the brawling sailors. To his horror, Henry realized they weren’t just brawling. They were swinging at each other with every bit of strength they could manage, their faces morphed into shock and terror, and overseeing all of it, a small, cat-sized yellow thing, hovering a few paces behind the stern while a nimbus of sickly pale light surrounded it, like some sort of malignant halo.

The thing looked like some sort of worm with four branching, fleshy limbs that seemed more like roots than actual limbs. It was small, but even though Henry saw no eyes, he could feel its attention settle on him as soon as he came into its line of sight, and he knew right then and there.

This thing was dangerous.
