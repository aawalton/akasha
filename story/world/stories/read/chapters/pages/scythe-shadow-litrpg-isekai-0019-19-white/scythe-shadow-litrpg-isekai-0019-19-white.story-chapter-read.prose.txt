Calliope and I jogged down the hill toward the woman and boy, leaving Jericho despondent in the wagon. I'd voluntold Nuzzle to stay with him and the little lizard miraculously obeyed.

I still had no idea what caused his sudden depression, but Calliope was more sullen than usual as well. She'd insisted I stay behind with Jericho, but there was no way I was just going to sit around if someone needed help.

We slowed a bit, approaching cautiously.

Calliope spoke in a whisper. "Jericho lost a son when he was about this boy's age."

So that was it then. Bad memories flooding back?

But he couldn't possibly plummet into despair every time he saw a kid, right? I could understand sorrow and loss. More than most… But it felt like I was missing something about this situation.

"What did Jericho mean when he said 'the boy's about to go white?'" I asked.

Calliope opened her mouth to respond, but closed it again, clearly having trouble finding the right words.

"You should go back to the wagon," she said instead.

I frowned and shook my head.

"Then you'll find out soon enough."

It didn't take long to arrive at the scene. The Ruby-cloaked woman stood over the bloated corpse of a spider-human hybrid soul with half its disturbingly humanoid legs hacked off. Her straight ebony hair flowed down past a leather-armored torso all the way to her waist, a streak of purple trailing down one side. The scythe in her hand dripped a sticky green ichor.

Her Will was even higher than Jericho's. According to Calliope, she should have at least been in the Canyon, if not the Desert.

The small boy now sat in the grass nearby, his knees pulled up to his chest as he rocked back and forth. Bloodshot eyes shot between blades of grass, trees, bushes—everywhere but the dead beast or the scythe resting beside him.

As I approached, I could hear incoherent mumblings. His lips were cracked and bleeding, face sunken and skull-like. The skin around his neck was red and scabbed, the strings of his Amber cloak tight like a wire cutting into his throat.

Fuck…

He couldn't have been any more than ten years old, yet he looked ancient.

"Don't touch him!" the woman hissed as she swept between the boy and me.

"He needs help," I said.

She glared at me, her dark eye threatening to bore into my soul. "What do you think I'm doing?"

The boy wouldn't move, wouldn't stop his rhythmic rocking, even as the Ruby lifted him and carried him to the fallen beast.

I started to follow, but a hand rested on my shoulder, pulling me back.

"It's too late," Calliope said softly. "Let it be…"

The woman placed the boy down, then returned and picked up his scythe. Her palm and fingers sizzled upon touching the shaft, snakes of smoke dancing up from her grasp. The smell of burning flesh followed.

She didn't cry out, didn't so much as flinch, as she brought it to the boy and placed it on his lap. Kneeling down she whispered something in his ear, but his mumbling only grew louder.

"You have to do it, bug," she said, tears forming at the corners of her eyes. "C'mon, bug."

I couldn't be certain, but something inside told me that wasn't just a made up name like Zephyr. The endearing tone. The tortured expression. There was an intimacy there.

I didn't know how, but this Ruby knew the boy. Truly knew him. In the Vitrial Plane—the 'real' world. Somehow they'd found a way to identify one another despite the System's strange censorship.

But she also knew, as well as I, that he wasn't going to harvest the Sin's Will. This clearly wasn't the first time they'd attempted this together.

The boy moved his lips, voice course and strained. Barely audible. "I don't want to kill."

The Ruby choked back a sob. "You don't have to, bug. I did it for you. All you have to do is hold the broom." She held up his scythe, fingers burning again, and wrapped his fingers around it, but they were limp, not gripping at all. "We're just gonna sweep the floor, okay?"

"I don't want to sweep the floor."

"We'll do it together."

"I don't want to sweep the floor."

"You just need to hold the handle. I'll do the rest, bug." Tears streamed down the Ruby's face.

Calliope latched gently onto my arm. I got the distinct feeling we shouldn't be here. That this was a private moment we were intruding on.

A farewell…

We stood there for several moments, transfixed and horrified, but unable to pull our eyes away as the woman tried—pleaded—for the boy to harvest the Will.

I examined the boy with my second sight. The physical deterioration of his body was mirrored in his Will. It burned dimly orange, a lamp on the last of its wick.

Then it snuffed out. The boy convulsed. His head lolled to one side, then rolled to the ground. It was bloodless. If I hadn't known any better I could've mistaken it for a mannequin head.

But I knew better.

Calliope's squeezed my arm tight. I didn't look at her. If I did, I'd probably start crying like she was.

The Amber cloak fell gently from the boy's back, fading to white as it hit the ground, and pulling his Will out from the hole in his neck with it. Instead of a shinning brilliant sun-like sphere, the Will was solid as steel and white instead of the Amber it should have been. Both the Will and the cloak disintegrated into the dark grass where the Ruby knelt beside him.

A life gone, just like that. Extinguished from the world.

She cradled the boy in her arms, hugging his chest to her face, even as the body rapidly mummified and crumpled into shreds that were cast away in the breeze. In seconds, he was gone.

The Ruby stood, her well of tears long run dry, and walked past us.

Then she fell forward toward her own shadow cast by the non-existent sun. Her cloak whirled around her, then swirled into a vortex and disappeared as she phased out.

Neither of us said a word.

Eventually, Calliope strode over to the spider-human corpse, pointedly avoiding the spot where the boy had been. She fixed her dark eye on the soul's Will, then inserted her scythe through the back.

I closed my good eye and watched through the dark as well, the beast's Will appearing within its chest cavity—the smooth Amber orb of a defeated soul. Calliope slid her scythe through insectoid exoskeleton and dead tissue. When the point finally hit the dormant Will, shining cracks shattered out across the orb originating at the scythe's tip. The shimmering in the cracks flowed across the surface of the orb and into her scythe bathing it in an Amber aura.

Then the remaining husk of a Will inside the spider-creature's body disintegrated into ash.

That's all the boy had to do, then relinquish it and he'd still be alive.

But then he'd be a killer. Even though these were monsters and Sins, he wouldn't do it. Couldn't do it.

He'd delayed it, allowing his Volition to fall.

I wondered if he'd known what would happen.

I wondered if there was anything I could've done besides stand there watching like an idiot.

I wondered how close I'd come to that same fate when my Volition had been low before. I thought I'd be relatively safe in this world. After all, if I lost my Health I'd just wake up back home—in a lot of discomfort, but alive.

But I'd been wrong. It wasn't that simple. It never was.

And Death would not hesitate to come for me too.
