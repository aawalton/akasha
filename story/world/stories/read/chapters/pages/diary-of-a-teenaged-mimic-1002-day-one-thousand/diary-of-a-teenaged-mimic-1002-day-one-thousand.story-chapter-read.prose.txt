Shit, getting shot full of arrows is traumatizing, but at this point I'm not sure if some poor kid got shot, they're gonna feel more or less traumatized if the shooter spontaneously combusts as his armor melts, then undergoes spontaneous deconstruction into sloppy meat chunks. To hear Saffron tell it, not to mention Dad, that's part and parcel of why I'm a Goddess of Children. Protector, Defender, Avenger, and as they've said, when there is an avenger around ready to do some avenging, there's likely to be maybe a little less need for that.

Fuck, I guess there's something positive to be said about me if the one thing that will focus me enough to channel one point six percent of Shaggy's full power is kids being in danger. Or worse, hurt.

I know I've been trying to be kinder with myself, not to mention other people, but some shit deserves to be scraped off my shoe and flushed down the toilet.

At any rate, got home and very carefully did not mention my whole worry about the millennial anniversary of my arrival portending danger to kids somewhere. Spent some time showing the new kids the kitchen. Well, for a value of 'showing the new kids the kitchen' that meant Marie and I making simple sandwiches for dinner with a whole fuckin' kitchen full of adorable sous chefs. Heh. 'Simple' sandwiches. Toasted, fresh baked bread, although those loaves were brought back from the Academy by Murder Mittens. I toasted a couple with Mana Blades, but most of them we did the old fashioned way in the oven.

Interesting side note, when she saw me doing my insta-toast maneuver, Davey said, "may I try?"

"Okay. Be really careful. You saw how I held the bread up?"

"Yes, Mama."

I held a loaf up and carefully slid a short Mana Blade through it, a slice dropping off onto the cutting board, nicely browned. "That's because with a Mana Blade the blade does the cutting; you don't need to saw or push against the cutting board. Also, I'm pretty sure a Mana Blade would cut the cutting board, and that would make Mommy sad."

From across the kitchen, Marie chimed in with a sorrowful, "It Would."

Davey nodded solemly, picked up a loaf, and carefully sliced through it with her little Blade. I tensed up a little when she did, as the slice fell off the loaf onto the counter. Where it lay there completely untoasted. "It didn't work?"

I drew my Mana Blade in, then picked up her slice. The cut had that utterly smooth look that required a stupidly sharp blade, but it wasn't even warm to the touch. Also, no crunch, just normal bread softness. "Huh. I guess my Mana Blades have more heat to them than Tallulah's."

At that point Ria called out, "Mother told me yours are remarkably less efficient than ours because of that."

"I guess so." Then what she'd said registered. "Wait, 'ours'? You know how to make a Mana Blade?"

My little Sidhe girl nodded solemnly. "I cannot maintain it for long but I know the basic Shape."

Made me a lot less worried about somebody jumping my kids to have another of them armed, but like I'd mentioned, a minority of the kids put in danger had been threatened by attacks. Also, one of my daughters knew how to make the most dangerous Shape I knew of, which was not a calming thought in general.

I took myself in hand again, took a deep breath and let it out, then turned back to Davey. "Well, I guess you can't instantly toast them, but your slices are smoother than mine. So I guess you could slice them up with your Blade, then we can toast them in the oven?"

"Okay, Mama!" I might have spent more than most of my attention carefully watching Davey slicing bread until we had enough, but... Fuck it, sue me, I was on edge. She got through the whole process without a single slip up, then proceeded to put the Blade away and take it back to stash it in her room. I almost asked her to keep it on her, just in case, but then I realized that 'supernaturally sharp blade' and 'hoard of school aged kids' was like, a recipe for exactly what I was trying to prevent.

I tried very hard to ignore the fact that there were seventeen kids in a fully stocked kitchen, with all the dangerous objects and substances that implied.

So, along with the fresh toasted bread, we made a hearty homemade mayonnaise, which a couple of the kids and Saffron all liked, mustard, and I'm talking about the brownish paste not the yellow goop, and even some ketchup. Not tuna salad, because we don't have tuna, but Marie managed to show us all how to make something a lot like that out of the fish we had.
