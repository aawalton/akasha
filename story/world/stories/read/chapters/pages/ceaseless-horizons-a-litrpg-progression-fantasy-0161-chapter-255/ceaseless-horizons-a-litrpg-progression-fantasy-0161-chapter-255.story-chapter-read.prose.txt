“That’s an interesting… thing,” Isabella sent, looking at the massive disk Lev was currently observing. “Actually, insanity describes it better. What the hell is that?”

“It is my latest masterpiece. Do you like it?”

“We should put it on a pedestal for new crafters as an example of what not to craft.”

“That’s a weird compliment, but I’ll take it.”

She just exhaled through her nose before relaying her actual thoughts. “I don’t think anyone short of an Exalted can really use this. How do you plan to?”

“It will serve as an excellent willpower training tool,” Lev answered with a grin, not even needing to turn to know how exasperated she was.

“Was this weapon meant to be used against the stag, Blessed Lev?” the hunter asked, standing to full height and peering into the enchantments. “The Words of Mana resonate strongly around the edges.”

“You mean the runes?” Lev tilted his head. “I suppose it is an entire language that calls upon the power of Mana. Yeah, I planned to use it as a last resort if everything else failed. What do you mean by resonance?”

“There are two potent effects borne of resonance in this… construct. Can you not sense them?”

Wait a goddamn minute…

Lev reread the enchantments, noting two of them. Dexterous Disk and Durability Nexus had both appeared without any corresponding runes, a signature phenomenon whenever good Epic items were crafted.

“So these ‘effects’ appear whenever the runes resonate very strongly?”

“Indeed,” the hunter straightened, moving away from the disk. “My knowledge is severely limited in the matter, but as far as I recall, all runes have their own influence, comparable to a very weak and linear aura. They can, however, combine into greater wholes.”

That finally explained the mystery of why he was dealing with enchantments randomly popping up in his items. It also confirmed that the phenomenon was wholly positive, though in the back of his mind, he noted extra possibilities. If these resonance enchantments were guided, they could be upgraded to even greater heights.

Let’s not try to fly before running, he reminded himself, looking at the sleeping forms.

“How long was I out?”

“Four hours, give or take. You can wake everyone up if you wish. They’ve been asleep for two hours,” Isabella answered.

“No need,” Lev breathed out and looked at the hunter. “Can you take the bubble even lower?”

The ground beneath them flowed around the round construct, gently pushing it downwards. Lev stared slack-jawed at the smoothness of the manipulation before shaking his head. He didn’t plan to add stone mana to his repertoire anytime soon, but it was quite a strong element.

When they were a few hundred meters below the surface, the hunter exited the bubble to scout, while carving small channels for airflow. That manipulation also woke up Melvine, who looked around warily before relaxing.

“I’ll sleep now,” Lisa murmured and passed out next to Rena after two seconds.

Lev just chuckled silently to himself and closed his eyes. His soul was hurting quite a bit. Not only had he bottomed out completely, but he had also asked Kora to use her buffs on him.

Now, he had over a third of his pool and a lot of pain to go along with it.

“Don’t do that again,” Melvine sent, moving into a corner and shrouding himself with mist. It dispersed after a moment, revealing her glistening and straightened hair, as well as a refreshed face.

Huh. That was pretty neat, Lev mused and answered. “It’s not like I chose to.”

“Still. Our three combined attacks would’ve been enough to wrestle the slash.”

“And also kill us all in the aftermath,” Lev countered. “I wasn’t even sure if the hunter would return. It would’ve been up to you and Isabella to keep us all alive.”

She didn’t ask how. Her element was only going to empower the lightning. Keeping the hunters from getting boiled alive would’ve been her task while Isabella and the rest fended off the explosion… somehow.

As usual, it took considerable effort not to linger on the sheer gap in strength between Lev and his friends when extreme measures were concerned. They, too, would only continue to get stronger with him.

That was more than enough.

“What now?” Melvine eventually asked, taking a seat near his own.

“Now, we wait. The stag is still alive and out there. A confrontation is pretty unlikely, but there are no guarantees. Be prepared for just about anything, I guess.”

“That doesn’t instill a lot of hope,” she answered calmly. “Here I thought nothing I’ll see again will beat the horrors of the forest. The next thing you know, I’m in a Titanium dungeon with a bunch of greenhorns.”

Lev refrained from rolling his eyes. “At least we’re pretty strong for our age. But yeah, no one could’ve predicted the dungeon, let alone meeting those two beings.”

“What if we need to kill it?” she asked abruptly.

He wished he had to think long. The answer, however, came immediately.

“Then I’ll need a lot of healing again,” he shrugged. “Also, good lord, I’m starving from all the healing.”

The pangs of hunger that had been suppressed so far returned with vengeance. Lev immediately summoned a large tray and different types of small and medium meals.

To the right were all the greens and salads, and to the right were all the meaty dishes. Melvine observed him curiously, and when he offered to summon more for her, she declined.

Five minutes of feasting later, which was quite a lot of food for his constantly pushed Perception, Lev leaned back with a satisfied sigh.

I hope I didn’t overeat.

Pushing the matter to the back of his mind, he stood up and hopped around a bit. No aches, no cuts, and no other wounds on his body. His Constitution was pulling its weight.

His soul, however, was still grumbling in occasional pulses of pain. They were dutifully ignored.

Another hour of resting later, Viktor stirred. He blearily looked at Lev, and it was impossible to mask the surge of relief in his eyes upon seeing him up and functional.

“Idiot,” he said once before stretching with a yawn. Then he slapped the back of Elias’s head for good measure.

The swordsman grumbled, peeking open an eye. He looked at Lev silently for a moment before shaking his head and glaring at Viktor. The latter was already up and washing his face from a refillable bowl.

“Are you usually so… flashy?” Elias asked with a sigh.

“You’re welcome,” Lev rolled his eyes. “And yes, my class’s strength rises explosively proportional to the amount of mana I use.”

“That suddenly doesn’t seem like too big a positive.”

“That’s how explosive classes usually go,” Lev smirked. “I just have a few advantages over the average Joe.”

“Few, he says.”

Everyone else slowly awoke in the next three hours, not forgetting to give Lev a few choice bits of ‘advice.’ Most of it boiled down to calling him an idiot in roundabout ways.

He didn’t mind. Far from it. Their concern was like a warm blanket that constantly reminded him of why he was doing everything that he was. Personal goals aside, he would be their shield against the horrors of Monarch eventually.

That, he could live for any day of the week. The progress to be made by doing that was secondary.

The passage of time accelerated when nothing attacked them. Lev kept the concealments on his armor active, and combined with the cores slotted in the bubble, it wasn’t surprising that no monsters found them.

What did surprise him, was the hunter’s absence. By now, Kora had made sure that everyone was topped off on mana, and even his pool was full.

Yet, no matter how much they waited, absolutely nothing happened.

The hunter showed up after a day, sending a pulse of stone mana through the ducts the being had dug for air flow to warn them. Lev opened the bubble for entry and waited for the news, hoping it was more good than bad.

“So?” Isabella asked the moment the hunter was inside. “How doomed are we?”

Zack poked her side, probably to remind her about subtlety, but she just brushed him off.

“I believe the situation is best explained through first-hand experience.”

The humans shared glances, confused and curious. It was one thing to receive bad news, and another to see the hunter so lost for words.

Above them, the stone split open. Clear skies could be seen above the passage as the bubble ascended, and Lev quickly took stock of the terrain.

Blackened hills dominated his vision. They were utterly ruined. Heaps of debris and rubble were layered near the base of some, and others were simply smaller than they once were. It was as if a tsunami had passed through, shaving off a few layers from the hills.

More inspections could wait. The hunter stepped out of the bubble and started making its way towards the dungeon’s entrance with giant leaps. The bubble followed, its enchantments pushed close to the maximum just to keep up.

Holy hell, the hunter is fast, Lev noted, astonished. The more he watched, the less certain he was about ever being able to outrun the being close to the ground. Really fucking glad that we’re not enemies. I sincerely doubt all of us combined could even wound it, let alone take it down.

Oblivious to his thoughts, the hunter became even faster, and within minutes of that pace, they reached the lake where the boss initially resided.

The stag was also present.

If someone asked Lev what his nightmares looked like, he wouldn’t shy away from describing them as the boss’s current condition.

Blood spurted out of the endless wounds covering its form. The once majestic antlers were cracked, outright broken in two places. All of the fur was stained with crimson, and all of it was made endlessly more harrowing by the fact that the stag was slowly killing itself.

Ridiculous amounts of mana coursed through its body. Its body enhancement was pushed to such heights that mana poisoning was slowly melting through flesh and bone, forcing its natural regeneration to focus on that instead of bringing it to full health.

The pool of the vital fluid forming beneath its form was constantly guided back into its body in an endless loop. Wounds were closed, only for new tears to form on its skin, releasing even more blood and worsening its condition.

Once the insurmountable obstacle and source of immense dread, it was reduced to such a state that Lev had to hold himself back from healing it several times. Despite the stag potentially being the worst enemy he had ever met, he wanted nothing more than the madness to end.

And, for the first time in his life, Lev was forced to think about whether this was what his friends had to go through every time he destroyed his body to bring out more power.

“What…” he began to ask, then froze when the stag moved.

With measured steps, the boss moved towards the bubble. Lev stepped out, understanding that this whole scenario concerned him, though he still sent the bubble away to not put the others at needless risk.

Eyes wide, he held his breath as the boss neared. It loomed over him like the hound had, and for a mere Master, the difference between the two beings didn’t matter. Either could end him with a thought.

His heart pounded. The stag was only a dozen meters away when it paused. A slight incline of its head showed that it was most certainly sane and in control of the situation. Not wanting to risk disrespect by not reacting, Lev also bowed his head, mimicking the action.

Some amusement cut through the frigid eyes of the stag. It might have been Lev’s imagination, for it was gone in a blink. Before he could think of anything to say, the boss’s head passed by him slightly. It was tilted away from him, and Lev had to take a moment to register what the action signified.

In front of him was a blood-stained neck, purposefully defenseless and at his mercy.

… What?

Just to be safe, he looked at the stag’s eyes and immediately regretted it. They were too stern, hollow in a sense that made his hair stand on end. Breath caught in his throat, and his armor’s dense enchantments suddenly felt like they were choking him.

He made it turn into its passive state, but that didn’t help. Goosebumps formed all over as their eyes remained locked in a staredown, and it was Lev that eventually closed his eyes.

An easy way out…

The thought almost made him vomit. No longer was the stag just a dungeon boss. It was a sapient being just like him, one that was sacrificing itself to him, a boon that would not only let them escape the dungeon alive but also rocket his class levels by an insane degree.

Lev had never encountered a more repulsive offer.

“We just have to clear the dungeon, right?” he said out loud, locking eyes again. “Do you still wish to live?”

This time, the emotion was obvious. Anger burned in the stag’s eyes, regarding Lev with something bordering on madness. Too long had it waited for death, and now the human had dared to deny the being its reprieve.

The question was meaningless anyway, for Lev had already made up his mind. He conveyed his answer to the silent offer through their clashing gazes, and when frustration crept into the stag’s eyes, fear finally reasserted itself.

Lev took a step back when the boss kicked the ground, visibly holding itself back from turning him to paste. He only had a moment to react and cover himself in sound wards when it raised its head and let out a chilling, warbling roar which continued for nearly ten seconds.

The sound echoed throughout the massive dungeon several times, yet it only served to push all the monsters away, instilling fear in their maddened hearts.

Having gotten his answer, Lev exhaled. It was his job to bring them all to their location, and the hunter standing protectively beside him understood that immediately.

Claws elongated on each of their beastial hands, extending to the lengths of human swords. Mana rushed through their thin body, entire rivers of it roaring in each limb.

Sound and invisibility enchantments were deactivated on his bubble. Two teams and two lone hunters stepped out, already armed to the teeth. For the first time, Elias was wearing his golden powered armor, deeming the situation bad enough to put his tournament reward to use despite lacking the understanding and practice to use it fully. Every little bit was going to matter.

Incensed breathing made him turn back to the stag. It glared at him with undisguised rage, and before things could take a turn for the worse, Lev called his Epic disk out of the bubble.

Summoning his armor and turning off all concealment enchantments on Stargazer, he stepped on top of the flat of the disk and ascended.

Powering up the armor’s and glaive’s enchantments to the fullest, Lev also released every last bit of his usually hidden aura. Combined with his equipment, the aura’s influence spread far and wide, attracting the whole dungeon’s attention.

Standing upon an Epic disk that was slowly powering up, he continued to force his presence to shine even brighter, presenting himself as a human flare in the confines of the dungeon for all the third-threshold monsters to see.
