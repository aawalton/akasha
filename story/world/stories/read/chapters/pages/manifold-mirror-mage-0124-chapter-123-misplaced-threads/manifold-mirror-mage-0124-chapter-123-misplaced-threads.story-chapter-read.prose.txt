The light dimmed to reveal a faded Arseya, cradled in the arms of her daughter. The Grand Weaver was back in her sprite-like form, though looking altogether diminished. With every breath, she seemed to lose some amount of definition, turning into a pile of barely-wound threads.

“Ahaha!” Arseya weakly coughed. “What fun.”

“Grandmother!” Chandrika shouted, rushing to her side.

“Oh, don’t you worry, silkworm. I’ll be right as rain within the hour… More embarrassing than anything.” Arseya said, reaching up with a doll-sized arm to stroke the cheek of her descendant.

“What happened?” Gio asked. “One second, we were fine, and the next- you’re all… stringy?”

“She has overdrawn her skill,” Baphelus answered. “And revealed that she has invested too much of herself into it.”

The lich’s concerned expression spoke volumes.

“What does that mean?” Chandrika asked. “She is the Grand Weaver of Bangla! Why does it matter how much work she’s put into her weaving skill?”

“Not work.” Headmaster Vespertine interjected with a frown. “Herself. She has imbued her very essence into her weaving skill… it is an incredible feat, to be certain… but it has certain, shall we say, risks.”

“You’re dancing around the subject, dears. I do not harbor such audacious ambitions as you’re expecting. You see, our friends are afraid that I’m going to try to turn myself into either a spirit or a god.”

Gio baulked, and Chandrika’s eyes widened.

“What?”

“Arseya,” Baphelus said, pinching his brow. “That’s not something for the children to hear. I would have thought that you, of all people, would have run away from this foolishness.”

“Well, it’s not exactly a secret, is it?” Arseya asked. “Ascension is history, darling. In fact, if I recall correctly, this school is responsible for the deaths of several upstart deities in this region.”

“They’ll learn about the Dead Gods of the Grasslands in class… during their fourth year.” Eudoria hummed. “It is a bit soon to induct them into such matters.”

“I’ve read about God-emperors and people being worshipped as deities, but I always thought that it was just allegorical or the inflated ego of a powerful person forcing the less powerful to worship them. I didn’t think that people could actually become gods…” Gio noted.

“They can,” Arseya replied. “But I am not going to. You are correct, Baphelus. I watched as the wretched thing that crawled out of the husk that was once my father became more and more monstrous during its reign as the Cannibal King in a foolish attempt at godhood. I have no delusions that I would fare any better at upholding my virtues and values than he did- and I fully intend to die human.”

“Going back just a second-” Gio interrupted. “Putting your ‘essence’ into a skill makes you a spirit… or god?”

“We don’t need to get into that subject now.”  Baphelus sighed. “I could teach you years of minutiae about this conversation and still find more things to say. To sate your curiosity - yes, that is one path to ascension.  A spirit is an incorporeal entity that usually embodies a single concept or subject, such as weaving, to a degree that it becomes a defining trait. This is, in a sense, what she has begun to do by investing her essence into her skill. Then, the main things that make a god different from a spirit are three: A name, a divine domain, and worship… though some scholars would likely try to lop my head off for such a reductive explanation. So long as Arseya is not garnering faith, she will not ascend to godhood… although if she fully abandons physical form, she will need to cement her identity in some way to avoid transcending into a spirit of weaving…”

“I’ve got it covered, dear.” Arseya waved him off. “It would take a great deal of effort for me to even become close to that point. I’m in no danger of slipping into the sky. What I am more interested in talking about right now is what Giorgio and I just witnessed. Firstly, I solved the mystery of why he eludes my sight, and why Chandrika cannot use her art in his presence.”

“Do go on, then,” Baphelus said, taking the cue to shift subjects.

“Where Giorgio takes one step, a thousand ‘reflections’ of him are projected out into the Weave. He is ‘causality’ made manifest, and the raw potential of Chaos is too much for even me to see plainly.” Arseya explained.

The entire room paused to take in the implications of such a bold statement.

“But… how? His reflection is the Aspect, not he.” Aayana replied.

“They are one and the same. One soul, reflected across dimensions. This is, apparently, true for all of us.” Arseya said.

“Ahh, the Lavanua Precept and the Vestige Argument.” Baphelus dictated, stroking his goatee. “You’re reawakening an old quarrel about the very nature of being. A fascinating bit of moral dilemma that deals with -”

“What my dear, dear long-winded husband means to say is that the nature of the soul regarding things like shadows and reflections is… debated. There was a time when taking a picture of someone was considered to be a form of soul stealing by certain… paranoid individuals.” Headmaster Vespertine translated.

“In their defense, I have also seen devices similar to cameras that did steal souls,” Baphelus muttered.

“I cannot speak for whatever arcane conjecture goes on in a school such as this one, as you know that I am uneducated in the arts of True Magic. What I am able to confirm, however, is that Gio and his reflection share a linked soul. I felt that link, and through it… We saw some of those frayed futures.”

The weaver crafted several tapestries of pure light, weaving tiny knots of colored lasers into a pixelated, blurry image. Gradually, she added to it until the image clarified.

The tapestry depicted a great burning colossus melting down the obsidian walls of the Ringed City, and the cackling witch with her odd book that they had seen in the vision. Headmaster Vespertine walked up to the tapestry, taking a closer look.

“That is Amity Sinclair… A former pupil of mine. I had her thrown out two years ago… It was a shame. Expelled at the end of her Seventh year because she chose to indulge in forbidden magic that I had expressly warned her off.”

“E-Expelled?” Gio blanched.

“Relax.” Saint Baphelus harrumphed. “The forbidden magic that you were burdened with is dangerous to the user. That girl was using a spell to leech the life force and talents of other students. She was quite clever, luring her victims outside of my domain… but she made a crucial mistake.”

“Underestimating Eudoria.” Queen Aayana guessed.

Headmaster Vespertine smiled. “You flatter me. The point being is that she has a grudge… and has been holed up in a settlement near Enou’s Watch, according to my informants. I’ve heard that she was looking for a way to get back at me… but this seems excessive, even for her.”

Saint Baphelus examined the tapestry closer, before stopping at the stitched book in Amity Sinclair’s hands. “What is that doing…?”

The Saint paused, searching the air for a moment in thought.

“No, that should not be possible. This book is currently resting at the bottom of the ocean in a wrecked steamcraft. Arbalum has placed wards to know if it has been tampered with, and it is guarded by a powerful creature, notwithstanding.” Baphelus said, furrowing his brows.

“What is it?” Gio asked.

“It’s called the Book of the Red Star. A grimoire of demonic knowledge and corrupt arcana… last we saw, it was being used to pilot a great and terrible device. But does this depict her using it to control a seedling from the Wicker Cradle?” Saint Baphelus wondered.

His eyes seemed to return elsewhere for a moment.

“I… I suppose that might be plausible. I need to make a call.” Baphelus said, answering his own question.

A shifting pillar of smooth jade stones melted out of the floor, continuously changing shapes before settling into a patterned slab. Gio’s skin crawled as he watched some of the stones grow legs and skitter about, revealing that the whole structure was made out of oblong beetles with smooth shells.

<BAPHELUS: Daria, are you available? I need to pass a message to Arbalum.>

The air was charged with a haptic buzz as Saint Baphelus used his magic to connect to the mass of bugs. Hundreds of eyes began to glow with pearlescent light as Saint Daria’s avatar took shape. Her beautiful yet unsettling figure appeared, projected in the air by some odd working of magic that seemed to involve electricity and insects.

The avatar had a smooth, feminine face with a pair of round, oversized glasses.  Her glasses reflected the light as if they were made of hundreds of little lenses, like the compound eyes of a flying insect. She had four arms, which looked like they were made of glossy green chitin and terminated in almost-humanoid segmented grabbers.  Her bug-like digits seemed to operate on some obscured device, as she responded.

<DARIA: On it. Patching you in now.>

Gio watched in awe as another picture was projected by the pillar of insects. This image seemed blurrier, like an image passed through a foggy condensation on a glass of ice-cold water.

An echoing voice of a stereotypical sea-salty sailor resounded as a titan of metal swam into view.

<ARBALUM: Bones, Bugs tells me that you’ve got something. What can I do for ya?>

Saint Arbalum’s warform was a hulking rig of valves, pipes, and glowing port-holes as he trawled along the ocean floor. As he spoke, he released a gasp of tiny bubbles. Gio looked at the miniature coral underfoot and got the impression that Saint Arbalum’s mech might be considerably larger than he appeared in this vision.

<BAPHELUS: Barnacles! Thank you for answering, and sorry for the interruption. I just got a scary tip from a trusted source… I am hoping that she’s wrong. Can you check on the wreck of the Cephalus for me? I need to see that the Book of the Red Star hasn’t been taken.>

Saint Arbalum’s image shifted as the behemoth of metal gathered a blinding sign of yellow and blue magic. A ringing bell echoed through the image before the scenery changed.

“[Port Call]” Saint Arbalum invoked.

And he was elsewhere. Gio couldn’t make out the details, but the underwater Saint appeared to be standing in the torn-open fuselage of some kind of twisted machine, now mostly taken over by sea life and corals.

<ARBALUM: Wards are still intact. That bonny ol’ Hydra is still at the entrance, though she’s noticed my visit already and might come ‘ere sniffing. Scanning the ship now… I think that you’re worried for nothing, Baph- I can already tell that the infernal Armanents are still there.>

<BAPHELUS: Understood. Thank you for checking. I just needed to know that the book hasn’t been moved yet. Do me a favor and reinforce those wards if it isn’t too much of a hassle.>

<ARBALUM: Hold on…>

Saint Baphelus frowned. Saint Arbalum pressed further into the gigantic wreck, and the image passed over red glowing glass walls and etchings of grotesque figures on gouged metal that unsettled Gio’s stomach to look at. Geysers of volcanic bubbles spewed in the foreground, and Gio nervously watched some truly upsetting fish swim into view, as if testing to see Saint Arbalum’s defenses were worth probing. Some of the larger, smarter-looking fish quickly scuttled away after sensing the metal Saint’s power.

<ARBALUM: It’s not here, Baph.>

Saint Daria had blurred the interior of whatever Saint Arbalum was presently looking at, likely for the benefit of the rest of the room. The image just showed an angry, pulsating red light.

<DARIA: According to my logs, Arbalum left it right there in the ritual chamber, in the arms of the hell priest.>

Gio’s eyes bulged.

Hell priest?

<BAPHELUS: Well… that’s that. Anything else missing?>

<ARBALUM: The rest of the room is as we left it. I’m staring at an untouched vault. This is hogwash, and it doesn’t make a lick of sense.>

<BAPHELUS: No… no it does not.  Thank you both. I expect that I may be in touch soon.

<ARBALUM: I’ll be reinforcing these wards… and checking in on some of my other sites. Thanks for the tip, and let us know if you find out what’s going on.>

<BAPHELUS: I am going to be investigating this further with help from some allies. Depending on what we find out… I may need to call a meeting.>

<DARIA: Understood. This is irregular. Keep us updated.>

The images faded out as the pillar of insects seemed to recede into whatever habitat or storage Saint Baphelus kept them in.

“Well,” The Saint announced. “You all heard. Someone has discreetly made off with that book. If the image that Arseya showed us is a potential future, then we must hope that Miss Sinclair has not already-”

An Inquisitor faded into view, whispering something into Headmaster Vespertine’s ear.

“It would seem that Amity Sinclair has just disappeared this morning.” She frowned. “Her last known location was around thirty miles from the radial border of the Wicker Cradle exclusion area. Unknown at this time if any book was in her possession.”

“That was quicker than expected.” Saint Baphelus frowned.

Chandrika’s grandmother leaned forward in her daughter’s arms.

“There is something… bothering me.” The old woman said. “I wonder, Eudoria, if you would have something personal of the girl?”

Headmaster Vespertine, without hesitating in the slightest, conjured a small sample box using the Manse.

“Would a strand of hair work?” Headmaster Vespertine asked.

“That would do nicely.” Arseya smiled.

Gio and Chandrika looked at each other with furrowed brows before turning to their headmaster.

“What?” Headmaster Vespertine asked.

“Nothing, Headmaster.” Chandrika deadpanned.

Arseya drew again upon her skill, further causing herself to unravel, despite the meek protests of her daughter and attendants.

“Ah-hhh-h” Arseya sighed, her voice stuttering in and out of clarity with the undulations of her form.

The weaver painted with light, as the image unfolded above their heads. A great mangled knot of threads pulsed above them. Arseya floated towards it, her own body appearing to be a storm of golden thread in the vague shape of a woman.

“How very unusual.” Arseya hummed.

She picked with a fingernail at two loose ends.

“This was her, yesterday.”

A dejected, drunk girl appeared in the strand. She sat with her cheek touching the cold stone of the table in some small inn, with a notebook full of mad ravings and a paltry sum of Bits in front of her. The image spoke volumes of an angry-at-the-world outcast, down to her last bit of fortune.

“And here she is mere hours later, in the dark hours of the morning.”

Blue strands erupted from her back. She marched towards the burning brush of the Wicker Cradle like a marionette being dragged along the forest floor. Her eyes were glued to a blazing red book as her legs carried her forward without even looking up. The mangled branches of the burned forest parted for her, and sooty shadows escorted her from just beyond sight.

The strand narrowed in on her, and the image flickered as a wave of illness washed over Gio. Arseya’s strand began turning blue.

The Grand Weaver of Bangla made a wretching, angry noise as she cleaved the strand before it could progress further.

Queen Aayana moved forward, embracing her mother once again as her magic faded.

“Mother…” Queen Aayana said. “That magic…”

“[Fatebond].” Arseya confirmed. “The shame of our past. King Mahishasura’s… no, my father’s curse. The Cannibal King’s defilement.”

Chandrika gasped. “So it was our magic?!”
