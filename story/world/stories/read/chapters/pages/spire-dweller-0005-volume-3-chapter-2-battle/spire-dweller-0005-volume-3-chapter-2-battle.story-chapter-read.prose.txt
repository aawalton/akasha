Samantha swore. Not at the spiritual pain—the Ancient Cliff Drake’s miasma had been much worse than this—but at the way it felt like memories from her past lives were seeping in from cracks in an underwater room.

Her older brother laughed and ruffled her hair with a rough hand. “Get stronger first, then try to follow after me. I’ll be waiting.”

She looked down at the three letters on the table. One for betrothal. One for gifts. One for the wedding. The matchmaker had sworn to her parents that the pairing was compatible, but she felt nothing for her soon-to-be-spouse. The owner kicked her out of the bar and told her not to come back. She sighed. She’d need to find another place to (hopefully) drink herself to death. Another town, maybe.

A millennium’s worth of experiences threatened to flood in and wash her current sense of self away, but she didn’t panic. She’d already been trapped by a soul anchor technique once, so she knew at least one way she could try to get out of it. [Piercing Strike] had been merged into [Whirlwind], and she couldn’t access the active effects of the Ability in spectral form, but she could replicate the effects somewhat. She’d been working with her own custom arts long enough that simply coating her hands with qi was within her capabilities, inefficient as it would be.

As she pulled energy through her soul cord from her cultivation base, she wrapped it around her spectral hand. She didn’t bother with any fancy patterns to infuse the energy with an elemental characteristic. She only needed raw power. Aiming a knife hand attack at the grabbing strands, she forcefully swept her arm down. Upon contact the tentacles unexpectedly absorbed the energy, stinging her unprotected spirit as it harmlessly bounced off them.

More memories leaked in.

The soldier clung onto her uniform and wept. “It should have been me, Officer! Why? Why didn’t you send me instead?!” She looked down at her firstborn with pride. He was healthy, and strong. More beautiful than she could’ve dreamed of!

Samantha shook her head to clear it and directed the next knife hand at her own leg. If she could just cut her foot off above the ankle where the stinging ropes twisted around her… but that didn’t work either. Her body was, shockingly, too dense for her to slice through. Running out of ideas quickly, she reviewed the spirit beast’s description in search of something that could turn the tides.

Silas’s immersion in her senses suddenly deepened. This was not, as they had both done over the years, a mere visitation of the other’s faculties. This was something new. Something more intimate and vulnerable, and that would’ve left her feeling violated if it were anyone other than Silas who were doing it. As best she could describe it, he was stretching his awareness to inhabit the full breadth of her own. The boundaries between their individual selves became blurred.

<Dodge!> Silas warned.

Like instinct was speaking directly into her spirit, she felt the urge to weave left and twist. As she did so, a swiping tentacle zipped narrowly past. Her “gut” told her to duck to the right afterwards, so she ducked. Again, an attack missed her by a hair… an attack that she hadn’t noticed was incoming.

Samantha caught on, and smiled. <Watch my back, would you Silas?>

<Already on it!>

Trusting him, she allowed him to help her in one of the only ways he could from this distance: keeping watch through her own awareness while her focus was split. Though he couldn’t influence her movements directly, she obeyed the intentions he was sharing with her. Combined with her keeping a watchful gaze forward, Samantha was somehow managing to circumvent dozens of tendrils trying to strangle her. All the while, she was reading through the [Inspect] notification and picking it apart with the aid of [Hyperfocus].

Were her body not so dissimilar to his own, these defensive maneuvers might’ve been all but flawless. Alas, all it took was one misjudgement of distance before tentacles were wrapping themselves around her other ankle.

<Behind us!> Silas called.

This time, she refused his direction to duck the attack. Instead, she headed straight for the jellyfish’s spiritual body. She’d found it! A clue. A potential path to victory… if she could pull it off. As in-tune as they were with one another right now, mind and body, she didn’t need to explain to Silas what she planned to try next.

The tentacles which had thus far been slowing her down no longer pulled at her because she was finally going where they’d wanted her to: nearer the main body, that she might be consumed. However, she wasn’t about to make herself an easy dinner for the spirit beast. She just needed to buy herself a little distance (and time) to get the placements for her art just right.

One detail in the beast’s description stuck out in her mind: “The two bodies are joined by a central tentacle, through which they share the energy from prey ensnared by their stinging tendrils.” To her that sounded a lot like a soul cord, and she knew from personal experience what happened when the soul cord was severed. Though she wasn’t able to cut through the jellyfish’s dense spirit with her own, there was one art she possessed that might be able to. So, focusing beneath the spiritual enemy, Samantha began opening a rift.

She poured several hundred qi into the art at once, taking no chances as to how much energy would be needed to create a set of oversized portals. One of them she placed horizontally with the sea, like the jellyfish might fall into it after it opened. The second she arranged vertically to bisect the beast in the event the first portal didn’t do what she hoped. Though aiming for its physical body beneath the surface was an option, she could not see it in her qi sight, and was afraid to delve blindly into the ocean depths when there were already fearsome spiritual foes aboveground she was struggling with.

The jellyfish’s attacks redoubled in speed and intensity as the first tears in space appeared, as if it sensed the danger she posed. All its tendrils lifted up and slithered toward her like a seething mass of snakes, leaving only the tether to its physical body unmoved.

No, no, no! Not now! she silently pleaded, but time waited for no one, and neither did her past.

“Avast!” The captain shouted at her. He stared up at the beast which shadowed their vessel with awe and fear. “Greater crews than ours sunk after firing ‘pon the Tyrant, lass. Fetch the offering instead.”

By the time she emerged from the invasive memory, it was too late. Most of her body had been cocooned in the creature’s tendrils such that there was only a small gap through which she could perceive the outside world. She witnessed her rifts opening, the jellyfish shuddering, the severed ends of its tentacles drifting down like dandelion seeds, and its disparate halves falling into the ocean… but the stinging strands did not dissipate. Nor did they stop poisoning her. She felt downright paralyzed and, losing control over her flight, was soon tumbling toward the ocean alongside her toppled foe. This far from land, she feared what would be waiting for her beneath the glittering surface. How far would she sink before she saw for herself what so-called “fearsome denizens” lurked in the depths?

Just before she struck the water someone grabbed hold of her. “Cor blimey! Ye still tellin’ tales?” the man asked.

She didn’t understand the question, but considering the unknown projectionist was gripping a fist full of the poisonous tentacles to keep her out of the ocean, she strained until she forced herself to make a sound. The man began urgently freeing her, saying quite a few phrases which, despite being unfamiliar, possessed the distinct cadence of expletives in the process. Thankfully, the less covered she was with stingers the less paralyzed she became, and she was able to aid in removing the final tentacles. Silas, feeling more secure about her immediate safety, pulled back on the degree with which he shared senses with her, though he still watched and listened to everything going on.

“Thank you,” she told the stranger earnestly.

It took mental effort not to stare too hard at the man, for he was unlike anyone she’d ever seen before. He was close to half a foot taller than she was, skinny, had red hair, and green eyes. His scruffy beard had streaks running through it that almost looked orange, which made it resemble a burning flame. Though he appeared to be in his late teens, he didn’t carry the uncertainty or inexperience with him that actual teenagers often had.

“Impressive as it be ye took down a Yang, t’would be better for ye to not need thank a savior afterward. Did yer sect not teach ye which beasties to avoid?” he questioned.

She shook her head slowly. “I’m not part of a… ‘sect’, was it? I’ve just ascended.”

“Aye?” He looked her over more closely. In particular, he focused on the direction her soul cord was heading. “In that case, yer lucky me crew’s sailing near if ye landed where I think. Only one isle’s that way, and naught else for leagues around.”

Samantha thought she knew where this was going, so broached the topic first. “What do you want in exchange for picking me and my companions up? There’s six of us, including myself, two spirit beasts, and two constructs.”

“Nay, we want nothing from ye.” Seeing the skepticism on her face, he added, “‘Tis part of The Code to take stranded ascendants aboard… and the sects pay us commission for bringin’ ye ashore to them besides. Work ‘round the boat to earn yer keep as we sail, and ye’ll owe us naught upon reaching safe harbor.”

Even with his reassurance, she wasn’t completely convinced. Though, it wasn’t as though her group had a lot of alternatives right now…

Amusement danced across his features. “Tell ye what. Lay yer deadlights on The Mariner’s Shanty yerself afore agreeing to stand on ‘er deck. I’ll show ye ‘round the ol’ girl meself.”

Samantha’s eyes narrowed slightly as she struggled to parse the unknown words’ meanings, but before she made much progress Silas gave her the psychic equivalent of a tap on the shoulder. <Is he speaking Common? You’re responding, but I can’t understand him at all.>

<Not exactly,> she sent. <It’s a dialect, or an accent. There’s enough overlap with the common tongue that I get the gist of what he’s saying, but I’m not catching everything either.>

She’d occasionally heard a similar style of speech used in memories from her previous lives as well, so the language had a certain familiarity to it even though she didn’t completely understand it. It brought up an interesting question, though. As she got more distant from the first floor during her ascent, would language and culture become less recognizable to her?

The man’s laughter pulled her from her thoughts. “Deadlights,” he explained, pointing to his eyes. “The Mariner’s Shanty be the name of our sailing vessel. Her ‘deck’ be her top floor, where ye can stare out across The Blue.” As he said those last words, he made a sweeping gesture toward the expansive ocean. “Ye’ll pick up our speak smartly enough, with time.”

She gave him an uncertain nod. “Sure. I think I’d like to see The Mariner’s Shanty. Should I retrieve the jellyfish beast core before we go?”

“Not unless ye be seekin’ a watery grave, lass. And ye’d need a way to kill it true, first.”

“It’s dead already, isn’t it? You even said so earlier.”

“I said ye took down its Yang, not killed it. Don’t get me wrong, ye wounded it fiercely, but its Yin remains. Won’t be long ‘till a new Yang be formed, neither! ‘Tis why ye’ll be wantin’ to join a sect. The knowledge they offer will keep ye alive better than chance.”

She stared down at the sea for a long moment. “Is a sect like a guild? What sect are you a part of?”

There was a pause before he answered. “I couldn’t say. I’ve joined no guild, and sects are only for ascendants.” He began to float in the same direction his spiritual tether was leading off to and motioned for her to follow. As she trailed after him, he continued. “Don’t worry yer head about ‘em yet. Ye’ll hear more about sects than ye can stomach once we drop anchor at the Choosing Grounds. For now, let’s be off before a fresh Yang appears, and I’ll introduce ye to the finest vessel south of Port Windfall.”

She traded names and chatted with the projectionist she now knew as Jack Rogers on the brief flight over. When they arrived, he grandly announced the ship as though he was revealing some hidden wonder of the world.

“Behold, The Mariner’s Shanty! Two-hundred-fifty feet long and forty feet wide, and every inch of her fair as a wench’s bountiful—” Jack hesitated and glanced over at Samantha before concluding his sentence. “—beauty! That be to say… she’s a beautiful craft.”

The corner of Samantha’s mouth quirked upward. Regardless of how he intended to describe the boat originally, she could agree with Jack that it had a certain charm and loveliness to it. It was made primarily of light colored wood and had three tall masts stretching up from the deck. The front two masts had sails which were hung horizontally and perpendicular to the long centerline of the boat, and the rear mast had its sails parallel to the centerline and stretched completely behind it on angled poles. In other words, two of its masts were rigged square, and the third was rigged fore-and-aft.

“She’s a fine barque, indeed,” Samantha complimented. It was only as she was saying it that she realized she didn’t know how she knew what the rigging on the masts were called, or that this type of boat was called a barque… but she did. The knowledge had come to her naturally, too, like it’d always been there.

Jack beamed with pride at the praise and led her to an area inside the ship where his body was meditating. The room he sat in had rows of empty cloth hammocks hanging from the ceiling and small wooden chests lined up along the walls. At once, the term “forecastle” came to her mind.

“I need to report to the Cap’n—she’ll nag me ears off if I’m late to another check-in—but I’ll be back to projecting and guiding ye in no time,” he informed her.

“No need, you can show me in person.”

He looked like he was about to ask for clarification when Samantha manifested a physical form in the room. Though she couldn’t see his projection as clearly through Qi Sight, the human-shaped mass of qi floating in the air appeared shocked. In the next breath the projection vanished and Jack stood. He reached toward her as if he were going to try to pass a hand through her, so Samantha extended her own hand and shook his.

“Huh!” He turned his wrist so her translucent hand faced upward and patted the back of it experimentally. “Sink me! Aren’t ye full of surprises? Come along as I give me report, why don’t ye? Else me crewmates might think ye a ghost.”

They both headed out of the room and through a narrow hall. Jack looked to be a couple inches shy of six feet tall, but he had to bend down slightly as they walked under supporting beams because the ceilings were so low. Even at 5’6” Samantha felt a bit cramped in the space, though she didn’t ever need to bend as Jack did.

“How large a crew does a ship this size need?” she asked as they climbed a steep set of stairs to the main deck.

“With the enchantments our lady has? Less than ye’d think. Just ten mortals could sail her if they be skilled enough.” He offered her a hand up the last couple of steps and grinned at seeing she was appropriately impressed by that news. “If a cultivator be manning the barque they could do it alone, but we still keep a crew of ten. More, if we can. The Cap’n has to rest sometime, and ‘tis easier to fight off the things aimin’ to make a meal of ye with some trusted mates guardin’ yer rear.”

Various men and women stationed across the main deck turned to observe Samantha as she and Jack made their way to the back—or stern—of the boat. Seeing Jack speaking comfortably with her, they lowered their heads in silent greeting or offered up short waves before returning to their various jobs.

The stern of the boat had a raised section of deck that created a small room. Following Jack through the singular door to the chamber, Samantha gawked at what she saw inside.

Several illusory replicas of The Mariner’s Shanty were suspended in the middle of the chamber, with each version showing something different. On one, crewmates’ positions were displayed as tiny green figures, the figure representing Samantha glowed purple, and dots clinging in bunches to the underside of the vessel were orange. On another replica, the rigging and sails of the ship were flashing with colors that shifted second to second. The tip of a large, fish-like fin could be seen swiftly passing through the waters a short distance beneath the boat on yet another version. Script-engraved instruments of all shapes and sizes were mounted on the walls.

A serious-looking woman sitting at a desk near the back wall was alternating between evaluating the images of the ship and the enchanted tools around the room. Similar to Tobias, she possessed dark brown hair and blue eyes—though her irises were closer in color to the deep hues of the sea than that of summer skies. Her demeanor became markedly more cheery and easygoing when she finally set her eyes on the two of them.

“Jack! You always bring back interesting reports, but I already think this might be the most interesting yet. Who might our guest be?”

<I can understand this one!> Silas excitedly told Samantha. She was nearly as relieved as Silas to discover not everyone on this floor spoke in the strange dialect.

“This be Samantha, an ascendant of the Cray blood. Samantha, this be Cordelia of the Rogers family, the beloved ‘n half-mad Cap’n of The Mariner’s Shanty.” Jack introduced the two women before jumping into his report. “I spotted ‘er tangling with a Yin Yang and—”

“Don’t tell me—!”

Cordelia seemed relieved to hear that. “How much time do you need to recover from the poison?”

Jack shook his head. “Me hands are a bit tingly still, is all. I’m fit for duty.”

The captain looked him up and down carefully before speaking again. “I’ll take your word for it. So, why have you only brought back her spirit? Something happen to the rest of her?”

“Nay. She and ‘er group are marooned on Marisol. The lass wanted to check we wouldn’t rob or gut ‘em afore accepting the offer to pick ‘em up.”

Samantha struggled not to appear outwardly as mortified as that too-blunt explanation made her feel inwardly. She was only glad the statement made Cordelia laugh instead of offending her.

“Bold enough to challenge a Yin Yang alone, but not quite enough to sail with strangers,” she playfully remarked as she met Samantha’s gaze. “Well, what do you think now that you’ve seen us? Shall we sail to Marisol and welcome you and yours aboard?”

Samantha reasoned that if these people were truly ill intentioned then they wouldn’t respect her refusal anyway. She’d accept for now, and prepare for their first in-person meeting in accordance with what she learned about them. “I’d still like to finish my tour of the ship, but you don’t seem like a bad sort. We’d be grateful for your help getting off the island.”

“‘Atta girl!” Cordelia cheered, extending her arm toward Samantha. Samantha was slightly caught off guard when, instead of shaking hands, Cordelia only gripped her forearm firmly. She let go once Samantha did the same to her. “Feel free to look around and speak to anyone you’d like. I’d help with the tour, but I need to keep an eye on things here.”

Samantha bowed low at the waist in respect. “I’ll take you up on that.”
