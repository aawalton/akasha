As soon as Ezra made the choice to try to push himself at the barrier between Initiate and Adept, he loosened his hold on his inner world. He hadn’t realized that he was holding so tightly to the aura there, and as soon as his attention weakened, the discomfort inside swelled tenfold. Instead of just feeling full, the strain of the aura looking to expand became demanding, forcing him to struggle to maintain control over his inner world. There was a faint tearing sensation inside as the winds swirled around, and Ezra groaned in pain.

He was used to pain. It’d long since become a companion to him. The pain of starvation, of beatings, of cold, of thirst, abandonment, hatred, exclusion, and everything else. The pain of his inner world revolting against him was a suffering more exquisite and overwhelming than Ezra had ever experienced before. Instead of the hunger that made his vision swim and his rationality fade, this was as if his soul were breaking. Instead of a physical or emotional suffering, his aura shredding through whatever made him Ezra was profound and inexplicable, and he barely realized when he toppled to the side.

You were holding tight, huh, kid? Jormun’s voice cut through the pressing agony. I’m glad you decided to try right now, then. I should have figured this would happen. Damn geniuses making trouble for themselves.

Ezra didn’t manage to say anything back at that, just groaning as he tried to roll onto his back. The movement was slow, and once he was laying down, he didn’t feel any better.

“What… now?” he asked as he fought to keep back any whimpers.

You can let it slowly do this, to tear at the boundaries until it’s done. Might just exhaust itself before that, though. If that happens, well… it fucking sucks. You’ll have suffered like this for no reason, because by the time you’re ready to try this again, the inner world’s walls will have repaired themselves.

“Other… option?”

You push the aura at the boundaries. Force them to break. This’ll hurt more, but is more likely to make the breakthrough than just waiting.

Ezra went silent at the thought of more suffering, his soul shrinking back if he had to feel it get worse. But the thought of this all being for nothing was even worse, so, with a thought, he mentally commanded the aura to press against its surroundings. The “walls” that already struggled to contain everything that Ezra’d stuffed inside himself strained and screamed, and Ezra himself bit down on a weak groan of pain.

Still laying down, he wasn’t even sure if he was still breathing as he commanded the winds within to rip through everything in their path. The taste of blood filled his mouth as he bit his tongue, but still he pressed the aura onward. As the tearing sensation grew stronger, Ezra felt his aura falter. There wasn’t enough of it, was there?

“No,” he breathed aloud, a thought occurring to him as he groaned and rolled on the ground. Without thinking further, he stretched his awareness deeper into the ground under him and started pulling that aura into himself, stripping the affinity from it as he struggled to transform the now formless aura into wind.

Fucking hell kid, don’t do that! You—

Jormun’s warnings faded as Ezra struggled to keep up with the aura inside himself as well as what he was pulling in at the same time. They overwhelmed him, and he groaned as he failed to master the ways that he was stretching himself. His inner world screamed for release, but Ezra refused to give up, to go through this a second time. With another roar of effort, he forced the winds to continue beating on their bonds while hoping that that would keep his inner world from repairing itself too quickly. The winds inside continued tearing at himself, and, for a frightening second, he forgot his name.

That didn’t matter. Names didn’t mean anything. Only stopping this pain.

He could stop trying, that would fix things.

No. Giving. Up. That would make it happen again.

Why would he try this?

His mind, frayed and wandering, seized on that as an escape, and started converting that aura without hesitation. The formless aura that he drew in was good. It needed to be good. It would stop the pain. It started to hurt more, though? But he knew he needed more. And so, he pulled even more aura into himself. He couldn’t say why that was, why he was so sure, but he kept doing it, converting it and ignoring the voice that told him to stop and accept his failure.

“No,” he growled aloud, and as he did so, something inside of him snapped, and the agony that he’d suffered through spiked to a blinding white. Consciousness faded, and he could see a shadowy figure in front of himself.

It was a man, his face shadowy. He wore armor, fancy stuff. Everything but his face was covered with tightly tailored steel plates, a sword on his hip, a shield in his hand, and a spear on his back. Arrows flew at him and were blown away, a sword clanged uselessly off his armor, and a small figure huddled beside him, looking for safety. Attacks came the child’s way, and he lowered his shield, protecting  them against a hail of blows, never breaking. When an overwhelming force appeared, the man flashed where he stood and suddenly there was nothing. Only wind remained where he’d once been, but a blade fell from the sky and cleaved the attacker in two.

Then the man looked down at him, his face seeming to hold high expectations.

And with that, Ezra remembered himself. That feeling of not being himself now terrified him, and he began hyperventilating as he sat up. Uncontrollable shivers shook him as he remembered who he was. Yes, that was painful, but throwing his aura at the borders of his inner world had wiped away his self-awareness for who knew how long. Looking around, nobody was in the tent, so either he hadn’t been as loud as he’d thought, or it hadn’t been long enough for anyone to come to check on him. Or nobody cared to check, which wouldn’t matter to him either.

Wrapping his arms around himself, Ezra fought to control the shaking that pulsed through his body. If he—

Ezra!

Jormun’s shouted voice pulled him from his worrying, and Ezra looked up at him. “I’m not a rock, I can hear you. What?”

What’s my name?

“You?” Ezra cocked his head to the side, confusion overpowering every other emotion. “You  gone senile old man?”

Answer the question. Jormun’s words shook Ezra to his core, somehow becoming unfathomably powerful and making Ezra’s vision shake as if the earth itself was rumbling underfoot.

“Jormun,” Ezra said, trying to keep his voice low and out of being noticed by anyone who happened to be close.

And your name?

“Ezra. I’m a kid from the slums, you suddenly appeared two months ago? What’s going on?” Ezra answered, failing to keep his voice as low as he wanted.

You fucking fool, Jormun’s voice cut through his questions. You brainless buffoon. Fucking splitting your focus on your first attempt at breaching the barrier between Initiate and Adept? It’s a miracle that you’re alive. I wouldn’t be surprised if you managed to cripple yourself. A fucking genius? What a joke. Just accept a loss when it comes your way! It’s not a shame to be unprepared.

“What are you talking about?” Ezra asked, his throat swelling closed.

I’m saying that it’s a miracle you can still remember yourself, Jormun shouted. You need to maintain a strong sense of self while you’re breaking through to the next tier, and you threw your sense of self away in pursuit of progress. Like a fucking idiot. If you hadn’t come back, then you wouldn’t be anything more than a vegetable.

“And whose responsibility was it to tell me what I should do?” Ezra demanded back, forgetting to keep his voice low. “You just told me to force it! To break my inner world open, and that I’d be fine, if only a bit miserable from pain. I could have lost my mind? You’re the fucking Master, I’m a kid who knows nothing. Why would you think  I should know anything?”

I didn’t say to forget everything in the hope of breaking through, Jormun hissed. I didn’t think you had a prayer of breaking through, and you didn’t. So, instead of taking your lumps and coming back to it in a week, you decided that, while altering your very self, the core of what makes you you, you’d disassociate from yourself? That you’d throw it all away?

“Of course I didn’t think that,” Ezra scoffed, his voice more bitter than he intended. “I don’t know anything about this. I assumed that what you told me was the truth and that I could trust you. Guess that makes me the fucking idiot, yeah.”

Jormun’s mouth and jaw moved furiously under his jaw, then, after taking a deep breath, he nodded. You’re right, he admitted. I have decades of training with training people, but the people I’ve trained have always been at least Adepts, usually Pseudo-Knights. They were trained from a young age to walk on the path to Sainthood, and though that may have impeded some paths, their teachers and parents taught them plenty about what to do and what not to do. I forget how little you know sometimes.

At that, Jormun went silent, and Ezra, unable to help himself, demanded, “And the apology for almost making me forget literally everything?”

I didn’t make you do that, Jormun protested, though his protests weren’t so fierce as before. He took a deep breath and said, Sorry, kid. I fucked up.

Ezra nodded, and the spiking fear that’d driven him for the past couple of minutes began to drain a bit. Even so, the adrenaline that’d flooded his system remained, and his hands shook as he took breaths to try to calm himself. There were only a few seconds that he had for himself before Petr and Amy rushed into the tent, the flaps snapping through the air as they met his eyes.

“What happened?” Petr demanded. “Why were you shouting? What was happening with all that wind?”

Biting his lip, Ezra felt a flash of shame and tried to gather himself, to come up with an explanation. Then, without much time to come up with an excuse, he settled on telling as much truth as he could.

“I was trying to break through to Adept. It hurt a lot, and I was complaining, I guess,” Ezra shrugged, feeling his face heat. "I'm not sure what was going on with the wind, as you're saying. Was there wind?"

Petr said nothing as Amy’s jaw dropped. “You’re trying to break through? That’s why you have blood coming out of your nose? What did you do? Did it even work?”

Only realizing then that he hadn’t even thought about it, Ezra disregarded her question about blood, he’d tasted it and wasn’t surprised to hear it was somewhere else as well. Instead, he just spread his awareness into his inner world, though not without a good amount of fear. After all, he’d just royally fucked up, and the thought of seeing his inner world after he’d managed to wreck it made him want to just run away. Instead, he squared his shoulders and looked in.

The sight he was greeted by wasn’t what he expected. Instead of ruins or some remnant of his failure, or even the same sight that he’d had before, what he saw could only be the results of his success. Instead of just a faint image of a swirling wind, Ezra’s inner world was now a storming cloud, so dark as to nearly be black. Wind whipped through it, sending tendrils of mist stretching out in every direction, though the cloud still pulled itself back in. The mass of clouds wasn’t constricted by the bounds of his inner world any longer, and Ezra could feel that there was something about the quality of his aura that’d changed.

“Ezra! What’s wrong?” Amy’s voice cut through his self-investigation, and Ezra shook his head. Looking up at her, he smiled.

“Nothing. I think that I managed to become an Adept.”
