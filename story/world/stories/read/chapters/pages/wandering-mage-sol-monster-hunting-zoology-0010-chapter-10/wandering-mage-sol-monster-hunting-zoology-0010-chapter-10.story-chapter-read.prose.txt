“Oh my gods! This is so great!” Tressa squealed with delight as she moved around the giant minotaur, staring openly at his thick, furred legs before suddenly dropping to the ground in front of his hooves.

Briadon stiffened. “What is she doing?”

She quickly sketched the split hoof planted in the mud and made a note of the texture and color. “Sooo cool!” she whispered in Elvish, reaching out to tap one hoof with her knuckle. “Forked hooves. Dense outer material. Similar to cattle, but larger. Much larger.”

Briadon’s tail flicked behind him, and Tressa’s attention snapped to it. She leaned to the side to peer around his leg, noticing the hole cut into the back of his pants to let the tail through. “Hehehehe!” She giggled excitedly as she stood, then inspected his hairy chest, reaching towards it.

“H-h-hey!” he snapped as he stepped back, grabbing her by the wrist and pushing her away.

“Tressa!” Lisha said as she caught the elf’s shoulders. “He doesn't like being touched.”

“But!” She said, trying to understand and think of the words for the common tongue.

“Thick! Uhh cow man! Research!”

“I'm not something to be studied!” he roared.

“This elf is truly unusual. I've only met a few but none who are so… hmmm…” Lisha folded her arms and tilted her head as if studying the elf girl.

The minotaur pointed at his head and said, “Perhaps she is a traveling scholar and hit her head in that fall and now lost her senses.”

“I suppose this is possible. Poor thing,” Lisha agreed, looking down and shaking her head sympathetically at the shorter girl, who was muttering to herself in Elvish and writing something in her notebook.

Tressa’s gaze suddenly snapped up to Lisha as she pointed at her. “You?” She made a drawing gesture with her hand. “Draw? Note taking?”

Lisha seemed surprised, but she let out a low chuckle. “Well… I did agree in exchange for helping us find the Farron Moths, but I didn’t think you meant right now.”

Tressa jumped up excitedly and started looking around on the ground and digging through her pockets, muttering in Elvish.

“Lisha, we don’t have time for this,” Briadon said. “We need her to help us find the Farron Moth, not write an entire book about my hooves!”

Lisha’s tongue flicked past her scaled lips as the elf circled around her and started taking notes, giggling just as excitedly as she had when doing this exact thing to Briadon. “You’re right…” Lisha said, and caught Tressa by the shoulder as the small elf girl came around her side again.

Tressa looked up at her, confused.

“I’m sorry, but we really must get moving now,” Lisha explained.

“No! Can’t go!” Tressa complained. “Research!” She puffed her cheeks, pouting up at the lizard woman.

Lisha said, “Tonight. When we camp.” She folded her hands on the side of her head as if sleeping. “After we find the Farron Moth, you can take as many notes as she wish.”

Tressa’s smile brightened as she made the hand gesture again and said, “Okay!” She then returned her sketchbook to the satchel around her shoulder, banished her mana quill and snagged her staff off the tree she had leaned it again. A slight pulse went along the staff’s gem as she cast a spell, and her feet lifted a few inches off the ground. “Farron Moth! You follow!” she said and started floating ahead of them.

“Uhg. Finally,” Briadon muttered, hefting his club up onto his shoulder as he and Lisha followed the floating elf girl.

***

Tressa limped along whenever the terrain became easier, as she wanted to conserve her mana.

“So how long will this take?” Briadon asked about an hour or so later.

Tressa paused, trying to understand. She knew he had said “long” and “take.” Maybe he was asking her for the distance to the moths?

She sat down and took a drink of water from the canteen they’d offered her, and her stomach gave a loud grumble. In truth, she had hardly eaten the last few days. And that probably was adding to her mana fatigue.

Lisha laughed, reached into her pouch, pulled out an apple and cheese, then handed them to Tressa.

“Sorry, all I can spare for now. We need to conserve supplies for our trip back.”

Tressa nodded. She gratefully took the food and ate the apple quickly. She had finished the rolls and butter that Narella had given her.

“So how long?” Briadon asked again, leaning down.

“Long?” Tressa asked, playing with the word on her tongue.

“Yes long,” Briadon said.

Tressa swallowed and looked confused.

“Farron Moths not long,” she said, making an extended gesture with her hands. She then brought her hands closer together and made out the shape of their oval bodies. “Like this.”

Oh, wait. Maybe he is asking about their wingspan?

“Gah, no! Travel. Distance. Long.” He snapped and snorted as his dark eyes stared at her.

Tressa made an O with her mouth as she understood.

“Ummm,” she said, trying to translate the words for half a day into the common tongue. She tapped her chin.

“Butt,” she said, hoping that was the word.

“Butt?” Lisha asked.

Tressa nodded.

“Um, no.”

“Butt? Not word?”

“Well… it is a word…” Lisha scratched a claw under her leathery chin.

Briadon let out a deep bellow of laughter, and Tressa stared, confused.

Standing up, Lisha pointed at her butt under where her tail connected and said the word. “Butt.”

Tressa instantly turned a deep red as she realized her mistake. She covered her face in her hands and let out a groan of embarrassment, which just sent the two laughing harder.

“So, Tressa. How long?” Lisha asked after she was able to get her laughter under control.

She looked around, trying to think. If butt wasn't the word, then she had no clue. After a moment, she pointed at the sun that was still rising high into the sky as it was closer to noon. Then she bent down, picked up a stick, and broke it.

“Sun break?” Briadon asked, looking deeply confused as he stroked his braided beard.

Tressa shook her head and pointed at the stick that was broken, then at the sun.

“I don't get it,” Briadon said. “She just broke a stick and pointed at the sun. Maybe she did knock a few brains loose from that fall.”

“Briadon!” Lisha snapped and slugged him again.

“Hey!”

Lisha then turned and looked at Tressa and the broken stick, then at the sky.

Tressa picked up both sides and held them together. “Full.” She separated them. “Not.”

“Hmmm, are you trying to say half a day?” Lisha asked after a silence.

Tressa tilted her head, not knowing the words, but nodded anyway, hoping that Lisha had gotten it.

“Half a day?” Briadon muttered, still stroking his beard.

“I think so. Gah! This so hard. I don't know any elvish, and she is doing her damn best to speak to us.”

“Hmmm,” Briadon said as he picked up the stick. “Maybe draw a half sun for her?”

Lisha's head whipped to look at him so fast they all heard her neck crack.

“Briadon, you are a gods damn genius!” she said.

He snorted. “About time you realized it.”

Tressa tilted her head, not catching anything they said, and wondered why Lisha looked so excited. ”Gods?” she repeated.

“Tressa!”

She jumped at her name being almost yelled to her.

“Half day,” she said and drew a half sun in dirt.

“Half day. Half sun?”

Tressa knew the word sun. “Sun.” She said and made a breaking gesture as if she was breaking a stick in half again.

“Yes! Half day.”

A smile crossed her face as she finished the cheese. “Yes.”

“There we got it!” Lisha said excitedly. “Thanks, Bridon!"

“Yeah, you're welcome.” He chuckled, scratching between his ear and horn.

With that, they stood, and Tressa led them north as they trekked along on a game trail till they came to the river Tressa had jumped into the day prior.

She pointed at the steep cliff high above them. “Umm, go!”

“Up that!?” Briadon looked horrified. “I can't climb that.”

Lisha looked up the cliff as well. There was no way they could climb it. The sheer steepness of it was unbelievable. She looked back at Tressa and pointed to her staff.

“Can you fly us up?”

“Fly?”

“Yes,” Lisha said, pointing at herself, then to Tressa.

Tressa shook her head, catching what she meant. “No. Um not more, ummm me.”

“You've only ever done just you, nothing else?”

Tressa nodded, trying to piece together the words. She felt like it was a never-ending circle of them trying to understand one another, as it was the same all over and over.

“What about my rope?” Briadon asked.

Lisha shook her head. “It’s not nearly long enough. Maybe we could find a spot halfway up and wait for her to bring it even higher… but I still don’t like the idea without proper climbing gear or safety lines.”

“Is there a way around the cliff so we don't have to climb? A bridge?”

Tressa looked at her and nodded. She pointed downstream. “Bridge.”

“So she knows bridge, huh?” Briadon said.

She did know the word bridge since there were plenty of small ones within the walls of Falidorn, the word came up easily while talking to the merchants. And while she wasn’t entirely familiar with this area of the forest, she remembered spotting a bridge when she was chasing the bird. However, she was sure it’d be a fairly long walk.

Tressa shook her head. “Half day.” Then she spread her arms wide. “Long.”

“So it’ll take longer to get there. I suppose there is no choice, however,” Lisha said.

Tressa began leading the way once more. She just hoped nobody from Falidorn searched too far out for her, as they would be getting closer to the city by taking this route to the Farron Moth nesting ground.

***

Two hours later, they stood in front of a rickety old rope bridge. The planks looked rotten and ready to fall apart at any second with the river hundreds of feet below them.

“Um, Tressa, there is no way this is safe,” Lisha said.

“I doubt it will hold my weight,” Briadon said. “That rope looks ready to snap, let alone the wooden planks… they are clearly rotted.”

Tressa looked at them, then at the bridge. She had no clue it would be in such bad shape. All she had seen was the bridge above the river when she flew over. Not the shape it was in. She stepped on it first. The board gave a creek under her weight. She frowned than used the regular levitation spell, easily hovering her feet above as she floated across, holding loosely to the rope as she went.

She reached the other side and looked back to see Lisha now stepping onto the bridge. She grimaced as it swayed. “You have got to be kidding me! I hate heights!”

“You got this, Lisha! Just don’t look down.” Briadon boomed.

“Will you shut the fuck up!” she screamed as her legs shook. “I don’t need to be reminded of how high up I am. Gods, I’m going to die.”

But she kept going. Halfway across, a plank broke as she stepped, and it fell to the river far below them. Lisha gave out a startled yelp as her foot went through.

“Lisha!” Tressa called in alarm.

“I'm fine! I'm almost there. I'm okay!” she said more to herself than either of them.

Finally, after about five excruciating minutes, she reached the other side and took Tressa's outstretched hand and collapsed onto the ground, breathing heavily.

“Never make me do that again!”

Tressa laughed, not really understanding, but knew Lisha was glad to be back on solid ground.

They watched as Briadon carefully put one hoof on the first plank, and it let out a massive groan.

“I don’t think this thing is going to hold me!” he called, pulling his hoof back. He had his massive club on his back for carrying and that added extra weight to his already massive size.

Tressa paused and reached into her satchel, digging through for her other notebook which had some of her rune research. She was trying to think if there was anything she could concoct quickly that might hold the bridge together just long enough for him to get across.

“What are you doing?” Lisha asked.

“Spell. Bridge strong. Help,” she said in the broken common tounge.

“You think you can do that?”

Tressa held up her hand, palm flat, and shook it back and forth to show she was unsure.

“What's she doing?” Briadon called as Tressa flipped through her books.

“I think she's trying to find a spell to hold up the bridge.”

“Well, I hope she has something!”

Lisha nodded and turned back to Tressa.

Tressa bit her lip and searched frantically, flipping page after page of her book.

There was that spell to lighten her bookcase. But that wasn't really the same thing. Using Lavant she could potentially ease some of his weight by him grabbing onto her but she honestly doubted it would be worth much compared to his massive size.

Trying to make a new spell was out of the question as it would require some deep thought and experimentation and she wasn't going to experiment when a person's life could be at risk.

After three minutes she sat back and shook her head.

“No.”

“Damn.”

“So did she find anything?”

“No!” Lisha called back.

“Damn elf,” Briadon growled.

“Just take it very slowly,” Lisha called to him. “It’s not very far. Even if it begins to fall apart, it is not as though it will all happen at once if you don’t make sudden movements.”

“Oh yeah, that makes me feel a whole lot better!” he growled across.

Taking a deep breath, he placed one hoof on again and gripped the rope tightly. He flinched at the creak of the board and placed his other hoof as he began steadily walking across. One slow, careful step at a time. But no matter how carefully he stepped, each plank groaned beneath his weight until—

A plank broke under his weight, and he stumbled, using the rope to catch himself.

Tressa let out a worried cry as she watched. She summoned her staff and thought intensely, trying to find some way to help. Looking at his club. How heavy was that? It was so thick, and the wood was obviously dense, she imagined it might have been a hundred pounds. She turned to Lisha.

“Weapon!”

“Huh?” Lisha asked. “Weapon, what about weapon?”

Tressa pointed at Briadon and made a tossing gesture. Then pointed at herself and Lisha. “Weapon.” She made a whistling sound to show she wanted Lisha to tell Briadon to toss his weapon to them.

Lisha nodded, then yelled, “Briadon! You're too heavy!”

“Oh, you fucking think so? I didn’t notice!” he yelled. “This thing won’t hold me, damn it!” A rope snapped to his left, causing the bridge to jerk downward. He hollered out, tensing himself and closing his eyes, but the bridge stayed up.

“Throw your club! It's adding to your weight!” Lisha called.

“I’m not throwing it away!”

“No, you beef brain! Throw it at us!”

“You fucking sure that's a good idea? The bridge might break out from under me!” He yelled, his legs shaking. He was nearly halfway across, far too late to turn back, and the bridge was groaning even more.

“Just do it!” Lisha snapped. “It’s an extra hundred pounds you don’t need right now!”

With a grunt, he struggled to get the club loose from its holster on his back. He accidentally put too much weight on one hoof, and his leg broke through.

“SHIT!” he roared, bracing himself and finding a new spot of good wood to place it.Then, with a deep breath and a careful underhanded toss, he threw the club. It sailed through the air, Tressa watching in fascination at his strength of being able to toss something so heavy and so far so casually.

It hit the ground several feet from Lisha with a thud, and Tressa couldn’t help walking over to see if it really weighed a hundred pounds. Sure enough, she could barely lift it an inch with all of her strength.

But the bridge seemed to almost sigh at the relief of losing the extra weight.

Tressa bit her thumb and watched as Briadin steadily moved. Each creak gave her more and more anxiety.

“Come on, just a little farther! You're past the halfway point!” Lisha called.

With each careful step, he got closer and closer.

But then, both Tressa and Briadon's ears twitched as they heard the sound of snapping rope.

Briadon looked behind him, and if minotaurs could pale, he did as he saw one of the main lines of rope snap.

The bridge instantly pitched to one side, the horizontal planks twisting beneath him. Briadon lurched with it, grabbing desperately for the remaining rope as his hooves slammed against the tilted boards.

“Gah!” he cried.

“BRIADON!” Lisha and Tressa screamed.

His full weight hit the remaining side of the bridge; the rope strained, then it snapped too.

The bridge broke in half, dropping out from under him and swinging him toward the jagged rock face of the cliff.

Tressa ran forward and leapt off the cliff without a second thought.

She thrust out her staff and cast a wind spell on instinct, trying to slow Briadon down as he swung toward the cliffside. The air caught around him, slowing him just enough that he wouldn’t hit the rocks at full force.

Then Tressa focused on another spell, but something felt wrong with her mana. By the time “Lavant!” slipped from her lips, she realized her mistake.

Her body began to drop, and she could only stare at the ravine hundreds of feet below.

“Lavant!” she screamed again, but again nothing happened.

She was out of mana, and she continued to fall.
