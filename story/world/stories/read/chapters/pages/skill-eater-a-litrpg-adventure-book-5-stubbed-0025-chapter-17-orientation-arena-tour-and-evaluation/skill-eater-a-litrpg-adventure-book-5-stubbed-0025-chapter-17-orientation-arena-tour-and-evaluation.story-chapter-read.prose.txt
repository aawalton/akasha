Krix led the applicants out of the annex and into the central structure.

A visceral thrill ran through Edge when he realized what was happening. He saw his excitement mirrored in Mel’s eyes as they followed the official down several flights of stairs, through a preparation area, and onto the fighting floor.

Krix smiled at their expressions, letting them breathe in the ambiance of standing in the center ring before beginning their orientation. It was exhilarating, even without spectators filling the seats above.

He had them form a circle a few minutes later and provided an in-depth explanation of how the ladder system worked. Krix outlined the differences between the main and specialty ladders, which included everything from weapons like polearms and swords to categories for fighters who used spells as their primary killing tool.

Edge already knew most of the information, but he listened carefully as the man detailed the arena’s seasons, challenge system, and exhibition matches, along with the intricacies of the magic protecting gladiators from being killed during the bouts.

After that, they were given a tour of the facilities the arena had to offer. The quality and cost of various services was determined by your rung in the main ladder. Everyone got access to discounted medical care and gear repairs. Higher ranks offered perks like priority access to trainers, VIP sections at the best bars and restaurants, and sponsorships from various factions. Even things like merchandising could be quite lucrative to popular gladiators, and he chuckled at the idea of having posters and action figures with his image being sold to cheering fans.

When the tour was over, it was time for the evaluation matches. First, the prospective gladiators were divided by stage, then placed into groups of early, middle, or late. The handful of stage-three applicants would go through a different process past this point. They were led into another annex while everyone else was getting organized.

Edge was placed in the late stage-two group and Mel was in the middle, which meant they were heading to different floors and would meet up a little later. They clasped hands, wished each other luck, and followed the attendants assigned to their group. His bracket was considerably smaller, and he hoped he would have time to catch Melenia’s final match when his assessment was over.

Krix wound up overseeing Edge’s group. The man led them back into the annex where they’d originally met, then climbed to the top floor. When they arrived, they were met by a pair of gladiators who were both stage three according to his skill senses, which felt sharper than ever now that Skill-Eater was no longer bound by the chains of oblivion.

The evaluation had two parts. First, each would-be warrior would spar with a random person from their bracket without using any skills. When that was over, they would be individually tested by an experienced gladiator while deploying the full breadth of their skillset, or as much as they were willing to reveal.

“For this assessment, we will be using a spell that transforms damage into paint. It will let us track your injuries without harming your bodies and gear. This level of protection is a great deal stronger than the magic we use during official matches. In part because it’s expensive to use, but mostly because the crowd gets bored if there isn’t a genuine element of risk once you step into the ring. You must weigh the cost of medical care and repairing damaged equipment verses your prospective winnings, balancing your financial, mental, and physical resources as the season progresses.”

Krix explained that Edge would receive his starting rung today, which the System would track from that point on. At first, he could only participate in low-rung brawls, but after he cleared the threshold, he could challenge anyone within a certain range, defend against challenges from other gladiators, and sign up for exhibition matches if he met their qualifications.

He would also be placed onto one specialty ladder based on his weapon of choice, which he would be unable to change until the next season began. He wasn’t sure how that would play out for people who primarily fought with spells. Since both he and Mel were using physical weapons they planned to wield for the foreseeable future, he didn’t bother to pay attention to that part of the explanation.

He passed the time by contemplating what he hoped to accomplish during his stint as a gladiator, on top of the weapon-handling experience he’d come here to obtain.

Edge had no intention of trying to reach a top rung in either ladder. The season was already half over, and he held no illusions that he could become the best of the best in such a short span of time. Many of the top gladiators had trained their entire lives before coming to Ord, and had participated in countless matches since, although he found the fantasy of being crowned king of the ring to be quite pleasant as he waited for the fun to begin.

If he spent enough time in the free city, he might eventually reach a rung where he was eligible to receive seasonal prizes, but his best chance of scoring big was participating in some major exhibition matches, which could be incredibly lucrative too.

Not long after, Krix had everyone draw numbers out of a hat to determine their opponents. Edge wound up being in the third of five pairings. He would be battling a woman who was so muscular she made Melenia seem thin—a body type he had come to associate with someone who had invested nearly all of their attribute points into Power.

Most people spread their points in a distribution that complemented their fighting style, and specialists who focused on a single attribute were rare. While there were obvious benefits to being better at one approach than almost anyone else their stage, the strategy created too many weaknesses and imbalances to be practical for hunters, who had to deal with a wide range of threats while traversing deadly biomes.

However, some individuals performed jobs where raw strength, speed, or agility was more important than versatility, and the calculus was different for fighting in the arena versus hunting in the field. Gladiators didn’t have to worry about running into unwelcome and potentially fatal surprises, since the arena’s magic prevented lethal injuries.

I’m going to have a tough time against this opponent. I’m heavily invested in energetic attributes, and I can’t use skills during this portion of the evaluation.

Even still, Edge was more excited than worried. This was an excellent opportunity to see what he could do without relying on Skill-Eater’s magic, and he was confident that could make up for a poor showing during the second, and presumably more important half of these proceedings.

Now that everyone was organized, Krix addressed them one final time before the testing began.

“For these matches, you will fight when I give the signal and stop when I say stop. While our magic is safeguarding your lives, there isn’t any reason to take things that far when the crowd isn’t watching. On that note, battling outside of a sanctioned bout or deliberately trying to maim your opponents will be dealt with harshly. That rule applies to every gladiator within World’s End while you are on the official roster.”

With that, it was time for the first match. As the pair of prospective gladiators faced off against one another in a ring painted on the stone floor, Edge ran his gaze across the room. A few dozen spectators had arrived and were watching from a row of benches serving in place of stands.

There was a magitech barrier installed to protect them against incidental attacks, although there wasn’t a reason to activate it yet. He wondered if they simply enjoyed watching amateurs going at it or were recruiting for the factions. Probably a mix of both, he decided, along with some gamblers and bookies collecting information on new fighters before their first official match.

He turned his attention to the ring when Krix gave the signal to begin. It was over in less than a minute. A young man wielding a pair of daggers was facing off against a veteran hunter holding a short spear. The youth was talented, but Edge could tell he had never fought with his life on the line, whereas the spear wielder had ample combat experience using his weapon of choice.

He must not have fought many people with it thought, or the fight would have been over faster. The youth unleashed a flurry of slashes, trying to get inside the hunter’s reach. However, his opponent kept backpedaling and circling while waiting for an opening to appear.

The daggers scored some minor cuts along the spearman’s forearms, giving Edge a chance to observe the magic that blocked the damage and converted it into red paint. It was the exact hue of fresh blood and the spell manifested pigment in proportion to the severity of the strike. The setup was entertaining and practical. It made it easy to judge the contestants’ performance, although actual injuries would have impeded their movement too.

Eventually, the hunter managed to land a stop thrust square in the knifeman’s stomach. He collapsed as red paint gushed from his “wound,” and Krix called an end to the match. It was interesting that not everyone who made it to stage two had much experience fighting other people, driving home just how different the situation in the free city was compared to Puppet Town, where even the uncored had battled jailbirds and beasts.

The next match was between two skilled swordswomen who were no strangers to weapon-on-weapon combat. Edge watched with interest as they crossed blades, trading blows and parries while racking up a collection of minor cuts. Eventually, one of them got in a slash that would have beheaded her opponent, causing a ruby fountain of pigment to engulf the loser as the crowd roared its approval.

It was finally his turn. Even though this wasn’t a serious fight, the competitive spirit filling the room got his blood pumping as he squared off against his foe. The woman looked strong enough to bench press Blue, which meant he would lose the match if he took more than a glancing blow.

She was wielding a club as thick as his waist that was easily six feet long. It didn’t seem to be a modified weapon, but it was clearly made from high-grade materials. He could have cut straight through it with Dragon’s Greed running, but under these conditions, he wasn’t sure what would happen if it met Opulence head-on.

As he stepped into the ring, the spectators were whispering to each other while furiously scribbling notes. When Edge heard Helena’s name, he realized the excitement was due to his polearm, not his reputation, so he addressed them before the match began.

“The glaive is called Opulence, and it’s Soulbound. If something unfortunate happens to me, the weapon will crumble into dust.”

He wanted that detail to accompany any rumors about the polearm, so no one would try to murder him for carrying a weapon that was worth a literal fortune. He had kept the glaive hidden in his vault while out on the street, but now that he was wielding Opulence in the arena, he hoped the modification would protect him from a knife in the back.

That might be the reason Helena chose it, although the mod has plenty of combat utility too. Either way, I’ll have to thank her the next time I stop by the Notched Blade.

Krix paused when Edge made his declaration, grinning at the ensuing spectacle while nodding in appreciation. When the chatter died down, he gave the signal for the match to start.

The instant he did, the woman raised her club and charged. By the time she arrived in front of Edge, he had come to several realizations. The first was that while she had an insane amount of Power, she wasn’t as lacking in her other attributes as he had assumed. It meant she had decent traits compensating for her inherent deficiencies, since they didn’t transform a warrior’s body like spending potentia did.

From her stance and form, he could tell she had considerable experience fighting weapon wielders. The observation made him even more curious as to why she had chosen such a specialized build and how she used it to cycle up, though there wasn’t time to ponder the matter now.

While he was outmatched in brute strength, it wasn’t all bad news. Her traits were solid, but his were better. He was faster and had superior reflexes, letting him leap out of the way of strikes that would have broken half his bones under ordinary circumstances. He had the impression that his opponent didn’t have much experience sparring without her skills. Edge didn’t either, but it gave him a fighting chance of coming out on top during the furious melee that ensued.

Over the next five minutes, both combatants did their best to lay the other low. Her weapon was heavier, but Opulence offered superior reach and could be used to thrust as well as slash. Blocking and parrying weren’t viable when dealing with such a massive weapon, which meant he needed to move out of the way while circling to keep his opponent off-balance.

They were both covered in paint before the duel was done. For the final blow, Edge leapt on top of the gigantic club as it came whipping around in a horizontal arc, driving Opulence into the hollow of her throat in an eruption of crimson pigment.

They looked at each other with newfound respect before clasping hands and sharing a nod between equals.

“Helga,” she said. “That was a fun fight.”

“Edge,” he replied. “I had a good time too.”

They saluted each other and bowed to the crowd, enjoying a round of applause he hoped would be a portent of the cheers to come when he made his arena debut.

When the fifth match was over, an attendant cast a spell that erased all the paint. His adrenaline began to flow as he got ready to face off against an experienced gladiator. Edge was glad there were two of them, which meant he wouldn’t have to watch nine other bouts. He would have enjoyed the show, but his time was a limited resource, and he couldn’t afford to spend too many minutes spectating instead of training or fighting.

This time, he drew the lowest number, which meant he was first in line. His assessor was Vivian, a slender, dark-skinned woman wielding a pair of short swords. Edge had next to no experience battling a dual-wielding opponent. Luckily, Beelo had offered some pointers for just such a matchup, including some exercises Edge had practiced every day.

When the final pairing was announced, Krix explained the rules.

“For these matches, you can use any skills you like. Magitech devices and consumables are not allowed, since they aren’t permitted outside of exhibition matches. The same protection skill is in place, and your initial rung will be determined by how long you can hold out and how many wounds you inflict on your opponent, combined with our evaluation of your general competency. Now get in there and show us what you can do.”
