The thing about taking on the Great Gods was that defeating them all alone would be nigh on impossible, even for someone of Ryland’s calibre. Especially when the Great Gods themselves were likely banding together, such that they could, given their circumstances and how they operated, against Marionys Ryland specifically.

He didn’t want to attribute the assistance they were rendering the Flourinus Order to their bias against Ryland, but it had undoubtedly helped.

But the point remained that the Great Gods were a much more difficult foe to tackle than practically anything Ryland had dealt with thus far. He still had ways of handling them, but it would need a great deal of forethought, planning, and preparations.

And maybe a few allies too.

“You understand that I cannot assist against the Great Gods themselves, Ryland,” Headmaster Alaric was saying. “Not directly, at least. And even indirect assistance will likely be limited, unfortunately. Just the fact that Acrypha might have been taken to the Overrealm is by itself an inconceivably difficult thing to believe.”

Ryland tutted. “You won’t help me, Headmaster? Even when I’m giving you the perfect opportunity to avoid Acrypha’s grandparents.”

The elderly members of House Lowen had appeared at Arcoryx Academy to tear it apart, if need be, so long as that brought back their precious granddaughter. It had taken a great deal of effort from Ryland, with some conciliatory behaviour from Alaric himself, before the two old but still quite powerful mages had calmed down.

“Is that blackmail, Ryland?” Alaric asked.

Ryland smiled. “It’s the truth.”

In all honesty, he wasn’t miffed at Alaric at all. He didn’t expect the Headmaster to be able to do much in the first place. Aside from being terribly busy with Arcoryx Academy and the chaos of Androvia taking in all the Rhellian refugees, he also couldn’t go against the Churches by assisting someone very publicly taking a stand against their objects of worship.

Plus, he was taking an active, positive hand in the refugee situation. A lot of the Rhellians were young. Academy-age.

Alaric was personally championing a campaign to enrol a great deal of the Rhellians as students into Arcoryx Academy, and thereby creating a pathway for both the younger and older Rhellians to settle in Androvia. For even if the older ones failed to find easy work or homes, at least their children would be on a path to relative prosperity.

Plus, taking care of the children would lend more sympathy to the Rhellians, whose sudden numbers were definitely burdening the ecosystem of Vyrd. Food shortages, rising shantytowns and lack of housing, labour shifts, and so much else.

It all felt like a massive headache for Ryland. He was happy to admit that administrative matters of that nature might just be well beyond him.

Alaric sighed. “I’m sorry. My hands are simply tied.”

“It’s alright, Headmaster,” Ryland said. “I appreciate that you at least got me a meeting with the most powerful man in Androvia.”

That was where they were heading at that moment. The Prime Minister of Androvia had been in Arcoryx for the better part of the last week, handling the refugee crisis personally. Despite the grievances of many Androvians, he wasn’t turning the Rhellians away completely.

Ryland appreciated that. In truth, the majority of Androvians weren’t entirely bent on sending the Rhellians packing either. They were just burdened and would appreciate some relief.

Which was what the Prime Minister was trying to instil.

“Ah, Ryland,” Klyne Faldor said. He had taken over the massive glass structure of Arcoryx’s City Hall as his personal operational headquarters. “I’m glad you’re on time.”

They had needed to make an appointment to see the Prime Minister. He was working with strict schedules nowadays, so he refused to see anyone without an appointment.

“I could have come earlier,” Ryland said. “But unfortunately, your guards would probably have panicked if I had used my rifts.”

“We wouldn’t have panicked,” said the grizzled Sentinel in golden armour accompanying His Honour. “We would just have ended the perceived threat before it grew to anything worse than perceived.”

That was a brazenly hostile admission, one that the Prime Minister clamped down on. “Now, now, let us not start this off on the wrong foot. Ryland, please have a seat. I know you have much to explain and much more to propose. Let us hear it.”

Ryland did so. Alaric unfortunately couldn’t stick around due to his duties, but that was fine. Ryland could take care of things on his own.

He explained succinctly everything that had happened on Rhel—much of which the Prime Minister was already familiar with thanks to his knowledge regarding the refugee situation—and then everything Ryland had discovered after his altercation with Mavar and the disappearance of Acrypha.

Then he moved on to his proposals, which was where the Prime Minister and his retinue started to look dubious.

“Are you certain about everything you just said, Ryland?” the Prime Minister asked.

“Indeed,” Ryland said. The Sentinel scoffed. All three secretaries nearby looked like they were seriously doubting whether they should continue noting things down or not. “I could prove it to you, of course, but it would take a little bit of time.”

“And time is something you don’t have a great deal of, yes?”

“Correct. I’d like to rescue Acrypha as soon as possible. Even the fact that I’ve waited days already does not sit well with me.”

The Prime Minister considered everything that Ryland had laid out before him. It wasn’t just what Ryland had discovered. There was also everything he intended to do going forward, and how exactly he intended to rescue Acrypha.

“You understand I cannot divert my attention away from the crisis we have here at Androvia?” Klyne Faldor said. “Especially not for one lone girl, regardless of whose daughter or granddaughter she may be.”

“I understand,” Ryland said. “So instead, I beseech that His Honour allows me to make use of some of the famed House Faldor treasures for my ritual.”

“The ritual that will tear a rent into the Overrealm.”

“The very same.”

“The ritual,” the Prime Minister continued with a very deadpan voice. “That will have both the New and the Old Church calling for my deposition immediately.”

“Your involvement doesn’t have to be public.”

The Prime Minister looked around at the others as if to ask, can you believe this fellow? He sighed before turning back to Ryland again. “The implications will unfortunately leave me in a dire situation, Ryland.”

“Yes,” the Sentinel said. He had the appearance of backing up his superior’s words and intent, but in truth, the gleam in his eyes professed that he was very happy that Ryland was being foiled. “His Honour speaks the truth. The Prime Minister of Androvia can in no way be associated with this blasphemous ritual you’re intent on conducting.”

That was fair enough, actually. As uncouthly as the Sentinel had put it, their logic was sound. No one would approve of working against the Great Gods.

“Unless,” the Prime Minister said, making all the heads in the room twist to face him in surprise. “You simply stole or took the treasures you needed for yourself.”

“Your Honour!” the Sentinel protested vociferously.

Klyne Faldor went on with a look of careful contemplation. “You’re an all-powerful mage, Ryland. Possibly the greatest we’ve ever had on Androvia. Surely, you of all people can invade my House’s stash and take what you need. By force or subterfuge, whichever you deem is best suited for the endeavour.”

Slowly, Ryland smiled. “And once I do so, I will simply take it to my ritual, where everything will be beyond your purview and jurisdiction. And you will be able to put it aside in favour of giving more attention to everything else you have on your plate, thereby gaining more adoration from the people of Androvia.”

Klyne Faldor smiled so thinly, Ryland was quite certain that he was the only one who saw it. “You speak like a politician, Ryland. Are you certain you’re unsuited to rule?”

“Quite positive.” Ryland nodded and got to his feet. “Thank you, Your Honour. My endeavours will be a success, one way or another. I’m grateful you’ve… chosen to look the other way.”

“Just know that we’ll have to apprehend a criminal upon his return into our jurisdiction.”

The Sentinel almost breathed out a sigh of relief at seeing that there was some catch that would negatively impact Ryland. Unfortunately, his bubble of hope was quickly popped by his own superior yet again.

“Except,” Klyne Faldor said, and this time, Ryland didn’t fail to hear the toying note in His Honour’s voice. “Of course, if you did something miraculous that allows a higher power such as myself to pardon all your crimes.”

Ryland nodded gratefully. He hadn’t come into the meeting expecting any real assistance. They already had a deal, and Klyne Faldor was fulfilling his part of it admirably well. That the Prime Minister was happy to help, if in a roundabout way, wouldn’t go unappreciated. “Thank you again, Prime Minister. Rest assured that, no matter who stands before me, I will win.”

There was no time for Ryland to meet with or talk with anyone further. He had spoken with the most important people, leaving behind one last request for them to relay the importance of rescuing Acrypha to those in power who sought to assist in whatever ways they could. So long as that didn’t hinder Ryland’s own operation, of course.

For now, Ryland met up with Viren again.

“Are you ready, Viren?” Ryland asked.

Just like it had done to Ryland, the discovery that there was a specific location that held Acrypha had lit a fire in the boy too. It didn’t matter if said location was the Overrealm. It didn’t matter if they’d have to fight the very Great Gods themselves.

They had a very specific goal now. They had a direction to take their power. And that was in and of itself a tremendous blessing.

“I’m ready, sir,” Viren said. “It’s not like I can focus on studying now. Let’s go.”

Ryland nodded. “[Translocate].”

They disappeared, reappearing on Rhel a moment later as Ryland’s rift broke open on a mostly shattered planet.

The battle between Ryland and Mavar had destroyed so much of the world, the Realm’s former inhabitants had little to no room to return. Rhel had been reduced to a near inhospitable state.

Smoke filled the skies so much that the blue had been removed entirely by ruddy black. In the far distance, eruptions speared into the heavens to spew more ash into the atmosphere, emitting a glare that further weakened the low light from the distant sun. The ground was crumbly, in part due to the ash that had settled everywhere, and what little water was left looked more like oil.

“Huh,” Viren said, enjoying the stable, unpolluted atmosphere around him thanks to Ryland’s cast of [Oneself World]. “This is much better than when I had last been here, sir.”

He wasn’t wrong. Terrible as it appeared right this moment, things had been far, far worse right after the battle between Ryland and Mavar had finished. Subsequently, over the next few days, Ryland had done some discreet cleaning up.

It would be difficult to return the Realm to a state where things were fine enough for the Rhellians to return and lead lives here, if they got over their trauma. Difficult, but not impossible. Not for Ryland. But it would take a great deal of time and effort, and he needed to reserve his energy and focus for Acrypha.

So, for now, Ryland had mostly focused on clearing up just enough of an open space for the ritual he needed.

“It’s not as impressive as it appears,” Ryland said. “I merely cleaned up the area I need.”

“Still, sir,” Viren insisted. “This is a lot. I couldn’t have done this by myself.” His expression fell slightly, for whatever reason. “I thought I could help with setting things up, but it looks like that might not be necessary.”

“Don’t worry, Viren.” Ryland was starting to understand Viren’s fears. He hadn’t come here to be a tourist. Viren was intent on giving as much of himself as he could to ensure Acrypha’s safe return. “I have some tasks in mind for you. I just don’t intend to leave you drowning with no information at all.”

Viren nodded. “Fair enough, sir. And thank you.”

They began walking through the cleared space. It was essentially a wide circular field that could have housed the entirety of Arcoryx Academy and the Rhellian Institute combined.

Viren ended up gaping a little as Ryland began channelling his mana. He wasn’t going to start a spell. Instead, his mana went down his body and inserted into the ground, slowly forming a wide field of Glyphs set in a very specific pattern arrayed over the entire area. Ryland focused on his mana, and everything remained silent for a little while, but he sensed some jitteriness.

“Viren,” Ryland said without opening his eyes. “What is it?”

Viren sounded very relieved Ryland had allowed him to speak. “Sir, you never explained how exactly you’re going to break Acrypha out of the Overrealm. Or how you’re going to take on the Great Gods themselves. I have faith in you, but… this is the Great Gods we’re talking about.”

Ryland smiled. He still didn’t pause channelling for the time being. “Remember this, Viren. Whenever you’re facing a stronger opponent, you always need to find some advantage that works in your favour. In our case, we have one enormous glaring condition we need to make use of.”

“What’s that, sir?”

“It’s the fact that the Great Gods are stuck in the Overrealm, where the flow of time is very different from our own. In other words, the best way to counter overwhelming odds is through lots and lots of careful preparation and planning.”

Viren took his time to absorb that, going by the ensuing silence before he spoke again. “I see. Well, I think I do. So sir, what’s the first step of our preparations, then?”

“Gathering strength. Specifically, we’re going to go meet the Planetary Archdragon.”
