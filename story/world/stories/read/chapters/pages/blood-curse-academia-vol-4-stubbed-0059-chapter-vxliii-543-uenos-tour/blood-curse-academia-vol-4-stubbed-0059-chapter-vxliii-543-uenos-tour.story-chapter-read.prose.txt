Chapter V.XLIII (5.43) - Ueno’s Tour

“And this is Kizu’s brewing laboratory and garden!” a cheery voice said, shattering Kizu’s meditative thoughts.

He had just completed sorting through all of Zayne’s memories, compartmentalizing the knowledge into different corners of his mind, and had been reflecting on that last interaction with the Genies. There was at least one more important scene to complete Zayne’s story, but he could feel the soul fragment holding it back for now. That was fine though. If anything, Kizu actually preferred to keep the resolution of his soul companion’s story for another day. With everything he’d learned, he had already more than enough memories and knowledge to organize.

He looked up from his empty desk, which he’d been staring at for the last two hours. Aoi stood in the doorway, a towering woman behind her. It took Kizu a minute to recognize her. Ueno, one of Inari’s henchmen. Half a year ago she’d attempted to murder both him and Anata on this very ship. Now, she glanced about like a nervous puppy, completely at odds with her powerful body structure.

Forcing back the immediate spike of anger, Kizu simply nodded in their direction. While he did not want them here now, it wasn’t as if Aoi had known exactly what he’d been working on when she barged in.

“I’m pleasantly surprised to have the opportunity to introduce you, Kizu,” Aoi said. “Do you remember Ueno?”

“She tried to blast my body apart. I’m not likely to forget the experience.”

“I… apologize,” the woman said awkwardly.

“Ueno just completed the geas ritual that bonds us together,” Aoi announced. “It went off without a hitch! She will be working as my assistant and bodyguard from now on. So get used to seeing her around.”

“Hm.” Kizu didn’t like that. Ueno’s memories had been altered by Roba but if she rediscovered Anata was a Harbinger, then Kizu wasn’t certain how the woman would react. In his mind, she was a liability and he wanted nothing to do with her ever again. “Next time, knock before entering my lab while I’m in here. There could have been fumes in the air you would have been unprepared for.”

“I did, actually. Three times. I was surprised to see you here since you didn’t respond. Figured you must have jumped out of here earlier. If you prefer though, next time I will also check for your soul signature.”

Kizu paused. He’d been so focused on Zayne’s memories he must have blocked out the noise. “Yeah. That would be good. Thanks.”

Aoi strolled into his chamber and began gesturing around, explaining the ins and outs of his workspace. Kizu was about to further protest Aoi disclosing all of his resources to a potential future enemy and kick them both out when he noticed a familiar figure standing behind Ueno. One that certainly did not belong on his ship.

“Is that… a James golem?” he asked, incredulous.

At the sound of his name, the golem looked up. When Kizu looked closely, he could see the wood grain that ran down the golem’s face, crafted to appear like an elderly man.

Aoi stopped her tangent about the recently uprooted soul reaver fern she’d helped him plant to address his question. She scrunched up her nose and looked back at the golem with distaste. “Yes. Until the end of the semester, he’s been instructed to monitor Ueno. It was a compromise with both my family and the academy staff. They don’t trust my magic and want her under strict surveillance.”

“Please do not mind me,” the golem said. “I will do my best to not impede any of your objectives. Carry on as if I do not exist.”

Aoi obliged the request, resuming her animated description of how she’d harvested and planted another of the soul plants.

While the princess yapped, Kizu’s attention fell on the bathtub set up off to her left. He still had that active portal that accessed the hotspring in Hon. From his brief experimentation, Kizu had discovered that it was a one-way path. It allowed passage out of Hon, not into the nation. He struggled to figure out a viable way to make the coin-sized portal viable for anything other than hot spring water. Still, he kept access open regardless. It took very little effort on his part and he suspected once it closed, it would be gone.

Once Aoi finished up her tour of his room, she departed with her new bodyguard in tow.

The James golem followed after them. Kizu thought about the last time he’d interacted with one of those golems, handing over his weekly blood test for the academy rankings. He wondered if the golems had a means in which to process the blood or if they required some sort of separate artifact. He knew how the blood was disposed of, tossed into a vat of magma under the academy, but he never learned how the actual testing happened.

He smiled as he thought about his next testing day. Roba would be both baffled and infuriated after she saw his new rankings. Which was ironic, since she’d been the one to teach him how to compartmentalize the memories into different parts of his mind.

“Now to unlock those the crone sealed away,” Kizu mused. “I’d need to find a mind mage strong enough to release the block she created.”

He considered Fuku, the witch with the owl familiar in the Hon Basin, but dismissed the thought. Even if the witch had been outside Hon’s borders before it got closed off, he wouldn’t trust someone like her with any part of his mind.

As far as he knew, Roba and the crone were the only two other mind mages in his life. He suspected neither of them would be particularly helpful. Though… it had been mentioned by Ione’s mother that the crone escaped her imprisonment in Hon. So, who knew?

Thinking of the crone, Kizu’s eyes wandered over to his stockpile of potion ingredients. They were getting close to the end of the semester, and that meant the true tournament would begin. He didn’t know who he’d be facing off against or what potions would be best in his upcoming fights.

A few minutes later, he was hunched over a small cauldron, offering it bits of silty mud and crushed antlers from a mountain jackalope. Learning every elemental branch and spell was practically impossible, the most powerful elementalist mages focused on one, sometimes two, different branches and mastered them. His potions offered him a different route. He decided to work on simulating the effects of some spells he’d been reading about in his textbooks about elemental magic. Obviously, the one-time-use potions weren’t nearly as useful as truly mastering the spells, but it was a short-cut and fantastic utility. Especially when he factored his spatial ring into the equation. If he was going to be facing off against his fellow students, he decided it would be best to use his greatest strength, brewing, to keep them on their toes.

On a whim, Kizu reached into a compartment in the back of his mind and opened it up to see what Zayne knew about potion-making. The ancient sliver of soul offered very little helpful information on the subject. He apparently knew of the practice, but it was a forbidden art. However, he did know a lot of strange potential ingredients. Unfortunately, they were all on the other side of the planet.

Kizu was about to close those memories and refocus on the earth tremor potion in front of him but, as he was placing the memories back into their mental box, one slipped into the front of his mind. He was still a bit clumsy with the enhanced memory organization tactic, so the mistake wasn’t much of a surprise. The memory itself, on the other hand, froze him in place.

During his desperate search for a means in which to punish the Genie for stealing Lahel, Zayne had been flipping through a book. One on ancient astronomy and different mythical potions. At the back of the book was the image of an old, familiar woman’s face. Wrinkled and spotted with warts.

Across an expanse of thousands of years and kilometers, the crone stared back at Kizu.

She winked at him.
