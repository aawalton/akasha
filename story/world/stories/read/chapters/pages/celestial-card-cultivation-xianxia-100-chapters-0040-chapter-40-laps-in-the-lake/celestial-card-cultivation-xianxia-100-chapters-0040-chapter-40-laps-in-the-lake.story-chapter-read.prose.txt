Ruling the sect was not enough. Not even close. The plane… was a start.  — He who calls himself Chi You, The Completed Works by He Shulin, foremost scholar of the Outer Realms

“What is this?” Elder Qin Ye asked, holding up what looked like regular ginger. The elder was the last of the five elders who had formed the deciding council during the test. He had been the one who had ended the questioning of Jianyi after some of the others had gotten agitated over the revelation that Elder Sun was his teacher.

It took a moment until a disciple slowly raised her hand and answered, sounding more like asking than answering, though.

“Right. I have better things to do than ask disciples that might not even have opened their dantians trick questions, so don’t hesitate to speak up if you believe you know the answers.” The elder paused for a moment and looked at the assembled disciples. A relatively small group, and from looking around, it seemed to Jianyi that quite a few of the nobles were missing. Maybe they didn’t believe that they needed to know about harvesting what nature provided, as it was known as a reliable way to create wealth, though slower than fighting spirit beasts.

“It is ginger, just like any mortal would consume it.” He put the ginger back on the table on the stage and then held up another piece of ginger.

“Now, what is this?”

This time a number of hands were raised quickly, and the same answer was given.

“You are right, it is ginger, but to be precise, it is Fire Ginger. A most basic natural treasure of the first realm. Just like cultivators and spirit beasts, plants, ores, crystals, and other natural treasures are also measured in realms. This ginger has most likely been growing in the vicinity of a volcano or another fire-aligned natural treasure of a higher realm.

“So the difference between those two pieces of ginger is that one is infused with qi, the other isn’t. However, it is important to understand that even this mortal ginger,” he held up the first piece he had shown, “has an affinity, or maybe it would be more precise to say it is aligned with one affinity. So, if you were to plant this ginger right beside a metal mine, even if of high grade, it would be much less likely to absorb the metal-aligned qi.”

The elder placed the Fire Ginger back on the table and then pulled a small green jade box to him. It had a symbol engraved on the top that Jianyi couldn’t quite make out, but the moment the elder pulled the jade cover to the side, he felt a form of pressure, not dissimilar to the aura of Elder Sun. At the same time, the temperature in the room grew rapidly to the steam of one of the fancy bathhouses that rich merchants liked to visit in the city—and it kept rising.

“I’ll be quick to protect its efficacy,” the elder said as he lifted the jade box and tilted it so that they could see the ginger inside.

If one could still call it ginger. Instead of the gray-green skin that Jianyi was used to from the herb, it had dark red stripes that looked almost liquid, like lava.

With a loud clack, the elder closed the jade box, and immediately the temperature in the room started to decrease again.

“This was Sublime Lava Ginger, a natural treasure of the fourth realm. Likely more than a thousand years old and harvested by an elder from the depths of a ravine in the central mountains. It will be used, eventually, to create an alchemical pill or potion that has sufficient qi density to help a cultivator in the Core Formation, or even Nascent Soul realm.”

There was quite a bit of murmuring in the room, and the disciples behind Jianyi speculated that the ginger was likely valuable and rare enough to fetch many rare, if not epic, cards.

“Any questions?”

Jianyi lifted his hand, and the elder nodded to him.

“Honorable elder, the feeling when you opened the box. Was that an aura?”

Elder Qin slightly tilted his head. “No. An aura is a representation of intent. Unlike spirit beasts, plants don’t gain sentience, which would be the minimum requirement for an intent. What you felt was merely the amount of qi contained in the treasure.

“Good, if there are no other questions, let us move on to a final topic for today. Cards.” He held up two cards. “Can one of you, maybe in the front rows, say what kind of cards these are?”

A young-looking boy raised his hand and responded so quietly that Jianyi, who did not sit much further back, struggled to hear.

The elder nodded, then repeated. “Exactly, these are qi and affinity cards. Where do they come from?”

Before anybody could respond, the elder answered himself. “I am sure many, if not all of you, thought the answer was beasts. That, however, is not true. When most people think of cards, what comes to mind are techniques, because those are the spectacular ones that are visible when one draws a hand in the midst of combat.

“I am sure one of my esteemed fellow elders will have explained the importance of qi cards and affinity cards to you by now, but for those who have forgotten, qi cards can be added to your middle dantian and will accelerate the speed with which you can cultivate. Every one of you has an affinity card in your lower dantian. As the core card you received during the heavenly trial is an affinity card, it allows you to convert unaspected qi into qi that you can use for techniques in your affinities.”

All of that was known to Jianyi.

“What most people overlook is that the source of those cards is not spirit beasts, but natural treasures. So, as valuable as the herbs, ores, and other natural treasures are for their ingredients, they offer even more value in the form of these essential cards.”

Jianyi sat in his cultivation chamber; however, this time he wasn’t practicing meditation or his qi control, instead he was thinking about the spear practice the following day.

In the two days since his badly thought-through commitment to Elder Fang, he had made little progress and stood now at 181 laps around the training field in the allotted time.

What frustrated him was that he clearly was capable of completing 200 laps. It was painful, very painful actually, but he could do it and did do it every day.

The only thing that he couldn’t do—or at least hadn’t done yet—was complete those in four hours.

Was this a physical issue? Yes? No?

Was he physically at the peak? Definitely no. He was sure that otherwise he would be able to run significantly more than 200 laps in the given time.

Did he go to the limits of what he could currently do? Yes. Towards the end of the run, every step was pain and misery.

However, he asked himself, if his mother’s life depended on it, would he be able to run faster? Would he be able to go beyond his limits?

Yes. Yes, he definitely would.

He stood up, put on his robes, and left for the training field. There were limits to break.

***

He felt the gaze of Elder Fang on him as he stepped onto the training field.

He ignored it.

He noticed Shuhua and Miyao wave towards him encouragingly.

He ignored it.

He saw Ruolan grimacing as she observed his focused expression.

He ignored it.

Instead, he mentally confirmed the speed he would need to hold for 200 laps to make it on time, then slightly increased it. Then he waited.

The signal came. He made the first step.

While at a sustainable pace for him, he could make it to a hundred laps or more before the pain started to become uncomfortable, at his current pace, it was sooner. Much sooner.

At lap 65, breathing became harder. At lap 80, it became painful. At lap 100, he started to question his decision.

Surprisingly, running with the bulk of his fellow disciples in group Lapis did help. Whether it was a tiny bit of slipstream, or the community, or the joined rhythm with which their feet hit the stone floor, he didn’t know. It was something that he was not used to. Maybe something to remember.

At lap 120, he was not sure that he would make it.

Why had he made this wager with the elder?

Why did he even care about being in group Lapis?

Those were unusual thoughts for Jianyi. He never questioned the need for discipline. He wouldn’t start on this day. He had decided to break through his limits, so he would do it.

He took as deep a breath as his ravaged lungs would allow him and closed his eyes as he entered the straight passage opposite the paifang-like gate to the training field.

As he opened his eyes, he didn’t find himself in the sect, but at the bottom of a lake.

Not any lake. This was the place he had visited before. His spirit lake.

He found himself pounding on the rocky bottom of the lake in an unceasing rhythm.

Slowly, the never-ending hits started to loosen some shards of granite that rolled away.

An indeterminate time later, it could have been minutes or hours or years, some of the cracks he had formed in the past appeared again.

In the real world, his fists would long since have turned into bloody pulp, but here, no pain reached him.

Hit after hit, smash after smash, the cracks became more pronounced, and finally one of them burst open, and a radiant ray of blue light shot out of the granite block and infused the water around Jianyi.

As quickly as it had burst into being, the ray of light vanished, but Jianyi felt new strength seeping into his spirit, into his will.

The next time he blinked, he found himself back on the training field.

He didn’t know how much time had passed, how many laps he had run. The pain was not gone, quite the opposite, but his will to endure was unshakable.

He was still with the bulk of the disciples, and from how tired some of them looked, it was clear that they were close to the end.

Jianyi did not celebrate before the finish line; he stayed focused on his body and setting one foot before the next, never interrupting his rhythm.

A clap on his back brought him back to reality.

One of the disciples running with him congratulated him on making it.

200 laps done. In a bit more than three and a half hours.

The pain was excruciating. He got on his knees, trying— futilely—to bypass his burning lungs when drawing in deep breaths.

Slowly, things got better, and he managed to stand up again.

He turned around and saw a furious Elder Fang looking in his direction. He bowed with an impassive face towards the elder, but the line of sight was blocked as he came back up from other disciples congratulating him and celebrating that they would now be able to start the actual spear training.

Ruolan seemed to have already left, and the other inner disciple just reminded them to be on time for the next class before he left as well.

Incredibly slowly and with every step hurting, Jianyi made his way back home. He needed to rest. Urgently.
