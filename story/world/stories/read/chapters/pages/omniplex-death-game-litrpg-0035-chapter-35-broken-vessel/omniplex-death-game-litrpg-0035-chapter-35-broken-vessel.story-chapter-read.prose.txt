The pain was overwhelming, yet for the briefest moment, Ciro felt like he forgot what had happened. The past few minutes, even the seconds, became a hazy cloud. It was him and the Xyphon. Nothing else. In this blue dome of caged carnage, only one of them would be left alive. He knew that well enough.

From where Tenryubis, the Xyphon leader, stood, a trail of smoke wafted from below his waist. Ciro looked downwards to see a stump where the alien’s leg should’ve been. It should’ve burned like fire. He should’ve been sharing Ciro’s pain. Yet Tenryubis did not stop. He did not pause. A sinister smile playing on his lips, even through the pain. He hopped towards him.

But Ciro wasn’t stupid. He could make out through that confident facade, his clenched jaw, the way it was tightened so hard his teeth could crack. The remnants of his leg, only a clump of hanging flesh, screamed as it swayed as he hobbled closer. He only breathed through his slitted nostrils. Slow and steady. His fists were clenched, his hands turning from silvery to white.

Ciro had to move. They were both without a limb. He could count his blessings that he could run. The Xyphon pounced at him, a wild charge. With only a single leg, he looked like a fish out of water. Ciro quickly pivoted and ran. The dome was the size of a football field, with ample space to run away. Tenryubis’s attack left him crashing into the wall of the dome. The sound echoed throughout the circle.

On the other side of the dome, Ciro’s heart raced, his right arm trailed the singed socket of his left side. His hand jerked back once he made contact, heat still radiating from his left shoulder. He was fortunate, his arm was taken cleanly. Tenryubis was not gifted the same fate, as Ciro watched him flail on the ground, trying his best to get up.

“You damn Earthling! I will get you!”

Ciro took a deep breath, slumping against the blue wall. He needed to catch his breath, most importantly, get over the shock. A ripple of anger overcame him, as he saw the Xyphon finally jump back to his feet.“Why did you do this? What is this Hakasha!? I thought this was going to be an honorable duel!” Ciro screamed out, as the Xyphon faced towards him once again.

“This is Hakasha. The most honorable of fights. An ancient duel, the first ever event on the Exofront. To sacrifice a limb and win a battle in Hakasha can only prove your strength does not lie in the body.”

The Xyphon hobbled back to Ciro, only slower this time, as if there was all the time in the world. “Watch as I wring your neck with only a single leg.”

Ciro’s eyes trembled. He shook his head in disbelief. “What about after? After you kill me. You’ll just be a cripple without a leg.”

“There are so many countless possibilities, possibilities that an Otherworlder like you couldn’t even comprehend.”

Ciro stood back up, with Tenryubis halfway to reaching him. It was almost pathetic to look at, but Ciro wasn’t dumb enough to get close. This vast arena came to his benefit. The Xyphon would never reach him. He smiled as he moved along the edge of the dome, staggering to the other side.

Both of them were in excruciating pain. One wanted to fight while the other sought to flee. This could go on forever, Ciro thought, as Tenryubis let out a grunt, turning with one leg towards him.

“You won’t be able to kill me,” said Ciro. “Not without your leg.”

“Oh I will just you wait.” Tenryubis smiled, his body shaking with the lack of balance from his legs. He didn’t move anymore.

The Xyphons stood still. His eyes focused only on Ciro.

What’s his game? Ciro gritted his teeth, the pain flaring in his left shoulder, as if reminding him he couldn’t afford to take a fight. He shifted his legs slightly, not taking his eyes off his enemy, just to ease the ache on his hamstrings.

That’s when he felt it.

It was subtle at first, the slightest of pressure against his back. Ciro had presumed he stumbled by accident. When he turned round, the blue wall was still there, solid as ever, pulsating at the slightest touch, like a ripple in a pond.

But there was a faint sound that hissed from it, as if the walls were breathing. Sweat ran down Ciro’s neck. He shook his head, quickly snapping his attention to the Xyphon who watched him with glee.

Panic jolted through him.

He stepped forward, instinctively trying to get away from the wall, but the wall moved him. The entire dome moved him with. It crept forward like a tidal wave, only in slow motion. The wall no longer pressed against his back, it slid forward, pushing him, forcing him to the center, forcing him to face the one-legged foe.

His feet shuffled, stumbling, as he tried to keep his balance, but the wall continued its advance. Uncaring of Ciro’s wants and pleas. “No, no, no,” Ciro muttered under his breath. The ground beneath him seemed to twist, like the earth was being turned upside down, all for the sake of guiding him towards his doom.

The speed of the walls only increased. Ciro broke out into a desperate sprint. With every step, the dome grew smaller, tighter and intimate. He tried pushing against it, noticing it wouldn’t stop. Unyielding, the wall didn’t stop. Ciro’s body was nothing more than a passenger to its movements.

“You can’t fight it, Ciro!” Tenryubis called out. “You will face me.”

“Don’t ever say my name!”

The Xyphon laughed, watching as Ciro was chased by the blue wall. “What is a name when you’ll be dead soon? Perhaps we’ll keep your friends alive. The identical ones would make great slaves. The larger one will die. We have no use for something that could bite back.” His voice was dripping with mockery. Ciro and his group’s lives merely a toy for him to use and throw away.

“Damn it!” Ciro screamed. He was nearing the Xyphon, he had no choice, there was no running. He increased his speed. Instead of running away from the wall, he ran towards the alien. Only then would this dome cease to exist.

“I’ll kill you!” Ciro charged towards him, drawing the knife out of his pocket. A single arm is all he had. And it was all he needed. Once the dome closed, and he alone emerged, that was proof enough. For he was stronger.

He brandished the knife tightly, his muscles exploding with force. With every step, the Xyphon’s figure seemed to grow. Tenryubis stood tall, his stance loose, hands out waiting for Ciro to attack, boots pounded against the uneven floor as he closed the gap. The alien’s mocking laugh drowned out Ciro’s ragged breaths.

Ciro slashed low, aiming for the Xyphon’s ribs. Tenryubis swatted it away with his hand, as if it was a game. He replied with his own attack. His long fingers struck not with a fist but a whip-like flick, driving it into Ciro’s shoulder.

A scream ripped out of Ciro’s throat, clenching his teeth. His eyes narrowed as a second attack whistled towards him. He spun his body, dropping to the ground, rolling just below his silvery hands. The attack struck the ground, a cloud of dirt and rock exploding into the air.

Readjusting his grip on the knife, he attacked again. Ciro lunged forward, he used a feint, pretending to stab, before twisting the knife and stabbing the other direction. The blade pierced Tenryubis’s chest. It barely grazed him, drawing a small trickle of purple blood.

Ciro cocked back the knife. This time, he would cut deeper.

He launched his knife towards the Xyphon’s neck.

The knife drove towards the creature’s neck. This was it. This was Ciro’s win.

Ciro’s eyes widened in surprise, his knife hanged frozen in the air, inches away from meeting its target. He looked towards his hand to see the Xyphon’s grip on his knuckles. Ciro tried to squirm and twist out of his grip, but it was too strong.

“I’ve got you now.”

Tenryubis, using his other hand, grabbed Ciro by his rugged shoulder. The touch of a hand on his wound sent a fresh wave of pain, a foreign sensation that he should’ve never felt. Ciro felt himself being toppled over, Tenryubis using his weight to push him down to the ground.

In the shrunken dome, where they did battle. The ground was where the victor would emerge.

Ciro felt his hand being crushed, bones cracking under the pressure of the Xyphon’s clasp on him.

Tenryubis brought his face closer to Ciro as he pinned him down against the ground. “Release the blade. It’s only fair. I carry no weapon.”

“No,” Ciro gritted his teeth, swaying and shaking, desperately trying to free himself. He used his legs to shuffle away, Tenryubis noticed quickly. Using his larger frame, he wrapped his single leg around both of Ciro’s.

Completely locked down, Tenryubis focused on peeling Ciro’s knife out of his hand.

He started with a single finger, his nails biting into Ciro’s skin. He grabbed a hold of his index and began to twist.

“Stop—stop dammit!”

The pain didn’t come all at once. It started with simple pressure, but then once he felt the angle of his finger, half rotated, stretched way too far, then did he hear the pop. A sickening meaty click as his knuckles shifted. And still, Tenryubis didn’t stop.

Tears streamed down his face, eyes growing with each new wave of pain. “Stop it!” he yelled out, his mouth frothing with saliva. The Xyphon only leaned closer, his eyes drinking in Ciro’s suffering.

“If only you had another arm.” His mouth curled past a smile of excitement. There was only pure glee.

Another jerk and Tenryubis snapped the finger backward, as if twisting it wasn’t enough.

The bone shattered.

Ciro screamed. His body wanted to convulse from the pain, yet Tenryubis’s lock on his body remained stiff. There could be no relief. The pain wasn’t momentary, feeling more like a permanent echo.

His finger hung uselessly, crooked and wrong, the skin already bruised and swelling. A green balloon.

Just as Tenryubis released his grip on his index, it was as if all his other fingers had given up. They each quivered open, the knife dropping down to the ground harmlessly. His own body betraying him.

Tenryubis rolled Ciro to the top, his leg clamping down on Ciro’s feeble knees.

He couldn’t move in the slightest. He curled up his only fist into a puny ball, flailing it at the Xyphon’s head. The attack so pitiful, it didn’t warrant a reaction.

“Why didn’t you just drop the knife, Ciro Adams?”

“Bastard…” Ciro wheezed out.

“What did you say?”

Ciro tried to laugh, coming out more like a painful cough. “You heard me.”

“Say it again. I didn’t hear it.”

“Bastard.”

Before the words left Ciro’s lips, he felt his head ring. Tenryubis’s fist connected with Ciro’s temple. The top of the blue dome shook. Is there an earthquake?

“Say it again.”

This was all a sick game. Ciro knew it. His fate was sealed.

But he wasn’t one to die feebly.

“Bastard.”

Another hammer blow met Ciro’s skull.

Blood leaked out from the top of his ears.

“Say it again.”

“Bastard.”

The next punch came in heavier, harder, feeling like a drill prying into Ciro’s brain. A kaleidoscope of colors whirled in Ciro’s vision. The game had to continue until it was over. There was no restart.

“Bastard, bastard, bastard, bastard, bastard, bastard, bastard, bastard, bastard, bastard.”

Ciro continued to scream out, a wild psychotic grin playing on his face. As he continued to be hammered down with punch after punch. The floor beneath him trembled with the force of the strikes. Blood spilled freely from his mouth, staining his teeth with red. His eye, unintentionally hit, was sealed shut, while the other was wide open, glassy and crazed.

Something in Ciro’s body gave way. His legs maybe? A wet gasp escaped him, and pain shot through his side. Through his narrowed vision, he could make out the Xyphon’s fist planted into his ribs. He coughed up blood.

But still, he smiled.

“You don’t get it,” Ciro rasped, his voice barely a whisper, the air wheezing out of the punctured lungs. “You won’t win.”

Tenryubis paused, his eyes narrowing.

Another punch met Ciro’s skull. His head snapped sideways, blood spattering the Xyphon’s body. His only arm twitched once, twice—then went still.

“It’s all yours…” Ciro whispered. He knew he was listening, he always was.

A voice cackled in his mind. Cold and amused.

So that was your plan. You’re a fool, Adams. Do you think I’m some sort of secret weapon you can pull out, like a rabbit in a hat? You’ve ruined our vessel. We have no left arm. Fingers snapped. And you think I’ll rescue us. Think again. Fix this mess yourself.

“But you’ll die too.” Dread gnawed at Ciro, his ace, the reason he thought he’d win. It completely vanished.

So be it.

And there Ciro lay in the grips of the alien, his death imminent.

There would be no help.

Only his end.
