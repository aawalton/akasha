Misen shot forward with a slow punch. To his shock, Hayden not only blocked it easily, but also cleanly. Misen was bewildered by the finesse, so he immediately struck with his other hand.

Flawless block.

Hook, jab, kick. His heart fluttered in baffled fear as his strikes were blocked in rapid succession.

These weren't blocks of someone who practiced punches in a forest for a couple of weeks—these were the blocks of a trained fighter with years of experience!

Misen subconsciously increased the pressure. He threw elbows, shovel cuts, overheads—back fists. He went for thigh kicks, side kicks, shin kicks—everything.

His mind drowned the roaring screams out, leaving only the sound of his thundering heart. He experienced tunnel vision, and he lost track of his surroundings.

And then—it happened.

At some point in the flurry of punches and kicks, Hayden stepped out of the way, revealing the very edge of the stage. Misen tried to self-correct, but as he turned, he lost balance and stumbled off it—ending the match.

His face fell into a patch of ground cover, smearing his nose with the sharp, fresh smell of ripped leaves. That scent woke him up, and he could suddenly hear the rapture of the crowd. Misen felt absolute shame, and it only got worse when Hayden approached, intensifying the cheering.

You already won… Misen thought. Don't make it worse.

Hayden ignored his silent pleas and offered him a hand. “You hear that?” he asked.

Misen listened and heard someone say, “This is so cool!” He turned and noticed that people weren’t laughing. They were excited for him to accept Hayden's hand.

“Uh… what’s going on?” Misen asked.

“Your virtue bled through,” Hayden said. “Now come on—the people are waiting.”

Misen accepted Hayden’s hand, and the crowd went wild. He stood, and then, for no particular reason, he lifted his hand, and the crowd boomed with approval. Then, he said, “I let him win!” and cracked a smile.

The laughing students welcomed him back like a king after that, and it felt… good.

It sucked that he had to go to Galma for another year—but it was also kinda relieving. If he won, he would have to fight Rosen, and he would lose. Not because he was worse at martial arts—but because his spirit was weak compared to Rosen's. But now? Misen would get to see Hayden kick that asshole’s teeth in. That more than made it worth it!

The Redemption Festival was no longer a series of tournaments—it was a zeitgeist—a place and time that had its own spirit, soul, and culture. Everyone wanted to be right, to stand up to the villain and triumph over them. So they trumpeted on, cheering on Hayden as he won match after match, and raining vitriolic hate and boos on Rosen when he did the same. The more Hayden won, the more vicious Rosen became until he was a full-on villain, and no one could wait to see how Hayden would do him in.

To Rosen's credit, he wasn't a pushover. Despite the students’ drive to injure him, he still made it to the finals intact.

Misen checked the time after Rosen finished the last match and yelled. “There's still time. Fight! Fight! Fight!” He pumped his fist, and the others joined in with the chanting.

Rosen couldn't have been more miserable. Everyone had spent the tournament trying to break his will and fighting him just to do damage. By contrast, Hayden was their champion, and he didn’t have a scratch on him.

“You can't do this,” Rosen complained, holding his knees. “People have been working to sabotage me non stop. At least wait until tomorrow.”

“Fight! Fight! Fight!”

Rosen couldn’t even hear his heart over the bloodthirsty crowd.
