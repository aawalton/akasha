The first day at the farming outpost had been rather easy. After receiving their equipment, they had been fed and told to turn in for the night.

And Rex was glad he went to bed early, as they were woken at the crack of dawn and shuffled off to their respective groups.

At the edge of the farmlands, Rex stood beside the other hunters, rubbing at the sleep crusted on his eyes as his new boss coughed something up.

A fog hung thickly over the land, and a chill passed straight through his new leathers, even with his rags draped over as a cloak.

On the way over, one of his new comrades had filled him in on a few details. Apparently, the farmstead didn’t just supply wheat and vegetables to Tartarus. Not only was fresh game in high demand, but there was a stream not too far away, nestled in the forest. At the stream, Credo’s men ran a mill where they ground the wheat down into flour and a distillery that turned the rest into whiskey.

“That’s better.” The hunt leader coughed and pressed a finger to one nostril as he shot out another snot ball. “Alright, quiet down. You lot might know what you’re doing, but Rex here is new. So let me introduce myself. The name’s Mulvaney. I’m the hunt leader here. Jango’s got the farmstead, but the forest’s mine. I make the rules out here.”

“Rules? You gonna put us through them again, Mulvaney? C’mon, let the rest of us get to work. We don’t need to stand around because of this kid.”

“Oh, shut your whining. A team needs to be on the same page. Besides, this will only take a minute. Now, where was I? Right, right. First things first. We’re looking for game, not monsters. We want meat, not levels! Got me, newbie?”

“Yeah,” Rex nodded.

“Good. However, if we see goblins out there, come find me. We’ll deal with them together. It’s best we cull those green little bastards whenever we get the opportunity. They might not be strong, but they breed like rabbits and can be a real pest if we get an infestation.”

“But as for other beasts and monsters, we try to stay clear of them. Of course, self-defense is allowed if necessary. But if I find out that any of you are risking the mission because you want to try gaining a level or getting some loot, they’ll be hell to pay. Got it?” Mulvaney said.

“We know,” a couple of hunters grunted.

“Good. And keep an eye on the newbie. Finally, if you see a shadow beast, stay the fuck where you are and hope it doesn’t see you. If it’s already seen you, say your prayers to whichever gods you serve. You’re already dead. Oh, and don’t get lost. Nobody is coming looking for you if you do. If you’re lucky, another hunter might bump into you out there. But it's fucking cold out here at night.”

“Other than that, every corpse comes with a bounty. A copper for a hare. Two for a duck, a dozen for deer. Eight for a boar, and an entire sack for a moose. Anything else, one to two coppers based on size. It might not sound like a lot, but if you’re good, there's decent coin to be made. Now, any questions?”

Rex shook his head. The bounties were the first bit of good news he had heard. The thought of having to pay for his own arrows had worried him. This contract was supposed to give him a foundation to work towards something better, not indebted himself. But if he could earn extra coin… well, that changed everything.

Although, in truth, the bounty amounts weren’t amazing. But as long as he was earning some coin, that was good enough for now. Besides, grinding the mines back on Dorog was infinitely worse.

“Alright then, what the fuck are you sorry bastards waiting around for? Get out there, and bring me back some meat!”

The hunters didn’t need to be told twice and immediately spread out, taking several different paths deeper into the forest.

I guess I’ll go this way.

Rex hadn’t intended to follow anybody, but coincidentally, he was walking in the same general direction as a big man with a sack of traps over his shoulder.

The man turned and snorted at Rex. One of his eyes was clearly glass. “Keep following me, newbie, and I'll make sure you won’t be coming back.”

“Following? Me? I wasn’t… but okay, I guess I’ll go that way.”

“Fuck off!” Shouted another man, who was already walking in the direction Rex pointed.

“Excuse their manners. They don’t want anybody taking their prized hunting spots. Like I said, good coin to be made if you know how,” Mulvaney said. “It’s a big forest. Just find somewhere that’s not taken.

“Gotcha.” Rex nodded and started walking. He’d just gotten here and certainly didn’t want to start a fight. Besides, he could tell from their auras that the hunters were all stronger than he was. But after several steps, he stopped and turned back to Mulvaney. “Wait, one thing.”

“Huh? What is it?”

“What does a shadow beast look like?”

“Ha! A shadowbeast? They’re fucking big, is what they are. Covered in lots of black fur, with mean fangs and claws. Real nasty stuff.”

“Thanks.” Rex flicked back a wave and kept walking.

Like the hells, I wanna follow any of your ugly mugs, anyway. Rex thought as he glanced around, making sure nobody else was walking in his direction. Besides, I bet none of these chumps have ever tried to hunt on Dorog.

Rex might have been new to this world. But hunting gerbils back on Dorog was like squeezing water out of rocks. And he hadn’t been half bad at it.

Okay, let’s get to it. First things first. Find signs of life.

Walking through the forest, he got to work looking for tracks, scat, and any other signs that pointed to life. Unfortunately, for as good as he was at hunting on Dorog, this was an entirely different world, with a different nature and different beasts.

The first few hours went by with nothing to show for them. He had found some berries, though, and mushrooms. Unfortunately, he didn’t know if anything was edible.

I really need to get a book or something detailing what’s edible in this world.

After another hour, he noticed the sun was at the solstice as he drank from his waterskin.

Half a day wasted and nothing to show for it. Perhaps I won’t be making pockets full of coin.

On the bright side, it had warmed up, and the fog was gone.

Sighing, Rex tried to clear his thoughts. If he got irritated, it would only make matters worse. He needed to be calm and relaxed. He took several deep breaths, and as he stood there in silence, he heard the faint trickle of water.

A stream, huh? Well, that’s something.

It didn’t take him long to reach the stream. But it was rather rocky, with fierce rapids.

“Not an ideal place for drinking.”

He followed it upstream in search of spots more likely to be used by animals. And after several minutes of following it, he heard chattering in the distance.

“Voices? They don’t sound like the hunters.”

He kept walking, and the voices grew louder and clearer. They were squeaky, nasally, and speaking a language he had never heard.

His grip tightened on his bow. The alien language could only mean one thing. And he wasn’t supposed to go looking for monsters. But…

I really should go back, shouldn’t I?

But Rex didn’t go back. He kept walking. If he went back now, his first day as a hunter would likely end in disappointment. It was only one day, but he wanted to hit the ground running. He had spent his entire, relatively short life locked away on Dorog, and he wanted to experience more.

I mean, they might not have bounties, but coppers aren’t everything…

Rex didn’t like to think of himself as greedy, but he also didn’t want to be stuck guarding farmers in a forest forever. And the sooner he had a few coins and levels to rub together, the sooner he could leave this place and go find a real party to adventure with.

And monsters have artifacts, don’t they?

His eyes glimmered at the thought. Real artifacts in his hands. Perhaps the thought was a little reckless, but it wasn’t like anybody was watching him. Also, he hardly took Credo and his men as some paragons of virtue. Intentionally hunting monsters would be betraying their trust, but did he owe it to them in the first place?

They weren’t particularly honest with me. And they don’t seem to care if I get myself killed out here. Yeah, you know what, screw those guys.

Rex had a bow, and he had spells. He could sit back, plan things out, and deal with the monsters on his own terms without anyone being any wiser.

I mean, I’ll just take a peek. If it looks manageable, I take my chances. If not, I disappear back into the forest and keep looking for game. It’s foolproof.

He crept toward the voices, head low, keeping to the thickest vegetation, and darting between trees as he closed in on his target. And it didn’t take him long to spot several green little figures meandering near the entrance to a burrow alongside the stream.

Goblins.

Monsters were special in the sense that they already had their own powers, even without blessings from the Shrines. Then again, monsters were connected to the mystery of the Gates, and the Shrines that appeared when they did.

But goblins were at the very low end of the monster scale. And Rex knew plenty about them, as they were prime training for young, newly Classed people.

Goblins could see brilliantly in the dark. They also reproduced at a stunning rate. But besides that, they didn’t have too much going for them. Assuming you didn’t come across a shaman.

Shamans were spellcasters and had nasty tricks up their sleeves. When it came to hunting goblins, most books said two things: don’t fight them in the dark, and avoid shamans.

Just be careful. It’s nice and sunny out, and as long as I don’t spot any shamans, this is a perfect opportunity.

Rex pressed against a tree and watched for a while. At first, he spotted three goblins. But he wasn’t ready to attack just yet. He wanted to get a better vantage point and carefully climbed up several branches of a nearby tree.

After close to half an hour of spying on the little green monsters, he counted five. But during that time, a couple had come and gone from the burrow, leading him to believe that there were likely more inside.

He also noticed that they had a couple of buckets filled with fish. No one had mentioned fish to him, but it was meat, after all. Even if he didn’t claim a bounty, the fish might just prove his worth. And while Rex didn’t particularly care for his new employers, having something to show for himself wasn’t a bad idea.

Then again, they’re probably going to ask where I got the fish from. Oh fuck it. I’m way overthinking this!

Unfortunately, the goblin gear looked worthless. Sharpened sticks, stone hammers, and rough pieces of horribly treated leather were used as patchwork clothing.

But there were still levels to be gained. A reward beasts didn’t provide.

And who knows, there might be something in that burrow.

Rex eyed his targets, waiting for the two who were coming and going to head back into the burrow, and nocked an arrow. There were only three of the little guys, and he was up a tree.

His first target was the one closest. A bare-chested goblin sitting beside the stream. He didn’t even bother with a spell-inscribed arrow. Taking aim with a regular arrow, and loosening.

Unlike his previous bow, this new one could make use of his new strength, and his arrow shot through the air with incredible speed and power, piercing straight through the goblin’s flesh.

The monster gargled for a few seconds as blood fountained out of its throat, and then toppled forward.

Rex had already picked his next target and loosened his second shot. Unfortunately, the little guy was a bit further away and was already moving, running to find out what happened to the dying goblin. And his arrow missed its target, stabbing into its shoulder, eliciting a high-pitched scream.

Fuck!

The third goblin hadn’t yet been alerted and immediately jumped to its feet at the sound of its screaming companion. It looked around wildly, and seconds later, spotted Rex in the tree, and started pointing and yelling.

“Argu flug! Argu flug! Argu flug!”

The goblin barely got done screaming its warning when the third arrow slammed into its chest. It looked down dumbfoundedly at the arrow protruding from it and then grabbed it. The stubborn little thing tugged at the arrow and immediately realized the error of its ways as blood poured out from the open wound, and it fell over.

Rex then turned back to the wounded goblin and nocked another arrow, but before he even got done drawing it, the two other goblins emerged from the burrow, followed by a third considerably larger goblin.

The big goblin didn’t look like the rest. It looked mean, brown-tinged its green skin, and it wore crude metal plates bent around its body like armor.

Rex loosened another arrow, striking the wounded goblin in the chest, and immediately nocked another. But before he even picked his next target, another goblin emerged from the burrow, and then another.

Oh, shit!

Rex was pretty confident with his bow skills. But there were already five of the little monsters, and who knew how many more might be in the burrow. And for now, he still had distance between them.

Okay, I think it’s time to bail!

If he descended the tree quickly. If he could hit the ground running, he’d have a good head start, and even though he wasn’t the tallest boy his age, his legs were still a damn sight longer than the goblin’s.

Climbing down, he made it a couple of branches lower. But he was no expert tree climber, and the goblins, despite their little legs, were rather nimble. They ran and pounced through the underbrush, reaching the bottom of the tree well before Rex managed to descend.

Double shit!

He had no choice. He had to fight his way out, and he had to do it quickly. Rex drew another arrow and loosened. At near point-blank range, the arrow went straight through a goblin’s eye, and it dropped dead immediately.

In response, the armored goblin pulled down a crooked and crude visor on its makeshift helmet and taunted him with a laugh. It the proceed to slam its fists against its thick metal armor.

“Bluk, bluk, bluk! BRAH-RAH-RAH!”

It seemed that the large goblin was rather confident in its crude armor.

The next arrow took a few seconds longer, as Rex engraved it with [ Rune Scribe ] before releasing.

The goblin’s mocking laughter and smug smirk quickly faded when the arrow melted straight through its armor and into its flesh. Unlike the monstrosity guardian, the goblin didn’t have several inches of flesh to sacrifice, and as a chunk of flesh was melted away from its chest, its eyes went wide, and it fell lifelessly forward.

The third goblin, seeing its big companion drop dead before it, fled back to the burrow.

Rex actually turned back toward the fish buckets for a second. But there were already a half dozen goblins by the burrow entrance, and who knew how many more inside.

“Yeah, back to retreating!”

The goblins weren’t just standing around, either. They were lighting torches, and two of them ran at him as fast as they could, throwing the flames into the brush beneath him.

The fire took off quickly, aided by what looked like some kind of flammable tar the other goblins were throwing at it.

He had no choice. He eyed an empty patch of ground and jumped. It was quite a distance, and Rex braced himself for the fall, but when he hit the ground, he landed perfectly, and it didn’t hurt a bit.

Right, I’m Classed now, aren’t I?

His Class had given him Strength, Vitality, and Agility, and in the chaos of the fight, he had half forgotten how much stronger and more durable he was.

Even as he fled the fight, sprinting as fast as he could through the forest, a smile gripped his face. This was what it meant to be Classed. He was strong, and adrenaline was pumping through his veins like never before.

That said, when he glanced back, he realized that the fire was spreading, and rather quickly at that.

Ookay, maybe they will find out about this. That’s just great.
