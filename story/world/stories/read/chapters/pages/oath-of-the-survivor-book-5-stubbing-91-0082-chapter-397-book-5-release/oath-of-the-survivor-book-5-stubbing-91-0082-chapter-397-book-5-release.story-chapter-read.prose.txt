Garth huffed and puffed, his breathing labored as he hefted his sword one more time.  The hordes of fungal creatures just. Kept.  Coming.  By now, virtually all of the D Grades were spent, barely able to recover.  The defense had fallen, predictably, to himself, Chester Drake, and Rochelle.

Another of the fungal abominations got close, and he snarled as he twisted his hips, slashing through the creature’s torso and giving it a boot to knock it off the earthen ramp.  While they’d been prepared for the spores in the atmosphere to jam up the ship’s propulsion, they hadn’t expected them to also include mana-denial properties.  With their mana regeneration cut by nearly half, the ship’s defenses quickly ran dry, and the other defenders found themselves burning through resources faster than they could recover.  Worse still, the damage they accumulated was more difficult than it should have been to heal, forcing their medical team to pivot to emergency triage instead of helping with more full recoveries.

Chester had responded by creating a narrow choke point, which Garth now held, but this war of attrition didn’t feel like one they had much chance of winning.  They’d already been fighting nonstop for hours, and eventually exhaustion would take its toll.  In the meantime, these fungal creatures felt endless.  For every one Garth cut down, two more came into view as they mindlessly surged toward the Corthian Mining ship.

“Tag out, Boltsbury,” a weary voice said from behind him, and Garth took a step back as Rochelle stepped in, cutting deeply into another approaching creature with her heavy dagger before knocking it back.

“Appreciate it,” Garth panted, taking a few steps back and drinking deeply from his canteen.  He checked his nav bracelet, though unsurprisingly didn’t see any news having come through.  The jammed communications were expected, but he could only hope that the strike team was close to victory.  Though, looking at the endless tide of creatures, he couldn’t help but doubt a bit.  While he couldn’t see monsters like Kyle and Skippy losing, he also never expected to see a C Grade raise an army like the one they now faced.

A loud scream caused Garth’s head to snap up, just as Rochelle dispatched another two that attempted to slip past her.  The fungal creatures all stopped as one, shrieking and writhing in a rhythmic, undulating pattern.  A grin broke across Garth’s face.  Looks like they did it, he thought.  At least, until the tide began to advance once again.

Their wails still echoed across the cracked ground as they continued their assault, pressing forward with fanatic vigor.  Rochelle swore, killing three more in quick succession before Garth stood in the gap alongside her.

“There’s no end to them,” she spat, activating a skill as a volley of shadowy blades sprang out of her outstretched hand and pierced dozens of oncoming creatures.  The maneuver bought them a bit of breathing room, which Chester capitalized on as the earth began to rumble, stone sliding down to close the entrance and crush all of the creatures trapped within.

Garth looked over his shoulder to see the robed man approaching, though his gait was unsteady due to the exertion.  The reason they’d kept the narrow entryway was to serve as both a funnel and pressure release, and if Chester was closing it now…

“Is the ship operational?” Garth asked.

“At least the propulsion should be soon,” Chester confirmed, though his expression was still dark.

“What’s the problem?  I thought we had a solution to deal with the spores?” Rochelle asked, brushing a few strands of silver hair back.

“The engineers aren’t confident that the propulsion systems will be able to last while this mana drain effect is active.  Once we lift off, we’ll have to leave.  Otherwise we risk the environmental mana being unable to sustain our flight, and we’ll be grounded until the formations can fully recharge.”

Garth frowned.  “In other words, once we take off, we have to leave.”

“Precisely,” Chester said.  “I don’t relish the notion of leaving our colleagues, but it appears as though they failed in their mission.  We have to look forward.”

“We don’t know that they failed,” Garth pointed out.  “They clearly did something that had an impact on the creatures.”

“And nevertheless, the creatures keep coming.  Mr. Boltsbury, we’ve killed tens of thousands – far more than we ever expected to face.  And yet, despite that, we are still on the brink of being overwhelmed.  Even if the mana-denying formation in the atmosphere fades, it will take time for our forces to recharge.  Time that we simply don’t have.  If we retreat now, we can evaluate conditions down the line.  If we do not, we die.  And if Mr. Mayhew and his companions were successful, they will be trapped here too.”

Garth seethed at the words, even as he understood the wisdom behind them.  Even with Chester’s fortifications, it would only be a matter of time until the fungal creatures scaled the earthworks.  When they did, the ship would be beset on all sides.  And when that happened, the expedition would die.

Rochelle seemed to take the message much better.  “Understood, sir.  Are we maintaining a presence out here, or returning to the ship?”

“That’s up to you,” Chester said with a sigh.  “I’ve got enough mana left for a final parting gift, destroying the battlements in the process, but once that’s done we’ll be exposed.  We’ll have to leave.”

Garth’s mind was working overtime, trying to find a better answer.  “So effectively, we have until they’ve about scaled the earthworks?  That’s our timeline?”

Chester nodded.  “It is.  However soon or long it may be, we can wait that long.”

“Then I’ll see about buying us a little more time,” Garth said with a sigh.

He was tired.  So, so tired.  But he wasn’t going to turn his back on Kyle.  Not again.  His boosting skill wasn’t designed for protracted combat, particularly when his party wasn’t nearby to further empower it.  However, with the expedition looking to leave soon…

Garth looked at Rochelle.  “You’re done fighting?”

The woman nodded.  “We’re not putting a dent in numbers like these, so yes.”

“You’re going to feel a pull on your mana soon.  It’s from me, let it happen.  Send word to the others inside.”  Garth then looked at Chester.  “I’m going to exclude you, the Healers, and the engineers.  Once it’s done, I’m going to see about buying us some time.”

“Good hunting, then,” Rochelle said, walking up the ramp into the ship.

Chester sighed.  “Don’t do anything foolish, Mr. Boltsbury.  I’m not endangering the rest of the expedition, not even for you.”

The warning in Chester’s tone only emphasized the words, but Garth didn’t particularly care.  He trusted that Kyle’s party would emerge victorious, and was going to do whatever he needed to in order to buy them time.  After waiting just another minute, Garth took a deep breath and activated Fight as One, the upgraded version of his Leader’s Mantle.

At first, he only received a small trickle of power, which quickly grew into a torrent.  The spectral cloak appeared around his shoulders, along with the instinctive knowledge of the new abilities his skill brought him.  The mana fluctuated and stabilized, and Garth knew that he only had a few minutes.  Now, he just had to make sure it was enough.

With a leap, the cloak helped him soar skyward, where he got a sense for the battlefield.  It wasn’t good.  The horde of fungal creatures seemed to be growing every passing second, with shambling creatures as far as the eye could see.  Exhaling slowly, Garth tightened his grip on his sword, and began channeling mana.  Though he didn’t understand all the abilities that he’d gained access to, one type was very familiar.  Fire.

Infusing his blade with fiery mana, he also wove in Rochelle’s Shadow Daggers skill before releasing a crescent wave of destruction.  The attack separated into dozens of flaming versions, striking the oncoming horde and exploding, clearing the wall in a section and leaving a conflagration that burned through more of the fungal creatures.  While the overall potency of the attack was low, it covered an incredibly wide area, which more than satisfied Garth.

Still airborne, he launched volley after volley, quickly clearing the earthen walls and leaving a veritable wildfire raging outside.  The inefficient techniques rapidly burned through his borrowed mana, and he could feel that the skill was about to disperse.  He started to fall, but just as the wave of weakness came upon him, he saw a gleaming, golden light in the distance.  A smile came over his face as he landed hard next to the ship.  Kyle was on his way.
