“What did I do?” I asked as I watched her eyes and facial features, trying to spot any hint of what had upset the woman.

“I uh, spoke to a few of my friends, while I was making calls, we uh, spoke about your cultivation master, for a bit…” she trailed off still looking reluctant to actually tell me.

“Uh, no… Well, uh, according to my friends, it seems he was pressured into taking three disciples, one shortly after you vanished another about a thousand years ago and the most recent was a couple of centuries ago. “So I have three juniors? Why are you making it sound like the end of the world?” I asked, feeling confused.

“It's not the end of the world,” she said, looking at me like I was asking something dumb. “It’s three connections to powerful clans that will pressure the ancestor of the sect into advising you to prove who you are before being allowed anywhere near them, and their perceived right to the ancestor’s position once your Cultivation Master dies. If you live, then you have the right to claim the position of Sect Ancestor, or at least a significant chunk of the inheritance left behind.”

“Ah, I see, sect politics,” I said, waving my hand dismissively as I turned and headed for the exit of the ship. “Sect politics don't interest me. I would rather remain out here enjoying myself than arguing with dusty fossils.”

“Your Cultivation Master and I know that, but the great clans don't believe that for a second. They believe everyone wants that position, or the prestige that comes with having a clansman in that position, just like they do,” She replied as she hurried to follow me.

“That sounds like a ‘them’ problem,” I replied, dismissing it from my mind.

“No, it is definitely a ‘you’ problem,” she snapped back at me as we rounded a corner, seeing the airlock a few dozen feet from us.

“How do you figure?” I asked as I glanced at her.

“Before, you were his only disciple. They had no claim on his position or his inheritance, but now he has accepted others, and they do have a claim. That means, while you were the only one, you were protected by sect law. Now that you're not, the great clans can, legally speaking, see you as a threat to their claim.”

“They wouldn't try to ensure I remain dead, in the eyes of the sect, would they?” I asked as we passed through the airlock.

“Nah,” she replied, drawing out the ‘a’ before adding, “they would send assassins to ensure you are actually dead.”

I stopped mid-step, feeling trepidation slowly creeping up my spine, before slowly turning to look at her. “Serious?”

“Deadly,” she replied as she nodded her head.

I slowly turned back towards the exit of the hangar as my mind rushed over the problem, trying to find an out, before finally giving up and asking “options?”

“Reach the immortal realm before they arrive or hope they suck at assassinating you,” she replied in a deadpan voice.

“Once I am finished talking to an old friend, I will go into closed-door cultivation, do not disturb me until Zeo Walker arrives at the station.” I glanced at her to make sure she understood.

“That would be a wise decision, I will see to it that you are not disturbed and will suggest everyone onboard follows your example, and cultivates more diligently,” she replied as she gave me a half-bow. “I will also return to the ship now and prepare your cultivation room for a closed-door cultivation,” she suddenly turned away, heading back to the ship.

I gave a small wave before exiting the hangar, leaving her and the ship behind. My mind immediately began racing, scrambling to form plans—only for each one to be blown away moments later by the same nagging conclusions: I had no idea what the dwarf leader intended to discuss, nor when the assassins might find me.

I let out a sigh, deciding that I would have to play it by ear, then started giggling as I realised whenever I began doing something crazy, I was always playing it by ear.
