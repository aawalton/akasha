Golden-white fire exploded upward across the walls and from the inner areas of the floor with the violence of a detonating bomb, atomizing the altar and blasting outward through every wall and window in the compound. The remaining stained-glass exploded outward into shards, the chapel doors blew off their hinges, and the roof detonated into the sky as a ball of radiant golden-white flames.

The blast wave caught Kate from behind, and her scream joined the destruction as she lifted from the floor and was thrown over the pew, her cloak, flesh, and armor igniting while something in the right side of her chest cracked beneath her half-plate. She struck stone somewhere within the chapel, bounced twice, and slid until her shoulder slammed into the chapel wall.

A shrill ringing filled her ears, and every other sound faded beneath it.

Kate remained blessedly—or perhaps spitefully—conscious, though she needed several moments to remember how breathing worked after the explosion. Her cloak continued burning across her back, and she rolled onto her side with a sharp hiss of agony to tear the flaming fabric away before the fire could reach her hair. Every single twitch sent a lance of pain through what she was identifying as broken ribs, but her Health—by some miracle—remained above 30%, and nothing in her body felt incapable of answering her desire to move.

Then, as if to drive home the nail of inconvenience, a System message populated the bottom left of her HUD, in her ‘chat’ window.

You are suffering from [Censured]!

Mana regeneration has been suppressed.

Dark mana restorative effects are reduced by 75%!

Remaining Duration: 00:12

I really fucking hate Light users, Kate decided after reading the message.

Instead of wallowing in her situation, however, she instead pressed her right hand against the ruined floor and forced herself to one knee, hissing in pain and forcing herself to look around. The rear half of the chapel had become a macabre demolition of guttering golden-white flames and shattered masonry, while smoke rolled through the obliterated hole that had once been the ceiling and roof, and obscured whatever remained of the altar.

Gareth, she noticed blearily, lay amid the wreckage near the center of the destruction—shield split apart, and his armor peeled open in brutal fashion by the results of the blast.

“Kay!”

Darian’s “Romulus” voice pierced the shrill ringing in her ears at the same time as his presence registered to her senses, and she turned to see him charge through the burning doorway. Lightsbane rippled with black fire, and Darian sliced apart a fallen roof beam blocking his advance, kicking it aside and marching toward her. Radiant flames twisted and hissed as they came into contact with his skull-adorned warplate, but Darian—or rather, Romulus, as she had to try to force herself to think of him in Eternus to avoid slip-ups—ignored the damage in order to reach her when his scarlet eyes found her against the wall.

You are no longer suffering from [Censured]!

All Dark Mana regeneration penalties removed!

“Took you long enough!” she rasped through the alert, her voice hoarser than she’d hoped.

Her best friend crossed the chapel in seconds after she spoke and dropped to an armored knee beside her, sheathing Lightsbane as his placed his gauntleted right hand against her bleeding flank and looped his armored left arm around her upper body, taking her weight with his strength. The tension in his face was extreme, bordering on outright fury mixed with panic, and Kate noticed his fingers tremble once visibly before he forced them to remain steady.

“Can you stand?” Darian asked after Kate saw the alert, while his eyes moved across her body, taking stock of her injuries with a faint, unfairly beautiful brightening of his delightfully deadly scarlet eyes.

“Help me up, dark knight, and we’ll find out,” Kate quipped, locking her jaw against the pain in her ribs.

Darian obliged her when she spoke, tightening his grip around her upper body and lifting carefully—taking her entire weight until Kate found her feet. The first attempt at putting proper weight on her soles and taking a step made her injured ribs grind together, white flash across her vision, and nearly folded her; but Darian caught her immediately and held her upright while she groaned through the pain.

“I can sort of walk,” Kate declared finally, once her breathing steadied.

“With help, yes,” Darian qualified in a voice that was far more controlled than the fear she saw lingering in his gaze.

“That still counts, jackass,” she retorted without ire, smiling up at him.

Darian didn’t argue further, and instead Kate produced one of the healing potions he’d given her before the Hunt. She swallowed it, felt a warmth move through her body, and watched her Health recover immediately despite the almost-gone [Censured] status. Apparently, the potions didn’t count against the prohibition.

“Did you get the Paladin?” she asked while lowering her hand and stowing the potion bottle away.

“Yeah. Roderick’s dead,” Darian answered, his eyes still searching her for hidden injuries in a way that made her stomach flutter.

“Good,” she said in turn, focusing on the world around them instead of his gaze, “I got the support caster, too.”

“I noticed,” her best friend replied, glancing away finally to take stock of the chapel. As he did, he abruptly froze when his eyes drifted toward the altar, and a moment later Kate saw his gaze brighten faintly, and distantly felt the use of Dark Mana emanating from him.

“Lulu?” she asked quietly, forcing herself not to call him “Dee”.

“There’s another heartbeat,” Darian—Romulus, she mentally corrected herself again—said with predatory coldness. “Beneath the rubble.”

Kate nodded and together they made their way across the ruined and burning sanctuary, with his arm supporting her, and crossed through the shattered remnants of the chapel until they reached Gareth’s corpse. The Squire was unquestionably dead. His armor had been ripped open from shoulder to spine, and much of the flesh beneath had been charred beyond recognition. His body remained curled protectively over the woman beneath him, however, and Romulus released Kate long enough to seize the corpse and toss it aside with a disinterest that only mildly perturbed her.

She had respected Gareth’s sacrifice. It almost felt wrong for her best friend to be so dismissive. The thought was filed away for later review as Sara came into view, and Kate cursed quietly.

The Priestess’s white and gold robes had been burned to gray and blackened in several places. Her blonde hair, once immaculate, hung loose and blood-stained around her face, and the wound from Kate’s [Null Arrow] remained visible within her forearm. Her staff had been shattered, and she looked as if she’d been kicked in the chest by a battering ram, but even so, her chest continued to rise with shallow, stubborn breaths.

Mortarius chose that moment to manifest alongside them both and looked down at the unconscious Priestess with cold disdain.

“Reap her,” the former Revenant-King advised. “She is dangerous even while defeated, and every moment you remain here gives the local forces more time to surround you, to say nothing of the mortal King’s garrison.”

Kate turned to Romulus and saw a strange expression on his face, somewhere between consideration and cold arithmetic, before he turned toward Mortarius.

“No,” her best friend said finally. “We take her with us.”

Kate’s eyebrows rose, and Mortarius’s expression flattened.

“A living Priestess of Hyperion is an extraordinary liability, Romulus,” the spectral former Autarch said warningly. “Her power is not something you can easily overcome. You were lucky she was occupied with aiding the Paladin. Her own magical ability far exceeds his.”

“He’s right, Lulu,” Kate murmured. “She almost managed to stop the explosion by herself.”

“I know,” Romulus agreed without argument, though the tone of his voice was not one of concession, and he turned to his spectral mentor. “But going up against a Hyperian Crusade without any information is just as dangerous, if not more so. You know Hyperion’s Crusades from when you were still Revenant-King, Mortarius, but you’ve been sealed inside the Necropolis for centuries while Hyperion’s Church has continued to exist, grow, change, and evolve outside of your observation.”

Mortarius’s expression tightened, but he didn’t interrupt as Romulus continued.

“The Priestess knows how the modern Faith works, Mortarius. She knows their tactics, their strategies, potentially even how they’d organize a march. I’m not assuming she’s going to tell us anything easily, or that she’ll tell us the truth at all—but if the choice is between killing her and receiving nothing or sparing her for potential intel, and then just killing her later if the matter is forced, I choose to not get rid of potentially the only source of reliable intelligence we’re liable to find about the fanatics coming to slaughter us all.”

Kate found herself nodding along to her best friend’s words with surprised approval, and turned to look at Mortarius, who was staring at Romulus in silence. The displeasure on the specter’s features was evident, but that seemed more tied to being reminded of how long the former Autarch had been removed from the world. His gaze returned to Sara after several more moments’ thought, and finally his features settled into resigned consideration.

“Your reasoning is sound, Romulus,” Mortarius conceded. “And the Necropolis has the means to contain her. It will also satisfy your quest requirements, since neutralizing her via the Necropolis’s holding cells is just as good as killing her.”

“Then we’ll need to—”

“Hey!” Kate cut in. “Can we debate the details of prisoner policy somewhere that isn’t collapsing?” she asked, glancing toward the unsteady walls.

Before either Romulus or Mortarius could answer, a new voice cut through the ruined chapel.

“Milord! Yer alive!”

All three of them turned to see Bonnie appear through the destroyed doors with her sword drawn, breathing heavily from what was evidently a sprint back across the city to the compound. Her eyes were wide as she looked across the state of the chapel, passed quickly over Kate’s partially-healed injuries, and settled on the unconscious Priestess around whom they stood.

“They watch be turnin’ back from the gates,” Bonnie warned before anyone could speak. “Whole city damn near saw the bloody flash, milord. We’ve minutes, maybe less, afore this place do be crawlin’ with Wardens.”

Kate groaned, and Romulus glanced at Mortarius before looking back to Bonnie.

“Find something we can move the Priestess on, Bonnie,” he commanded immediately. “A litter, a cart, anything—and something to cover her with, too. We don’t want the city asking too many questions about why we’re carrying away a Priestess of Hyperion.”

Bonnie blinked at him, and then simply nodded and disappeared toward the attached living quarters without questioning why Sara Lightspeaker remained alive.

Kate knelt beside the Priestess in the interim, groaning at the subtle ache still present even after the healing potion, and started searching her immediately for magical accouterments—stripping away her rings, her sunburst necklace, her bangles, and anything else that could reasonably pose as an artifact or means of channeling her powers.

Romulus offered her his [Bag of Holding], and she deposited everything inside of it with a wry smile, methodically searching the Priestess across every inch of her body—and trying not to be too invasive about it—before she was satisfied. Rings, prayer beads, several scrolls, and a small golden icon joined the rest in Romulus’s bag before she was done—and Romulus used Lightsbane, for good measure, to destroy what little was left of her shattered staff.

“Better you than me,” Romulus muttered after Kate rose carefully to her feet.

“What, you don’t want to feel up the hot priestess?” Kate asked slyly.

“Firstly, she’s not that hot,” Romulus grumbled, “and secondly, no, that would be pretty creepy—even if she’s, you know, Eternian.”

That was code for “NPC” without saying it, Kate knew, but she understood the sentiment.

“Well, she is that hot, but yes, I get it,” she said in reply and patted his pale cheek. “Good to know you’re still captain chivalry beneath the fangs and blood.”

Romulus grunted at her and muttered something under his breath that Kate didn’t catch while Mortarius simply observed them flatly.

Bonnie returned less than a minute later, following that, with a narrow wheeled casualty litter taken from the compound’s infirmary. Romulus immediately bent to scoop up Sara after she did, and Kate proceeded to bind the Priestess’s wrists and ankles, as well as gag her, by tearing off pieces of her dress from non-exposing areas. Bonnie also used a blanket to cover her from head to toe, carefully positioning it so it wouldn't obstruct her breathing.

“Can you get us past the patrols and into the Necropolis, Bonnie?” Romulus asked when they were done.

“Aye, milord,” Bonnie agreed, “though it do be a shitstorm out there. Y’may want t’be prepared fer the worst.”

“Always,” Romulus agreed, and then glanced at Kate. “Ready to go?”

“Ready,” she agreed, and this time when he stepped closer to help her walk, she didn’t argue. Not only because she was still in pain, despite her body slowly healing, but because—and she’d only admit it to herself—it felt nice to be held by him.

Together, they set off into the night as Blackstone devolved into chaos, led by a spectral undead King, identities shielded by an enthralled guardswoman, and leaving behind them enough bodies and carnage to fill a mausoleum.

All in all, Kate thought as they moved through the city, it was a fruitful day.
