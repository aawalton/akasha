Magda, the Paragon of the Sliding Surge, just wanted to head to her lair, feast on a few monsters, and wait for all of this mess with the God of Thunder and the Stormblade to blow over.

She’d done her part, after all.

Her deal with the God of Thunder had been simple. Shockingly so. She snorted, but held back her laugh as she curled up in her floating underwater cavern. And with it finished, she had the God of Thunder’s assurances that not only would he avoid destroying the portal world she called home, but he’d leave behind a gift. Any of the Placid King’s core he didn’t need to fuel his own ascension, he’d let her use to try empowering her own push to S-Rank.

“I’ve been trapped for too long,” she muttered as she pulled herself tight around her missing fin.

That was true. Magda had been A-Rank before the God of Thunder was more than a battery for Stormlord Nalthelas. She’d accepted her lot in life—an immortal Paragon, without the power to make her will manifest in either a portal world or her own Path. The Sliding Surge was an incomplete Path, and nothing she could accomplish at A-Rank would change that.

But she’d had powerful allies. The Placid King had found her and brought her into the fold. She wasn’t a battery. Her core was all but worthless to someone as mighty as an SS-Plus-Ranked Paragon like the Placid King. But her knowledge…

Magda knew things.

She was ancient, after all. By some measures, she was one of the four or five oldest beings remaining in the universe. The Crone was older, as were a handful of other stalled Paragons who’d settled between B and S-Rank.

The God of Thunder was significantly younger. She’d been cradle robbing from the moment she met him.

Magda was in the middle of reminiscing about her core bonds with a younger, less obsessed with ascension God of Thunder when—

Tension.

Electricity.

She stiffened. Was he here?

No. No, his overwhelming presence was absent. In its place was something darker. Something that both slunk in the shadows and drew them around her like a veil. The Shadow Witch. And the Stormblade.

They’d figured it out.

The Guardians had dug in around the Wickenberg Portal.

It didn’t matter.

We rushed through, Ellen trailing Darkness behind us and me with lightning arcing from my every footfall. We didn’t bother fighting. There was no need to. We wouldn’t be in the Wickenberg Portal for more than a few minutes, anyway.

And we didn’t bother with subtlety once we got inside, either.

Straight to Magda’s floating fortress of a lair. We dove and emerged inside after a handful of seconds, the Storm’s Blade in my hand and Ellen in her purple and black Voidwalker form. Her aura crashed out, overwhelming the A-Rank Paragon’s own. My sword flicked out, stopping inches from her massive, wide eyes.

“How many times will you break into my home?” she asked, trying to make her voice as haughty and imperious as she could. I wasn’t fooled. She shook slightly, her remaining fins wobbling back and forth, and her eyes reflected a fear-distorted version of me.

I’d seen that look before. It was usually right before a playground bully realized that I was not going to stop until they changed, and that I wasn’t afraid of the teachers, the principal, or anyone else—including Dad.

I rolled my eyes. “We know you set us up.”

“Why are you still playing this game, Magda? You’re too small for it.” Ellen let her Voidwalker form fall away. She looked exhausted, and she hadn’t fully healed from the wounds the God of Thunder had inflicted on her.

Magda’s wide mouth opened, revealing dozens of small, sharp teeth. Then she closed it. When she did speak, her tone had shifted. “Yes, I set you up. Sorry about that.”

“Sorry?” I asked. Fury dripped into my voice.

And Magda noticed. “Yes. I’m sorry. I’m a small fish, and the Placid King and God of Thunder swim in big ponds. So, yes, I set you up. I offered exactly what the God of Thunder was willing to trade—information on his past, and true information at that. He didn’t lie to you in that world. He can’t lie to you in that world. Everything you saw is something I’ve known about him for a long time, and it’s all the truth, I swear.

“And in return, you’d take the bait. As the timer ticked down, you’d be distracted. He knows you, and he predicted that you’d focus on what you’d learned. What he let me show you. You’d leave someone vulnerable.”

“He was right.” I ground my teeth.

“Of course he was right. My core bond partner—though I suppose at this point I should say former partner—has been doing this for twenty thousand years, Stormblade. You can’t outmaneuver him.”

Another drip of hubris found its way into Magda’s voice, and I balled my fist as she spoke. My every instinct screamed to lash out with the Storm’s Blade. I could kill this A-Ranked Paragon in a handful of seconds, and she hadn’t even uncurled to fight properly. She lay in a ball, trying her best to look small and pathetic.

And it was working. I hated that it was working, but it was.

“So, you were out of position when the timer ran out, and that swordswoman with the black eyes—“

“Tanat,” Ellen muttered.

“—Yes, her. The swordswoman was alone.”

“Why her?”

Magda’s gigantic eyes flicked back and forth from Ellen to me. Then she sighed. “Probably because she was the third best target, and the only one alone.”

“Who else?”

The question escaped my lips before I could stop myself. I didn’t need to ask. The answer was obvious already.

“The Shadow Witch was the second best, and the one he’d been hoping for, I think. But the one he wanted, ideally, was your sister.”

There was no coming to terms with Magda.

She was a dagger pointing at my weakest spot. She knew about Jessie, and she knew—because the God of Thunder knew—that I’d do whatever it took to protect her.

But I couldn’t eliminate her. Not because I didn’t want to. Her pathetic act wasn’t convincing enough to make up for what she’d done. But if I did, it’d be resources, and the God of Thunder was waiting. Our battle plan was waiting. As soon as Ellen and I returned to the Portal Tyrants’ castle, it’d be time to go to war.

Spending resources on fighting Magda would be a mistake.

I knew that. But even so…

The Stormbreak fired before I could stop it. The positive around me, the negative around her. Her eyes widened. She started to say something.

Flowers of lightning rippled out as my Mana drained to fuel my most powerful Unique skill. Lightning surged into the eel, ripping at her half-healed wounds and coursing across her slippery scales. She screamed. One pulse after another. Five. Six. Seven. Eight. Nine.

I readied the Storm’s Blade. She’d make a move. Surely, she’d make a move. A pressure built up in my core as Stormbreak ended. My sword hovered inches from the massive eel’s eye.

“Kade, we need to—“

“I know, Ellen.” The sword was ready. I could plunge its lightning-and-wind-wreathed length into the Sliding Surge’s skull and end her entire Path right here. Her body was already twitching and writhing from the electricity.

She opened her mouth to say something.

“Don’t. I’ve beaten up dozens of schoolyard bullies like you. Nothing any of them said mattered. You want to live? Stay in this god damn floating lair until this is over. If I see you again, I will kill you.”

The Sliding Surge sobbed slightly as I sheathed my sword, her entire body collapsing as tension poured out of it. We dove and headed for the portal world’s exit.

“I should have killed her,” I muttered as we stepped through and blazed past the C and B-Rankers who’d pulled guard duty.

“I’m surprised you didn’t,” Ellen said.

“Well, I recognized the bully in her. She was broken. Maybe not utterly defeated, but broken. Especially after Stormbreak. I don’t think she ever intended for her relationship with the God of Thunder to get this messy, or for her servitude to the Placid King to end in his death.”

“That’s not all, though.”

“No.” I sighed. We kept running until we passed over the 303 Wall, using Lightning Chain and Stormrunner to catapult over the barrier. I had to carry Ellen for that part, and she rolled her eyes when we landed. “Sorry. No. The more important bit is that Stormbreak. It pushed the skill. It’s ready to reach S-Rank, and I didn’t want to do that there. If we’d had to fight, it would have gotten harder to avoid pushing it to S-Rank mid-fight.”

“That’s not a bad thing.”

“No. But it’d be better to do it somewhere safe. I’ve got a feeling.”

Ellen nodded. “I trust you.”

We ran silently until we arrived at the Portal Tyrant’s castle. The place was in absolute chaos. Everywhere we looked, Portal Tyrants hurried down the halls, armoring up and readying weapons. I closed my eyes, relief washing over me. Jessie and the Portal Tyrant himself were clearly hard at work. They’d rallied his entire guild, and now…now, things were going to get tricky. The God of Thunder wanted to target my sister. He knew my weak point. But at the same time, I needed her at the GC Tower for this next part.

But before that…

Before that, I ducked into the room Ellen and I had claimed. I wouldn’t be there for long. Probably not five minutes.

But Stormbreak pressed on my core. It needed to grow.

I closed my eyes.

I expected the Stormblade Path’s tower, or my mountain.

Not my old living room.

My butt was planted in an armchair, a cup of steaming tea on the oak coffee table. That had been destroyed during my first Stormbreak. So had the entire living room. I’d fought the Vision of Family here, too—but that had been different. That had been inside the Fallen Delvers Portal. This felt both more real and much more fake.

And, of course, a chessboard, the pieces in the middle of a game. It took only a second to analyze the board and come to a sobering conclusion. I was losing. And that meant…I looked up.

Across from me sat the Stormkin Avatar. Not my dad, but the Avatar of him. His lightning-framed body rested on a wooden chair, leaning against the straight back. “Kade.”

I stiffened. The Stormkin Avatar had never spoken before. But his voice was correct. A tear formed in my eye, and I reached up to wipe it away. Then I stopped. This was my father. He’d seen me cry hundreds of times—from pain, from frustration, from impotent rage at situations I couldn’t solve with my fists or thoughts. I could cry here.

“Dad.”

“Son.”

Silence. It wasn’t uncomfortable, but even though time didn’t pass during a skill rank-up or Law-Learning trance, I couldn’t help but feel the pressure. After what felt like an eternity, Dad spoke again. “Tell me about your promises.”

“I’m doing my best. I’ve taken care of Jessie.”

“How?”

I sighed. My eyes closed again as I tried to compose myself. “You know how she is.”

“I do.”

“She still gets to her appointments. Maybe not the last three days, but I make sure she gets her therapy and medications. And…well, it was rough for a while. We were in a one-bed apartment until I got my delving build figured out, but I always made sure she had privacy and space I wasn’t in, as best I could. Then there was the Governing Council job. She wanted to do her part. To help out. That’s all she wanted, and I decided she needed it.”

“And that helped take care of her?”

“Yes. If I hadn’t let her take that job, she’d have found something similar online, but somewhere I couldn’t make sure she’d be okay. The GC seemed like a safe option at the time, and she was ready to take more responsibility.”

Silence. This time, it lasted a little too long to be comfortable. Dad’s electric brow raised as he watched me. I reached down and moved a pawn.

“About time, Kade.” He quickly moved a bishop, and I winced. He’d set that up. Against anyone else, I’d have accused him of cheating—of rigging the board—but this was Dad. He’d never lost to me. Ever. As I scratched my head, he continued talking. “And your partner. Eleanor Traynor. The promise you made to her.”

“Right.” I moved a knight to block his bishop. It was backed up, so it’d be a viable roadblock unless he decided to trade. “Ellen’s my Dual Skill Progression partner, and my…I’m not sure girlfriend is the right word. It feels like a lot more than that. I told her I was with her for the long haul, and I meant it. We’re S-Rank together, and we’ll go as far as we can without losing ourselves along the way.”

“Why promise that to her?”

“Because…Dad, I can’t explain it. She knew about Dual Skill Progression, but she didn’t say anything about it until she was already using my shoulder as a pillow in a tent somewhere in the New Mexico desert. She never tried to use her last name—you know about the Traynors—to get anything, or to have things go her way. It’s always felt like a very equal partnership, and I’d only gotten that with Jeff before. But with Jeff, things are different.”

Dad laughed. He moved the bishop, taking the knight, and I saw it. My face fell, and he nodded. “Yeah. Without that knight, you can’t threaten my queen. Check next turn. Nothing you can do about it.”

The game ground on. Our conversation continued. My commitments to the GC. To Phoenix as a whole. The promise I’d made, implicitly, when I’d cleared the Fallen Delvers Portal—that I’d defend the city using that power. One to Jeff, made just a few hours ago. I’d help him recover after he used the null core.

With every promise, the situation on the chessboard grew more and more dire. I found myself hemmed in. Beset on all sides. The balance of power in the pieces grew lopsided; he had almost all his power pieces, while I had a bishop, a rook, and a handful of pawns. One by one, those pawns vanished.

Then he raised his hand. “Last promise, Kade. Do you think you’ve made me proud?”

I swallowed. The chessboard sat unchanged. It was my move, but I couldn’t think of anything that’d get me out of the mess I’d found myself in. I’d blundered over and over, and Dad had played a perfect game. I shrugged. “I hope so. I’ve tried my best to keep my promises. I’ve kept the battle trance in check as much as I can. Ellen, Jeff, Jessie, everyone I’ve given promises to…”

Silence for a third time. It lingered. Dad’s electric eyes trapped mine. I couldn’t look away if I wanted to.

“I have no idea if I’ve made you proud. I thought I had. Back when I won the Fallen Delvers Tournament, it felt like I’d won it for you. And then I got the Stormkin Avatar. I thought that was confirmation. That I’d never get the chance to know the answer for sure. But now, you’re here. So, I guess it’s a question for you.” I stared Dad right in the eyes. “Have I, Dad? Made you proud?”
