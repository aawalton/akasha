Ryland felt a little vindicated that his suspicions had been realized. Yet, the vindication wasn’t pleasant. He’d much rather have been wrong about the fact that Mavar Val Arona would present himself to stop Marionys Ryland.

At least he had been justified in bringing Viren. With the protections layered over Viren, he should at least remain safe no matter what happened.

“My, my,” Mavar Val Arona said. “Have I, the Demiurge, left the great Marionys Ryland stupefied?”

He floated in front of the glowing white-gold moon disturbingly lacking any pockmarks or craters like he was observing a racket match. The casualness was almost forced, though still carefully considered. His gold-and-silver robes, the black bits of his scaled armour, the lone amulet he had around his neck. It all seemed an everyday getup for someone of Mavar’s stature.

Not something he’d wear to fend off a threat that eclipsed him in true prowess.

Right now, Ryland wasn’t in the mood to bandy words. For good reason too. “I’m afraid I’m only considering how much of my time I should waste on you, Mavar. I have more important things to handle.”

“Like your missing student?”

For the first time in a long time, Ryland had to work to maintain an internal equanimity. “Where is she? Answer me, Mavar, before I rip apart every single thing you’ve strived to achieve across the centuries.”

“I’m afraid that I cannot. I need the girl. More importantly, I need the connection to Living Mana she possesses.” Mavar turned contemplative. “I don’t know what you did or where you found her, but her very Soul Glyph is infected with Living Mana. I’ve never seen such a thing. I can’t believe it occurred without my knowledge.”

He said the last bit with what sounded almost like relish. But it confirmed that there was indeed a conspiracy afoot in the Crypts.

“You bastard.” Viren couldn’t quite control his motion in his weightlessly floating state in the cosmos, but he did a good job of shaking his fist at the ruler of Rhel. “Give Acrypha back now!”

Mavar pointedly ignored him. A good thing, according to Ryland.

“Can you not wait, Ryland?” Mavar asked plaintively. “It won’t take long. Unfortunate incidents have made me accelerate operations, so I will be done before long. Just have patience and I will be done.”

“And what will happen to Acrypha once you’re done?” Ryland asked.

Mavar frowned. “Extracting Soul Glyphs is tremendously difficult, especially when there is Living Mana involved. Especially when it’s a Bloodline Glyph that evolved with Living Mana.”

Ryland wanted to counter that he had removed Mavar’s Soul Glyph from all the people he had infected with relative ease, but the case was certainly different. For Acrypha, the Soul Glyph was her own. And then there were all the other complications the Demiurge had mentioned.

“But why?” Ryland asked. He had begun to unleash his manasoul, the chains around his Incarnation form snapping away to imbue with unrestrained power. “Answer me, Mavar.”

The Demiurge made a show of huffing. Of acting insulted, almost offended, that Ryland was so ignorant that Mavar had to waste his precious time explaining things. “Have you not figured it out yet? I want to gather Living Mana.”

“I figured as much.”

Mavar shook his head. He seemed to be growing more intense as he had to explain more, his body moving more animatedly. “All the Living Mana, Ryland. All of it. In all the Realms. And unlike you, I’ll deal with it for good.”

Ryland closed his eyes for a moment. Memories rose high, incidents where he and Mavar had fought together. Not just against those who had sought Rhel for its people and resources, but against internal threats too, many of which had come as a result of the Living Mana that had been rampant on the world centuries ago.

“Was that what this whole exchange semester was about?” Ryland asked. “A means for you to gain access to the Living Mana across the other Realms of the cosmos?”

Mavar tutted. “Don’t say it like that. You know Vyrd, Kuldor, and Jure all agree with the idea. You aren’t about to oppose your own Realm’s plan are you?”

“What do you intend to do with the Living Mana, Mavar? Why are you gathering it all at the moon specifically?”

Mavar slowly turned around, happily putting his back towards Ryland, well assured that he wasn’t going to be attacked from behind. Not by Ryland of all people. “You understand, don’t you, that destroying every bit of Living Mana from every Realm in existence is a tremendous undertaking?”

“Living Mana doesn’t exist in every Realm in existence,” Ryland said. “The Calamity it spread from was only able to reach certain, specific worlds.”

“Regardless. The point remains that it’s an extremely difficult and time-consuming undertaking. My goal is to gather all the Living Mana and stuff it into one container and then destroy the container itself. The rate of gathering means I’ve had to do this slowly, over decades, but I can now accelerate the process to my liking.”

“Using Acrypha.”

Mavar tutted. “Using what she has shown me is possible. Living Mana wants to live. It possesses anything it can grant a semblance of life to. Including mana. What better vessel than an entire moon constructed via magic?”

Ryland still didn’t understand the full implications of what Mavar was planning, nor the technical details of exactly how Acrypha was assisting his project. The Demiurge wouldn’t reveal it. Otherwise, Ryland would end this entire operation far more easily. In his current state, his choices were limited.

But limited options wasn’t the same as no options.

“Viren,” Ryland said, uncaring how much Mavar heard. “I want you to go inside the moon.”

Viren blinked. “Me? Inside?” He looked over their target, past Mavar, at the giant planetary body covered in a smooth barrier that even [Dragongod’s Endbreath] hadn’t been able to get through. “How, sir?”

“Simple. I will send you inside.”

Mavar glared at the two of them ignoring him. “You will do no such thing. Not while I draw breath. Not if you wish to prevent sending another child to his needless death.”

“There won’t be any deaths,” Viren said ferociously. “I don’t care what you’re trying to do. I don’t care that you’re trying to stop the Living Mana. You can keep fooling yourself and everyone else, but you’re not going to fool me. The way you’re doing things, the way you’re willing to sacrifice others is abhorrent!”

“Why do you keep such rats next to you, Ryland?” Mavar asked, ignoring Viren in turn. “Is it because you’re so desperate to cling to the old reality? That you believe you’ll lose yourself if you didn’t have a handy reminder to be good and nice and moral?”

Viren looked at Ryland like he could explain what in all the Realms the Demiurge had meant by that. But honestly, Ryland had talked enough for now. Any further conversation was just giving Mavar more time to complete whatever plan of his he had set in motion. Ryland intended to stop it.

As such, he cast [Translocate].

The rift swallowing Viren should have deposited him right into the interior of the moon. Ryland’s Inscribed Glyph of Cartography had already granted him knowledge that the satellite body orbiting Rhel was hollow inside.

But his spell failed. Strangely, the barrier that Mavar had so carefully erected wasn’t letting Viren through.

Wasn’t letting anyone or anything at all through.

Mavar Val Arona, the Demiurge, unleashed his Transcendent-ranked manasoul. The sudden outpouring of furious magic had Viren whipping his head towards Mavar, eyes widening with growing alarm.

“As if I hadn’t thought of you doing your usual, Ryland,” Mavar said. “You would do well to cease your futile attempts to stop my crowning glory.”

“[Translocate],” Ryland cast again, changing up several of the Subglyphs. It still failed.

“Enough.” Mavar spat. “[Death Flare].”

An unfamiliar spell emerged at their exact location. It came so suddenly, shooting out so much power so instantaneously, that Viren cried out. Flashing light that was probably overtaking the star keeping Rhel lit up erupted into being along with blistering heat, the throes of a newborn sun flaring out to clasp them in a vaporizing embrace.

Of course, Ryland’s barriers were effective in stopping the spell from leaving a mark on either of them. Although, in this case, Ryland wouldn’t be able to call his defence absolute. Not at all.

Viren cried out as the heat still managed to sear his skin and hair, his clothes catching fire at multiple locations. Even Ryland’s own robes were starting to get fried, though his physical body was protected thanks to several high-Tier Inscriptions.

“[Silence],” Ryland cast, focusing the spell on Viren.

Normally, he used it exactly as its name suggested. [Silence] was a way to draw energy away from vibrating particulate matter and ensure no bothersome sound reached his ears.

But the same principles could be applied to other forms of energy as well. In this case, Ryland had quickly applied to the burning heat, light, and other radiation trying to eradicate them at a particulate level too.

He didn’t go any further. The intensity of the spell meant Mavar could safely assume that Ryland and Viren had been burned to cinders and ash.

This naturally gave Ryland an opening to attack his actual target.

So, with the help of [Omniscience], he focused on the barrier around the moon. In rapid moments, Ryland deciphered just what was going on and how it had been constructed.

The kind of power needed to ward off even [Dragongod’s Endbreath] was already proof that it was a supremely powerful barricade. Now, by noting the Subglyphs of the Inscription and the Tiers suggested by their powerful hues, Ryland was convinced the entire smooth-faced edifice had to be surrounded by a barrier that even he would have difficulty rivalling with regular spells.

Honestly, he was rather impressed Mavar had come up with something like that. It had clearly taken him decades to construct, but it had been well worth it.

For a time.

In the midst of the deadly energy, of incalculably strong magic, Ryland began reconstructing his [Translocate] spell. He rewrote a great number of the Subglyphs making it up, quickly reorganizing ones he judged would be needed while replacing those that no longer functioned correctly in the face of Mavar’s seemingly impassable barrier.

So, as the blinding, burning spell from the Demiurge finally faded, revealing him floating with his manasoul ablaze with deadly intent, Ryland cast a new and improved [Translocate].

“No!” Mavar shouted. “[Infinite Planes]!”

Too late. The rift that swallowed Viren this time tore through not just the protective veil in front of the artificial moon, but also its surface as well, a beam of light digging into the smooth rock to send Viren in.

“How?” Mavar asked. His spell was already reconstructing the hole that Ryland had caused, but the job was done. Viren was through.

“You didn’t need to cast it again,” Ryland pointed out. “I had already figured out how it worked. Quite ingenious, I’ll give you that, Mavar.”

In truth, Ryland had quickly located the most important Subglyphs in the massive spell circle Mavar had wrought around his moon by noting their Tiers. The strongest Glyphs were the most significant components of the spell.

These included the Subglyphs of Space, Mirror, Extension, and a few others that essentially created what Ryland liked to refer to as a reality ripple. Mavar had more or less created an invisible pocket Realm where space stretched to a seeming infinite bound, where anything that struck it experienced that infinite, and was thus defeated in the face of eternity.

The Demiurge wasn’t called the Eternal for nothing, after all.

But once it had been determined, it wasn’t that difficult to counter. All Ryland had needed to do was attack the very conceptual nature of the important Glyphs and attach certain Glyphs of his own to counter Mavar.

For instance, infinite space might seem insurmountable normally, but when faced against infinite time to cover infinite space, it just tended to create a mathematical equivalence. It might seem like a paradox in reality, but magic was a thing of will and intent. Ryland had replaced the ripple with a far more reasonable reality.

Unfortunately, Mavar hadn’t waited to hear Ryland’s sermon on just how he had overcome the Demiurge’s barrier. He swiped his hand through the air.

“I was afraid there would be no reasoning with you,” Mavar said. “You’ve changed too much. I once knew a Ryland who wouldn’t have hesitated to sacrifice just one person for the greater benefit of all the Realms.”

The triumph of overcoming a spell Mavar was so proud of faded, replaced with a coldness clasping Ryland’s heart.

“Liar,” he said. “You never knew such a Ryland. You only knew the Ryland who helped you create Rhel from scratch. You only knew the Recreator.”

“But I’d heard of him! I was a part of your blasted Realmbreaker Wars too! I know the atrocities you committed, the beings you subdued, the worlds you shattered.” Mavar’s voice was filled with near-loathing. “You think I was ignorant of your other monikers? The Realmbreaker, the Archslayer, the Father of Calamities! I knew you were a mere monster seeking redemption.”

That phrase dug up a very different consideration Ryland had almost forgotten about in his single-minded goal to free Acrypha.

“That last one,” Ryland said. That title was only used by one select group of individuals—the Flourinus Order. More proof that Mavar had been working with them, to some extent. “Where did you hear that moniker?”

“I am done entertaining your fanciful notion that this is an interrogation.” Mavar’s form lit up with incandescent magic. “You are to die. You should have died a long time ago, but others were merely too incompetent to see it through.”

Ryland sighed. It would seem he’d have to beat the answers he sought out of Mavar. Which was fine by him, in all honesty.

“Then come,” Ryland said. “Let’s see what the centuries have taught you.”

Mavar scowled, his expression far from his own Kindly moniker. “I’ll show you what a true Transcendent is capable of.”

A heartbeat later, world-shattering magic exploded above the skies of Rhel.

Viren couldn’t believe where he had been transported. This was supposed to be the interior of a Calamity-cursed moon? All these metal passageways and platforms, all these cubicles and pods and wires, were supposed to be a part of a planetary body orbiting Rhel?

Something was terribly off.

Even the fact that he found Acrypha in the end felt wrong somehow, like he really should have failed instead. Because he was in the innards of a moon. How had he not gotten lost yet?

Ryland.

As always, he had Ryland to thank for it all.

Somehow, that enigmatic, unknowable mage had not only found a way inside that purportedly impenetrable barrier, but had also taken Viren to the exact location he needed to be. The glowing lines running along all the pipes and wires were mana manifested into reality. Living Mana. And it was all leading to one specific location Viren had hurried towards.

Only to find Acrypha sealed inside a pod in a large chamber that somehow felt like the centre of this entire gargantuan construct.

Calling this entire edifice artificial was an understatement. This whole place had to have taken ages to build, taken more manpower and effort and thought than Viren could fathom. And magic too, of a scale that perhaps only Ryland could have rivalled, though even that was questionable with how integrally it was connected to various Glyphtech.

Viren did his best to ignore it all and focus on his main goal. He had to free Acrypha.

She was trapped inside that pod, unconscious and suspended inside some sort of liquid. He was assured she still lived, somehow, thanks to the bubbles emerging from her nostrils.

Viren looked around. Hundreds of glowing wires and pipes were connected to her pod, probably dousing her in Living Mana. Why and what for, Viren couldn’t really figure out just then. First, he had to free her.

Now… he just had to figure out how exactly he’d go about doing so…
