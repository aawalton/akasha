“Burn the damned letters faster!” Demetri Ballenci, first son and heir to the fallen barony of Ballenci, shouted at the trembling servants, tearing up his correspondence with the Goldenhearts Duchy and the Pandorian Empire. He slammed his foot into a servant girl’s backside, sending her sprawling. Shredded parchment was scattered across the floor.

“Gods-fucking-dammit!” he roared, stomping on her midsection until something cracked in her frail, mortal frame. “If she kills me, I’ll make sure she takes every last one of you with me. Burn it faster!”

“Milord.”

Sebastian, the Ballenci family’s head butler, burst into the study. His normally stoic expression was twisted with fear and despair. Demetri could have sworn the man’s hair had been black only months ago. Now, it was stark white.

“What?” Demetri snarled, releasing a pulse of fire-aspected mana at a pile of letters one servant girl was tending. Both the letters and the girl erupted in flames. She screamed as her black hair ignited, her shrieks soon choked by burning flesh. The acrid stench filled the room, but not a single servant glanced her way.

Their obedience and terror, once a balm to Demetri’s ego, left him hollow. The dread in his gut eclipsed any satisfaction he might’ve drawn from the sweet, agonizing death of the mortal girl.

“They’re nearly here,” Sebastian said, ignoring the snap in Demetri’s voice. On any other day, Demetri might have punished him for the insolence. But not today.

“And Father?”

“He’s with the horses in the tunnels. Your mother, too. You must go. Now.”

This was all that damned bitch Brianna’s fault. She just had to befriend the murderous bitch masquerading as a duchess. He’d held out hope, even after sending a few assassins, that Brianna had kept her mouth shut. Right up until last week, when word came that the Scarlet March had set its sights on House Ballenci.

“Psychopathic bitch,” Demetri muttered, snatching his coat from a servant’s outstretched arms and shoving past Sebastian. Next time, he would not give her any benefit, blood or not.

At the end of the hallway, a thought struck him—simple, elegant, and cruel.

He could silence the servants. Destroy the evidence. All of it in a single fell swoop.

Demetri snapped his fingers, and a torrent of mana surged. His study erupted into a firestorm behind him, screams of the burning servants echoing down the corridor. He resisted the urge to skip after Sebastian toward the hidden escape.

Let's see a Court of law condemn me to prison now.

Sebastian led them swiftly down a flight of curving stairs toward the basement. The familiar paths of the family mansion were now strewn with destroyed tapestry and servants frantically running with no direction or clue what was happening. Some stole, some continued to do as they were bid, knowing nothing else, and some were stealing no doubt in an effort to make it rich in the period of chaos.

All that was apparent to Demetri, however, were the sounds of battle and death that could now just barely be heard in the approaching distance, and how that somehow sounded like the end of his reality.

They left the winding staircase behind and turned a sharp corner down a path of the alley Demetri had to admit that he'd never known about, not that he was one for exploring. Sebastian all but hurled himself through a wooden door, shattering it off its hinges as he ushered Demetri forward.

The noise died behind them as they went further into the mansion's dungeon, coming upon another flight of stairs. Unlike the ones in the mansion above, these were narrow, bare, and descended into darkness.

Demetri followed on Sebastian's heels as the butler led them through the basement, or perhaps calling it a dungeon would be more apt, the echoes of their steps bouncing off the stone walls slick with age and moisture. Had Demetri not summoned a small flame to light the way, they would have been utterly pitched into darkness.

Though by the way Sebastian forged ahead, light or not, it was more than likely that the man had memorized the pathways.

After a few moments, the staircase ended in a small, broom-like room of stone walls. Sebastian knelt beside a slightly raised portion of the floor and wrapped his knuckles against them so hard, Demetri was honestly surprised he didn't hear a crack in the man's bones. A grinding sound soon followed, and the slab lifted from the floor before sliding aside to reveal a vertical shaft leading to a spacious path of tunnels.

Below, Demetri saw them: his parents.

His mother, as usual, wore the finest beastkin garments—fur-lined, ivory-stitched flesh and fur. Her obsession with such things baffled him, though it had become fashionable in the Pandorian Empire these last few years. Her hair was, somehow, finely combed and curled, her face covered with layers of makeup.

His father, on the other hand, appeared a nervous wreck. His cravat was tied wrong, sticking out of his coat in bizarre, unassuming ways. His hair, normally combed and gelled to quiet perfection, was reminiscent of a bird’s nest. Demetri had once looked up to his father, but the man had quickly revealed himself as a coward. A boot licker. He’d used that modicum of intelligence to polish the shoes of everyone with more power than he, when he should have usurped them.

The tunnels beneath were astonishingly wide—broad enough for five horses to walk abreast. The ceiling, however, barely cleared ten feet. Demetri had always known about these passages, but he’d never realized just how expensive they must have been to construct. In his mind, they’d been more like narrow crawl spaces, not grand subterranean corridors.

Flanking his parents stood half a dozen armed soldiers, each radiating the composed strength of bronze core knights. Not that it mattered. They would be little more than speed bumps against the Scarlet March—cultivators ranging from Bronze to Gold realm, backed by powerful mages.

“There you are!” his mother cried out as Demetri and the others dropped through the hatch, landing with a dull thud ten or so feet below. As always, her voice dripped with theatrical flair. “Oh, my poor sweet baby. Are you hurt?” She rushed over, hands fluttering, patting him down like he was a broken doll.

“I’m fine, Mother,” Demetri muttered, gently holding her at arm’s length.

His father, on the other hand, had remained largely oblivious to Demetri’s… unique appetites when it came to servants and mortals. It was mostly thanks to his mother’s intervention that his indiscretions had been swept under a long and well-decorated rug.

But clearly, not swept far enough.

“Why is she coming here?” his father snarled, finally finding his spine as he stormed forward and grabbed Demetri by the collar, yanking him close until they were nose to nose. Hot, fetid breath hit Demetri’s face like a slap.

He’s drunk, Demetri realized, shame settling into his gut like a lead weight.

“Why?!” his father shouted again, shaking Demetri hard enough to rattle his teeth. “We’re under the Duchy’s protection! We may have bent a few rules, but this... this purge makes no sense. I’ve never betrayed the crown. Your mother hasn’t either.” He jabbed a finger into Demetri’s chest. “That means you did something. What was it?” He shook Demetri again, but Demetri only stared at the terrified glint in his father’s unfocused gaze. “What did you do, Demetri? Killing servants wasn’t enough? Raping peasant girls didn’t satisfy you? Controlling your sister didn’t keep you busy?”

Demetri blinked, stunned by the venom in his father’s words. The next words slipped out before he could stop them. “You knew?”

“Of course I knew, you blasted child!” his father bellowed, backhanding him across the cheek. “You truly thought your mother had the power to bury all that death without my knowing?” He shook his head in disgust as Demetri touched his face. The sting barely registered.

Weak, drunk, and cowardly.

“As I’ve said before, Father,” Demetri replied coolly, “I have done nothing.”

“By the Gods…” the baron groaned, rubbing his eyes with clenched fists as if trying to claw them out. “What did I do to deserve a murderous bastard for a son? Couldn’t you have kept your depravity within the barony?”

Demetri tried to hold back, but the anger broke through. “Why did you have to be so weak?”

His father’s eyes bulged. “Weak? I’ll show you—!”

A thunderous explosion rocked the ground above, drowning the baron's next words with the noise of war. Dust fell from the ceiling like snowflakes, covering everything with each pulse of battle.

“My lords,” Sebastian interrupted, gesturing urgently toward the horses. “We must away.”

Demetri pulled free from his father’s grip and, with a smoothness his father could never dream of, vaulted onto the black horse the servants had brought for him earlier. “The butler is right. We’ll resume this little conversation later.”

“Oh?” came a lilting voice from the shadows ahead. Feminine, familiar, but not quite right. It struck a memory that, in the heat of the moment, he struggled to place. “What disagreement would that be?”

Demetri’s heart lurched. For one sickening moment, he thought it was Duchess Lilliana Alistar.

But the woman who emerged wasn’t the Duchess. She had flaming red hair, sharp avian features that made Demetri wonder if she was beastkin so alike was she to a bird, and a visage peppered with freckles.

“Who are you?!” barked Calduin, one of the barony’s head knights, drawing his large steel blade with a hiss that rang through the underground tunnel like an uncoiling snake.

Before the woman could answer, a deep voice rumbled behind her like a mountain coming to life. “The lady asked, 'What disagreement?'”

From the shadows stepped a man of titanic build, his scar-laced muscles bulging beneath a roughspun tunic. His unkempt brown beard lent him a barbaric appearance, but there was no humor or room for condescension in the fear he inspired.

He wielded two war hammers. Not ceremonial ones. Real, heavy things meant for ruin. Even at a distance, Demetri could see flesh and blood staining the hammers' faces.

Demetri couldn't help the way his knees trembled at the sight of the war hammers, images of a gruesome death flashing through his mind.

A single word came to mind, whispered by the soldiers around him. “Berserker.”

It was a hint of pride he felt that the usurper duchess had sent her Berserker after them and not one of the Scarlet March’s paragons of lesser renown.

But Demetri wasn’t a coward. Berserker, god, or Lilliana herself, he would stand his ground.

“How did you get in here?” Demetri growled, drawing his own blade from the sheath strapped to his saddle. It wasn't as heavy as Calduin's, nor was it crafted for war, but the embedded rubies flashed against the flickering flames of the nearby torches held by the soldiers, giving it a rather murderous appearance. “This passage belongs to House Ballenci. You are tresspassing. Leave now, or—”

The two strangers glanced at each other and chuckled.

Demetri’s grip tightened. Why are they laughing?

But he knew why. And, despite his words, it was obvious who they were.

“You’re part of the March,” he muttered.

The woman nodded. “We are.” She stepped forward, still not brandishing a weapon despite the platoon of trained soldiers ready to cut her down. “Demetri Ballenci, you are hereby under arrest for treason against the crown, for the murder of nearly fifty commoners under your father's rule, and for the attempted murder of your sister, Brianna Ballenci. Baron Teach Ballenci. Baroness Elise Ballenci. You are both charged with conspiracy to commit treason and accessory to multiple counts of murder. You will be tried under the laws of the Kingdom.”

Her gaze met Demetri’s—hot, unyielding, like miniature suns boring into him with the sole purpose of melting his soul.

“Demetri Ballenci,” she continued. “Under the authority of Duchess Lilliana Alistar, acting under wartime powers granted by His Majesty Zer’Nack of Lysoria, you are sentenced to immediate execution with no right to trial. If you resist, we have full license to make your death as painful as possible.” She paused and then offered him a dry smile. "I have been told by your sister that she recommends you struggle to the end."

Demetri opened his mouth, but the ground trembled again, deeper this time, like a god had taken a step. All the strength in his body fled him as a terrible pressure began to flatten him, pushing him toward the dirt. Then something, some creature from the depths of all the Hells, roared with the power of a volcano, shaking the very foundation upon which Demetri stood.

His horse's legs collapsed simultaneously, pitching them both to the ground.

He hadn't expected the Scarlet March to break through so quickly. He'd known the army was powerful, but how had they broken through the barriers? The mages? The Ballenci family may have been a fallen noble house, but they had coffers filled with coin that went to hiring the most skilled mercenaries available.

He'd heard rumors that the duchess had found some dragon-kin creature. Was that it?

Despite his doubts, however, Demetri could not ignore the obvious.

The two ahead of them were not scouts, and the Scarlet March wasn’t just in the process of breaking through.

They were already here.
