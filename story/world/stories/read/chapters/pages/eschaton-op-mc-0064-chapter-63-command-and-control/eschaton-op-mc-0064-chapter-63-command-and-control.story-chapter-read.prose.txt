I find Captain Rains in the Pineapple Express’ hangar bay.

“Sir! It’s good to see you,” he says, saluting. “How can I help?”

“It’s good to see you as well, Captain. Sorry I haven’t been around as much. Very busy elsewhere, unfortunately.”

“Completely understandable, sir. No need to worry about us. How can the National Guard help you today, sir?”

“I’ve got some news, and most of it isn’t good. I’m going to need your help.”

“Understood, sir. What about?”

“Best if we get everyone together before I tell you. Don’t want to tell it multiple times.”

“Very good sir, right this way.”

Captain Rains guides me to the motor pool where 2nd Squad is working. They’re carefully going over a trio of vehicles. Specifically, two eight-wheeled armored personnel carriers called Strykers, each with a top mounted .50 caliber machine gun, and a Bradley IFV. A familiar face is messing with the IFV’s autocannon, grease covering his hands as he digs around inside it with a tool.

“Tom? Is that you?”

He jerks in surprise, and curses after dropping something inside the gun. It clatters through with the clink-clink-clink mechanics never want to hear.

“Goddamn it! Holy shit Seth, you fuckin’ startled me. Don’t friggin’ sneak up on me like that!”

“How? I’m not exactly stealthy, dude. Maybe you should just pay more attention?” I suggest.

“Oh, shut up, like you’re one to talk. How many times did you have to copy my friggin’ notes after drifting off in class?”

We stare at each for a moment, and everyone else looks almost uncomfortable. We both break out in chuckles, but it’s not long before they become belly laughs.

“It’s good to see you, dude,” Tom says. “We all missed you, me and the boys I mean, after, you know, everything.”

“Yeah, I know. I just… couldn’t.”

He nods. “Yeah. I can’t imagine it. I’m sorry, man.”

“I have bigger things to worry about now other than feeling sorry for myself,” I lie.

“If you say so.”

The rest of Second Squad is looking at Tom with a mixture of shock, awe, and maybe a little outrage. All except one, of course. Thor is still his very reserved self.

“It’s good to see all of you again,” I tell everyone else, changing the subject.

“I hate to be blunt, sir, but what are you here for?” Sergeant Callisto says. “Not trying to be rude, sir,” she adds.

“I’m here for more than just needing something,” I say defensively. “I really did want to stop in and say hello.”

“Well, say hello, everyone,” Sergeant Callisto says dryly. After a barrage of ‘hellos’ from the assembled 2nd Squad, she continues. “Well, now that’s out of the way, what’s the deal? I assume it’s more than just a runway show for your new cape.”

“Cloak, but yes. Let’s get inside the Pineapple Express, we’re going to want to include the command staff on this as well.”

The Pineapple Express was actually slightly modified during Second Squad’s downtime, allowing me to ascend the rear ladder and for the first time, enter the crew deck.

I still have to crouch to fit in the fairly cramped crew deck, but at least I don’t have to stay on the cargo deck with all the vehicles anymore. There’s a difference there. Maybe I am a person, and not a weapon. I might still be a machine, but being a person doesn’t require being human.

Normally the crew deck would be used for troop transport. With seventy-five seats available, it’s not a small amount either. In this case, a vast majority have been torn out and replaced with a great deal of command and control equipment.

Captain Rains sighs and sits down in one of the many chairs in the Pineapple Express’ command center. The entirety of the command staff—eight additional officers in total—is joined by Tom and Sergeant Callisto.

“We got the news flash this morning, sir, but they definitely pulled some punches in the announcement.” he says after I explain the impending famine. “It’s really that bad?”

“Worse. You should also know that the ‘battleplans for the War on Hunger’ they mentioned is us,” I tell him. “We’re it.”

“Ah,” Callisto says. “No backup plan?” She asks hopefully.

“No.”

I fill them in on the more specific parts of the plan the Council has come up with.

“An actual, real-life, damned prophecy,” Tom mutters. “Well, we can’t even start working unless we know where this”land of sun and swamp” is. There’s got to be hundreds or even thousands of swamps across the world that would count.”

“Twenty-seven major swamps in North America alone, though I think only about twenty-four of them could be considered ‘land of sun’. Still, that’s over 40,000 square kilometers of wetland to search.”

“I’m not sure who I’ll need to call to get that done,” Captain Rains groans. “Probably the damn President.”

“I’ll do that.” I tell him. “He gave me a blank check.”

“I’ll go get us held on the line,” Lieutenant McKinely says. “When we need to get off the ground, we’ll be in the air in five minutes or less.”

“Okay, break. Work to do,” Captain Rains orders, and everyone returns to their stations with a nervous energy. Callisto and Tom scramble down the ladder, sprinting off to collect their crews and vehicles. I back into a corner as to not crowd everyone else—a difficult task for me, given how large I am—and call the President. He picks up almost immediately.

“Machina, how can I help?” He asks.

“We received a prophecy from Pythia. We’ll find the first of three things we’ll need in a ‘land of sun and swamp’. Unfortunately, that’s a very large area to search, not even counting everything outside of the US. I’ll need your help.”

“…Right,” He says after a long pause. “I’ll get some air patrols roaming over the various swamps in the US. I’ll see what I can do to get it done globally. Anything else?”

“Not right now.”

“Alright, call me if you need something else. I’ll get to work on my end. Good luck.”

It takes nearly two days before anything fitting the bill is found. An aerial patrol aircraft over the Everglades in South Florida spots a Fracture deep in the swamplands where the news of it eventually reaches us. True to Lieutenant McKinely’s word, within five minutes of hearing of the Fracture, we’re in the air.

Captain Rains is busy working with his staff to coordinate everything we’ll need to properly exploit the Fracture as much as we can. The briefing is carried out by Lieutenant McKinely an hour and a half into the three hour flight.

“It fits the general profile we’re looking for. It’s in an overgrown and swampy area that receives lots of sunlight. There’s a scouting force on site already. They’re reporting it as a Cat-3, though they haven’t sent in any drones yet.”

Tom grimaces. “Lieutenant, given how Fractures tend to replicate their environment, and how swampy the Everglades are, there’s no chance we can bring the heavy armor. They’ll sink in the mud. Not to mention going into a Cat-3 is… well. Risky, the say the least.”

McKinely offers a tablet to Tom.

“The Marines are offering up a prototype amphibious vehicle for our use. Lieutenant Matterson, you should be aware that it’s almost entirely untested. It’s been in a singular engagement outside of a Fracture, where it performed well against Cat-1 fodder monsters. Meet the Amphibious Combat Vehicle.”

“It looks a lot like the Stryker,” Callisto notes while looking over Tom’s shoulder.

“The ACV-30 variant is armed with a 30mm autocannon,” Tom notes appreciatively.

“Though they don’t come standard with the ACV-30, the combat engineers have bolted a Javelin launcher to the ACV-30s, so you should have the firepower to give the larger monsters something to think about. Whatever the 30mm doesn’t pulp, anyway.” McKinely says.

“What does this mean for us?” I ask.

“It means we’ll be coming with you, sir,” Tom tells me. “The ACV is capable of swimming, so the terrain won’t be an issue sir. As long as it’s not too muddy.”

“The Fracture is currently in The Big Cypress portion of the Everglades. That’s on the western half of the state, north of the Florida Keys,” McKinely tells us. “You’ll have to use the ACV-30s to even get there. There are off-roading trails that should make the route easier, but we are in the peak wet season. Everything will be flooded. Expect at least four feet of water, and as deep as six in some places.”

“Right, I’ll go show the guys this, hopefully some last minute cramming will be enough,” Tom says. He grabs another tablet and moves off towards his vehicle crews, and Callisto joins him.

Fortunately for us, there’s an airport actually right next to The Big Cypress, the Miami-Dade Training and Transition Airport. Unfortunately, it was abandoned during the initial panic of the Apotheosis due to its isolation making it difficult to defend. A forward team bringing the ACV-30 prototypes and their supply chain will land only an hour before us.

Once we land and come to a stop, we quickly pile out of the Pineapple Express. We get a good look at the reactivated airport.

Several Styker variants, mounting a 105mm main gun, roam around the airbase. They’re on the hunt for any intruding monsters that might be attracted by the rapid influx of people. The MGS variants might be lightly armored, but 105mm is a lot of hate. Only tough Cat-2 bosses could survive more than a handful of shots. If they get the first shot off, they have a pretty good chance of winning a fight against even some of the tougher monsters.

By the time we land, the forward scouting party deployed some drones to scout into the Fracture. Lieutenant McKinely gives us all one more update on the Fracture itself before we leave.

The interior is much like the exterior. The water is deeper, and the trees larger, but overall it’s nearly identical in structure to the rest of The Big Cypress. It’s effectively a shallow lake with a forest of cypress trees growing in it. The trees get taller and the water deeper the closer to the center of the Cypress dome—the name for a grove of this kind.

Second Squad is given a very brief primer on the ACV-30 and how to operate it. It’s similar enough to the Stryker that they should be alright. Both are eight wheeled vehicles with V-hulls and pointed noses so they look very similar, though the ACV-30 is larger and much heavier. It does have an engine twice as powerful as the Stryker, so hopefully it should be close enough.

Diving into a Cat-3 Fracture is probably one of the worst times to switch to a nearly-untested vehicle without any training. Unfortunately, only bad options exist these days. With just six months before mass starvation sets in, there’s very little time to waste.

It takes us nearly two hours to make the six mile journey to the Fracture itself. The water level is as high as Lieutenant McKinely proclaimed, and it makes the trek extremely difficult.

Fortunately, the ACVs prove their capabilities, and easily ford the water. I’m forced to knock down trees and break apart blockages. The forward team was deployed via helicopter, and no one has been out here to maintain the trails for obvious reasons.

Once we get to the Fracture the commander there, a very nervous 2-Star, is backed up by even more nervous Typical infantry. He gives us a quick run down of what they’ve learned. A few monsters were spotted inside and relayed back by the automated drones. Giant alligators and crocodiles were a common sight to little surprise. One drone checked the depth. Just inside the Fracture itself the water plunges to a depth of twelve feet.

The ACV-30s are more than capable of that, and might actually have an easier time. They’ve been rated to swim in the open ocean, as long as the waves aren’t too bad. I will unfortunately be forced to tromp along the bottom. The ACV might be able to carry me and still swim, if slowly, but it’s not worth the risk. If its attacked by a monster it may become unbalanced. If I slip, I could flip the entire vehicle and it would drown. Trying to use the guns while swimming has never been done before and carries enough risk as it is.

“Well, are you all ready?” I ask Tom.

“As ready as we’ll ever be,” he says over the radio. The vehicles rumble to life.

“Alright, let’s go.
