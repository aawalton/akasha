After the first day at my new job, I came back to Juliette’s room, practically bursting with excitement.

“It is a little less manly than blacksmith apprentice,” she said with a smirk, “but it’s great news. I’m happy for you.”

“Thanks,” I said. “I wanted to bring you some underwear as a gift, but the shopkeeper wouldn’t let me.”

“That… that’s fine,” she said, embarrassed? “Anyway, we should definitely celebrate!”

“Celebrate? Yes. Like—what did you have in mind?” I asked. Hopefully nothing that would cost a ton of money. We still needed to save up.

“I was thinking,” she said brightly, “we should buy fruit cakes. They’re relatively cheap, and we can bring them tomorrow to the tribe. I bet the kids never had anything like that. They’ll be ecstatic!”

“That’s an awesome idea!” I said—and meant it. “There are some fruits I want them to try too. But I’ll miss work on my second day…”

“That’s fine,” explained Juliette. “It’s not like real employers here. They don’t mind if you skip a day now and then. Think of it more like contract work. They just don’t like you leaving midday without finishing the quota—that’s what gets you a warning.”

“Oh, cool.” I had no idea what “contract work” meant exactly, but I got the idea. Maybe I could still make it to work after the forest trip and finish my underpants quota super fast and still get paid. That’d be ideal.

I placed my hand on Bob, who was lazily stretched out on his pillow, and said, “We bring fruit cakes! Very yammi!”

He didn’t know what a fruit cake was, so he was skeptical. But he’d change his mind soon enough.

“So tomorrow,” I said, “we go to the market, then pick up Bob, then to the forest?”

“Sounds like a plan,” Juliette replied.

“Great.” I yawned. I was so tired. Apparently doing actual work tired you out. Who knew?

“I have money now,” I said casually. “I could… rent a room at the inn.”

“We can save faster,” Juliette said, brushing hair from her face, “if you keep sleeping here. On the couch. If you don’t mind.”

I was hoping she’d say that. “I don’t mind at all. I actually like it. If you really don’t mind, I mean.”

“You’re a lot less creepy than my first impression of you. A lot less.”

“Thanks.” That was… a compliment. I think.

The next morning, we woke up happy and full of energy, Just like the past few days. We headed to the town market to get fruit cakes and some supplies for the tribe. It was also... sort of our first date, I think. But I didn’t ask Juliette to confirm it.

We each put in 2 silver coins and filled a large bag with fruits, vegetables, and baked goods. We also bought two big fruit cakes, each wrapped in brown paper and tied with string. We were going to have a feast.

But as we walked back toward the mansion to get Bob, we saw something out of place.

I mean really out of place.

A young Asian man was wandering through the market, looking completely lost. He was wearing a Led Zeppelin T-shirt and had that specific confused, puppy-lost expression I recognized too well.

“Look,” Juliette pointed at him.

“I know,” I said. “Another player. Should we...?”

“Wait,” Juliette said, grabbing my arm. “We need to think this through. Our situation is more... complicated than it was before.”

“I guess,” I agreed. “Let’s follow him a bit, see what he’s doing.”

We blended in easily. Wearing local clothes and knowing how to act, we passed as ordinary NPCs. He had no clue we were watching.

He was definitely in stage one of the isekai experience: asking NPCs where he was, what this place was—getting only robotic, weird answers. It was... honestly sad.

“At this rate, he’ll get himself in trouble,” I whispered. “He needs help.”

“If we tell him about ourselves,” Juliette said, “he’ll want to join us. Maybe even go into the forest.”

“It’s not something we could really hide if he join us,” I said. “And if he finds out about the tribe, he might not understand...”

“Bob wanted us to bring more players,” Juliette reminded me. “He wants to sting them, of course.”

We were silent for a while. I was pretty sure we were having the exact same thought.

Would it be okay—morally—to let another person get stung by a scorpion-surikata and have his mind altered, so he’d love and care for Bob and the tribe?

Of course it would be.

That guy would be lucky to get stung by Bob.

But... there was another issue.

Bob wouldn’t be only ours anymore. He’d have another human. He’d rely on us less. The thought of Bob perched on another person’s shoulder... it hurt. It was selfish, I knew. But I wanted Bob to ourselves.

“I… I think we shouldn’t,” I finally said.

“Our situation is too complicated,” Juliette agreed.

“But we still need to help him somehow,” I said. “I think I have an idea.”

I walked up to him and switched on my best Skyrim-style NPC voice.

“Greetings, young adventurer. I am John, the Guide. How shall we call you?”

He jumped a little, startled, and then relief flooded his face.

“Oh thank god! I’m Lee Hyun-woo,” he said. “I have no idea how I got here. I was just studying in my room. Where… where even is this? And why does everyone here speak Korean?”

“This town is Kamatu, in the Kingdom of Aldioli.” I hope I got the name right. “Let me give you some words of advice.”

“Please,” he said. “Anything.”

“Do not go to the forest,” I said in a flat, rehearsed tone. “There are extremely dangerous monsters. You will find your end quickly. Do not leave the town unless you are with a strong and willing party.”

“Party, like in… a game?” he asked.

I ignored him and continued.

“Respect the people of this town and their property. If you violate the law or take what is not yours, you will be punished.”

He nodded, eyes wide.

“You are advised to find honest work. Visit any workshop or store and ask for a job. An honest day’s work for an honest day’s pay. With silver, you may buy food and rent a room at the local inn.”

And with that, I turned and walked away like a good sociopath NPC.

“Wait!” he called. “That’s it? What kind of guide are you!?”

But he didn’t follow me.

Poor guy. I didn’t think he was a gamer. That would make it even harder for him.“That was… kind of mean,” Juliette said when I joined her. “but also.. nice?”

“It was the most useful thing I could do for him,” I shrugged. “If he followed my advice, he’d be fine… probably”

“We should still check on him sometimes,” she said. “Keep an eye out.”

“Good idea,” I said. “Maybe he really can join us... after, you know, all this.”

But even as I said it, I had no idea when ‘after’ would be... or what exactly ‘all this’ even meant.

We returned to the mansion and picked up Bob.

It was back to the forest for us—now one of my favorite places.

We didn’t tell Bob about the new player.

And when he eventually asked if there were other humans in town?

We’d lie. We weren’t ready to share.
