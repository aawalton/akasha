Chapter 152: Saw the Worst

Dozens of impacts struck the reinforced crystalline barrier of Lattice, enough to feel some of the reverberations even in the sub. The creatures slammed into the barrier without compulsion and went into spinning storms of slashes to cut gouges out of it. They attacked in a kind of wheeling sequence, hitting, falling away from drag, and quickly recovering to charge again.

They must hunt shit much bigger than them on the regular…

When Jack checked which secondary feeds he could access, he saw the buggers all around the sub. He had no idea how many they’d collectively taken out, but the answer was certainly ‘not enough.’

The crystal endured the barrages with spiderwebbing cracks and gouges torn into it. It held, but the damage wasn’t promising for the long-term, and her ability to regenerate it seemed limited and not fast enough to outpace the damage.

Crash and Permafrost were continually doing work in close proximity, picking off groups under concentrated attacks of water gouts and suddenly freezing ice, but they had to keep maneuvering to dodge the suddenly agitated beasts coming at them at the same time. A sudden jerk here or there when one got too close showed Bo’s handiwork, and sudden distracting flashes showed Délight’s. Numerous beasties seemed to be slowed down and effectively debuffed from damage, and Jack soon ascertained most of those were from the sonic deterioration of Snapper.

Nice. He’s switching targets rapidly, doing just enough damage to inhibit and moving on. Good adaptation.

The watery dynamic duo was more or less stuck with trying to cover every angle of attack other than Jack’s starboard flank. They absolutely protected the backside propulsion — at least there was no propeller to worry about for the advanced deep-sea vessel — and everything else was just a chaotic, impossible scrum. The only unneeded angle was to the front, as the sub had pretty much steered away from any remaining frontal threats, screening with a few forward detonations in the process. It was small consolation, however, as they were getting ruthlessly swarmed on all sides.

This is fraggin’ dire.

In any case, Jack spared no more time as a spectator because he could still help on his assigned flank. While they were still not directly threatening the sub itself, he reached out with Interpret and grabbed some material still ‘anchored’ within the relative speed of the sub to attack the beasties, now thankfully from their blindsides.

Recalling he hadn’t tried it yet, he also decided to start experimenting with heating the metal despite not particularly knowing the results deep underwater. Over a few seconds, the results were not particularly visible, but Jack knew the water was rapidly taking that heat. It also wasn’t helping that they were constantly moving in the water, giving a great swath of new ocean more heat-sinking surface area.

But, eventually, the core heat was such that any time the metal got near the beasts, they spazzed out erratically, showing they were sensitive to the change. They tried to avoid it.

Well, well, what have we here? Sadly, I don’t think it’s smart to try heating the whole sub if it comes down to it, and I certainly don’t have time to create some huge barrier at this point.

Nonetheless, he started moving some metal into intervening spaces between the beasties and the sub, to act as shields. Meanwhile, he kept up with quick stabbing injuries and general distractions in their midst, to mitigate the horde’s continual attacks. A few times, he managed to wrap around a ligament, and that caused a furious whirlwind of agitation as the beast would flail around trying to get the burning metal off.

Any sort of serious wound slowed them down and inhibited their attacks. Some that were damaged enough abandoned their charges entirely, though the exact recipe of what triggered the behavior wasn’t clear, because some kept on pushing even when they were bleeding profusely and had lost the speed to properly keep up.

Regardless of his efforts, there were just too many to counter across too many angles, and the damage to his flank’s crystal was extensive. He reported this in the gestalt twice, but Solvent was stuck supporting Lattice, while bouncing secondary support most efficiently through Crash and Perma, who had raw eyes and a little more ‘skin in the game,’ as it were. He couldn’t argue.

After a period of grueling half-effective defense like this, Lattice reported in the team gestalt, “Failure imminent up top, and we’re not good on starboard, either.”

“Sorry,” Snapper said. “Not pulling my weight, here.”

“I’ll take advice on improvement,” Jack added, likely with involuntary irony. The most likely improvement would be preparation through additional information. He was looking forward to hearing more about these terrors.

“Just keep working!” Solvent commanded. “Do your best for now.”

Jack’s metal began reaching high enough temperatures to start coating it in a weird sheath of what he gradually realized was a blanket of trapped, superhot gas. This made movement oddly easier, and less heat seemed to escape the ‘system,’ requiring compensation for greater control. But once he got the hang of it, anywhere this anomaly went, the beasties tried their damndest to avoid it, and if they weren’t fast enough, they got a nasty scorching wound that soon had them finding the speed to flee.

Hot damn, this is where it's at!

On the heels of feeling like he had a shot of protecting his flank, Lattice declared, “Breaching up top!” and her words were immediately followed by a loud shattering reverberation that shook the sub…

What ensued was pure chaos, as the beasties began charging through the broken crystal shield and striking at the sub. Jack pivoted to interlace and reinforce the metal, and Solvent also pivoted to him to hyperboost his efforts. Initially, they managed to prevent catastrophe on the top through a tough and quick-regenerating hull, but as Lattice’s shield fell apart on all sides and she was expended, they were getting hit from all angles, and Jack couldn’t reinforce the whole surface area the same as one vector.

The terrifying sound of a hull breach, screaming women — and men, for that matter — was swiftly followed by complete disorientation as the sub went into a tumble, and then it was all abruptly interrupted by Solvent loudly calling out, “End simulation!”

In an instant, they were disoriented — in a good way — by being out of the hole and standing around in a white void. The simulation even did them the honor of removing the more claustrophobic kit they’d been wearing, so they were all in their simple uniforms.

Numerous people were doing such things as holding their heart, whispering prayers, or staring wide-eyed at nothing. Jack meanwhile just rubbed his neck and grimaced, annoyed that he’d not done better. On top of it, his fatigue status showed Serious, which he knew was due mostly to the final sequence where he was in crisis mode and way over normal output.

The vets, for their part, seemed calm, if a bit grim. Solvent cleared his throat. “While there are some instructors that would make you go through the rest, I don’t find the science supports needless trauma being productive in a soldier’s life, so we’re skipping it.”

“I really appreciate that,” Lighthouse muttered faintly.

Crash crossed his arms and huffed, stone-faced and shaking his head. “Lucky little rooks.”

“What the hell were they?!” Bo asked in great agitation, still panting. She wasn’t the only one — Permafrost had completely flopped down.

“Initially, they were whispered about from sailors as the Carving Current,” Solvent replied, “as their moving mass is usually so vast it can be first detected and confused with typical oceanic abnormalities. Unfortunately, that’s usually too late for their preternatural speed. In any case, we’ve named them something less mythical: seasaws.”

Numerous ironic exclamations and barked, bitter laughter answered this.

“They hunt really, really big things, don’t they?” Jack asked.

“Indeed so,” Solvent replied with a nod. “And they seem to either confuse submarines and ships with food, or simply regard them as territorial violators, because they come at them with extreme aggression. They hunt in large schools, and even in what we call ‘legions’ that are multiple schools separated, but which come together for problematic prey. Over time, they seem to have slated us and our technological vessels as one such.

“They communicate across very long distances by poorly understood signals. Sometimes, the signal passes through a sensor on a sub or drone and causes subtle, multispectral blips. Sadly, there are so many of these all the time it's hard to differentiate. Only the best techs in their best moments manage it. That, or an ANP specialist is needed, and naturally, they’re few and far between. We have one at Fort Sledge who constantly receives relays to analyze, and this is a decent, but not completely failsafe, warning system, especially thanks to the issue of ‘tech reliability’ and frequent long-distance signal corruption.”

“What do we do about them?” Snapper asked.

“Before we get into that, I have to ask: what did we learn?”

“Are you serious right now?” Bo asked saucily. At Solvent’s raised eyebrow, she swallowed and looked away. “Sorry.” She closed her eyes and took a deep, calming breath.

“As a group?” Jack asked, but then answered, “We’re maybe weak to this type of enemy. They swarm in ridiculous numbers, and they’re just too tough for too many of us to deal with, at least without being boosted.”

Solvent replied with another question. “And what do we do to improve, based only on what we know so far?”

“I think that’s a matter of individual adjustments. I know now I should create an extensive heat barrier right off, because they absolutely can’t handle the trapped… steam pockets or whatever they are I can make.”

“At this insane depth simulated, you mean. It depends how far down we are, but yes, beyond a certain depth, the vaporized water gets trapped around your heated metal. Quite a nasty weapon.” He waved his hand, and a hologram recording played in front of them all, showing what appeared to be a glowing, shimmering space darting through the ocean outside the crystal-enclosed sub. The murder-sheath slammed into a seasaw and scored immediate damage on its hide, burning through it and causing it to dart away in apparent terror. “I’ll say there aren’t many types of attacks that cause this behavior in a seasaw, but this is up there.”

“Need something on the sub for me to work through,” Lighthouse said. “Assuming they aren’t immune to getting zapped. At the least, I can burn hard when they’re closer to the hull, maybe all around the sub. That could’ve saved our asses at the end.”

“Could Lattice enclose a group of them if buffed?” Délight asked. “That would drag them away and stall a large portion while we deal with another.”

“Good idea,” Lattice said with raised eyebrows. “I’ve never actually fought these damn things… Hope I never do, but I think you’re on to something there, if we are so cursed.”

“My brain hurts,” was all Permafrost added, while flopped down face-first.

Solvent nodded along, one hand on his chin, flicking his finger at each suggestion. “Good, good. I like the considerations, especially Perma’s idea. Nice work, Perma — you are a true genius.”

“Nnrrgh.”

“Okay, I’ll tell you our actual best bet, here: hoping like hell we can avoid them in a fairly stealthy deep-sea specialty submarine. We actually don’t use a vessel like this normally, so these bastards are more used to our cheaper conventional subs. We will have dummy drones running parallel above us to attract their attention if necessary. While they will pursue to those depths, they don’t prefer hunting there. The scenario is still possible. However, we have decent chances to catch them earlier in their little trap, giving us more time to figure out a solution. So we’re preparing for the worst case here.”

Crash added, “Yeah, basically, if detected sooner, I can engage from further away, maybe even hitting two or three groups and gumming them up. Meanwhile, our support structure will be converging and working to occupy them, hopefully effectively neutralizing a portion of the threat through total misdirection instead of simply stalling them. They don’t quit easily, and they’re faster than the sub, so it ain’t easy, but it’s still plenty feasible.”

“Yes. Finally, under most circumstances, more submarine firepower will indeed be brought to bear. More torpedoes at long range, stronger and more aggressive drones, and one other weapon source not used, graded for shallower depths we’ll spend most of our time in or in range of: anti-torpedo swarms of mini-torpedos, which nicely double as seasaw killers. We can unleash a vast number from external pods in a short-period burst, at the right depth. As relentless as these beasts are, at some point enough casualties will cause them to flee.”

He shrugged. “So there you have it. Our chances should be better, but now you know how wrong things can go if enough people down the line frag up.”

After a pause of relative silence, Conflux suddenly asked hopefully, “Break time?”

Solvent smiled. “That it is. We need an hour after this expenditure. We’ll meet back up afterward for more underwater fun and excitement!”

A sarcastic cheer answered this as people began blipping out in the sign of fleeing from the virtual void. Jack was an enthusiastic participant himself.

The monsters out there really are endless in variety as well as numbers. We have our work cut out for us, alright.

HRVR training continued in the simulated submarine environment, now against larger threats, and these were strangely easier to deal with than the seasaws had been. Crash’s constant waves slowed down any advance, and often forced sudden spin-outs the freaky beasties had to recover from.

Their first encounter was practically a non-starter against a giant, eyeless mass of dark tentacles called a ‘Bundle.’ Crash, and to a lesser extent, Permafrost, battered the thing from long range and forced it to constantly play catch-up in ensuing speed bursts. The submarine’s torpedoes detonating in its… ‘face’... seemed to wear it down enough to eventually just give up.

Jack didn’t strain himself too much, but did practice at long-range attempts to track torpedoes with his senses, and upon impact, to push fragments harder or more in the right direction. It occasionally seemed to do something, though it felt blind every time due to the distance.

Once, Solvent boosted him for it, and it was a notable improvement as they scored actual wounds on some of the tentacles. Unfortunately, the hideous thing ended up regenerating it.

Ensuing beasts put up something more of a fight, including creatures that hunted in small packs known as ‘ultraworms’ — greyish worm-like things almost as big as the sub, longer, and very fast and maneuverable, capable enough to end up slamming the submarine a few times in their charges. Fortunately, the keen skills of Crash always dampened these blows with a last-second parry of sorts, as he pushed water hard against them and blunted the force.

Jack’s strategy of heat didn’t work for them, as they were resistant, and they fought at depths that made for rising torrents of steam bubbles instead of the intense, trapped pockets. Great for blocking senses in some cases — kinda like a smoke screen — and he successfully confused a worm once with it, misdirecting it.

Otherwise, everyone scrambled to keep the things from getting a ‘wrap’ around the sub, which was clearly what the creatures wanted to do and would be bad news to say the least.

Snapper shined in the altercation, focusing down one worm after the other as the others fended them off, continually with gradually intensifying degenerative damage that made them flee once injured enough. Once three fled, the remaining two gave up and disengaged.

After submarine warfare, though most were getting close to their long-term fatigue limits — even after another break — the group pivoted to some actual underwater escapades, mostly as a wild, unlikely situation considering most of them were straight doomed at deep-sea pressures. Jack decided to renew his familiarity with breathing water to get better acquainted with the oddity, which could be simulated in HRVR pretty much exactly as it had been in reality. Neex was there for the virtual assistance to remind him what to do.

They went through fairly basic horde exercises against such things as greenfin piranha, ‘winged giant crabs,’ and something called ‘spinegouts’ that spit aquadynamic small darts from their mouths. Though the enemies were relatively simple, the water environment made things a bit awkward, and the fights were mostly just a matter of figuring out their necessary adaptations.

Crash and Highfive shined in protecting and leading from the front — Highfive was especially fresh and animated after being cooped up so long in a sub. His powers also worked extremely well in the water, and he moved very fluidly, if a bit more slowly.

Solvent even commented that the adaptation was good training, as real-world scenarios sometimes made fatigue a real issue to manage. Contrary to the typical image of a hero pushing through such ‘immaterial’ matters as physical drain with gritty ease, the performance hit was just unavoidable for ANPs at some point.

Permafrost, for example, did absolutely nothing during the latter fights because she was too spent and not allowed to push herself. Bo, and to a lesser extent, Délight, also didn’t do much thanks to steady fatigue dealt over the periods.

When all was said and done, everyone other than Highfive, Lighthouse, and maybe Spiral — it was hard to tell — were looking absolutely beat, with burnt-out, glassy eyes all around.

“Great job, everyone,” Solvent said when they were once more in the white void. He yawned and stretched. “Ahhgh… Alright, get some rest, everyone. You definitely deserve it.”

Jack met Lattice’s eyes, and they both mutually sighed. Then yawned.

One last thing to do, and I’m not quite sure I’ll succeed whatsoever at this point…
