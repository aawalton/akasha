Brand looked different out of the context of royal events. He was dressed in fine, yet simple clothes, the kind of thing a merchant's son might wear, and his posture was less rigid than it had been during the showcase.

He also looked nervous, which Jane found rather comforting. At least she wasn't the only one who found this situation awkward.

"I'm sorry for bothering you," he said quickly. "I know you probably have things to do. But my mother pushed me to come. She said you mentioned you might be willing to take a look at my magic, and she thought sooner was better than later."

Jane remembered the conversation with the queen. It felt like it had happened weeks ago, rather than just the day before.

"Yes,” she said. “We did discuss that. No problem. Come in."

She stepped aside to let him enter, noting the guards standing casually on either side of the door as she closed it.

Brand walked into her shop. For a moment, he looked around blankly, like he couldn’t understand what he was seeing.

"Oh! Right." He slapped himself on the forehead. “I almost forgot, somehow. You're a baker. You have to bake somewhere. This is where you made those things for the showcase?”

"It is."

"It's nice."

"It suits my needs." Jane moved toward the back of the shop, gesturing for him to follow. "Would you like something to eat? I made extra batches that I didn’t bring to the showcase."

Brand's face lit up. "Yes, please. Do you have any more of those meat buns? I wished I could have had more than just a sample."

Jane retrieved her extra batch of meat buns, unwrapped them, and set them on the counter. Taking one for herself, she pushed the rest toward Brand. He grabbed one immediately and bit into it.

"These are really good," he said around a mouthful of filling. "Different from what I'm used to eating.”

"That's the idea." Jane ate her own bun more slowly. "I hadn’t really had them either, when I was introduced to the recipe. Then I changed it even more."

Despite the strangeness of the situation, Jane found herself relaxing. Brand was easier to be around than she had expected. He didn't seem to need constant entertainment, which had been her initial worry.

"So," she said. "Your magic. What's the problem?"

Brand set down his half-eaten bun. His face instantly shifted into the expression of someone remembering an old frustration.

"My tutors tell me I have fairly respectable magical reserves. Better than average, if not like an elite’s. I seem to be fine with simple spells. But anything more complex, I fail." He shrugged. "It just doesn't work. I can do the basic exercises. I can recite the theory for more advanced spells, and I even think I understand it. But when I actually try to cast them, something goes wrong."

"Wrong how?"

"They just fall apart. Like I'm trying to mold water into a statue, but it won’t hold in any shape once I pull my hands away."

Jane considered that for a while. Several potential explanations presented themselves, one of which was the most common by far. She could hardly believe his tutors would have missed it, but it was still the first thing to check.

"Show me a flame," she said.

Brand nodded and held up his palm. A moment later, a flame sprang to life above his hand. It was bright and steady, which was good. Less good was the fact that it was about half the size of a loaf of bread and burning with an unusual amount of vigor.

Jane raised her eyebrows. "You weren't lying about your magical reserves. But why so big?"

"What do you mean?"

"The flame. It's enormous for a demonstration. Why?"

Brand looked at the fire dancing above his palm as if seeing it for the first time. "I don't know. My teachers were always impressed when the flame was big, so I just got used to doing it like that. They said it showed good potential."

"It does… but now make a small one."

His brow furrowed in concentration. The flame shrank, condensing down from its original size to something about as large as an orange.

"Like this?"

"No." Jane shook her head. "A small one."

She held up her own hand and called a flame into being. It started out roughly the same size as Brand's, which was still significantly larger than most mages would consider appropriate for the exercise. Then she began to squeeze it.

The flame compressed, shrinking down as she tightened her control around it. The light it gave off dimmed as it got smaller, but the intensity remained constant. She kept squeezing until she had a tiny flame hovering above her palm, about half the size of her smallest fingernail. It burned there, perfectly steady. Her magical power consumption dropped to nearly nothing.

"Like this," she said.

Brand stared at the tiny flame for a long moment. Then he looked at his own, still burning at orange-size above his hand, and his brow furrowed again. The flame above his palm began to shrink. It got down to about the size of an egg before it started to waver. He gritted his teeth and kept pushing, trying to compress it further.

The flame flickered, then finally broke apart.

Shaking his head, he let his hand fall onto the table. "I can't. I can get it smaller, but when I try to go past a certain point, it starts to feel like a hard spell."

"That's the first step, then." Jane let her own tiny flame wink out of existence. "Complex spells take all different sizes of magical power to run. Some parts need a lot, while other parts need almost nothing. If you're pouring the same amount of power into every component, the spell falls apart. The pieces don't fit together properly."

Brand was listening intently now. “So what, then? I have to run dozens of spell components at different sizes, all at once?”

He did, if Jane was being truthful. At least, she did, at her level. Then again, some of the spells she was expected to perform were legendary things. Others were cobbled-together combinations of multiple spells that were individually complex. Brand wouldn’t ever need to get that far, really. He was already a very good amateur. Simply understanding control would make him an excellent one.

“Not all at once,” she replied. “With most spells, once you get the flow right, it locks into place and can worry about itself.”

“So there’s hope? It’s just this control part?”

"Probably. If you really understand the spell and it still isn't working, the problem is almost always control. You have plenty of power. What you don't have is precision."

"My tutors never mentioned that."

"They might not have noticed. Or they might not have felt like they should criticize you. A failure on their part, but it happens." Jane moved to a clear space in the middle of her shop floor. "Come here. We're going to do some drills."

She ran him through exercises for the next few hours. They started with flames again, making them bigger and smaller in controlled increments. Brand struggled with the smaller end of the scale. His fire broke apart whenever he tried to compress it past a certain point. But he kept trying, and slowly, incrementally, he began to make progress.

After the flames came wind. Jane had him push a piece of paper across the counter, trying to get him to use just enough power from both sides to balance it on end. This was even harder for him than the fire had been. The paper shot across the room, or else didn’t pick up from the table at all. Finding the middle ground seemed almost impossible.

Meanwhile, Brand was sweating. Nothing about fine control was easy. It was just as tiring as big, flashy magical use, just in a different way.

But Jane wouldn’t let him stop. She knew she would have to tire him out. True control came from being relaxed, and in magic education, that relaxation was best unlocked through exhaustion.

They moved on to other exercises. By the time the afternoon light had started to fade, Brand truly was exhausted. He slumped against Jane's counter, breathing hard, his fine clothes damp with sweat. He looked like he had just run a race.

"How can it be so difficult?" he asked. "It's just making things smaller. It should be easier than making them big."

"The stuff we miss in our initial training is always more difficult to pick up later." Jane retrieved more of the meat buns from the cold box and handed one to him. He took it gratefully, biting into it with less enthusiasm than before but with equal hunger. "Your tutors taught you well, in most ways. But now you have to work on the missing bits. It’s going to be harder.”

"That seems unfair."

"It is, probably." Jane chose a bun for herself and took a bite. "The magic doesn’t care. But it's also fixable. You can’t tell, but you made progress today. Tomorrow, once you’ve rested, you’ll be in a better spot."

Brand looked at his hands, turning them over as if they belonged to someone else. "I didn't know I was missing something so basic. All this time, I thought I just wasn't talented enough to use the more advanced spells.”

"You can use them. You just need more practice." Jane gathered up the cloth wrappings from the buns and set them aside for washing. "Rest well tonight. Come back tomorrow, and we'll run through more drills. With any luck, by the time your family leaves, we'll have you in a better place."

"Thank you," he said. "For taking the time. I know you have your own work to do."

"This is my work too, in a way." Jane smiled as she led him to the door. "I'm glad I can help."

She watched him walk away down the street. He would be sore tomorrow, she suspected. Magical exhaustion was its own kind of weariness, no less real than physical fatigue.

Well, she thought, closing the door and leaning against it. I suppose my part in all this isn't quite done, after all.

—

Brand came back the next day, just as Jane had asked him to. Unfortunately, he came back right in the middle of her afternoon rush.

That was her fault. She hadn’t given him an arrival time, and there was no way the poor sheltered prince could know much about working day schedules.

The shop was packed with customers, all of them wanting bread and all of them wanting it now. Jane still spotted Brand the moment he walked in, mostly because the customers nearest the door went suddenly quiet and started exchanging glances.

Of course they recognize him. He was just on a stage in front of the whole town.

Brand seemed to realize he had caused a stir. He stood awkwardly near the entrance, clearly unsure whether to approach the counter or retreat entirely. Jane caught his eye and gave him what she hoped was an encouraging nod.

"When should I come back?" he asked, raising his voice just enough to be heard. “A few hours?”

"It's fine. Just wait outside, near the lake. Enjoy the weather." Jane handed a wrapped loaf to the customer in front of her and accepted their coins, wondering exactly what rumors they would carry out with their bread. "I'm in my last big rush. I should be done soon."

Brand nodded and slipped back out the door, leaving behind a murmur of curious whispers that Jane did her best to ignore.

The next half-hour passed quickly, eaten up by transactions and the mild annoyance of knowing her relationship with the prince was already being misinterpreted. Once the last stragglers had collected their purchases and headed home, Jane let out a long breath and surveyed her nearly empty shelves with satisfaction.

Not bad.

Her delivery box sat outside the front door, waiting to be loaded. She gathered up the remaining loaves and cookies, bundled everything into cloth wrappings, and started carrying them out.

Brand appeared a moment later.

"Let me help,” he said, seeing her arms full of cloth-wrapped bread.

"You don't have to."

"I want to."

“Then go get a few bundles and bring them out. These deliveries have to be sorted.”

What followed was a surprisingly efficient system. Brand ran back and forth between the bakery and the delivery box, bringing out bundles while Jane arranged them in the proper compartments. He moved quickly and didn't complain. Within a few minutes, the work that would have taken her twice as long on her own was done.

"This is clever," Brand said, examining the delivery box. "People just come and take what they ordered?"

"That's the idea. They leave payment in the slot at the bottom if they haven't already paid."

"And you built this?"

"Allen built it." Jane smiled as she closed and latched the last compartment. "He figured out how to make it work."

Brand nodded slowly. "He's your boyfriend, right? Allen?"

"Yes. We started seeing each other just after I moved here."

"That's good." Brand’s manner was pleased, like she had confirmed what he’d hoped was the case. "He seems like a good person. And you're a good person. It fits."

"I'm glad you approve." Jane meant it as a joke, but realized it wasn’t, really. Hesitating just a moment, she went on, trying to keep her tone light. “Before you came, there were rumors. About your father wanting to form a match between you and me."

"Actually, that's true."

Jane blinked. The straightforward admission had caught her off-guard.

Brand shook his head with a wry smile. "Don't worry about it. My father is pushy sometimes. He has many ideas about what would be good for me. He's not always wrong, but he's not always right, either. If it comes down to it, I'll tell him to stop. And he will."

"Just like that?"

"Just like that. He's my father, but he doesn’t own me. He understands that."

Jane found herself oddly reassured. Here was the man himself, telling her it wasn’t a problem. If she wasn’t going to listen to him, she wasn’t going to listen to anybody.

"Come on," she said, gesturing towards the bakery door. "We have work to do."
