"Are you sure you want to leave so soon?"

Jade stood just outside the front door of her house, keeping it slightly ajar with her foot. The town of Lysara stretched out above and below her, illuminated by shafts of mottled sunlight that shifted and wavered as the gentle breeze rustled through the trees. Elyx stood on the walkway in front of her, a large pack slung over her shoulder.

"I'm sure." Elyx nodded once. "The longer I wait, the fewer of my people I'll be able to help."

The demon was dressed for travel, garbed in a well-tailored tunic and pants. It was a sharp contrast to the worn and ragged ritualist's robes Jade had first met her in. Jade smiled.

"Would you like any help? I don't get out much, these days."

"I may take you up on that if I run into trouble, but I think it'd be best if I give it a try on my own, first." The Lugren gave her a half shrug. "I can't keep relying on others forever.""Relying on friends isn't a weakness." Jade advised her, thinking back to her own adventures. "It took me too long to realize that.""You're probably right. It took me a long time to even begin to grasp the concept of friendship. Where I come from, the closest you could get to someone outside of your clan was an "ally"."But you'd like to go on your own, regardless?"

"I would." Elyx nodded. "I have to show that to my people. Those of us who refused to follow the greater demons to Gremnagar, at least. I will show them that there is a place for us in the Labyrinth, if only we can learn a better way of life."

While the goddess had helped the Labyrinth seal the portals to the demon home world, a large host of demons had already entered the Labyrinth. The best estimates placed the number of them around ten thousand, not counting any who may have found a way into the Labyrinth beforehand. Under the leadership of their commanding greater demons many of them had immediately fled to Gremnegar, home of the Blood Witch and supposed ally of their cause. Many, however, refused to follow their cruel leaders any further, choosing to scatter into the depths of the Labyrinth instead. Elyx, having spent the past few months improving Lysara's monster wards and adjusting to the local ways of life, had decided it was her duty to help her lost kin.

"Then I wish you the best." Jade clapped her on the shoulder and gave her a gentle squeeze. "Just don't forget to visit."

"Of course not." Elyx grinned, looking up at the cozy home looming over Jade. "You were one of my first friends. I might still be learning, but I know to keep in touch."

"Plus, you like the food Ashar makes.""That too." Elyx raised her hands in defeat. "If I see him in Illysport, I'll have to ask for his schedule. Knowing what days he'll be visiting you here would help.""Oh get going, you." Jay snorted, making a shooing gesture with her hands. Her voice softened. "Just be careful, yeah? The Labyrinth might not spawn monsters anymore, but there are still plenty of dangers out there.""Thanks." Elyx nodded seriously. "I will be."Jade bid her farewell. She watched the Lugren depart, setting off down the winding walkway that snaked its way between the trees. Four months. Had it really been that long, already? That was two-thirds of the time she'd spent adventuring as a Wayfarer, but it had gone by in a flash. Days really were a lot more routine when ravenous monsters weren't trying to kill you on an hourly basis.

She would miss Elyx, the Lugren had a sharp mind and a certain sharp wit that Jade appreciated. The people of Lysara had grown to accept her as well, especially after the ritualist spent many weeks working tirelessly to improve their magical defenses against monsters. Even if the Labyrinth wasn't creating more strongholds, everybody felt more at ease with better defenses following the undead incident this past year.

Jade re-entered her home, closing the door behind her and moving towards the living room, where she could hear soft conversation. Elyx wasn't someone that she'd ever thought she'd get along with. After all, the demon was at least partly responsible for opening the portals to the Labyrinth, a chain of events that had ultimately led to the deaths of two of her friends, among countless others. But Jade's mental senses were finely honed these days, and she could sense no malice or other malignant emotions churning within the woman. Instead, there was only weariness, regret, and a tiny spark of hope. Hope for a new way of life, free from oppression. Jade couldn't bring herself to dislike her, after that. As long as she stayed true to those feelings, she would always be her friend.

"It's what Luis would have done."

The pain was duller, now, but no less present for the widening gulf of time that separated the two of them. It would always be there, a hole that no one else could fill. But that was okay. The reminder of his absence served as motivation to do better, and Jade knew the rest of her companions felt the same.

Speaking of, Jade thought she recognized one of the voices coming from the next room.

"Focus is the most important aspect of spellcraft. It doesn't matter how much mana you have or what tier of spell you can cast if your concentration slips. For hundreds of years people have relied on the Labyrinth's system to manage spell mana for them with soul imprints. Now that the Wayfarer system is gone, they're in for a rude awakening. Take Jade, for example."

Jade stepped into her living room just in time to see Tara gesture back at her without taking her eyes off her pair of students.

"Jade, have you been keeping up with the mana management meditations Aylin and I taught you?"

"…Sort of?"

The tek'kalla sighed, planting her hands on her hips and turning, somewhat dramatically, to face her.

"Jade, you're never going to progress past C-tier if you don't practice the fundamentals. The system assist won't work on any new spells you learn. If you don't want to be left behind, you'd better get with the program."

"Yes, professor."

Tara rolled her eyes, but Jade saw the glint of amusement in them. She turned back to her students.

"Alan, I hope for your sake you're more diligent than your sister. You too, Maria."

The two teenagers nodded earnestly, voicing their assurances. Tara waved them away.

"Good, good. Now, make sure you practice the exercises I taught you today. I'll check on your progress next time."

Jade followed Tara out of the living room, leaving her brother and Maria to pore over the texts the mage had lent them. While nice and quite comfortable, the house wasn't very large, and the two of them soon found themselves at the front door.

"Heading home?"

"Not yet," Tara said, punching a command into her wrist display. It beeped and flashed a blue light. "I'm making a stop in Rimestead first.""Dropping in to see Fenrin? Tell him he needs to visit more often.""You know you could always go see him, right?"

"I've never been to Rimestead, so the nodes can't get me anywhere near there." Jade complained. "It'd take me, what, two weeks to get there? He could be here in a few hours."

"That wouldn't have stopped you before." Tara said, giving her a slight grin. "Are you getting soft?"

"As if." Jade snorted. "I'm just waiting for things to settle down out there before I go looking for more trouble.""Oh, so you can learn." The tek'kalla's grin turned sly. "I'm relieved.""Oh be quiet, you're just as bad."

Tara raised her hands in surrender.

"Actually, Fenrin told me he'd like my help with some ritual magic. I think he's ready to bind a new familiar.""Really?" Jade perked up. "That's great news! Let me know if I can help."The team, the whole cosmos, really, owed a lot to Fenrin's first familiar, Mirella. Jade had felt just how deeply her loss had cut into him, but he'd persevered despite that pain. Looking back, Jade wished she'd been more supportive. If there was a way she could repay his efforts, she would.

"I will." Tara nodded. "The kids are quick learners. Their skills are coming along well.""Really?"

"Absolutely. In fact, they have something of a leg up on the rest of us.""How's that?""They're learning to use magic properly from the start, without any of the bad habits the system instilled in us. Even I have to make an effort to not rely on it. With enough effort and time, their growth will eclipse that of people who don't adapt well to this new age."

Jade gave Tara the side eye.

"…are you talking about me?"

"Only a little." Tara laughed. "Jokes aside, you're doing okay. But you really should spend more time meditating.""Okay, okay, I promise."

Tara opened the door, but before she could step outside, Jade laid a hand on her shoulder.

"Thanks for doing this for them."

The words came out a little awkwardly. Jade's social skills had improved over the past year, but straightforward sincerity had never been her strong suit. But this was important, so she made herself say it anyway.

"Of course. Your brother has been through a lot. And so has Maria." Tara glanced away, breaking eye contact. Jade picked up what remained unspoken. They each had their own way of honoring the memories of those they'd lost. "I want to help them however I can."

"Well, I appreciate it."Tara's wrist display beeped and she let out a groan.

"I really have to go. Is Elyx around?"

"Sorry, you just missed her. You'll have to catch up with her later.""Damn, I had some books to give her. I'll drop them off here next time I visit.""Why are you in such a hurry?"

"I have to be back aboard the Intrepid this time tomorrow. You know, for the confinement?"

Jade raised her eyebrows.

"That's still going on? I can't believe they keep you under house arrest for helping stop an interdimensional invasion."

"It's only one week a month, and we did steal an airship." Tara pointed out. Then she muttered. "It's still a huge pain, though.""Didn't they promote you, too? I don't understand your people."

"And I don't understand yours. Seriously, Jade, some of the things you humans put on the Internet are just…" The tek'kalla trailed off and shook her head. She walked off, still muttering under her breath, and Jade saw her off with an amused wave. When she closed the door, all the energy seemed to flow from her body. She leaned heavily against it, sighing. It wasn't that she disliked socializing, but it left her feeling she'd been hit by her own draining kiss attack.

"Jay? You alright?"

The sound of Alan's voice made her stand up properly again. She gave her brother a tired smile.

"I hear you and Maria are quick learners. Nice work, champ."

She made a fist and pressed it into his shoulder, just like she had when they were kids. Before he'd fallen ill, before her injury, before the whole Universe had turned upside down. Everything was different now, their relationship was different, but they still had each other. Sometimes, it took painful losses to make you appreciate what you still had.

"Really?" Alan eyed her skeptically. "The way Tara talks sometimes, you'd think she was one step away from losing it with us.""That's just how she is." Jade laughed. "She's happy with the two of you."

Alan smiled. It wasn't the forced, painful look she had seen so many times before, but a genuine expression of happiness. The sight warmed her heart. They talked for a little, about nothing important. About when he'd be back to visit again, when Jade would come visit family, and when he'd get to see Nythis and her husband again. Their relationship had changed so many times over the years, and it was undergoing yet another transformation. Jade was fine with that. After all, it meant that he was still around to have a relationship with.

He left a few minutes later, returning to the living room to study some more with Maria. Jade's gaze was drawn to the click, click, click of his cane against the wooden floor as he laboriously made the short walk back across the house. Healing magic had its limits, and Alan's illness had pressed against more than one of them. He was healthy, now, but that didn't mean that his normal was the same as everyone else's. Sometimes, even all the power in the world couldn't get you what you wanted. Real life wasn't a fairy tale, and not everyone got their happy ending. More often, you were left with a strange mix of victories and failures, and it was up to you to decide what to make of them. The choice between dwelling on all that you'd lost and appreciating what you still had sounded like an obvious one to Jade, but in practice, it wasn't so simple. Every day, she fought the temptation to drown in a sea of what-ifs and self-doubt, and she knew she wasn't the only one.

They said time healed all wounds, and so Jade was content to remain in Lysara. There, she kept the home of her first elven friend safe while giving herself the time and space to sort through the wounds in her heart. One day, she would be ready to venture forth and have another adventure, but that remained a distant prospect. For the time being, her goal was to learn how to live a normal life again. One devoid of life and death battles, risky missions, and dark urges. There, she'd caught a lucky break. While living as a succubus had its benefits, Jade had always resented the unpleasant instincts and desires her new body frequently forced upon her. That problem had lessened slightly after she'd drained Seraphine and her spirit gained a celestial aspect, but the change was minor. After draining Ira, however, the light in her spirit had grown noticeably brighter. It didn't completely drown out the temptations and urges of her demonic aspect, but it did blunt them. For that, she was grateful.

The pealing ring of a distant bell snapped Jade back to the present. Evening sunlight was filtering through the hallway's high window, painting lines of vibrant orange and crimson across the wall and floor. Had it gotten that late already? Alan and Maria's voices filtered in from the living room, and she heard the two of them laughing together. That's right! They were staying the night, and she barely had any food left in the house. She hurriedly pulled on a coat, calling out that she would be back shortly. Could she make it to the market before it closed for the day? Jade grinned. If she took the gondolas, likely not. Fortunately, she had an alternative.

A pair of leathery wings burst from her back, along with horns and a supple tail. She took to the sky, weaving her way between platforms and walkways suspended between the tall trees of the forest, making her way to the higher levels of the village. Sure, there were downsides to the whole experience of being a succubus. The prejudice, the violent urges, the libido…

Actually, she could go either way on that one.

Jade shook her head, dispelling the thought, and a smile lit up her face. It was at times like this, when the wind whipped against her hair and the open sky stretched above her, that she knew in her heart that it all was worth it.
