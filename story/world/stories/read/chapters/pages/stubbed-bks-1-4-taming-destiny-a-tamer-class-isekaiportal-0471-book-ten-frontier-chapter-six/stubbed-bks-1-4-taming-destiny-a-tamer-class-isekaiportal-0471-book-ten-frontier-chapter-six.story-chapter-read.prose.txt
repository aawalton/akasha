Markus, what’s happening? Elarion’s terse voice asks over our Alliance Bond. I don’t know whether the Tier four before me is capable of reading thoughts I send along that route, but I decide to err on the safe side and moderate my response.

Can’t you guys hear for yourself?

No, Valence interjects impatiently before Elarion can answer. Ever since you went out, we haven’t heard anything. Even my scouts’ Skills are failing to transmit your conversation.

I wonder why – the Tier four’s domain? Then focus on the pressures of the present.

It turns out that this is Kalanthia’s mate, I tell them wryly.

There’s silence.

And then they all speak at once.

Then why is he attacking us?

Did your Bonded order this?

Why is he still clearly antagonistic?

I’m almost there.

The last is Josiah, the last to join the party.

Hold on everyone, I sigh, patting the air in front of me in a useless but automatic gesture. Let me explain. I outline the situation – mentioning that the Tier four still fears we’re holding his mate and cub against their wills, despite Lathani’s staunch defence and that he’s demanding I bring his mate back before dawn tomorrow.

Is she not far away? Layton asks with a touch of anxiety. I thought you said she’d left us a while ago. Is she close by?

Yes, yes, and no, I answer him in order.

Then we must prepare to fight, Elarion concludes with steel in his voice.

Not necessarily, I reply. There’s a long moment of silence again.

Markus, can you explain that? Valence asks politely but sounding like he’s holding onto his composure by a thin thread. It’s been a long few days for us. Heck, a long few weeks, so I don’t blame him for being a bit worn. I feel it too. But we can’t let the situation get to us now, not after everything we’ve been through.

I have a Skill that allows me to summon my Bonded to my side. Theoretically, I could use it to pull Kalathia here in seconds.

Theoretically, Elarion repeats flatly.

I highly doubt that I have enough mana in my pool to teleport her at all, let alone across the distance between us, I admit honestly.

The lords are silent for a long moment, no doubt feeling the same frustration as I am. Arguably, I could send a summons down the Bond to Kalanthia, but it would still take her far too long to get here even if she came immediately.

What about a ritual? Valence suggests.

A ritual? I question, blinking.

Like the ones you did with Earth Mages to create our settlement, only dedicated to empowering you to complete the Skill.

Is that even possible? Josiah questions. Elemental circles are well-known. What you’re suggesting is something very different.

The other lords dissolve into a debate on whether it’s possible, but I’ve stopped paying them attention.

A ritual…it’s certainly true that an elemental circle wouldn’t work for this. But I’m considering the other rituals I’ve been part of since coming to this world – the Curse I laid on Kyrian, the blood adoption and, more relevantly, the one that allowed me to find my companions when they were kidnapped.

I need to speak to Sarran, I say in the Alliance Bond, my interjection breaking through the lively debate still taking place.

Sarran? Layton asks doubtfully. What would he know of this – he isn’t a mage.

No, but he’s Nicholas’ manservant and my…head of House is accomplished with rituals. He might know something simply from being around him. But you have a point – I should get Tiria involved.

Lady Keshaka too, perhaps, Elarion suggests. House Artifice is known to dabble with rituals from time to time.

Good idea, I praise.

Nodding to myself, I focus back on the Tier four.

“I shall see you shortly,” I tell him. I glance at Lathani. “Would you prefer to stay out here with your father or come back with me?”

She hasn’t had a chance to answer before the Tier four’s snarl is once more ripping through the air.

Where do you think you’re going? he demands rudely.

Honestly, powerful Tier four Beast King or not, I’ve had enough of his attitude.

I cross my arms over my chest and stare him down.

“Do you want me to bring your mate back or not? Because I can’t just snap my fingers and do it or I would have already. It will require time, research, and preparation – if it’s possible at all.”

The Tier four’s snarl intensifies again.

I’ve warned you already – if you don’t bring back my mate before dawn–

“Yes, I get it,” I interrupt him, refusing to be intimidated. “You’ll wipe us all off the surface of your territory. Have you actually considered what might happen if I’m telling the truth and Kalanthia gets back to find out that her mate has killed all of her friends and traumatised her cub?”

I don’t want to start arguing about who is stronger so I don’t share who I think would be the ultimate victor in that fight. But we killed the Wind roc in conditions far more advantageous to it than these are for the nunda, so I’d put money on my side. But it’s clear that any victory would be hard won and we’ve had enough of battle for now.

My words are enough to give the Tier four pause and his snarl quietens until it’s silent around me again.

He doesn’t speak, but I nod – the silence is answer enough.

“Since I also don’t want to explain to her why I’ve hurt or even killed her mate, I’m willing to do my best to comply with your terms. But I will not be bullied or have my movements curtailed by you in the process.”

The Tier four looks at me with curiosity in his body language.

You are oddly outspoken for one far weaker than I am.

I give him a humourless smile.

“If you know anything about humans, you should know not to underestimate us.”

The nunda eyes me. I can’t read his thoughts like he can mine, but I get the feeling he’s at least a little impressed.

That’s confirmed when he blinks slowly, the white glow of his eyes dying down to reveal pale golden orbs beneath. The sense of static electricity in the air around us also fades as he lies down.

Then I shall wait. His head turns to face Lathani. Daughter, keep me company, he demands imperiously. I clear my throat. He turns his head to pin me beneath his golden gaze. I stare back at him, unimpressed. He chuffs quietly and turns his gaze back to Lathani. Please, he sighs.

It’s your choice, I tell the black nunda firmly.

She hesitates for a moment, then moves to lie a short distance away from the male.

Mother never told me much about my father. I think it hurt her to speak of her mate, she tells me contemplatively. I would appreciate learning more.

I smile at her, the expression only a little stiff, then step over to rub behind her ears, fully aware of the heavy golden gaze watching my every move with predatory intent.

Let me know if you change your mind.

With a final glance thrown at the Tier four, Bastet and I head back to the settlement moving quickly but not in a way that would indicate a fearful retreat.

As soon as we step through the doors, we breathe a brief sigh of relief – the wards on the wall are able to resist the Tier four’s domain now that he’s no longer actively directing it at us, and the lifting of predatory dominance is like a weight off our shoulders.

I glare at my hands as they tremble for a moment with the last traces of adrenaline in my system, then just allow myself to breathe through it until my shoulders loosen.

But I only give myself a few moments – there’s no time to waste.

Within a few minutes, we’re back in the command building and I’ve called Sarran, Tiria, and Keshaka to join the other lords and I in the strategy room. Loran slips in too and I decide to allow it. I doubt he’ll have much to offer to the discussion, but he might prove himself useful in other ways.

The three newcomers listen to my retelling of the situation. Sarran has a grave look on his face, Keshaka looks politely interested – as she almost always does – and Tiria looks fascinated. I finish by explaining our potential solution and then look at them hopefully.

“Do you know anything that could help us?”

Tiria practically bounces in place, words bursting out of her as if a dam has broken.

“I’ve never heard of anyone using a circle of mages to empower a Skill, but it would be excellent for my research to see it done!” I send her an exasperated look.

“That would require us to figure out how to do it, first,” I point out to her.

She subsides with a faint pout on her face.

“I might have an idea,” Sarran offers thoughtfully. I raise my eyebrows at him. He glances pointedly at our company. “Do you wish me to share the details with everyone? It concerns your original arrival here.”

The way he subtly emphasises ‘here’ tells me that he doesn’t mean the Lost Continent.

I consider the question for a moment.

“Would…Lord Nicholas consider it a Titanbend family secret?” I ask slowly. I have a good deal of respect for Nicholas at this point, and don’t wish to cause problems for him even if they wouldn’t then reflect on me as the House’s heir.

Sarran hesitates.

“I do not believe so. However, he may not wish the details to become common knowledge.”

“Then tell us what you think you can safely say in company. If there is more that you cannot say, I will raise a Seal and you can tell me privately.”

I glance around the group with a raised eyebrow, challenging anyone to object. None do – if there’s one good thing about being with a group of nobles, it’s that they understand the importance of privacy, even if they may not like it. All their Houses have secrets they would not wish others to know.

“I believe it is common knowledge that you came from another world,” Sarran starts. I see several nods and no shock – after it was the subject of a meeting of the Great Houses, I’m unsurprised that the information has spread widely. “However, what might not be so well known is that the power required to open the portal was considerable – and my lord did it alone.”

Keshaka leans forwards.

“Did he use a mana battery?” she asks with curiosity.

“Not in the way you mean the word, my lady,” Sarran answers politely. “Instead, it was a carefully-constructed ritual that used Cores and reagents painted on the floor around him to direct the power through him.”

“A reverse battery,” Keshaka murmurs, her expression fascinated. “Instead of power concentrated in a single object and drawn out to power a device, it was collecting inside Lord Titanbend. Why have we never considered this before?”

“I wonder what that would have looked like in his internal matrix,” Tiria interjects. I feel a moment of fond exasperation when I see that she’s pulled out a piece of parchment and a pen and is scribbling notes. For a moment, I wonder whether it was a bad idea to have invited the two magical equivalents of scientists to the meeting.

“Both interesting topics, I’m sure,” I say a little impatiently, “but we have a deadline here. So if we could focus….”

Both ladies look briefly abashed and turn their attention back to me. I do notice that Tiria lays the pen and parchment aside rather than putting it back into her Inventory. Clearly her focus is only temporary. It’s good enough for now.

“So essentially, Sarran, you’re suggesting that instead of using other mages to help me power the Skill, I use a ritual that involves Cores and specific reagents?”

“Indeed, my lord,” he agrees, “as it removes the requirement of the other mages involved to have a similar Skill to the one you are using.” Since I doubt anyone else has anything like Bond Teleport, that would be useful. “However, Lord Nicholas was not using a Skill when he summoned you through the portal, so the ritual as he did it would not be immediately appropriate.”

I consider the words then nod.

“Alright, looks like we need to put our heads together and figure out something that takes the Skill aspect from the traditional mage circle, and the empowerment from N-Lord Nicholas’ ritual.”

A quick glance around me indicates that everyone is on-board with the idea – no one wants a fight and this looks like the only idea we have to avoid one. And, as my new watch informs me, dawn isn’t as nearly far off as we’d like it to be.
