The days of the march passed quickly, with Ezra spending them in what was nearly a secluded state. Others would talk to him when he would let them, but that was infrequent. In the morning, he would retreat to some place outside of the camp to practice with his aura, able to conduct two tests. Each of these was largely unsupervised by Jormun, though Ezra made sure to run any new ideas by the experienced ghost. Jormun had internalized the lesson from Ezra’s dangerous breakthrough, and Ezra didn’t take that for granted.

Each of these experiments was fun, and mostly showed Ezra how much practice Helen and Artemis had put into using their aura creatively and effectively. Helen’s fire was sharp and disciplined, and Ezra was fairly certain that she’d trained to use less than what he was beginning to think of as a “typical” amount of aura per flare of fire. With his own lack of discipline and practice, he was creating massive pillars of wind and even a four-meter blade once, while her fire was concentrated but not deadly.

Then there was Artemis’s use of water on her shield. That’d been an amazing application of her aura, trapping a weapon while fortifying herself. Ezra couldn’t say there was any chance he’d have thought of that any time soon. He was too focused on hitting things hard, he knew. It was what had killed the orc the way it did, so it wasn’t all bad, but Jormun’s constant reminders to be flexibly minded stuck with him, and his experiments were often at least half focused on helping himself jump far (which often had him landing in a tumble and covered with bruises) or to push something away from him (which always had him spitting leaves and grit from his mouth).

Even so, some of his experiments were also successful, like making his spear’s blade extend or to help himself throw it further, though he did manage to snap his spear’s tip on a half-buried boulder doing that.

“You’re not getting another one,” came Georg’s command, and Ezra couldn’t fault his lieutenant for the decision. A week after taking the hit to his head from the orc, Georg could now walk, if unsteadily, and he still spent half of the day in a cart, quietly cursing his fate as his legs didn’t quite listen to him the way that they used to.

After his morning’s practice, Ezra helped strike camp and get on the road, walking and meditating as much as he could to be able to practice when they stopped for lunch. That meal was always quick, less than half an hour to stretch, rest their legs, and eat some dried rations. On the first few days of the trip, he hadn’t had the capacity to recover enough of his inner world to conduct another experiment at this time, instead dedicating every spare second to more meditation. After a few days, he’d improved enough to be able to recover about half of his total aura reserves on the march. Thus, he would have one more opportunity to try something at lunch, then throw himself back into true meditation to recover.

The afternoon’s march was just the same as the morning’s, moving along, talking when talked to, but largely just continuing to focus on his inner world. In the evening, when they set up camp, Ezra would hustle to complete his own duties before finding a quiet spot to meditate, recover his reserves enough to be able to try two more aura-fueled experiments, and then go back to his tent. There, he allowed himself to loosen up a little bit more, having a conversation with his fellow recruits, but his single-minded focus was causing those relationships to suffer, he could tell.

Boyd continued being his same rambunctious self, seemingly unconcerned with anything, just glad that they had all managed to leave the battle alive and would be returning to steady employment, though he didn’t badger Ezra any more. Cord and Leah, however, had tried to break Ezra out of his self-imposed exile the first few days to no avail, and now seemed to have largely given up on being able to have a longer conversation with him. At night, he tried his best to make up for the days of quiet, but he could feel the difference in how he was being treated. Instead of incessant questions about becoming an Adept or a Knight, or anything else, they exchanged pleasantries, maybe gave some quick thoughts about returning home, and then went to bed.

It’s easy to grow resentful, Jormun said when Ezra complained about it to the old ghost, when someone you considered a peer quickly outpaces you. Then, you’re not helping by being able to give them any secret information to help them catch up, and you’re too absorbed in what makes you special during the day to talk to them. It isn’t a surprise that they feel like you think you’re better than them.

“Of course I don’t think that,” Ezra snapped back. “I’m just nearly positive that I’m going to get pulled out of Villy to do whatever the Duke tells me to do instead. So, I need to make sure that I’m ready for that so I don’t get humiliated or hurt doing that.”

So you think you’re more special than them, that you’re going to get taken somewhere else where only the elite of the elite recruits can go. Jormun’s voice was even, unconcerned, and he maintained eye contact with Ezra, who just tried to create a blade of wind to no avail.

“Of course I don’t think that,” he scoffed. “If I don’t get pulled out of Villy, that’s fine with me. I’ll be happy to stick around with people who I’ve made friends with instead of having to go off and try to get a read on whoever the Duke thinks is worthy of serving him!”

Really? Jormun pressed. You really don’t think that you would be unhappy if you didn’t get that special treatment? You’ve been getting it until now; don’t you feel entitled to it?

“Of course I’m not entitled to special treatment,” Ezra snorted. “I’m a slum rat who’s lucked into one thing after the next, and I am now just waiting for the other shoe to drop, to crush me back into my place. I have no illusions about it, I have no connections, no power, and nothing beyond a higher-than-average aptitude for cultivating aura.”

And me.

“And you,” Ezra allowed. “Even still, I’m just a random bastard from the streets of a shitty village, whose most valuable possessions are ones that were given to him by the cheapskate Count of that village. I don’t deserve shit, and I’m lucky to have what I’ve got.”

But you would still be disappointed if you don’t get pulled away to be trained by the special strong subordinate of the Duke, Jormun insisted, his eyes piercing Ezra’s.

The young man was the first to look away, sighing as he admitted, “Yes, I’d be disappointed. You’ve said that having somebody to physically teach me will be more valuable than just having a ghost do it, even though you’re better than them and we’ve established that you just didn’t want to teach me before.”

That’s not really the point right now, Jormun deflected. You’ve hurt feelings. And you haven’t really cared as much as you should have because a part of you was convinced that you’re more special than them and it wouldn’t matter in the long run. Now, you’re reaping the results of that.

“But if I do get called to serve the Duke,” Ezra protested, “I’ll need to be better than I am now! I won’t be able to keep up with anybody who’s had years and even decades of training anyway, so I might as well make sure that my aura is as strong as possible to try to not be as outclassed in that way.”

I told you before, I’m not going to tell you what to do. I hope to help you see things as they really are, but this is your decision, Jormun raised his voice, the sound reverberating in Ezra’s chest. I don’t think that what you’re doing is a bad decision, but it is a natural consequence of acting like this is a temporary place and you won’t be around for long. Of course if other people feel like you don’t care, they won’t treat you as if you’re going to be around for long. It’s just reciprocal.

“So how can I make things better?” Ezra asked, feeling the loneliness settle in as Jormun said it was his fault.

You can slow down with the throwing yourself into meditation while you’re marching and instead talk more, show more interest in the things they have going on at home. You still don’t really know why each of them has joined in with the army, because all of you talking until now was about how you were going to fight against the goblinoid army, Jormun said, not needing to hesitate. It’s up to you if you think that it’s worth it to do that, if you think that socialization is more important than the training. You’re inevitably going to lead people, and dealing well with your allies and subordinates is an important part of leadership.

That answer left Ezra frustrated, unsure of which to choose. Eventually, he decided to ask Georg what the old man thought Ezra’s chances of actually being picked up were.

“Technically,” Georg answered, his steps unsteady as he walked down an incline, “I don’t know, and I can’t tell you. In reality, you saved my life, I respect you, and you’re a good kid. For that, I’ll tell you: don’t get too comfortable in Villy.”

“Does that mean…” Ezra asked, trailing off and hoping for a specific answer.

“That means exactly what I said,” Georg answered, saying nothing else. “Thanks again for saving my life. I look forward to your journey ahead.”

With as much of an answer as that was going to be, Ezra nodded his thanks, steeled himself, and returned to his previous routine, engaging in polite, if meaningless, conversation between furious bouts of meditation while preparing himself for a future that Georg seemed to be promising.

***

The rest of the journey home passed uneventfully, though Ezra was beginning to find some success with, instead of making an attack, strengthening his whole body with aura. Now that it carried the wind affinity, it did more than simply strengthen him, making his steps lighter, his strikes faster. Though he wasn’t testing out the strength of his hits, Ezra was fairly certain that, where he had sometimes struggled to punch into the vitals of the goblins, every attack he made while strengthening himself like that would punch through their defenses without issue.

It’s not a bad idea, basically creating a minor shroud for yourself, Jormun approved. It’ll make your reserves go further while you are still an Adept, but you really should be more flexible with what different ideas you can achieve with your aura. Everybody, once they become a Pseudo-Knight, can use a shroud. Adept is the time where you start to have enough aura to experiment sometimes, but not so much that you’re accidentally going to kill yourself. So, keep trying this out, but make sure that you don’t stop testing out the limits of what you’re capable of now.

Ezra took that to heart and continued to think of different things to try. He managed to twist his ankle a dozen times as he tried to jump a dozen meters, only becoming capable of not hurting himself when he limited himself to only half that. Otherwise, it was just experimentation with little to show for it.

And then, two and a half weeks after they had marched out, rumbles passed up and down the column of soldiers, because Villy was in view.

They were home.
