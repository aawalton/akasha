I wanted to stay on the asphalt. Everything was healed and returned to normal. My arm was mine again. There were no bruises, cuts, scabs, scars—nothing. It was like I had never entered the Instance at all. My body felt lighter, and despite my earlier fatigue, my body felt as if it had just woken up from the best nap of its life. After all of that, I had nothing to show for it.

I stood. My mind was racing, and I could feel my eyes still scanning for threats before I even knew it. On all sides, I was surrounded by unknown people, and it was all the self-control I could muster to not dart off right away.

There was an opportunity here. The one I had been searching for when I went to find an Instance. These people may know something about The Endless Dream and, unlike Nate and Maggie, probably had no reason to want to kill me. That was only an assumption. I knew nothing. I had been reminded of that time and time again. Murder could've been considered normal for all I knew.

I wondered if the two of them made it out or if they had been consumed by the Instance. I hoped- I didn't know what I hoped, but I knew if they were wandering about, it wasn't safe. There was also Kay. Was she okay? I scanned the crowd from the periphery, trying to stay near its edges for a quick getaway. Several people stared at me from their own little groups. The ones that stared wore complicated expressions—their eyes widened slightly in a vague recognition that made my skin crawl. Most paid me no mind. They were too focused on comforting each other or finding loved ones.

I paused, staring at the sheer number of Visitors, unsure what to make of them. They looked normal, like they could've been from my world, if not for their more Renaissance fair style, but even that was interwoven with more complex textiles. I was at a loss for what to do, and before I could decide, static took half of my vision.

[Jeren has offered you a mutual contract. No associated party may harm or trap the other for (10) hours. The Presence cost has been paid by the offering party. Do you accept? Y/N]

I liked the wording. I didn't see a caveat. I selected yes. Nothing outwardly changed, but the static did reform.

[You are now in a mutual contract with Sable Horizon Exchange and associated independent parties.]

A voice—sturdy but obviously exhausted—called to me from a few feet off, just out of my vision. "Are you looking for someone?" I turned to see a larger, portly man with a scraggly blonde beard looking at me.

I fought the urge to ask 'what' or 'excuse me' on instinct. I knew what the man had said; it was just taking my brain a second to process it, so I stayed silent until I was sure of my response.

"The traitors. Nate and Maggie–" I began, but the man cut me off.

"They've been handled. We have Maggie in custody. She'll stand trial once we descend." He gave me an appraising look. "I remember seeing you. Our minds were foggy when we were those creatures, but we remember the end better."

I tensed. I kept my hand loose by my side, but mentally prepared myself to pull Peeler out to kill this person if they attacked. Then I remembered I couldn't do that because of the contract.

"And Nate?" I pressed.

The man sighed and shook his head, "The Dream has him now."

I said nothing. So that's why he was so sensitive about Presence. I had assumed as much, but having it confirmed for me felt-. I don't know. Odd.

"Do you have a group with you?" the man asked, his arms crossed.

I nodded, lying, "Yeah. I'm their scout."

The man moved slowly, extending his hand to me, "My name's Jeren. I'm one of the leaders of this caravan. Most of us are from Raylen, but there are a few other Visitors from other worlds that were also trapped."

"Dorian," I responded. I didn't offer my hand—no way was I going to give someone a grip on me.

Jeren's smile faltered somewhat as his arm lowered to his side. To his credit, he recovered quickly with a small head shake, "Well met. Our other caravan leader wanted to talk with you. She's hoping we could answer some questions for each other."

"I like answers," the quip came out flat, but Jeren laughed anyway. His voice was heavily accented, elongated at the beginning, and sharp at the end. I didn't know if I had a good analog for it.

"Good. Good. She's just over here." Jeren gestured to a spot on the other side of the clearing and began walking off. I hesitated, but there could literally be no harm done to me by them right now, so I followed.

The bearded man led me to the edge of the clearing where a woman sat with a pair of younger adults – around my age. As we approached, she quirked up a brow and sent the young adults away.

"Jeren," she nodded to the man before studying me just as this Jeren had. She nodded and hesitated before extending her hand towards me.

"Thank you. Some of us came earlier than others. We remember … bits and pieces of being those creatures." She shook her head, the same complicated emotion on some of the others crossing her face, "Your crowbar is quite memorable."

I gave a sheepish smile but accepted her hand.

[Quinn has gifted you 50 Presence]

"A thank you. For saving our lives," The woman who had to have been Quinn spoke.

I shook my head but didn't deny the gift. The System told me I needed 2,000 before it would tell me anything, so any little bit counted.

"We couldn't believe you were actually doing it. She shook her head, "But then people started flooding out of the Instance, talking about a fire. We may have stayed trapped in there for who knows how long if not for you." She hesitated before continuing, "May I ask what World … or Instance you're from?"

I took a seat, a sigh escaping my lips as I shrugged. "I wish I knew, ma'am."

She seemed taken aback by that but nodded. "What do you call your world? There are those among us who may know more."

"Huh? We call it Terra."

She pursed her lips, rolling the name around her mouth before shaking her head, "I've never heard of it myself, but I'll ask around. How did you come across us? Did someone send a bounty?"

I raised my hands up before she finished speaking, exasperation painting my face despite my efforts to hide it. "Hold on. Before you start asking questions, my answer to all of them is going to boil down to 'I don't know'. This is my first real Instance from after the tutorial".

The woman paused before sitting down. Jeren's eyes grew wide as he stood. I could hear the smile and maybe even a little greed in his voice. "You are truthful? You did all that without much experience? What about the rest of your party you're a scout for?"

I looked away and cleared my throat, "About that. I lied. I didn't know if I could trust you and thought maybe that could be a deterrent."

If anything, that relaxed Quinn and Jeren considerably as it felt like an invisible pressure I wasn't aware of suddenly lightened. "That is good. A group of unknown Visitors can often be ... dangerous." She seemed to understand the hidden implication before course-correcting herself: "Instance-oriented groups at least. We're a caravan company, we mostly just trade with other worlds."

Jeren rejoined the conversation, "You ventured this far alone? That's very dangerous, is there no one else you could explore with if you were going to? Did you also get moved in the shuffle?"

I let out a short bark of laughter, "If I tell anyone about this, they'll think I'm crazy. No one knows about The Endless Dream where I'm from. Or at least it's not public knowledge."

Jeren looked thoughtful between Quinn and me and offered a theory, "Your world could have only recently been added to the Dream. It happens from time to time. Rare, but it happens. Tell us. How many Boons and Restrictions do you have?"

"1 Boon 1 Restriction," I replied easily. Worst case, I had six hours to get the fuck out of here, so I didn't mind telling them.

Jeren whistled, "Damn. That's unfortunate."

Despite myself, I couldn't help but let out a chuckle. "Yeah I've started to notice. Instance Holders seem to be fucking vicious with the Restrictions they give."

"Oh yes," Jeren nodded, stroking his beard like an old sage. "The first thing we learn in the academy is to always take a mediocre Restriction over a good Boon." He changed his voice slightly, as if quoting someone, probably because that's what he was doing, "a Boon changes how you play. A Restriction dictates it."

Quinn shook her head. "There's nuance to that, but it's still good advice." She paused, "you must have a lot of questions and be very tired. I can't answer them all now, but if you write them down, I can get to them when I can. I have to organize my people to get ready to descend layers."

I nodded, even though that gave me another three questions, but I was drained. All of my injuries were healed. I was good as new. There was no real excuse to stop moving. I was just tired. Tired wasn't an excuse.

I had another eight hours before I was forced into the other Instance.

As time passed, more and more people began staring at me. It started with glances and eventually settled between some fear and awe. Eventually, a middle-aged woman waved me over; a circle of people around her age had formed, and they all seemed to be eating something.

"Here. We made some Chili. Thought you might want some." She handed me a bowl and turned back to her group. I didn't thank her, and she didn't ask me to. That was the start of smaller gifts. A piece of food, a swig of booze. Then the younger boy, not more than thirteen, asked me to recount what happened in the Instance, so I lied my ass off.

The Instance was a truly horrific experience, and I'm glad I set it on fire, but there was no point in giving a child all the gory details. I could see it in his eyes. He didn't fully understand the weight of what happened. That he had almost been eternally trapped as a monster in some Instance in the middle of presumably nowhere, with no help to come. Maybe he did, though, and wanted me to tell him that it was all alright.

It didn't matter to me. I wasn't going to traumatize a kid on purpose. So I told the story. Embellishing where I could. I talked about meeting Nate in the field. How I almost took down one of the plant monsters, but was dissuaded last second. One of the men laughed and called out, clearly half drunk, "Hey that was me! I swear my eyes still hurt!" which got a hearty round of laughs. The more I spoke, the more people gathered around to listen.

I talked about entering the Greenhouse and feeling something was wrong, about how I went out to the fields and discovered the secret of the Chimeras. I was vague on the details, not wanting to let them know about Peeler's abilities. How I used Nate's Restriction to freeze him, and how the amulet he used to control the Chimeras couldn't do anything because I tied it down to a stake. Of the monster in the house and how I baited the man into protecting the phone so I could burn down the barn with all of them inside.

As I finished my tale, a cheer went out. I didn't fully understand how they were so okay hearing all of that, and I didn't know how I was okay telling it. I suppose, removed from the details, it sounded heroic, almost like an adventure. Even the joke that the one man made about his eyes. Maybe this was the way to cope with the horrors of the Dream. To just talk about it. Maybe. I didn't know. It didn't make me feel any better.

If I had to bet, I'd still have nightmares about that place. I paused. Now that I think about it, I didn't actually know if I could still dream. Every time I fell asleep in the Waking World, I ended up here, and every time I slept here, I ended up in the Waking World. Maybe the only nightmares I'd have to deal with would be the waking ones.

Afterwards, the crowd dispersed somewhat, but there seemed to be a lot more levity in the air. It was still solemn, but nowhere near as dreary as it once was.

Eventually, Jeren and Quinn found me again. It was Quinn who spoke, "We'll be here a while longer, another few days. Now that we know what to expect, we'll wait for this Instance to recover and try again. That should give us enough Presence to descend. We can't keep offering contracts, but Jeren and I discussed, and you're welcome to stay."

I actually smiled at that. I really needed the friendly faces. "I wish I could, but I really only have..." I checked my timer. "About seven hours before I'm thrown into another Instance."

Quinn looked shocked; Jeren frowned, "What. How? You make it sound like there is a lack of choice is why I ask."

I grimaced, "Yeah. An Instance stole me from my world, but the System didn't like that very much. It expelled me and offered an alternative Instance, but no matter what I'll be dragged into it in six hours."

"Ah. Quite cruel for someone so young to this world," his voice was layered in something somber, and it took a second for him to compose himself. "What does it look like? You said you only have 1 Boon and Restriction each, what is required of the Instance."

I pulled the static at the edge of my vision up and looked at my options.

[Instance: Garden of Stone]

[Win Condition: Leave the mourning circle]

[Base Reward: +300 Presence]

[Lose Condition: Do not complete the Win Condition]

[Base Punishment: Lose one person]

[Requirements: 2 Boons, 5 Restrictions]

[Alternative Instance: Haunted Graves]

[Win Condition: Have at least 4/10 individuals survive the night][Base Reward: +500 Presence, 2 boons, car keys]

[Lose Condition: More than six individuals failed to survive the night.][Base Punishment: Lose one person]

[Requirements: 2 Boons, 3 Restrictions]

Jeren practically had to pick his jaw up from the floor as I shared the details with him. "I am sorry, Dorian. It seems like an Instance Holder decided to be quite cruel to you. Five Restrictions and two Boons is a steep Instance. I am not surprised the System stepped into right it. Although" he gave me a sad smile, "it seems like they've only made it more fair, not decreased the difficulty."

I frowned. "What makes you say that?"

"The rewards," he clarified. "They're too high for just any random Instance. A massive amount of Presence. Two boons and an item. I'd say you're in for a rough one. Although I have to admit I'm not sure what the punishment means."

I shrugged, "Yeah, me neither." The man paused before manifesting two what looked like motes of light in front of him. "You did right by us. You could've left us in there, but you sacrificed the win to help us. Even I can recognize that. Only good that I do right by you. Pick one. It's all I can spare".

I looked at the tickets, the static rearranging itself to form the words.

[Boon]

[Wayfarer: Charge (Continuity x 30) Recharge rate (Identity x 0.1 per hour) Space in a twenty-foot radius becomes a safe zone, consuming one charge per second. No enemies may enter this zone. If an enemy is already in this zone when activated, it will be shunted to the edge of the zone in a straight line.]

[Restriction]

[No Free Kill: Whenever an enemy is defeated, its strength remains in its corpse. When another enemy walks by a defeated foe, it inherits a portion of its strength.]
