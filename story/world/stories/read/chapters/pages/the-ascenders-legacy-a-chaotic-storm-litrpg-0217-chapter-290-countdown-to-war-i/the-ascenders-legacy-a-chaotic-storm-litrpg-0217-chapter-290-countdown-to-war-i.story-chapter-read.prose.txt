Duke Solaris Valerion glared at the portrait of Nine—no, Cyrus—that he’d only recently hung on the wall. His son glared back—furious, frozen, disappointingly alive in oil and canvas but maddeningly absent in reality.

It had been a full week since his kidnap—a full week with only minimal progress from the alliance. The last news they’d gotten from Artemis was news on the discovery of the inverted ziggurat.

They hadn’t heard anything since then, and Duke Solaris was growing impatient.

The whole thing was incredibly frustrating, but more than that, more than anything, it was a disgrace. A stain on his reputation and position. His only legitimate son and heir was left to suffer in the hands of cultists like a mere commoner.

But Solaris was aware that there was nothing he could do but wait and hope. He had done his part already, turning the entire sector on its head for the first three days after his kidnap before the discovery of the anchors had put a stop to his violent search.

Sighing heavily, he glanced back at Nine’s—no, Cyrus’s image, the scowl on his face deepening as he regarded the features that looked too much like him, yet nothing like him at the same time.

Cyrus was his ninth child. He should have been the last. But the moment Solaris had laid eyes on the mewling, bloody newborn, he had just known it would be another disappointment.

In all honesty, the boy wasn’t completely useless. In fact, by all standards, he was a genius—a source of immense pride for his mother, the duchess.

But Solaris just couldn’t take pride in the boy’s victories. And it was all because of one thing.

The boy had awakened the wrong affinity—just as he’d suspected the moment he’d held the mewling newborn in his hands. Even then, he’d sensed the wrongness in the boy’s spirit. The disappointment is yet to come. And it had tasted like bile in his throat—like the mocking laughter of fate as it twisted his desires and dreams for its own amusement.

Of all the children he’d fathered, only one had inherited his bloodline and affinity—Nikolaus, but the mere thought of that rogue gave him an instant headache, and he sighed.

“Virgil, come in here.”

The door opened, and Virgil stepped in, his butler’s uniform a perfect blend of fabric and armor.

“You called for me, my lord.”

“Yes,” Duke Solaris lowered himself to his table. “Any news from the alliance yet?”

“None, my lord. Nothing since the last hour you asked me this very same question.”

Duke Solaris closed his eyes in frustration, his lips twisting into a grimace. “It’s been hours since they found the ziggurat. Why haven’t we gotten any update yet?”

Virgil hesitated. “Perhaps it was another dead end, my lord. If they had found Master Cyrus, Artemis would have con—”

His words were cut off by a loud knock and then a voice. “My lord, I have a message from the Conquestia.”

The door jerked open before the messenger could even finish, and in a blink the letter was yanked from his hands. The last thing the messenger saw before the door slammed in his face was the deep scowl on the duke’s face as he read the contents of the letter.

“Good news, my lord?”

“There is to be a war,” Duke Solaris replied instead, his expression easing into a slight frown as he read through the missive. “A deciding war to bring an immediate end to the cultists.”

Virgil’s expression lightened. “So, they’ve found the cultists then.”

“Yes,” Duke Solaris responded, his expression darkening. “But they haven’t found my son.”

His expression deepened further, and with a growl of frustration, he destroyed the message with a small flare of solar energy and said to Virgil,

“Notify my guards, Virgil. I’m going to get my son myself. I cannot stand idle any longer, hoping my priorities align with that of the alliance.”

Virgil bowed and immediately stepped out of the study to carry out the duke’s order. When he shut the door, Duke Solaris glared back at the image on the wall.

“You may be a disappointment, but you are still my heir. And I will not have this ridicule persist any longer. You better be alive when I get there.”

Cyrus’s furious frown was his only response, and Solaris scowled. “The bastard boy never knew how to carry out a decent conversation.”

While Lutia gathered its armies, Aldric sent word home concerning the current situation of things—and though it wasn’t the exact news Unrid or Synové wanted to hear, it was still a step—multiple steps even—in the right direction.

“Soon,” Unrid whispered to Synové as he dismissed the message, hugging her tightly to himself. “They’ll be home very soon, my love. We just have to be a bit more patient.”

Synové nodded, desperately hopeful. She had bawled her eyes out over the past few days and had finally run out of tears. But her fear and worry had only grown by the day—her mind cooking up scenarios of torture and pain.

She tried not to imagine it, but her mind seemed to have a will of its own, plaguing her waking and sleeping hours with imaginations and nightmares.

“We just have to wait a little longer,” she whispered, more to herself than anyone else. “The alliance will find them alive and well.”

“Yes, they will,” Unrid responded and stretched a hand to Yue, beckoning her to join the hug.

Yue fell into their arms with a sob, savoring the warmth of family and comfort that enveloped her. Without Aldric here to help them manage their emotions, they’d all been forced to carry its full weight alone.

Unrid and Synové had taken to tending and expanding the back garden, planting awakened flowers, and trying to create new ones by grafting. They had made little progress so far, but the task kept them busy enough that it served as a satisfactory distraction.

Unrid would have loved to be more productive with his time like Yue was, but he couldn’t in good conscience leave Synové to deal with things on her own. Despite the age-reversing effect they’d both gained from Aodhán’s freak vitality experiment, Synové seemed to have aged several years in the last few days alone.

And between his wife and his advancement, Unrid had chosen his wife.

Yue, on the other hand, had nearly drowned in the despair and longing that ate within her. She loved her new family, but she had joined this family to be closer to Aodhán. To be his family. Without him here, it almost felt like she’d lost a part of herself—a part that made it hard to breathe, much less live.

But Yue had pushed through it, forcing herself to deal with the emotions through intense cultivation and meditation lest they engulf her. Her dogged determination had yielded great fruits too.

In the last five days since she began, she had gained two inspirations about her affinity, evolved her understanding once, and had gained a total of two advancement tiers. Each inspiration had granted her a new skill, one offensive, the other utility, and though she was yet to advance beyond a grasp of the origin plane, she was proud of how far she had come.

But more importantly, she was sure Aodhán would be proud. And that was all that mattered.

By the time he returned, she wanted to make sure she had exceeded his expectations.

Within a hollowed fragment of infinity itself—a pocket of raw, bleeding endlessness carved from the spaces between realities and chained into existence by runic arrays that screamed with the strain of containing the uncontainable—the patriarch of the Rune family, Azratheon Rune, sat in a meditative pose, cultivating.

The chamber should not have existed. The very concept of walls was a polite fiction here, where three-dimensional space folded and collapsed under the weight of infinite possibility.

A dangerous chill permeated the air, so cold that the very notion of heat didn’t exist. And the air—if it could be called air—shuddered with each breath the patriarch took. Reality warped and twisted around him at the tiniest twitch of a muscle, undulating around him like water circling a drain, except the drain led nowhere, or perhaps everywhere at the same time.

But above it all was a weight no one else could feel or sense. It pressed down on Azratheon like a mountain, exuding a pressure so oppressive that Azratheon could almost hear his old bones creak.

The weight had first appeared the moment he ascended into the calamity class, subtle at first. But with every tier he gained, the weight grew heavier—a silent acknowledgement of his approach to ascension.

That weight was the system.

And now, at the 197th tier, Azratheon could hardly move a muscle without feeling the crushing weight on his existence.

It was time for him to ascend. Any advancement he made now would only double the weight of the system, rendering him completely immobile.

But Azratheon wasn’t ready to ascend yet. Not without knowing a few things, and for that, he needed his eighth grandson—the only paralleler to have awakened in the last few centuries.

Unfortunately, however, Azratheon couldn’t even imagine how the boy had been kidnapped by cultists—a shame really that a spawn of his family be soiled by cultists.

Fortunately, Xylixis had been able to keep the situation under wraps and out of the public eye to avoid a kingdom-wide panic. But until Sebastian returned, Azratheon was determined not to ascend.

He had endured the crushing weight for decades now. He would endure it for however much longer it took his grandson to be found.

A knock echoed in that moment, coming from everywhere at once.

Azratheon blinked an eye open, lilac runes gleaming brightly in place of irises. “Come in.”

Reality rippled, and an ethereal phantom of his son came in, grimacing at the harshness and density of the essence within the chamber. Even while projecting an image, he could still feel the strain on his mythic mind.

“Father,” he whispered, his voice traveling through the endlessness of space even though his father seemed to be seated only a few feet away. “You are very close.”

“I am.” Azratheon replied, not bothering to explain any further. “How goes the search for my grandson, Xylixis?”

“Damien says they’ve made great progress. Apparently, they’ve found the cultist’s hideout with a way.”

Azratheon’s expression shifted—surprise, then amusement. “A Way, you say? That’s where they’ve been hiding?” A deep, resonant laugh filled the chamber. “Clever. Very clever.”

Xylixis frowned. “Forgive me, Father, but I fail to see the humor.”

“Of course you do,” Azratheon said, smiling faintly. “You are still young. But to anchor themselves within a Way—ah, the irony. Perhaps I could conduct my ascension within it as well…” His eyes gleamed. “Though doing so might rip the entire thing apart.”

“Whatever you choose, Father,” Xylixis said warily. “The alliance is already preparing an army to enter the Way.”

Azratheon smiled. “Then you must join them, Xylixis. Retrieve my grandson quickly and bring him to me. There are certain parallels I need him to look into for me. Variations. Possibilities. Every path my ascension could take—I need to see them all before I choose. I cannot afford to mess up my ascension. Not after how far I've come."

While the continent of Lutia gathered their armies in preparation for battle, the situation within the Order plunged even deeper into disarray.

For centuries, they had believed themselves untouchable—secure within the Way. There, they had planned to bide their time, to slowly rebuild their strength and power until the day came to strike at the world once more.

So entrenched was their arrogance that despite the knowledge that the alliance had been, however, that confidence shattered the moment they realized that not only had the alliance found a path into the Order, but an unimaginably resilient monster was also carving a trail of blood and death through their ranks.

Shock was too simple a word to describe what they felt. For a heartbeat, absolute silence reigned.

Until Jethro finally barked, “Don’t just stand there. Find the Skalk!”

Elders raced out of the amphitheater at once, essence erupting as they teleported away, senses spreading through the Order to locate the creature.

While chaos surged outward, Jethro paced, panting as panic coiled within him. He wasn’t strong enough for this yet. He was only at the fiftieth tier.

He had known the war would come. It was his entire destiny after all. But he hadn’t expected it to come this soon. This early.

He looked up, feeling the gazes of hundreds on him like a physical weight. They were all waiting for him to speak. To give directions. As the Fated.

The weight of responsibility slammed down on him like a mountain, and Jethro suddenly lost his composure. He had been chasing after physical power—aiming to stand toe-to-toe with his patron—but now he realized he was lacking in more ways than just power.

He lacked the battle-hardened mind of his patron, his military acumen, and his strategic mind, but more importantly, he lacked the gift of speech that had helped Sárán move hearts and commandeer armies with his words alone.

He had none of that.

No fire. No faith. Only a hollow crown for a trembling boy and a pedestal given to him by—

“The Watchers…” he breathed. His gaze widened, and his shoulders straightened. His voice grew firm, and he hastily commanded. “We must convene with the watchers.” To himself, he added. “They will know what to do.”

He glanced at the first elder and ordered. “Gather your people and meet me there.”

The elder bowed, and without hesitation, Jethro teleported out of the amphitheater, appearing just outside the entrance of the watchroom. He barged in without knocking, mouth opening to speak.

Before he could, however, he was met with a bizarre sight.

The Watchers were standing.

For as long as Jethro had known them, they had never once risen from their thrones—not even to address the High Elders. Their bodies had long since withered, their bones fusing with the black stone of their seats until it was impossible to tell where flesh ended and throne began. But now...

Now they stood—dust falling from their limbs like ancient sand as their frail forms straightened, the faint sound of cracking echoing through the chamber.

Jethro froze, his voice caught in his throat, as the watchers turned to glance at him, urgency evident on their faces.

“You’re late, Jethro.” The Fate watcher spat, her bones rattling from the force of her utterance. “Where are the elders?”

“I—” Jethro began, but his words were cut off as a knock sounded on the door. The karmic watcher flicked a finger, and the door opened, revealing the high elders—all seven of them.

The elders rushed in, eyes wide in shock, before collapsing to the ground in reverence.

“Watchers,” Elder Connor’s head touched the cold floor. “We are under attack…

“We are aware.” The Karmic watcher spoke, golden eyes bright as he turned his skeletal frame to glare at the man. “And though we did not expect this, we are not unprepared either. Gather the best soldiers and equip them with as much awakened armor as we can find. Do that immediately.”

“Yes.” Elder Connor raced out of the hall immediately, pulling another elder alongside himself to carry out the watcher’s order immediately.

“You,” the time watcher pointed to Elder Voss. “Get all the weapons and artifacts we have in the storage out, and begin…

For the next few minutes, the watchers dished out orders like robots. They never complained, but it was obvious they had little strength.

Jethro watched them silently, learning even as he watched, until finally, the last elder left the room to carry out the watcher’s orders.

And then their gazes fell on him.

Jethro gulped. “What… what do I do in the meantime?”

“Stay in the amphitheater, and harvest as much power as you can.” The Fate watcher responded, her eyes hard. “We may not have expected this battle, but we cannot afford to lose it either.”

The time watcher nodded. “We’ll stall the alliance for as long as we can. We may not be as strong as we once were, but we’re still stronger than mere Mythics.”

“But you won’t last long against them.” Jethro protested. “You do not have the stamina.”

“Worry about yourself, boy.” The karmic watcher cut in sharply. “And the destiny that you bear. You bear the legacy you do for a reason. Do not let it go to waste.”

Jethro swallowed again and then nodded, determination slowly rising within him.

He nodded again, this time with much more confidence, and bowed. “I will make you proud.”

The time watcher smiled at him. “See that you do. Now return to the Amphitheater. All of this will be over very soon.”

Jethro smiled then, feeling an immense weight slide off him instantly. The Watchers would handle everything. He just needed to do his part.

The image of a certain transmigrant flashed within his mind in that moment, and Jethro’s jaw clenched. It was high time they accelerated things. The second level had stretched too long already.

It was time he forced Aodhán Brystion into submission.
