Zev reached for the antechamber door.

It opened and a woman walked in.

“Bakka, is that you in here?” she said. A look of shock and horror twisted her face as she saw the three men standing in Liora’s room.

A shrill scream burst out of her as Zev sprang into action, slamming the door. Yaret ran forward, grabbing the woman.

Elion lunged. “We’re here to help!” he hissed. “We don’t want to hurt you!”

But her shriek grew in strength, cut off only for a moment as she drew a deep breath and prepared to let out another cry of bloody murder.

Elion activated his ability

> << Save a Friend >>

The power flowed out of him, enveloping the woman in a protective cocoon, freezing her in place, scream still caught in her throat.

Zev dismissed his sword. “Eh, nice move,” he said. “Good use of that ability. Should last long enough.”

They left the woman lying on the bed, and exited through the anti-chamber.

“Should we find some black robes or something?” Elion asked as they slipped through the halls.

“That wouldn’t work,” Zev said. “This isn’t the movies. They’d be able to tell we aren’t warlocks.”

“It might confuse them,” Yaret said. “I think it’s a good idea.”

“There aren’t going to be any warlocks in the halls,” Zev said, as the ground rumbled beneath them.

“Dorian will have them at the entrances and protecting the throne room.”

"So what is the plan, then?” Elion asked.

“Kill the warlocks. Kill Dorian. Get your sister and hope Dorian hasn’t done irreparable damage to her.”

“Just charge in?” Elion asked.

“Just charge in.”

They reached the guard hall outside the throne room. A dozen warlocks stood around in their dark robes, looking bored.

Zev activated his armor as he sprinted down the hall. Elion did the same, running alongside his uncle.

> << Manifest Armaments >>

They’d been rushing through Erod for most of the day; Elion’s weary muscles were thrilled to receive the boost of the ascended state.

The rush was much stronger than he was used to. Leveling up at the altar had granted him significantly more strength, speed, and energy. He guessed he’d also be able to maintain his ascended state much longer than before.

The warlocks scrambled for their staves, but Elion and Zev were in amongst them before they could coordinate a defense.

Zev split warlock wants in half with his sword, triggering explosions as Zelian energy burst forth. Elion dodged in close, striking with his knife, cutting through flesh and armor like butter.

A laser rifle blasted, the wet splatter-thump sound cutting through the air. Teal streaks of light burned through warlocks with surgical precision.

Bodies hit the floor. One panicked warlock tried to run to the throne room, but Zev threw his sword and severed her spine before she reached the doors.

Within 60 seconds every warlock lay dead on the ground.

“Wow,” Elion said, smiling as he flexed his forearms.

“We out level these warlocks, by two or three levels,” Zev said. “They’re highly skilled; they’ve trained for decades, but you can’t get to level twelve without help from ascendency stones.”

Zev pulled the doors of the throne room open and barged in.

The throne room was a huge space; large enough that you could fit Elion’s earth home inside of it. The ceiling sparkled and shone, gemstones embedded in black, like a million stars in the night sky.

Across the hall, the golden throne stood on its dais, dominating the room. Liora sat atop it, screaming.

Her scream dragged out, echoing through the chamber, reverberating across the halls and cutting Elion to the core.

“Dorian,” Zev growled. “Dorian!”

The man stood beside the throne, holding Liora down as she writhed in the seat and screamed. “Come on!” Dorian shouted.

The room’s acoustics carried the sound, as though Dorian stood beside them.

“Come on, you’re nearly there,” Dorian said. “We just need to find Zev!”

“DORIAN!” Zev cried out, racing across the room. Dorian’s head snapped up, and he spun to face his brother.

Zev jumped into the air, Aurelian wings glowing behind him as he soared forward. Dorian sprang to match the attack, Zelian fire burning around him. They collided in the middle of the room.

Liora screamed again, and Elion ran to her side.

Gold and purple flame burned as Dorian and Zev clashed, sending devastating pulses of power into the ceiling and walls.

A wave of Tephalian suppression swept through the room, momentarily disabling all Praxian powers.

Zev and Dorian stumbled apart from one another. Dorian held one arm gingerly, and blood trickled from a cut on Zev’s forehead.

Elion gasped as his added strength disappeared. Liora’s eyes rolled back into her head, and her body began shaking. Elion tried to pull her away from the throne, but some unseen force held her in place.

“Elion leave her!” Zev yelled. “She’s holding back the Tephalians!”

“She’s dying!” Elion screamed.

“The Tephalians are a lie,” Dorian shouted, his voice filled with glee. “I’ve proven it!”

The man cackled and drew a knife from his belt, preparing to attack Zev again.

The wave of suppression faded, and Praxian power kicked back in. Zev and Dorian resumed their fight. Yaret sheltered behind a pillar, tracking the two ascended through his rifle but unable to find a shot.

Elion knelt beside Liora. He grabbed her hand. “Come on,” he said. “Come on.”

She shuddered, then gasped, her eyes popping open. “They’re coming,” she gasped. “They’re coming.”

Her eyes rolled, staring sightlessly around the room.

Maybe he could strengthen her?

He activated his new skill.

> << Empower >>

His hand glowed with light, as he tried to pass his strength and power into Liora. She screamed, her eyes glowing gold.

She was going to burn out like a fuse if he tried to force any more power into her.

“Screw it,” Elion hissed. “I’m not coming this far to watch you die.” He looped his arms under hers, and pulled, heaving with all his might.

Something resisted him, but as he pulled, it gave way, bit by bit.

“No, Elion!” Zev shouted from across the room. “She needs to keep up the shields or we’re going to be totally overrun by tephalians!”

Elion ignored him, and continued to pull.

The throne shut down. Lights and indicators on the armrests died.

Liora slipped free. Elion fell backward, pulling his sister with him. She lay limply in his arms.

“Crap, crap, crap.” Elion tried to remember how to search for a pulse. Stupid. Pointless. He needed his ability.

> << Save a Friend >>

The spell swirled limply around Liora. Nothing seemed to change though.

Zev dashed past, Dorian in pursuit.

“Take the throne, Elion,” Zev said. “You’re our last hope.”

Then he sprinted for the exit doors, Dorian in close pursuit.

“No, no, no,” Elion muttered. “She’s not dead. Save a friend doesn’t work on corpses.”

He patted her shoulders, arranging her arms to be more comfortable. She wasn’t dead. She couldn’t be dead. She lay there, still as a corpse, but that was only because of Elion’s preservation spell working on her.

Yaret stepped up beside him. “Is she—”

“No,” Elion said. “She’s not! I don’t think so.”

“Zev’s right,” Yaret said. “Someone needs to get on that throne. If there really is a tephalian invasion, then you need to stop it.”

“What’s so great about a stupid throne anyways?” Elion hissed.

“It’s the access point,” Yaret said. “It’s how we work with Praxis. When a Starholder sits on the throne, you commune with Praxis, and wield the power of the sentinels across the planet. You can stop Dorian, Elion. Strip him of the power. Turn back the oncoming tide of the tephalian invasion. You’re strong enough to do it.”

Elion stared down at his sister. “I’m not the heir,” he said. “She is.”

“Sit on the throne,” Yaret said. “And we’ll find out.”
