Without thinking further, I turned to the hallway, walking… or maybe running in disguise over the marble floor.

I was definitely late today. I could sense that even without checking the clock, since nobody was outside. Normally, most of the students would be roaming outside by the time I go for dinner, not today.

I turned left. The mess hall was near the far end of the corridor, with one of the exits present even further past it.

The lights in this hallway especially were off. It was completely dark, if not for the glaring white light spilling out from the mess hall.

Please stay open, please stay open! I prayed, stepping toward the mess hall. Yes, I wasn’t running now, didn’t want any complaint against myself for breaking someone’s beauty sleep.

Reaching closer to the open door, I noticed the quiet of the mess hall, and I entered, interrupting it.

A gust of cold air from the Icelure blew at my face, it was too cold, uncomfortably so. My eyes darted around. Still open, yes. A few students… two students lingered inside, eating their dinner in silence.

I gave them a quick look; seniors most likely… probably. I mean, I hadn’t seen them in my class, nor did they look like the staff working in the kitchen; they were too young for it.

Turning toward the buffet, a smile spread on my face. Not wanting to look like a weirdo who had never seen food, I suppressed my smile.

Then again, is smiling at the sight of food you were most probably going to miss due to foolishly engaging in a conversation with an idiot… Mabel, really bad? Probably not.

I walked to the buffet line on the right, a line that swelled up on special events and whenever parents… so-called high-ranking nobles visited their kids.

Though even on a normal day there were a lot of options, options I didn’t have, cause I was fucking late.

Five out of eight of these containers were basically empty. Nothing!

My heart and eyes were both dropping in succession, and just when I was about to drop to the floor, I stumbled upon a container with the most important thing.

Roasted meat. Still there. Of course, only the bony pieces were left, but I didn’t care. If a dog can eat this, so can I, I thought.

Seven of those bony pieces piled on my porcelain plate, and then it hit me:

Did I just compare myself to a dog?! Ahhh, I screamed internally.

Cringing at my thoughts, I moved to the other container, one filled with rice, cold and stale, maybe strong enough to replace brick even! I took a spoonful of the rice and moved on to the next container.

It wasn’t a container per se, but a plate where boiled eggs were placed. My right hand stretched, claiming two eggs.

I turned around, somewhat satisfied with my plate. My seniors were currently occupying both of the right sections, so, not wanting to disturb them, I turned to the left one and began walking.

The seating area of our mess hall was divided into five segments. With a large oval brown table at the centre, it had thirty-two seats around it, all reserved for the high-ranking nobles and students’ friends invited by them.

Not sitting at the centre wasn’t an official rule, but instead one reinforced because of fear.

It was a rule that didn’t come up suddenly, but had been continued since the start of the institution. I glanced to my right, at the wall filled with numerous paintings, the first batch being the largest.

One could easily find—all the people of importance at the centre, with the future barons and children of knights at the corners. At that time, commoners weren’t even admitted to Celera.

Taking my seat at the L-shaped table, I began to eat. The first thing on my menu was rice… stale rice. With no gravy, with no meat, not even eggs.

Mostly because cold rice didn’t deserve a partner in crime; it would only sully the taste of my dried, roasted meat, and eating egg with rice was something I didn’t even want to try.

"If only there were some soup, or vegetables, or anything!" I yelled in a whisper. How did I even do that?

I took a bite and then drank a sip of water. Surprisingly enough, water works great with rice. I am lying. I continued eating. By the sixth bite, I had finished half of the rice. I also realised the truth about our world.

The saying, ‘Anything tastes great when you’ve worked hard for it,’ is a scam!

It’s a lie. Those priests from the church are liars. Those old guys who were flaunting their muscles to me after a hard day at work and saying they liked their wives’ cooking only cause they worked hard were liars too! I am pretty sure their wives were skilled cooks.

And that the saying was a lie.

Anyway, after finishing rice, and drinking two glasses of water, I took my first bite of that oh-so-bony roasted chicken. That’s when I realised the truth about the world.

I think food tastes better after a day of hard labour… Ahhhh food, anything except rice!

The roasted meat was absolutely breathtaking. The spice mix clung to its outer layer, seeping into every shred of flesh. Mhmmmmm. It was good. My taste buds rejoiced, even as half the bites ended up being nothing but cartilage, which I wasn’t entirely sure was meant to be swallowed or spat out.

I ate it though, after chewing it properly. It was pliable and crunchy, maybe that’s how dogs feel like gnawing bones?

Anyway, I finished eating the meat in a minute or so, leaving nothing but a few bones on the plate. The next thing I had left was eggs, which I carefully stripped, and then brought closer to my lips.

The first bite was pretty sweet, though not technically sweet since it tasted like egg, but next I had that yellow thing to take care of. Yolk! Something I hated, but according to instructor Mirayel, one can’t become a proper adult if they don’t eat egg yolk.

No idea what that had to do with his expertise as a dark mage, but I still took his advice and finished my food.

With my food done and my energy drained, thanks to the sheer effort of chewing dry rice, I moved out of the mess hall, dropping my plate on the return counter by the door.

The hallway was dark, the same as before. The door to the outside was now locked; maybe the warden had locked it. I turned left and tip-toed my way to my room.

Like Mabel had promised, she went out only after locking my door. I turned the golden knob to the left, opening the door.

Cold air smacked me in my face as I stepped inside my dark room. The Icelure was working at its highest capacity, the ice crystal released cold, gentle air everywhere in my room.

I turned around, locked the door, then I walked towards my table and picked up the key, finally locking it for real this time.

"Ahhh," I moaned, tired, sleepy, and comfortable in the ambience I was in. Slipping out of the shirt and pants I wore to bed earlier, I walked up to my study chair and pulled it out from under my table. It was cold. I placed my pants on the handle and shirt on the seat, stretched my arms over the neck.

Attempting to relax my muscles, I noticed something.

"What’s that?" My eyes squinted as I noticed a paper kept beneath the fountain pen Mabel was fidgeting with.

I walked closer and grabbed the paper. The pen rolled down the table as I brought it closer to my eyes.

"Hmmm… right, I can’t enhance my vision yet," I murmured, relaxing my squinted eyes.

Turning to the chandelier, I raised my arms. There were two ways to light it up. Either mumble Luminous, or something better, release a small stream of mana from your hand and light the bulb up by itself.

Cool, right?

"Luminous," I mumbled, since the cool way didn’t work, this time too. "Hah, this thing is busted!"

I turned my attention back to the thing in my hand. It was a single sheet of paper. With a drawing… an indescribably bad drawing of a, hmm, a hurricane?

I began reading the paper. The heading was written by the black ink of my fountain pen. No, I can’t tell by the looks. I just guessed since it was above it.

[All About Inferno Spell~ but concised] "Mabel Weeble" was its title.

Wait, Mabel wrote this!?
