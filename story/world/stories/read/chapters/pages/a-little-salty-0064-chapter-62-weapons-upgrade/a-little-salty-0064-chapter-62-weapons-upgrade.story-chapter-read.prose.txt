After a couple days of trial and error, we find a way to strap all the water skins together and shove them into Álvaro’s Inventory. Álvaro is annoyed by this, because he usually keeps his Inventory empty to store whatever Quest objective he needs to haul into town.

“What are we going to do on the way back?” he asks. “We’ll only have used half the water at that point. And I’m not volunteering to drag a dead adult lindwyrm all the way back to the city.”

“It’s not like your Role will let you leave it there, will it?” I point out.

He narrows his eyes. “I feel I’m being taken advantage of.”

“Maybe we can divide up what’s left of the water,” Cyros suggests. We’re back in Álvaro’s flat, which has become the unspoken meet-up place, mostly because it’s bigger than my apartment. And has better snacks.

“That’s a lot of water,” I say, unsure.

From the prior experiments, we found we can strap ten water bags into my Apothecary before the System starts rejecting them. I guess that becomes the tipping point where the Inventory stops classifying the items as a part of the Apothecary and officially an entirely separate object. I’m not sure if it’s based on mass or volume—another thing I’ll play around with later. But I suspect if I enlarged my Apothecary, I’d be able to fix all the water skins to it. The only problem is that the bigger my Apothecary gets, the more unwieldy it becomes, and the more space I’ll need to even remove it from my Inventory in the first place. Not ideal if I need to grab something from it while I’m on the run or in a fight.

“We’ll figure something out,” Cyros assures me. “I’ll carry it all if I have to.”

I’m a bit skeptical of that claim considering how much water we need, but there’s no point in arguing. Cyros is set on this.

What does he need that adderstone for? We have plenty of access to healers from the Guild—heck, even I can make a minor healing potion. I bet I could boost it with what little I have left of the augmentation potion. But Cyros has never asked me for it. Why would he? He could afford something way better from Zee’s shop. What can an adderstone do that conventional healing potions can’t?

And who’s he trying to heal?

Anytime I want to ask, my tongue remains glued to the top of my mouth. It feels private, and Cyros certainly isn’t volunteering these answers on his own.

It’s none of my business, anyway. We all have our secrets to keep. And I mean, it’s not like we’re close, right? He’s just my Guild sponsor. He’s got no obligation to spill his guts to me.

So I bite my tongue every time I glance his way to find him frowning with an intensity I haven’t before witnessed. He’s taking this job more seriously than anything else I’ve ever seen him apply himself to. The look is concentrated and hard—for a reason I can’t really explain, it makes me nervous.

Gathering the food for the trip is much easier than the water. It takes up a lot less room, and I’m able to pack it all into my Apothecary. The whole ensemble is definitely a bit bulky and over-stuffed, but it’ll get more manageable as our trip wears on.

“All that’s left are weapons,” Cyros says as we go over the Inventory.

I tilt my head. “Weapons? We’ve got our knives, he’s got his whip. What more do we need?”

“Something that can hurt a lindwyrm,” Cyros says flatly. “Your potions will probably help out, but I’m not sure knives will be enough. Álvaro, are you proficient with anything else?”

“Hey, a whip is pretty useful!” he objects. “I can wrap it around the wyrm’s mouth to keep it from eating us, for one.” He glances between us when neither of us seem impressed. “But yeah, technically I have skills that say I can use a spear and sword. Swords are heavy, though. I need to stay agile for any of my spells to be of use.”

“What about a spear?” Cyros asks.

“Too bulky?” I wonder.

Álvaro grins. “Nah. Whips are just way cooler.”

I can’t roll my eyes any further back into my head, but Cyros actually chuckles.

“Let’s get you a spear, just in case,” he says.

“Alright, alright.” Álvaro holds up his hands on mock defeat. “But I told you, I’m basically out of money.”

“That’s alright,” Cyros says. “We’ll get you one.”

“We will?” I ask. I mean, Cyros and I aren’t exactly flushed with cash either. Well, I’m not, anyway. I have no idea what Cyros spends his money on.

“Yep.” A flicker of Cyros’s typical spark reappears in his eyes. “Ever had the chance to check out the Guild armory?”

I hadn’t had access to the armory back in Fairwood, but I’ve unlocked plenty more rooms of the Guild since then. I never particularly paid the armory any attention since they only have physical weapons, so no potion stuff, and I’m only proficient with knives, anyway. And since I only get a lot of my Rogue Chef bonuses if I’m wielding kitchen knives (well, any cooking instrument, really,) the Guild’s swords and maces all felt a bit pointless.

But hey, maybe Cyros knows something I don’t.

We have to leave Álvaro behind when we head back to the Guild, of course, even though he’s practically dying of curiosity to see the assassin’s hideout (annoyingly, using Echo to learn about the Blackcloaks in the same way I had originally used her against Cyros). The side-cave housing the armory has a sword and shield marking over its entrance. The sigil lights up as we stop before it, and the Blackcloak spells in Cyros’s cloak and my scarf likewise briefly flare as our credentials are approved. Then Cyros steps forward, and I follow him.

I stop almost as soon as I set foot in the chamber. “Woah.”

Cyros looks back at me with a grin. “Right?”

The cavern is enormous. I feel like I’ve stepped inside a castle’s treasury room. Racks of every kind of weapon imaginable are lined up in rows, dozens of varieties of each type. Some appear simple while others are crawling with spell runes or embossed with precious jewels.

How did the Guild even get their hands on all—actually, nevermind. I’m pretty sure I know exactly how they came by everything in this room.

“This is wild,” I say, following Cyros down one of the rows, craning my head every which way in awe. “All these weapons, just in this city?”

“Actually, no,” Cyros admits, reaching out for the nearest morning star. His hand passes right through it, and its image flickers. “Illusions. This represents the entire armory of the Blackcloak Guild across all locations. I don’t know where the weapons are actually kept—which is by design, I’m sure. But once we settle on what we want, we can take the corresponding token to the telepad, and the item will be summoned for us.”

“Just like that?” I ask, still in awe. “No catch?”

“Well, some catches.” Cyros gestures to the plate at the bottom of each weapon. Some of them have names, others simply describe the weapon; some plates describe a weapon that appears to be missing from the racks. "They're locked based on your rank. So you can take everything up through B, and I can take anything up through D.”

“So cool,” I say, stopping at a shelf of sais. I admittedly don’t know how to use one, and I’d probably hurt myself a lot before I gained a skill that would help me wield them half as decently as my knives. But it would be pretty cool to have them for appearances, if nothing else.

“Nothing is to keep,” Cyros adds, crushing my dreams. “This armory is lend only. It’s designed for any specialty equipment you might temporarily need for a job or two. The max time limit is about a month, I think.”

“Aww.” I knew it was too good to be true. Still, a weapons library is pretty sweet.

“Come on,” Cyros says, gesturing me further back. “Let’s grab a spear, first.”

“We can check weapons out for people outside the Guild?” I ask.

Cyros stops, turning around to give me a hard look. “Absolutely not. Doing that could get us in a lot of trouble. Understand?”

Is Cyros breaking the rules? Knowingly breaking the rules? Woah. I didn’t think he was capable of doing something this rash.

Then again, he invited me to join the Guild.

“Understand,” I say.

He stares at me for a moment longer, then nods. “Good. Then let’s head this way.”

I’m not sure what Cyros is looking for in a spear, but in the end he selects something that’s C rank. I couldn’t tell you what type of spear it was or if there was anything special about it. It has some sort of red fluff or tassel near the speartip that I’m sure Álvaro will love. After that I trail Cyros over to the sword section. He browses the racks while I simply take everything in, still not sure what weapon I could use that would be more effective than my cleavers.

I can get throwing daggers, which I’ve become way more proficient with in the last few months, but unless I amp them up with some potions and magic, I doubt they’ll do much against the hide of a lindwyrm.

Ranged weapons are out, I think. I don’t have proficiency with a sword, spear, glaive, or any of the other weapons I come across, and I certainly don’t have time to train myself up on one before we reach the wyrm’s nest. So where does that leave me?

Cyros picks out a simple looking sword, but when I Check it, I note it’s imbued with some spells. Well, now. That changes things.

I’m mentally smacking myself on the forehead for not thinking of doing that to my own blades. I mean, I do add poison and stuff to them, but nothing permanent. I also don’t have any artificing skills—but I know you can commission some more shady spells to be added to weapons within the Guild.

Too late for my own weapons, but I bet there are some magic knives here that might work for me.

“How about you?” Cyros asks when he’s done. He’s grabbed the tokens for the sword and spear but hasn’t turned them in to get the actual weapons yet—I’ll have to check the Rank C spear out for him, anyway.

“What’s the largest blade that still counts as a knife?” I wonder.

Cyros frowns. “I’m not sure. Does it matter what it counts as?” Even so, he starts to lead me over to the smaller weapons.

“It does,” I say. “It’s a System magic thing.”

“Ah, right.” Cyros gives me a curious look. “You still haven’t explained much about all that.”

I chew on my lip, glancing at the rows of throwing knives and arrows so I don’t have to look at Cyros. “Dunno if there’s much to explain.”

He snorts. “If you don’t want to tell me, you can just say so.”

I wince. “It’s not like that! I mean. It’s just. It’s personal, okay?” I frown, as if that can scare the upset flutters in my stomach into submission. I drop my voice. “It’s not like I wanted it. All it’s given me is trouble.”

Cyros is quiet for a minute as he trails after me. I’m halfway down a row when I realize I haven’t even processed anything I’ve been looking at.

“Like Álvaro’s Role?” Cyros finally says, his tone also quiet. “This Quest thing he has to do?”

I breathe out through my nose. “Yeah. Mine is that I have to cook things every day. Or do some other kind of meal prep.”

“What, really?” Cyros asks. He drops his voice again. “I thought you were just cooking every day because… well, you know.”

I raise an eyebrow at him. “Know what?”

He shrugs, his gaze evasive. “Well, those innkeepers in the forest you were staying with. They were teaching you to cook, right? I sort of figured it was… I don’t know. You missed them or something.”

My throat tightens, and I swallow down a knot that’s threatening to form. “Well, you figured wrong. I only cook because I have to. If I don’t, the System drives me crazy. Literally.”

His eyes widen. “It punishes you? Why?”

“How would I know?” I throw my hands in the air, spinning away to check out some dual-wield knives. “It’s just always been like that. Like I said; not like I wanted this.”

“Sorry,” Cyros says. “I didn’t realize. Honestly, it sounds kind of like a curse.”

“Certainly feels like it,” I grumble. “But I don’t think that’s it.” I’ve done some digging on curses, actually, and they’re a loosely grouped field of magic that triggers under pre-determined conditions to afflict all sorts of hazardous effects on the target. None of them are permanent; they’re all dependent on how much mana was used to power the spell. This System thing is something else entirely.

“Is that why—” Cyros stops himself.

I wait for him to reword his thoughts, but he doesn’t follow through. “What?”

He grimaces. “Nothing. It would have been insensitive.”

I give an exasperated sigh. “Just say it! Now I’m going to be wondering.”

Cyros takes a moment to reply, and when he does, he’s as tentative as a rabbit approaching a wolf. “I was wondering if this Role thing—the way it can drive you mad—was why the Captain Enrold job ended the way it did.”

It feels like I’ve been dunked in ice water. Memories of that night flash to the forefront of my mind. I clench my teeth and try to shove them away, blinking back the sting in my eyes. I turn back to the shelves, focusing on a crack on the hilt of a butterfly knife.

“Yeah.”

That’s all I say, and Cyros doesn’t ask me to elaborate.

“Thanks for telling me,” Cyros offers after a time.

I glare at the weapons. “Between Piria, Álvaro, and Shirasil, doesn’t feel like I had much hope of hiding it, anyway.”

“I guess, Cyros says. “But even so. I’m glad you told me. And I still might not understand everything that’s going on with this System, or you Travelers, or the gods—”

“You and me both,” I grumble under my breath.

“—but I want to help however I can,” Cyros finishes.

My throat won’t let me reply. Is this a mistake? Every time I get close to someone, they get hurt. My parents, Talia, Iski and Gugora. Layf, Rena, Terimus. Anyone I tell about my nature just gets tangled up in this mess with the Heavens and thrown into the line of fire. I don’t know how many more deaths on my conscious I can withstand.

I guess it’s too late now. Álvaro let the cat out of the bag. Cyros is involved, whether I have a say in it or not. I suppose—

I pull up short, and Cyros nearly runs into my back.

“What he asks?”

“These,” I say, reaching out for a pair of weapons. “What are these?”

[Check: These so-called “felis claws” are a weapon favored in South Dunmora. Each glove bears three blades, throughout which different spells can be inscribed and activated at the user’s discretion.]

“Really?” Cyros says as I marvel at them. “They’re not as practical as a sword. And you’ll probably stab yourself at some point.”

“I will not!”

The claws are silver, runes flickering across the surface, while the glove portion appears to be simple leather. Useful for a desert climate, but I suspect they’re more than meets the eye.

Another Check confirms this: the gloves are imbued with a spell that summons large ethereal claws, one that’s supposed to make the blades sharp enough to cut stone, and one that lets you channel your magic affinity through the weapon.

“Besides,” I add, “it’s not about practicality. Cool factor wins here.”

He snorts. “You sound like Álvaro.”

“Hey!” I round on him. “Now you’re just being hurtful.”

Cyros grins, and I swipe the token from the stand. As I remove it, the image of the felis claws flickers out. It’s C tier. Technically I could have gotten something fancier. But Echo confirms that these qualify for my knife proficiency, and even if I’ll lose the bonus for them not being kitchen utensils, the spells more than make up for it.

After using the summoning circle to swap our tokens for the real things, we leave the Guild, weapons in hand. I’m pretty excited to try out the claws. I wonder if I could buy some of my own after this.

I’m so absorbed by the weapons that I almost don’t notice the champions until it’s too late.
