Jareth marched into the barracks without another word. He shut the door behind him, leaving us in the growing shadows of evening.

“Why does he call you that?”

The question was ringing through my mind, as well, but it was Royce who voiced it. He stood with one corded arm braced against the fence, frowning at Renner.

I half-expected the man beside me to give a snide response- perhaps triggering another argument between the two- but instead he answered, very calmly, “Because I’ve killed things.”

Royce gave a little growl. “Right. Obviously. But you’re going on about nobility and following orders like they’re… salt, crimes. So what exactly is he hinting at?”

“I’ll paint you a nice picture later, farmboy.” Renner turned, sneering at the larger man. “Maybe you’ll get some good nightmares out of it, and maybe you’ll even have an actual reason to hate me. But not here.”

“Back to the inn?” Teela said with a sigh, glancing between the pair. “I mean, what else are we supposed to-”

“They found something,” I whispered. My gaze drifted northward, to the distant, shimmering cloud of smoke. “That’s what the signal smoke’s for, right?”

Renner nodded. He was eyeing it, too, and rubbing his jaw thoughtfully. “Yeah. Looks like maybe… an hour north? Two? Once we’re past the city gates.”

Royce was shaking his head. “You can’t want to go see! It’ll be nearly sundown when we get there, and dark the whole way back!”

“And we’ll be surrounded by paladins,” Renner added, as if the fact should have been obvious. “Joselia won’t let civilians wander back through the woods alone. She’ll send a guard or come back with us herself.”

I drew my cloak tight against a breath of wintry air. “If they found something, I’d like to see what it is. Maybe it won’t mean anything, not to me, but… well, I can tell him tonight. If he truly does want to stop this other Fae, maybe he can shed some light.” I winced at the entirely backwards thought of Velidan ‘shedding light.’ But what other choice do I have? If there’s clues to be found, ideas of what’s going on and how to stop it, who else can I turn to for help? “Renner, is Joselia… like Jareth? The strength and speed and… all of it?”

“Most feared warriors in the realm. Anyone blessed moves like him. Better, even; older, more experienced.” He shifted away from the wall, closing in on me with a familiar light in his eyes. “And I’ll say it again; giving one of them your weapon is a good idea. Let them be bait. They can handle it. They could probably kill him.”

I swallowed, stepping back, but he followed. I suddenly felt quite crowded and quite lost all at the same time. I’d dismissed the idea so resolutely before, but after seeing Jareth fight…

Could one of them do it? If they had the shard and Velidan came for it, would they be fast enough? Strong enough? Could they actually kill him?

I almost believed it possible. I almost dared to hope. And then a tiny, meek thought followed that one.

Do I want them to?

I hated the question, the flicker of my hesitation, and the logic behind it all. But the truth remained that perhaps right now my monster was the lesser of two evils, even if I didn’t fully understand what the other evil was.

Renner wasn’t much taller than me, but he still managed to loom. Fir shadows played across the planes of his face, now so close to mine that I could make out every tight, fierce line around the corners of his mouth.

I just shook my head. “No. Not yet. Not until we know more.”

Gloved hands curled into fists as he snarled a sigh. Warm breath washed over my face. “Fine. Fine.” He nodded towards the smoke. “Then let’s find out more.”

“Great,” Teela groaned, throwing both hands skywards and tromping across the arena. Every step kicked up a puff of dust. “We finally got to civilization and you two want to immediately charge back out into the dark, spooky forest looking for some underground crypt where there’s probably a bunch of monsters just waiting-”

“You don’t have to come,” I offered in a tiny voice.

She looked at me incredulously. “Oh, no, I’m definitely coming. The last time you two went on an adventure without me you both almost died.

Renner barked a laugh as he went about gathering his pack and cloak. “Uh-huh. And you would’ve been a big help against the hag. Could’ve picked her pockets while she was murdering us.”

She stuck her tongue out at him.

My mouth curled and I paced towards Royce, who stood with both hands clasped behind his neck and a deep frown aimed towards the smoke. “Well,” I elbowed him lightly, peering up, “it’s a good thing there’s four of us, now. What was it you said before? Better odds?”

That earned me a heavy grunt and a look that seemed to say ‘I know what you’re doing and I refuse to be cheered up.’ But after a moment he let out a resigned sigh. “Better odds. Right.”

I bit my lip. “Do you feel up to coming? I… do want to talk to you. Fill you in about last night and earlier and… all of it. We can talk later, of course, but…”

He offered a lopsided grin. It didn’t reach his eyes. “Yeah. Yeah, ‘course I’ll come.”

It didn’t take long to reach the northern city gates. Renner seemed to more-or-less know his way around Sedgewick Vale and he led us at a brisk pace through winding, smooth stone streets. We passed bright cottages with crisp-smelling box gardens, shops with foggy glass windows lit by flickering candles, a bakery that smelled of honey and earth, and a slanted tavern built half from weathered blackstone and half from smooth pine. Booming laughter echoed out along the stones as we hurried past.

Strangers wrapped in flowing shawls and hooded cloaks bustled alongside us, occasionally sparing glances but mostly ignoring our little band in favor of their own business or conversations. The late hour, the chilly air, and- perhaps far more so than the others- the earlier attacks seemed to have thinned the crowd from this morning. Most street corners held a pair of guards standing at alert, watching each passerby with a wary eye.

Four more armored, iron-broached Watch members manned the heavy wooden gates. They took our names, asked our business, and asked no further when Renner mentioned the Thalessians and the signal smoke. It seemed as though the guards had been made aware of the unusual sight and what it meant.

Our path north led along a broad dirt road through dense, short pines and the occasional swaying maple. Dozens of footsteps, hoofprints, and cart-tracks had paved markings along the trail. After about a half-hour the smoke led more to the northwest, so we left the road and forged ahead through a sea of steadily deepening, fragrant firs.

Once enclosed by dark green nettles and the music of chirping crickets, I told Royce about my nap. He listened with a series of increasingly unhappy grumbles.

He stomped ahead as I finished, then practically shoved a low-hanging pine branch aside. It snapped back with a groan and a chorus of hissing nettles, nearly taking off Teela’s hat and my head. “Brin, you can’t actually be considering this! Salt, surrounded by monsters? They’ll tear you apart!”

Pinecones and crinkly brown maple leaves crunched beneath my boots. “He said I’d be safe.”

“The monster. The monster said you’d be safe. The Fae who sat around watching you sleep, threatened to kill everyone at the inn last night, then made you almost die using a rune. That guy said you’d be safe.” He turned, blue eyes bulging. “You hear how that sounds, right?”

Teela pirouetted over a trio of fallen branches. “I think it makes sense. He needs her alive, right?”

“And she’s gonna get kidnapped either way.” Renner’s muddy cloak snapped with every step. He aimed a glare at me over one shoulder. “Unless we’re giving a Thalessian the shard.”

I said nothing.

Royce shoved another branch. “And we’re not telling them anything, apparently?! Brin, what changed?”

My lashes lowered with a wince. “I… well, Renner?”

He stopped midstep, turned, and aimed a dangerous look at the larger man. His explanation was detailed and he did not, thankfully, repeat his little ‘demonstration’ of what my consecration would look like. Though I couldn’t help but think that watching the two men go through those motions would have given Teela and I some very unique entertainment and a much-needed laugh.

Better not suggest it. He might grab me instead, and of course that would be… er, not appropriate in front of… and of course there’s no bed here, just pine needles. He can’t very well toss me onto… well…

That idea brought an entirely new image to mind alongside a rise of startling heat. By the time Renner finished speaking I was thoroughly flushed, staring at him, and had sunk my teeth into my lower lip.

He glanced at me, looked away, looked back, and raised both brows. Then, with all the darkness of his voice shifting into a tone I knew all-too-well, “Ladyship. Something on your mind?”

“No.” I looked away, cheeks burning.

“Uh-huh. Bet it’s something nice.”

I nearly choked.

“None of that can be true.” Royce was shaking his head. His tan cheeks and nose were flushed from the wintry evening air. “I mean, Brin… right? Saints, Joselia and Jareth are good people! They wouldn’t do all that! Especially not to you!”

“They would.” All traces of Renner’s momentary mirth died. Steel took its place. “She dies if they find out. It’s as simple as that.”

I sagged against a fir tree, inhaling the crisp smell. My gaze trailed upwards towards a canopy of dark, rasping fir boughs. The sky peeked through in patches of sapphire and, to the northwest, the shimmer of a pearly white plume.

My breath hitched. “Ashes, after tomorrow it might all… none of will matter. If I actually do learn something valuable, of course I’m going to share it. And… well, and my life certainly isn’t worth more than anyone else’s.”

Three unhappy stares were leveled my way.

“Don’t look at me like that. I get it, no one here wants me to be dragged off and stripped naked and, saints, tortured to death, but… if it saves lives? If it helps the paladins protect people? If they can stop all these attacks- not just here but everywhere? Of course I’m worth all that! It’s… it’s cruel to say otherwise!”

Renner’s jaw tightened. “Uh-huh. And the shard? The monster who’s gonna show up looking for it, tearing heads off if he finds it on someone else? Who wants you alive?”

I pushed off the tree and continued on, brushing branches aside and crushing leaves with every step. “I’ll… I’ll… I don’t know! I’ll hide it somewhere away from people. He can go get it without hurting anyone, since apparently me begging for that doesn’t-” my mouth went dry as I thought of earlier. The dismissive, mocking answer to my pleas. I shook my hair, which snagged a lock in a large pinecone, then began untangling the mess with a fierce scowl. “And if I die it’ll inconvenience him, but that’s it. He won’t do anything about it. Ashes, he’ll probably think it’s funny if I run off and get myself killed!”

The hair came loose with a hiss and a tug as an image loomed unbidden in the back of my mind. Velidan’s face, framed by a halo of silver starlight as he dropped a mangled lump of gold at my feet. Staring down while I lay bleeding and bound, furiously demanding to know who-

I actually thought he cared that I was hurt. And of course that was exactly what he wanted. Part of the act, part of him trying to… to… ‘sway me with charm,’ like he said.

Saints, he didn’t care. He probably did think it was funny!

I ground my teeth, furiously biting down anything that felt remotely like shame.

And I fell for it. I was actually foolish enough to hope… it doesn’t matter. Not now. And it’s not something I need to feel guilty about.

“So your life doesn’t matter,” Renner snarled, jerking me back to the present.

“I’m not saying that! Saints, I don’t want any of those things to happen! But if… he used the word massacre, Renner! Massacre! If I can help stop-”

“So you’ll write it down. I’ll buy you a salted book. A nice thick one full of blank pages. You can fill it with everything you learn, then take the horse and run while I-”

“And what happens to you?” I lunged into his path and swung around, forcing him to either stop or knock me over.

Fortunately, he stopped.

I folded both arms, searching his face as a few timid crickets chirped around us. “When they find out that you lied, that you helped me run, that-”

Copper eyes held mine, and his voice was even. “Nothing worse than any of the other times I’ve stepped out of line. I’ll be locked up for a bit and get some nice one-on-one time with Vhaik. Maybe he’ll even get creative for once.” He scoffed as I stepped back, heart pounding at the declaration. “Nothing I can’t-”

“I’m sorry?!” Teela exploded from behind him. Her jaw hung low. “They’re gonna torture you?! Us?! For helping?! Why didn’t you say that sooner?!”

Royce was shaking his head. “That’s not… not real! The paladins aren’t going to hurt any of us! They’re not going to torture people!!”

“I’ve tortured people.” His tone was a flat, dead thing that sent ice prickling down my neck. “Under orders when I was templar. Orders from paladins. It doesn’t happen often and no one’s proud of it, but when there’s lives at stake? Innocents being held hostage? Not everyone has the luxury of playing nice.”

“That’s you!” Royce fumed, storming towards us. The chirps and rustles of forest life fell silent as he looked at me incredulously. “Ashes, Brin, are you hearing this?! He makes fun of them for playing at nobility, for helping people, but he’s just blaming them for the awful things he’s done!”

Renner spat at the other man’s feet. “I’ve done worse than that, and I’m not blaming anyone for it. I’m just telling you what the world’s like. I took a turn with the prisoners before Vhaik showed up, farmboy, and guess what? I wasn’t the only one in there, and I wasn’t the only one drawing blood.” He bared his teeth, looking almost feral in the growing shadows. “I was just the only one without a white cloak on.”

The image of Renner calmly, so calmly, slamming Gil’s head against a counter flickered behind my eyes. Then the way he’d flexed one hand earlier, when he’d joined Teela and I at the inn. I turned my back on the pair, feeling suddenly very lost and very alone in the darkening forest.

Teela’s voice pierced the creeping quiet. “Go back to earlier. What’s going to happen to me and Royce if Brin admits everything?”

“Nothing terrible. You’ll be questioned, but that’s it. I just have…” he paused, then gave a mirthless laugh, “enough history to make things ugly. Vhaik’ll get angry, I’ll be my charming self, he’ll get angrier, eventually lose his cool and draw blood, and then Joselia and all the other higher ups will tear him a new one and that’ll be the end of it. Same old story.”

“Renner, that’s…” my throat thickened. I couldn’t seem to turn and face him as the sea of rustling nettles grew blurry. “That’s an awful story. Saints, why-”

“Nah, don’t start feeling sorry for me. I always had it coming.”

“What’d you do?” Royce took a step back as I turned. Evening air ruffled his hair and cloak as he looked the smaller man up and down. “What’s the history?”

Renner eyed him evenly. “Told you. I killed things.”

My friend’s throat bobbed. His face paled as my own blood turned cold with a swell of dread. “What things?”

“I think,” he said slowly, facing Royce and Teela but eyeing me, “a mad dog should be put down.”

“Not dragged across the realms and…” I echoed his words from the previous night, covering my mouth with one hand. An acrid taste rose up the back of my throat.

“What? I don’t get it.” Round, red eyes glinted as they cut between the two of us. “You killed dogs?”

He didn’t answer for a long while. Just watched me, lips pressed together and face unreadable. Then, every word heavy and low, “Five years ago, I asked my superiors why we were tormenting pacted. Said it was cruel, inhumane, made us just as bad as them. I was told to put it out of my mind and follow orders. So I did. I went on a few more missions, dragged a few more people back to the temple, and watched a few more consecrations. Got sick of it pretty quick, and decided I was done following orders.”

Ice snaked through my stomach. Cold flooded my lungs. I met his eyes for a moment longer, then couldn’t bear to look anymore and turned away. I drew my cloak tight, but the soft fabric did nothing to chase away the chill.

“So you…” Royce choked the words out. “What, you started murdering people? Killing prisoners? You-”

“It’s not like I was slitting people’s throats in their sleep,” he growled. “Thalessians are supposed to take pacted alive whenever they can. We’re trained to disarm and incapacitate, and to never go for a kill unless someone else’s life is at stake. I started… missing, I guess you could say.” He let out a harsh, bitter laugh.

“And that’s why Jareth calls you killer,” Teela heaved a sigh. She sounded less horrified and more… tired. Resigned.

“He knows?!” Royce roared, making me jump. His voice resounded off the trees and once again, the forest around us grew deathly quiet. “Why hasn’t he… why aren’t you locked up?!”

“Because there wasn’t any proof. Combat’s hectic, especially when there’s dark magic flying around. Most times, no one even saw who got the kill.” Frigid silence settled between the four of us until he added with a heavy breath, “I got questioned plenty of times. There was usually a body or two on any mission I went on, and I’d run my mouth about taking prisoners enough that a few people guessed what was going on. Jareth knows. Suspects, at least. He also knows why, and he walks a nice line between thinking I’m right and thinking I’m blasphemous, even though he’s never seen… well. Doesn’t matter.” He sniffed.

I couldn’t bring myself to turn around, but I felt quite certain that the look on his face would very much say that it did matter.

“You’re sick. Ashes, you should be the one dragged off to Pinnacle.” Every word was hoarse, practically vibrating with rage. I’d never heard Royce sound so furious before.

“There you go, farmboy. See?” Renner’s voice was dark with menace. Pushing, prodding, almost as if he wanted this to escalate. “Bet it feels good to have an actual reason to hate me. Besides jealousy.”

Choking quiet closed behind me. I stood frozen, muscles locked into place and half-expecting the sounds of hissing steel or a fist cracking against a jaw. Wondering with dazed, spinning panic how Teela and I could possibly pull the two of them apart.

But there were no sounds of violence. Just a pregnant pause and then the crash of heavy boots on dead leaves alongside the coo of an owl.

“Brin.” Heavy warmth closed over my shoulder, spinning me around to look up at an ashen face and wild blue eyes. “Brin. He’s a murderer. You’re hearing this, right? He’s a murderer. He’s the bad guy, not the paladins! We need to ask them for help, we need to-”

“You do that, she dies.”

“Shut up! Don’t listen to him!” His other hand closed over my elbow. “Brin? Are you listening?”

I couldn’t seem to look at Royce. Not at Renner, either. My gaze was fixed on a nearby fir.

A very familiar figure sat perched atop one branch.
