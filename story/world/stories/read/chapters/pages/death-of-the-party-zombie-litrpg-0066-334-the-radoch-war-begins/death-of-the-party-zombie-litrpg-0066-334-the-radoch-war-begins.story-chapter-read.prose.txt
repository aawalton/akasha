The following days seemed to drag minute by minute, yet were gone in an instant. While most of the world trained, farmed, and prepared, the Outsiders were no different. Other than having to sit for the occasional meeting, they spent their time on the moon in duels, scouring Sanctuary for helpful items, or planning out how exactly they were going to kill so many Radochs.

While they waited for Archie to return, the rest of the group sat around the round table in the meeting room. Sally had her face buried in her arms as she tried to nap. Ren was reading through a book while Max idly shuffled through a pack of cards. Theo stared at the blank wall until he couldn’t take it any longer.

His crimson eyes switched to the magician. “I’d suggest we play a card game, but I’m not sure how fair that would be.”

Max’s attention broke from his train of thought, and he gave the vampire a dry smile. He held the deck of cards in his hand and tapped them on the table. Five cards split out, hovering in the air above the rest of the pack. One by one, they turned to reveal themselves.

“Five aces.” Theo shook his head. “No wonder you put Edward in the dirt.”

“He seemed to believe that because I was part demon, I would not betray him.” Max shrugged and returned the aces. “But I’m also human twice, and one of those is a complete showboat.”

“At least one,” Ren murmured as she turned a page.

“The rule was no magic at the poker table,” he continued. “Technically, what I do is all sleight of hand. Even having those cards float just now was me adjusting their position via my Inventory.”

Theo pulled a face. “That sounds… exhausting.”

“Oh, it is, depending on how many objects and how frequently I need to interact with them. I can freely manipulate things within a roughly five-foot circle around myself. Many of the tricks I spent months learning back on Earth are now easily replicated with just a thought.” Max waved a hand over the deck, and it vanished. “That just means I had to be more inventive to continue impressing the crowds.”

Sally groaned and lifted her head up. “You can summon a cannon and turn into a demon. That’s better than most.”

Max tipped his hat slightly. “Well, what can I say?” He opened his mouth and a waterfall of cards tumbled out. As they bounced off the table, they each flipped upright. All were jokers.

“Idle hands,” Sally grumbled. “I bet you this goober has been mentally working out how many items drops he has missed out on based on loot percentages and the time we’ve been waiting here.”

Theo adjusted his glasses. “Well, it’s just natural to look at the opportunity cost of any activity and-”

Any further explanation cut off as a flash of blue illuminated their side. Archie exhaled and leaned on his cane as he eyed up the others. He hesitated, which drew their full attention. After a moment, the wizard gathered his senses.

“The Radochs will be here in a matter of hours.”

Dent stood at the crest of the hill, overlooking a valley. The sound of people organizing and talking behind him felt dull. He scratched at his stubble with his metal hand before returning it to his sword pommel.

A flash of blue made him wince, and he turned to see who had arrived.

“Ah, Humphrey. Doing some last-minute checks?”

The Death Knight shook his head. “I’m to be stationed here, at least at the start.”

“A Guardian and a Champion?” The swordsman frowned. “Isn’t that putting too many eggs in…” He looked back at the gathering adventurers. “No. We are one of the weaker fronts, right?”

Humphrey didn’t respond, but joined Dent in looking out over the valley. It was difficult to plan a defense against an unknown threat. Setting up artillery or cavalry squads made little sense when the enemy approached from the skies. Sitting atop some high ground, hoping the bugs would drop to the lower environment was all they could hope for.

“How is Chuck doing?”

Dent pulled a face and sighed. “You know how he gets. Now more than ever he seems more of a… process in a machine than a person. I managed to peel him away from the screens for a goodbye hug, but his eyes were unfocused. He was in the System.”

“He pushes himself too hard. But, we will all reap the rewards of his efforts.”

Humphrey looked up as a message came through the STAR+

[Happy World Health Day!][Gift Boxes now unlocked]

“It has begun,” he growled.

Sally placed her gift box on the table, but paused and glanced at Archie.

“Another new holiday?” she asked.

“The System allows an allotment of certain yearly celebrations, offerings boons and the like. Chuck has been keeping a few unset in his back pocket for special occasions.” Archie placed his own box on the table. “For twenty-four hours, we have increased resistance to disease and poison, and slight health regeneration outside of combat.”

“All that for a holiday?” Max asked. He had already opened the box, and his eyes were running through the items within via the STAR+.

[Healing Potion (3)][Party Regeneration Scroll (1)][Hearty Meal (1)][World Health Day Pin]

“Not the worst care package, given everyone in the world has received one,” he surmised.

Sally wrinkled her nose as she brought the pin up.

[World Health Day Pin][Grants Herd Immunity when worn on WHD][Herd Immunity: Increased Disease and Poison resistance per nearby ally with this passive]

“It’s not full immunity,” Archie said. “However, it should mostly negate any attempts to gas or plague Sanctuary. At least for a day.”

They each attached the pin—which was a small enamel world with the letters WH embossed on it—onto their shirts. Consumables went into their Inventories.

The wizard sighed and rubbed his head. “I’m not much for speeches. You all know what needs to be done, and you’re the most prepared and capable of doing it. Keep your Aspects on hold unless you really need to use them, with the exception of Ren, who should use it immediately.”

She nodded. It was a plan they’d gone through several times.

“I can teleport you into your first fight, but after that, it’s up to you.” Archie hesitated. “Regroup here if necessary, or in Hell if we perish. Should you die after that, then hold tight. The afterlife doesn’t quite deserve you yet.”

Theo adjusted his glasses. “We are being tracked, correct? There’s a leaderboard.”

“It’s not a 'leaderboard'.” Archie narrowed his eyes at the vampire. “Yes, we track your kills, but that is just to determine the state of the war. We also know your health status and current position at all times. Even what abilities you use.”

Theo turned in his chair slowly to look at the others. A grin grew on his face, revealing his fangs. “Bet you I get top score.”

A flash of blue light signaled Ren’s arrival to a rocky hill. From the looks of the dry terrain, it was somewhere in the Wasteland. It had a decent view of the sky. It was difficult to believe the spotless blue above was about to be the docking point for hundreds of alien ships.

She shuffled her feet in the loose dirt to feel comfortable. Then stretched out her arms and neck. It took considerable effort to keep her breathing regulated, which only turned into bubbling ire. Not that it had much time to simmer.

Thunder cracked across the clear sky with a rumble that shook the ground. The air was immediately thicker, as if compressed by the intrusion of what came next. Five large Radoch ships burst into the sky, followed by at least a dozen smaller ones.

Ren spat on the ground, partly to get the fresh taste of sulfur and spent magical energy out of her mouth. Mostly because a cold anger ran through her. While her bright blue eyes burned with fury, she used Aspect of Order.

The ground around her feet cracked and shifted, eventually rising to build around her. As the ships above slowly lowered in the sky, her mound of stone kept growing, fully encasing her until it was as high as a two-story house.

A hollow tree trunk made of gnarled bark burst from the front, stretching out almost thirty feet. Gravel and debris clattered down the hill as the barrel-like trunk rose and pointed toward the alien vessels.

++Defilers of Sanctuary, I offer you no reprieve++

Energy pulsed around the Aspect, scorching the stone. Air rippled from the increased heat. A high-pitched whine screamed from the end of the tree cannon.

++Your presence is abhorrent to our natural Order++

The air shook with intensity, and all sound became muffled.

++Become nothing++

A bright crystal of blue light flashed out, and the resulting shockwave turned the loose sand into glass almost sixty feet away. In the blink of an eye, the projectile punctured through one of the giant ships, putting a large hole through the underside and an even wider one through the top.

Even as the ship dipped and lost altitude, the crystal exploded high in the atmosphere. Hundreds of smaller shards rained down on this section of the fleet, cutting through the alien metal like butter. One smaller ship exploded outright, and another lost control, striking a third in flight. Fires broke out on one of the mile-long vessels.

The cannon hummed as Ren loaded her next shot.

A burst of blue took Sally from the cool white of the moon base to the harsh reds and dark metal of a Radoch ship. Archie had some kind of teleport-to-living-entity spell cooked up, allowing the Outsiders to be put directly into the Radochs hands.

She spun on her feet and immediately inserted Skeleton Key into the unlucky roach she had anchored to. The Radoch keeled over, bleeding out and emitting a soft hiss.

It looked like she had been a little unlucky as well. This room was small, and the dying bug was the only enemy present. Her gut directed her to the greatest group of living beings. From the feel of it, they hadn’t deployed any troops to ground level yet.

She stepped out of the small room into a corridor and used Summon Zombies. With a Mortis Bomb in hand, she stalked her way toward something important to chew on. Getting into the hangars where the rank-and-file bugs would be waiting would be a massacre, but her job might be quicker if she went for the command deck.

Blue light flickered beside her as a faint projection of Chuck formed. His eyes were blank as he turned to address her. “The land below will be a major front in the pitched battle. Terrain is advantageous for the enemy to bunker in.”

He flickered again and vanished.

Sally pulled a face and growled. She’d make sure they would regret stepping on Sanctuary. With a hand outstretched, she called her skeletal dwarves with A Score To Settle.

“This is a hostile takeover,” she commanded. “I’m the captain now.”
