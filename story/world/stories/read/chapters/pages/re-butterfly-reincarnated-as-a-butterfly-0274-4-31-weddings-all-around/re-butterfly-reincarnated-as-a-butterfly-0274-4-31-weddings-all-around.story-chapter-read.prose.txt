Adon’s feelings on the sudden announcement of his and Rosslyn’s equally sudden engagement were almost entirely positive.

He and the Queen had grown very close, and even if the impetus for the sudden declaration was to head off any further discussion of a political marriage, he could feel the other intent behind it. Rosslyn was sincerely willing to marry Adon. They had only known each other a couple of months, but she felt she could rely on him, they cared for each other, and she preferred him over every other potential partner she had ever met.

Even if he couldn’t read minds, Adon would have understood that much from the fact that she invoked her father in explaining the decision. She said that King Alistair had authorized it. That was a lie, of course, but it was one that she could not easily walk back. It would make the decision extremely awkward to change her mind about later. She would be going against the plan supposedly approved by her dead father.

It felt good. A warm sensation ran through him. Adon felt special. There was guilt mingled in, too, like he was taking advantage of her present situation, but he couldn’t feel that too strongly. This had been her idea, not his suggestion. She had even called it being selfish.

If that’s being selfish, I’d hate to see what Rosslyn being selfless looks like.

King Janus lowered his voice conspiratorially. “So, your plan to secure the realm is to renew the royal line’s connection to magic? Well, the power of a bloodline is certainly important. No one could argue against that. The potential of children conceived directly with a mystic beast would undoubtedly be impressive, especially if the old stories are true…” His eyes darted to Adon, and the King’s eyes twinkled. “And it seems I have been unspeakably rude. Adon, I have been treating you as a mere accessory to the Queen, boorishly ignoring you, rather than addressing you as a Prince Consort and including you properly in the conversation.” Despite his jovial exterior, the King was actually slightly nervous.

I am not officially a consort yet, Adon replied in the most nonchalant tone he could manage. There hasn’t been a wedding.

Inside her mind, the Queen smiled at him, though her actual expression was an excellent poker face.

“I was there for this decision,” came a voice from the other side of the room.

Rosslyn’s eyes widened, and Adon fluttered into the air to get a better look.

Frederick was sitting up in bed. Goldie stood on his chest, poised as if she was excited to see him awake and alive and apparently healthy.

The High Priestess looked slightly tired, Adon observed, but he could feel that she was also pleased with herself and with the results of her actions.

“You were,” King Janus said. “Well, I had not asked for a witness, but—”

“The King was quite insistent,” Frederick said. “Almost as much as the two lovebirds.”

What is Frederick doing, exactly? Rosslyn thought loudly, a little nervous. Not that I do not appreciate the support, but I fail to understand the purpose.

It only took Adon a brief pry into the young lord’s mind to understand.

In a moment, he’s going to want you to return the favor, the butterfly sent. Just replace your father with Frederick’s brother.

“That was the same time when Goldie and I decided to become engaged,” Frederick said. “My older brother approved at the time.” He turned and looked over at William’s body, still lying in the bed next to his, and Frederick’s face fell. Adon felt the intense emotional currents running through the young lord’s mind at the time. It was mostly grief, but there were other complicated notes mixed in, including gratitude and determination.

King Janus scoffed. “You and the mystic beast decided? Even with your brother endorsing it… You are the same Lord Frederick whose father is the ruler of Dessia, correct? What will your father say—”

“Nothing,” Frederick interrupted with a cold expression. “My father will say nothing at all, except perhaps a prayer of gratitude to the Goddess. His wayward son has found his way at last. He should thank the Goddess, thank Goldie, thank… my brother. If he disagrees with my decision, he is welcome to disinherit me. Although that would mean his heirs would be our cousins instead.”

The young lord’s expression twisted into a harsh mockery of a smile.

Telepathy indicated to Adon that the Duke despised the cousins Frederick was thinking of.

I guess that means Frederick gets what he wants, Adon thought. Good for him and Goldie.

King Janus’s expression changed slightly.

“The group of you are all bonded quite tightly together, hmm?” he asked no one in particular.

“We have fought alongside each other more than once,” Rosslyn replied. “William died in support of the Kingdom, and Frederick has just narrowly survived the same scrape with death. There was bound to be a certain close bond.”

The Queen and the young lord exchanged a look of mutual understanding.

“Yes, that is very well,” King Janus said, one eye twitching slightly as he processed everything. “Weddings all around.”

Adon could feel the Parmonian King reluctantly giving up on any of the gains he might have expected from this situation, though the butterfly could also sense that the King’s acceptance of that status quo might only be temporary. He would still try to find some way he could profit from the situation without dishonoring himself.

He’s a persistent guy, Adon thought. I’ll say that much for him. I guess… you don’t get far in politics without that quality. He did have to be elected King in his country, I remember.

“Let us begin discussing how we might deal with our Empire problem, rather than dwelling further on these personal matters, then,” King Janus continued. “As we arrived too late to relieve the Siege of Wayn, our options are somewhat limited…”

The ruler began outlining the condition of his army and the countryside they had passed through, as well as his intelligence about the circumstances closer to the border with the Empire. There was no intelligence about the capital yet, of course, because the Demon Army had only just taken it.

But he promised to send scouts and spies to observe and infiltrate the demon-controlled territory.

As King Janus continued giving his analysis of the situation, the mood in the cottage shifted. Most of those present had been relieved that the Parmonians—and especially the High Priestess who accompanied them—had arrived, but now that the Royal Family was no longer in immediate grave danger, the gravity of the military situation could finally hit home.

The Empire had the capital, the walls were still up, they had wrecked much of the countryside behind them, and if not for the Parmonian force that was presently gathered nearby, the Empire could simply roll over the remainder of the country.

This… is pretty daunting, isn’t it? Adon thought.

The faces all around him were fairly grim. Even Rosslyn’s mood was dark.

“I have to believe the people of this land will rally to you, Rosslyn,” Frederick said quietly. The words were more kind than genuinely optimistic in Adon’s opinion, but they were still welcome.

“That does seem likely,” King Janus said. “When we rode through the land, many of your peasantry were eager to follow alongside us in the hopes that we would liberate the capital, and they would either participate or at least get to witness it. The difficulty of keeping up with our horses caused many of them to give up, but if Your Majesty were to make an effort to recruit soldiers…”

People do not want to give up their homes to strangers, Goldie sent. Even at the risk of dying or losing loved ones. They will fight, despite all common sense, for the people and places they love.

Those words felt directed at Adon, at least from the butterfly’s point of view. He wondered if the spider blamed him for the loss of Samson. For that matter, he suddenly wanted an update as to how Goldie was coping with her child’s death regardless of whether she blamed Adon or not.

Goldie, are you all right…? Those words floated through Adon’s mind for a long few seconds before he decided to think longer about what exactly to say to her. He wasn’t giving up on having the difficult conversation. He was choosing his moment carefully. This wasn’t him sliding back into his old ways.

It was just…

Of course she’s not all right. That can’t be the first thing I say to her to open this subject up again. The first thing I say that directly addresses her dead son, my dead brother… I have to actually act like I have some emotional intelligence for a change!

He had managed to avoid having too many awkward and embarrassing conversations when he and Rosslyn started thinking of each other romantically, but that had been sheer dumb luck.

You have to develop some real social skills at some point, he thought.

The conversation about military matters was continuing, he realized dimly.

“We have an entry point into the city if we need it,” Frederick was saying.

“Only if the Empire somehow fails to find the route after having a hundred or more of their assassins turn up dead in that place,” Rosslyn replied.

Adon suddenly found he was capable of contributing.

The Empire might not know about where their assassins were killed, if you’re talking about the same assassins who Samson defeated, the butterfly sent.

“How is that?” asked King Janus in a tone of genuine curiosity.

It’s possible that the assassins were looking for the Royal Family, and they had some method of tracking their location, but they hadn’t told the Empire exactly where they would be, Adon replied. It would make sense that they must have kept it to themselves. When Rosslyn and I arrived on the scene, there weren’t any Imperial soldiers standing guard around the cave. It would have made sense to leave some there in case the Royal Family turned out to be difficult to handle. Which the assassins should have really expected, considering their previous fight with Rosslyn…

The butterfly realized that he was not only able to contribute, but he didn’t even feel very nervous about answering King Janus’s question. Maybe it was because the topic played to his genuine knowledge and talents. He had a perfect memory for everything that had happened to him, after all, and it was searchable very quickly in situations like these.

“But they still would have found the assassins’ bodies after the fact,” King Janus said slowly. “Would that not mean…?”

They will never find any bodies, Goldie interrupted, her voice flat. No bodies left after what Samson did. Including his.

Adon couldn’t help noticing how the spider’s speech pattern seemed almost to have regressed to what it had been back when they had just met, when she would semi-randomly leave words out of sentences. Was that the effect of grief, or was it just that she was speaking quickly to interrupt the King and answer his unfinished question?

“The bodies had begun to disintegrate when we were escaping the cave,” Frederick explained. “Adon entered and confirmed that it even affected the one who used that magic in the first place.”

“Sounds… powerful,” King Janus said.

“But horribly unstable,” Rosslyn replied. “Not something we can harness ourselves unless we are willing to die for the privilege.”

“What if we—” began King Janus.

There was a knock at the door, however, and he broke off his question.

Rosslyn moved to answer the door immediately.

There was a general thought among the other occupants of the cottage, to the effect of, The Queen cannot answer knocks at the door, someone else has to do that for her, but no one else moved quickly enough.

King Janus didn’t think he was the appropriate person, since he was of equal status to the Queen and was an outsider here anyway, not someone who should be acting like this cottage was his residence. Frederick was too far away from the door and still weak from being healed. The High Priestess was still weak from healing him. And Adon and Goldie would need to transform away from their mystic beast forms so that they could use hands to turn the doorknob.

The Sergeant was there. He wore a serious, business-like expression as he saluted the Queen.

“Your Majesty, there is a group of peasants here to see you,” Angus said.
