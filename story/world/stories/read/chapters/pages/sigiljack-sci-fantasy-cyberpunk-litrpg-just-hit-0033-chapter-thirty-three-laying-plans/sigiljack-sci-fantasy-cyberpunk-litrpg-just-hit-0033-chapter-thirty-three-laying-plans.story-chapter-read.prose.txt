They drove in quiet after that, with only occasional small talk. The city's glow slid across the dash. The luxury blacked sedan’s premium suspension smoothed the drive over the broken streets into the sort comfortable ride few in New Cascadia ever experienced.

When they reached John's apartment complex, the fancy car slowed as it pulled into and idled in the dirt lot out front.

"Thanks for the ride," he told Nabe.

She pressed a button on the control console on her door and his door, and the backseat's opened. "You are welcome, John."

“Come on,” he told the military droid, which was still holding the snoozed aerial drone like a nervous parent might hold a new infant.

John stepped out of the car.

The droid climbed out after him. The drone its was holding lifted out of the robot's arms and hovered by John’s shoulder.

“I will have protection in place by tonight,” Nabe said, getting out of the car as well and speaking to him across the top of the lowset sedan. “Allow me a few hours. It will be safe for you to rest tonight.”

He leaned on the roof, carefully, as the car's exhaust rolled out into the chilly and misty night air. “Just make sure they’re people you actually trust?"

“Of course. I will choose those I have worked with personally.”

John nodded and then said what he felt he had to. “I’m going to need to know where they’ll be and who they are.”

Nabe weighed him and his suggestion with a pointed look. “You understand that discretion and anonymity are valued highly by those Kaito-sama and I work with?”

John thought back to how both Kaito and Velca had, to differing degrees, gotten ornery when their vampiric nature had been addressed directly.

“Sure. But you’ll know who they are, and I'm working with Kaito too, so what's the difference? This is my family. Unless you want me staking the first guy who eyeballs my door too long, I need to know.”

Her lip twitched at the word “staking.” “You should be more careful with what you say.”

He noticed her concern, but pressed the issue anyway. The only people in the world who gave a real damn about his family was him; their safety was his responsibility. “Please. I hate to ask you for favors already. But do this one for me, Nabe?”

She frowned, then nodded. “Very well. I will send the information that I can before the first shift of our men arrive. Please do not accost anyone unduly.”

Relief loosened his shoulders at her words. “Thank you. And our date? Where are we going?"

She had mentioned liking spicy food during the idle small talk they'd had during the rest of the drive. He didn't really like spicy food, beyond spicy sushi, but he could deal with it.

“I will have to see where I can get reservations. I will be able to step away from my duties this Saturday. In the evening. Does this work for you?”

“That's fine. Still sure you want to pick me up?” he asked, smiling now.

Her eyes slid to his beat-up car, still also parked in the lot they'd entered, with something like fond disdain. “Yes.”

He almost laughed. “Don’t judge. Figured you knew you were slumming it."

“I do not do that," she said, hinting that she might not look down on the poor, or John, like most people in her socioeconomic position.

Then again, John realized he didn't really know how much Kaito paid Nabe.

“I’ll see you Saturday, Nabe.” He pushed off the roof. “Drive safe.”

“I will see you, John.” She moved to slide back into the sedan. "Wear something nice, yes?"

"Jacket and jeans not going to meet the dress code?" he called after her.Nabe rolled down the passenger side window, leaned over the center console, and responded to his remark. "No. You will need something better than that. Should I bring something close to your size for you to wear?"

"I'll figure something out," he told her.

She nodded. "I'll call you soon."

"I'll be looking forward to it."

He watched her smile to herself and pull out of the lot.

The droid next to him lifted a hand and waved awkwardly after Nabe's car. John glanced at the robot. “You like her?"

The droid’s optic zeroed in on him. The droid pointed at him in a way that seemed like a rebuttal.

“Yeah, me too. Come on,” John said. “Home sweet home.”

They crossed the dirt lot and entered the apartment's dim lobby. John noticed a strung out sleeper huddled into a few white trash bags. The place smelled like mold and depression. Like always.

I have to get them out of here, John thought to himself about Claire and Mona.

His unit's door unlocked and slid open with a command from his threadlink as he approached and entered through it. The apartment was dark. He flicked the wall switch manually when the threadlink command he sent failed to turn on the lights. Looked like he needed to fix the receiver for the lights. Again.

He looked back at the droid, who'd followed him inside warily. “Let me check on my family and eat something. Then we’ll see about fixing you up.”

He then sent a mental command to the aerial drone hovering quietly near the droid. He ordered it to go and settle down on the room's coffee table and to go into rest mode.

John moved down the apartment's narrow hallway and opened a door, peering inside. Claire was asleep, huddle into her bed and holding an aging stuffed animal her mother had given her a long time ago. She was safe. He walked into the room and took the family's spare pistol off Claire's night stand. She didn't needed it anymore, he was home.

He closed Claire's door and checked on Mona next. His aunt was in her medical pod, her vitals green on the monitors.

He exhaled and closed the door to Mona's room as well. Then he moved back through the hallway, into the small living room and ventured into the narrow kitchen.

Athena materialized then, leaning on the yellowing kitchen counter. John sat the pistol he'd taken from Claire's nightstand down on the counter next to her.

“You’ve been quiet,” he said to her, opening a cabinet and taking out a discount nutrient bar.

She folded her arms, her threadlight skin glowing with mystical spirals and ghost code. “Are you mad at me?”

He sat and unwrapped the bar. The droid hovered in the doorway to the kitchen like a confused puppy, following him wherever he went. And having no idea who I'm talking to right now, John realized and then looked over to the robot to give an excuse. “I’m on a call. Go sit in the living room and please don’t touch anything.”

The droid scratched the back of its metal neck and obeyed. John looked back to Athena. “I told you I'm not mad.”

“I know. You said you were concerned,” Athena said, almost sounding like she was pouting.

John didn't address her demeanor directly. “You said you’re still stable, right?”

She nodded. “As I said, other than some emotional turbulence, I am stable. My core construct integrity remains intact. There is no risk of me ascending.”

He sighed and bit into the brown nutrient bar.

“That tastes like cardboard,” Athena said, tasting what he ate through his taste buds.

“It doesn’t grow on you," John admitted a bit starkly. "But they say it has what you need if you eat enough of them.”

“It does not. I am detecting that there is nearly no zinc or calcium in it," her pupilless eyes glowed brighter, something John had picked up to mean that she was accessing the threadnet. "The producer's product page claims otherwise. They are either lying or their quality control is atrocious.”

“It’s still something. And I’m starving," John replied.

“You should buy groceries," she said, not uncrossing her arms.

“We have a little over five thousand credits. The arm we designed would take that much to get together. And I'm not even sure that makes sense to do right now. If I start spoiling myself or getting careless, the money will run out fast.” He took another bite of the nutrient. “Lifestyle creep is a bitch.”

“It is groceries we are talking about, John. Eating real food is not spoiling yourself," Athena argued, sounding a bit like his aunt had when raising him; he'd always been mindful of how much he cost to support, even as a kid and teenager. "We could still make minor, impactful improvements to your arm without designing a new one. And you have access to your magic now. If you practice that and your ki, the return may be greater than that of a replacement limb."

“Retooling the arm is on the table,” he agreed. “We also have the droid. He’s beat up and, considering he might be alive, I’d feel bad if we didn't fix him up. The aerial drone is a newer model and seems solid. It probably just needs ammo. It'll be cheap to upkeep. But I still want to scrub through its programming.”

“All prudent choices," Athena said, hesitating as she processed information in her analytical way. “What do you wish to do with the rest of the money?”

“Save it? Put it away for my aunt, maybe,” he said. “Or put it toward a better apartment. Five thousand doesn’t scratch what I’d need to pay for Mona to be cured, anyway. And this place is a dump.”

He thought about his new access to biomancy. He could heal himself to an extent now, as long as he didn’t piss off the Titan again. Maybe, one day, he could heal his aunt too. He didn't want to turn her into a test subject though. He couldn't try anything until his magic was stronger and he knew exactly what he was doing.

“And groceries?” Athena asked, a small hopeful smile breaking through her insecure and frustration expression.

He stood and grabbed a water bottle from a crate next to the fridge. “Fine. I’ll stock the kitchen tomorrow. I still need to call Red. The dungeon you picked up should open soon. We need to check on that to make sure the situation hasn't changed. And pay the Retainers a visit to figure out if they can help us make some cash off it."

“You have not considered asking Kaito for help?” Athena asked him, seeming to calm as he began to plan with her again, as if having a task to think on centered her or made her feel accepted.

“Putting all our eggs in one basket like that feels like a bad idea. And I still don’t know if I trust him. Besides, he'll have more work for us soon anyway. And his hands seem full.” He twisted the cap off the water bottle and drank, then set it down on the table. “Listen. I get that the Angel makes you uncomfortable. Never mind the weird visions we got hit with after we saw it. But we need to figure out why that shit happened," he frowned as his thoughts turned darker. "And the Grin have to go now.”

“Because they threatened your family?”

“Damn right.” He drank a bit more of his water and then pitched the empty bottle into the ancient recycling bin. “The Angel matters to them. As far as I can tell, it or what they can get from it, is what’s stopping Kaito and his people from wiping them out. We take it off the board or free it. Maybe we figure out how it connects to you. Even if we don’t, I bet the vamps finish the Grin for us once we take away their secret weapon.”

Athena smiled, softly and hesitantly. “You are different now. More confident. More yourself.”

“That’s because of you. Which means you get some grace too,” he said, feeling a bit relieved to be actually talking to her again. “But I need to know… can I still trust you to have my back on this?”

Her arms loosened. “Of course, John. I am with you. I am proud of you.”

“Good. Thanks," he said. "Guess we both have issues now. But I figure we take them one step at a time like everything else."

Athena set her palms down against the kitchen counter. “What is the next step? Do you still want me to force you to sleep?”

He shook his head. “No. We'll head to the workshop. We’ll get our new friends up and running and look through their code to make sure there aren't any surprises waiting for us there. After that I might call Vexi and see if she can source us some UV lights.”

“UV lights?” she asked.

“Yeah. A few big enough to flood every room and hall in this place if we need to. Maybe some high-powered handhelds we can mount in the palm of my arm while we're thinking about upgrading it. I'm not working with vampires without covering my ass. Hopefully UV works on them like the stories say. We haven't actually seen any of them fry in the sun, but it's the only idea I've got right now."

“I will help you implement everything,” Athena said in his mind, and dematerialized as he stepped into the living room.

“Come on. We have to fit you through some ductwork,” John told the military droid, now sitting rigidly on the couch. “It's time to patch you up.”

The droid turned only its head toward him, single optic bright. Then it nodded, quick and happy.
