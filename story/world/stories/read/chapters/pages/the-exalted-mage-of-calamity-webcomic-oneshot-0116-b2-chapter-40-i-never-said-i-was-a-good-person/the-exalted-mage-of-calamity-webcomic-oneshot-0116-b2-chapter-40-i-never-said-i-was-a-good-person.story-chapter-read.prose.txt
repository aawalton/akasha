Veronica and Gwen reached the base of the central staircase.

An attendant standing nearby spotted them and stepped forward. He was a young man with neatly combed hair and a silver badge representing the auction house pinned to his lapel.

“I’m sorry, but the second floor is not open to the public at the moment,” he said politely.

Gwen peered past him. The man followed her gaze.

Near the other railing, another attendant spoke with a man and his wife, both impeccably dressed and likely nobles. After a brief exchange, the attendant bowed, and the two walked up the stairs.

Gwen frowned and looked back at the man.

The attendant smiled weakly and chuckled nervously. “Haha… they… work here.”

Gwen gave him a long, hard look.

“Alright, alright,” Veronica said after a moment. “What kind of status do we need to enter the second or third floors?”

The attendant maintained his shaky smile. “I’m sorry, but those two really do work here. The second floor is—”

Gwen leaned forward, arms crossed, growling low like a guard dog.

The man looked away slightly. “Haha… um… well, you either need to be part of one of the great noble houses, belong to the Caroline or Trailing Star commerce groups, or have registered an item for the auction.”

“Would a signet from Viscount Leopold work?” Veronica asked, presenting it to him.

He looked down at it, then shook his head. “Unfortunately, no. A viscount’s status is too low.”

Veronica lowered her arm.

That was unfortunate. Even a viscount’s authority was too low to enter one floor of an auction house? Well, it was only a viscount’s authority. In her past life, she wouldn’t even have spared a viscount a glance.

“I didn’t want to resort to this just yet, but I suppose I have no choice,” she said softly.

The attendant’s smile vanished. His eyes widened as he took a step backward. “Ma’am, violence is not permitted here—”

“What are you talking about?” Gwen interrupted. “Veronica isn’t the type to blow you up just because you denied her entry.” She leaned closer to Veronica’s ear. “You’re not going to blow up the auction house, are you?”

Veronica sighed. “No.” She looked back at the attendant, who appeared ready to flee. “Arrange a meeting with one of your auctioneers. I’d like to submit something to the auction. And bring me some paper and something to write with.”

The attendant stared at her. “Ah… right.”

Inside a small room at the back of the first floor, Veronica and Gwen sat on a luxurious couch, drinking feiyo tea. It was made from feiyo trees grown only in the coldest regions of the east, beyond the kingdom’s borders.

At least, that was its reputation. In reality, the trees were easy enough to cultivate as long as someone could provide sufficiently cold ice.

After a few minutes, a large, round man entered in a suit accented with gold. Two beastfolk girls followed behind him, both wearing collars around their necks. They were probably around Gwen’s age, perhaps a year or two younger. Each wore a maid uniform and clasped her hands in front of her.

Beastfolk slaves.

Veronica narrowed her eyes but otherwise kept her expression neutral.

She hadn’t seen many in Greystone or Ronswick, but in a city like Maftem, they were far more common. People simply didn’t display their slaves publicly very often. Most were kept at home, assigned whatever duties they had been purchased to perform.

Gwen, sitting beside her, had an almost opposite reaction. She didn’t look at them with disdain. No—she looked afraid. Her fingers clenched in her lap.

“Ah, are you the woman who wishes to auction—”

“I’m sorry, but I’ll wait outside,” Gwen said.

Before Veronica could respond, she stood and walked past the man into the corridor. The door shut behind her.

“Hm?” The man turned toward the closed door. “Did I do something to offend her?”

Veronica pressed her lips together. “No. You didn’t.”

It was the truth. With slavery still legal, he had done nothing wrong. And perhaps it would remain that way for several more years. In the future, it had taken Veronica and several other archmages years of effort to outlaw slavery completely. But as she was now, she had made no progress toward that goal.

Nor would she until she reclaimed her status.

The large man glanced between her and the door Gwen had exited through, then gave a polite but confused smile before settling into the chair across from her.

“Well then,” he said, clasping his hands over his stomach. “My name is Jansen Pelbrant, one of the senior lot registrars for the Pelotema Grand Auction. I was told you wished to submit something?”

The meeting lasted nearly half an hour.

When the door opened again, Jansen Pelbrant walked out first, his two maids following behind him. He still wore his polished merchant’s smile, but there was now a sharper look in his eyes: interest and greed. The kind that appeared when a merchant smelled profit.

Veronica followed him into the corridor.

Gwen waited near one of the decorative windows farther down the hall. Her arms were crossed, and her expression remained tense, though some of it eased when she saw Veronica.

“Miss Everwells,” Jansen said, turning back toward her. “As discussed, the Pelotema Grand Auction will submit both theory manuscripts for immediate verification. If our mage appraisers confirm their authenticity, they will be accepted as late lots.”

Veronica nodded. “Very well.”

“The first manuscript will be listed as a compression theory for reducing select second-tier spells to the first tier. Likewise, the second manuscript will cover the compression of third-tier spells to the second tier.” Jansen’s smile widened slightly. “Once verification is complete, you will receive registered-seller status. You’ll gain access to the upper floors of the pre-showing and a VIP balcony seat for the main auction. I imagine the bidding for your submissions will be quite lively.”

“I hope we can both make a significant amount of money, then,” Veronica said.

Jansen grinned, as if those were the best words anyone could ever hear. “I will send an attendant to notify you once the initial verification is complete. It shouldn’t take more than twenty minutes.”

“Thank you.”

Jansen gave her a small bow before walking down the hall. The two beastfolk maids followed silently behind him.

Gwen watched them leave. Once they were gone, she looked back at Veronica. “What happened?”

“I submitted two spell-compression theories so we can access the upper floors of the pre-showing. We’ll also receive VIP seating during the auction.”

“Spell compression? Wait, aren’t those what you gave Hugh?”

Veronica nodded. “The same ones.”

Gwen blinked. “I thought you wanted to share those for free. Isn’t that why you told Hugh to send them to the Mage Association so they could release them publicly?”

“Yes.”

Gwen’s jaw fell slightly. “Then… won’t whoever buys those notes get screwed?”

Veronica shrugged. “Money is more important to me right now than some rich merchant being scammed. That’s the risk of buying information. Besides, I don’t have that many spell-compression theories I can sell. Most of what I can do with spell suppression is only possible because of my twin mana cores. I have to leverage what I can.”

“That doesn’t sound like something a hero who is trying to save the world would say. Or more importantly, do.”

“I never said I was a good person.”

Gwen raised an eyebrow. “You are a good person.”

Veronica smiled. “Thanks.”

“Well, besides the whole scamming thing.”

Veronica rolled her eyes.

Hugh sat at his desk inside the Ronswick Mage Tower. A communication crystal rested in front of him. He tapped one finger against the desk.

He was nervous. Understandably so.

He had spoken with the Mage Association several times over the past few weeks—far more often than he had ever expected. Most tower mages could work their entire careers without speaking directly to anyone from the Arcanum Spire, let alone one of its representatives.

But he was in a unique position. The spell-theory notes Veronica had provided had turned him from an ordinary head of study at a regional mage tower into a direct liaison.

The crystal flashed. A figure formed within its surface from pale blue light. The image was faint due to the immense distance between the two crystals, but it was clear enough to reveal a man in black robes seated behind a desk. His hair was neat, his posture straight, and his demeanor reserved.

“Hugh. Head of studies at the Ronswick Mage Tower,” Lucian said.

Hugh straightened. “Sir Lucian. Thank you for accepting the call.”

“I assume this concerns Veronica Everwells?”

Hugh paused. It wasn’t difficult to infer. He had never contacted the Mage Association so directly before Veronica entered his life.

He sighed quietly. “Yes. It does.”

“I thought so.” Lucian adjusted a document on his desk. “If you’re looking for an update, the compression theory has already passed repeated verification. We’ve tested it with several different second-tier spell structures. Some were more compatible than others, but the underlying principle remains sound.”

“That’s good to hear.”

“Mm. Very good. It has been a long time since we’ve seen someone so pragmatic and so eager to share her knowledge. Especially something like this, which will change how we understand spell structures going forward.”

Hugh couldn’t argue with that. Many mages were selfish with their discoveries, keeping them exclusive or demanding royalties whenever someone built upon their research. Veronica wanted none of that. Even sharing recipes with the mage association came with an incentive—a permanent royalty whenever a potion was sold.

Lucian continued, “As for distribution, the Association intends to release the first prepared version to the main branches in approximately one week. Afterward, each regional tower will receive copies through the usual channels. Wider academic publication will take longer, but the branches should have access soon.”

Hugh’s fingers tightened slightly around the edge of his desk.

Lucian watched him through the crystal. “Is something the matter? Is this not what Veronica wanted?”

Hugh hesitated. “Would it be possible to delay the release?”

Lucian’s brows lifted. “Delay it?”

“By a week or two.”

“For what reason?”

Hugh exhaled. “Veronica contacted me this morning. She is currently in Maftem for the Pelotema Grand Auction.”

“I’m aware she is in Maftem.”

Hugh blinked. “You are?”

Lucian ignored the question. “Continue.”

Hugh cleared his throat. “She asked whether the compression notes had been released publicly yet. I told her they had not. She then informed me that she intended to leverage some of those notes during the auction.”

Lucian stared at him. After several long seconds, his lips curved faintly. A few quiet chuckles escaped him as he looked down at his desk. “So she intends to profit from the notes before they are inevitably released for free.”

Hugh rubbed his forehead. “Yes.”

Of course, he had opposed the idea when she mentioned it to him. It was dangerous, especially since she couldn’t predict exactly whom she might antagonize with such a scheme. But she had reaffirmed that she needed money and was willing to take some risks.

Hugh continued, “She asked whether the Association could delay the release long enough that the buyers wouldn’t feel cheated immediately after the auction ended.”

“They will feel cheated regardless once the notes spread..”

“I know.”

Lucian released a quiet breath, almost a laugh. “How very direct of her.”

“That is… certainly one way to put it,” Hugh said cautiously.

Lucian tapped a finger against his desk. “Does she not have other theories she could provide? You have already sent several documents, and each has proven more useful than the last. At times, the knowledge seems almost endless.”

Hugh’s expression grew guarded. “She probably does.”

“Probably?”

“Yes.”

Lucian studied him through the blurry crystal. “Hm. But you cannot ask.”

“Under my agreement with her, no,” Hugh said. “She gives whatever she chooses to give. I simply act as her intermediary.”

Lucian hummed. “Earlier, you asked how I knew she was in Maftem.”

Hugh straightened. “Ah, yes.”

“News reached us recently from the Willow Mage Tower branch. More specifically, from an alchemist named Rennia. Veronica Everwells purchased a Blue Dryad’s Potion and exchanged information of significant value in the process.”

Hugh briefly closed his eyes.

Of course she had. Of course she had walked into another mage tower and traded away more information. She was essentially a walking encyclopedia of magical knowledge.

Lucian continued, “That report, combined with your submissions and the matter involving Archmage Ballamus’s sister, made her location a matter of interest.”

Hugh opened his eyes. “Interest? What do you mean?”

Lucian folded his hands neatly atop his desk. “Archmage Ballamus wishes to meet Veronica Everwells personally. However, he is currently occupied with his sister’s recovery. The cure succeeded, but recovering from years of immobility is not an easy task. She is awake now, but walking again will require time, care, and no small amount of patience.”

Hugh’s expression softened. “Then… she truly recovered?”

“Yes.”

For a moment, the room felt strangely quiet.

Hugh had known the cure had been delivered. He had known the instructions had been accepted. But learning that it had truly worked was something else entirely. Although he had only passed along the recipe, part of him still felt responsible. He hadn’t created the cure, but he had helped make her recovery possible.

He leaned back and released a breath he hadn’t realized he was holding. “Thank the heavens,” he murmured.

Lucian’s expression eased slightly. “Archmage Ballamus shared the sentiment, in his own way.”

Despite himself, Hugh smiled.

Lucian continued, “Since he cannot leave his sister’s side yet, he has sent several trusted mages to Maftem under his authority. Their task is to remain discreet, observe the situation, and ensure no one acts foolishly toward Veronica Everwells.”

Hugh frowned. “Observe? So they’re keeping tabs on her?”

“No,” Lucian said. “Not in the way you mean.”

“Then what?”

“If she attends an auction and sells valuable knowledge, powerful people may take an interest. Some will be polite. Others may be less accommodating. Archmage Ballamus intends to ensure the impolite ones remember their manners before matters escalate. We already suspected she might leverage some of her knowledge there. We simply didn’t expect her to leverage the same knowledge she had given to the Mage Association under the condition that it be released publicly.”

Hugh stared at him. Then his shoulders slowly relaxed.

“As for your request, I can delay the release of the notes by two weeks. After that, we will be forced to publish them. We have already made several promises and arrangements.”

“I understand.”

Lucian reached for something beyond the crystal’s view, likely another document. “Then that should conclude our business, yes? I have a great deal of paperwork now that Archmage Ballamus is on an extended vacation.”

Hugh released another breath, this one lighter than before. “Ah, yes. Thank you, Sir Lucian.”

Lucian nodded. A moment later, the crystal flickered and the connection cut.

Hugh remained seated at his desk, staring at the fading blue light. Then he leaned back and rubbed both hands over his face. Lowering them, he stared at the ceiling.

“I miss when tower politics meant arguing over herb transport budgets,” he muttered.
