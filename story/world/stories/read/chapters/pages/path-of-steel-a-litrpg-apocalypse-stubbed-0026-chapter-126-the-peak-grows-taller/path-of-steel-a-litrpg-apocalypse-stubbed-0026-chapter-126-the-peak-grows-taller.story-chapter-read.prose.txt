“Disciple, that’s enough!” the deacon yelled, having caught the Lord of Pot’s pot. Cog collapsed to the sand behind him.

“Cog!” Alicia shouted, jumping down from the stands. She glared at the Lord of Pot. “You didn’t have to do that! He was clearly going down!”

“He would have done the same to me,” the Lord said with a shrug.

“No, he wouldn’t!”

“He might have,” Asim said, appearing beside her on the sands. “This is the second time, Lord of Pot. Pray we never meet in the wild.”

“Climbers never pray,” the Lord replied. He leapt back to his place in the stands, where he sat cross-armed and alone.

Asim placed a hand on Cog’s forehead. Swimming purple aura rose from it, shaped as tentacles, then was swiftly retrieved. “He’ll be fine,” Asim said, to Alicia’s visible relief.

“Lord of Pot qualifies for the finals. Sean Cog is disqualified. Please take your friend to the stands,” the deacon said. Though his words were measured, his voice was kind.

Cog was laid on an empty trio of chairs. His head was disfigured, the metal caved in in places. Just like flesh, it would automatically mend itself. The bodies of metallic lifeforms contained tiny robots which repaired such fractures over time.

“Interesting,” Asim said, cupping his chin at Cog. He reached to turn the gear at the back of his head. “Hehe.”

“Asim!” Alicia said.

“What?”

“Don’t grind his gears!”

“Okay. By the way, that Lord of Pot is an asshole. I still think we should jump him.”

“Gary said no.”

“Gary’s not my boss.”

“Yeah, but… I mean, it’s wrong. We can’t just jump people.”

“Are we supposed to let him get away with injuring you and trying to kill Cog?”

“I don’t know…”

“He hurt our people. We can’t let it slide,” Asim said.

“Why not?”

“It’s a message. We’d be telling the world that hey, you can attack us, and nothing will happen to you. Who’d be afraid of us then?”

“Why do we need people to be afraid of us?”

“Because if not, we die.”

“Who’d kill us?”

“Anyone interested,” Asim said. “You’re soft, Alicia. You can’t be soft. Someone will stab you in the back or force you to the wall. You must be a hunter, or you’re prey.”

“I don’t believe that. How would you even know? Maybe this world is different from your planet.”

“I had a friend once, with seven siblings. A gang raped his sister and they did nothing. The gang saw that and took another sister. His father threatened them, but it was too late. Nobody took him seriously. In the end, he bled out on the street.”

Alicia wrapped her arms around herself. “That’s chilling,” she said.

“It’s how things work. From what I’ve seen, the clans and families are just a bunch of legitimate gangs working together. Everyone’s looking for a chance to rob you.” He shook his head. “In a world of thieves, the best protection is looking tough. Easy targets get gang-raped.”

“Asim!”

“What? It’s the truth.”

“No, it’s not. You’re just— Ugh! You’re making me sick!”

He shrugged. “Better I do it than the world.”

With those words, both settled in to watch the matches. They sat on either side of the unconscious Cog.

On the sands below, impressive fighters clashed, one pair after another. Lin Xin fought for real this time—she faced a skilled blaze-wielder in a battle that wasn’t hard but not easy either. In the end, she froze his spear, completely extinguishing his flames. The man gaped for a moment before resigning. He had zero losses anyway, so he was guaranteed to pass. It was the same for Lin Xin. The match-ups had been arranged to keep the final number of participants as close to sixteen as possible.

Sui Duang also came on stage. His tall physique visibly dominated his opponent’s, and it was the same for their skills. Sui Duang’s Razor Wave flowed alongside his spear, each attacking in turn in a beautiful dance of water and competence. In the end, his opponent never broke the stream, and was eliminated without putting a single scratch on Sui Duang’s robes.

When Gary’s name was announced, the audience erupted into uproar. Banners flew. Gongs appeared. “Baldinator! Baldinator!”

“Don’t fret guys. He’s missing again,” someone in the crowd said. Everyone glanced at the disciple seats. Sighs arose. The gongs went back in. Someone cursed and started wrapping a twenty-foot-long banner.

“This guy is totally ignoring us!” someone yelled.

“Yeah! It’s like he wants to humiliate not just his opponent, but also us!”

“Isn’t that kinda cool though?”

“He’ll certainly be here for the finals. That’s when the real fight will happen. Imagine if Lin Xin loses her hair!”

“Ohhh! We must support him no matter what!”

“Baldinator! Baldinator!”

Alicia could only shake her head. “They’re impossible,” she said.

There was, however, one person whose reception eclipsed Gary’s. Feng Shen was called for the final match of the day. Facing him was a blond woman with piercing blue eyes, who already had one defeat. She’d convinced herself to try her hardest—no battle was impossible until it was truly lost.

As Feng Shen appeared, however, her last hope vanished. The woman visibly deflated into a husk of herself, and she chuckled bitterly. Because, as Feng Shen floated towards the sand, the aura he exuded was almost beyond the Birth of Aura realm.

“He broke through!?” someone in the audience asked.

“No, look! He’s suppressing it!”

“Already? Wow! The tournament was held three months early, and he’s already a half-step into Core Formation!”

“Then, isn’t the winner already decided?”

“Hey, Feng Shen! My daughter is beautiful, wanna marry her?”

“Dad!”

It wasn’t uncommon for the top disciples to reach the peak of the Birth of Aura realm before or during the tournament. However, it was common sense that they’d suppress their breakthrough until after the tournament to keep things fair. Otherwise, people with imperfect foundations might also rush their breakthroughs, which would harm their potential.

Feng Shen touched gently down on the sands. He cupped his hands at his opponent. “My apologies. Your strength is good, and you might have passed today, but you got matched against me. It was the will of fate.”

“You don’t need this win, Feng Shen. Can’t you just give it to me?” she tried.

Feng Shen gave a half-smile. “That’s not how it works.”

“Then, don’t blame me if I go out swinging. Deep Dive!” Her spear launched forward like an arrow. Her body was dragged behind it as if it was just an afterthought. It was a dominating strike, one which had demolished most of her opponents so far. Even Lin Xin, who’d defeated this woman before, had used her full force to just barely block this strike.

Feng Shen only raised two fingers. As the spear arrived at hellish speed, he gently brushed it away, letting the woman sail past like it was choreographed. She crashed into a wall. When she recovered, still grasping her spear, blood flowed from her palms. She chuckled bitterly.

“How did you do that?” she asked. “Parrying my spear is not enough to redirect the strike. It should have crashed into you slanted.”

“My fingers parried the tip of your spear, but the true parry came from my aura field, which guided you and your spear into a new trajectory as a bank to the river,” Feng Shen said.

“I didn’t even know that could happen.”

“Everything can happen. It was a powerful attack. Congratulations.”

She shook her head. “I admit defeat,” she said, then jumped into the stands without waiting for the deacon’s announcement.

“What? Feng Shen is suppressing his breakthrough? How many months did I miss?” Cog said, awoken by the loud cheering.

“Cog!” Alicia yelled, wrapping him in a hug.

“Ow. Ow. Ow.”

“Sorry!”

“It’s fine,” he said. He rose to a seating position, holding onto his misshaped head. “Hmm?” he said. His fingers toured over his head, finding the bumps and craters. “Oh. Oh, no. That wasn’t supposed to happen. I lost, didn’t I?”

Alicia made a sad expression. “I’m sorry.”

“Damn.”

“You tried your best, Cog. I know how much this meant to you…”

“It’s okay. I guess I wasn’t ready yet.” He smiled, not quite hiding his pain. “But did you see? I almost reached the finals! All that hard work paid off! It sucks ass, but who knows what I’ll achieve if I keep at it!”

“There is always a next time, Cog. We’re just getting started. We can do it!” Alicia said.

“What do you think, Cog? Wanna jump the guy who did you like that?” Asim asked.

“But that’s illegal,” Cog said.

“Really? Now you care? I didn’t see you complaining when Gary gave you Muscle—”

“Then again, what are laws? Who can dictate right and wrong?” Cog said, leading to everyone’s laughter.

Thus concluded the last day of the tournament’s preliminaries. Seventeen finalists emerged, which didn’t quite fit the brackets, so two randomly chosen finalists with one loss each were called to duke it out immediately. One was Oracle Jay, whom Gary had defeated on the second day, and the other was a person who looked like a walking tree.

Both were tired from their earlier battles, but in the end, Oracle Jay emerged victorious and moved to the finals. The treeson—that was the name for the tree person’s species—was distraught and tried to burrow roots into the sand before the deacon stopped him.

“Tomorrow will be a rest day,” Elder Feng said, having jumped into the arena. “On the day after that, the rewards and brackets of the finals will be announced, and the first round will be held. Eight fighters will be left after the first day, four after the second, two after the third, and on the fourth day, the winner will be crowned. Our disciples look forward to demonstrating their strongest techniques for you. To the heavens!”

“To the heavens!” the disciples roared, shaking the arena. The audience clapped. Slowly, they dissolved.

To Gary, the tournament’s happenings were far away. He was entirely sunken into training, day in and day out. The sun dipped. It rose again. He was ever thrusting, pouring his heart and mind into the Nine Songs of the Spear.

He’d already mastered thrusting with the first and second song. He was now on the third song, which had proven to be immensely difficult. The concepts of water made no sense. Splitting his aura into drops was impossible, because they acted as lightning and started arcing to each other the instant he split them up.

And yet, he needed to make it happen.

“Concentrate,” Master Colorful said. “Your aura isn’t lightning, it’s just used to behaving as such. Turn it off. Let it be pure. That’s the only way for you to use the third song.”

“I’m trying,” Gary said. A splitting headache assaulted him, and it reminded him of lightning. Every pulse neutralized his work so far.

“Push through. If it’s not the pain, it will be something else. Your vibrations shape your understanding—everything will seem like lightning. Ignore those instincts and purify your aura.”

Gary sat cross-legged, sweating bullets. Whenever he opened his eyes, his vision swam. He fought to stay upright.

“You can’t do it like this,” Colorful finally said. “It’s my fault. Maybe I pushed you too far. Go home, sleep for four hours, then return.”

“I can sleep here,” Gary replied weakly.

“You need the change of scenery.”

On the way back, Gary fought not to stumble. The world was a kaleidoscope. He felt drunk. While his mind was currently unable to work, he’d already made progress. He was close to distancing his aura from lightning, which would let him practice the third song. His hard work was paying off—the only question was, would he make it in time?

He stumbled towards his cabin, ignoring familiar faces to reach his bed as quickly as possible.

“Gary!” came a voice.

He turned to find Alicia. “Hey,” he said, fighting not to show his dizziness.

“Are you okay?” she asked.

“I’m fine.”

“You look a little…off.”

“I’m a little tired,” he replied. A hidden urge stopped him from showing the true depths of his exhaustion. “I was actually just going to sleep,” he said.

“Okay…” Disappointment flickered in her eyes, but Gary had to go. He paced for his cabin.

“Gary?” she called out again. He turned around.

“What?”

“The preliminaries ended, and, well… You’d promised to take me for dinner…”

He recalled it through the haze. His heart softened. “Listen, Alicia. I will keep my promise, alright? Just not now. I’m sorry. There’s even more work to be done than I expected, and I really can’t afford the time. Plus, I’m a walking corpse. It wouldn’t be fun. I’d probably sleep on the table.”

“I understand,” she said. She turned to leave. Gary gritted his teeth.

“Alicia,” he said.

“What?”

“I might die in a few days, so going out and having fun now is pointless… If I survive this, and the future is open, then I’ll take you to dinner before we have to leave again for the invasion quest. I promise.”

Her eyes simultaneously brightened and darkened. “Okay,” she replied. She smiled. “Sleep well.”

“Oh, I will.”

He reached his cabin and collapsed on the bed.
