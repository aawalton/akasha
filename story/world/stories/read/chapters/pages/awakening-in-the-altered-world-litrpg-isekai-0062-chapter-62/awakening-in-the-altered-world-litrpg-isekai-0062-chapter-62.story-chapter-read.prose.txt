Sage’s spear struck the Mana Shield, and Alex saw the force of the recoil vibrate up Sage’s arms; there was a nexus of cracks on Alex’s Shield, but that was all.

The O2 talent was forced to stagger a few steps, reeling; his spear almost flew from his fingers.

It was as many backward steps as he’d taken all of his previous fight, and by Sage’s eyes, he still seemed surprised at how well Alex’s Shield had walled him. A few of the cheers, from the O2 side, cut off, replaced with sounds of confusion.

And then Alex’s Mana Spears screamed for Sage’s neck.

Sage was forced back yet another step, and then again, barely deflecting two with his blood spear. He’d yet to regain his balance.

CLANG!

When his back struck a Mana Shield.

Sage was forced to face the next two Mana Spears head-on; one he had the reflexes to slip, but the second crashed into his side.

“You tricky fuck,” said Sage, grinning.

New, bloody energies coursed through his body, even as he fell; his pupils flickered blood-red.

Sage’s eyes locked on Alex’s position; he cried out, teeth bared, a certain anger in his eyes.

He’d seemed a very instinctive, opportunistic fighter to Alex, after seeing that last fight. Rather emotional, driven by whims, and possibly a sense of bloodlust.

After that, he really wants to get me back, Alex mused.

Sage stepped, blurring into motion; once, twice, picking up straight-line speed.

The air shimmered a few feet in front of Alex’s face as his eyes widened; two Mana Shields appeared as well—like he was alarmed at Sage’s sheer speed, and was now scrambling to build a little fortress around himself.

His hope was Sage would interpret all this as a sign of weakness and fear. And it wasn’t unlike, in his mind, a playground trick; waving his left hand in the air as a distraction, as he struck the guy with his right.

Alex saw Sage’s pupils dilate ever-so-slightly more; Alex’s lips pursed. Wait.

Almost in the same instant Alex’s decoy Shields appeared, drawing Sage’s eyes, a third Mana Shield, significantly smaller, appeared about two feet off the ground, horizontally.

CRSHH—

Sage felt the Shield materialize, and glanced down, was a fraction of a second late to react. It nearly caved his knees in.

Sage went hurtling sideways, as another Mana Shield horizontal materialized mid-air; once you got someone in the air it was rather easy to tell their trajectories.

This one was rather like a dinner plate, four-ish feet long; Alex wanted it at a length where Sage’s feet were off the ground, so he couldn’t generate force. Sage slid along this one, and—CRNK!

Struck the vertical backstopper Shield nearly face first, killing Sage’s momentum.

And then Sage was rather like a patient on an operating table, served up on that dinner plate, as the Mana Spears crashed down on his head; Sage snarled, tried kicking off, which seemed rather comical when his legs met air. Alex was incredulous.

Sage was, at least, quite well-geared up, and was able to take plenty of the spears’ damage on his bloody-red armor set.

He was bleeding from a few more places now.

For the first time, Sage seemed a little embarrassed; he was still grinning as he scrambled to his feet, but his face was flushed.

“Nice,” he said, licking his lips. “Okay, I see how it is.”

His speartip flared red, and a deeply red aura started to leak from the gems in his armor. His veins started to glow bright blue, even as his eyes cracked with blood vessels.

“If that’s how it’s going to be… let me try—this?”

Three seconds.

CLANG-CRSH!!

Sage’s face slammed against a Shield. He tried to right himself, saw two Spears racing low, an instant from crashing into his legs, so Sage’s teeth gritted; leaped five feet into the air, muscles glowing with Life mana.

Only for four Mana Spears to crash into him, one after another.

Blood spewed, and he screamed as he fell. “You FUCKING—”

Sage swerved, hard, and now his anger was clearly starting to break through.

Once again he went for Alex’s neck, his blood aura deepening, his face contorted.

Four seconds.

CLANG—CLANG-CLANG!

Sage fell, ping-ponged between two Shields, and ate two Mana Spears to the spine.

He screamed, more out of anger than pain, righted himself, and saw a face staring back at him with cold eyes.

Sage went at Alex like he meant to carve his eyes out of their sockets.

Three seconds.

In this time, Sage’s mouth ate a Mana Shield, breaking teeth—it’s actually pretty hard to get the neck, practically, Alex observed curiously. He’s bobbing too much as he runs.

Seconds which ended with Sage almost getting blinded by a Mana Spear, stumbling, glancing upward, and seeing a Force-backed Shield flying downward at his head; Sage lunged, and it crushed only his left leg.

In the time it took to free it, more blood spilled.

Those blood gems on his armor are weakening, he’s starting to tap his energy reserves, Alex realized.

Sage’s entire body started to leak a violent, bloody energy; it was like he’d shifted into a second gear. All his physical attributes started climbing drastically.

Except Alex had also had a series of realizations.

Sage was a very opportunistic and instinctive fighter.

His abilities were obviously very dependent on exploiting weaknesses, bleeding whoever he was facing and feeding off his victim’s vital energies, and gaining strength as he went. He was an ambush predator, relying on surprise and panic.

And especially as his bloody aura grew, as his energies grew, he was sheer aggression; his physical attributes were easily starting to eclipse Alex’s own, at rest.

Except his physical attributes were never truly the issue. They were always actually quite decent; they weren’t what was allowing Alex to strike him.

You’re far too instinctive. And your entire fighting style’s just, instinctive sadism? It requires that I flinch and break mentally, first.

Sage cried out, flying back, before a force-backed Mana Shield launched him several feet into the air—

Use your brain?

Six Mana Spears sliced into Sage mid-air. A second Mana Shield flew at Sage, as if to volley him for another strike; Sage barely managed to stab it with his spear, deflecting himself back down.

Another few exchanges went by, and the walkways, including all the O2 Guilders, were silent.

He’s getting physically stronger, even now, but he’s also getting bloodlusted—it’s animalistic, almost? He’s getting less rational as that aura increases.

“I’LL KILL YOU!” screamed Sage.

All it is is mindless aggression.

It’s—

It’s an utterly unserious fighting style.

As Alex watched, Sage’s aura was reaching its zenith.

And Sage managed, at his peak, a charge which nearly matched Caide’s for speed, but—again, his speed was never the issue, was it?

A series of Mana Shields, perfectly timed and placed, crippled Sage’s momentum before walling  Sage utterly. As Sage was falling back, his bloodshot, cracked eyes were wide; his bloody pupils met Alex’s impassive, cold eyes.

Alex wasn’t certain what he saw, but it seemed to shock Sage.

Maybe he was seeing in Alex’s eyes a certain realization.

Because a final realization had struck Alex, one which was very, very bad for Alex to realize, when it came to Sage’s chances in this fight.

Fuck…

I—

I can do whatever the fuck I want with you.

Can’t I?

It was perhaps one of the most dangerous things to happen to a person in a fight; when your opponent loses all respect for you.

I can just—

Alex’s eyes brightened, breath quickening, ideas almost visible in his pupils.

Alex unleashed.

We’re really ranked the same, you and I?

Is this your ‘genius’?

***

Blood splattered the station.

Alex wiped his blade with a handkerchief from his Interspatial Ring, inspecting the reflective material; in the end he’d had to use it, once.

It was just a terrible matchup, Alex recognized. If you need a weakness, if you need to get any bleed stacking going, and your enemy doesn’t just panic, and give it to you…

He fights like—

Like, well—it’s his first week fighting.

In that light, actually, it did make sense. He’s just a beginner, he’ll learn.

It’s—well, it’s like a prodigy that’s never been tested before, in all his weeks fighting. It’s kind of expected he’d perform like this?

Alex turned his eyes on Sage’s bloodied form, lips pursed. Sage was still on his knees, his hair matted to his face with blood; his head was hung.

He recalled Sage’s eyes.

I might’ve broken his spirit by the end.

He seemed scared to charge, like even he didn’t believe he’d actually land—you can’t charge, while also flinching and bracing for the reprisal.

He glanced across at the O2 Guilders, and was surprised.

Why are they looking at me like that?

They look—kind of scared?

And after glancing around, Alex realized why.

Alex was facing a blood lacerating specialist, and almost the entire station was splattered with lurid red blood.

Except the area at the center, where Alex was standing, was pristine.

As Sage died, bleeding out, not a single drop of blood stained Alex’s gear, and not a single drop of blood was his own.

Alex watched as Sage fell, and thought—

This is kind of sick, I shouldn’t do this.

Alex felt kind of bad; but he also recalled how Sage’s ranking changed after he finished his last fight. That’s the optics game, I guess. And if I've already decided to play it...

And, well, you shouldn’t give it, if you can’t take it.

Call it karma.

As Sage’s bloodied, bleeding body hit the ground, Alex turned, and bowed slightly to the evaluators’ pane of glass.
