Veronica looked upward toward the ceiling. She wasn’t looking at anything specific, but she had grown used to Sage speaking as if she were watching over her.

Her mouth was hanging wide open.

“What… do you mean I get stronger by killing demons?”

[With every demon’s death, their demonic energy dissipates, but a trace amount always lingers unless purified. When slaying a demon, you can absorb their demonic energy into your demon core. Any energy you do not wish to keep or convert into mana can instead be converted into Extra Processing Power, or EXP for short. Reaching certain thresholds will allow me to finish and deliver new upgrades. I have already planned out the first six upgrades in sequence, but I require demonic energy to complete them.]

“Like… the ascended body perk?”

[Like the ascended body perk.]

Veronica remained silent for a moment.

New perks and upgrades were more than welcome. The ascended body perk, which allowed her to mimic an ascension mage and bolster her strength and speed, was an incredible power. Flow State was the other perk, allowing her to react faster. In its enhanced state, it even let her respond to any attack immediately, almost to the point of foresight.

But… if she could get more upgrades like that, all just for killing demons, then was there any better news she could hear?

“What about demonic attacks? Can I directly nullify them now without a barrier?”

[Attacks made by demons, along with absorbing their energy while alive, will not be possible. Their energy is in too unstable and chaotic of a state. Only when a demon is dead does their energy stabilize, which is when you are able to absorb it.]

“Damn…” she said. “And here I thought you basically just made me invincible to demons. If that were the case, then I can’t even imagine the look on Xertal’s face when I finally kick his—”

“Veronica!” Gwen called from outside, down the hall.

She looked up just as Gwen reached the door and opened it. “Veronica. We have some visitors on the deck.”

Veronica climbed the stairs toward the airship’s deck with Gwen. Nasha was already standing there, waiting, still wearing an apron. In front of her were three cloaked figures that Veronica recognized instantly.

The representatives of Archmage Ballamus.

“Veronica Everwells,” the middle one said as she approached.

“Lucian,” Veronica said calmly.

The man looked at her for a moment. Then he pulled his hood down.

Veronica studied him. He had neatly kept dark hair, sharp facial features, and straight posture reminiscent of an uptight professor. He appeared to be in his mid-to-late thirties, which made sense if this was him fifteen years ago. If she hadn’t known any better, the Lucian in front of her could have been the son of the one she knew in the future.

“I’m curious how you know my name,” he said.

“I know many things,” Veronica said with a friendly smile.

The two mages behind Lucian shifted slightly. Both of them also pulled back their hoods, seeing as Lucian had done the same. One was a woman in her twenties with sharp eyes and a calm expression, the studious type, if Veronica had to guess. The other was a young man who looked serious, as if he took his title as Lucian’s junior and a representative far too seriously.

That seriousness only lasted until his gaze began flicking toward Nasha.

She, of course, was still wearing the apron from preparing breakfast with Gwen, resting one hand on her hip as if she wanted this meeting to end quickly so she could go back and practice. The moment she noticed him staring, she gave him a light smile. Not a large one, barely even a proper one, but a polite one.

That was enough to make the man’s eyes widen a fraction before he quickly averted his gaze, as if embarrassed.

Nasha’s own smile deepened, clearly happy to tease.

Veronica noticed and watched all of this, of course, but decided not to comment. She’d felt the same pull inside the Crimson Court. She couldn’t blame the guy.

“So?” Veronica asked. “What’s the reason for your visit today, Lucian?”

Lucian studied her. Veronica could almost read his thoughts: Who is this woman? Why is she calling me so casually? More importantly, how does she know my name?

“I’ve come to make sure everything is going well for you, Miss Everwells.”

“Just a checkup?” she asked, raising a brow.

“Yes,” he replied. “My assistants and I will be returning to the Mage Association today.”

“Already?”

He nodded. “Our main purpose in visiting Maftem was Hugh’s request.”

A look of understanding washed over Veronica’s face. “I see. So that’s why you stood up for my notes.”

“It was a request, after all.”

“I requested for the Mage Association to delay the release of the notes. I wasn’t expecting you to come personally.”

“Did it not cross your mind that you might make several enemies with the stunt you pulled?” he asked, almost accusingly.

Veronica crossed her arms, brushing off the accusation. “Of course I did. I just wasn’t expecting to draw that attention during the auction. It’s all that old busybody with the white beard’s fault.”

She looked at him directly. “Of course. What idiot would dare cause trouble against a mage backed by an archmage?”

“That may be true, but those conditions will no longer apply once you leave these walls. Not to mention, we will be taking our leave today. While Archmage Ballamus is indebted to you, there is only so much he can do. Now that you’ve saved his sister, he must ensure she remains in good health.”

“That’s fine,” she said. “I don’t expect him to protect me forever. I just hope he sees me as a friend in the future and helps me with something when I need it.”

Lucian raised a brow. “You require another favor?”

“What? You think buying me an airship is enough for saving his sister?” Veronica said, narrowing her eyes as she pointed at him lazily with one finger. “Even if you gave the top researcher a hundred million vix, I doubt they could make you a cure in ten years.”

His eyes widened at that. Not because of what she said, but because of her composure and the surprising sharpness of her reply.

He quickly composed himself. “Speaking of which, how do you want to handle the cure for nether-grasping disease?”

Off to the side, Veronica spotted Gwen speaking to the female mage behind him. On the other side, Nasha was surprisingly talking to the shy-looking mage. Somehow, she had already broken him out of his shell.

“Since I’ve shared it with the Mage Association like this, I’ll go ahead and claim ownership with commission on it. Talk to Galland if you want to know the finer details,” she said.

“Right. You’ve opened a merchant company of your own, co-founded with another. Zenith, it was called?”

She nodded. “It’ll be one of the biggest merchant companies in all of Vitian in the future. So, word of advice, try to make some connections to it while you can,” she said with a smile.

Lucian chuckled despite his reserved composure. “When you say it with that much confidence, it almost feels like a promise rather than a dream.”

Veronica stayed silent and shrugged, not letting out any other details.

“Very well. Given your contributions to Archmage Ballamus and the theory notes you’ve provided us, we’ve made considerable advances in spell compression over at the Arcanum Spire, which I happen to oversee.” His eyes narrowed. “Though, seeing as you know my name, I’m sure you already knew that as well.”

She nodded.

“Right, then. Because of that, you will not need to fulfill any Association obligations for the next six months. However, after that, we will expect you to complete some of them.”

“Don’t worry. I haven’t forgotten. I’ve just been busy,” she said.

He looked at her, as if half-questioning whether that was true. “Right.”

He stepped back, calling both of his juniors to his side. They stood behind him and straightened. Then all three of them pulled their hoods back up. They floated a few inches off the ground, but Lucian still had one final thing to say.

“Whatever you plan to do in the future, Veronica Everwells, I suggest you be careful. With your seemingly reckless nature and carefree attitude, I have no doubt that you’ll gather many enemies in time,” he said, his tone flat. “While you may have Archmage Ballamus’s protection now, there are many within the Mage Association itself who may vie for your knowledge or achievements. We can protect you to a certain extent, but not every corner will be safe. It’s not every day someone appears out of nowhere with a forged background and changes an entire field of magic, after all.”

Veronica raised a brow. “You knew I forged my identity?”

Beside her, Nasha slapped her forehead.

Veronica looked at her, then turned back to Lucian.

“No, I didn’t,” Lucian said, almost jovial. “That’s good to know, however.”

Veronica let out something between a groan and a sigh.

“It’s been nice meeting you, Miss Everwells,” Lucian said.

She nodded. “Likewise.”

Lucian turned to his juniors. “Let’s go.”

The three mages rose from the deck and left together. Their cloaks fluttered as they passed the railing, and slowly, they disappeared toward the east side of the city, where Maftem’s warp portal was located.

“Sounds like you have a few enemies in the Mage Association now,” Nasha said, turning to face her.

Veronica nodded. “Most likely. After all, no one likes a rising star, especially if they’re young,” Veronica said flatly. “I know that all too well.”

“What’s the plan now?” Gwen asked, running up to them after waving the mages off. “Are we focusing on Avalon?”

“Hm, maybe,” Veronica said. “It’s not hard, but it’ll take some time to gather all the remaining materials. It would be nice to get a staff made in the meantime.”

Among the things brought onto the ship, the large chunk of raw starmetal ore was resting in one of Mirabelle’s storage rooms. The problem was finding someone who could refine the metal. Unlike the future, she didn’t have connections with the King, nor could she post a recruitment notice across all of Vitian.

“You girls remember that dwarf, right?” Veronica asked.

“The one in a feud with the city’s commerce groups?” Gwen said.

Nasha crossed her arms over her apron. “You planning to see if he’ll be able to do it?”

Veronica nodded. “He must have wanted the starmetal for some reason. If he’s able to forge with it, or if someone he knows can, then it’s all the same. He’s someone I need to meet. I’m sure he’ll be happy to see me.”

“There’s still one more thing missing for opening the portal to Avalon, right?” Gwen asked. “You mentioned we need elven magic to open the portal. You only know Vitian-style magic, so we have to recruit someone. And if it’s only temporary, then we’ll need to take them with us when we travel into Avalon. It’ll have to be someone trustworthy.”

“That pushes guild commissions off the table,” Nasha added. “It’s the quickest way to get an elven adventurer with magic like that, but they won’t be trustworthy at all if we take them into a place filled with treasure.”

“Actually, that won’t be necessary,” Veronica said.

They both turned to face her.

“Why’s that?” Nasha asked.

Veronica lifted a palm. She turned her consciousness inward, feeling the space inside her core room. Three cores. One, not like the rest. She pulled on it.

Nasha’s and Gwen’s eyes widened as a small black flame appeared atop her hand. It didn’t look like a regular discolored flame. No, it moved almost chaotically, as if it were sparking and igniting all at once, barely able to remain steady in her palm.

“Morgan’s book didn’t say we only needed elven magic,” she said, eyes fixed on the black flame in her palm. “Demonic works too.”
