Cascading Threads was Luke's only real answer to massing hordes of monsters. With it, he could reach out with Threads of Mana and touch one monster, then jump over to the next, and the next, and so on. The problem was that it required a ton of mana. So far, his solution to that had been to take that mana from his targets. That wasn't an option with demons, since their brand of mana burned him from the inside out. It wasn't a comfortable experience. One good thing about having friends was their ability to shore up his own particular weaknesses.

Curtis and Hannah were both beasts in the arena of area of effect skills. Hannah's shadow had stretched to encompass the entirety of the street, leaving no safe path for those approaching. Lemira rode over it without issue, but the lesser demons didn't get the same free pass. Instead, their screeching forms fell into the shadow and disappeared. Hundreds of them fell in as the wave of thousands pushed them forward through sheer momentum. Those at the front saw what happened to their ilk and, through some bestial intelligence, understood that they should stop, but by then it was too late. Far too late.

They varied a little in shape and size, which was interesting. After a few hundred had fallen in, the lesser demons tended to be bigger. Some leapt incredible distances but still fell into Hannah's pit of shadow. A few others charged across the face of the buildings before falling in. When one lesser demon with wings appeared, they couldn't deny the fact staring them in the face any longer.

"They're evolving," Luke said, shaking his head at the impossibility of it.

Ray nodded, swallowing hard. "In real time."

"They're still stupid," Curtis noted, using an orb of darkness to strike at the flying demon, sending it into the shadowy maws below. "They haven't tried to go around us."

"Don't give them ideas," Hannah said. She looked pale, and her breaths were ragged.

"You okay?" Luke asked, walking up to put an arm around her shoulders. Curtis and Ray both gave him strange looks, but he ignored them.

"There are so many of them. I'm starting to feel a little full."

Lemira came hurrying up to them. "You're eating them?"

Hannah gave the newcomer a weak smile. "Kind of, but not really. It's hard to explain."

"More of them have wings," Curtis noted, slapping two more out of the air.

"Friends!" Nash shouted from over at the edge where street met shadow. "Seems like the demons are endless!"

Sam and Mas readied themselves, streams of red reaching from their shoulders like eager, vampiric silly string.

At that moment, their guildmistress confirmed Nash's fears.

Milla (Guild): "I've received updated reports from our scouts and other guilds. More rifts are opening up, and so far, the demons just keep coming. Focusing on the lesser demons will drain us of energy until we have nothing left."

Milla (Guild): "This means we need to shift the objective. A number of larger demons have appeared. After conferring with a source who has some experience with system events, we now believe that defeating these larger demons will be the path to successfully completing the event."

Milla (Guild): "Report to me if you wish to hunt bigger prey. If not, you should return to guild headquarters, where I can protect you."

Milla (Guild): "I have reached out to the new US Integrated Army as well as the DIA, but no help is coming. It's on us to beat these bastards back!"

"Hell yeah!" Nash shouted.

Luke glanced over at Lemira. It didn't take a genius to figure out who the source of Milla's information was. "The larger demons, huh?"

She squirmed a little, and her nod didn't look very confident. "That might be it, but I can't be sure."

"Guess it's the best option we've got."

Lemira pointed to the dead demon on the ground. "Did you get anything from Arezak?"

He hadn't thought to check. Rookie mistake. Walking over to loot the corpse, he did indeed find something interesting. Taking it out as he stood back up, he raised it into the air so everyone could see.

"What is it?" Ray asked.

Luke turned it this way and that, then shrugged. "No idea. Looks like a garnet crystal. Hold on."

Inspecting items didn't work outside of dungeons, except for those in his inventory. Putting it in there, he found an answer. Not a good answer, but an answer nonetheless.

Demonic Crystal of Pride: A physical representation of sin.

Telling the others about the description resulted in a lot of head-shaking and expressions that said they didn't have a clue.

"So are demons, like, actual demons?" Ray asked after a moment as Curtis and Hannah turned back to the onslaught of lesser demons.

"What do you mean?" Lemira asked.

"We don't have time for this," Luke said. "I'm heading out to find more of these crystals. Milla is right. Killing the small fry won't do a thing if they just keep coming."

A lot of members of The Band had turned up at that point, but most continued on to the plaque and disappeared into guild headquarters. Luke didn't blame them.

Milla: "Luke! Are you heading out or what? Phoenix Phalanx took down theirs and found a crystal of some sort. Go gather more of them!"

Luke: "Aye, aye, cap'n! Do you have the location of one?"

His sister provided the information he needed. A group of Integrated from another guild was having trouble with one not too far away and had requested help.

"I'm dropping the shadow!" Hannah shouted. "Get ready to run!"

By that time, most lesser demons were able to make it over the shadow-pit, but it still served to slow them down somewhat. Tentacles of shadow reached out of the ground to grab demons, but it was far from enough.

"I'll give us a little breathing room," Curtis said, and the massive amounts of darkness he'd gathered began to spin faster and faster, turning the sky into a patchwork of confusing patterns twisting in on themselves, flowing like rivers, falling like droplets, or pooling on surfaces, no matter if they were horizontal or vertical.

Whatever Curtis was up to, it was going to be big, considering the build-up. Quite a few members of The Band fought alongside Nash, Sam, and Mas, keeping the lesser demons at bay. Emma joined in as well, having been rescued off the top of that burning building, and her bow was a huge help.

Since he had a moment to himself, Luke dove into his inventory to check what new options appeared when he reached Level 40. First, he added all five available attribute points to Focus. The skills, though, were where the new level threshold made itself known. The same skills as before were still available, but some notable new additions had been mixed in.

Threads of Life [Passive]: The mere presence of the Lifeweaver's tools is enough to bring health to those without.

Will of the Weaver [Passive]: Make those who dare clash with the Lifeweaver tremble under the weight of their will.

Reject Intrusion [Active. Mana Cost: Moderate]: Experience in delving into others has given the Lifeweaver knowledge in the act of keeping others out.

Weave Integration [Passive]: Allows for greater control in all aspects regarding weave manipulation and understanding.

Explosive Reach [Active. Mana Cost: Extreme]: The Lifeweaver may extend their reach for a moment in time.

Quite a few new and interesting options, and that wasn't all. Several core skills could rank up.

Rank up skills available:

Threads of Mana

Needle of Life

The Healer's Staff

Weaver's Rupture

With five available skill points, Luke could, for the first time ever, not pick every skill and rank-up he wanted. What a pain. Threads of Life would be a minor boost with some sort of healing, he figured. That, he could pass up for the time being. Will of the Weaver, though, was just what he'd been looking for, considering his most recent fight. The description was as vague as most skills', but any boost regarding will would be welcome, since that would be his most potent weapon going forward, as well as a great help with healing.

Reject Intrusion sounded a lot like Weaveguard, which was supposed to shield him from outside influence. Perhaps this new skill was what he needed if the other failed? Not having either of them was beginning to feel like an oversight. Weave Integration was next. Luke couldn't think of anything other than help him with Manipulate Thread, locating echoes, and seeing the weave. Not turning up in places, vomiting blood, and feeling like he was about to die would be a neat improvement. That left Explosive Reach as the final new skill. This one, at least, explained its own effect. More reach by spending a ton of mana. In one word: awesome.

Curtis's skill reached its apex. When he'd last shown it, the whole thing failed and collapsed. Not this time. Hannah withdrew the shadow, looking like she was about to vomit, and Curtis made his move. The patches of darkness separated into pieces small as raindrops, then showered over the massive horde of still coming lesser demons. Luke watched the first drop touch a monster and saw it freeze in place. As he watched, the lesser demon darkened to a pitch black, turning into darkness. That darkness rose from the ground and turned into new droplets. Each monster touched turned into a new source of darkness, multiplying the effect a hundredfold.

"It'll keep going for a minute or so," Curtis said between heaving breaths.

"Let's go," Luke said. He waved for everyone to follow, but then saw Nash on the ground, rolling around and clutching his leg. Entranced by the possibilities of his new skills, Luke had missed someone in need of healing. That wasn't great. Seeing Nash's reaction when he regrew a leg in seconds was, however, pretty great. After that, Luke's former party leader decided he would stay behind.

In the end, it was just Luke, Curtis, Hannah, Ray, and Lemira who took up the hunt for what Luke now thought of as greater demons. If that was what they needed to complete the system event, then that was what they would do.

As they made their way to the location Milla had indicated, Luke selected the new skills he wanted, as well as rank-ups.

Will of the Weaver.

Weave Integration.

Explosive Reach.

After selecting those three, it was time to rank up two of his core skills. To his surprise, ranking up Threads of Mana gave him options to choose from:

Silent Threads of Mana: Your threads are no longer visible to the naked eye.

Deadly Threads of Mana: Your threads can now inflict mana-sickness on your target, corrupting their mana channels for permanent effect.

Life-Giving Threads of Mana: The mana cost of your healing is reduced.

Spellweaver’s Threads of Mana: Form the weave and will any spell or skill into existence. With this option, Threads of Mana will lose their capacity to heal, and all healing-associated skills will be lost. Class name will change to Spellweaver.

Integrated's Threads of Mana: Your threads are one with the system. Allows for greater control and increased capacity to thread the spaces between.

Destroyer's Threads of Mana: Greatly enhance the destructive nature of your threads by harnessing the weave's power.

This was... a lot. Creating his own spells or increasing the threads' ability to destroy were both interesting options that seemed to beckon him with the promise of power, but Luke knew right away that he would not pick either option. He was a Lifeweaver through and through. Removing the option to heal was too heavy a price to pay for any amount of power. While he might spend almost as much time fighting as healing these days, he still saw himself as a healer first. That wouldn't change.

Not wanting to dwell, he made his pick and continued to Needle of Life.

Having invisible threads would help a lot, no matter what he did, or perhaps he should pick the one that would allow for even more healing, considering his previous point, but they weren't the right choices either. It stood out to him, screaming for Luke to pick it up. Integrated's Threads of Mana didn't seem like much compared to some of the effects offered by the other skill variations, but Luke's gut told him to pick it. Anything that brought him closer to The Greater System and its mysteries would prove a wise choice. He just knew it.

That left Needle of Life. Just like with Threads of Mana, this skill, too, offered class-defining changes. This time, the choices were fewer, but perhaps even more decisive.

Needle of Death: Relinquish the right to mend wounds and embrace the power to create them.

Needle of Life-Giving: Enhance the Needle's ability to heal. Diminishes the destructive ability of the skill.

Malleable Needle of Life: The shape of the Needle will conform to your will.

The first question that popped into Luke's mind was regarding what would happen if you mixed and matched the two rank-ups. Would selecting the better healing Needle and then the option to remove all healing skills to allow for casting spells remove it? The risk of destroying your build and thus your future wasn't negligible with these sorts of options. He shuddered at the thought as he picked the third option. Malleable Needle of Life would let him use the skill as a weapon to a greater degree when necessary, without taking away his ability to heal. Luke wanted to heal and to do harm. This meant the choice was already made for him, since he couldn't pick one of the others without relinquishing one of the aspects, and that was unacceptable.

When he leveled up next time, he would go for one of the skills he'd rejected before. Strengthen Thread. If he was going to use his threads for fighting even more going forward, making them stronger was a priority. Perhaps even the whip skill might become useful. All of a sudden, Luke was hurting for skill points.

He would gain experience using the new skills and the improved ones in the next fight. Judging by what Milla told him, he'd need every edge he could get, even when surrounded by his friends. As they ran, Luke glanced up at the broken window where his staff lay discarded, and he sent a mental note of farewell to the weapon he'd attempted to use so many times. The weapon he'd failed to use so many times.
