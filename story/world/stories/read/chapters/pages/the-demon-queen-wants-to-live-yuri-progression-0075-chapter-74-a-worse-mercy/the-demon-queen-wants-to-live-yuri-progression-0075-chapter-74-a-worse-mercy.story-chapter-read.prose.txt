I took a deep breath. My choice was made. I took back the great ball of Ruin. When I used it again, I used only thin tendrils of the Requiem, and with them, I searched inside the girl.

Most of the corruption was rooted inside the girl's Sigil. And from there, it branched inward and upward into the rest of her. Even as I looked at it, it grew and spread. I pressed a thread of Ruin against the nearest branch. I planned to erase it with the Requiem, as I had before. My power touched the corruption and…the power pushed back.

I froze. Nothing had ever pushed back against the Ending before. The girl's Lightning, despite its higher stage, had done nothing. Something in me that had never been afraid took a step toward being so.

The impressions came, and I did not ignore them.

The first impression was the image of a stance that had been corrected over many long and patient months. A foot placed a half-step farther than it should have been. An older hand corrected that flaw, and many others. And each time that hand did it patiently. And the child whose stance was always corrected one day declared that someday she would be as strong as he was. That someday she would make him proud. He had looked at her, and he had said that he waited for that day.

Then, one day, healers filed out of a white room that smelled of herbs, one after another, and each of them had their heads hung low. He had never risen again.

I forced the impressions away. A small part of me could not help but wonder if my own generals had carried wounds like these for me, in some fashion.

The strange corruption began to move through the Ruin's black. I blinked, certain that I was mistaken. I was not. It was creeping up along the lines of Ruin and towards my own hand.

There was no time to call for Ash. Perhaps her Sever might have found some way to choose between the wrongness and the mark, if the vow even let her inside this ring. There were only seconds left to act. And so I decided that I would not part the corruption from the mark. I would take the mark.

I channelled the Ruin down to the root. To the place where the branches of the Sigil grew out from the arm and into the body. Where they were thickest, I pushed hardest. The corruption fought me for every inch, and I gave it none. I burned.

I erased the Sigil. All of it. Root and branch and the mark it had grown from.

Lena screamed. Blood came up out of her, and her whole body spasmed beneath my hands.

When it was done, I inspected myself for even a hint of that wrongness. It was not on my body, and I felt it in none of my mana channels. I found nothing.

With the root of darkness gone, the rest was simple. I reached with my left hand, and I poured white fire into the empty place the Ending had left.

The mana channels came back slowly. The mark did not. They reformed, one after another, thin and pale. And with the Cradle came its impressions. The Cradle showed me the same things the Requiem had. I had thought healing would show me something kinder.

The black lines receded across her skin, one at a time, retreating from her jaw, her throat, her collarbone. And where the Sigil had been there was now only a ghost of a mark in branchings so dim and pale I had to look to find them at all.

She looked almost like Corin before Ash and I had tended to him. I finished, and I fell to my knees.

That had taken far more of me than I had expected it to. I looked at my forearms. The two Lines stared back at me, black and white, faint at the edges and just slightly bent. They had branched into nothing at all. I had been certain that I would look down and find two Sigils where the Lines had been.

What more do you want from me? I asked them. Neither mark answered. I sighed.

Lena's eyes were open. I had not noticed them open. She was staring up at the false sky of the Arena, and her chest rose and fell, and her skin still had a sickly cast to it, but nothing like what it had been.

"I can't feel it," she whispered.

I looked at her. She brought one arm up across her eyes, and she began to cry, just faintly. I did not understand at first. I had healed her. I had done a thing that, by any measure, deserved-

"I can't feel the mark anymore. What…what did you do?" Her voice cracked at the edges.

I had known, even as I did it, just what I had been doing. The girl had asked me for an ending. What I had given her instead might be the worse of the two.

"I did what I had to," I said. "And it was better than you deserved, for the things you have done."

She was quiet for a time. Then, so low I nearly did not catch it, she said, "Guess he'll never see now." Her body twisted. "Not that he would have anyway."

I did not understand all of what she was saying, but I understood more of it now than I once might have. I said nothing. For there was nothing that I could give her, and I did not know if she deserved the giving. So instead, I asked the question that mattered.

"Where did you acquire it, girl?" I asked. "That Essence Heart. Who gave it to you?"

"I don't remember."

I stared at her. "That is a graceless lie, girl. Have some dignity in defeat at the very least."

"I don't remember!" she said, and there was pain in her eyes. Her face strained, and then she flinched. Her eyes, when they found mine again, held too much to be a lie.

"Very well," I said. She truly did not know then.

"I yield," she said. There was a hollowness to her voice now. Something in the air shifted. I could not have said what.

The Elf was in the ring and at the girl's side faster than I could see her move. "Step back," she hissed.

I stepped back. She gathered Lena up as though the girl weighed nothing. And as she lifted her, Lena tried to look at me. I watched her try. Her gaze came toward me and slid off. I saw the strain in the girl's neck, and it did not matter. She was carried the length of the ring and out of it, and not once did her eyes find me. The vow had been literal then, rather than interpretive.

The Archon stepped into the ring then. He looked at my bare forearms, at the black and the white running down them, plain for any eye to see. Then he looked at the floor.

I followed his gaze. A single thin tendril of creeping black writhed on the pale stone. And then it thinned, and then it evaporated into nothing at all. He looked at the empty place where it had been for a long time.

"So this is what you chose," he said, meeting my gaze.

"It is."

"It will cause complications," he said quietly. "Many of them, I suspect. Many you don't know, more still that I cannot imagine."

"I had expected it might."

The old man was silent for a moment. "I told you once that you might have years, and then that you might have months." He looked out at the stands, at the thousand faces. "I fear you may have only days now."

On one side of the ring, near its very edge, were the two bracers. The Archon regarded them, walked forward, and slowly scooped them up. He slipped them inside his robes before he sighed.

"I had thought," I said, "that you would be happier with this arrangement."

"And why is that?"

The demonbloods were standing. All along the stands, they were standing. Just as they had for Edda, not very long ago. But now, more than just standing, many of them were cheering. The demonbloods that day had stood as if standing was all they could do. These had found it in themselves to do something more. And it wasn't just the demonbloods either. Many of the humans were cheering right alongside them. Not all of them, but more of them than I would have expected.

Ren was on her feet with her fists in the air. Malachor was standing, and the half-demon was so tall that I could not see those standing in the row above him. His right head was cheering. Even his left head had the trace of a smile. The frost-haired girl from the arena was clapping.

A Hero's words came to me then. In the infirmary, Ash had said something incredibly naive. Words truly fit for a Hero. Be so undeniably good that the lies cannot hold. Was this what she had meant? I did not think that sparing one girl had moved this crowd. But it had been moved, still.

"They are doing more than standing now," I said. "Do you see?"

The Archon was quiet for a moment. "So they are."

The Archon followed my gaze, his eyes sweeping over the stands. And in that moment, the old man looked a little younger than he had before the duel had begun. The Archon raised one hand, and when he spoke, his voice carried to every seat in the hall. "The winner of this duel," he said, "is Lysanthia." The crowd grew louder. All of it thanks to the demonbloods.

"Rest, for today," he said to me. "I suspect your time in this Academy is… limited. But that is tomorrow's concern. Whatever comes will wait for tomorrow at the least."

I did not mind the dismissal. I turned and walked towards the doors, idly glancing at the stands as I went. The Marquis's podium was empty. Not that I had been trying to find him. For in the western tiers sat Ilyasviel. Our eyes met over the distance between us, and even from here, I was certain that I saw her give me the faintest of nods. I turned and walked without returning it.

A thousand gazes followed my slow walk to the doors. As they often had, during my long life. These ones felt different, in some fashion.

I stepped through the doors and left all those gazes behind. For all of three steps, before something crashed into me. I fell to the floor, and Ash fell on top of me. "You're okay," she said. One of her hands pressed against my chest, directly over where the Ember Core was. She pulled it away two seconds later. She could have done so earlier.

Sol let out a chirp of tremendous volume from atop Ash's head. She craned her neck forward, and in so doing, lost her balance. She wobbled, chirped again, and fell squarely on my forehead.

I felt briefly annoyed. Then I sighed. "Did you run here?" I asked.

"Something like that," Ash said. She began to raise herself off of me, and the color came up faint in her cheeks. "Err. Sorry."

"Ash," I said. "The corruption felt different from the others. When I reached into the girl, it reached back toward me, somehow."

She looked at me. "What do you mean?"

"It followed the Ruin. It tried to use my own power to reach me. My Ruin did not instantly erase it." I paused. "We must learn more. The girl did not know where the Essence Heart came from."

Ash slowly nodded. Her mouth opened to say something.

"Holy shit, they're on t-"

I heard the voice from down the corridor. I looked past Ash in time to see a crop of red hair duck behind a corner.

"Ren, you were too loud!" Edda's voice carried and only just.

"I mean- look at them!" Ren said.

I sighed. I looked back at Ash. She was framed in the light from the doorway. The sight was not displeasing. It was not displeasing in the slightest. "But today, Ash," I said. "Today I am simply going to rest."

Sol chirped her agreement. This time, I was sure it was agreement.
