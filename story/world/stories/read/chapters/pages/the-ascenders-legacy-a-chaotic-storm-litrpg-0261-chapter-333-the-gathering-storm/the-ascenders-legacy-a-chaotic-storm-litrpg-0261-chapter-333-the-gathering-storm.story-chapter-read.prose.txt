High-Elder Torvas glanced at the lanky man. "No, Calamity Rowan. We are not."

The agents had first arrived a few days ago, carrying missives of their alliance. There was no prospect of betrayal from them—not yet. Their goals were, after all, aligned.

But that alliance was fickle.

As much as these agents and their sects were ready to defend the Storm Sect's rise, they also wouldn't hesitate to pull back in the event of their downfall.

They were unreliable allies at best.

But they had numbers, especially the Wind and Water sects. The Lightning Sect had lesser numbers, but they were more brethren than allies. A sub-sect, if one will.

But despite the situation, it wasn't all bad. With the Origin Plane's continuous expansion, they had pulled far more allies than they'd thought they would. The Sect of Entropy and Force was a surprising addition. The Sect of Shadow had also sent word of alliance—they were a tricky bunch, and the Storm Sect wasn't counting on them until they showed up.

The other sects also muttered their alliances, and a few minutes later, they all returned to their individual sects, leaving the High Elders and Luseph alone in the hall.

"This is bad, Torvas," High-Elder Tharn, the fifth seat, stated. "We had no plan aside from leveraging our strength and numbers. But with the increasing number of our opponents, that plan is no longer viable."

"You don't think I know that, Tharn?"

"Calm, please." High-Elder Velora sighed. "We can't afford a dispute right now. We need a plan, and we need it fast."

"What is this good news?" Zarxan asked.

Luseph glanced at Zarxan and then said, "Just yesterday when I took Aodhán to the Elder Pavilion, he had three paths and a budding fourth warring for his spirit. Today, however, he has only one. He has merged his path. And he calls it the Path of Ruinous Authority."

For a moment, the High Elders were too stunned to speak, and then, one after the other, they smiled.

But High-Elder Zarxan didn't.

Because unlike the others, he knew Aodhán wasn't planning to stay long enough within the sect to fulfill that potential. Whatever advantage they hoped to gain from Aodhán's merged path would only come when Aodhán created his Supremacy and became a Calamity.

And he wanted to leave at the hundredth tier. Which meant if this war was to be of any true benefit, he had to find a way to convince Aodhán to stay. To leave his family back in Lutia and build a life for himself here.

He had to convince Aodhán to choose between his old and new family.

And he feared Aodhán would choose the former.

Two days passed in a blur of training, rune scripting, and item forging for Aodhán.

News of the agents had spread through the whole sect, and now, as Aodhán returned home that evening after hours of item creation with Elder Gumi, all he could see everywhere was preparation for war.

It had begun on the day the agents left, but it got worse every day, even in the special sect where their population was less than five dozen in total—underlings and elders included.

But the sect wasn't the only one preparing for war, if Aodhán was to be honest.

In the last two days, he had delved two different rifts—fire and nature. And this time, Aodhán hadn't forgotten to gather as many cores as he could. He'd sold off the majority to a core pavilion in exchange for sect points, but he'd spent the rest on himself, pushing himself up four tiers.

Tier: 42—19.36%

It wasn't much, but four tiers at once was dramatic even for him. But the benefits had been undeniable.

Not only had his control increased drastically with each advancement, but his spirit had also shifted—seemingly becoming more solid within him. That tangibility manifested in the potency of his aura, which he had tested multiple times against Sophran, Seamus, and Mirelle, who had only recently come out from under the effects of the twisted effect.

And the results had been both terrifying and enlightening.

Against Seamus and Mirelle, the effect of his aura manifested in the same way it had against Darien—an overwhelming control that made it incredibly easy to simply hijack their skills and turn them against their wielders.

It wasn't even a battle of wills.

He could simply say no, and their skills suddenly became his. Beams of lightning simply refused to attack, and spears turned against their wielders.

It was insane—the authority his path granted him, and Aodhán feared it might even alienate him.

Fortunately, it wasn't quite so effective against an opponent like Sophran. Not just because of the tier difference between him and the other transmigrant, but because of the strength of his spirit, which refused to be so easily subdued.

Aodhán wasn't sad about that in the least. And when the day came that he could easily suppress Sophran, he had decided not to show it. Not because he didn't want to exercise his authority, but because he wanted to keep his friends, and lording his authority over them all wasn't the way to go.

Surprisingly, his path had only pushed the double inheritors to work harder, claiming that if they were so easily suppressed by someone more than a dozen tiers below them, then they weren't strong enough yet.

This desire for strength, and a counter to his aura, Aodhán was sure, had caused many of them to lock themselves in their cultivation rooms to meditate. Even Crixis.

He still hadn't absorbed his treasures yet, but every time Aodhán saw him, he was either meditating or reading, preparing his mind for the miracle rain. It made the sect feel lonely, but Aodhán didn't mind the loneliness one bit.

A war was looming, and everyone was training for it.

Sighing, Aodhán flexed his fingers, feeling a little stiff after all the pendants they'd forged in the last few hours alone. He arrived at his mansion a few minutes later, wanting to just take a long nap. However, he'd barely taken more than a few steps into his compound before his core sense picked up a gathering of cores within his living room.

He frowned at that, surprised they'd all gathered after spending the last few days meditating in their individual mansions. But it wasn't just the other specials who were gathered in his living room—it was their underlings too, meaning nearly a dozen people were gathered in his living room, waiting for him.

A spark of nervous tension flickered in his heart, but Aodhán pushed it aside firmly, walked up to the entrance, and pushed the door open, eyebrows climbing slightly when he took in their numbers with his eyes.

They all turned to glance at him immediately, the underlings bowing in respect while the other specials simply waved.

"Welcome, Aodhán," Seamus grinned from his position on a couch. "We've accosted your living room once again."

Aodhán scrunched his brows in humor. "I can see that. What's going on?"

This time, it was Sophran who responded. "We need your help, Aodhán. The truth is, all of us here, aside from Mirelle, would like a drop of the miracle rain. We would do it ourselves, but we know that means we won't be affected by it. Mirelle can't do it, and we don't have time—"

"So you want me to do it," Aodhán stated with a smile, his gaze turning to Crixis. "Are you ready to do this?"

Crixis nodded, and Aodhán's smile widened.

"Then we'll need a much larger seal room. Where can we get that?"

"The library," Sophran grinned. "You won't believe the number of times people gain seals within libraries."

Aodhán chuckled, his exhaustion vanishing at the sight of his friends, many of whom had spent the last two to three days gathering knowledge and insights about their paths.

Giving them the miracle rain now would be like breaking a dam of inspiration open.

And Aodhán couldn't wait to see what would happen.

"Are you all ready?"

Darien nodded. "Let's do this."

With a deep inhale, Aodhán raised both hands into the air and summoned a cloud of roiling storm into existence. The storm manifested with a loud boom of thunder, lightning arcing, and winds blowing.

The storm was ready.

And so was he.

Heat erupted within him as the quintessence of four seals was drained in a single motion, merging with the boiling pool of willpower and chaos within his spirit. But unlike before, he didn't lack control this time.

And so he kept pouring. And pouring…

More energy. More willpower. More chaos.

Then with a single activation of {Tempest of Transformation}, he channeled it all into the storm above.

Sophran went first, then Seamus, then Darien…

Gold flashed crimson and then a luminous amethyst color Aodhán had never seen before.

Aodhán had no idea what union the path of superiority and Authority would create, but it was sure to be powerful. And if Crixis was to survive the coming war, he would need that power.

The amethyst drop splashed against Crixis's forehead an instant later, and Mirelle shut the chamber immediately.

Then Elder Kudrov asked, "What… what now?"

Aodhán turned to look at the stunned woman, his heart racing wildly.

"Now we wait."
