Xavier floated in a vast sea of nothingness—he could not see, hear, feel, taste, or touch.

All he had was that sixth sense, his spiritual sense.

The instant he’d done this, he’d felt a massive surge of regret. Every other sense he’d singled out had been a natural sense, something he’d had with him since the moment he was born. And yes, it was true that he’d had his Immortal Soul lens since he was born—even if he’d never been able to access it—but that was different. Very different.

There was something deeply unnatural about being in this state. Something terrible and disorientating and frightening. He felt light. Airy. He felt like…

Nothing at all.

In fact, he was quite sure he felt as though he was dead.

This is what it must be like to be a spirit, without a body, just floating, only feeling through the spiritual sense…

But a spirit’s spiritual sense grew incredibly powerful, he knew, as the spirits he’d met—when their spiritual senses had been unrestricted—could see far more than they should have been able to see. Besides, soon enough, he would be a spirit. He would use Astral Projection to start spirit gathering. This was a sensation he would need to become used to.

The first thing Xavier did was scan his own body. He thought he wouldn’t be able to sense everything in his own body with the spiritual sense. That there would be much he was blind to—but it wasn’t like that at all.

Energy was in everything, and Xavier had infused a plentiful amount of refined Celestial Energy into his body at every opportunity. It was in every inch of skin, every muscle, every bone. It was in his claws, his eyes, his wings.

It was everywhere.

With his spiritual sense, he could see almost a perfect outline of himself. He wasn’t veiled to his own sense, after all, so nothing was concealed to him. He could feel the energy cycling through his body, too, and he adjusted that cycling here or there. He’d gone a long while without being able to feel his channels with his spiritual sense, and there were adjustments to be made.

Something told him there would be further adjustments when he switched all his senses back on. The way he’d been able to alter that spell so effectively, on pure instinct… it made him wonder just what else he could refine.

A lot, I imagine. Quite a lot.

Once he’d scanned his entire body, changing the flow of energy through his channels, even slightly tweaking the flow in his cores, he pushed his spiritual sense outward. Not specifically toward the wall—not yet. Rather, he pushed it far down the Dark Tunnel, toward whatever being he’d sensed down there.

Instantly, he sensed that same faint outline that he’d sensed before. It was farther away, as he’d retreated back to the start of the Void Dungeon when he’d decided to improve his senses, but it was there all the same. And, actually, the outline wasn’t quite as faint as it had been before, though he couldn’t feel much more than a dark, powerful presence.

Another Void Dungeon-created beast. A more powerful one than the Gorgotlin.

He would face it soon. He looked forward to that.

When he’d examined the beast as thoroughly as he could with his current level of skill, Xavier turned to the wall, to where he knew the outlines of the runes should be. Now that he was focusing only on his spiritual sense, he hoped he would gain the ability to see the strange runes through it.

He sat, cross-legged on the ground of the Dark Tunnel for a very long time, the full force of his Immortal Soul-lens enhanced spiritual sense on the cold stone wall where he’d sensed the runes in just about every other way. Yet no matter how hard he tried, he was blind to them.

Does that mean they don’t have energy?

His first thought, when he’d first realised what they were, was that they might somehow be void runes. But if they were void runes, he would be able to sense the Void Energy within them. He didn’t have trouble sensing Void Energy.

But he had been able to feel these things… these strange, rotten runes. They were there, he knew they were.

Why couldn’t he sense them with his spiritual sense?

Xavier sighed—not that he could feel the sigh in his current state. He’d spent enough time at this. There was a dungeon to explore. Beasts to kill. Refinements to be made. He couldn’t sit here staring at the wall forever.

He switched on all his other senses. There was no feeling of overwhelm. Not this time. Only an intense feeling of calm. Of rightness.

Though Xavier was feeling defeated on this matter, he wasn’t about to leave this spot without asking the Personal System about what he’d discovered.

I have no information about this.

The Personal System’s voice sounded flat. Flatter than usual, that was.

I want you to identify them.

I cannot discern anything you haven’t already. There is no useful information in my database available about these rotten runes.

Xavier frowned. What was the point of having Identify on steroids if it didn’t help him actually identify things?

Derrida has something to do with this. She’s either steering me toward figuring this out myself, or hoping I won’t get distracted about it…

Xavier stepped over to the wall once more and laid his hands on it, feeling the outline. He could see it, now, with his natural sight. He even heard something off about it. A dull, painful sound. He smelled it too. Though he hadn’t gone so far as to lick the walls, something told him he would be able to taste it as well.

What the hell are you? he wondered. Are you even supposed to be here? Are you even apart of the Void Dungeon? If you are… why are you rotting?

As Xavier started slowly walking down the tunnel, he felt as though he had failed, but he knew that was a harsh assessment. He hadn’t come into this trying to decipher the runes on the walls—he hadn’t even known they’d existed. He’d done what he’d done to improve the senses he already possessed, and he’d done that, far more powerfully than he’d imagined he would.

Even as he walked, he benefitted from what he’d done. The gravity didn’t feel as intense as it had before. He stepped with a grace he hadn’t previously known. And all the while he was delving deeper into the way his energy flowed inside his body. Now that he could sense his energy channels with more than just his spiritual sense, there were things he could do to improve them. He could feel those channels with his touch sense, and he could even hear the music they made as they moved through his body. He couldn’t smell, taste, or see them of course, but that was no matter—he gained plenty of insight to improve their efficiency, finding that soon every one of his cores was improved.

As he’d made the adjustments, he wasn’t even sure where the ideas came from. It was as though someone put a puzzle in front of him and his body got to work out of sheer muscle memory, putting the puzzle together perfectly even though he didn’t understand what it was. When the puzzle was complete, he simply stared it a while, then stared at his hands, and wondered how he’d managed such a thing.

It was the same with his cores, but he wasn’t about to examine it too much. This was a thing of instinct. Of mystery. And for some reason he could tell that, at least for now, it needed to remain that way. For if it was overexamined, he might lose something of it, and he would very much need it as he progressed through this dungeon.

When his refinements to his cores were complete, Xavier stopped walking. He could still see that outline, sense that strange, dark presence just beyond that was watching him—and he felt the very walls watching him, too, the eyes of the Void Dungeon on him not just from the beasts it created.

Another step, he was sure, would have the beast ahead running toward him.

He didn’t feel a hint of fear. His cores were running more efficiently than ever before. The way his body moved was perfectly in unison with his thoughts. Everything was in sync, and his expanded senses allowed him to have an awareness of his surroundings that he’d never gained even with The All-Seeing Eye. This wasn’t a spell—this was sheer powerful instinct at play. He knew where every mote of dust, every atom, every stray drop of energy was placed around him, and he had the power of mind to track them completely.

As he stood there, he examined the unnatural darkness, and instantly he knew what it was. Energy. From the Void Dungeon. Something it had created to obscure the way forward, to make things feel more dangerous, more sinister. With a thought, his Living Soul the driver and his Immortal Soul the eyes, he scattered the unnatural darkness with incredible ease. It did not take as much of his Soul Energy to accomplish the task as he’d imagined, it simply happened, while before he hadn’t even been able to sense the energy at all.

Xavier tilted his chin up as the strange beast was revealed to him. It was larger than the Gorgotlin had been. A lot larger. The outline he’d sensed could have told him that, of course. As large as it was, it took up the entirety of the narrow Dark Tunnel. It stood on six thick, pudgy legs that ended in sharp hooves. There were scratch marks on the stone beneath where it stood, and that did make him a little wary, for as powerful as he was, he’d not been able to scratch the stone when he’d tried. Though he hadn’t tried since expanding his senses, and he knew that through his sense of touch he had gained a better ability to recruit his very muscle fibres.

The thing had a head similar to that of a hammerhead shark, and its smile was roughly the same too—if one could call it a smile. Fangs as long short swords sprouted from its gums in an uneven line that reminded him of some unkempt graveyard more than a mouth. Though perhaps it made him think of a graveyard because of the decomposing smell that was coming from gaping maw. He’d smelled a hint of this back where he’d been in the tunnel, but only a hint. Now he was up close he wondered why he hadn’t smelled more of it—perhaps the air simply didn’t move enough in this place for it to be carried toward him.

Xavier was about to think Identify when a notification popped up from his Personal System, intuiting his thought before he’d finished having it.

Name: Ragatni

Type: Void Dungeon Beast

Multiversal Standard – Class C7

Ragatni, an artificial beast much like the Gorgotlin, are only found on some planets in the multiverse. Though they are artificial, their origin is somewhat natural, for they are grown from the seed of a dying planet—from the energy inside the hot, molten core. Planets hold a vast amount of energy that is rarely exploited by the beings of the multiverse, as it is very difficult to access and very difficult to turn into raw Celestial Energy, but those who know its inner workings have found great power in exploiting a planet’s core.

Though this Ragatni was created by the Void Dungeon, it shares the same qualities of its seed-grown brethren, as the Void Dungeon has the power to recreate much.

The Ragatni are beasts of Earth and Fire, and above all—as they come from dying planets—they are beings of Death. These natural elements intermingle, allowing them strange powers.

Their three main spells are:

Lava Death

A Song for the Departed

Roots of the Grave
