King Janus looked Rosslyn in the eyes for a long, intense moment.

In the years that followed, she would sometimes wonder what had gone through his head in that moment. When she asked Adon about it, he would say that he didn’t know, that the King’s mental defenses on that day were too strong.

Never mind that there would be other instances in which the butterfly read Janus’s mind without apparent difficulty.

Whatever he was thinking, the King did not hesitate for longer than that appraising moment.

“We have far better than mere healers with us,” he said quietly. He raised his voice and called back, “We need the help of the High Priestess! Someone fetch Cunigunde!”

Rosslyn’s eyes widened in disbelief. This went far beyond what she would have dared to hope.

“The High Priestess?” she repeated.

The High Priestess? Adon asked.

The head of our religion, Rosslyn thought in response. The Goddess’s closest representative in this world. Also the most powerful healer in the world. She can, without a doubt, save Frederick.

From beneath the Goddess banners, Rosslyn could barely perceive movement. The figure was so far back from the front of the formation that visibility was poor.

Then she saw her.

A tall, radiantly beautiful female figure dressed all in white had stepped away from the horses onto the ground. As Rosslyn watched, the woman began walking toward them.

“Well met, newly made Queen of Claustria!” called the High Priestess.

We have a new High Priestess since the last time I encountered the head of the Church, Rosslyn thought. I had forgotten. They never live long lives. To channel the power of the Goddess is not a task that is undertaken lightly.

“Thank you for your kind greetings, Your Holiness!” Rosslyn called back after a moment of being stunned.

As the woman approached, and the sun caught her robes, she almost seemed to glow.

“She truly evokes the Goddess, eh?” said King Janus. “Every time I see her, I think of all the statues of the Goddess we have erected. Is this not the perfect model for such a statue?”

Rosslyn kept silent. The King’s comments were vaguely blasphemous, but she didn’t care. Apparently, the High Priestess did not mind him either, since she was traveling in his company.

As High Priestess Cunigunde reached them, Rosslyn fell to one knee and kissed the hem of the holy woman’s robes. She only rose again once the High Priestess gently tapped her on the head.

“You do not need to show such fervor, holy maiden,” Cunigunde said. “The Goddess knows your courage and devotion well. Lead me to your young friend.”

“Right away, yes,” Rosslyn said.

She turned and led the High Priestess through the trees and toward the hamlet where she and the others had stopped for the night. The Queen was dimly aware that a few others from the King’s entourage were following, but she did not concern herself about that.

“The cottage is right—”

Rosslyn stopped in mid-sentence as she felt a power unlike anything she had ever experienced before surround her whole body. She knew in a fraction of a second what this was.

The Goddess’s divine light… I am unworthy.

She felt the pleasant pain and itch and sensation of restoration as muscles that had been permanently damaged were simply restored, scar tissue vanished like a mirage, and her ruined eye sent signals to her brain that showed it was not ruined any longer. She could see.

Even Rosslyn’s fatigue, which had not gone away entirely after the full night’s sleep, dissipated instantly.

This was the sort of boon that money could not buy, a pure matter of divine favor and the High Priestess’s personal discretion. It was also exactly what Frederick needed.

“Your Holiness, I did not need the assistance, my friend the Dessian lord did,” Rosslyn said. She quickly added, “Though I am of course grateful!”

“There will be enough favor for all of the Goddess’s champions today,” Cunigunde said evenly. “Do not trouble yourself to decide what you need, Your Majesty. The Goddess will provide what She believes you need, and you will surely do the rest yourself.”

“Yes, Your Holiness,” the Queen replied, bowing her head. “This way, please.”

She led the High Priestess up to Sergeant Angus’s cottage, knocked, and then opened the door. Everything and everyone were virtually as they had left them. Frederick lay in bed, unmoving. Goldie was actually finally visible, sitting on his chest. Sergeant Angus watched the door. And Carolien stood there watching over Frederick and Goldie, though her jaw dropped when she looked over at the door and saw who it was. She knew from the raiments exactly who the woman with Rosslyn was.

Carolien fell to her knees and lowered herself in a deep bow, arms folded in front of her body.

Sergeant Angus recognized the situation a moment later and hastened to follow Carolien’s lead.

“All may rest easy,” the High Priestess said in a tranquil voice. “There is no need to show your reverence in this way today. All of you have shown remarkable fortitude and courage in defense of our Goddess, your homeland—” she paused and walked right up to Frederick’s bedside—“and your friends.”

She reached down, and the holy, pure white light engulfed Frederick’s body from head to toe. The most beatific smile overtook his face, his body shook, and tears ran down his cheeks, and he experienced the same bliss that Rosslyn had at being healed.

Right, I no longer need this, the Queen thought. She reached up, removed her eyepatch, and threw it to the ground. She blinked and confirmed it. Perfect binocular vision.

Yes! Yes!!! My sight is complete again. This is the power of the Goddess. This blessing…

A tear came to her eye.

I wish you could have been here for my father. Just a day or two ago. Maybe more like weeks ago, before the attack began.

“This High Priestess has a much different attitude than her predecessor,” came the voice of King Janus from behind Rosslyn. “Would that she had been here sooner, she would have surely helped your father.”

The Queen swallowed nervously.

Right. My father, who he had the alliance with. That was King Janus’s phrasing.

“Thank you for coming to our aid, King Janus,” she said, turning to face him.

“We would have been here a day sooner, perhaps,” he said, “but that madwoman insists on stopping to help half the injured peasants she comes across. I think she can tell who is devout and who is not. She said that I should refrain from getting hurt in front of her, because I might be disappointed in her verdict about me.” He let loose a low, rough chuckle and shook his head. “Still, I cannot help but admire the ferocity. Who does not like a fierce woman, eh?” He seemed to recall who he was talking to a moment later. “That is, I mean to say, um, I am glad we came to your aid. Of course we did. Your father and I made an alliance. Parmonia keeps her alliances. An easy choice.”

“You have brought the entire Winged Knights Corp,” Rosslyn observed. “Will the border with Orthonia be safe?”

“As it happens, they are in the middle of a succession crisis,” King Janus replied with a wry smile. “Since their previous ruler was mysteriously poisoned.” He winked. “I wish them the very rapidest resolution to their internal issues. In any case, that freed up all but a token force to leave Parmonia and come to Claustria. Thankfully, Her Holiness also made her way to my little Kingdom in time to join us. She always seems to know the right place to be at the right time to achieve her ends.”

“You called the High Priestess a madwoman,” Rosslyn said.

The King shifted uncomfortably, then pointed.

“Look, just look at what she is doing now!” he exclaimed.

Rosslyn turned and saw High Priestess Cunigunde had begun working her miraculous power on Sergeant Angus. After a few seconds, his peg leg fell away, replaced by a new, moist, fresh limb.

“Miracles,” Rosslyn said. “Almost every High Priest performs them.”

“Many do not,” King Janus replied in a low voice. “With good reason. Every time they use the power, it shortens their lifespan. She has been doing it as if she has an urgent appointment with the Goddess that she wishes to keep.”

“A blasphemous remark,” Rosslyn said almost automatically. “Still, she has been remarkably… generous.”

“That sells it short, young lady.”

Rosslyn fixed him with a stare, locking her eyes onto his until King Janus chose to look away, facing back at the High Priestess.

“Why did Her Holiness say you might be disappointed if you got hurt in front of her?” she asked.

“Presumably because she does not know if she wants to waste her powers on me,” the King replied stiffly. “If you want to know the details, you are welcome to ask her.”

“They are of no concern to me,” Rosslyn lied. “I was only making conversation.” She reached up to her shoulder. “In my haste, I have failed to make proper introductions. This is my companion, the mystic beast Adon.”

Adon, please read Janus’s mind regarding anything I ask him about, she thought as loudly as she could. I need to know his real thoughts and any secret intentions he might have.

“Ah, it is a pleasure to meet you, Adon,” said King Janus, looking down at Rosslyn’s shoulder, where the butterfly remained perched, slowly moving his wings as if trying to avoid a cramp. “I would shake your hand, but I fear I would only crush one of your delicate limbs in these meaty paws.” He raised one of his large, hairy hands, looked at it with an expression of faux disgust, and shook his head.

Well, you know he thinks he’s funny, Adon sent to Rosslyn only. He is also attracted to the High Priestess.

Disgusting, Rosslyn thought. Cunigunde is easily young enough to be his daughter.

King Janus had not ascended to the throne of Parmonia until the onset of late middle age, while High Priestess Cunigunde was easily in the same age bracket as Rosslyn, perhaps even younger than her.

It’s a pleasure to meet you as well, Your Majesty, Adon sent, broadcast so that everyone could hear.

“Do you happen to have ever met the mystic golden eagle?” the King asked curiously. “Our family’s noble mystic beast has not been seen in some years, but we still hold out hope for a renewal of our blood ties to the creatures.”

There was a silence on Adon’s end that felt, oddly, almost awkward, as if it was not the King who had caused the conversation to deviate into irrelevancies.

Rosslyn slipped into the gap in the discussion.

“I hate to skip pleasantries, Your Majesty, but now that you are formally introduced to both myself and Adon, I think we need to discuss some important business,” the Queen said.

“Well, you are welcome to bring up any topic you wish, of course,” said Janus with the air of an indulgent grandfather offering to buy his granddaughter a sweet.

“You brought your army into my country with the intent of helping in the defense of Wayn from the Empire, correct?” she asked.

He nodded. “That was the main intent when we left, yes.”

“Do you still intend to assist us?” Rosslyn asked bluntly. “Knowing that my father is dead and the city has fallen? It may be a longer and more difficult struggle than you bargained for when you agreed to the alliance.”

The King stared at Rosslyn for a long moment again, clearly assessing her.

As he opened his mouth to speak, Adon sent a telepathic message exclusively to her: He is trying to figure out what kind of advantage he can gain by leveraging his help. King Janus apparently has several sons around your age, and two of them are unattached.
