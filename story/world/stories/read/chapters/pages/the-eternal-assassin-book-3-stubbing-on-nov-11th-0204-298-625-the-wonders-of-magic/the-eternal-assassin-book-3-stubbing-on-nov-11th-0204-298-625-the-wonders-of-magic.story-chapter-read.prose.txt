“Kroult, you alright over there?” Asher asked, keeping his gaze locked firmly on the two halves of the man’s torso before him. Despite the man not being an undead, he wouldn’t put it past The Lost Lady to have made her minions all but unkillable somehow. The lack of blood flowing from having both his legs severed was an indication that there was something going on with his body, as if the dozens of glowing runestones embedded up and down his chest weren’t enough of a dead giveaway.

“I am uninjured,” Kroult said, brushing off his robes as he got to his feet. “Apologies for the hassle. The man grabbed me out of nowhere and took off. Their physical strength and resistance to elemental attacks is incredible.”

“Let’s just be glad they’re not resistant to being cut in half,” Asher said, frowning even as he continued staring at the unmoving torso halves before him. “...Actually, as wild as it sounds, I think they might be. I cut him in half and he’s still not dead. Neither half by the looks of it.”

Asher’s blade had struck the man just to the left of his neck before traveling down to his groin, so while he hadn’t cut his head in half, the rest of his body was very much in two pieces. Yet despite that, the amount of the blood on the ground was practically less than what Asher himself had lost when Grant kicked him in the face. Leaning down, Asher’s brow furrowed as he looked more closely at the body parts before him, noting how the flesh had somehow managed to seal itself shut almost instantly. It hadn’t so much as healed over the wound he’d inflicted as it had stretched and pinched itself shut, managing to sort of get around his Sever the Thread skill to a certain degree.

While it wasn’t true healing, the quasi-healing factor of the minions put his own Eternal Regeneration to shame when it came to stopping bloodflow. Somehow, even the half that was missing the large runestone shoved where the man’s heart should have been was still ‘alive.’ Based on the continued pulsing and flashing of the lights radiating off the different runes, it seemed almost as if they functioned as complete arrays rather than each one handling a specific task like he’d first suspected.

“I don’t know what The Lost Lady has been doing all these centuries, but I think I can firmly say it’s freaky as hell,” Asher declared. “We need to get all this back to the palace. I doubt Donvath will know anything, but Richard might be able to help. If there’s a chance we can save Grant and get him back to his family without risking our own lives, I want to try.”

“You are a good man, Asher,” Kroult said, moving to join him above the minion’s body. Kroult’s flaming eyes flickered as he peered at the fallen man, and he sighed. “What of this one?”

“Unfortunately, he’s already gone,” Asher said, gripping his dagger harder as the two halves of the man’s torso began moving. The fingers on each arm twitched and reached out, clawing at the stone floor of the cave as they attempted to either drag the man to safety or search for a throat to rip out. “The least I can do is give this man the closure he should have received decades ago.”

As quickly and gently as he could, Asher cut the man apart, making sure to avoid harming the glowing runestones as he did so. To his astonishment, the minion was harder to put down than any of the undead he’d fought over in the Deadlands. Despite the fact that his attacks cut through tritessence, there seemed to be so many redundancies and magic flowing through the man’s body that it took ages before Identify confirmed he was well and truly dead.

“Physically powerful, element resistant, and beyond durable,” Asher muttered, finally sheathing his dagger as he started picking up the bloody runestones. “If they were more inclined to fight or came at us in numbers, they’d be a deadly force for just about anyone other than me. I’m willing to bet any wound inflicted upon them without Sever the Thread would heal near instantly, just like on The Lost Lady herself.”

“You are her natural counter,” Kroult nodded, his eyes flickering as he turned to look upon him. “If I were her, I would go to great lengths to ensure the two of you never meet.”

“That’s unfortunate, because I’ve got a few things I really want to say to her,” Asher frowned.

After pulling Kroult into his rift, Asher Recalled back to the team, popping into existence beside Samantha instead of Moxy.

He’d already been hit in the face once today, and he was in no rush to add to that number.

“Asher?” Samantha asked, giving him a concerned look as she laid a hand against his face. “Are you okay? You’re covered in blood.”

“Don’t worry, it’s all mine,” he grinned, happy that his teeth had already grown back. After pulling Kroult out of his rift, he sighed. “Kroult and I had a little run in with two of The Lost Lady’s pet projects. Which brings us to a new problem.”

Asher quickly ran the three of them through what he’d discovered, getting pretty much the exact reactions he’d expected. Moxy was largely indifferent about the fact that Grant and possibly others who hadn’t been under The Lost Lady’s thrall for too long might be able to be saved, whereas Samantha and Rulfar were immediately on board with doing what they could to rescue them.

“Those poor people,” Samantha muttered, shuddering as she looked at the no-longer-glowing runestone in Asher’s hand. “You really think we can save Grant?”

“It’s hard to explain, but I swear it felt as if his own aura was reaching out for help,” Asher shrugged. “Let’s wait and see what Richard has to say on the matter. Knowing him, I’ll probably have to burn the first of my two favors for this, but that’s fine by me. I almost feel like I owe Ulma for helping me unlock my first blended skill. If it weren’t for her, who knows how much longer it would have taken before that skill manifested. If trading the favor Richard owes me gets her her husband back, that’s a worthy trade.”

“Eh, I’d rather ask for him to make me some sort of sparring golem that I could fight with whenever I wanted,” Moxy admitted, frowning as she furrowed her brow. “Wait, why haven’t I done that yet?”

While Moxy contemplated that, Asher removed his Eternal Mark on her, placing it down on the cave floor instead. With a mark there and another one on Grant, who was still running even deeper into the earth, Asher felt confident about their ability to return whenever they wanted. Summoning a bubble around them with Spatial Lock, Asher focused on the royal palace and dragged them across the continent.

“It’s pretty late, so this is as good a time for a break as any,” Asher decided. “Let’s rest up and resume the hunt in the morning. Hopefully by that time Grant will have reached The Lost Lady.”

“Want someone to go with you to talk to Richard?” Samantha offered as the others filed out. “I know you said he’s been better lately, but I can’t imagine he’s suddenly become someone fun to be around.”

“He’s definitely not fun, but he’s bearable now,” Asher chuckled. “Don’t worry, I’ve got it. That was a long day of being on edge, you should take the night to relax. I’ll be back in a bit. Maybe we can do something together then.”

“I’d like that,” Samantha smiled, giving him a tender kiss before wishing him good luck.

After making his way over to Richard’s large suite, Asher paused in front of the far-too grandiose doors, rolling his eyes as he rapped his knuckles against them. “Richard? It’s me. Here to see if our own resident wizard can help with a magical problem!”

Asher stood there, waiting for any sort of signal that he’d been heard. As far as he knew Richard rarely ever left his rooms, seeing as they functioned as his own magical workshop as well as living quarters. Beyond the occasional call from Donvath or the royal guards requesting help from his magic, the wizard was very much a homebody.

“Richard?” Asher tried again, banging on the door a bit harder. He was half tempted to poke his head through the door with Astral Dip, but one glance with Identify was all the confirmation he needed that it was enchanted. And knowing Richard, he didn’t have the slightest doubt that the man had added an anti-spatial enchantment to his room solely to keep Asher out. Just as he was wondering whether to keep banging or find a royal guard to ask if Richard was actually helping with something for once, he heard what sounded like a loud, heavy crash come from within Richard’s room.

“Richard?” Asher repeated, taking a step back from the door as he spotted bright, colorful smoke beginning to seep out from the cracks of the door. While Asher hadn’t been able to see what Richard’s experiments looked like the last time he was in there due to the wizard having a weird blurring enchantment set up to protect his work, Asher could only imagine the bizarre magical doohickeys and alchemical experiments the kingdom’s only wizard would have on hand. If that crash had been something going wrong…

This entire palace might go up in magical flames if I don’t do something, he decided, sighing as he pulled out his void dagger and took a deep breath. The moment his dagger left its sheath, Asher winced at the sensation of the blade beginning to hungrily devour his tritessence. No matter how many times he used the weapon he never got used to the sensation. Not wanting to drag it out any longer than he had to, Asher slipped the void dagger into the lock on the door, not feeling even the slightest resistance from the enchanted wood as the lock was eaten away instantly. He could practically already hear Richard whining at him for ‘destroying’ his fancy door, but Asher decided he’d deal with that annoyance once they got to it. For all he knew Richard was currently lying half-dead on the ground in his room, having accidentally inhaled the magical equivalent of carbon monoxide from his experiments.

Sheathing the dagger and returning it to his hip, Asher pushed the door open, careful not to breathe in any of the colorful fumes as they washed out over him and into the hallway. Richard’s entire room was filled almost from floor to ceiling with the rainbow smoke, and Asher frowned as he walked into the room and heard the crunching of glass beneath his feet.

Yep, something definitely broke. Now what—

There was a flicker of movement from within the smoke, and Asher recoiled as something sharp and heavy latched onto his left wrist, tearing back and ripping his hand clean off in an instant. Immediately slipping into the astral, he held up his bleeding stump in shock, staring at the severed limb.

“What the hell?!”

The colorful smoke was even more difficult to pierce through within the astral realm, but as it began flowing out into the hallway, Asher managed to catch a glimpse of what had claimed his hand. A large, jet-black wolf almost as tall as he was stood in the center of Richard’s room, licking its bloody teeth clean from its unexpected meal. The wolf’s fur was jagged and pointed, and half of what looked like some sort of tapestry was stuck to one side of it, its own fur having half-shred the thing before it got stuck. Though while its size and clear appetite for human flesh was concerning, that wasn’t what Asher was fixated on.

No, Asher was far more interested in how the wolf growled and narrowed its eyes at him before lunging forward, into the astral realm as its serrated teeth moved to close around his throat.
