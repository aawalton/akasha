They ran out into the clearing and the storm vanished. The heart of the blizzard was a cylinder of wind frozen mid gust, snow flakes floating stationary as if it was a snapshot moment of time. The sun shone directly overhead, illuminating the eerie stillness as there wasn’t a cloud above them.

Five snow white robbed men, two troglodytes and three Frigidi stood together in a circle around a floating white sphere. The sphere was like snow, but it shone like a tornado in Caleb’s wind senses. They had captured a storm and frozen it, shackling it to their will. The humans had long white beards, and were similarly clad it white like the troglodytes, but their clothes were far better tailored if made from the same material

The mages saw them enter the eye, and one of the Frigidi broke away, casting magic but a troglodyte uttered something to the man in an unintelligible tongue.

The words lit a rage within the eyes of all the Frigidi that heard the words.

The four continued their ritual while the man who broke off began to chant. When the man left, the others repositioned themselves, each suddenly exerting more control over the captive storm to make up for the loss. As one they pulled vials off their belts and downed them to continue to fuel their magic.

Caleb threw his spear at the lone defender as Bog and Grisim charged him. The ground erupted as the snow covering it shot up to block the spear. It only took a nudge for the spear to be forced off target and Caleb watched with remorse as it flew out into the storm.

I need to stop doing that, he noted distantly.

He moved to spray nails at the man, but he suddenly had larger issues. The snow hadn’t stopped erupting with the simple spear deflection. It writhed and swirled, forming into two hulking humanoid shapes nearly three meters tall.

One went for Grisim while the other intercepted Bog. Grisim dodged the attack, heading for the summoner, and Caleb threw his hammer at it to give his brother the opening. The magically guided throw struck the reaching arm, hardly damaging it but knocking it off course.

Caleb summoned the hammer back to him, running to meet it. Bog for his part jumped onto the ice elemental, digging his claws deep into it as he worked up a spit.

With Grisim running at him, the Frigidi summoner called up more snow from the ground and it formed around him into a white set of sleek plate armor, covering him head to toe. It had overlapping joints that would give Grisim an opening. He drove a spear at the man, aiming for the chest but it struck the shoulder, only instead of glancing off like the ice armor they were used to, this armor surged, pushing the blade off target much like the elemental had to Caleb’s spear.

The summon conjured a snow saber surrounded by a miniature blizzard and lunged at Grisim who narrowly dodged. Caleb watched the fight through his affinity senses as he faced off against his own enemy, the blizzard around the sword giving him a reference point to hone his often-vague perception on.

Through their bond, Caleb got the sense that Bog was struggling but not in danger. He sensed the dragon’s frustration at how hard the ice of the elemental was as his claws and teeth skidded off ineffectually. If not for his ability to work up a large heat within him, the dragon would have already been dead, grabbed and crushed by the much larger enemy.

The heat however, was weakening his claws. He could cause cracks to appear from the thermal shock of contact, but those quickly repaired themselves.

Caleb wished he could help both of them, but they had a purpose. The summoner and ritualist had to die. The elemental, unfortunately, was aware of their purpose and fought to keep itself between Caleb and the five ice mages. The elemental was slow, but slow for a magical beast was faster than Caleb’s feasible stats.

Contrary to popular belief, speed was a function of both strength and agility. Caleb’s strength was at a mere 11 while his agility was still an abysmal 8. The swings of the elemental came fast, and he missed dodging the first one. He brought his hammer up to block, and out of an abundance of caution for both the hammer and his life, he activated Immutable Metal.

The skill saved his life. It reinforced the metal—which from his affinity sense he determined hadn’t been necessary—but more importantly, once the metal was under the control of his Imperium fueled skill, his Imperium joined the strength of his harm in keeping the hammer in place.

It was like a mirror to Arcane Power Attack. His arm held the weapon in place as a shield, but the force of the blow didn’t pass through his arm, but directly through his whole body.

Now, this wasn’t to say Caleb blocked the blow. That would imply the blow did little. Caleb went flying back across the ground, the snow breaking his landing and slowing his slide. Instead of his arm snapping like a twig and his own hammer driving into his chest killing him in an instant, the full force of the giant’s attack had been distributed across his whole body, but much plate did in a small area and that blow had been great. He was certain that without his added constitution he’d have died right then and there.

Bog sent concern through the bond but Caleb reassured him as he rolled to his feet.

Maybe this can work for me, Caleb thought, pulling a handful of nails out of his sack.

From Bog’s experience, he knew the nails would do nothing to the shell of the creature, but his hammer would be more than enough for that.

He ran at the elemental, shooting the nails ahead of him in a spray. As expected, they bounced off with a soft ting and lay scattered around the elemental. Fearing a trap the element stood its ground with its arms raised in a defense.

Bog sent pain through the bond as one of his strikes led to the loss of the talons on one claw.

As he got closer, Caleb used his Imperium to press the nails into the ground and then as the elemental came swung down at him, Caleb pushed with his Imperium on one of the nearby nails. The nail budged just a few centimeters before catching in the rocky ground beneath the snow and then Caleb shot out to the side, narrowly missing the blow with a speed his body couldn’t match.

He continued to close it, losing a little momentum as he regained his footing, and struck with an Arcane Power Attack, using mana just enough to bring the full force of his Willpower down on the elemental.

His attack struck with the sound of shattering glass as the pick of his hammer drove deep into what would have been its ribs, then he dropped a nail and pushed himself back out of range for the counterattack.

Caleb kept this up, the elemental fighting to keep itself between Caleb and the ritualists. If he could just get closer, he could end the storm with a spray of nails. But, Grisim was having better luck with his opponent. The skill level gap between the two combatants was apparent, but magical snow equipment that attacked of its own volition did a lot to make up for the difference.

Caleb sensed as the summoner brought his sword back for a powerful strike, and sent a gust of wind do disrupt the aim, allowing his brother to dodge.

Bog sent eagerness through the bond as his own claws finally punched into the creature. Caleb risked a glance to see a green metal had replaced the claws on his right claw, and that the metal glowed with magic, not the red heat that shone through the gaps in his scales. He raked with the claw, and three gouges appeared down the surface of the elemental.

Things seemed to be turning their way, until the nail Caleb used for a push slipped, and instead of sailing gracefully out of the way of an attack, Caleb took the blow straight to his armored chest. Immutable Metal once more saved his life, but from the pain of the blow and the landing, he briefly considered death might have been preferable.

This isn’t sustainable, Caleb decided as he watched the elemental reposition itself to block its charges from attack.

He had nails all over the battlefield but using them to move side to side was unreliable. So, he decided to go up.

He ran at the golem once more, hammer held up like he was going to throw it—and he did. Into the ground between them. He ran on, using his Arcane Power Strike to aid in a jump, which sent him sailing in a tumbling roll up two meters. Once he was over his hammer, safely wedged into the ground, he pushed with his Imperium and soared higher above the elemental. The conjured beast was too slow to reacted, and it turned helplessly to watch as Caleb flew overhead.

Caleb still had an uncontrolled tumble to deal with less this trick lead to a painful landing, so he drew upon his Imperium Ventorum and called the wind to stabilize him. The captive wind in this unnatural stillness answered readily and eagerly, and far more power came to his call than he expected. His tumble straightened out immediately, and the wind even helped slow his fall without him needed to ask.

He landed gracefully in the snow, with the elemental behind him and four ritualists in front of him. Reaching into the sack at his belt, he pulled out the last of the nails and threw them out in front of him, putting the full power of his Imperium behind each.

The nails flew through the air. Most missed there targets, but there were a lot of nails, and the white snow cloth of the ritualists was no defense to the cheap, unaspected steel of Dimel’s forge.

The ritualists collapsed, and the storm around them rapidly began to close in, though it was not just generally inward that the storm closed.

As it filled in the gap in the eye, it also rose up into the sky, losing its light gray hue and turning black like a storm. The snow turned to sleet, and then the sleet to cold rain, and then the lightning came. The summoner was first, killed outright from the first blast that send Grisim and Caleb flying.

Caleb sensed absolute terror from Bog through their bond as the dragon rightfully feared the electricity born of the sky, but Caleb sent reassurance through the bond that this storm meant them no harm.

As soon as the summoner was struck, the elementals lost their form, turning back into a snowy dust that the rain pushed to the ground.

More lightning struck the dying but not dead ritualists, ending them and this time sending Caleb flying back from the proximity. Each lightning bolt was deafening, and the thunder struck his whole body with the force of the elemental’s blows.

The storm rolled on, moving rapidly to get out of the area and in less than ten seconds he was outside of it. He saw it sail away down the mountain toward the empire at large, the opposite direction of a natural storm’s flight. In its wake it left dead troglodytes and Frigidi, though it far from killed them all.

With the storm gone, they saw the true face of the foe matched against their village, and the battle had only just begun.
