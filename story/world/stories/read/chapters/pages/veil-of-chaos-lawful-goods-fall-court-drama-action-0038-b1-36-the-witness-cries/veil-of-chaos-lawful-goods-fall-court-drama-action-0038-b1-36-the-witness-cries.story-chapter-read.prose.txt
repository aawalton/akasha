Khronar.

Evening had settled fully by the time she reached the merchant quarter. Lanterns bloomed one by one along the canals, their reflections breaking across the black water like molten gold. The city breathed around her—fishmongers closing stalls, priests ringing the last bell of dusk, lovers slipping through fog toward Titania’s gardens to the southeast of the harbor.

Merana moved among them unseen, the calm after confession carrying her steps. Every sound seemed sharper now, every scent alive: the tang of brine, the resin of ship ropes, the faint metallic thrum of divine wards buried beneath the paving stones.

The grief was still there, but lighter—a stone turned into a compass. She followed its pull inland, up the slope toward the heart of Bluerise, where white marble gave way to pale crystal.

The palace rose in the distance, white towers catching the last rays of the sun. She could see the royal wing from here, the balcony where the Seat of Judgment’s divine residue still hummed faintly in her awareness.

King Enrir would be there. Alone, most likely—his wife locked away to avoid seeing her daughter condemned, his grandson exiled to the underworld’s mercy.

She hadn’t planned this.

But Damon’s soul had shown her things the king didn’t know.

Family pain. Political traps. The weight of trying to be righteous in a system designed to punish flexibility.

He deserves to know his grandson doesn’t hate him. He wants to understand him.

That alone was worth the flight.

“Ready?” she asked her bonds, using the physical enhancements of her gear to jump out of the streets and onto a nearby building. “You’re right, Luna, we do need to make progress before summer’s end.”

Luna chittered in agreement. Korina’s presence solidified into readiness.
