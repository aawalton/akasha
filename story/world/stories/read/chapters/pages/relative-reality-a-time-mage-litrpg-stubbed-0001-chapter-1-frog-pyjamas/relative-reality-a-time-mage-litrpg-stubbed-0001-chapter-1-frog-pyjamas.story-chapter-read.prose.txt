Rowan woke to a place very unlike the one he had fallen asleep in. While he was still wearing his pyjamas, he was most definitely not in his bed.

He sat up and blinked bleary eyes at the soft grass surrounding him.

“Wha?”

He was in a beautiful clearing in the middle of a forest of tall redwood trees that stretched far into the sky above. It was the middle of the day, with the leaves of the redwoods allowing a gentle light to filter through and land upon his bare arms.

Turning to his left, he saw a perfectly spherical object that looked like a bowling ball with an animated surface. It had swirling colours in shades of black, white and grey that moved of their own accord, mixing and separating in a mesmerising manner.

He stood up to get a better view of his surroundings, wondering what was happening. Fear and anxiety began to war in his stomach, but he looked back down at the sphere as if it were calling his gaze.

Leaning down, he picked it up. As he did so, a small, blue box appeared next to it.

Reality Essence - Fabled - Consumable

Awakening ritual requirements:

Absorb to awaken the powers of Reality.

Along with the blue box, he felt a bit of information enter his mind. It was an image of a circle made of salt with ten equidistant lines leading into the centre where a second, smaller circle sat. Some kind of ritual to… awaken it? Whatever that meant.

What? The powers of Reality?

The strangeness of the blue box talking about the powers of reality formed a dissonance in his mind as he looked at a meandering path that led from the forest, through the clearing, then out the other side.

What the hell?

Despite the alien surroundings and odd sphere, the main question on Rowan’s mind was the whereabouts of his bed. That and his apartment of course. Oh, and the entire apartment building it belonged to.

He considered for a moment if it might just be an extremely vivid dream. The startlingly real texture of the grass under his bare feet told him that it wasn’t, yet what else could it be?

While he wasn’t one to chase lucid dreams like some people did, Rowan knew all about them from his friend Lucy. She loved lucid dreams, to the point that Rowan had nicknamed her lucid Lucy on their messages. Well, now that he knew he was dreaming, he should be able to control it.

He closed his eyes and attempted to summon his bed. He opened one eye, peeking quickly to see nothing before closing it again and thinking about his bed really hard.

Of course, nothing happened.

Thinking he might have been the victim of some kind of prank, he looked around for a camera, maybe someone hiding with a phone. He saw nothing.

“Alright guys, real funny. I don’t know how you got me out of my apartment without waking me up, or how you even got into my apartment but give it up.”

Nobody replied.

A rumble went through the ground, dirt and forest litter shivering. Rowan braced himself against a tree for support, eyes wide, as he waited for the world to settle once more. When it finally did, he breathed out a sigh of relief. Apparently, wherever he was, happened to be tremor prone.

His thoughts turned from possibilities of lucid dreaming or way over the top pranks to possible brain damage or a coma of some kind. Neither of those really made sense to him either though, since he still remembered his life, still felt like he was in control of his mind. He remembered closing his laptop in frustration as he was unable to finish off his architecture homework, then going to bed with images and thoughts of structural supports and environmental conditions swirling through his head. Then… something strange. A twisting sensation, like when you tried to sleep while drunk. What had that been?

He looked down at the orb in his hand, then at the frog in a wizard hat that decorated the leg of his pyjamas. A magic orb and a magic frog. He really needed to find a house or… something. Who knew how big this forest was? If he was stuck far out in nature, he wouldn’t survive with what he had.

Rowan kept the strange orb tucked under his arm safely as he started walking and though the thing was roughly the size of a bowling ball, it wasn’t anywhere near as heavy. He soon became thankful of that fact as minutes began to turn to hours of walking.

The forest was strangely serene as he walked through it. No birds called to each other and no animals crawled through the undergrowth, odd for a forest that appeared both remote and natural. The only sound was his bare feet hitting the rough but beaten earthen path, nothing disturbed despite the tremor that had passed through the ground.

Why he kept the orb, he couldn’t say. It being the only object next to him as he awoke might have contributed to his actions, yet he had a distinct feeling in his mind that it was important. He could have sworn that someone had told him that once, yet had no memory of who. Besides, he hadn’t ever seen the thing before, so how could he have had a conversation about it?

Rowan lost track of time and his worry was beginning to take the forefront of his mind. He wasn’t sure where he was or if he would run into any sign of civilisation other than the compact dirt path soon. Thoughts of what he would need to do to survive — find shelter, fresh water, a source of food — raced through his head. The forest had been nice to begin with, yet now it brought him a sense of dread.

That sense of dread spiked when he finally heard a sound other than that of his bare feet on the packed dirt path. He turned, watching between the trunks of the redwoods as a clicking sound got closer. It stopped, then started again and Rowan’s eyes were drawn upwards to where an ant was climbing up a tree. The ant, which was nearly as big as Rowan, stopped, its antennae flicking about as it turned towards him.

He yelled in shock, then ran.

The clicking sounds came faster as the massive ant chased him down the path. Rowan sprinted as quickly as he could, adrenaline pumped by his heart beating to the tempo of his legs pounding the dirt beneath. The clicking behind became softer as the ant reached the path. The forest began to clear in front of him and… there was something there, a structure, some kind of building. A house!

It was at that moment that Rowan tripped on a root, slamming into the ground with a thud. He flipped onto his back just in time to kick the now close ant in the head. It recoiled slightly before jumping at him again, mandibles grasping the stone still in Rowan’s hands. He yelled, pulling against the ant as it tried to take his now only worldly possession. They struggled back and forth, Rowan managing to get back to his feet before he yanked the sphere from the ant's grip.

He then bashed the ant in the head with it.

Rowan wasn’t a particularly strong person, in fact he was quite lanky. As the sphere came down with a dull bonk sound however, he heard a crack from the ants head. It stumbled back dazed and Rowan didn’t give it time to recover. He jumped forwards, smashing it in the head over and over until he stood panting, covered in ant ichor.

After taking a moment to recover, Rowan knelt down to inspect the ant, though he didn’t touch it. He wasn’t any kind of biologist, but it very much looked like a normal ant that had far outgrown its normal size. It may have been capable of killing him had it not been trying to take his sphere. He only spent a moment wondering what the ant had wanted with the sphere as he heard a cracking in the treeline. Looking up, he spotted a much larger and far more armoured ant appear.

“You’ve got to be fucking kidding me.”

Rowan ran towards the house again, past the carefully tended garden beds and hedgerows with fist sized bumble bees meandering above, to the door. As he reached it, he realised that the door stood almost nine feet tall. It swung open to reveal a massive person, wearing an immaculate suit with a cloth draped over his arm and fine, dark brown feathers growing from his face.

“Are you quite alright there sir?”

Rowan slipped off the steps as he took a startled step backwards. He dropped the sphere in his grip as he twisted and flung his hands out to catch himself. Impacting the ground; his arms, weak as they were and tired from bashing the first ant, buckled. His elbows hit the stone with a thud, skinning his arms and mixing some of his own blood with the ants ichor.

He lifted his head just in time to see the second ant leap at him, its pincers gnashing between the plates of armour on its legs. A resounding thud and a crack sounded as what Rowan could only think of as a giant, bird butler punched the ant square in the face.

The ant retaliated, its sharp pincers grabbing onto the butler's arm and crushing down. He yelled out in pain as another figure left the house, this one wearing a fine dress with skin coated in sleek, white feathers. She drew a series of golden, glowing symbols in the air and a blade of wind, visible to the eye, sliced the ant clean in half.

The butler peeled the ant's pincers off his arm with a groan of pain, then dropped the remaining half of its body to the ground.

“Oh Hoster, are you okay? Go inside and see the healer, I’ll take care of this business.”

The bird woman’s voice was a little high pitched, but smooth, as she turned and spoke to Rowan.

“A fine morning to you sir, are you alright?”

“W-what in the hell are you!?” Rowan asked, finally jumping to his feet and taking a wary step backwards.

The bird person propped one hand on her hip as she reached down and picked up the sphere he had dropped.

“Well, that’s a little bit rude, don’t you think?”

“Oh…”

Rowan deflated a little, suddenly feeling awkward as he watched the butler walk back inside holding his arm.

Able to take a moment to examine his surroundings, Rowan noticed that the house before him, better described as a mansion, was entirely constructed to the giant stature of the bird people. It was made of wood and stone, large blocks making up the foundation and supporting structure of the building, while planks and beams of redwood fashioned the upper sections. The roof was covered in clay tiles, much as Rowan was used to. It was quite ordinary, if well built, except for how large the building was.

“Well, I’m sorry I’ve just never seen, um, someone like you before.”

She cocked her head at him.

“You’ve never seen an avian?”

“No! Why would I have seen an avian before? We don’t have avian’s where I’m from.”

Even as Rowan stared at the avian, she stared right back, each of them looking quite confused.

“Which is where?”

“America, it’s… Where am I now?”

“I’ve never heard of America. We’re in the middle of Erendel, just outside of its capital, Lyel. Also, as far as I recall, avians can be found everywhere in the world. Are you feeling alright? Were you injured?”

Rowan held his head in his hands, shaking it. Avians? Bird people, all over the world. That couldn’t be right. Giant ants were trying to eat him and, though he wasn’t the best at geography, he’d never heard of a place called Erendel or Lyel. What the hell was happening?

“I don’t know. I’m not hurt. I just woke up in the middle of your forest over there and have no idea how I got here. I also have no idea where either Erendel or Lyel are.”

She held out his sphere.

“This is your essence, right?”

Her voice was cheery, as if she was just realising that he was very, very confused and growing more concerned as time went on. He cautiously took the sphere back from her, apparently called an essence. That’s what the strange blue box had called it too.

As he took it back, his hand brushed hers and brought up another of those boxes.

Avian - Tier 2

“Was there anything else you needed?” she asked gently.

Rowan scratched the back of his neck, still feeling awkward.

“Um, yeah, maybe?”

He glanced up at the sky, towards where the sun was setting. Night was coming soon and the forest was apparently filled with giant ants. He had no food and did not feel like trying to sleep where something could eat him. The giant bird people were a lot nicer than the ants, at least.

“I know it might be a bit much to ask but I don’t really have anywhere to stay the night. I promise I’ll be out first thing tomorrow if you can point me in the direction of uh, Lyel you called it?”

She nodded.

“Yes, Lyel, it’s the capital of Erendel. As for staying the night, I might be able to convince my parents to allow it. I’m Helja, by the way.”

“I’m Rowan. Nice to meet you Helja, and thanks for saving me there.”

He looked down at the ant remains, then back to Helja who gestured for him to come inside. Feeling more and more out of touch with reality, he stepped up onto the porch. He had to be dreaming, didn’t he?

He walked into the house as Helja closed the door behind him, staring about in wonder. The place was huge, evidently constructed with the large avian frame in mind. The entrance hall had three doors leading out of it and a set of stairs that split going up left and right towards the second floor at the back, where there were undoubtedly even more doors. Painstakingly carved into the walls of the entrance were depictions of various avian people, some dressed in armour wielding weapons while others were more common looking, if the mythical race could be called that. To his unsure eyes, it looked like a history of their people, or perhaps their family? The giant eagle with a blazing sun behind it struck him as particularly beautiful.

Helja walked past him over the cold marble floor towards the stairs at the back, calling out for her father as she went. An even taller avian appeared on the landing and met Helja halfway down, giving her a warm hug. He asked her something that Rowan couldn’t hear, then offered warm praise at her response before turning towards Rowan. The man, assumably Helja’s father, came down the stairs to greet him.

Rowan stood awkwardly as the avian looked him up and down, the brow of his strangely human-but-not face creasing.

“And who might you be?”

His voice was deeper than Helja’s, though just as smooth and not a small bit huffy. He was similarly dressed in finer clothes and, though they were tailored to his body perfectly, they did not seem to fit him quite right.

“Um, Rowan… sir.”

True to his tone, he let out a huff and turned back to his daughter.

“Oh Helja, I know you to be a kind soul, but must we entertain this… ruffian?”

Rowan was taken aback at being called a ruffian of all things, but Helja just lightly slapped her father’s shoulder.

“Father! Don’t speak in such a way about him, he’s had a rough time of it. Besides, he is obviously at Tier zero, we have nothing to fear from him.”

Rowan perked up. So Helja knew something about tiers?

“Wait, Tier zero. I saw you were Tier two, what does that mean?”

Helja and her father turned from each other and stared in pure, mind blown wonder at him.

“Maybe we should fetch a doctor for him?” Helja’s father whispered out the side of his mouth.

Helja glared at him.

The way Helja’s father spoke reminded Rowan of his own father… Rowan ignored the pang of pain at the memories and shoved them far, far down.

“You don’t know what Tiers are? Do you even have any idea what that thing you are holding is?” Helja was asking.

Rowan looked down at the sphere, then back up.

“You called it an essence?”

“Perhaps he’s an idiot?” Helja’s father muttered.

“Father!” Helja exclaimed again.

Helja took Rowan by the arm and led him up the stairs and into a room. Inside there was a lush bed of king size, with bedposts and a canopy above. Set into one wall was a fireplace, except instead of wood or fire, there was a circle carved into the stone with what looked like runes etched all around it. Helja walked over and muttered a few words over it and, to Rowan’s surprise, the circle and runes lit a bright red before a fire started.

“Look, you should absorb that essence as soon as possible but we can talk about that tomorrow, it looks like you’re going to sleep well tonight.”

Legs sore from the long walk and head pounding from all the confusion and discoveries of the day, he barely heard Helja’s words. Once she left, he crawled under the blanket and passed out the second his head hit the pillow.

Rowan woke the next day and blinked several times as his vision focused on an avian opening the beautifully crafted curtains. He sat up straight in alarm, finding that what he had thought was a dream was, in fact, still reality.

The avian looked over at him with a questioning look before continuing on with their tasks. They put out the strange rune fire and left.

“What the fuck is happening,” Rowan whispered to himself.

As the maid left, Helja walked in.

“Good morning! You’ve a few minutes to get yourself ready before breakfast. Father expects that you shall be joining us? We would love to talk to you about where you have come from.”

Rowan stared at her, then looked down at his frog pyjamas. His stomach growled.

“Um, sure, yeah.”

Helja clapped her hands together twice.

“Wonderful, we managed to find some clothes that may fit you, join us in the entrance hall when you’re ready and…” She looked at his bare feet sticking out from the covers, “Unfortunately we do not have any shoes that might fit you just yet, sorry.”

At the two claps, another servant came into the room and deposited a pair of dress pants along with a button up shirt and a vest to go over the top. The white shirt had some small frills fashioned like feathers in various shades of golden and tan white, while the black vest and dress pants had embellishments of gold. Helja and the servant left, the door clicking closed behind them quietly.

Rowan got out of the bed, thankful he had at least woken up in the same place he had fallen asleep this time. Running his hand over the gold embellishments in the pants and vest he thought that it appeared to be real gold, though he couldn’t be sure. If it was, they had somehow made thread out of it, perfectly sewing it into the material. How they had managed that he might never know.

He got dressed, finding the clothes a little long while simultaneously a bit too tight around the shoulders and waist. It wasn’t anything too bad, though he worried that Helja’s father would still make a comment about it.

Feeling incredibly uncomfortable and still very confused, Rowan picked up his sphere and went downstairs to Helja.

“Wonderful, they fit for the most part,” she said, “Hoster!”

Rowan’s thoughts were focused on his experiences of the day before and the way Helja had killed the ant with a blade of wind.

“Hey, what was that you did to the ant yesterday? It was a strange day for me, what with waking up in that forest, the tremor, this essence thing. I can’t take my mind off of all this… magic.”

Helja’s expression turned concerned.

“My magic is wind related, yes. A tremor you say? We might need to speak of that later, it doesn’t bode well.”

Rowan was about to ask what she meant, when the butler from the day before stepped up, having heard Helja’s call. He had his cloth draped over one arm held horizontally to his chest and gave a little bow as he stopped in front of them, apparently no longer injured.

“Yes Ma’am?”

“Kindly adjust our guest’s clothes if you would?”

“Of course Ma’am.”

The massive avian man, Hoster, turned to Rowan and looked him up and down, though not in the same dismissive and slightly insulting manner that Helja’s father had. With a few quick movements of his wrist a little circle of soft tan coloured runes appeared in front of him. Rowan’s clothes stretched out at the shoulders and waist whilst simultaneously shortening in length, fitting themselves perfectly to his body.

He stared down in wonder, then back at Hoster.

“Thank you for that… yesterday too. You kinda saved my life and I don’t know how to repay you for that.”

Hoster gave a little bow.

“Worry not sir, it’s all a part of the job. I don’t mind a light scuffle here and there if it means saving the life of a young lad such as yourself.”

“Oh Hoster, ever the gentlemen,” Helja murmured, before linking her arm through Rowans and dragging him around the stairs, through another door.

“Just this way!” she exclaimed, evidently excited.

Rowan took a deep breath, steeling his nerves as he was pulled along. What the hell was happening?
