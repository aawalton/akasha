Callie's mouth opened and closed as she tried to come up with a reason. Chief's eyebrow furrowed as he glared at her. Callen looked like he was about to tell him the whole truth. If she didn't say anything, Wiggles would be hunted.

"I-I made it… from combining poisonous herbs," she managed to say, her hands shaking underneath the table.

It felt like such a horrible lie, but perhaps with everyone so eager to blame her, they would accept it. The punishment terrified her so much that she didn't even know how to explain it. Her stomach was hurting; that's how bad it was. But it was all to protect Wiggles.

"Y-Yes! She would know how to… Being Granny Ethel's apprentice and all!" Aurelia was quick to point her finger at Callie.

Gerda nodded vigorously, although her face was filled with obvious shock. "Y-Yes! It was poisonous herbs!"

Heavy silence descended upon the room as the adults stared at each other, not saying a word. Why weren't they saying anything? Callie dared to glance up at the chief, hoping to understand how mad he was. But his expression was completely unreadable.

"Everyone, except Callie, Rowan, and Ethel, leave," Chief Aemon ordered. "I'm going to talk to each of the girls in order, starting with Calluna.

"I'm going to stay—" Callen began, but Dad put a hand on his shoulder.

"The chief ordered you to wait outside, and that is what you shall do," Dad said firmly.

There was no room for arguments. Callen gave Callie's hand a last reassuring squeeze, then reluctantly left the room. Callie watched him leave, horrified to be left alone. But Granny Ethel was still here, so she looked hopefully at the elderly woman.

"It'll be alright, Callie. Don't worry," Granny Ethel said reassuringly.

That did help Callie calm down a little, but her stomach was still churning. Dad and Chief had such hard expressions on their faces that Callie didn't dare to look at them.

Only once everyone was well and truly out of the house did the chief speak again. "Calluna, can you explain to me why you are trying to take the blame for this?"

Callie's heart seemed to stop at that moment. He had caught her in her lie. It wasn't really that surprising—she had never been a good liar. But what was she supposed to do now?

"How about you let me talk to her? You two gruff men are just scaring her with your menacing stares. How is a young girl supposed to say anything when you two look like you will cut her head off at any moment?" Granny Ethel chastised.

Dad and Chief both flinched back, looking surprised at the reprimand. Dad even rubbed his chin as if trying to fix his severe expression.

"I… I see your point. We'll leave for a moment and let you two talk. I'm going to question Gerda in the meanwhile," Chief Aemon said, and together with Dad left the room.

Callie watched them leave with a relieved sigh, the heavy knot in her stomach untangling a little.

"Oh, Callie, my dear. Do tell me what happened. And no lies! While I see that the situation is not in your favor, lying is not something I'll tolerate from my apprentices," the elderly woman said sternly.

Callie nodded. If it had been just Granny Ethel from the start, then she wouldn't have lied. She understood arcane beasts and certainly wouldn't start hunting Wiggles over what happened. In fact, she actually might be amazed at Callie's discovery.

Feeling excitement rise within her, Callie said. "I'll tell you everything. You won't believe it!"

She then explained all the events, without skipping any details. How Callie had managed to light the fire by herself and how the girls once again confronted her, being angry that she was following a mireling trail. When she was telling Granny Ethel about mireling and its ability to change its slime to either acidic or completely harmless, she felt… bad. While the discovery itself was amazing, people getting hurt wasn't.

"Thank you for telling me everything, Callie," Granny Ethel said calmly, patting her head gently.

"You can't tell the chief about Wiggles! He will want him hunted," Callie insisted.

"I understand that child, but if Wiggles keeps hurting people, I won't be able to protect it. It shouldn't enter the village. It is too dangerous for both him and the people. The little critter likely can't differentiate good from bad. But killing it for that isn't right either."

"I'll try to keep him away," Callie agreed.

"Good. I'll go talk to the chief. You should go home for now."

Some people were still gathered outside. Gerda's parents in particular glared at Callie, but Callen was quick to block their lines of sight.

"Let's head home, Callie," Callen muttered and they left. After they had walked some distance he asked, "What did you say?"

"I told Granny Ethel the truth. I trust her," Callie said, then threw an accusatory glare at her brother. "You were about to tell them about Wiggles! How could you?"

"Callie, if the choice is between you and some monster—"

Callie pulled her hand free. "Wiggles isn't just a monster! Stupid brother! You don't understand anything."

While in reality she didn't really mean the words she had spat at her brother, she felt far too overwhelmed by everything. Callen nearly telling everyone about Wiggles was the kind of betrayal she couldn't endure, even if he did it only to protect Callie.

She ran home to her room, slamming the door behind herself and burying her face into her pillow. This day had been too much. She didn't even know how to process it all, what to think. So instead of thinking about that, Callie thought about Wiggles, wondering what the little mireling was doing at the moment.

The most important thing was that he was safe. Granny Ethel would never do anything to harm an arcane beast, so she wasn't worried that she would tell the chief about what happened. With that comforting thought in mind, she allowed her eyes to close.

Callie woke up from a knock on her door. "Callie, come out! I have returned with the chief's decision."

Her eyes snapped open right away, and she scrambled to her feet. Her heart thundered. What did the chief decide? After the short nap, her mind felt a bit clearer. Callie was certain that she wouldn't be let off without punishment, even if Granny Ethel somehow managed to convince them that Callie wasn't the one to inflict burns.

Perhaps the elderly woman didn't even try to convince them of Callie's innocence. After all, protecting Wiggles was more important, and Granny Ethel surely understood that.

Whatever the decision, I have to accept it.

After taking a deep, steadying breath and exhaling, Callie opened the door of her room. Dad was looking down on her with his usual stern expression on his face. But he didn't look quite as mad as she had expected him to look. In fact, he appeared more… tired more than anything else.

"Sit down, Calluna," Dad said, pointing at the dining table. Callen was also sitting there, throwing an apologetic look at Callie.

She pointedly ignored her brother. While she wasn't as mad at him as before, Callie needed to prove a point. Calling Wiggles a monster and trying to sell him out to save Callie was not okay. Once Callen understood that, Callie would forgive him.

She sat down, after which Dad sat down too. "Okay, let's not beat around the bush. The chief will not be punishing you for burning Aurelia and Gerda. All of us adults agree that you couldn't have done that. Granny Ethel said that she hadn't even allowed you to touch any of her poisonous herbs yet, not to mention mixing them into acids or anything of the sort."

Part of her was relieved, while another was panicking. If she wouldn't be punished, did that mean that the chief knew about Wiggles? Were they searching for the mireling right as they spoke?

"However, that doesn't mean you won't be punished, because it was also clear that you were lying. Granny Ethel claims that you have a good reason for doing so, but refused to elaborate. I suspect it has something to do with a monster, doesn't it?" Dad narrowed his eyes.

"Arcane beast," Callie corrected in a barely audible voice, although her hands were trembling with fear.

Dad sighed. "I have no clue why you value those creatures over yourself, but I won't press the matter. If you have decided to lie, then you shall accept the punishment. Double the chores at home, and you'll be helping me out in the smithy for one hour each day. You'll also be responsible for preparing the ointment necessary for Aurelia's and Gerda's recovery."

Callie blinked in surprise, staring at Dad. Was that it? Sure, it was a nightmare trying to imagine doing even more chores and helping Dad in the smithy. All the jobs in the smithy were difficult. But she had expected worse.

Dad frowned. "Don't look so relieved. I'm going to work you to the bone! Not to mention that Chief Aemon warned that he would be keeping a tight watch on you from now on."

Callie shrank back in her chair. The chief watching her was certainly nerve-wracking. Would she even be able to visit Wiggles? Or do any magic? Perhaps it would be smarter to wait for the adults to calm down.

"Okay…" Callie nodded.

"What about Aurelia and Gerda? Are they not going to be punished? No doubt this all started because of their bullying. They should be the ones to get punished," Callen demanded.

Dad sighed. "The chief promised to handle the matter, although I expect the punishment not to be too harsh since Callie didn't say anything about what they did. Which makes me question—why?"

Callie blinked, looking up at her father, who had a somewhat pained expression on his face.

"Why?" Callie asked.

"Why haven't you ever said anything about them bullying you? This is the first time I've heard of it," Dad said.

"I… I didn't think you'd believe me," Callie admitted reluctantly. After all, aside from Callen and Granny Ethel, nobody ever believed Callie, saying that it was just her imagination or that she was too obsessed with arcane beasts or something similar.

Dad looked shocked at her words. He opened his mouth to say something, and for a moment, it appeared like he would get mad. But then he relaxed his clenched fist and smiled. Kinda. It was a forced smile; even Callie could tell that. Dad rarely had a genuine smile except when he and Mom thought that nobody was looking at them.

So Callie had already learned that this was not his genuine smile. But why was he forcing himself to smile? It would actually be better if he just got angry.

"Callie, if they bully you again, you come and tell me, okay?" Dad said.

"I… I'm sure it'll be fine," Callie shook her head.

"I insist. Even if nothing bad happens, tell me if those girls bother you," Dad said, the fake smile replaced with his usual stern face.

"I… Okay…" Callie finally agreed.

Dad nodded. "Good. I will not tolerate any more lies from you either. Even if there are monsters involved, be honest with me," Dad said.

"Only if you promise not to kill them…" Callie muttered, but quickly shrank as she realized that Dad had undoubtedly heard her muttering.

His eyebrows furrowed into a grimace. "Callie! This is no joking matter. They can be dangerous!"

"Dad, I think it's been a long day for everyone. Now isn't the right time to discuss this matter, don't you think?" Callen suggested.

Dad glanced at him and nodded. "You're right. Let's make dinner instead. Callie, you'll be peeling all the vegetables today."

"Yes, Dad!" Callie said, hurrying away to bring the potatoes inside before he got mad again.

Overall, this wasn't a bad outcome. The chief watching her was a bit worrisome, but she wouldn't get flogged or expelled from the village. Wiggles would also remain safe, which was all that Callie could ask for. But there was one thing that worried her.

Wiggles. How could she convince the mireling not to hurt people? He hadn't hurt Callie, so it was certainly capable of not burning people with its slime. It likely used the acidic slime to protect itself or in this case to protect Callie. But she had to convince him not to use it against villagers. But how?

All of a sudden, Callie really missed Mom. She got a feeling that if Mom had been here, things would have turned out better. But she had been gone for several days now. This made Callie worry. Had something happened?
