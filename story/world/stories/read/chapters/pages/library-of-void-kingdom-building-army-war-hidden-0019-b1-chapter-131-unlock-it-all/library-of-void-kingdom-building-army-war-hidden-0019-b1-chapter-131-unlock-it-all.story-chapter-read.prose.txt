After the events in the underground dungeon, Vector and the others made their way to a private dinner hosted by Arthur at his house.

Located at the heart of Vantara Village, they were greeted by the sight of a two-story stone villa—sturdy and elegant—with warm light glowing behind its windows. It stood out amidst the natural beauty of the village, serving as a clear symbol of leadership and legacy.

Arthur stepped forward from the group and welcomed Vector and the others with a warm smile and a slight bow of his head.

“Young Master, please! Welcome to this humble abode.”

Vector smiled politely and replied,“It is we who should thank you, Chief Arthur, for inviting us to dinner.”

Arthur gestured for them to follow.“Young Master, you are Vantara’s benefactor. It is only proper that I offer suitable hospitality.”

Making a welcoming gesture toward the entrance, he added,“Please, this way.”

Inside the living room, servants moved about busily as they prepared the dinner arrangements.

After arranging the seating for the guests, Arthur went to inspect the dinner preparations while the servants brought out a jug of fresh water along with an assortment of snacks. Vector and the others accepted the refreshments, calmly sipping and waiting.

A few minutes later, Arthur returned to the living room after checking on the preparations.

“Young Master, dinner is ready. Please join us in the dining room.”

Inside the dining room, the table had been carefully arranged. A woman and a young man stood nearby, preparing the final dishes.

Arthur introduced them with visible pride.

“Young Master, this is my wife, Emily Venyar, and our son, Leo Venyar.”

Vector offered a respectful smile.

“Hello, Aunt Emily… Hello, Leo. It is a pleasure to meet you.”

“Welcome, Young Master Vector,” Emily replied warmly.

Leo, who appeared to be about the same age as Rhea, also extended a courteous greeting with a respectful bow.

Emily gently ushered everyone to their seats. The dining table was round, encouraging easy conversation, and it was already filled with a variety of freshly prepared dishes.

Arthur took the seat beside Vector and spoke with quiet pride.

“This meal may not possess the refinement of the feasts found in the cities, but everything here is made from ingredients grown or raised within our village. It is simple, but we hope it will satisfy you.”

Vector took in the aroma of the food appreciatively.

“It smells better than anything I have had in a long time.”

The dishes were indeed humble yet heartfelt—roasted vegetables seasoned with fragrant forest herbs, slices of smoked meat wrapped in soft, freshly baked flatbread, and a warm broth rich with the earthy flavor of mountain mushrooms. It was not luxury, but it was genuine, and that mattered far more.

As the group began to eat, Vector spoke between bites.

“Uncle Arthur, I noticed several stone buildings in the village. That is rare for a forest settlement. Such structures are usually more common in cities.”

The term ‘uncle’ did not provoke any discussion, as Vector had already clarified the boundary between personal and professional matters. Since this was a dinner invitation, the occasion naturally fell into the realm of a personal gathering rather than a formal one.

Arthur nodded thoughtfully.

“You are right. Those buildings were constructed by former village chiefs, using the wealth they earned from their adventures in the outside world.”

“We Venyar have lived in this forest for generations,” he continued. “Our roots run deep here. We have survived beast attacks and natural disasters alike. One of the greatest reasons we have endured is because of something that very few people know.”

He paused briefly before revealing it.

“We possess an Epic-grade Body Refinement Manual.”

Vector’s eyes widened slightly. Arthur’s words were both unexpected and impressive. An Epic-grade manual was something that even marquis families would treat as an ancestral treasure, storing it in their most secure vaults.

Yet Vantara—a small and unremarkable village that had never established an active connection with any city, not even Ironfang Hill City—possessed an Epic-grade Body Refinement Manual. This also explained Vantara’s abnormal strength, which was comparable even to that of Carnatic City.

Arthur added,“I myself am a Third-Stage Body Refinement expert, and the three elders you saw with me earlier are Second-Stage practitioners.”

He smiled faintly and gestured toward Leo beside him.

“Leo has already reached the First Stage of Body Refinement. He has inherited our legacy.”

Then his tone grew serious as he continued.

“But the existence of the manual is a closely guarded secret. Only a few trusted villagers are permitted to train with it. If word of it were ever to spread… greedy forces would swarm this village without hesitation.”

Vector leaned back slightly, absorbing the information.

Leo was also a genius in his own right to have reached the First Stage at such a young age. He appeared to be about the same age as Rhea and the others, which meant Leo could truly be considered a genius who appeared only once in hundreds of years.

“You no longer need to worry about secrecy,” Vector said firmly.“From now on, the Astrum Family will supply Vantara with Legendary manuals, elixirs, and cultivation resources. Our strength will grow together, and Vantara will have far more powerful warriors in the future.”

Arthur’s eyes shimmered slightly, deeply moved.

In Vector, he saw not only power, but also vision—and most importantly, trust. It was not merely because of his noble bloodline, but because of the way he carried himself. This young man possessed strength and leadership far beyond his years.

Arthur’s instincts also told him that Vector was far more powerful than he appeared as a First-Stage Warrior, which likely meant the young man already possessed the strength of a Second-Stage practitioner. To achieve such power at his age was nothing short of a genius seen perhaps once in a millennium.

The conversation continued, drifting between stories of the village and plans for the future. Despite the looming threat of war, the dinner carried a sense of warmth—almost hopeful in its quiet atmosphere.

…

After dinner, Vector entered one of the rooms arranged by Arthur for his rest.

Inside, the flickering light of an oil lamp danced across the stone walls. The room was modest yet well-kept, with an attached balcony. From the balcony, the dense canopy of trees stretched into the distance, while the scattered lamps of Vantara flickered wildly on the horizon in the absence of Somira in the night sky.

Vector sat cross-legged on the bed and entered the Simulation Space.

The familiar steppe land appeared before his vision as he stepped into the simulation. A stream of Chaos Energy began fusing with his body, a clear sensation that he was unable to feel in the real world.

As the System interface appeared, his eyes traced toward a particular section—the gift that still remained unclaimed.

He addressed the System.

“System,” he said softly, “can I now construct the library building gifted by the Will of the Void?”

[System Notification]

[Affirmative. The host now possesses designated territory. Library building Construction Protocol can be initialized.]

[To proceed, Host must designate a location within controlled territory. Once confirmed, the system will anchor and materialize the structure.]

A breath escaped Vector’s chest, and visible relief appeared on his face.

“So… it’s possible now.”

But he wasn’t ready—at least, not yet. He had not selected the location where the library would be anchored.

I’ll find the right place tomorrow, he thought. A location hidden from plain sight, where the library can remain protected and unseen.

After observing the terrain of Vantara, he had already decided that the Void Library would eventually be established here. With the Astrum forces expected to arrive soon, the village would inevitably transform into an Astrum fortress. Anchoring the library here would therefore be the most logical decision.

With that concern resolved, his thoughts shifted to another priority—his training.

He had previously honed the Silent Arrow Art, leveraging the experience he had gained as an archer in his previous life. But long-range combat had its limitations.

A bow was silent and precise, but eventually enemies would close the distance.

He needed something more.

A close-quarters skill—quick and lethal. Something reliable when the bow was no longer an option.

Tonight, I’ll train in knife techniques.

He knew he could not rely solely on ranged weapons. Mastery in close combat was equally necessary. And with the presence of the System, achieving Basic Mastery in only a few hours was entirely possible.

Before beginning that training, he decided to perform another test.

He conjured the looted musket within the Simulation.

A realistic metallic musket materialized in his hand.

After loading the ammunition, he aimed at a target placed one hundred meters away and fired. With his Basic Mastery in archery aiding his stability and focus, his aim remained steady.

The bullet struck the target with ease.

He reloaded the weapon and fired again at another target.

The primary disadvantage of these early firearms was their slow reloading speed and limited accuracy. However, their penetration and destructive power were significant enough that even a Second-Stage warrior would be at risk if struck directly.

After observing the result, Vector dismissed the musket.

He was about to begin training in Knife Arts when a sudden thought formed in his mind.

Without hesitation, he acted on it.

An M4 carbine rifle appeared in his hand—naturally created through the power of the Simulation.

As he looked at the black metallic weapon of the twenty-first century, a wide smile slowly spread across his face.

Not because he was seeing a firearm he had once known on Earth.

Not because he was holding it again.

But because of the action he was about to perform next.

“System,” his enthusiastic voice echoed loudly through the Simulation Space.
