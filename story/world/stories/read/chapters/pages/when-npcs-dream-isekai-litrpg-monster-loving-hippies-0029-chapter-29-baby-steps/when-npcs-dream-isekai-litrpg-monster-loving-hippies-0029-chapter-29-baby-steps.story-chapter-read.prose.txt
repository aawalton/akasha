I lay in the tent, eyes open, waiting for sleep to take me, while contemplating how the fuck it all turned to shit.

From the very beginning, my whole plan was simple: play it safe. Play slow. Don’t take risks. Don’t piss off anyone too dangerous. And now? Now somehow a small army of superhumans wanted me dead.

So what was the root cause of all this?

That we decided to play this in reverse, side with the monsters and goblins against the humans? Become a group of forest-loving hippies? Heroes of justice who just had to rescue a talking bird and make sure all NPCs are treated nicely?

Yeah. That sounds about right.

Now, I was afraid of Farhad’s group attacking us in the night. Or maybe Santiago and Charlotte would stab us while we sleep. Or a random forest monster would get us. Or maybe we survive the night just fine, but in the morning, Farhad finds us and we die anyway.

So what to do?

I know. Baby steps.

Step 1: Survive the night.Step 2: Survive the morning.Step 3: Come up with a plan to survive the entire fucking week.

Yes. Focus on step one. So… Go. To. sleep.

I did just that. I was dead tired, after all. And not only that—I went above and beyond and actually woke up in the morning.

Step 1: Complete.

“Geshem,” Juliette’s voice called from outside the tent. “You up?”

I crawled out, eyes still half shut. “Yeah. What’s the plan?”

She frowned. “I don’t know. What do you think?”

I tried to push my brain into gear despite still feeling like a zombie. “We should assume worst-case scenario.”

“Meaning?” Asked Juliette,

“Like… a few hours after we left, they realized we weren’t coming back. Then they noticed Santiago, Charlotte, and Miguel were all missing. Then they called Arjun’s group, who’ll tell them we’re sneaky bastards traitors. So now they think we killed or kidnapped some of their people and stole their gold. In worst-case scenario they're organizing a manhunt right now with ten or more people after us. If they have some magical tracking device or something, We’re screwed.”

“I don’t think they’ll come after us today,” said Charlotte, emerging from her tent with Santiago.

“Why not?” asked Juliette. “We’re still close to the city. Now would be their chance.”

“Farhad will be pissed,” said Charlotte. “He probably wants you dead too. But he’s also very calculated and cautious man. Think about it—his new guests turned on him, three people are missing, and he’s not even sure if they betrayed him. There are too many unknowns. He’s not going to charge blindly into the forest like that.”

“And also,” Santiago added, “he knows where you’re going. There’s nowhere else. He doesn’t need to chase. He’ll just plan carefully, prepare for the assault on Kamatouu like he was already going to, and then… he gets both the town and us.”

“That makes sense,” I admitted. “But he also could just be really pissed and look for us anyway. Better to assume he does and not get caught like idiots.”

“Geshem’s right,” said Juliette. “Let’s skip breakfast, pack up fast, and walk for a few hours to reach deep forest. We’ll be safer there.”

“What about the magic armor?” asked Samar. “Shouldn’t we wear it?”

“Better later,” said Charlotte. “I didn’t try them myself, but I think there’s a learning curve.”

The strength rings we were already wearing. they were… doing something. Maybe boosting my muscle power by around 20%. It wasn’t a game changer, and I didn’t have time to test it properly. And no, we couldn’t wear multiple rings to stack the effect.

“Alright. Forest first, armor later,” said Juliette.

“And Arjun’s group?” Samar asked. “Could they cross paths with us?”

“If they come straight from where we left them,” I said, “they’ll move through another part of the forest. But there’s still a chance. We’ll have to stay alert.”

“If Naked-Nula come close,” Duchess squawked proudly, “I will hear them. From far, far away! My hearing is the best! And you all Naked-Nula stomp around like feedlebunchers.”

God, I hoped she wasn’t exaggerating. For once, it’d be nice if Duchess was actually useful.

“What’s a feedlebuncher?” asked Santiago.

“A type of nookletrasket,” answered Duchess, “but noisier, and it has a fritsekleter instead of a handle.”

“…Right,” Santiago muttered.

We walked fast. Tired. Hungry. But also scared shitless—which is great for motivation. It’s a lot easier to hike on an empty stomach when you picture murderous gorilla-men chasing you.

Me, Samar, and Kim were leading. I handled navigation. Samar and Kim made sure we avoided deadly monsters. Between us, we were efficient. We made great distance, half the way between the cities, into forest we actually knew well.

When Dubadilu started dragging his feet, we found a clearing and took a break. He flopped down like he was going to die. Same, buddy. Same.

We laid out food—some bread, fruit, and weird forest greens—and mixed in some rations Santiago and Charlotte brought. It was a half-decent breakfast.

“So what’s the deal with the talking bird?” Santiago asked me quietly as Duchess napped nearby, snoring loudly. “Is she like... a tamed monster, or...?”

“She’s a player,” I whispered back. “Like us. But alien. From another planet.”

“Your mother’s cunt!” he Spanish-swore. “Are you serious?”

“Language!” said Charlotte, smacking him. “But… seriously?”

“Yeah,” I explained, “this world is a mix of different alien species and creatures from their lore and fantasy. If I’ve got this right, we’re a mix of at least three planets. But as for players, I’ve only met Big Bird over here.”

“That’s insane,” Santiago muttered, awed. “Do we know which planet? Like… are they even in the Milky Way?”

Charlotte snorted. “Are you sure we’re even in the Milky Way now?”

“Hey,” Juliette interrupted us. “I know it’s super important to figure out which galaxy we’re in—but I have more urgent stuff in mind.”

“Like surviving the week?” I suggested.

“Exactly that.” Juliette turned to Charlotte. “How long do you think Farhad will take to attack? What kind of preparation would he need?”

“He wanted enchanted gear,” Charlotte said. “The stuff he asked you to buy. That was supposed to tip the scales in the attack on Kamatouu. That was before all this happened.”

“But now,” I continued, “he has three fewer soldiers, and we have five more. Plus the enchanted gear worth sixty gold. From his point of view, the odds just got worse.”

“Maybe, but you shouldn’t underestimate Farhad,” Charlotte warned. “He’s 25, and was a high-ranking engineer back in Iran. He’s smart. He won’t move until he has a solid plan and good odds.”

“Can you estimate how much time it will take him to prepare?” asked Juliette.

“It’s hard to guess,” Charlotte said, “To get the magical gear, he might needed wait for new players to appear. Or raid smaller shops with fewer guards, though their gear’s worse quality. He also still has two or three people at level one, so he might want to level them up—maybe even get someone new to level three. My best guess? Two to three weeks. But really, that’s just a speculation.”

“We have something going for us,” I said. “Farhad probably thinks we can’t improve much in that time. He assumes we’re just... sitting ducks.”

“But aren’t we?” Santiago asked. “Sitting ducks?”

“Oh, no,” I said. “We can do a lot in two weeks.”

“You planning to ask the tribe for help?” guessed Juliette.

“No,” I said. “They’re still recovering from the White Stripes battle. And I don’t think they’ll want to get involved.”

“I know!” said Samar. “Recruit the goblin army!”

“Ding ding ding. 1000 points for Samar.” I said. “The goblins already hate Farhad’s group. They killed goblins in patrols. We can explain to them that after Kamatouu, the bad humans will come for their camp, to kill them for XP. They’ll be thrilled to help us. And also, we’re sort of friends.”

“You’re friends with a goblin army?” said Santiago. “That’s badass!”

“Yeah it is,” I grinned. “So let’s see what we’ve got: around 40 guards in Kamatouu, a goblin army, 40 gold worth of enchanted armor, and two level 2 players.”

“Actually… one,” said Santiago sheepishly. “I’m new. Still Level 1.”

“No, two,” said Juliette, with a sigh. “I’m also Level 2. I put the point into Strength.”

That got everyone quiet. Of course—she killed a level 3 player. That would’ve leveled her up.

She probably didn’t want to think about it much.

But wow.

Juliette had super strength now.It was weird to think about. She could kick my ass. Like, easily.

That was… kinda hot.

“How did it feel?” I asked.

“It wasn’t as weird as I thought it’d be,” she said. “I felt my XP ‘well’ fill up. Then… it was like the XP wanted to spill into something. I was scared I’d accidentally put it in a dumb skill, so I just lifted a heavy rock. I think that gave me the general strength skill.”

“Could you lift Dubadilu now if he didn’t want to move?” asked Samar.

“I’m not that strong,” she said. “I could probably lift a 50 or 60-pound rock, but not bigger. That’s about twice what I could before. Not really superhuman. A trained man could probably do better.”

“That’s crazy strong!” said Samar, impressed.

No, it’s not. Something’s off here.

“Samar, your weight is about 110 pounds, right?”

“Hey! That’s rude and not even funny,” she shot back. “I’m 60 pounds, last time I checked.”

“And your height—what is it?”

“About 160 inches, I think. What’s with all these weird questions all of a sudden?”

“Oh my god—the system’s translating units wrong!” I said. “If I said the temperature is 77 degrees Fahrenheit, it would probably sound really hot to you.”

“Yes, okay,” said Juliette. “But honestly, it doesn’t really matter. Nobody cares about your weird American units. Just do the conversion in your head and talk normal.”This system was so lazy. How had we not noticed it until now? Jesus, this could actually be dangerous—like if we planned a route through the forest and mixed up miles with kilometers. Good thing we caught it.

“Okay, so you can lift a 60-kilogram rock,” I said. “That means you’re really strong. Do you… feel different?”

“Um...” she hesitated. “Honestly, yeah. It actually feels good, but it’s mixed with me feeling like a murderous bitch, so... I don’t want to talk about it too much.”

We rested some more, let Dubadilu eat, and Duchess nap (and snore like a possessed vacuum cleaner). Then Kim and I tried again to teach Dubadilu how to hold a spear. Yeah, it was a really slow process—and it was starting to piss me off.

Santiago joined us. Apparently, he worked with horses back in Argentina, and he was thrilled by the idea of training the care bear. With Kim helping translate… yeah, he kinda took over. I was happy to let him. I had zero patience for care bear training.

By the end of the hour, Dubadilu was holding a spear with decent grip. Good job guys!

Next: magic armor training.

It was… disappointing.

I expected Super Saiyan powers. What I got was “baby giraffe learns to walk.

After some testing, I figured it gave around a 50% strength boost, which is great, but it boosted only specific muscles. Not all. I’d stumble just by walking because the strength wasn’t distributed evenly, and I didn’t instinctively adjust for that. Plus, it was tiring. Energy had to come from somewhere, right? I guess the magic was still bound by conservation laws.

Yeah okay, it wasn’t useless. It had potential. But it would take a few good days of getting used to before it became reliable.

We woke the bird and resumed walking. Fast pace, smooth progress. Even if Farhad’s group tried to chase us, no way they’d match our speed through the forest. Not without getting eaten by something.

“Its getting late,” I said when we found a clearing. “Let’s camp here. Tomorrow we can reach Kamatouu.”

“Charlotte,” Juliette said gently, “if you prefer, you can sleep in our tent with me and Samar. We have room.”

“That’s okay,” said Charlotte. “Me and Santiago… we’re sort of… together.”

“Oh, cool,” said Juliette.

THEY. ARE. WHAT!?

Santiago’s been here like two weeks and already got a cute girlfriend!?And they’re sleeping together?? In a tent??At night?? Doing who knows what!?

Life is unfair.

The next morning, we packed up and moved fast. Smooth travel. No issues. Well, except for the fact that Santiago had a girlfriend, and I was really jealous.

We reached Kamatouu in the afternoon. There was only a single guard station at the town’s main entrance. I glanced at Charlotte and Miguel—they looked nervous as they walked toward him.

When they joined us, they agreed not to hide from the guards and accept their judgment. They held hands—it was actually cute.

We figured there were three ways this could go: guards attack them right away, guards fine them or drag them to the baron, or—best case—the guards just ignore them.

To everyone’s relief—especially theirs—it was option three. The guard completely ignored them.

I figure the criminal record didn’t carry across from the capital cities to a distant place like Kamatouu. Or maybe the system already recognized they changed parties and didn’t commit crimes themselves.

They walked a few more steps into the city, looked around... smiled. And hugged.

I hadn’t thought much about it, but it probably wasn’t easy for them. Being dropped in an alien world was bad enough—but being rejected by the system, labeled a criminal, hunted by guards? That had to feel awful.

Now? They were free.

We explained our way of life here, working alongside NPCs even for small pay. They didn’t seem disgusted by that idea, unlike Farhad’s group. In fact, they were excited. Talked about job options, NPCs they wanted to meet.

We dropped our bags at Juliette’s place and let Kim and Duchess pass out. They deserved their beauty sleep.

Then we went straight to Hyun-woo’s bakery.

He was there. And not alone.

A big guy stood beside him—broad shoulders, light brown skin.

“Hi! I’m Nayeem, from Bangladesh,” he introduced himself.

“Charlotte,” she replied, offering a hand to Nayeem and Hyun-woo. I noticed she and Santiago didn’t mention their countries, which made sense. It didn’t really matter anymore. Honestly, it was probably better to leave that stuff behind.

“So you’re back early,” said Hyun-woo. “Did anything… interesting happen?”

I couldn’t resist. I wanted to deliver the bad news in the most casual way possible.

“Yeah, actually,” I said. “We met a large group of super strong players!”

“That sounds great!” Hyun-woo said.

“Little downside though… we’re kinda at war with them now. They’ll probably come kill us in a week or two.”

Hyun-woo’s face went pale.

Juliette rolled her eyes. “Sorry, Hyun-woo. We’ve got a lot to talk about.”

And so she started retelling the story—everything from the moment we left to the moment we returned—while we ate fresh pastries straight from the oven.

And somehow… despite everything, I didn’t feel so pessimistic anymore. Sure, we were in trouble—big trouble. But we were stronger now, smarter. We were building something. And maybe… we could actually win this.

With the new guy, we were now seven humans, plus Kim, Dubadilo, and that bird. We were starting to look like a real group. Or maybe… a tribe? Is “tribe” the right word?

What about a crew? A team? A brotherhood? A band? Yes—like a mercenary band! I liked that.

Oh my god, we actually needed a name. Something epic, like the Band of the Hawk… also known as Death on the Battlefield.

I grinned to myself, hand brushing the magic books I’d stolen and the amulet that would stabilize my circles.

I was going to be the band’s mage.
