Chapter 62

-oOo-

Whoosh! Wh-whoomp.

White feathers flashed overhead. A three sisters roc caught the air with its mighty wings. Zephyr slowed to a stop. With one last flutter, the giant falcon settled onto the ground, its talons digging into the earth. The bird’s head turned, beady black eyes gazing in Sylvia’s direction.

Riley Smith hopped off the saddle. The blonde girl swept her goggles up and onto her forehead.

“Hey ya, Sylvia!”

Wearing a wild grin, Riley crossed the grass to step onto smooth stone. The freckled asteri was dressed in tight, brown leathers, making her the spitting image of a WWI fighter pilot. The blonde’s hair was in a short, messy bob which ended above her chin. Strands, like golden wheat, were thrown out in a punk style.

Sylvia returned the smile, hopping off her Stella Iecit. Riley had chosen her hairdo back in Charm Club, much to Sylvia’s envy. These days, Sylvia preferred her long and adorable tails.

“Hey ya, Riley,” the silver-haired girl returned.

When standing beside the blonde, she drew a sharp contrast. Sylvia wore the Gown of the Water Maiden. The blue dress’s skirt hung beautifully around her knees, a puff of pink lace underneath the ruffles. Cute ties and ribbons adorned the shirt dress, running up its placket. To this ensemble Sylvia added a snazzy beret and a pair of shiny, blue pumps.

“It’s been a while, hasn’t it?” Riley commented, extending a gloved hand.

The blonde’s broad smile revealed her cute dimples, emphasizing her freckled cheeks. Sylvia took the offered limb, only to be dragged into a short-lived hug.

“Yeah,” Sylvia agreed as they parted. “Sorry about that. I’ve been busy working in Starport. It’s been hard to find the time.”

“You don’t have to apologize for anything,” Riley laughed. “I’ve been just as remiss while working with the Blades. You know how it is. First you find a handsome boy. Then, next thing you know, you’re neglecting your friends.”

“Ha,” Sylvia half-laughed. Her lips twisted into a sneer, voice tinged with exaggerated disdain. “I don’t know anything about handsome boys.”

“That’s good. I wouldn’t want to rat you out to Emmy,” Riley teased.

Sylvia grinned.

Time never stopped, and even fey grew older. There was too much work to be done in the Cloud Island Wilderness. Work that involved different dreams, different places, and different people. So Riley and Sylvia had grown distant. This trend had been exaggerated when she’d moved in with Emmy at Sylvia’s Landing.

Riley, on the other hand, lived in Viridian City. Where Starport was the home of industry, Viridian was the bastion of adventure. As gamers left the Beginner’s Village, they aggregated there. Then, they’d dive south seeking the monsters and resources found in the hills and forests beyond. Only a handful of players would pass through the planar gates seeking Starport for business or the Moonlight Academy.

Viridian City had a second major advantage: Yaalon Lake. While gamers weren’t permitted to revive in those waters, the fluids spilling down the river were caught, then filtered to form a natural resurrection pool. This made Viridian City, hands down, the cheapest place to resurrect.

“Has Henry been treating you right?” Sylvia broached. Her pastel pink eyes narrowed into slits. “If he hasn’t been, just let me know. I picked up this incredible new curse in Tartarus and I can promise, his death will be tragic.”

Riley’s eye twitched.

“Please don’t joke about that,” the tomboy refused, sounding weak. “The last thing I want is a disgusting memory where Henry’s chest bursts open to spill hundreds of worms on the floor.”

…

Sylvia pinched her chin, eyes gleaming with keen interest.

“So, you’re telling me that if I use worms of rot, you’ll abandon him on the spot,” she observed, tone utterly bland.

The blonde tomboy glowered. Riley punched the smaller witch in the shoulder, with just enough roughness to make Sylvia sway. The silver-haired witch laughed.

“Never change, Sylvia,” Riley said, shaking her head. “Never change.”

“You know I won’t.”

Riley smirked.

Slowly, with intense green eyes, the tomboy swept Sylvia from head to toe. Riley’s gaze paused on several features. Resonant Tear’s silver chain wrapped around Sylvia’s wrist like a bracelet. Anbaht, in the shape of a pink teardrop, hanging over Sylvia’s breasts. Sylvia’s colored nails and glossy lips. Riley even took note of the earrings twinkling between the silver-haired girl’s lustrous locks.

“I don’t know,” Riley drawled. “From where I’m standing, it looks to me like Emmy has turned you into a princess.”

Sylvia felt a spike of embarrassment before squelching the emotion ruthlessly. There was no reason to be ashamed of being cute. Instead, the silver-haired witch huffed.

“You’ll know she’s won when I’m wearing a tiara or headdress instead of a hat,” Sylvia retorted, chest puffed with disdain.

“Yeah. You’ve got me there,” Riley laughed. The blonde’s expression turned serious. “I’m glad to see you’ve become comfortable with your femininity. You’re a gorgeous girl. Be proud of it.”

“Esmeralda is gorgeous,” Sylvia refuted. She put on a wan smile. “I’m just cute.”

“Sylvia, your level of cuteness can’t be described with the word ‘just’,” Riley returned, shaking her head. The blonde changed the subject. “How is Emmy doing?”

“She’s as busy as I am,” Sylvia answered. “Between her position as mayor, her research into alchemy, and her oversight of the Academy, it’s a wonder she gets any sleep.”

Sometimes, Sylvia missed Emily’s playful moments. But she also appreciated Lady Vallenfelt’s labor.

“Please tell me you aren’t dumping your work as governor onto her lap, too,” Riley interjected.

“It’s called delegation,” Sylvia deadpanned, before pronouncing it again syllable by syllable. “Del-e-ga-tion. It’s a sign of a skilled leader.”

“Uh huh,” the blonde tomboy sounded in return.

Sylvia offered the green-eyed girl an exaggerated scoff.

“Most of the paperwork is handled by Emmy’s retainers,” Sylvia dismissed. “Besides, I’ve been putting in extra hours to replicate alchemical equipment for her.”

In fact, Sylvia had already invented a process to reproduce potion bottles. Simple alchemy, such as smelting materials in a furnace, involved stable reactions. Advanced alchemy often depended on highly unstable substances. Having glass containers which could hold these essences was, therefore, incredibly important.

Also, Sylvia did her fair share of government work. Bang, goes the rubber stamp. See? Done. After careful review of Earth-side politics, Sylvia had concluded this was exactly the right level of intelligence to apply to her job.

Really? What was even the point of being governor, if the lobbyists weren’t going to think for her?

…

Incidentally, she happened to be one of those lobbyists.

“I’m teasing,” Riley relented. “I know you work hard. Too hard, to be frank. And Mayor Marshall has done a lot of good for Viridian City.”

Sylvia nodded. As a member of the Viridian Blades and the guild master’s girlfriend, it was of little surprise Riley and Zoe Marshall crossed paths. However, having brought up one of Esmeralda’s retainers, Sylvia was immediately reminded of another.

“Dinah has been trying to snatch my property,” Sylvia complained.

Dinah Sabel was mayor of the Beginner’s Village. In Vallen, she’d served as Esmeralda’s secretary. Despite the smaller size, the Beginner’s Village was a huge hassle. Noobs were like babies. They had to be handheld through everything.

“Oh please,” Riley scoffed, rolling her eyes. “Just let her take control over your properties. It’s not like you won’t be able to collect rent.”

“I won’t collect as much rent,” Sylvia groused.

Though, she was tempted to let Dinah succeed, if only so she wouldn’t have to manage the people who were managing that segment of apartments. Still, as a red-blooded American capitalist, Sylvia had to make the government fight for it. It was her patriotic duty!

“I heard you sold the coliseum,” Sylvia stated.

Riley scratched a cheek.

“More like, I swapped my shares for partial ownership of the arena in Viridian,” Riley replied. “It’s better this way. I wasn’t really paying the business any attention and it was getting out of hand. Seems I’m not cut out for that work.”

“Funny,” Sylvia said, voice full of false threat. “Because you haven’t been shy about cutting into my business.”

Riley grinned. “Hey. You can’t blame me for that one. Emmy kept sending me whiny PMs about muscly men riding brooms. As a good friend, I had no choice but to start selling rocs to select customers.”

Sylvia shook her head, a wry smile on her lips. Esmeralda and her silly prejudices.

Emmy and Riley were a weird contradiction. Riley wanted nothing to do with gender. If the whole notion just vanished, she’d be a happy camper. Sylvia, by and large, agreed. This was one reason the two of them had always clicked.

Emmy though, Emmy would be horrified if gender vanished entirely. How could she be ladylike if there were no activities reserved for ladies? Big men on brooms? No way! Brooms were for witches. Witches, Sylvia!

Little things like that were why utopia was impossible. One man’s paradise was another man’s nightmare. Societies were a web of conflicting interests. Two cannot place first in a race, much less two million.

“Should we head up?” Riley questioned, pointing at the sky.

“Introduce me to Zephyr first,” Sylvia said, pointing at her bird.

“I forgot this was the first time you’ve met him,” Riley said, gesturing.

The girls crossed onto the grass. Riley and Sylvia were beside Yaalon Lake, the deep pool glimmering with faint green illumination. The colors of life were cast by the amniotic essence dissolved into the water. A platform of smooth stone ran up against the shore, two podiums protruding from the rock. Once, a pair of gargoyles had resided here along with the waygate.

All had been moved to Viridian City.

It was safer. While the city already had planar gates, keeping the waygate in a side room served to provide emergency access. That way, should anything go wrong, Sylvia could zip on over and do some repairs. Also, waygates were more convenient for a high-level witch. The planar gates were in constant use by the public. Which meant buying a ticket and waiting in line, because groups and goods were transferred in single-unit lumps.

Sylvia stopped in front of the falcon.

“You can pet him if you want,” Riley encouraged with a grin.

Zephyr was a tall beast, his curved beak hanging two meters over the petite witch’s head. The falcon’s primary coat was white, with the underlayers speckled with black. The roc’s chest, however, was colored pale gold. The same hue decorated the ridges of Zephyr’s wings along with the tips of his feathers.

Sylvia stepped close, hand extended.

“Kiao!”

With an angry chirp, Zephyr raised his left foot. The bird spread its giant wings, glaring down in warning. Hmph. Sylvia let out a huff, aura surging.

The falcon froze.

Zephyr trembled. A three sisters roc was a Class II high-rank phantasm. According to the System, their level ranged from 250 to 300, with some rare specimens going beyond that. This made them quite powerful as phantasmal beasts went. When Sylvia had first crossed the Cloud Island Wilderness, they had been a major danger. In fact, they were still a major danger, but only during flight.

Here on the ground, with Zephyr isolated, the falcon faced a soul radiating thrice its strength.

“Hey, hey,” Riley interrupted, green eyes flashing in warning. “Don’t bully Zephyr.”

“Your roc was going to eviscerate me,” Sylvia returned blandly.

Riley gently stroked Zephyr’s neck, cooing softly to her pet. “Don’t worry. The evil witch won’t hurt you.”

With dead, pastel pink eyes Sylvia gazed coldly at the falcon. “If you dare attack me, I’ll be eating chicken for dinner.”

With a malicious smile, the silver-haired witch ran her fingers through the feathers on the roc’s chest. Soft and fluffy. It was like grabbing a warm, stuffed animal. Sylvia kind of wanted to hug it. No wonder Riley liked her bird so much.

“Have you made a soul stone for him yet?” Sylvia suddenly asked, head turned in the blonde’s direction.

“We haven’t,” Riley answered. The tomboy grabbed the reins, then leapt onto Zephyr’s saddle. Adjusting her goggles so the dark lenses were over her face, Riley looked down at the silver-haired witch. “I don’t want to risk him until we’ve got the process down pat.”

Sylvia mounted her broom, floating up so she hung a few meters in the air.

“Send a few roc cores my way. I’ve been making gargoyles with Emmy. The processes have a fair bit of overlap. Between the two of us, we’ll figure it out.”

Plus, creating soul stones would be a nice warm-up before trying to create a golem core.

“Ha,” Riley laughed. “I see right through your scheme, Sylvia. You just want a share of my business.”

Sylvia returned an evil smile. “That’s right, I’m stealing your corporate secrets. Beware, because I might even patent your IP.”

With a grin, Riley nudged Zephyr into the air. The roc rose with a flap of his wings, Sylvia zipping up faster. Compared to a Stella Iecit, the three sisters roc had worse acceleration. The bird’s horizontal velocity, however, exceeded hers by a touch. Zephyr could also take sharper turns at high speed.

Of course, if Sylvia were to ride a Lunam Fuga, her flight performance would be better. But she’d left hers at home. Generally, she preferred the Stella Iecit, because the broom was lighter.

Wings spread wide, Zephyr swept around Yaalon’s massive trunk. Sylvia glided at the falcon’s side, her cute curled tails dancing in the wind. The tree’s bark shimmered in the midday sun, glimmering veins of crystal running through the surface.

Viridian City came into view.

On the south side of Yaalon Lake flowed the Viridian River. This green watercourse cut through Axis, piercing out of the cauldron and into the lands beyond. There, the river continued, growing ever wider thanks to the many tributaries before pouring off the edge of Pyrinas.

The center of Viridian City was set five kilometers along this course. Though they called it a city, it was closer to a town, holding no more than a thousand persons. The richest part was on the western shore, from whence arrived the players of the Beginner’s Village.

There could be found a collection of shops, guilds, and government buildings. Furthest to the north was the marbled Greek structure which held the planar gates. At the end of Park Street was City Hall, whose architecture was inspired by renaissance castles. For now, this building doubled as the city’s guild hall.

On the eastern bank could be found a few stores, warehouses, and the slums. And ‘slums’ was the best word for it. A hundred meters from the shore was a collection of ramshackle housing. Most of the plane’s gamers were struggling for money. What coin could be scrounged up was spent on tools, cultivation resources, and equipment.

Thus, many elected to save money by building a hut by themselves.

Zoe Marshall, mayor of the city and one of Lady Vallenfelt’s retainers, allowed it. But as the city grew, these slums would become a poison pill in the urban guts. Fortunately, this problem was easy to solve. With the power of property taxes, Sylvia would drive this scourge toward the fringes.

Was that cruel?

Yes. Yes, it was. But it was also practical. Viridian City shouldn’t be stalled by short-sighted rot. Besides, gamers were tough. With their phantasmal bodies, players could easily live in the wilderness. And if they couldn’t? He he. There would be plenty of apartments waiting for them, most built by Wells and Swallow.

Wind roaring in their ears, bird and broom continued their spiraling flight around Yaalon’s towering mass. As Viridian vanished, Sylvia spotted the courses of the Eastlake, then the North Mountain. Both of these rivers flowed from the mountain ring into Yaalon Lake. Right now, there was no civilization in either direction. In the future, Sylvia would build ‘beginner’s villages’ on each river.

And between these towns, she would add dungeons. Lots of dungeons. Sylvia wanted to fill all of northern Axis with low-level dungeons, to ensure resources, experience points, and adventure. Eventually, licenses would be sold for their conquest, favoring newbies rather than the high-level players.

Slowly, they ascended.

Ten kilometers above Axis, they passed over the first branches of Yaalon. The world tree’s canopy was as wide as the tree was tall. Fifty kilometers of leaves shrouded Axis, glowing with colorful gradients. The hues varied based on the surrounding elements. Or, perhaps, it was the other way around, with the patches of color changing the character of the local ether.

The result was aerial rivers. Flows of purified ether swept through the sky in thick streams. Sylvia flew through them, crossing bands of vaporous earth, water, wind, and fire. Her broom sputtered, deprived of life in three of the four domains. From her palaces of lightning and ice, the witch breathed life into the instrument.

“Cha-reee!”

Three eagles, all dwarfing Zephyr, sailed through the sky below. The nether beasts were all from the same species, brown and red feathers covering their backs. As Sylvia watched, a trailing eagle burst into fire. Flames wrapped around the nether beast, a blazing inferno akin to a phoenix’s silhouette. The creature charged into the lead, its sudden acceleration spurring its fellows into an incendiary chase.

Lesser guardians.

Forty-seven nether beasts lived in Yaalon’s canopy along with the lone immortal, High Queen Telma. Half of these creatures paid tribute to the Queen’s Court. The rest moved as they wished, caring only for their territory and their divine patron’s protection.

Unlike Nithe and Lenape, High Queen Telma was not born from an Egg of Origin. Instead, she obtained her soul by eating soul fruits two thousand five hundred years in the past. This made her, technically speaking, a lesser guardian. It was only through the accumulation of strength and wisdom that Telma evolved, becoming an immortal beast.

Which, by no means, was to denigrate Telma’s stature. Even the arrogant dragon, Nithe, treated her as a peer.

The queen’s nest, Hallowleaf Spire, came into view.

Glued to a crook between thick branches was a fortress. The nest was built from layers of cloth, material chewed by thousands of wasps, then vomited up into sheets of paper. The central turret of this insect city stood two hundred meters tall, its body bulging with equal breadth. From this mass extended three hives, turrets welded to the side of a castle.

Hundreds of man-sized wasps were buzzing in the air.

Of the lesser guardians who served High Queen Telma, eight were Awakened wasp queens. Five lived in Yaalon’s distant branches, guarding against vile interlopers unworthy of Yaalon’s grace. The rest lived in Hallowleaf Spire, a mighty fortress managed by the queen herself along with three of her vassals.

The silver-haired witch descended.

Her broom vanished as she fell, rod and bristles disintegrating into motes. In the last few moments, her ki spread out. Her ruffled skirt puffing with petals, fabric fluttering as she gently set a heeled shoe on Yaalon’s crystal-veined bark.

Whoomp.

With a flutter of his wings, Zephyr landed beside her.

The word branch did not do their position justice. At its base, Yaalon’s trunk was three kilometers in diameter. The world tree’s limbs grew thinner as they grew higher and further, but even here the branch’s breadth was akin to three city blocks. It was so wide, Sylvia could barely see the curve of the surface from where she was standing.

And in this great space existed another wilderness. Dirt and moss clung to the bark, plants emerging from the humus. In addition to the guardians, a million ordinary phantasms called the canopy home. A world tree was not just a representative of the domains primordial and chaos, but also that of life.

Riley slipped off of Zephyr’s back. Warily, the tomboy studied the insects buzzing above.

Steel-needled wasps most closely resembled the hornet. Like the mortal creature, they had yellow heads and striped abdomens. The colors were tinted with a dull, metallic gray. Some of the beasts bore different hues. Orange, green, and blue could be found beside yellow. These colorful creatures were stronger. The typical wasp was C-II/Low. Those found here were C-II/High at the least, with a quarter reaching C-III/Low.

Such a monster could easily match a three sisters roc.

“Is it safe to leave Zephyr here?” Riley questioned, looking nervous.

“High Queen Telma won’t harm her guests,” Sylvia replied. The witch gestured forward. “In fact, our honor guard has already arrived.”

Two wasps approached, both 20% bigger than those Sylvia had seen before. One was hued green, while the other was orange. Her starlight eyes in conjunction with the System picked out a pair of cores in each. The first was set in the thorax, as typical for a steel-needled wasp. The second was in their abdomens.

Interesting. These wasps were C-IV/High. Did Telma have a means to generate chimera of sorts?

“You know, I used to be proud that I wasn’t one of those girls who freaked out when they saw a bee,” Riley commented, looking a little pale.

“These wasps are slightly bigger,” Sylvia allowed, deadpan.

“Slightly,” Riley managed, looking tense.

The orange and green insects landed on either side of them. Their thin, iridescent wings buzzed. Interlaced with this sound was a crude psychic message. “Come… come… queen.”

Steel-needled wasps weren’t like hive ants, who shared their queen’s consciousness. For these wasps to use spirit speech implied a degree of training or intelligence. Few nether beasts could speak. Slyde certainly hadn’t learned, and this trait was even rarer among phantasmal creatures.

“Remember your etiquette,” Sylvia warned as they started along the path.

Riley followed, standing very close to her silver-haired friend. “Immortal beasts care about human etiquette? I thought they had alien souls.”

“They can tell when they’ve been offered respect or refused it,” Sylvia explained. “And they can learn human rituals.”

This was particularly true when the insect queen in question was connected to the internet. Though High Queen Telma might not mind, it wasn’t worth the risk of upsetting her.

“If you don’t mind, I’ll let you do all the – ”

Riley’s words cut off. Both asteri stopped. Sylvia’s expression turned into a grimace.

Coiled in front of the nest was a mass of dark, red scales verging on black. The creature’s eye opened, its socket a slit pit of infernal abyss. Nithe unwound, the massive god beast rising onto all four legs. The serpentine creature was fifty meters long from head to tail, with horns extending like a crown of spikes from his head.

“And finally, our so-called apostle arrives before me,” Nithe sneered, his voice a deep rumble like turning stone.

Sylvia gracefully dipped, raising the hem of her skirt in an elegant curtsy. “Lord Nithe,” she greeted.

Nithe was bigger than Asmodeus. Where the archdemon commanded calamity, this dragon ruled over the abyss. Abyss was the magic of the deep, catalyzed from fire, earth, and water. Hardness, gravity, and powerful grinding forces existed within its domain. Of the basic elements, only ice, wind, and lightning were truly foreign to the abyss.

Spurred by Sylvia’s motion, Riley mustered an awkward curtsy of her own. Without a skirt, the blonde tomboy was forced to pinch the air.

The dragon strutted forward, steps so heavy Sylvia could feel the ground shake. The wasp guards buzzed as Nithe approached. The god beast ignored them, snout leaning close enough that Sylvia could reach out and touch it.

“I came here to make known that you are unworthy,” Nithe spat. A giant eye glared in Sylvia’s direction. “Do you think a lowly monkey can raise my kin? Arrogance!”

Sylvia’s gaze turned cold. An ancient universe of fractal pink stared into the boiling abyss. She knew Nithe could kill her in a snap. The dragon was weaker than Asmodeus for sure, but the power he held was far beyond her own.

“With all due respect, Lord Nithe, you are mistaken about two things,” she said, her voice polite but cold. “First, I have never once called myself the Apostle of Fire. Second, it was Yaalon who accepted this quest. You speak of arrogance. How dare you challenge the will of the world tree?”

Nithe’s head reared back at Sylvia’s sharp retort. The witch could feel the dragon’s rage. His snout loomed over her head, his dreadful aura pressing down upon her like the weight of the world.

“Hmph!”

Nithe huffed. The gust from the dragon’s nostrils stirred Sylvia’s dress.

Dragons were prideful creatures. In an immortal beast, this attribute showed more than in a demon. Demons had human souls, tempering those arrogant emotions with their human nature. Manners and politeness, therefore, were a must. But dragons also disdained the weak and admired courage. If Sylvia didn’t respond to Nithe’s challenge, she’d only earn his disgust.

Sylvia held her cold glare.

Nithe’s head swayed. “Do not fail me, or I will devour you. Again and again, every time you are reborn until you have suffered a hundred years of misery for your mistake.”

With a great leap, the colossal beast took to the sky. Huge wings, as black as night, covered the heavens. An entire football field could fit beneath them. Whomph! Their beat was like a cannon. A hurricane of wind roared in release. Sylvia leaned into the gale, hand on her hat while her twin tails flailed behind her.

The silver-haired girl flicked a hand back, an arm of air catching Riley before she could be thrown. Nithe’s thunderous voice sounded before he vanished.

“And little witch, if you ever dare deny that title again, I will murder you on the spot.”

The titanic dragon plunged beyond the branch’s horizon.

…

“What a nice guy,” Riley commented through gritted teeth.

“I like him more than the last dragon I met,” Sylvia replied blandly.

She allowed her friend a moment to gather herself before entering the hive.

Hallowleaf Spire was thick with wasps. Thousands of them buzzed through the air, crawling in and out of the hexagonal holes. The fortress was a maze of dark corridors and passages. Thankfully, their honor guard showed them the way. After minutes of travel, the witches arrived at the middlemost chamber. The room was huge. The ceiling was a good ten meters above their heads, a glowing crystal set at the pinnacle.

High Queen Telma waited.

To a human, the immortal beast’s appearance was all but identical to the drones that served her. Excepting one feature: her size. Telma was giant, her length three times that of all the wasps Sylvia had seen so far.

“Welcome to the Hallowleaf Spire, Apostle of Fire.”

Telma’s words came out in a series of buzzes and clicks. The noise would’ve been impossible to understand if not for the psychic intent carried by the reverberations.

“It is my honor to be here, High Queen Telma,” Sylvia said, with a curtsy.

Telma’s antennae swished.

“My people have no similar courtesy,” the wasp queen said. “But to stand with His Apostle is my honor, not yours.”

How polite. Telma was friendlier than Empress Idetta. Either that, or Sylvia’s level and her achievements had earned her greater respect.

“This is the one who will raise the child?” the wasp queen questioned, compound eyes reflecting the blonde witch.

Riley rose, uncomfortable with the attention. “With a lot of help from Sylvia and Lady Vallenfelt.”

“I am not the wyrm who questions the will of Yaalon,” Telma buzzed. The wasp’s antennae bowed in reverence. “The Great Work is upon us. You are the people He has chosen. It is only natural that the third child is raised by those who hold His holy fire.”

Sharp green eyes shifted toward Sylvia, curious. Ha. Riley had noticed the apostle this and the apostle that.

“My apologies, your majesty,” Sylvia interrupted. “For now, Prometheus’s hand is better hidden from the gamers, excepting a select few.”

Sylvia gave Riley a look of warning. The tomboy’s posture conveyed the message: you’d better explain later. The silver-haired witch responded with a nod. It was about time her friend learned of Lucifer. Hopefully, Riley wasn’t secretly religious. Or if she was, she didn’t react too badly after learning they were all serving the Devil’s plot.

…

Actually, did Riley even know about Lucifer’s cult and the associated netherworld history? Sylvia felt a headache coming on. That conversation was going to be painfully long.

High Queen Telma’s antennae twitched. “Is she not of your council?”“She is,” Sylvia asserted.

“Then there is no concern,” Telma buzzed haughtily. “Bring the box.”

Four wasps entered the room. A hexagonal box, made from glued paper, was set on the ground between them. Sylvia stepped forward to confirm the contents. Forty-two fruits with a purple peach sitting in the center. The peach’s skin was hard like a shell. She could see it pulse with anima, the breath of life.

An Egg of Origin. Yaalon’s third greater guardian was already incubating.

Once the qilin hatched, it would be fed fruits of the elements: psychic, blood, and soul. A process to be repeated every month for the first year. Sylvia’s heart ached at the unfairness of it. 130,000 merit points. What a waste. What a terrible waste. Just the thought of all those beautiful merit points going down the gullet of a glutton made Sylvia want to cry.

Luckily, Riley would be responsible for feeding the beast. Otherwise, Sylvia might break down and snatch food from a baby.

She closed the lid. Everything was in place, even the two fruits Sylvia had requested for future experiments.

“The knights who brought you here will serve as the child’s guard,” Telma commanded. “Yaalon’s great protector must never leave their sight.”

Riley blanched. Sylvia gave a teasing smile, before dipping in gracious acceptance.

“Thank you for this kindness, Lady Telma.”

“It is not kindness. It is duty,” Telma returned, mandibles clicking. “Apostle of Fire, you have ventured across the planes. I have seen visions of this, cast upon His glorious windows. You have witnessed our enemy. How do you deem my army would fare?”

Sylvia held her tongue while she studied the wasp, struggling to read the high queen’s alien emotions. Honesty, in this case, was for the best. The trouble was, how could she couch her words so they weren’t insulting?

Empress Idetta had a psychic connection with her children, one that’d grown many times over with her evolution and level. The ants could march in the millions. High Queen Telma could influence a few thousand wasps at most. Counting all the queens who served as vassals, she could bring fifty thousand into war.

If such an army faced Remiel’s fleet, was victory possible?

In the starry void? Not a chance. A wasp’s wings wouldn’t work in the astral world. In the skies above Pyrinas? Maybe.

Sylvia imagined Telma’s force descending upon an aerial fleet, a throng of wasps supported by a score of guardians. The cruisers would open fire, scouring the masses as they closed. Tens of thousands would be reaped before things descended into melee. Then the wasps would face angels and Heaven’s phantasms.

Against Remiel, they could do nothing. The thrones and dominions would likewise stand invincible against the insects. But the powerful could not defend everywhere at once. The flood would slip through the gaps, crawling into the ether intakes to reach the ships’ main beams.

World Trees

A world tree is a hybrid phantasm consisting of two or more trees which represent the domains: chaos, primordial, and life. If only two of the domains are present in the chimera, the result is a ‘minor’ world tree. Only a tree with command over all the elements is considered a true world tree.

Yggdrasil is the most famous world tree in the netherworld’s history, having been born during the Age of Myths. There is some dispute whether Yggdrasil formed naturally, but without a doubt, the tree was the greatest of its kind. By the Golden Age, it had already reached legendary scale with its roots extending into seven planes and one material world. This feature allowed for easy travel between these locations.

Yggdrasil fell during the Third Great War, Ragnarok. The destruction of the tree brought the plane of Asgard to near ruin. Even today, Asgard has huge fissures where thick rivers of dissolved ether run.

While in theory world trees can be hybridized using multiple species of phantasm, the most consistent recipe is to combine a primordial pine, a cosmic ash, and a tree of life. Non-traditional formulas exist, having had various levels of success. Some are easier to create, though less potent. All the hybrids are finicky and prone to unexpected issues centuries or millennia into their growth.

When it comes to breeding world trees, the Fey Federation is the unquestionable number one expert. Unsurprisingly, this major power owns 22 trees. Heaven, by comparison, has 14. The smaller number is offset by the greater average age of their world trees, several of which come from the Ancient Era.

Hell, as usual, is the weakest, having only 6. These numbers have been fixed by treaties signed after Ragnarok. It is broadly known, however, that the Fey Federation has 10 additional trees frozen at the moment before maturity in case the treaties are ever violated.

During war, world trees are a powerful asset as they can be positioned in the starry void. This provides an assault platform and a logistical base for planar conquest. Indeed, without a world tree, it’s extremely difficult to take a sovereign plane from any power simply because transport time slows resurrection, giving the locals a massive advantage.
