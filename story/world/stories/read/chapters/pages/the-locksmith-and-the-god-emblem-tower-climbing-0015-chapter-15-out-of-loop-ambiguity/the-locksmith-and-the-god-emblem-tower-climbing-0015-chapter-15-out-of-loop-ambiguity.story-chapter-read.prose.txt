"That seems arbitrary." Harry said while scratching his head. "How should I know exactly the type of ability I'm going to get?"

Devon thought for a moment before answering whilst carefully cleaning his glasses. "Depends on the ambiguity of the symbol."

The young man groaned. "Just give me a straight answer!" He demanded. "We've been talking hypotheticals and variables for almost an hour now."

"Look, kid, you may not like it," He leaned over a table in front of them with a diverse set of documents and scribbles laid out. "But it isn't an exact science. The common sense is that if you chose an Emblem whose symbol represents something mundane, such as a... chair, for example, in the Circle Format, you will be able to summon and manipulate... chairs."

"How's that any useful!? Besides, if two Emblems are very alike, how would they powers differentiate?"

"You'd be surprised how much you can accomplish when you start small. And even the simplest of symbols can have dozens of implications. The same concept could give an individual completely different abilities. You don't gain it all in one go, you know?" He replied, sighing deeply. "My point is: the simpler the symbol, the easier it becomes to control. It's not about learning to control power, but extracting it. Emblems, of course, have a way of trading expectations for reality in a... curious way. In the end, it's your job to interpret the power and how it should fit within your own capacities. Like I said, though, the simpler and unambiguous the symbol is, the clearer the purpose of the Emblem will be. In the end, they're very similar..."

The young man knew better not to call out the inconsistency in a system that Devon, as much as he'd like to deny, seemed pretty knowledgeable in. It seemed a subject of great interest to the librarian. But even so, Harry wouldn't let a mere uncomfortable moment ruin this grasp at understanding it.

"...Is this a perception you have or a major, accepted theory in how Emblems work?" Harry asked, glancing sideways at Devon while pretending to shuffle some papers.

It was already dawn when their studies began. After almost a month, it appeared that every session generated even more qualms that penetrated deep in the wrinkles of Harry's underdeveloped brain. Why is it necessary to use Moon Rock to create an Emblem? What is the need for maintaining the ambiguity of a symbol if the power isn't going to be revealed after the process either way?

The old librarian was tired. Harry's questions and the flame of the candle in front of him were the only things keeping him awake. The young man could keep this up for hours and hours, but Devon was no longer 15 years old.

"It's a me thing." He replied, taking away his glasses. "Well, actually this idea is getting more accepted within the community, so I guess both... Like I said, kid, Emblems aren't an exact science. The one who showed it to the world has been lost to time, so that's all we have to work with."

"The Astronomer, you mean?" Harry asked, leaning over the table, eyes shining. "He's in the Logos Tower, right?"

"The Logos..." Devon chuckled. "No, Harry, that's just a tale. A tale told to drunk sailors created by drunken sailors. Some believe that if you write the three Emblems that were found near his first house, a portal would appear, one that would lead to the Logos Tower. It'd be nice, though, to talk to the man who created it all."

"No one ever found his body." Harry replied, not yet convinced by his mentor. "The only thing they found is the book."

"And the three Emblems who would supposedly lead to said Tower, correct?" Devon added. "How convenient, don't you think?"

"He vanished, Devon."

Unable to continue with the discussion, Devon ceded. "...That's true. He did vanish, and he did leave the Emblem Theory. I'm not saying you shouldn't believe it, but just think about how convenient would be for the only Emblems that could lead to the supposed Logos Tower are behind bars in the capital. To me, it's clearly a way to scare other continents and nations with the supposed power of the unknown. And that's not even talking about all the prisoners they threw in there, supposedly. I mean, why would you go through all the trouble to send a murderer to a Tower beyond the universe instead of just... killing him. I don't know, it's just... it's all too weird."

Harry couldn't argue against it. It really didn't make any sense to send people to the tower, let alone prisoners.

"Maybe we should wrap it up, what do you say?"

"What!?" Harry's pensive state was broken by Devon's words. "No. Of course not. You're gonna bail out just when things were getting interesting? Please, come on, you know how I like to hear you talking about this. You're such a... great teacher. Right?"

Devon narrowed his eyes as he scanned the boy. He stood for several moments before showing a hint of a smile.

"You know..." He slowly sat on the chair that creaked with his weight. "It is not wise to complement the elders out of greed."

"Why not?" Harry asked with a smile. "I'm asking you to pass down your knowledge. Knowledge you gained from being an elder. In retrospection, I'm respecting you."

"Because greed leaves the mind insatiable, and time doesn't wait on ravenous fools." He shared, his hands laying over the table. "Anyway, I'm just messing with you." He sighed deeply. "You know what? Let us go outside for a moment."

"But it's freezing—"

"Come on, don't be a child." The man grinned. "I think better amidst the sound of the wind."

Devon then stood up once more and picked up his glasses. He left his notes protected by the candle as he turned to go to the stairs. They were on the second floor of his shop, near their respective rooms. Like many merchants and business owners, they lived in their establishments, reducing housing costs and making their activities more efficient. It was a behavior frowned upon among the nobles. They considered it disrespectful to have to submit to buying in a place that was also someone's home. They tried to pass a law preventing this, without success. Conclusion: nobles were unbearable. For the most part.

Harry, without Devon seeing, made a face of discontent but followed his orders. He stood up and followed the man whose bones creaked like the old chair they were sitting on. In the darkness, they descended the stairs in silence. Devon opened the door and gestured for Harry to go first.

"Be a gentleman and see if an old man like me can take in the cold." Devon grinned once again.

Rolling his eyes, the boy stepped outside and immediately felt the cold on his cheeks. He turned quickly and went back inside the bookstore.

"I don't think we can go outside, given your condition and age, I don't think the cold will—"

"Oh, shut up and go already." Devon pushed the boy who was complaining softly. "As long as the wind doesn't carry me away, I'll be fine."

They were on the streets of New Cavebrook, now deserted in the early morning. It was a narrow avenue, even narrower during the day. It was a great place to do business, but otherwise? With the prices and rents of the houses, it was easy to see why many people conducted their business from home.

The nobles' palace could be seen on the horizon on one side and the slums on the other. Even Harry, who wasn't a fan of politics, could see the visible inequality among the people of the capital. He didn't care much, however; he had a place he could call home, he worked, and he could leave his past behind. That was enough.

Devon closed the door and locked it with two locks. He glanced through the window on the right and turned to see the boy trembling.

Cloudless, numerous stars filled the sky, which was now drowned in darkness. The two of them continued along the rocky road, the route still unknown to Harry.

"H-How are you not shivering?" He asked, his jaw clenching. "I'm actually surprised it hasn't snowed in those last few days. Fuckin' hell."

"The secret is in your respiration." Even outside their study room, the man found himself teaching Harry. "Take long and deep breaths. Accustom yourself to the cold."

After a few seconds, Harry said. "It's not working."

"Just chill a bit, would ya?" Devon said, hands behind his back. He arranged his glasses with a grin. "You know that monks on the Berry Mountain have to endure this type of weather — even worse, actually — every day? Even there, it's all in the breathing, Harry."

"Well, I'm not a monk." Harry replied muttering. He paused for a second, then added. "How do you, um... How do you know all that? The Emblems and all those concepts."

Devon took a while to answer. Was it wise to teach a young, curious mind, about the Emblems? He saw so many losing themselves to the obsession hidden in those objects. Always searching for another Emblem, another meaning to which they can weaponize. I'll be a winner, Devon. You and I will be gods once I get the second one! He still had nightmares about that day. That's what he feared might happen to Harry.

Even so, he couldn't barricade Harry from the world. Emblems existed. Magic symbols and the control for power, were real. The Astronomer was real.

"Devon?" Harry asked once again, not shivering like he was. "Are you okay?"

"I—Yes, Harry. Sorry." He shook his head as they continued strolling into the night, coming near a theater. "I, um... I used to be from a group that embellished and indulged in those activities. It included Emblems and other... things. I was in the intelligence, so it was required to know such things."

Intelligence? Harry had a hunch to what the other things meant, but for the sake of their relationship, refrained from asking further. Everyone has secrets. He added inwardly.

Devon then stopped to stare at a brightly lit shop window, displaying various pieces of clothing based on characters from a plat. On one side, there was a titan of man conjuring what appeared to be logs from his leg as he prepared to hit a woman and her child; his smile menacing.

"Huh," Devon said, narrowing his eyes. "Each day that passes, the moon gets more unstable, but all they care is entertainment."

"What's it about?" Harry asked, interested.

Maybe it was hypocritical for Devon to say that about the nobles since he himself owned a library. Hate the game, not the player. He thought to himself. In the end, the man had lived enough and wanted a simpler life. Was that such a bad thing to want?

"Dracno." He replied, not taking his eyes off the shop window. "A brute who nearly collapsed the government back in the old days. He's basically a legend today. A demon, almost. He had an Emblem that could control logs, if you would believe it. Of course, they couldn't erase this part of history, and instead told a story about how he was the devil's child. All to cover up the existence of Emblems."

They walked for a few more minutes, each respecting the other's silence. Harry gazed at the stars while Devon was lost in thought.

"Which Emblem do you want to choose, Harry?" Devon asked.

Harry stopped immediately, raising his eyebrows. Seeing Devon's confusion, he continued walking and could barely contain his excitement at hearing his question. Until then, they had only talked about theories and connections throughout history involving the Emblems. Not that Harry didn't appreciate his classes, but he wanted something more... concrete.

"...Are you serious?" he asked, his eyes shining and the cold seemingly gone.

"About what?" Devon said with a teasing smirk. "There's still a long time before we have that conversation. You need maturity, and I need Moon Rock. However, it wouldn't hurt to ask..."

This was a huge step for the young man, who had already imagined himself with millions of different abilities. He considered making ant Emblem with the symbol of a Rope and imagined all the flexibility that this could give him. He had thought of more severe things like the symbol of Fire or Water — natural elements in general — but after that session, reconsidered his decisions.

The young man remained silent for a while, Devon patiently waiting for his decision while the cold failed to shake the only individuals on the streets. They walked alone along the capital's roads, the same roads that Harry would one day use to escape the officers.

The young man then remembered his mother's profession. He remembered her passion and how hard she worked for her two sons. It was a difficult time, and yet she still managed to smile to Harry and Matthews. Only now did he realize his naiveté and how much he had failed to help her. He knew he was just a child, but he still blamed himself for his lack of attention.

Tears streamed down his face, and as soon as Devon noticed them, Harry frantically wiped them away, smiling.

"Has the Lock Emblem already been taken?"
