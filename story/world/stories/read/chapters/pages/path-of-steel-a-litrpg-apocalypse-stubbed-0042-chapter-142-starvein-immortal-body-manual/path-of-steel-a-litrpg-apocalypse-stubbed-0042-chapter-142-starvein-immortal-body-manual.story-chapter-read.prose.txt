The birds chirped afternoonishly. Low conversation slipped through the windows. A slanted ray of sun landed on Gary’s face, rousing him from his slumber, and as his eyes opened, he felt calm. Rested. For the first time since the drafting, and even before it.

He lay in bed, limbs spread widely, breathing in and enjoying the warmth of his blanket and his head on the pillow. Even Goldie was asleep.

I should get up to train. Why am I in bed in the afternoon? some instinct called out, but he ignored it. That was overwork talking. Gary turned around and fell back asleep.

When he awoke again, it was sunset. He dug his head deeper into the pillow with a grin. Shit. How will I sleep at night? he wondered. Nah. I could sleep for three days straight.

He felt even more rested than before. Restless. The evening was beautiful, and he was just lying there by himself. Memories of his duel against Sui Duang came unbidden. It had been hell, but he won. He even completed his quest afterward, smacking that awful Elder in the face.

Life is good, he thought with a grin.

His eyes flickered to the window. He could see the sky. He’d made it. The world had closed around him before the duel, everything disappearing forever until he won. He’d won. The world had returned. He was back.

And Sui Duang wasn’t. His world hadn’t reopened—it had crushed him into nothingness. But such was the reality of battle.

Groggily, Gary opened the blue screens he’d received after the duel.

You are at the peak of Birth of Aura. Excess aura will be safekept. Further aura gains restricted until you break through.

Face-Slapping Quest Completed.

Congratulations! You slapped a very hostile Branch Elder in the face and survived for one minute. How cheeky!

Calculating rewards:

1x Face-Slapping Manual (Uncommon)

1x Crate of Satan Shakes (Rare)

1x Starvein Immortal Body Manual (Legendary)

Gary’s brows shot up. These rewards sounded extremely promising, but he tried to keep his hopes down.

He started from the face-slapping manual. A palm-sized booklet appeared in his hand, with an open hand carved on its front. Gary sighed. Still lying down, he flipped through the book, which was empty.

“The hell?” he wondered.

The book suddenly dissolved into motes of light which entered his hands and traveled through his body to his brain. Synapses fired. For an instant, Gary could sense his entire nervous system and see himself like he was transparent. When the sensation went away, he suddenly had intimate knowledge of the physiology around a person’s cheek. Not just humans, too—he knew the facial structure of over a dozen species, some of which he’d seen in the Shores of Glory. If he struck them in the face, he knew exactly where to aim to hurt them most—and, if he wanted to offend them, he suddenly had knowledge of each species’ most sensitive subjects.

For example, the lion-like humanoids were big on pride. Insulting it publicly was the greatest humiliation. The mantis-like creatures could be riled up if you questioned their loyalty to the hive, and felines would be appalled at comments about their inability to secure allies.

As for humans… Well, a lot of things irritated humans. He had a whole list.

“What the hell?” he repeated, grabbing his head. “How did they just give me information? I thought… Oh man, I don’t know if this place is amazing or terrifying.”

The crate of Satan Shakes was, well, a crate. It appeared with a poof next to Gary’s bed. He sat up, scratching his head at the devil’s face printed on the lid. It wasn’t the skinny, evil look he was used to from Earth. This Satan had thick, robust features, a square jaw, and a manly half-smile, and he was pointing a thumb at himself. Of course, he was red.

“Wanna become like me? Drink my Shakes!” Gary read the tagline.

Back when he’d gotten the massive Vitality increase from the Seed of Eternal Flesh, which he obviously hadn’t wanted to reveal, Asim had advised him to say he’d found a box of Satan Sprinting Shakes. It was a valuable resource which sometimes fell from the fourth floor to the third. That was the extent of Gary’s knowledge.

“Who the hell is Satan, and why is he selling gym supplements?” he wondered. The lid came off with a pop. Inside, Gary could see three sets of three shakes, all neatly in their own little cooling bottles. The first set held three red coolers, in a long box laden with red wood chips. Then came three blue coolers, and three green coolers. He picked one up.

Satan Lifting Shake (Uncommon)

Tired of being a weak-ass bitch? Wanna lift weights, buff up, and get the chicks? Satan Lifting Shakes are all you need! For a limited time only, buy four and get the fifth for free!

Satan Lifting Shakes work best when combined with exercise and a low-carb diet. Consult your doctor if you’re a bitch.

This description was paid for by Satan Jr.

“Jr.? Who the— Since when can we pay for descriptions!?”

Gary: Quirk, what is this shit?

Quirk: Sup bud. I assume you’re referring to the Satan Shakes, the Tower’s most premium low-grade body enhancement supplements.

Gary: Why are you selling them to me???

Quirk: I have a script, bud. I can’t ignore sponsored products. Anyway, yeah, it’s high-end body enhancement for low-level climbers. Like the Muscle Sap but better.

Gary: Then why the hell is Muscle Sap illegal?

Quirk: Because it can be mass-produced. Satan Shakes are much rarer and twice as effective.

Gary: So?

Quirk: Hey, I don’t make the rules, I just bend them. What was your question?

Gary: What the hell is this shit?

Quirk: Just try them.

Gary closed the chat and re-inspected the cooler in his hands. It was red and opaque—no clue of what lay inside. He popped the lid and took a swig. The contents were a little too spicy, even for his enhanced body. It felt like drinking fire that tasted like shit. He made a face as he swallowed, expecting his strength to rise, and nothing happened.

Sighing, Gary downed the entire cooler. It felt like chugging a waste pipe. Strength +2, came the notification. “Oh!” he said. “That’s actually really good!”

He looked at his physical stats.

Strength: 49

Speed: 47

Vitality: 99

They were coming along nicely. Especially Vitality. It was only five months ago that his stats were two, three, and two.

If each of these is two points… Gary thought, looking at the eight remaining shakes.

Half an hour later, he was sprawled on the bed, holding onto his belly. The blue shakes were called Satan Speedy Shakes and increased Speed. They tasted like stale water. The green ones, the Satan Sprinting Shakes, increased Vitality and had little pieces of what tasted like liver swimming in what tasted like fat.

Never again, Gary swore, desperately holding the thunderous gas in his intestines. He’d made the mistake of releasing it once and could swear the mountain shook. Finally, he couldn’t take it. He grabbed his spear and sprinted into the forest, little pockets of poison leaking behind him.

“Where are we going?” Goldie said in a sleepy voice.

“Close your ears,” Gary replied.

“Why?”

He reached a clearing deep in the forest behind the mountain and let it rip. The leaves were ruffled. A squirrel fainted.

“Woah! Is that a new attack?” Goldie said, manifesting as a golden snake. “Blegh,” she said, dispersing immediately. “Gary! What did you eat?”

“I drank some shakes,” he replied weakly. “I think I’m clear now.”

“Did you save any of those shakes? You could feed them to your worst enemy.”

“Hey, there were benefits.”

Opening his stats again, he glowed at the change.

Strength: 52

Speed: 52

Vitality: 104

The nine shakes together had increased each of his stats by five. That was a whooping fifteen points in total—the equivalent of almost four levels in Birth of Aura. His battle prowess had just noticeably improved, and the value of what he’d ingested was evident by how crazy people went over a single drop of Muscle Sap, which increased Strength by one.

However…

Gary: Hey! I just went over a hundred points in Vitality! Shouldn’t I get a reward or something?

Quirk: If we rewarded every climber who got past a hundred points in a stat, the Tower would run out of rewards.

Gary: Come on!

Quirk: Do you really want a reward for that?

Gary: Yeah?

Quirk: Really really?

Gary: I guess?

Quirk: Good news—you already have it! Check out that body manual. And stop bothering me for hand-outs.

Gary: Not my fault your system is cheap.

“I was just asking…” he muttered by himself.

Gary thought about his quest rewards. The face-slapping manual had been a little silly, but this crate alone made the quest worth it. If Gary faced Sui Duang again, he was confident the battle would unfold differently. He’d still be losing, but less.

“Wow! I’m so glad I chose you, you’re getting awesomer by the day!” Goldie exclaimed when Gary explained the shakes.

“Thanks, Goldie,” he replied with a smile.

“What about that body manual?”

“Let’s find out…” Gary said, licking his lips.

The previous—and only—legendary item Gary had used was the Temper Serum, which had had tremendous impact on his cultivation. If this was on the same level…

Then again, it did come with the face-slapping manual and the satan shakes. How good could it be?

Gary tried to temper his expectations as he willed the third reward to appear. It did—with a disappointing lack of fanfare. An old-looking, dusty manual appeared over Gary’s hands. It was short, maybe fifty pages long, with a brown cover that had turned gray by the years. It was also covered by a layer of dust. If Gary saw this in the market, he wouldn’t spare it a second glance.

“Uh, excuse me?” he said.

The manual had appeared in a soft sphere of light, which slowly deposited it to Gary’s hands. As soon as the light disappeared, however, Gary’s entire complexion changed. Gravity tore the book from his hands. It landed on the forest floor hard enough to quake it. The dull thud echoed.

“What the…” Gary muttered, looking at his red hands. He had over fifty strength—almost twenty times that of a pre-drafting person. “How heavy is that book!?” he exclaimed.

“Hnnng! It’s really heavy!” Goldie said, putting her tail under the edge of the cover. “I can’t lift it Gary, help!”

“Careful,” he said, pulling her tail out. Noting how the book had sank deep into the dirt, he grabbed the cover and pulled.

“Woah!” he said, finally raising it upright. When he pushed it out, the cover fell to the other side, again shaking the ground. Gary was left staring.

“This is so heavy!” Goldie said.

“Yeah…”

“It must be really good! Quick, Gary, read it, read it!”

Gary shook his head to clear it. The cover had been plain, but on the very first page, in large, rough letters as if carved with a knife, it spelled, Starvein Immortal Body.

“What is this made of?” Gary muttered. Then, and only then, did the description appear.

Starvein Immortal Body Manual, Legendary

Buckle up, buddy. It’s lore season.

Body cultivation is a separate type of cultivation which focuses purely on the physical body. Instead of using aura to affect the world around them, practitioners use it to make themselves strong, fast, and durable—ultra buff, essentially.

It sounds cool, and it was popular in the early days of the Tower until energy cultivators started organizing in sprawling clans. The problem with body cultivation is that it requires massive amounts of resources, which only get more massive as you rise in realms. It also demands extreme willpower of the user, as they often have to subject themselves to abject torment while forging their body. For these reasons, it impossible for body cultivators to create large clans, so the practice was abandoned.

The few remaining body cultivators are usually wanderers or small, isolated families. The scions of rich clans may engage in body cultivation on the side, but few do it since it demands time and effort that would be better used in training their primary aura principle.

All this goes to say that body cultivation is hard and probably not worth your while. You’re already an energy cultivator—cultivating your body as well will make you stronger in your current rank, but it will slow down your growth, and it will become meaningless if you cannot keep it up with your energy cultivation. Dual-cultivating energy and body is a hyper-ambitious path that can only work through tremendous resources, extreme dedication, and impossible fortune. I’d say drop it. Or sell it.

However, since the System gave you this manual as a reward, you should at least take a look.

The Starvein Immortal Body Manual was a highly-respected scripture even in its time. The family that pioneered it was one of the strongest in the Tower, and it is rumored that even one of the Trailblazers cultivated this method. Its popularity experienced a massive drop, however, and the reason why wasn’t properly catalogued. Almost nobody has cultivated it in the last few millennia. I thought all copies were lost, but apparently we still had one in the Vault.

Why it was given to you, I have no idea, but I don’t think the Overseer likes you very much. Food for thought.

Gary finished reading the description and thought about it for a long time.

He flipped through the book, struggling with every page. The first section was an introduction. It described that practitioners of this art siphoned aura from the stars to turn their bodies into living constellations. They could cause earthquakes by stomping the ground, break off mountain peaks to throw at their enemies, throw punches that shattered spacetime, and walk into the core of stars with their bare bodies.

“No way,” Gary muttered.

“These are all lies!” Goldie said, reading over his shoulder.

Gary kept flipping. According to the book, the creator of this body cultivation method had been inspired by the Starvein Immortals, a race of star-spawn humanoids that once dominated the universe. The strongest of them could lift celestial bodies and detonate punches with the power of exploding stars. They could pluck stars out of the night sky and hurl them at their enemies. Their greatest feature, however, was their indestructibility, hence their name. Even if you killed a Starvein Immortal, they could regenerate completely from a single drop of blood.

Goldie shook her head. “Nonsense. If they were that strong, where are they now?”

“It’s obviously an exaggeration, but maybe there’s a hint of truth? I don’t know. I didn’t think aliens and magic existed until a few months ago.”

“Maybe you can cultivate it and find out?”

“Yeah. Well, not right now. It’s my one rest day. I’m not cultivating anything until tomorrow.”

“Okay,” she said, wrapping around his arm and resting her head on his shoulder.

“You know, if this doesn’t work, it’s still an ancient manual. I could probably sell it to a collector for tons of aura stones,” Gary said.

“Woah, great idea! You’re smart!”

“Why did you sound surprised?”

“Because I am.”

Gary laughed. He closed the manual again, moving one page at a time. As much as the contents sounded like an exaggeration, the book’s weight was real. Its age was real. The promises it made were impressive, and most importantly, his high vitality made him well-suited for it—the introduction clearly stated that a hundred points in Vitality was the absolute minimum requirement to begin practicing the first stage.

He couldn’t help getting excited. “I think we struck gold this time, Goldie. Remind me to never doubt a quest again.”

“Okay! But this won’t help you beat Feng Shen tomorrow, right?”

“Right, but everything else will,” Gary replied with a glint in his eye. “Everyone says I can’t beat him, but we’ll see.”

“I believe in you!”

“I know you do, Goldie. I couldn’t do this without you.”

Her little snake body puffed up with pride. “I’m awesome, too!” she said. “Don’t worry, Gary. Even if we don’t beat Feng Shen this time, he won’t kill us, so we can become stronger and try again!”

“Don’t say it like that. We’ll win. I’m counting on you.”

“You’re counting on me? Then, I’ll give you a thousand percent of my power! Let’s break his face, Gary!”

He laughed, closing the last page of the manual and trying to pick it up. He failed, of course. The book was so heavy he could barely move a page at a time. Gary looked around, at the wide open forest, then at the book just lying there. “Shit.”
