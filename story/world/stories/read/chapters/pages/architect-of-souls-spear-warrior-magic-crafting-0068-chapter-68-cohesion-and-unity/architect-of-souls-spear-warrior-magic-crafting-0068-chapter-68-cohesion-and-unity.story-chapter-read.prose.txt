The next morning after breakfast, Ferris and Jade set out for the library.

When they arrived at the massive stone building, they joined the queue of people in the entryway. The line moved quickly, and soon Ferris was at the front speaking to an attendant.

“Welcome to the library, Adept. My name is Amelia Hollowfield. How may I be of service today?”

“Hello Amelia, I am Adept Ferris Bluereef, retainer of Hollowfield. I am here to purchase a technique, as well as view one I previously purchased. Additionally, I would like to request information on another subject.”

She took him to a private room with a table, a couple of chairs, and a cot for meditating. Once they were settled at the table, he gave her more details.

“I would like to purchase access to the technique for learning the tier one ability Acid Resistance. Then, I would appreciate some time to view it and the Basic Crossbow Construct Crafting technique with a jade slip reader.”

Amelia nodded, then asked, “And the other question?”

“I am interested in any information or techniques related to spirit animals.”

“I will see what we have and be back in a moment.”

Jade began exploring the room as they waited, while Ferris sat at the table watching the slime’s patterns. He was starting to learn how Jade would move, even without the subtle information sent over the bond. Thinking about it, Ferris realized he had a better understanding of any room Jade had explored. He constantly sent his vision over the bond, but maybe Jade was doing the same with their mental map.

Ameila returned with an identity reader, a jade slip reader, and the two jade slips.

“The price for Acid Resistance Ability Construction is twelve merit points. When you are ready, you can place your personal token here to finalize the transaction.”

He noticed that she didn’t mention anything about the two introductory technical manuals that Gilbert kept bringing up. Either there was a note about him that said they had already been suggested, or Gilbert was just more willing to give advice than Amelia. She hadn’t given her family name, like Gilbert Booker had—not that he really expected her to. He’d been surprised when Gilbert had, after all.

Ferris tapped his token, and she handed him the reader and two jade slips.

“For the information on spirit animals, I am afraid that we don’t have anything available for tier one. There are options available for tier two Adepts, but you would have to seek permission from an Elder to access them.”

“Thank you,” Ferris said. That matched what his uncle knew… but it contradicted what Adept Nyoka said. Or at least what she had implied.

“Our records indicate that this is the first technical manual you have purchased that includes spiritual delivery of an item. Everything should be on the jade slip. Please let me know immediately if there are any problems,” she said as she stood up.

Ferris nodded but wasn’t sure what to say. He would just need to check the jade slip to learn more.

Amelia left him alone, and Ferris slotted the jade slip for Acid Resistance into the spiritual device. The two pieces of jade looked identical, and if Amelia hadn’t pointed out which was which, he would have been guessing.

He laid down to meditate on the cot and called Jade over into his arms, holding the spiritual device in one hand. Meditation came as easily and quickly as normal; or in other words, it came slowly and with effort.

Still, about fifteen minutes later, Ferris found the emptiness of his soul. He passed over entering his soulspace to find the device’s gate.

After entering the gate, Ferris and Jade found themselves in what appeared to be a massive hall. The ceiling was tall, and the room stretched out in all directions.

In the center of the room was a strange building. The first impression Ferris had was of an abstract porcupine. It was like an upside-down bowl with rods coming out of it. At the end of each rod, there was a ball. The surface of the bowl itself wasn’t solid. Instead, there was a mesh of triangular metal beams. The rods were sticking out from the center of each.

All around it were smaller constructions. Upon inspection, each looked like a specific part of the structure in the center, except displayed by itself. Each had a sign with a number.

Near the gate, he found two pedestals. On one was a large book, and the other had a white stone with a note. Jade immediately left to go explore while Ferris approached the pedestals.

Looking over the note, it was instructions for how to learn the ability. There were three things he needed to do. First, he would take the white rock, called a pattern stone, to his soulspace. The pattern stone apparently had a ‘magical resonance’ matching the Acid Resistance ability. Next, he would construct a structure around the pattern stone following the instructions in the book. Lastly, he would acquire several magical resources that support the concept of the ability. Each magical resource would be placed on the pattern stone, which would absorb them.

After that, he would have to wait and see if his Core Tree would produce an ability fruit for Acid Resistance. All of the steps merely increased the chance of it happening, although enough to be worth doing. Once he had a seed, it could be placed on the pattern stone, which would grow to use the resources and building for the ability.

He’d long known that Adepts could share abilities, but this was the first time he saw how it was done. Nothing explained how the pattern stone was created, but it likely needed a working ability to create a ‘pattern’ of.

At the end of the day, it still relied on the Core Tree. Luck could be fickle, and Ferris hoped he wouldn’t need to wait too long to receive a fruit for the ability.

The process made him wonder, what would happen if two cultivators tried to share a seed from the Core Tree? There must be some reason that doesn’t work, Ferris thought. If it did, there’d be an entire market of Adepts selling each other seeds. I’d have heard about it.

With a sigh, he picked up the book and began reading.

Acid Resistance is an ability with two sides: connectivity and sacrifice. The connectivity side follows the ideal of stability through cohesion and unity. Each piece stays strong by relying on the support of those around it. The sacrifice side creates a piece to give up in place of what is important.

Following this guide, you will build both. Together, they can hold you together while sacrificing qi to resist active acidic attacks.

Ferris thought back to the library books he’d read on mundane acid. They claimed acid worked by bonding, which had the consequence of separating after becoming something different. From that perspective, resisting acid by sacrificing something made sense, as the acid would bond to the sacrifice instead.

However, using connectivity confused him. Acid didn’t tear things apart directly, so how did holding tightly together counter it? He knew some magical acids worked by aiding the process with components that weren’t technically acid, like heat or vibration. Was the same logic used for defensive abilities?

The book didn’t explain, but maybe Serena would.

Ferris flipped through the pages. The first was a list of requirements. The materials listed were 3,280 qi and two items: a handheld metal pyramid (32mp), and a lizard’s tail (32mp). Ferris had been told abilities would require magical resources, so he assumed both items were special in some way. He wasn’t familiar with how this all worked, but the names appeared a bit… simple. Shouldn’t it be something like ‘Morrigan’s Wonderous Pyramid of Mystery’ or ‘Thunderous Lizard King’s Tail’ like the stories? Would a tail from any lizard work, or was it a specific monster? There were a number and a unit—thirty-two mp—next to both items’ names, but no other details. He didn’t think it meant thirty-two pyramids or tails from the way it was written. He desperately hoped mp didn’t stand for merit points.

Interestingly, the two items had several alternatives mentioned. Their names were just as simple, yet Ferris saw a pattern where they followed the themes of connectivity and sacrifice. The pyramid could be replaced by a metal bar, a spider’s web, or a honeycomb. The lizard’s tail listed a porcupine’s quill, a bee’s stinger, and a scorpion’s tail as replacements. Ferris remembered his comparison of the building to an abstract porcupine... perhaps the similarity was not accidental.

The confusing part was the absence of anything other than qi and the two items. The giant metal mesh cage alone would need a lot of base materials… was it all made from qi? Ferris still couldn’t numerically measure qi, but the amount listed looked very high and very specific at 3,280. Ferris remembered the simple crossbow he made was supposed to cost 60 qi—although he had no idea how much he actually spent… especially with his mistakes.

The next couple of pages were diagrams of the completed building. They had a lot of measurements and were very dense. After that, the book appeared divided into sections, with each one having a number. Each section would describe how to build a component, with a much smaller diagram and set of instructions.

Walking up to one of the smaller constructions, Ferris compared the number on the sign to the book’s section. They matched, and the page described how to build the example behind the sign. It also listed how many of the components would be needed in total.

Flipping through, the descriptions got more and more complicated. They all referenced parts built in previous sections.

After that, the book had a glossary of techniques used in the construction, and a reference to where to learn each technique. This part worried Ferris, as there were many different terms he had never heard of before. At least they all had a single common source for where he could learn them: Mental Materials.

It was concerning, yet not unexpected. Ferris would just need to see if Serena’s help was enough, or if he would need to purchase the other manual first. He did need it eventually. If he remembered correctly, it cost six merit points.

Looking at the book in his hands, Ferris wanted to copy it onto his own paper and then move it over to his soulspace. Only… the book had over one hundred pages, and Ferris didn’t have enough qi to make that many pieces of paper. It also had precise drawings and diagrams, which he wasn’t confident he could duplicate. Lastly, he would need to sketch the example components, probably from several different angles. That would need at least another twenty-five pages, one for each example.

Likely, copying the book would need to wait. He could work on building the ability while meditating at the library, and then referencing the book directly when needed. When he had the spare qi, he could start duplicating the book. He had almost ten years of his contract left with Clan Hollowfield, after all.

Flipping to the front of the book, Ferris looked at the first section and then walked over to its example.

A simple rod of metal, about the size of a broom handle, sat in front of the sign with a #1 on it.

The instructions stated to qi form 160 of these metal rods with the precise dimensions listed in the diagram. As for how to make them… several techniques were listed, but the book didn’t explain the details.

Ferris sighed; the first step was already beyond him. He knew that abilities required knowledge, time, and effort to develop, but seeing it so directly was a bit disheartening.

After spending some time getting familiar with the different parts, Ferris decided to hold off on anything else until he met Serena. He picked up the pattern stone off its pedestal, called Jade over—the slime had been wandering around between the constructions—and left the spiritual device. Thankfully, the gate let him take the stone, unlike when he tried with the tools inside the spirit gem refinement device.

Back in his soulspace, Ferris decided to create a new box to store the pattern stone in. He expected that if anything happened to it, the clan would happily replace it… for more merit points. The instructions said it would absorb magical resources, so he liked the idea of locking it up by itself. He chained the new box to the one holding his seed for Assimilate Object, and then left the soulspace.

Waking up, Ferris stretched. Acid Resistance was going to be a lot of work, and he wasn’t even sure where to get started. Hopefully, Serena would point him in a good direction.

In the meantime, he had another jade slip to look through. When he first purchased Basic Crossbow Construct Crafting, he’d been in a rush.

Now, it was time to take a more thorough look.
