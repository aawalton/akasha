Nyxia stepped back, her crimson eyes narrowing as she critically evaluated the drape of my ducal cape. She reached out, adjusting a silver clasp on my shoulder with a sharp, precise tug that felt suspiciously like a reprimand.

The air in the private dressing chamber was stifling, not from heat, but from the suffocating pressure of absolute, unified female judgment.

I stood completely rigid, afraid that if I breathed too deeply, I would ruin the "line" of the fabric.

Behind Nyxia, seated around a low, glass-topped table, were three more distinguished ladies sipping tea and offering their unsolicited commentary. My mother, Lyra, and Ela.

"The shoulders are still a bit... aggressive, don't you think?" my mother mused, setting her porcelain teacup down on its saucer. She tilted her head, analyzing my silhouette with a general’s eye. "We want him to project authority, not look like he's about to lead a cavalry charge."

"It's his posture, Mother," Nyxia murmured in agreement. It still caught me slightly off guard how easily that word slipped past her lips now; my mother had drilled the familiar address into her with terrifying, affectionate persistence since the day she arrived on the ship. "He stands like a drawn bowstring. Alarion, loosen your jaw. You look like you're trying to bite through an iron bar."

"He looks very shiny," Lyra chimed in, swinging her legs off the edge of her chair. She took a messy bite of a scone, spraying crumbs across the expensive rug. "Like a very fancy beetle."

Ela, sitting beside her, nodded in solemn agreement. She was currently occupied with feeding small, sugary pastries to Kaelus.

The Dragon Prince, lounging luxuriously on Ela’s lap in his cat-sized form, looked immensely smug. He was reveling in the VIP treatment, and I could feel his amusement vibrating through our bond. He was enjoying my suffering far more than the pastries.

You look ridiculous, Brother, Kaelus projected, his mental voice dripping with schadenfreude. Like a peacock that fell into a vat of blue dye.

I control your food supply, I shot back.

Ela controls my food supply, he countered smoothly, opening his maw as the little girl popped another treat onto his tongue. And she likes me better than you.

My father had recognized the trap hours ago. When my mother first mentioned the "importance of proper presentation" for the upcoming diplomatic call with the Dwarven High King, Kaelen Wight had suddenly remembered an incredibly urgent tactical meeting regarding the deployment of his nonexistent troops. He had literally sprinted out of the room, leaving me alone to face the firing squad.

This was the nineteenth outfit I had been forced to put on.

[Correction, Master,] Tes’s voice whispered in my mind, cold and precise. [This is the twenty-ninth iteration of your current wardrobe assembly.]

Thank you, Tes, I grumbled internally. The salt in the wound is deeply appreciated.

This was just a video call. A long-distance, holographic transmission over a secured network. But to the women of my family, it was a summit between world leaders, and they were treating it with the gravity of a coronation.

If a video call required twenty-nine outfit changes, I dreaded the thought of my actual, public wedding ceremony. Would they make me try on every single piece of fabric synthesized in the Obsidian Fang? I would probably have to build a dedicated AI just to manage my wardrobe schedule.

I braced myself for the inevitable rejection. I waited for Nyxia to shake her head, for my mother to sigh and summon the next abomination from the racks.

To my absolute shock, Nyxia gave a slow, measured nod of approval.

I looked at the gallery. Ela gave me a shy nod and a tiny thumbs-up, a gesture she had recently learned from Varuna. My mother narrowed her eyes for a long, agonizing moment, scrutinizing a seam near my collar, before finally offering a single, decisive nod.

I looked at Lyra.

She was the final judge. The Supreme Arbiter of Fashion. She held her head between her thumb and index finger, her sapphire eyes narrowed in deep, critical thought.

The room held its breath. Even Kaelus stopped chewing.

If she rejected it, I was doomed. I could see the edge of the thirtieth outfit hanging on a rack nearby—a velvet monstrosity that looked like it belonged on a particularly flamboyant pirate. The metallic clinking of the hangers swaying gently on the rack sent a cold shiver down my spine.

Tes, I projected, desperation creeping into my thoughts. Put 'invent comfortable, modern clothing' at the absolute top of the priority queue. These medieval formal clothes will be the death of me. I need suits. I need trench coats. I need clothes that don't require an instruction manual and three assistants to put on.

Lyra slowly slid off her chair. She walked toward me, her face deadly serious. She stopped right in front of me, her brow furrowed. She leaned closer, her small hands reaching out.

She plucked a single, microscopic loose thread from the hem of my ducal cape. She examined the thread, then carefully patted the fabric back into place.

She stood back, placing her hands firmly on her hips, and lifted her chin high.

"Perfect," she declared triumphantly.

The tension in the room vanished. The maids, who had been standing rigidly by the door holding the next wave of outfits, let out a collective sigh of relief. A consensus had been reached. The final garb was locked in.

I caught a glimpse of myself in the full-length mirror.

It was an elegant ensemble in the deep, signature blue of House Wight, paired with a charcoal-grey, high-collared tunic. The heavy, silver signet ring of my house rested on my finger. The dark, flowing ducal cape hung heavy on my shoulders. I looked polished, regal, and utterly ridiculous. I looked like a character straight out of a shojo manga from my old world.

This entire ordeal had started innocently enough at breakfast, when my mother casually asked what I planned to wear for the meeting with King Valdric.

I had suggested my standard, black tactical uniform. Or, better yet, the Reaper armor.

I decided to try my luck one last time.

"I still think the Reaper armor would be a better fit to meet the Dwarven King," I said, adjusting the cuffs of the tunic. "They respect steel. Showing up in silk feels... soft."

Almost as if on cue, the doors at the end of the room hissed open. The Reaper armor stepped into the room. It often operated on autopilot via Tes, following me around the ship or waiting in a nearby staging area, ready to deploy via mag-lev transport in seconds. It stood there, its crimson and black plating gleaming, an imposing, faceless titan of war.

My mother didn't even blink. She slowly turned her head and looked at Patricia.

Patricia didn't need a verbal instruction. The head maid walked smoothly across the room, grabbed the towering, multi-ton suit of advanced combat armor by its heavy collar, and unceremoniously dragged it backward out the door. She locked it in a large, reinforced storage closet down the hall.

Technically speaking, the Reaper’s servo-motors possessed enough torque to rip a bank vault in half. It was vastly stronger than Patricia. But the authority of my mother was a force that even Tes did not dare oppose. I suspected the AI’s subroutines recognized my own profound unwillingness to argue with Seraphine Wight, and adjusted the armor's behavioral protocols accordingly.

The high-altitude staging ground in the northern peaks of Khaz'Modan was a flat, windswept plateau carved directly into the living rock. The air was thin, biting cold, and usually silent, save for the howling wind.

Today, the silence was broken by the whine of repulsor lifts.

A heavy Revenant carrier broke through the cloud layer, escorted by a tight formation of Wyvern fighters. The blocky, utilitarian transport settled onto the stone plateau with a heavy crunch.

The rear ramp slammed down.

The Aegis diplomatic and engineering team inside expected a frosty reception. They had prepared for intense scrutiny, suspicious glares, and perhaps a ring of heavy dwarven ballistae pointed at their hull.

When the massive, hydraulic ramp finally locked into place, the Aegis personnel were greeted by a wall of armored dwarves. The heavy crossbows and rune-etched axes were indeed present, aimed squarely at the dark interior of the carrier.

But the moment the dust cleared and the silver crest of the roaring lion—emblazoned on the chest plates of the descending automata—became visible, the atmosphere shifted violently.

The weapons were lowered. The stern, suspicious glares melted away.

And then, the dwarves swarmed.

They didn't attack. They flooded the landing pad, rushing past the bewildered Aegis guards and swarming the carrier.

An Aegis engineer, clad in his bulky Mark III-E armor, let out a startled yelp as a stout dwarf with a braided red beard practically tackled him. The dwarf didn't draw a weapon; he snatched the engineer's diagnostic data-pad right out of his gauntleted hands. The dwarf began tapping the screen frantically, his eyes wide with manic intensity, muttering rapidly in a dialect of deep-mountain Dwarvish.

Another group of dwarves bypassed the personnel entirely, swarming the exterior hull of the Revenant carrier. They ran their calloused hands over the seamless, adamantium-weave plating. They tapped the metal with small, specialized hammers, listening to the resonance. They peered into the intake vents of the repulsor lifts, completely ignoring the intense, ambient heat.

It was a free-for-all.

None of the Aegis team was more exasperated than the lead envoy sent by Nyxia. Morrigan von Ingelheim stood near the base of the ramp, her arms crossed over her chest. She was an elite mage of House Black, trained in shadow diplomacy and subtle manipulation. Since her Lady had married the scion of House Wight, Morrigan ’s assignments had grown increasingly absurd.

She pinched the bridge of her nose, feeling a massive headache building behind her eyes. She was an ambassador, a master of deadly, polite conversation. She was currently watching a group of heavily armed, ancient warriors behaving like unsupervised children unleashed in a candy store.

What broke her composure entirely was the sight of the dwarven monarch himself.

High King Valdric Ironhand, a figure of terrifying legend, was currently lying flat on his back on the freezing stone. He had shimmied underneath the hovering Revenant carrier and was enthusiastically poking at the primary gravitic emitter array with the handle of his warhammer, shouting excitedly to a group of scribbling gnomes.

They were completely unfamiliar with the concept of molecular bonding. They were looking for rivets, for welds, for the seams holding the massive ship together. The seamless construction was blowing their minds, and they had lost all pretense of diplomatic decorum.

Morrigan spotted one anomaly amidst the chaotic, squabbling mass of craftsmen. A single dwarf was walking purposefully toward her, navigating the crowd with practiced ease.

He was the only dwarf in the immediate vicinity not currently trying to dismantle her transport ship.

He stopped in front of her, offering a stiff, formal bow. "I am Hedric," he rumbled. "Ambassador of the Adamant Forge-Kingdom. Please, forgive my people. They are... unable to hold themselves back when presented with unfamiliar craft."

Morrigan took a deep breath, marshaling her fractured patience. She had a strict deadline. The Lord Commander required this secure, communications array established and operational within three hours. How was she supposed to organize a construction crew while the locals were busy trying to reverse-engineer her landing gear?

"I am Morrigan von Ingelheim," she replied, her voice cool and perfectly modulated. "Ambassador of Aerthos. It is a pleasure to make your acquaintance."

She pointed a slender hand toward the king, who was now arguing loudly with a gnome over the thermal output of the repulsor vents.

"The Lord Alarion wishes to converse with your King," Morrigan continued, struggling to maintain her diplomatic facade. "And I require my personnel to set up the communications array. Would you assist me in organizing them? We cannot establish a stable, secure network while your monarch is actively trying to pry the casing off our landing craft."
