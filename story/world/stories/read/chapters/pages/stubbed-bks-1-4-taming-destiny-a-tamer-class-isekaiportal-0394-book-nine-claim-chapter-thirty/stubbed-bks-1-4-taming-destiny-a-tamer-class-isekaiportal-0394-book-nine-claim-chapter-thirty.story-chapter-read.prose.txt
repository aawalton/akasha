Of course, my first move is to alert the leaders on the other ships. As it turns out, Elarion was about to contact me about the same thing – one of his mages had also noticed the odd behaviour of the water.

The other lords are varying degrees of alarmed, though none of them are able to explain what the reason for the water might be. Even Captain Sor’az has apparently never seen it before, which is alarming in its own way. His accusatory stare is unfortunately justified.

We quite quickly agreed to pull all harvesting teams back from the roc and to make preparations to set sail as soon as possible. Josiah is a little reluctant, but he doesn’t argue this time – he’s visibly perturbed by the water investigation.

The oddest factor of the situation is that the water is only noticeably different when someone interacts physically with it. As I’d observed, and as is reinforced by the reports of other mages, it appears normal when viewed through magical sight or any magical Skills.

“What could affect water in such a way?” I ask Sarran quietly as we stare over the railing of the ship at Sor’az’s vessel. As the smallest, lightest, and most agile of the fleet, he’s the one testing just how much leeway we have with the current.

My Expedition Steward hesitates for a long moment.

“There are few things that I have read of, none of them good,” he answers finally. I glance at him, seeing the tight expression on his face.

“For example?”

“A rift seems to be the most likely option. There is little research about their effect on water, but they’re often sources of oddities, whether through the beasts they release onto the world, or the rift itself.”

I sigh tiredly.

“Another rift? We’ve only just finished with the last one,” I complain. And I know that it’s illogical – just because a rift appeared recently in Azaarde means nothing about my chances of finding one in the ocean. Yet, something doesn’t ring true about that explanation.

Out in the sea, Sor’az’s vessel is managing to move. He first tried to sail against the current – we need to know if we can go back to where the current is less syrupy. That met with failure, but sailing across the current seems to be slightly more successful. He’s still losing ground, though – the current is pulling him forwards even as he sails.

I turn back to Sarran.

“Why would the soothsayer talk about ‘energies are drawn to energies’ if it was a rift? They don’t actually draw energies towards themselves, right?”

Sarran hesitates again, his expression conflicted.

“For all that rifts are both incredibly bothersome and occasionally valuable, there isn’t enough research done about them. Partly this is due to how different each rift is from each other – though they tend to open in our world in the same way each time, there is no similar uniformity to the contents. I cannot say that it’s impossible that a rift might not be so devoid of energy that it acts like a vacuum on those around. Equally, it’s possible that something emerged from the rift that is capable of drawing, ah, energies to energies. Or, possibly, this soothsayer’s words do not mean what you think they do.”

“Or it might not be a rift,” I point out. Sarran inclines his head slightly in acceptance of the point. I’m opening my mouth to ask about the other possibilities when a cry interrupts me.

“Obstacle visible ahead!” calls the lookout in the crow’s nest. “A…rock?” he continues, sounding far more uncertain.

I exchange a concerned glance with Sarran. A rock this far out in the ocean when I can sense that the seabed is so far away that I don’t have a hope of touching it with my earth-magic?

“Get those harvesting teams in and the ship prepared,” I order him tightly. “We need to be ready to sail now.” I reach out to my companions, asking them through the Bond to help the teams return. Even Kalanthia doesn’t argue, though I feel her reluctance as she stands up from where she’d been gorging herself on the roc’s meat.

A moment later, I leap into the air, my wings spreading from my back. I rise quickly to the height of the lookout.

“My lord!” he exclaims, jerking in surprise. I grab him just before he accidentally steps off the small platform.

“Careful,” I chide him absently, my eyes already scanning the area ahead for the rock.

It doesn’t take too long for my enhanced eyesight to spot it, low as it is in the water – a greyish, rounded lump about half the length of my ship in width. It barely sticks out from the waves at all, but any variation is obvious in the flat and featureless ocean. In fact, it’s odd that no one has spotted it sooner.

I frown as I gaze at the rock. The water is moving oddly around it – not lapping at it as it normally would, but…swirling around? And then I realise something else and a shiver goes down my spine – the rock is directly in the direction towards which the current is pulling us.

We need to know more. Is this rock an obstacle…or the destination?

Sirocco, can you get closer, please? But be careful.

The bird agrees wordlessly and I see her start flying towards the rock. But before she can even make it more than a ship’s length away from us, the rock begins growing, slowly rising above the water.

It doubles in height, and then doubles again. And it doesn’t stop there. It surpasses the size of one of our ships, and doesn’t show any signs of slowing. With a flicker of hysteria that makes me drop briefly in the air before I catch myself again, I wonder whether it is a mountain emerging from the sea, not a rock.

Even more disconcerting, there is nary a ripple in the water around it to indicate that something so massive has just appeared. I’m abruptly certain that we have just found the source of all the strangeness.

And then, when its rounded top is towering above us, at least twice the height of my – unbroken – mainmast, it stops. For a moment, it’s like the world freezes, the sheer immensity of this thing intimidating even the sea birds above into silence.

A moment later, mountain cracks open along myriad lines, the ‘rock’ peeling back to reveal a cavernous mouth that extends the width of the mountain, most of its height, and probably just as far into the sea below. There are no teeth, no eyes, no other features at all. Just a massive, cavernous hole that’s big enough to swallow even the roc whole.

A second later, the pulse of an impossibly intimidating aura washes over us with a strength that I’ve never felt before.

I lose control of my wings as my magic quails within me, and drops through the air towards the deck. Desperately pulsing sluggish magic through my channels, I catch myself only a moment before I slam into the deck. The last-minute save turns my impact into one that bruises rather than breaks, but when I come to a halt, I realise with dawning horror something else: I can’t move. But it’s not physical paralysis caused by my fall – if the silence that surrounds me is anything to judge by, everyone else is just as frozen.

Even after the strength of its first pulse as the ‘rock’ opened its mouth has ebbed, the aura is unlike anything I’ve felt before. It’s not like Kalanthia’s predatory domain, or even the roc’s wild liberty. This one has no intent behind it at all. It just is. It reminds me most of my Battle of Wills with Nicholas – where, even without trying, his massive Willpower meant that I had to struggle to get close. This feels just as undirected.

And if it’s undirected, then perhaps that means it’s not the aura that’s pinning us down – but our own fear. Instinct tells me that I’m right.

That’s more easily identified than overcome, though – something inside me is utterly convinced that the slightest movement will draw the attention of whatever this is upon me and that it will snuff me out like a candle in the wind.

Come on, just a finger, you can do it, just a finger, I tell myself gritting my teeth. It’s like I’m trying to force myself to vomit through sheer force of will. And then, finally, the tip of my finger twitches.

Fear swells inside me like a tide, some part of me sure that this is the moment I die.

And then it passes – and I live.

Moving my hand is easier than moving my finger, and then moving my arm is easier still. Having managed to convince my survival instincts that moving isn’t going to automatically be my death, I’m able to even make it to my feet.

“Get up!” I shout at everyone still pinned in place. “Get up!” I use Air-Shaping to send my voice all over the ship – and beyond it to the other ships. “If you continue lying there you will die. If you move, we all have a chance of surviving this!”

As if my voice has broken some kind of spell – and perhaps it has: the spell of fear – people start shifting. It doesn’t take long for panic to take over, but I don’t have time to deal with that – one of my Bonds is tugging weakly at me.

Sirocco! I realise, remembering how my wings failed me just now.

Without a second thought, I dive off the side of the ship, pressing air-magic into my wings to hasten my movement. There!

She’s already been pulled under and is struggling weakly underwater to reach the surface, her eyes wide and panicked.

I don’t dare touch the water surface – but, thanks to Nicholas, I don’t have to.

Bond Teleport!

A moment later, a dripping, coughing, and very unhappy bird appears next to me. I grab her out of the air before she can fall, her wings too water-logged to fly, and pull the water coating her off with Water-Shaping, then use Flesh-Shaping to ensure that her lungs are healed from her brief underwater experience.

“I’m sorry,” I apologise to her, holding her tightly. If I’d been standing, my legs would feel shaky as relief goes through me.

Not your fault, she tells me firmly, glancing resentfully towards the open mouth that might be still a distance away but, thanks to its immense size, is looming all too close.

Water pours into its gullet, and the current has picked up a bit of speed, the water surface smoothing out. Sor’az’s ship, which had been managing to press across the current in a perpendicular manner is now struggling to even cut a diagonal line through the sticky current.

I head back to the ship as fast as my wings can carry me. At least the air doesn't seem to be affected – not that that helps the ships that are stuck in the water.

If only one of the enchantments had been to allow the ships to briefly take flight! I wish fervently – and uselessly. The amount of mana that would take both to make and to use would be incredible, and since the threats in the air are as risky as those in the sea the enchantments weren’t deemed worth the trouble. I sincerely regret that now.

Approaching the deck of the ship, I find chaos. Sailors and fighters are either running frantically back and forth or cowering in a terrified heap, many creating just another obstruction on the deck. There is none of the order I’m accustomed to seeing with Captain Tolly at the helm.

“What’s the issue?” I ask Sarran, landing next to him. His expression is tight and more fearful than I’ve ever seen from the calm man.

“What isn’t the issue?” he actually snaps; just as quickly, apology fills his expression. “I’m sorry my lord. The situation is stressful, but that is not an excuse.”

I act on impulse and use Seal – something in his eyes indicates he has something to say which perhaps shouldn’t be shared.

“What is it? No one else can hear,” I assure him.

“The harvesting teams are on board – your companions helped with that – but the loss of Captain Tolly along with his first mate and some of the other crew members is creating chaos. Then there’s the fact that the repairs still very much are not finished – even those who would normally know what to do are uncertain because of that. And that’s not accounting for the fact that some people are gripped by an existential dread that leaves them useless.”

My eyes narrow on him.

“But that wouldn’t put the fear in your face that I see there. What is it?”

Sarran swallows, the fear I was referring to glinting more obviously in his eyes.

“Markus…this is clearly a beast and I don’t think it is a Beast King.” I frown at him, but part of me knows what he’s going to say before he does.

“I think this is a Beast Emperor. A Tier five.”
