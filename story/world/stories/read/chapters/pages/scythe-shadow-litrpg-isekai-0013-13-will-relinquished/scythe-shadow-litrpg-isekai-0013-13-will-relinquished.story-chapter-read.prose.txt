I slid down from the white horse, happy to finally have more than zero distance between my crotch and Calliope’s butt.

I held up a hand to assist her, but she ignored it and hopped down.

“A gentleman all of a sudden, are you?”

“Just trying to be polite, oh great savior of my life.” I mock-bowed deep.

She blew sharply out her nose, then shouldered past me. "If you wanna be polite, then thank Allium for lugging your ass all this way."

"Allium, huh?" I rubbed a hand along the horse's neck where the flesh and hair were mostly intact. "So you named a zombified horse, because…?"

"Same reason you named a lizard. Allium isn't a zombified horse. He's my zombified horse." She stepped up beside me and pulled a handful of oats from a pouch on her belt. Allium ate them from her palm and leaned his head against her forehead. Nuzzle crawled up to the top of Calliope's head and rested his entire face between the horse's eyes.

They did seem to have more of a bond than the stallions Jericho had, which were more like workhorses.

"And yes," she said, glancing sideways at me as she pulled away and started walking toward the tavern. "He is indeed named after a flower."

I shook my head, but couldn't keep the hint of a smile from my lips.

“C’mon, there’s a pillar behind the buildings. I’ll show you how to relinquish that Will.”

"Should we get Jericho?"

“He was on his fourth Bloody Mary by the time I left to find you. I doubt he’s in any state to walk, let alone teach you a thing.”

Great, my not-a-mentor is a burgeoning alcoholic. “The man loves his buckets of blood…”

Calliope glared back at me for a moment, clearly not entertained by the joke. “Don’t judge what you don’t understand.”

I stared as she continued on, not entirely sure how we’d plummeted from playful banter to death glare in a matter of seconds. Clearly hit a sensitive spot there.

She led me around toward the back of the tavern, which seemed even more shockingly small now that I knew what the interior looked like and how large it was. From everything I knew about physics—and, you know, general logic—it shouldn’t have been possible. But then again, when had anything in Penumbra been about logic?

I crossed around the last corner of the building, close behind Calliope. There, several feet back from the wall, began the edge of a perfect circle of dead grass, like an obscure crop circle. It must have been thirty or forty feet across. Toward the middle, frost had formed on the brown blades. At the very center, a stone pillar stood nearly my height with a beacon of black light towering into the champagne and orange sky.

Ah, finally found the source of those waypointy things I'd noticed when I first arrived.

The silver-cloaked Reaper who’d passed me at the tavern entrance earlier stomped out of the circle, boots crunching on ice. Droplets of water sparkled on his silver armor and dripped to the ground as if he'd just run through a sprinkler.

I waved to the man. “Hey, again!”

He paused, gazing at me unblinking, then shook his head and walked past, his armor clanking with each step.

Once he was out of sight, Calliope punched my arm.

“Ow! What the hell was that for?”

“Are you trying to get us killed?”

“What?”

“You don’t just go around saying ‘hey’ like an idiot to the second-highest ranking Reaper in all of Penumbra. Especially that one.”

“I talked with him before, I just figured—”

“Wait. He spoke to you?”

“Well, I mean… No… He really just said ‘In or out?’ because I was blocking the entrance to the tavern.”

“Damn,” she said, exhaling long. “Not many Reapers have ever heard him speak. He must have been in a good mood. No idea what the heck he’s doing out here in the Meadow, though.” She looked into the sky thoughtfully.

“You said he’s the second-highest ranking Reaper. Is that why his cloak is so different?”

Calliope nodded. “His name is Lancelot, supposedly. And even that, I’ve only heard second-hand, obviously. He’s a Diamond rank.”

So the cloak wasn’t silver, but Diamond. Somehow, that made him even more intimidating. I looked back in the direction Lancelot had left. He was long gone now, but the imprints of his heavy armored boots remained in the grass. I recalled how he’d brushed me aside without an ounce of effort in the tavern. Second-highest rank. He must be ridiculously fucking strong.

“Iron is the highest,” Calliope continued. “Hence the common idiom: ‘Iron Will.’ Though there are rumors of ranks above that, no one I’ve talked to has ever seen one. Descending rank from there are Diamond, Ruby, Sapphire—that’s Jericho. Emerald, Amethyst, and Amber. Us.”

After the difficulty of my first two battles, I had a feeling I’d be at the low end of strength, being new and all. But I didn’t expect there to be a concrete ranking of Reaper’s power. Hearing it all listed out like that highlighted just how insignificant and weak I truly was.

“Enough of that though,” Calliope said. “The focus right now is relinquishing the Will you harvested.”

I nodded, eying the circle of dead grass before me. A blue bird flew through the circle a few feet above the ground, singing and trailing a black mist like Nuzzle. It didn’t fall from the sky, which I supposed was a good sign.

As if reading my mind, Calliope took a step onto the edge of the dead grass. “It’s harmless. But the pillar does emit a strange force that saps heat from the air.” She rubbed her forearms, where goosebumps had already formed.

“Right,” I said. “That sounds completely harmless. Unknown magical force sucking warmth from the world…”

“You know what…” She took a step back outside the circle. “I was planning to show you how it’s done, but if you’re gonna be facetious then you can figure it out yourself.” She stuck her tongue out at me.

Nuzzle returned the gesture and I couldn’t help but smile. Maybe she hadn’t completely stolen his affections after all.

“How hard can it be?” I said, closing my eyes and stretching my left leg forward into the circle.

The cold air hit immediately, not overpowering, but enough of a change from before to be clearly noticeable. Like a brisk autumn morning. It kind of even smelled like one.

Nuzzle hopped from my shoulder and scampered back outside the circle, curling up on Calliope’s boot.

Traitor…

I continued toward the central pillar, each step colder than the last, descending into winter and beyond. My breath fogged. Closer to the pillar, the grass was coated in light frost, which thickened to ice at the nucleus of the strange force. It crunched under my boots. My arm hairs froze and teeth chattered.

Fuck, I didn’t expect it to get this cold. I hurried my steps as frost coalesced on my clothes.

Finally at the pillar, I examined the stone structure. It was an irregular shaped crystalline stalagmite sticking up from the ground stretching almost as high as my head. Pumping veins of dim white light snaked around the structure like the blood vessels of a strained bodybuilder. I reached out and touched the pillar, then retracted my hand immediately.

My fingertips steamed, pain radiating.

It was… hot.

Of course. All the heat pulled from the air must have transferred into the structure itself.

I imagined Calliope and Nuzzle behind me, laughing at my reaction, but I refused to look back.

I hefted my scythe, still pulsing softly with the Amber Will I’d harvested. If I couldn’t touch the pillar with my hands, obviously it had something to do with this instead. I leaned in closer to the stone. Thin slots dotted the exterior of the pillar in a disorganized fashion. But they were the exact size of a scythe blade.

I smiled and slid my scythe into the closest one. This was easy. I didn't need Calliope and her explanations.

The handle pulsed with staccato energy in my grip, then an unpleasant warmth washed into it. I tried to let go, but my hands held tight, as if the muscles and nerves were completely out of my control. The scythe and pillar glowed burnt orange in unison, brighter and brighter.

[Will Relinquish Commencing.

Rank: Amber.

Affinity: Zeal.]

I felt waves vibrating and crashing in my mind from random directions, mimicking the patterns on the pillar. They were violent and angry, like emotions given a physical impulse, threatening to topple me over.

My knees buckled under the pressure, but my hand wouldn't release the scythe, the skin on my fingers prickling and peeling from the heat. The scent of burning flesh filled the air.

A tingling rose up my spine. My neck involuntarily twitched sideways at an unhealthy angle. Vision flashed to black.
