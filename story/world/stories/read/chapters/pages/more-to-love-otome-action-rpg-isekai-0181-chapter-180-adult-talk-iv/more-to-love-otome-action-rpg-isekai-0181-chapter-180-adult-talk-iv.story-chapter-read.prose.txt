“That name’s a work in progress,” Sue interjected, scratching her temple with what Aida could now see was a closed fan. She giggled with embarrassment. “But otherwise, yeah! We found something that I can work with, and is fun to use!”

Sue snapped her fan open and covered her face demurely, batting her eyelashes coquettishly at Aida. Aida laughed.

“It looks good on you! And it seems like these fans amplify your fire?”

“Yeah!” Sue handed a fan to Aida to examine. “It’s a beginner fan so I can get used to using it. But as I get better, I’ll be able to buy other fans and start specializing. This one is well-rounded, you see? Heavy enough to take a physical beating, material quality good enough to channel my mana…”

Sue started pointing out the workmanship and demonstrating the balance with her other fan while Kawa added his own assessment of how there could be a fan for every occasion.

“You can get one fan specifically for casting and one for fighting—“

“—oh, that would be amazing. Can I carry more than two at once, in case I break one?”

“We can even get you a big fan if you find you’d rather be a caster primarily—“

“That seems super restrictive—”

“It is a bit, yeah…”

Aida hefted the closed fan in her hand, observing the glitter and shine of the weapon. The fan’s outer guards were made out of a thick and sturdy yet lightweight wood, polished to perfection. The rivet contained a gleaming ruby, and as she pressed her fingers against the stone she could feel that it had already attuned to Sue’s mana.

“This is super cool,” Aida said, handing the fan back to a beaming Sue. “I wish Maglica had trained us to use real weapons.”

“I know,” Sue groaned. “I might have found my affinity for battle fans sooner!”

“Hey, at least your foundation is there,” Kawa said comfortingly. “You’re comfortable with hand-to-hand combat and elemental manipulation already, so now you won’t be dependent on any particular weapon. That’s Abedi’s biggest weakness and bad habit that Arryn will have to break him of…”

“Do you want to try to find your weapon, Aida?” Sue asked mischievously, elbowing Kawa. His eyes brightened as the two of them leered at Aida.

“As much fun as that sounds, I don’t really have the time to,” she said, chuckling awkwardly. She glanced up at the sun, which was high in the sky. “It looks like it’s about time for me to head out…”

She still had time to scope out the Buddington InterTravel Terminal, which Levi had informed her was where she would have to go if she wanted to find one of the large golems to take her all the way to Burston City. She fully expected to get lost as she tried to navigate the golem routes on her own, so she needed all the free time she could get.

“Well, okay, you can always drop by whenever to swing something if you feel like it,” Kawa said cheerfully. He propped his fists on his hips as he looked down at Sue. “Should we take your fans out to the woods, see if we can find any local pests in the area?”

“Sounds good to me!” Sue said enthusiastically. She looked at Aida. “Will you be okay getting home on your own?”

“Of course,” Aida assured Sue. “I’ll see you later!”

~ * ~ * ~ * ~

Aida had overestimated the difficulty in finding the InterTravel Terminal; it turned out to only be a mile away from the Gullbeaks, and she didn’t even have to take a golem to the station.

She was headed towards an appropriately secluded bench so she could observe the behaviors of would-be passengers when she suddenly brushed up against a familiar, brooding aura. Freezing like a small prey animal, she slowly turned her head towards the source.

Spotting the perfectly poised figure of a young woman with long, dark blue hair under a broad-brimmed hat, Aida wondered if she could still escape without drawing attention—except the moment she even entertained the thought, the woman turned towards Aida.

Aida blinked as Lara’s glassy eyes sharpened as she caught sight of Aida, rooting her to her spot. The two of them stared at each other, each daring the other to make the first move.

Perhaps foolishly, Aida decided to be optimistic. I’ll just continue doing what I was going to do, and she can continue doing whatever she was going to do.

She gave Lara a polite nod, just to acknowledge her and show she meant no harm—and skirted the perimeter of her dangerously brewing mana, walking at a sedate pace to her target bench.

Unfortunately, Lara didn’t go on her own way. Aida even dug through her bag to feign her occupation, indicating that she wasn’t looking to engage in conversation.

“Are you following me?”

“Excuse me?” Aida looked up from her bag, where she was checking again to see if she still had her school wand and graduation badge with her.

“It’s odd to meet you twice in one star cycle. And this time… it seems as if you’re alone.” Lara’s gray eyes stared piercingly at Aida, reminding her uncomfortably of her cousin.

Aida scoffed as she gestured to the rest of the crowd. “Do you think all of these other people are following you as well?”

The elegant young woman sniffed. “These other civilians don’t know that I’m acquainted with the Riolts yet. So even if they were following me, it’s not for any nefarious purposes.”

Aida gave her a withering gaze. “Great. And what good would following you do for me?”

Oddly enough, an expression of guilt flashed across Lara’s face, creasing her perfectly smooth brow and making her lips tremble. But in the next second, her face was as cool as marble again.

Aida frowned as Lara simmered, glaring at Aida. “Lara… are you okay?”

“What? Of course I’m okay,” she snapped, crossing her arms. “I don’t need sympathy from you.”

Geeze Louise.

“Fine, sorry I asked,” Aida said sarcastically, lifting her hands in surrender. “Feel free to walk away. I promise I won’t follow you.”

Aida turned her body away from Lara, looking back towards the crowd pointedly.

After an uncomfortable moment, Lara exhaled. “I’m sorry.”

What?

Seeing Aida’s stunned look, Lara shuffled her feet. “Can I sit?”

Huh?

Lara didn’t exactly look miserable, but she wasn’t as defensive as she was before.

“Fine.” Aida shifted over to the other end of the bench, allowing Lara to sit gingerly on her end.

The two of them sat in silence, the sounds of children’s laughter and exasperated orders from responsible adults wafting between them.

“Have you ever wondered if you’re making the wrong choice?” Lara’s voice was so quiet Aida wasn’t sure she heard her correctly.

Why is this happening? Aida lamented her refusal to experiment with weapons; it surely would have been much less uncomfortable than a heart to heart session with Lara Lorr.

But Lara was peeking at Aida, uncharacteristically shy, waiting for a response.

“All the time,” Aida said shortly. “I regret a choice I made barely an hour ago.”

“That choice you made… would it affect your family’s love for you?” Lara asked, actually scooting closer to Aida in her persistence.

“No,” Aida admitted reluctantly. She didn’t have it in her to continue being sarcastic to Lara, not when she seemed to be in genuine distress. It was incredibly weird to see this side of the posh girl—but it wasn’t the first time she had softened. She had actually said something human to her in front of the fancy Lucky Envelope.

Cherish your relationships.

She gave a long sigh. Here goes nothing.

“Is there something you want to talk about?” Aida asked gently.

Lara’s eyes glimmered, and Aida thought she saw a hole open in the normally hard-as-steel gray eyes—before it closed up again, though it didn’t entirely harden to what Aida was used to seeing.

Lara inhaled, as if preparing to give a speech.

“Hypothetically speaking, if you were offered the chance to save many people’s livelihoods, but it meant sacrificing your principles… would you do it?”

“Oh. Wow. That’s a big question,” Aida hedged to give herself time. “I guess… it depends on what’s at stake? A matter of scale?”

Lara nodded seriously. “What if the principle you are being asked to forsake is what defines you? It is your guiding principle throughout life.”

“Um, sorry, the abstract nature of the question makes this hard to discuss,” Aida said apologetically. “Although I guess I’d start with what the consequences are for each scenario. What would happen if I held to my principles, and what would happen if I gave up my principles. And then compare the severity of the consequences.”

Lara stared unblinkingly at Aida, shifting her gaze between both of Aida’s eyes. Finally, she leaned back in distaste.

“You are so naive.”

As Aida swelled up with indignation at Lara’s response, she held up her hand.

“I apologize. I sprang this obscure, philosophical question on you at a moment’s notice, and am judging you for your answer when you don’t even have all of the information.” Lara cast a bitter smile at Aida. “I’m quite certain you would prefer I uphold my principles no matter what… if you had all of the information.”

As Aida stared at Lara in disbelief, the young woman stood up and composed herself. Once again the stoic, regal, self-righteous girl she had been at school.

“Thank you for your time. I bid you good day.”
