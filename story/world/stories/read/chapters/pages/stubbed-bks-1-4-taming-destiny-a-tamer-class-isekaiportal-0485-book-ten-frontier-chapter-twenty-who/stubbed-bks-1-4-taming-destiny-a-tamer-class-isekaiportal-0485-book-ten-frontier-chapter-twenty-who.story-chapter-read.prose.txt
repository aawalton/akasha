Loran looks up as someone enters his office. He feels like sighing when he sees the pile of parchment in the messenger’s arms, but he doesn’t – as the manservant to the lord of the expedition, and head steward in training, that’s apparently below his dignity.

Below my dignity, my arse, Loran thinks to himself uncharitably. I used to battle rous for scraps. But his mentor always seems to know when he’s been ‘undignified’ and makes him pay for it later.

“What is it?” he manages to ask – mostly politely.

“The reports ye asked fer from the head o’ the crafters, sir,” Raoul tells him, dumping the pile onto Loran’s desk.

“Thank you,” Loran tells him, keeping in the groan that also wants to escape. Sometimes he regrets learning how to read and write – if he couldn’t do it, he wouldn’t have to look through the reports and summarise them for Markus ahead of the meeting that evening.

Raoul is waiting expectantly.

Loran flicks his gaze around the piles on the table. He’s had reports from several of the groups, but not… “I need the latest reports from the kitchens. Can you get them for me?”

“Of course, sir.” Raoul agrees happily, but doesn’t move.

Loran flips him a copper that he catches deftly. The young boy flashes a grin at Loran and then scampers off.

Loran watches him go for a moment. Though the messengers are paid for every day they work, he likes to give them little tips when he can. He remembers his own days of running errands for anyone who was willing to give him a copper or two for the service. Sometimes, a tip for a quick delivery was the only reason he and his siblings could eat that day.

Then he found that stealing was far more profitable and his ability to quickly run through the streets to make deliveries was just as useful for running away from angry marks.

He hopes these kids will never need to learn the desperation that drove him to start stealing – or the greed that had kept him doing so.

Loran finds his hand has crept up to his throat without his permission. It’s been several months since he was freed, but sometimes he still dreams that that was the dream, and that the collar still sits around his throat.

Sighing, Loran leans back in his chair and redirects his hand to rub at his eyes that are beginning to pain him. It’s an unfortunately common occurrence – and seems directly linked to how long he spends cooped up in a room, poring over reports. But he has a deadline, so can’t justify taking time off right now.

Hopefully I’ll find some time in the next few days to see Master Ulim, Loran thinks wistfully. Carpentry can be detailed work as intricate as reading and writing sometimes, but it’s a good deal more satisfying than dealing with reports.

“Is this a bad time?”

Loran sits bolt upright, recognising the voice immediately and feeling a surge of guilt and annoyance that the moment Markus comes by is the moment he’s slacking.

His lord clearly feels his reaction as, before he can speak, Loran already feels reassurance coming down the Bond.

“I know you’re busy,” Markus tells him apologetically. “But it’s been a while since I checked out your internal matrix. But we can do that later if you’re too occupied now.”

Sometimes Loran can only marvel at the fact that this is a lord. No one Loran has served – or seen – is this concerned about whether it’s a good time for his underlings. Now I feel guilty about complaining earlier about the amount of work, Loran thinks.

“It’s fine, my lord,” he says. “Markus,” he adds with the hint of a sigh when he sees the man’s pointed look, chiding himself a little.

It’s a minor thing – Loran shouldn’t have any difficulty in addressing the man as he wants to be addressed. But part of him feels awkward at going against every rule that governs interaction between such unequal positions as their own. There’s also part of him that wants to call Markus ‘lord’ – he’s earned it more than anyone else Loran knows who bears the title. Though the other lords in the expeditions aren’t so bad.

Underlying both of those is something more insidious – a part of him that fears that if he gets into the habit now, he’ll earn punishment later when Markus changes his mind. He’s already grown into his role as lord in so many ways – surely there will be a time when he demands his due respect.

Loran stands. Technically, he should have already bowed before his lord and employer, but he knows that would just make the man uncomfortable.

“Where would you like to do this?” he asks Markus, moving past the moment briskly. A good manservant ensures his master’s comfort at all times, Sarran’s voice tells him, the memory of the man’s words enduring even while his mentor is absent. “You could sit here, if you like.”

“It would be better in my office,” Markus suggests, turning to head that way. “There’s more space – and two chairs.”

“As you wish,” Loran agrees easily. It’s true that his office is relatively small, but he chose it because of that. Too much space – and especially too many things to hide behind – make him feel uneasy, even in this place which is meant to be safe. His office has a window that lets in the crystal light to indicate whether it’s day or night, and he has an enchanted chest of drawers in which to store all of the parchment-work that isn’t immediately needed. That suffices for him.

Before he leaves, he ensures that the enchantment is activated both on the chest of drawers and his desk. That way, even if someone manages to pick the lock on the office door, they still won’t be able to get at the reports. Sarran has impressed very firmly on him how important it is that details about the settlement are kept secure.

Loran didn’t understand at first, but, like in many things, Sarran was patient with him. And when he likened it to a plan of the house, Loran got it immediately. After all, the chances of a successful heist increase significantly if the thieves conducting it know where the enchantments are, or when the guards are likely to pass the entry point.

He’s had much to learn, but he’s been surprised at times how similar some aspects are between being a manservant and being a thief in the employ of a gang. Particularly when it comes to keeping secrets.

By the time Loran has locked up his office and walked down the corridor to Markus’, his lord has shifted his chair around his desk to sit not quite facing his visitor’s chair.

“Sit, would you?” Markus asks, gesturing at the chair when Loran hesitates.

Loran obliges. It shouldn’t feel this discomforting. But the last time they did this, they were on the ship which felt far less formal than the settlement does now.

Markus puts his hand on Loran’s shoulder.

“Alright, great. You go into your Core space and I’ll meet you there.”

“Yes, m-Markus,” Loran agrees, correcting himself at the last moment. To little purpose – Markus has already closed his eyes and probably isn’t listening anyway.

Loran casts a brief glance at the door – it’s open. He should have shut it when he came in. But he can’t get up now – movement will force Markus to lose contact with him. Loran doesn’t know what would happen then.

It’ll be fine, he tells himself. What threat is there in the middle of the settlement? Now that the parasites have been dealt with, anyway?

Swallowing, he forces his thoughts away – he needs to get into the right mindset to descend into his Core space. Fortunately, he’s had enough practice that it isn’t hard.

Closing his eyes, he breathes, imagining the breath pulsing throughout his body. Then, following it with his mind, his awareness of his exterior fades as his interior gains strength. Sometimes, he wonders whether the faint lines he almost sees as he transitions are the vessels that pump blood around his body. At any rate, they don’t correlate to the tarnished silver-coloured Energy channels that soon reveal themselves to his mind’s eye.

Finally reaching his Core space, he sees that Markus is already there, a bright golden light that zips around easily, inspecting the different parts of his matrix.

Loran feels nervous. It’s changed a lot since the last time Markus saw it. Where once there had just been two lines extending in opposite directions, curling a little back on themselves, now he has six main channels that connect in the middle, each with a mass of silver filaments winding back and forth around them.

Loran sometimes likens them to arms with closed fists and delicate jewellery spiraling around them. From the ‘fists’ emerge tiny filaments, barely visible, that extend to the edge of his Core space. Not that it’s very far to the edge – less than half the length of each major channel.

One of the channels is particularly wide – he noticed that growing particularly when he ate Energy-rich foods, though he was lucky not to have the Energy poisoning that others suffered from. Since then, he’s been trying to even out the other channels – Tiria thought it would be a good idea to do so and the instinct Loran has been following to form the channels in the first place agrees with her.

Markus’s light comes closer to where Loran is hovering, emanating a sense of wanting to talk.

Obediently, Loran pulls out of his Core space – always quicker than entering it – and opens his eyes. Markus is already regarding him thoughtfully.

Loran swallows.

“Is it…acceptable? What I’ve done?”

Markus’ expression isn’t giving anything away, and he has a tight control on the Bond between them so that doesn’t reveal anything either.

“What you’ve done? You’ve been directing it?”

Loran hesitates for a moment, then nods.

“How?”

Loran considers the question thoughtfully.

“By moving the Energy from the Cores in the way that felt right?” he replies tentatively.

Markus nods slowly, still not revealing any approval – or disapproval.

“Do you have any sense of being close to Evolution? Any feeling of something building or being almost ready to tip over?”

Loran again gives the question some thought.

“I…maybe. It definitely feels like something’s been growing, like something is…heavier now than it used to be. But I don’t feel like anything’s about to tip over.” He tilts his head to one side. “I suppose…it feels a little…frustrating.”

“Frustrating?”

Loran nods.

“Yes. As if something is…held back. That wants to be free.”

“Interesting,” Markus murmurs, looking thoughtful. Loran feels a moment of trepidation at that – when his lord looks that way, it usually means a lot more work for him. Markus’ finger taps his lips. “The weave at the ends of the channels is very tight. Is there a reason for that?”

Loran blinks, not having expected this question.

“I…don’t know?”

“Can you try to find out?”

There was only one answer to that question.

“I’ll do my best.” He hesitates. “Can I go back into my Core space?”

“Sure, whatever you need.”

Wondering why this is so important, Loran descends back into his Core space. Approaching the fist-like weaves, he contemplates them, attempting to work out why it’s so tight. It feels safe, but….

Dipping into the Energy channel itself, he follows its twists and turns. Markus is right – it is tight.

And, as he follows the channels, going from fist to arm to arm to fist, he realises something. In some way he can’t quite explain, it reminds him very much of his life before he was enslaved. Before the gang, even. The long stretches of long, wide channels remind him of the times when he wandered about, searching for a mark or someone who needed an errand done. Then the fists remind him of the frantic twists and turns he made while trying to shake a pursuer. As he continues twisting through the fists – the mazes – he feels echoes of that time. Of wariness, of fear, of desperation – of the sense of safety when he found a small hiding place and his pursuers ran right past it.

Pulling out of his Core space, Loran relates to his lord what he found. He feels a little awkward at first speaking of how the tight twists and turns make him feel safe.

“But does it feel right for you? Does it reflect who you wish to be – or just who you were before?” Markus asks after he’s finished.

Loran pauses before answering, not sure what to say.

It feels right, yes, but…is it what he wishes to be? Has he really not grown past that time?

He’s experienced so much since then – both bad and good. He’s lost all rights over his own body, been forced to face torture and possible death for the most minor of mistakes. He’s seen the depths of what people can do to each other – and done some of it himself.

Yet he’s also seen the good – how even when filled with fear, and perhaps because of it, people can still be brave. How even without control, they can still make choices. He’s offered himself up to suffer on behalf of others, and been rewarded for it with the means to buy his freedom. He’s reunited with his siblings, and started rebuilding bonds that were damaged so long ago by neglect, poor decisions, and absence. He’s become part of a new family, unconventional as it is.

Why should he be defined by the period of his life when he was a desperate pickpocket? He is older and – he hopes – wiser now. Shouldn’t his internal matrix reflect that?

Barely aware of how he’s slipping downwards, the bright channels of his internal matrix reappear before his mind’s eye.

I am manservant to a Great House heir, he tells himself firmly, feeling and seeing the silver threads shiver and brighten as it feels like he’s speaking truth to his deepest being. Trusted by him and our companions. I am a brother, an apprentice carpenter, and I will become the first human to develop a Class without a stone!

I no longer need to run and hide.

Flashes of the battle with the roc come back to mind – and their survival.

I am safe.

Emotions, remembered and also current ripple through the Bond.

I am accepted.

Warmth cradles him as the pack members feel his need for reassurance.

I am…loved.

The shivering of his channels has become outright shaking as it feels like the bedrock of his being is changing before his eyes.

Reflecting his desire to no longer be bound by the fears of the past, the ‘fists’ on the ends of the arms open up, relaxing and allowing the channels previously crushed within the knot to expand.

As what look like fingers – though many more than five on each hand – extend outwards, Loran notices how the ‘palm’ of the fist begins glowing brighter. The tarnish falls away, leaving pure silver alone.

The silver pours down the ‘arms’, each stream meeting in the centre.

And now Loran feels that sensation Markus mentioned earlier – the feeling that something is on the edge of tipping over. Like reality is on a knife’s edge.

Loran has the distinct sense that he has a choice to make: hunker down and retreat to what is safe and let this sensation crash over him without changing him; or embrace it and the painful transformation it offers.

Loran knows what choice he would have made before he met Markus, before he realised that there was a different way of living – that he could be different. Nothing good ever came of being noticed, of being too obvious, of being too greedy.

But he has grown since then. There are times when we must stand up even when doing so puts us at risk, he murmurs to himself, thinking about his choice to offer himself to his old gang boss instead of his more vulnerable companions.

Then he thinks of Markus and what he does with the power he’s been given – and earned. Seeking power to protect others is not foolish. And hiding doesn’t always keep us safe.

Loran smiles.

Well, come on, then. Bring it.
