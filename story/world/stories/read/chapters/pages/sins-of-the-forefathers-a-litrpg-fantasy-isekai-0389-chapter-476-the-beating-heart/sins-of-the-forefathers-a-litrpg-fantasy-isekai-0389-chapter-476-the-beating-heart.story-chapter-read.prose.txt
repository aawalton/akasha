There was a Calamity across the ceiling of the mountain, and it was the most disturbing thing I had ever seen.

Not because of the presence of a Calamity all by itself. No, at this point in my life, after all I had seen, I had already encountered and technically slain two other Calamity’s. They were massive, and powerful, and almost invariably malicious…but I could keep my cool in the presence of one.

What was terrifying about this one…was what the Korvoks had done to it.

This thing was a prisoner. No…not just a prisoner. These mad cannibals had turned it into a trophy.

A living one.

Across the broad ceiling of the Mountain Hold of Karag Korvok, there was what I could only describe as a snake. A massive one, hundreds and hundreds of feet in length from snout to barbed tail. The entire range of its body had been curled into a spiral across the stone in the center of the roof, with a section of its torso in the center. Normally, I would have thought that was a strange contortion, as both the head and the tail of the creature were on the outside of the spiral that the rest of the body was twisted into.

That would be, if it weren’t for the tortures that Korvoks had inflicted upon it.

Rising from the central point of the Hold city were ten of the same, flesh-covered, pulsing spires. Only, all of these were taller than each of the small ones that comprised the monstrous city they surrounded.

Tall enough to reach the roof…and pin the serpentine Calamity to the ceiling. That…that was how the creature was actually held in place upon the ceiling. Those same, sharpened spires of skin-covered stone were what had affixed it so firmly to the roof of the mountain.

Nine were arranged in a broad circle, there in the center of the city, and each needle-like tip was inserted into a segment of the snake. They ranged from just below the neck of the serpent to just above the worn spikes on this creature’s barbed tail. Both ends of it were thus slumped over and limp, as if the creature itself was unconscious.

And yet, it was still alive. It was actually easy to tell.

Because the tenth spire, the largest one, arranged in the center of the circle created by the other nine…

Was buried deep within the still-beating heart of the Calamity.

The visible heart.

It was monstrous in its cruelty…but I had found the source of light that these degenerate monsters masquerading as Dwarves used to light their festering pit.

The heart itself was emitting the sickening crimson light that oozed out to cover Karag Korvok. It shone up above in much the same position that the far less distressing stone of Karag Varla had. The Korvoks had skinned and then cracked open the chest of the serpent, splaying open it’s visible ribs as they did so, to expose the still beating heart for what it was. Even more disturbingly, it hung outside of its chest, below the ribs, as if it had been deliberately suspended upon its own arteries. Possibly the only reason that it hadn’t been ripped fully out of the creature's carcass was because the tenth, central spire had pierced it.

More than even the other nine spires, the summit of the tenth truly narrowed to what might have been a needle-thin point. It traveled straight through the muscle of the shining, pulsing heart, pinning the body on the other side to the ceiling.

As I watched, horrified, the heart pulsed in a show of life…and a shower of blood traveled down the spire. Oozing, it slowly traveled down the length of the tower, running like a grotesque waterfall down its surface, tainting the skin upon its surface a gory, crimson red.

Something happened then that caused my heart to actually, physically skip a beat. The breath in my lungs froze as, in the distance, I saw a single, great eyelid of the titanic serpent twitch open, revealing a slit, amber colored eye larger than my lighthouse home back in Blutstein.

It swiveled in its socket…and turned in our direction.

Toward me.

Somehow, someway, I knew. That Calamity was looking right at me. There wasn’t an ounce of malice in that intelligent gaze. No, instead, that eye was filled with a mix of anticipation and relief.

And then the eye closed, and I was released from its gaze.

I had to swallow a lump in my throat as a dawning realization crystallized. “I guess…” I whispered to myself, inaudible over a growing din in the distance. “I won’t have to search for Nehushtan after all…”

Because I was suddenly more certain of anything else that had happened to us on this journey.

That Calamity was Nehushtan. This was why he had so desperately contacted me multiple times. He wanted to be saved from this grisly torture.

I…

Helpless at the enormity before my looked down first to Sena on my left, and then to Fade on my right. Both of the Spirit Beasts were just as horrified and dumbfounded at the monstrosity that was Karag Korvok. “Can you feel it?” I asked them urgently. “Is that a Spirit?”

The gaze that Sena turned on me, then, was wide enough that I thought her feline eyes would outright pop out of their sockets. “What the Hells do you mean, is that a Spirit?!” She sputtered. “That’s impossible! There aren’t any Spirits with a corporeal form that massive! Not even Mother at her greatest can match that!”

“No,” Fade suddenly cut in, audible to only Sena and me. “Nate…you’re right. I can feel it in my antlers, even from here. That’s…it’s not a Monster. I can’t feel a Core. Whatever it is, it…almost feels like a Spirit. Like it’s something related to us, almost-”

“Like Hermus…” I breathed, shaking my head in astonishment. “A Spirit that incarnated as a physical existence…”

“But not even that feels right,” Fade said uncertainly. “It’s close, but there’s something…something off about who or whatever that thing is.”

I wanted to confirm with both Spirit Beasts whatever the Hell was going on here, but I didn’t have that luxury.

Karag Korvok was starting to react to our presence. And I do mean that literally.

The city structures themselves were reacting.

Where before the flesh-covered spires had been pulsing in time with the slow heartbeat of what I assumed was Nehushtan, there was a sudden skip. The needles of Karag Korvok fell out of time with the splayed open heart of the Calamity above and began to pulse faster.

And from the depths of the city, somewhere in the center where the largest heart-piercing tower resided, came a lone howl. Not like what Fade himself could produce. It was neither canine nor lupine in nature.

No, instead, I could tell that this came from the throat of a humanoid. It was loud and long enough to reach every corner of the hollowed-out mountain that this monstrous Hold resided within.

It was answered, shortly, by more of them. Hundreds and hundreds and thousands of answering howls echoed out from the depths of the city, each of them carrying notes of madness and fury within them.

In the distance, down the ramp that even now Rafael’s undead were marching down, I began to see them. Small, almost insignificant specks, pouring out of previously hidden openings at the base of the spires. At first, there were only dozens of them, sprinting out of holes in the wall like mice scared from their burrows. But that number only increased with every passing second. More and more and more of those distant dots flowed from the abominable towers of Karag Korvok, until they filled the streets like a wave of ants, all of them howling, all of them sprinting.

Straight at us.

God, the sound of their feet and the din of their wailing…I don’t think I would ever forget it.

There were just so many. Far more than I, or anyone else, for that matter, had predicted. They had to outnumber our forces at least ten times over, and that was when I included all of Rafael’s risen Varlayans.

And yet, oddly…from what I could see, the Lich didn’t seem bothered by the seemingly endless horde that was even now sprinting for our throats.

If anything, the Lich looked like he was enjoying himself. There was a slight smile across his desiccated lips as he gazed down at the mass of insane, slavering Dwarves on approach. I was close enough to his floating form, at the back of his ad hoc army, that he was able to notice me. I don’t know what kind of expression I had on my face…I don’t know, possibly existential dread at the possibility I had ordered all of us to our deaths, perhaps.

But a distinctly amused cast surfaced in Rafael’s smile, beyond the mere satisfaction before.

The Lich winked at me, and with a wave of his hand…

His creations abruptly accelerated, breaking out into a sprint down the ramp that led into the city proper.

I, along with all of the forces of the expedition, had gathered at the edge of the mining platform that the Korvoks had created to burrow their way into Karag Varla. Many had been watching the oncoming horde in dread, because very few of us were fools. That dread didn’t relent when we watched, down at the very base of the ramp, as Rafael’s undead crashed into a veritable unending wall of screaming Skarned.

It became very obvious, very quickly, that an individual undead was not a sufficient threat to an individual Korvokian Skarned. For as easy as my forces and I had found it to slaughter them by the dozen, it seemed like it took nearly two or three undead to handle a single Korvok. Judging by the grim looks starting to appear on my soldiers' faces, I wasn’t the only one to realize what this meant.

It was only a matter of time before the undead were exhausted and we were overrun.

But then…something odd started to happen. Down at the point where animated dead met screaming barbarian, I watched as one of the Korvoks who had triumphed over one of Rafael’s undead, bloodied, bitten, and scratched, was suddenly tearing into one of them…

Stopped. He entirely paused, hunched over the scraps of meat that comprised one of the Varlayans his people had already slaughtered. It was only thanks to my heightened Perception that I was able to watch as his incredibly bloodshot eyes…drained. The life went out of them, quite literally, turning a pale, milky white…

…not unlike Rafael’s undead.

In a jerky motion, the Korvok jerkily stood up from the ravaged corpse he had been ravaging further…and turned on the nearest Korvok to where he stood. In moments, his teeth had found the screaming maniac’s throat. Moments later, there was a second milk-eyed Korvok that was also attacking his former ‘comrades’.

This was happening all across the front. Korvoks who had been engaging the undead and sustained any kind of battle wound were turning on others. It didn’t matter if it was a bite, or a scratch, or anything. If they were hurt by an undead, it only took moments for that Korvok to stop in place and join the ranks of Rafael’s minions.

My mouth fell open as I comprehended what was happening. “It’s like a virus…” I whispered to myself in disbelief, hope suddenly reigniting in my breast. “It doesn’t matter how many of them there are, because-”

“They’re all just meat in the end,” Rafael’s smug voice suddenly reached me. Without my noticing, the Lich had drifted over to join us. He still seemed to be directing his minions in some way, judging by the minute twitches of his fingers, held out before him. But he still had the attention to be smug.

And to be fair, I think he had a reason to. In only moments, the battle that had looked to be a near rout was starting to shift. The Skarned didn’t have the sense not to throw themselves at the claws and teeth of the undead. Even if it took two or three undead to handle a single Skarned, that didn’t matter. More and more of the Korvoks were falling under Rafael’s spell every second, in an almost exponential manner.

An unexpected chill rolled down my spine as I realized the possibilities of something like this. A horde of undead like this was downright apocalyptic. Forget the Skyfall or the collapse of global wards.

If this spread, all life on Vereden would fall in a matter of weeks.

I was knocked out of my sudden renewed dread by the sound of Rafael laughing mockingly. When I turned my gaze back to the floating Lich, I found him rolling his eyes at me. “No need to look so frightened, Nathaniel,” He said in a bored tone. “I must actively maintain this enchantment. Worry not, there’s no risk of this contagion escaping into the general population. You may thank the Great Sage for this specific rising Spell. With it, we shall conquer this city in only a matter of hours.”

I…alright then. It was still disturbing to watch Korvoks fall like dominoes into Rafael’s control, but I could live with it.

Especially if it accomplished our objectives.

I shook off my thoughts and motioned my suddenly energized forces forward.

No reason for us to all just sit around gawping. We should pull our own weight.

At the very least, we could play ranged support.
