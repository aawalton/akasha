There really was just something about riding an absolutely massive dragon and laying waste to all your foes.

Henrietta hadn't yet managed to get the big dragon Jacob had killed actually flying, despite many modifications to its wings in her Sketchbook, but it could still cover a remarkable amount of ground just running down the Ironroad. The Jungle itself had too many trees for anything too fast, and it could simply trample most of its kin as they passed, leaving dozens of crushed draconids in her wake.

The defenses at Ironworks had failed again, and as such the task had fallen to Henrietta to clear out the rampaging dragons, fix and reload the ballistae, and repopulate the diggers responsible for harvesting the hematite their Blast furnace relied on. At least running out of iron to feed into it was less of a problem than it running out of charcoal, and they had a massive buffer of each. But still, restarting the thing after any disruption was an enormous hassle they would all rather avoid. So, any interruptions to either supply line needed to be resolved quickly.

They reached the pit-mine, and Henrietta launched herself off the back of her dragon with a tendril, flaring her wings to cushion her landing, and threw enormous chunks of stone at every bit of hostile dragonflesh. Behind her, her dragon was on its own rampage, but it was all much too rote for Henrietta to pay that much attention.

Once the hostiles were neutralized, Henrietta swiftly determined that the perimeter had been breached due to a singular ballista breaking, one of its arms snapping nearly in half. Dragons had then capitalized on the gap in the defenses, overwhelmed the inkling defenders permanently stationed there, and generally caused an absolute mess of things. Fixing a broken bundle of reeds wasn't the easiest task, but Henrietta had brought enough replacement materials to get it working again.

This would be much easier once she got her big dragon flying. Technically, she could have already deployed the enormous beast for guard-dog duty, and easily defended a dozen locations. But, she was regularly dismissing and resummoning the thing as she kept tweaking its wings, so they were still primarily relying on automated defenses. In the long-term, that would be essential, but for now the growth of The Factory wasn't about to outpace her ability to create inklings. She didn't know what would happen if it did, and that lack of a plan irked her. Planning was her job, and while her team were all performing their duties quite admirably, she… kept failing.

Henrietta sighed and stepped around a cloudy puddle. Clay squished underfoot, and a dragon she'd thrown across the mine splashed down into its own body of water, accompanied by a small landslide of stone pitter-pattering down as well.

She reached out with a tendril and sketched out a drawing of a digger in the soil, thanks to ⟨Etch⟩ and ⟨Universal Canvas⟩, then watched as ⟨Epizeuxis⟩ mirrored it twenty-one times into the surrounding area. The saber-toothed mole-things pulled themselves into existence a moment later, their ink-sketch appearances taking a moment to stabilize before they began meandering around to continue digging downwards. They were excavating far more ore than they were actually processing, and even with that the ore vein showed absolutely no signs of slowing down. If anything, it might have been getting richer as they continued the descent, but overseeing mining wasn't what Henrietta was here for.

She threw herself back into the air and glided onto the back of the big dragon, directing it back to First Tower.

Clark handed her a bowl full of stew, and Henrietta accepted it with a bit of an absentminded thanks. While still not on the level of food in a fully-developed society, they'd recently found a number of aromatic herbs and flowers which their Healer had been putting to great use in their meals. That typically consisted of a kind of ground-nut porridge for breakfasts, various smoked meats packed in papyrus to be eaten during main-day breaks, and a meat-heavy stew in the evenings.

Oliver and Jacob had even made them metal utensils recently, but Henrietta kept using reed chopsticks for no real reason beyond habit. Perhaps the atypical eating utensils made her feel more sophisticated, though odds were good that anything other than eating with her hands would make her feel that way. Ultimately, it didn't really matter, but as the Commander it was her duty to ensure the mental health of her crew stayed good, and that included herself. Analysis of her own thoughts in trivial matters helped with that.

Oliver and Alyssa were sniping at one another, but there was no real heat to it at the moment. Her best understanding was they were doing it more out of pure habit than any kind of real animosity. Not that it couldn't get more heated, but they were just all a bit tired from long days at the moment.

Even during their meals, the work never truly stopped. The sound of metal scraping against metal came from where Jacob was filing a set of gears for the Lumbermill kiln. Tully bobbed in and out between Clark's feet as he cooked, hoping for scraps. Alyssa aggressively lounged on a bench, kicking her feet in the air while she flicked a small flame between her fingers, juggling fire for practice. On the other side of the table, Oliver absently ate with one hand as he focused on his spellwork. The Light Crystal embedded in his hand glowed, the light would pull itself free of the crystal for a couple of moments, and then it would fade and the process started anew.

Sleep came easy these days, but still not instantly. The scent of Shadow was always present to Henrietta's nose, but when she only smelled it strongly while inside their sleeping-hut, her brain had learned to associate it with rest. Jacob was already fast asleep, Alyssa tossed a bit as she tried to get comfortable, the light Oliver shed dimmed as he drifted off, and Clark was murmuring softly to Tully, the petalfur curled up on his chest.

And then it was morning.

Breakfast, the discussing of daily priorities, then they split off. Alyssa was doing a run-around to check on everything. Oliver was working on making wrought-iron rollers that would be put to use in creating metal sheets or plates. Clark needed to get another pile of charcoal burning. Jacob was moving wood around Lumbermill. Henrietta built out the physical clay structure, long but not terribly tall, of a kiln for drying out their lumber.

As they wore themselves out, they reconvened around the kitchen common area. It had a roof these days, and furniture made out of wood, fired clay, reeds, even leather in some instances. Clark had been experimenting with using ⟨Refine⟩ as a means to make leather without the normal intensive chemical processes required, and while there was still some ways to go the initial results were promising.

Henrietta was among the last to join the group, moving clay around not being that physically taxing thanks to her tendrils, and tonight kept working on making her dragon fly. With only basic ink to work with, it had been challenging, but she'd gone flying the night before and encountered a fourgle. While a giant, four-winged eagle had very different flight mechanics than a more typical dragon, she'd had plenty of practice combining far more disparate elements.

Jacob was experimenting with ⟨Rimeforge⟩. He'd found that the most interesting results had come when using harvested trinkets of animals and dragons, and was now attempting to intuit a pattern behind what combining parts from different creatures would net him.

Oliver kept working on his floating light spell, and his face brightened when a casting resulted in a stable bit of magic, the wispy mote not self-extinguishing almost immediately, "Yes!"

"Oooh, you finally got your party trick working?" Alyssa looked on in false awe. "So much work... for something I think I remember one of my friends in high school doing as an afternoon project."

With a flick of his wrist, Oliver shot the spell at the Ranger. Even though the spell wasn't even slightly tangible, Alyssa still dramatically bent backwards over herself to avoid their touch.

"Anything can be easy if you start from an arbitrary level of existing infrastructure," Oliver huffed, then swept his hand to recall the light and set it about orbiting his head. He dramatically stood up and relocated about five feet away, where he started carving into a spell-tablet.

"Nooo.... don't leave me be...." Alyssa dramatically fell over herself. "Fiiiinnne. I guess your tiny little light is super impressive, or something. They can illuminate almost as much as my spells can, and I really shouldn't mock the noble efforts of a fellow mage to reach the same heights as his more capable senior."

Oliver ignored her, busy weaving magic in his personal project, and Henrietta caught a glimpse of Jacob stifling a laugh.

"Oliverrrrr....." Alyssa reached out, like she was dramatically trying to lay hands on something life-saving. Oliver kept ignoring her, and Alyssa drew out her syllables until she started choking over her own words, breaking into a coughing fit.

Said coughing fit also drew to a close by the time Oliver finally looked up, "Did you say something?"

Alyssa tried to speak, but her voice came out as an incomprehensible croak.

"Run it past me one more time. I want to make sure I can follow the full design," Henrietta's eyes traced out the gears laid out before them. In truth, she already knew how it all should work, and her studies in runistry were helping her to pick through the general design of the multi-layered enchantment even if she hadn't known what any of it was. It was an impressive feat of engineering, all told, especially given just how much they couldn't do.

"None of it is really new," Oliver habitually protested. "It's just putting it all together that the trial is about. The autonomous placement brackets work basically the same way as they do in the Bronzing Crucible, where I've got [Order Mana] running on loop to ensure the brackets just move forwards. The heat enchantment is just a lessened refinement of what we're using in the Blast furnace for hot air provision."

"The one using a smelter enchantment-modified ⟨Inlay⟩ of [Flamewind]?"

"That's the one. And the Attunement... well, okay, I guess that one is kind of new. But it's based on the Bronzing Crucible, and mostly the Catalytic Orb we've got inside of it."

"What about the sensor enchantments?" Henrietta poked, knowing that was only half of what was going on.

"Oh. I used the Window of Words and Winds for reference there. But those aren't really... enchantments? Okay, I guess they are. They do meet the definition of enchantment. I just took measurements for what the output of 'raw wood' and 'processed lumber' were, and if the [Appraise] ⟨Inlay⟩ gives off the magical signature of the latter, then the switch flips."

"And the moisture sensor?"

"That one really isn't an enchantment. It's just..." Oliver sighed and caved, "An enchantment with an inverted motion slide-based effect, such that two gears will stick together. I just inserted a load-bearing glyph that's dependent on water being physically present within the sample area. No water, the enchantment stops working, and the gears turn independently."

"What about the motion conditional? You made quite a big deal about figuring that out, yet you haven't even mentioned it."

"I mean... it definitely works. And I know you know how it works."

Henrietta turned, raising an eyebrow at Oliver. He seemed totally unfazed, so Henrietta held back a sigh and asked more explicitly. "I wish to be reminded."

It wasn’t about her figuring out how it all worked, of course. It was about getting Oliver to think through everything one last time before live trials truly began, in case there was something obvious that hadn’t been found yet.

"I've got the magic circles inscribed on the outer edge of a gear, and I only used glyphs that are mana-sticky, where they 'hold on' to mana passed through them for some amount of time, so even if the enchantment stops getting fed power, it will keep working briefly," Oliver indicated the relevant symbols by using a floating light as a pointer. It was a bit jerky and jumpy, but he'd been doing so for a lot of tasks as a means of practice. "And by using [Order Mana], I limited all mana inflow for the enchantment to a single point. So long as the gears are turning, the glyphs will all be exposed to the mana inflow, but once the gears stop turning, they'll burn through whatever stores they have and stop functioning shortly thereafter. I haven't been able to test that it doesn't do anything weird, but they do definitely lose their primary effect not long after the power cuts."

He fidgeted a bit nervously, but Henrietta was satisfied. "Alright. I'm convinced, do your thing and get it assembled."

Any kind of priority ordering for their endless tasks was something of an exercise in futility, but if one were to exist, the automation of lumber-drying was fairly low on it. With their current focus of trying to create an artifact that was purely made of metal, wood wasn't in the highest demand. As they ramped up production, and wanted to make transport carts and the like, it would definitely become more important, but Henrietta had learned her lesson about solving problems before they came up.

However, it did make for an excellent test run for more advanced machines, because it was such a simple process. Move a stack of lumber into a place, activate a bit of magic, move it out of the place. Placement brackets, following a current of variably-activated [Order Mana], picked up hand-stacked piles of wood a few inches off the ground, then moved it forwards until a lever was tripped, at which point power was cut to the brackets and the lumber thudded into place. At that point, a combination of a drying enchantment and Attunement spell would activate, and only once both of those processes were complete - each of which were variable in length, and it wouldn't do to eject the wood while it was either still wet or incompletely Attuned - would the placement brackets start moving again, this time to take the pile of freshly-dried lumber out of the kiln, and reset, bringing in the next load.

Henrietta only half-watched as Jacob and Oliver worked to assemble the enchantment gearbox the wood kiln was using, devoting most of her attention to her Sketchbook. The dragon was barely flight capable, mostly just able to glide, but she wanted a true flying dragon for a means of transport. She'd had luck shrinking it a bit, but didn't want to compromise its combat prowess too much as she did so.

Impressively, Oliver and Jacob managed to get it all assembled before she finished, and she looked over the clockwork mechanism.

The largest and most visually imposing portion was the reset mechanism, as it needed to have enough power to mechanically reset everything else about the device. It was designed to sit vertically, and only had three moving parts. The first was a gear with half of its internal disc removed, so it was highly off-balance. It was mounted such that the heavy side was on the left, at the bottom there were four notches designed to catch on a small rod, and the top had four ratcheting teeth as well as two pegs, one in the solid section of the gear, the other in the ratcheting teeth's edge. The pegs were each intended to engage with a counterbalanced lever-and-pawl at the top, to either trip it into catching on the ratchets, or to trip it out of engaging with the ratchets and allowing gravity to do its work, rotating the gear back to the start.

At the bottom of the reset gear, there was a wheel with exactly one peg sticking out of it. Designed for exactly whenever each of the three enchantments completed, it was connected to three other gears, each of which were configured a bit like a ratcheting gear, where an additional key was needed for the turning gear to have any 'bite,' and said key disengaged after one rotation of the gear turning.

The reason why three enchantments had four steps was because the motion enchantment was used twice.

The gears powering the enchantments themselves were fairly simple. They were slightly stacked, all of them overlapping the stream of mana meant to power the design, and all three with simple checks dictating whether they were connected to the output of the clockwork heart running the device. The motion enchantment was mechanical, tripping based on the physical presence or absence of lumber in the kiln. The Attunement enchantment relied on a bronze-and-glass lever that physically changed states based on if it [Appraise]d 'lumber' or 'wood,' and as Oliver described, the moisture content detector kept two gears moving in unison only if sufficient water was present in the kiln.

Through a bit of clockwork wizardry - the mechanical kind, not the mana-based kind - any time one of those systems disengaged, it diverted its power to the signal gears... which would rotate once, cause the reset gear to advance a stage, and then spin until the system restarted. In that way, it didn't matter which enchantment finished first, so long as all of them did, the system would recognize it, and finish when appropriate.

Deploying the enchantment didn't go perfectly smoothly, of course. No engineering project ever went perfectly. Instead, it took several hours of tinkering, a brief incident where a placement bracket flung itself at high speeds into the river, and lots of messing about with the mechanical switch meant to determine if lumber was present or absent in the system, prompting Henrietta to quietly add replacing it with a more magical sensor to her to-do lists.

But eventually, eventually, they got to celebrate as the pile of rough-sawn lumber picked itself up, slowly slid into its kiln, and a hot breeze picked up in the structure.

It may not have been the most visually impressive piece of magic they'd worked, but it was nonetheless phenomenal to behold.

They were getting there, one click at a time.
