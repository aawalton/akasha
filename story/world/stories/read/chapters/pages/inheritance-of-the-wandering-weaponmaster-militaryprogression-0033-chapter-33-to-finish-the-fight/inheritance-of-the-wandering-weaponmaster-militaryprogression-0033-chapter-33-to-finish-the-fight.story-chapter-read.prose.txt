The cleanup went much more slowly than Ezra’d imagined. From the time that the fight had begun to when the last Dragonkin was killed and the goblinoid lines broke… it was maybe half an hour? He was only on the lines for half that time, at most, but it was nearly impossible to tell how long it’d been. As Petr’d said, fighting was a strange flux of time. It was hard, the time blurred, and he’d found his chest heaving for breath more than he expected.

“Meditate,” Georg commanded from where he lay nearby. His head was bound with strips of cloth that were red with his blood, his neck braced with a wrap of linen and something else Ezra didn’t know. “You’ve exhausted your inner world, but you’re not wounded. You need to get on the lines sooner rather than later. The unblooded levies need someone with experience nearby.”

“Yes sir,” Ezra answered without hesitation, closing his eyes and pulling some earth aura into his inner world, but focusing more on the wind that he’d paid less attention to until now. That was what had allowed him to kill the orc, a burst of wind that pushed his spear forward in the throw. Now that he thought about the wind more, he could feel it swirling around inside him, moving at a sedate pace through his inner world. He’d been thinking more about a physical thing there, and realized that the wind’d already been present as he was experimenting with the other affinities.

Instead of taking the time to experiment, to recover more slowly but to explore more, Ezra started pulling wind affinity aura into his inner world, changing the slowly swirling wind to instead start whipping around at the boundaries of his mind.

Stop that, Jormun’s voice cracked like thunder in Ezra’s mind, startling him out of his meditation. At his side, Georg stirred, his eyes opening to look at Ezra, and the younger man hung his head again, whispering under his breath to speak to Jormun.

“Why?”

It’s not common that an Initiate has an opportunity to completely fill their inner world with an affinity that’s suited to them. I know you’re in a rush to help other people, and that’s good. I won’t stop you from doing that. However, if you rush right now when you can take some time and benefit much more, you’ll regret it.

There were plenty of thoughts about other things that he would regret if he had the opportunity, but Ezra couldn’t rightly talk about them to Jormun right now. Instead, he could only listen as the gruff ghost continued speaking.

You’ve recovered enough to be able to go and help mop up the broken army. They aren’t working together, and they’re fleeing while you have experience killing them. I’d say go now, kill some more, and then, when you get a break, we can talk about the opportunity you have right now.

With just a grumble, Ezra got to his feet, looked down at the reclining Georg, and said, “I’m good to go for now.”

The lieutenant started to laugh and shake his head, but his face twinged in pain as he did so. Instead, he sighed and kept himself still once again. Then, he said, “I haven’t had the opportunity yet to say it. Thank you, Ezra. You saved my life, as well as most of the squad, from what little I can remember. Most of what I know comes from what Amy and Troy have reported to me, and I know that, even as you were in danger yourself, you saved your squad. That’s what a damn hero does, and I’m glad to have you in my squad.”

The cloud that’d hung over him since Helen’s death was pushed back by that, and Ezra allowed himself to smile. “Thank you, sir.”

“No, say you’re welcome.”

“You’re welcome, sir,” Ezra laughed as he stood and found his shield. His spear, he only learned after being brought here to recover, had been ruined by the move he made, but Georg’d happily offered for Ezra to take his, and as the weight settled in his hand, Ezra was ready to go.

Marching out of the convalescents’ tent, Ezra found one of the sergeants nearby, Sergeant Oscar, the one who seemed to be the soldier in charge of the rank and file soldiers.

“Reporting back to the lines, Sergeant!”

“You’re clear to get back on the line?” Oscar asked, staring into Ezra’s eyes.

“Yes sir.”

“Good. The lines have pressed maybe another kilometer further ahead, with the trap laid keeping the goblinoids from escaping. Go ahead, find anyone in command there, and follow whatever they tell you.”

“Yes sir.”

Sergeant Oscar waved him off after that, and Ezra just nodded as he followed the command. He jogged forward, the weight of the shield tugging at his elbow and hurting but not throbbing so bad that he couldn’t continue. In less than a minute, he was out of the range of the camp, and maybe a kilometer and a half away, he could see the battle lines. There weren’t hardly any people between where he was now and where he was going, and he hurried along.

“Why do you think I should wait?” Ezra asked in a patterned breathing as he jogged forward.

There’s benefits to filling your entire inner world up with aura that’s an affinity that’s suited to you that you yourself have transformed from one affinity to what suits you.

“And you’re not going to tell me what those benefits are?” Ezra laughed. Just as Jormun started to open his mouth, the young man shook his head and continued, “I know, and I’m starting to understand why you say it. This is an internal path I’m walking, nobody else can walk it for me. I never would have been able to do that with my spear if you’d just told me to pay attention to the wind in my inner world. Now, I can better feel it inside than I think I would have been able to if you’d just said it.”

Jormun didn’t say anything, just floating along and grinning as Ezra continued on his path towards the front lines.

“So, it’s better that I experience it for myself, just listen to advice that you give when you give it,” Ezra sighed, wishing he had more aura in his inner world to strengthen his hurt elbow. It was only a couple minutes before he couldn’t think about that any longer as he arrived at the front lines, and chaos reigned in a way that he hadn’t expected. The goblinoids were panicked, fighting each other nearly as much as the humans who were slaughtering them, trying to push forward. However, the passage out of the valley was largely blocked by a landslide that seemed to have occurred sometime recently and the path was, about another kilometer further back, full of loose stones of various sizes.

“You, soldier!” a voice Ezra didn’t know commanded. He looked, and saw someone with a metal helmet and breastplate over a uniform with the Duke’s crest on the shoulder.

“Yeah?” Ezra asked.

“Speak with respect!” the man snapped, taking no time to reprimand Ezra. He felt his hackles raise at that, but he forced down the frustration, instead bowing his head.

“Where can I help, sir?”

“Get on the front lines, cover a levy and kill whatever you can. Now go!”

Ezra nodded again and continued on his way, grateful to be out of this asshat’s vicinity. He hustled forward, and with his spear held high, he closed in on the nearest goblin. It was being pushed back, towards the humans, as its fellow tried to rush away from certain death. Now confronted with an inevitable fight, the monster threw itself at the levies who stood there without hesitation, its face twisted in terror and rage. The levies thrust forward, their spears scraping off the creature’s hide as it pushed the spears away.

Both men screamed as their enemy closed in on their guard, one turned and dropping his spear as the other smacked his spear’s haft into the goblin’s head. The wood made a satisfying thunking sound, but the goblin wasn’t fazed and closed in, one hand holding a dagger more than long enough to gut the unprepared levies.

“Out of the way!” Ezra barked as the man hesitated, but his command was more than enough to get the man to duck back. The goblin grinned widely as it thought its prey was fleeing, but Ezra’s spear caught it in the chest. Green skin was covered with purple blood as Ezra’s attack pierced through its sternum and the goblin slumped and weighed his weapon down. With a twist of the spear, the corpse was dislodged, and Ezra grunted as he settled himself on the front line.

“Get back here!” he commanded to the coward as he gestured for the other one, the one who hadn’t dropped his spear, to set up on his other side.

“We’ll push forward with everyone else,” Ezra said, making sure they both stood with him. “I’ll keep you safe, you just focus on stabbing the fuckers as hard as you can. Got it?”

Both men nodded, and Ezra led their charge forward, his spear finding plenty of green, warty flesh to pierce.

***

Maybe two hours later, the last of the goblinoids were slain, and Ezra found himself somewhere to sit in the camp, his shoulders screaming in pain as his legs, hips, and back weren’t much better. With a groan, he took his food and settled onto a log that was laid somewhere away from everyone else. The casualties had been taken for the company, and there had been precious few deaths, though Ezra didn’t know how many, just that Georg was surprised to hear how many.

While he sat, hoping for some peace and quiet, Boyd, and in his wake, Cord and Leah, found him and set themselves up nearby.

“Ezra, that was fucking impressive,” Boyd was the first to speak, sitting beside him and nudging Ezra’s left side. He grunted in discomfort as his elbow jostled and his plate nearly fell.

“Back off you walking boulder,” Ezra grouched as he elbowed Boyd back. “My elbow’s been killing me since it got cracked, and you just hit it.”

To his credit, Boyd laughed and scooted away, taking a big mouthful of his stew as he did so. “How did you do it? You said you weren’t an Adept, right? But only they can use aura like that.”

“I did before, when I was trying to get accepted as a soldier,” Ezra explained between bites. “Knocked me out when I did it last time. I think I got myself strong enough that this time it was only real fuckin’ uncomfortable instead.”

“So Initiates can do that? It seemed cooler than what the two Adepts were doing,” he said, though Cord didn’t hesitate to shoot his younger brother a disgusted look. Boyd shrugged and asked, “What?”

“One of them fucking died, you idiot,” Cord shook his head.

“And they both did a lot more than I did,” Ezra added. “I knocked myself out with a single attack, they both took care of the two bugbears as well as standing against the Dragonkin for a while when I didn’t have a prayer of it. They could control themselves, I couldn’t. I just had one good attack while Helen sacrificed herself for all of us.”

The elite recruits went quiet at that, their faces different displays of thoughtful, and Ezra enjoyed the quiet. After a minute or two, Boyd spoke up again. “But that was great, what you did. I wish I could do stuff like that. You sure I can’t do anything to feel aura better?”

Ezra sighed and said, “Petr said he’s never been able to. Some people don’t figure out how to, I guess. Maybe you’ll be like that? Maybe you won’t and just take some time. I really don’t know much, though, so don’t ask again, man. It’s driving me crazy.”

The others nodded, and, fortunately, before long, Ezra was finally alone to speak with Jormun again. This time, the old mentor just motioned for Ezra to take his time to convert any aura he could take hold of into wind attribute. It was strange, he thought, to take ambient wind aura and then mentally transfer it to attributeless aura and then back to wind attribute aura as it was pulled into his inner world. Even so, there was definitely a different quality to it when he’d created the aura himself, something deeper, maybe richer?

There were a couple hours that he spent happily refilling his inner world before the camp was stricken. He was exempted from that work, with Amy and Troy both pushing him away from collecting his things.

“You’re our hero today,” Amy laughed as she started collapsing his and the rest of the recruits’ tent. “You deserve to take a little break.”

“Saved me from being the next hit by the orc,” Troy agreed. “I couldn’t have stood up to it. Thanks.”

With his chest burning with pride, Ezra went off to find the sole person he cared about whose fate he didn’t know. Wading through the unorganized levies, it took a while before he finally found the question that got him in the right direction:

“Have you seen the levy that was wearing soldier equipment?”

That, fortunately, brought him quickly to where he wanted to, though the looks of envy the levies exchanged with each other as he asked it were plain to see. He didn’t say anything, he’d been on that envious side plenty of times to sympathize. Even so, he didn’t talk to them, instead only doggedly pursuing his old friend. There was no luck, and as he continued to narrow down his search with no results, he found himself getting more and more sick to his stomach. Finally, when he found the squad Adam was meant to be with, the commander there shrugged and answered, “I think I’ve seen him.”

Before Ezra could begin to lose his patience, Adam popped out of a nearby tent, his face streaked with grime and some blood, but alive.

The two smiled and embraced, the arguments from before forgotten in the relief of survival. They only had a minute or two before they were pulled apart by circumstance, though they parted with a final promise: “I’ll talk to you again soon.”

And then, with an inexorable force, the two were pulled to their own squads, and made to depart back to the mouth of the valley, where they’d entered.

“It was just a single day to fight,” Boyd scoffed. “Seems like a whole lot of work for something so short.”

“It was only that quick,” Petr scolded him, “because we won. Without Chloe killing the Dragonkin so quickly, it would have gone the other way. We were lucky by how quickly she and Basil turned the tides.”

Instead of engaging with the rest of the squad as they talked about that, though, Ezra allowed his mind to wander, to begin pulling more aura into his inner world and converting it to be wind attribute. After all, he wanted to see what would happen when he finished filling it this way, as Jormun had advised him.
