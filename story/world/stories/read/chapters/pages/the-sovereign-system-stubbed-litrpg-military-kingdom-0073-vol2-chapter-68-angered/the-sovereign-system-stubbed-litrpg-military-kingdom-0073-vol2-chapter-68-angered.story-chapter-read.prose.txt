Luke turned his head after the first crunch, unwilling to witness the carnage unfolding behind him.

Every few seconds, a sickening squelch echoed through the air—like a watermelon being stomped—sending shivers down his spine.

“Are you okay, Richard?” he asked, placing a hand on the man’s shoulder, more to distract himself than anything else.

Richard was covered in soot, but beneath the grime, his pale skin peeked through. He coughed intermittently, likely from smoke inhalation, but otherwise seemed to be in one piece.

“This was your plan all along?” Richard rasped.

“Well, not exactly,” Luke admitted with a small shrug. “But given the circumstances, it was the best option. Can you tell me what happened?”

Richard coughed again, harder this time, spitting out a thick black substance. Only then did Luke realize the man was in no condition to speak.

“First, let’s get you some water. There’s an alchemy workshop nearby. We’ll bring some back for the other survivors too,” he offered, helping the larger man to his feet.

“Keep an eye on things here. I won’t be long,” Luke instructed, earning a silent nod from the pale-faced Kayson.

Riann, a short distance away, was still dispatching the unconscious attackers. Thankfully, he’d finally drawn his sword—no longer reducing their skulls to pulp with his boots.

As they arrived at the workshop, Richard’s gaze briefly lingered on the headless body of the assistant before quickly averting his eyes. Luke could sense the quiet sorrow radiating from the big man—and it wasn’t surprising.

It was one thing to fight and kill on the battlefield. But here? This was supposed to be home. These people were supposed to be allies—men who shared the same banner.

Countless powerful figures in the kingdom had died tonight. And Luke knew that the number would only grow.

He retrieved a pail of water from the nearby well and handed it to Richard. The man coughed once more, then seized the bucket and drank deeply, letting the water stream down his soot-streaked robes without a care.

Only after a full thirty seconds did he stop and release a loud belch.

Without a word, he refilled the bucket and turned to leave.

“We should hurry. Uncle Riann inhaled more smoke than I did.”

Luke nodded, and they headed back toward the inner court.

He was about to repeat his earlier question, but Richard beat him to it.

“You’re better off asking my uncle what happened. All I remember is the Right Minister being stabbed… and then everything went to hell.”

“That’s okay. I’m just glad you’re safe,” Luke said, placing a hand on Richard’s broad shoulder. “I don’t think Prince Lyon would forgive me if anything happened to you.”

At the mention of the second prince, Richard’s eyes sharpened, the question forming without a word.

“He’s fine—generally speaking,” Luke replied, recalling the haunted look on the young prince’s face. “But it’ll take time for him to recover from losing his family. I’ll probably need your help with that soon.”

“I’ll do what I can.”

A sudden shout shattered the quiet.

“W–What the fuck!?”

The cry echoed down the hall, sharp and panicked. Both Luke and Richard flinched before instinct took over—they bolted toward the courtroom doors.

The smoke had begun to thin, but it still clung to the high ceilings in wispy, lingering strands. As they pushed forward, Luke’s eyes immediately locked onto a figure standing amidst the haze, facing off against Kayson and Riann.

A familiar dread welled up within him—deep, visceral, almost instinctual. His gut twisted in revulsion as he took in the man’s silhouette.

He was skeletal—thin to the point of malnourishment—draped in sharp, shadowy robes. His posture was unnaturally straight, and in his right hand he held an ornate cane, gripped like a weapon prepared to strike.

But it wasn’t the cane or the robes that disturbed Luke.

There was something wrong.

A darkness coiled around the man’s head, thick and viscous. It didn’t behave like smoke—it hovered with intent, as if alive. Its inky blackness was too dense, too tangible to have come from the earlier smoke bomb.

It reminded Luke of something.

The thought had barely formed when the figure jerked unnaturally, its head swiveling nearly 180 degrees. A loud, grotesque crack-crack-crack of twisting bones filled the air.

A chill ran down Luke’s spine.

Glowing red eyes snapped into view—slitted, reptilian, embedded deep within the skull. And then he saw it.

Kayson’s sword was embedded in the man’s neck.

The blade jutted out cleanly from the other side—yet the man didn’t bleed. He didn’t even seem bothered.

Luke froze, rooted to the spot beneath that haunting gaze.

“Kill him!” Kayson roared, scrambling to grab another weapon. He raised it overhead, ready to strike again.

But before he could bring it down, the skeletal figure collapsed forward—and began to slither.

Gasps rang out.

The man slithered across the floor with obscene speed, his limbs contorting unnaturally as he surged toward Luke like a serpent.

“What!?”

The sight was so grotesque, so utterly unnatural, that everyone froze in stunned disbelief.

Everyone except Luke.

The haze clouding his thoughts was gone now, the Soul Curse fully purged. His mind moved like lightning, dissecting the scene with terrifying clarity.

He’s possessed.

That’s not a normal human… There’s another soul inhabiting his body.

The sensation was unmistakable. Luke had felt something similar before—back when the Bishop of Lhair had revealed his serpentine soul form.

But this one… it was different. Less oppressive. Weaker.

Not the Bishop, then. Possibly a subordinate— or something spawned from the same dark power.

He gritted his teeth.

I can’t just hack at the body. I need to destroy the soul inside it.

The world around him slowed as his thoughts raced.

But how do I activate my soul form? How do I even attack a soul?

His mind flashed back to the two times he’d interacted with soul forms.

The first was during his battle with the Blood Knight. He had used Eagle Eye, projecting his consciousness into the sky. Somehow, through pure instinct, he’d entered his soul state and landed a killing blow.

The second was in the church—when the Bishop of Lhair dragged him into the soul realm without warning. That time, he hadn’t done anything. It had simply happened.

But perhaps… that wasn’t entirely true.

Both times… I was outside of my body.

His eyes narrowed.

Eagle Eye lets me project my consciousness. Maybe that’s the key. Maybe I can replicate that sensation—without relying on the skill.

If I can do that… maybe I can enter my soul form voluntarily.

Luke’s eyes locked onto the glowing red orbs staring back at him with hatred.

But beneath that seething malice… he felt it.

Fear.

A subtle shift tugged at the corners of his lips, rising into the barest hint of a smirk. A supreme confidence surged within him—quiet, resolute, unshakable.

I am the Sovereign.

The Dragon General.

Luke closed his eyes, showing no fear, no urgency. To the others, it might’ve looked like madness—turning away from the monster still crawling toward him—but inside, he was tranquil.

He cast his mind back to those surreal moments when his soul had separated from his body, when he had transcended flesh and bone.

He remembered the sensation of being unbound—no longer shackled to this familiar yet foreign shell.

He even recalled the presence of Luke Nero’s lingering soul, hovering for hours after his own transmigration into this world.

For a moment, Luke wondered: Could I have spoken to him? If I’d known more then…

But he chuckled softly.

Pointless thoughts.

Suddenly, his senses dimmed—as though he had been cast into a dark room, weightless and adrift.

Everything around him faded to a distant hum, as if the world itself had stepped away.

And then—he opened his eyes.

Golden light burst forth, radiant and divine, pouring from his gaze like twin rays of heaven itself. The hall sprang into vivid detail—every crack in the stone, every ripple in the air revealed in staggering clarity.

In front of him stood a wide back, shielding him from the crawling menace.

Richard?

Luke shifted his gaze to the skeletal figure. Its glowing red eyes had widened—fear now dominant, eclipsing the hatred that once burned behind them.

The creature froze mid-slither, its body rigid with terror. Then it let out a shriek, unnatural and sharp, as an inky black mass began to seep from within.

The smoke took shape—twisting, writhing—until it formed a small serpent. Far smaller than the Bishop’s colossal soul form… and far less imposing.

It spun midair and darted upward, phasing through the ceiling in a desperate bid to escape.

Luke glanced at the motionless body the serpent had left behind—an old man, crumpled on the floor like a discarded puppet.

Then he looked down at his own slumped body.

I’m sure I’ll feel that later… he mused with a wry grin.

Turning his gaze to the heavens, Luke summoned his will. In response, two majestic golden wings unfurled behind him and flapped once, launching him into the sky with divine force.

In moments, he soared above the palace.

Though it was still the dead of night, his vision pierced the darkness as if bathed in daylight. He could see everything.

The guards pacing along the city’s edge.

The bustle of the Jade Silk Pavilion.

And beyond… movement in the noble district.

He narrowed his eyes. His vision zoomed in, sharpening on a group of soldiers—some of them familiar. He recognized a few faces among them, and the corner of his mouth twitched upward.

Not now.

He shook his head and turned his attention back to the skies, sweeping the area for the fleeing serpent.

It didn’t take long.

He’s quick…

The spectral snake had already slipped beyond the city walls, darting westward—toward Lhair.

Luke flapped his wings again, the golden appendages slicing through the air. In a single beat, he surged forward, bridging half the distance between them in an instant.

The worm-like figure grew panicked, its movements frantic as it tried to escape, accelerating with everything it had.

Luke flapped his wings once more.

In an instant, he appeared behind the serpent. With a thought, a golden claw materialized—shimmering with divine brilliance—and lashed out, seizing the fleeing soul with ease.

A grip that would leave any enemy in despair.

The serpent writhed violently, its body twisting and contorting in pain, but Luke’s hold was unyielding.

“Speak, worm. Who are you?” he commanded.

“S–Save me, my Lord!” it squealed, voice thick with terror.

Luke frowned.

His claw squeezed tighter.

The soul shrieked, a piercing, pitiful sound that echoed across the sky.

“N–No! Please don’t destroy my soul!” it begged, the panic in its voice raw and trembling.

Hmm?

Luke eased his grip slightly, granting the creature a moment’s breath. He didn’t intend to end it just yet—there were answers he needed.

“I said speak,” he growled. “Who are you, and what was your purpose in Ralis?”

“My Lord… please, I did everything you asked. Spare me—I was loyal…”

Luke’s eyes narrowed. The ramblings were disjointed, desperate. He wasn’t getting what he needed.

“Listen here—if you don’t start talking, I’ll destroy your soul right—”

He stopped.

A deathly wail tore from the serpent’s mouth. Its body convulsed once… then went limp in his outstretched claw.

A heavy silence followed.

And then, slowly, the inky black mass began to dissipate—like smoke being peeled away by the wind.

“Fuck!”

Luke hurled the fading soul downward in frustration. Rage surged through him. Judging by the soul’s last words, it had been terminated—remotely.

The Bishop.

It had to be him.

Luke grit his teeth. That man possessed Soul Poison, Soul Curse… of course he had a method to kill his own minions from afar.

He turned his gaze westward, eyes locking onto the distant horizon as though he could peer across kingdoms toward Lhair.

He was just about to return to the capital when a glowing message appeared before him:

[The effect: Soul Curse has been negated]

Luke blinked in surprise—then smiled.

A slow grin spread across his face before he let out a breathless laugh.

“You’ll have to try harder than that, Bishop!” he roared, his voice ringing with triumph.

Then—

“I WILL CRUSH YOU!”

A voice thundered across the realm, distant but blazing with fury. It hit like a tidal wave of wrath, shaking the air around him. Luke flinched at the sheer intensity, eyes narrowing as his laughter faded.

But then—two more messages flashed before his eyes:

[The Sovereign’s Blessing has negated the skill: Soul Poison]

[The Sovereign’s Blessing has negated the skill: Soul Tracker]

That did it.

Luke broke into a fresh wave of laughter, loud and unrestrained, echoing across the skies above the mortal world.

It took nearly a full minute before he composed himself.

Still grinning, he raised his scaled golden claw and extended a single talon—the middle one.

He pointed it westward.

“We’ll see who crushes who.”
