The days settled into rhythm before she realized it. Dawn brought work, dusk brought repair, and the hours between blurred into a quiet pattern of motion and focus.

Elynara rose early, stretched the stiffness from her limbs, and began her training – small, measured forms to keep her mana flowing steady. When her energy grew thick in her hands, she fed it into the spirit’s light. That had become her constant ritual.

Each morning, the spirit shone a little stronger. Its colors deepened from faint white to warm gold, and its movement had grown playful, almost impatient.

The machine, in turn, moved with more grace; its joints no longer groaned at every shift. Even its voice had softened, though words still came slowly, deliberate and few.

Elynara marked progress in small things.

The way the hum of the ruin’s air felt less heavy now. The way Starpuff darted through the clearing without her needing to call it back every few minutes. The way she found herself smiling more than sighing.

It was quiet work, yet far from lonely.

Then one morning, the spirit changed.

It appeared before her in a rush of light, bright enough to make her blink. Spun around her like a flame caught in wind, leaving a trail of faint shimmer that tingled across her skin.

The energy in it was wild – no longer the gentle pulse of recovery but something alive, restless, and eager.

Elynara laughed, shielding her eyes.

“Alright, alright, what’s gotten into you?”

Tis’ a joyous time. That the day would really come for me to feel so full of life again, it is a wondrous feeling. Thank you, my friend.

Elynara smiled at that, her own mood rising in turn.

“You’re most welcome. Seeing you so lively would make even Starpuff jealous.”

Your little spirit companion, yes. An energetic child, that one. They are quite fond of you. And the stories they speak of, quite wondrous. In turn, I have shared what little I know and understand of our ways with them.

“Ah, thank you! Starpuff having a friend is a good thing. I’m sure it’s been thrilled to have you to talk with.”

Please. There is no need for thanks here. The feeling is mutual.

It paused then, considering their next words.

And… There is one more favor to ask, if you are willing.

Elynara felt the pull before it reached for her. Its energy brushed against her own – hungry, asking.

“You want even more mana?” she guessed.

The pulse that answered made her fingertips buzz, but the spirit restrained itself to answer first.

Indeed. Tis’ feels quite thankless of me to ask for more. We have received so much already. And yet ask more I must still, if you would be so kind?

Elynara watched the spirit carefully. Saw how sincere it looked, yet worries she might refuse.

There was only one possible reply here. And so she smiled and raised her palms.

“Take what you need.”

Thank you, truly.

The transfer began easily, a soft thread flowing from her to the spirit. But this time, it didn’t stop at one cycle. The connection deepened until it felt less like giving and more like breathing together. Her magic flowed outward, the spirit drew it in, and sensing how much it was taking, Elynara took to gathering the mana from around them too.

Together they moved through a rhythm that settled into her bones. Breath, pulse, light.

The hum filled the clearing, rising and falling in time with her heartbeat.

When it finally faded, Elynara staggered back, a little dizzy but grinning.

“Better?”

Indeed! …Yet still not quite enough. I will have to impose upon you some more. My apologies.

“Oh, it’s quite alright. So long as it’s helping I really do not mind.”

The spirit spun once in reply, bright and triumphant. Its light reached the machine and poured into its chest, setting every seam aglow for a heartbeat before fading again.

“Well,” she said, steadying herself with the staff. “That was new.”

Starpuff trilled from its perch on a nearby root, clearly unimpressed. She flicked a small pebble at it in mock warning, and it zipped away with an indignant cry.

Life, at least for the moment, felt steady.

And when she wasn’t feeding mana, she explored around.

The ruin still sprawled far beyond what she had seen, a maze of half-buried halls and broken walkways. She learned to navigate by light and instinct, marking her path with small script on the walls.

Most chambers were empty, save for the ever-present rubble – hollow reminders of a world that had outlived its purpose as whatever the chambers once held was gone or decayed.

But some still breathed faintly.

In one sealed corridor, she found a wall etched with lines of script – hundreds of tiny markings that shimmered faintly when she brushed her hand across them. It was not writing of any kind, the lines etched into the wall itself, inlaid with some crystalline substance.

If it were even a language, she felt it was one of function, not poetry. She spent an hour tracing each line, trying to follow the logic.

Yet whatever the script did, or was for, she could not really discern. So instead she committed what she could into her memory, and traced down some of it in her notebook.

“I could spend years here,” she murmured.

Starpuff zoomed right into her ear from half across the room, where it was inspecting some fallen piece of fabric. Then, satisfied at her yelp, let out a small questioning trill.

“No,” she said, smiling. “Not yet, and maybe not ever. We have other places to go first.”

Further in, she found sealed boxes that hummed faintly when touched. Their surfaces were smooth, marked by symbols that flared to life under her mana.

Inside them rested shards of glass and small, flawless crystals. What little text remained untouched by time spoke of data storage. Of knowledge compressed into light.

Most were cracked, their contents fractured beyond recovery. But a few remained whole, and those she gathered with care.

Her early assumption that nothing fragile could have survived had been wrong. The air here had preserved more than she dared hope, yet less than she would have liked.

No paper records seemed to have remained anywhere – if any were even here at all.

Elynara just didn’t know.

In another chamber she discovered thin sheets of metal, each etched with diagrams of things. Maps, perhaps, or designs. Some glowed faintly when held at an angle, showing layered patterns that shifted in the light.

She packed those too, wrapping them in cloth.

The discoveries didn’t change her mission, but they gave it texture. A sense of continuity. Each piece she found whispered of people who had worked, built, and dreamed here centuries past.

She carried them back to camp, setting them near the fire as if proximity to warmth could keep their memory alive.

And between her scavenging and the spirit’s growing strength, the days passed faster than she expected. Every night she recorded what she had found, sketching rough outlines in her notebook and noting the details of each recovered artifact.

She wasn’t sure who would ever read them – but writing it down made the quiet less vast.

The machine sometimes watched her while she worked. Its eyes glowed faintly, the light steady and calm. When she caught its gaze, she would grin.

“Don’t worry. You’ll get to tell your story too.”

It always answered with silence, but the hum in its chest seemed a little warmer afterward.

By the end of the week, even the ruin felt different. The air no longer carried the stillness of a tomb. The plants that had once clung to the walls now grew greener, drinking from the faint pulse of returning mana. The spirit glowed high above the clearing each night, painting the broken stone in silver and gold.

Elynara sat beneath it, her boots propped near the fire, journal open across her knees.

“Not bad,” she said aloud. “For a dead place.”

Starpuff landed on her shoulder, humming low in agreement.

The spirit drifted overhead, its glow reflecting off the metal of the guardian machine, and for a moment the whole clearing shimmered with life.

Elynara didn’t know how much more mana the spirit needed, but it felt like they were getting there, slowly but surely.

And after so many days staying here, Elynara noted down one more thing:

It was warm here, the nights nowhere near as cold as outside.

Whether it was thanks to the closed off nature of the ruins, or the faint echoes of the city machinery still running underneath it all, she did not know.

Whatever it was, it made her sleeping and camping all the more pleasant.

Though as she bit into one of her rations, Elynara decided she would splurge on tastier ones next time.

Elynara refused to grow idle.

The ruins had become a rhythm – days of work, nights of restoration – but she made sure her own training fit between the hours.

She began small. Mana threads first, pulled slowly through her fingers until they shimmered faint and thin. Where she once hurled fire without much thought, now she shaped it into focus – fine, deliberate streams that flickered along her palms like woven strands.

The practice, as always, demanded patience.

Sweat gathered at her temples as she kept the flames balanced, not too strong, not too weak. When one spark slipped control, she snuffed it between her palms and started again.

“Precision,” she told herself. “Not power.”

Starpuff bobbed on a rock beside her. ‘Popping’ short trills in mock boredom. A trick it recently learned to annoy her.

“Don’t start,” she warned without looking up. “You still can’t even light a candle.”

Whatever her little companion did during the fight last time, it still wasn’t very good at it. Giving it mana more often resulted in failures or shining light rather than anything Elynara would consider a spell.

Still, it was growing, and with the ‘adult’ spirit for comparison now, Elynara had high hopes for it.

The puff trilled something defensive, then launched into a lazy spin that nearly toppled it off the rock. Elynara snorted, the distraction breaking her concentration. The flame sputtered out, leaving only a curl of smoke.

“Perfect. Now we’re both useless.”

The puff trilled louder, zooming around her in a smug rhythm.

She laughed despite herself and tried again.

Her second attempt held steadier. The flame bent to her will, no longer spilling mana into wasteful heat. When she ended the exercise, the faint notification rang in her mind – crisp and clear.

She checked on her blinking dot, and found a very welcome change.

Her Fire Magic has leveled up!

Elynara grinned wide enough for her cheeks to ache.

“Finally.”

It was proof she was still improving, even here.

Growth in the middle of decay.

No matter her location, Elynara would continue.

She tried again, feeling the improved control instantly. Everything came just a little easier again.

And as she made a perfect whip of fire, and then a lasso in one spell, she could not help but grin.

Her grasp on magic was growing. And Elynara looked forward to what else she might soon be able to do!

When night fell, she sat by her small fire and watched it dance against the backdrop of ruin. The glow painted her hands gold, reflected in the polished fragments of metal nearby.

She felt stronger, steadier, but every flicker of light reminded her there was more work waiting beyond herself.

Behind her, the spirit hovered in its usual quiet orbit, its glow soft and rhythmic. It had grown brighter over the past week, its movements far more fluid, but now it trembled faintly – light rippling in uneven waves that tugged at her attention.

Elynara turned.

“What is it this time?”

Apologies. It is so close, I feel. Centuries of waiting I endured. And yet I grow impatient when the goal is so close.

“Pfft. And here I thought it might be something else.”

It shook its head vehemently.

She sighed, already smiling.

“Alright, greedy thing. Let’s see what you’re up to.”

Thank you.

The spirit’s pulse quickened in reply, a wordless tug that reached through her chest. Impatient for more.

Starpuff buzzed around her head, trilling in anxious tones, but she waved it aside.

“I know what I’m doing.”

She sat cross-legged and steadied her breathing. The air smelled faintly of ash and rain, the night’s chill brushing across her face. Slowly she reached out with her magic, feeling the familiar thread that connected her to the spirit – thin but vibrant.

“Ready?”

The light pulsed once, affirmative.

Elynara let the mana flow.

It began smoothly, the exchange familiar from their daily work. She focused on gathering more while her energy ran into the spirit, and the spirit brightened in turn.

But tonight, the pull didn’t stop. The spirit drank deeper, drawing from her reserves with a hunger she hadn’t felt before.

Her pulse quickened. She steadied the channel, tried to slow the flow, but the current pressed forward regardless.

“Alright, easy,” she murmured. “Not all at once.”

The spirit didn’t listen – or couldn’t. Its glow surged brighter, light curling through its shape in swirling colors. Threads of blue, gold, and white wound together in a storm of motion. The energy wasn’t wild, but vast.

It pulled at her core, testing how much she could give.

Elynara could only focus on gathering more mana faster.

And faster.

Pulling from further away.

Her hands shook. Sweat beaded across her brow. Still, she didn’t stop.

“Fine,” she muttered through gritted teeth. “You want more? Take it.”

She closed her eyes and leaned into the rhythm. Inhale, draw from the air; exhale, give it back. The cycle repeated, steady but taxing.

The mana she drew from the environment coiled through her chest, through her arms, until her palms burned with heat.

Only to then be taken away by the greedy pull of the spirit.

The spirit’s glow flared higher, so bright it cast sharp shadows across the ruined walls. Starpuff darted back, squealing in alarm, but Elynara stayed still.

The strain reached her bones now, her muscles trembling under invisible weight.

Then the light steadied.

The pull eased, the balance returned.

Elynara gasped, lungs burning.

“You–” She stopped to catch her breath. “You could warn me next time.”

My sincere apologies. To feel complete again– I could not help myself.

The spirit shimmered, its color rich and full again. Where before its edges had blurred, now it looked whole – solid spirit light shaped into form. Its movements were smoother, almost graceful.

Elynara smiled tiredly.

“I can somewhat relate. But a warning would have been appreciated nonetheless.”

Apologies. And thank you again.

The spirit floated close, pulsing in a gentle rhythm that brushed warmth across her face. She reached out, fingers passing through the glow.

It was soft – warm without heat, pressure without touch.

“You’re welcome,” she said quietly.

The spirit lingered a moment longer, then drifted toward the machine resting nearby.

Its light spread across the guardian’s surface, sinking into the metal until faint veins of color traced along the seams. The old runes she had uncovered during repairs flickered faintly, one by one, until the entire chest plate glowed with faint silver light.

The machine stirred, a low hum rising in its core. The eyes lit faintly, not bright, but aware.

Elynara sat back on her heels, exhausted but grinning.

“You’re both waking up, aren’t you?”

Starpuff landed on her shoulder, buzzing a quiet note of concern.

“I’m fine,” she said, patting her puffball friend. “Just tired.”

The puff trilled again, unconvinced.

The night air cooled further, and she reached to stoke the campfire. Its flame responded easily – steady and narrow, shaped to her will without excess spark and with little mana used. The sight made her smile all over again.

Progress, everywhere she looked.

The spirit hovered near the machine’s chest, its light rhythmic and calm now. The guardian’s hum joined it, low and constant, until the two blended into one gentle resonance.

Elynara rested her chin on her knees and watched the glow play across the clearing.

“Alright,” she murmured. “Tomorrow we try something bigger.”

The puff chirped a single hopeful note.

“Don’t look at me like that,” she said, amused. “It’ll be fine.”

The spirit pulsed in soft agreement. The machine answered with a faint echoing tone.

Elynara’s laughter carried softly into the night.

The next day, the exchange happened again.

The spirit was greedy again, though more constrained in its pull.

Yet the exchange still tired her out nonetheless.

The air still shimmered faintly from their exchange of mana. Elynara hadn’t expected the spirit to need more mana again after becoming ‘whole’, but it did – and now its glow had stabilized, strong enough to throw color across the stone and make the machine’s plating gleam pale gold.

She watched the light ripple across the clearing, breathing deep to steady the ache in her chest from the drain.

“You really shouldn’t scare me like that again,” she said quietly. “You’re too important to go burning yourself out now.”

Although Elynara said it to the spirit, it had been a reminder to herself too.

Though she trusted the spirit – and knew her control of mana enough to sever the exchange, if she felt the need to – it would be far too easy to give too much too often.

She needed to remember that a person could only ever give so much.

The spirit hovered in front of her, its form now shifting in slow waves.

There was no answer this time, only that same bright steadiness she had come to recognize from her spirit companion as emotion made visible.

Starpuff fluttered between them, letting out a questioning trill.

“I think it’s fine,” Elynara said, smiling faintly. “A little too fine, actually. Look at you.”

The spirit pulsed once, a flash of warmth full of love and acceptance brushed across her skin.

Then, without warning, it moved.

A shimmer reached for her – gentle, weightless – and before she could step back, it touched her shoulders. The warmth wasn’t heat; it was something deeper, filling the space between heartbeats. The contact lingered only a breath before it drew closer, enveloping her in a hug of light so soft she almost forgot to breathe.

It wasn’t just light. It was intent.

Gratitude.

She heard no words, yet the strength of meaning struck clear as thought.

Thank you.

Elynara froze, half laughing, half breathless.

“Wait, what are you–”

The glow flared brighter, and then the spirit turned.

It flowed forward in a ribbon of light and dove straight into the machine’s chest.

“Wait!” Elynara reached out instinctively, but her hand met only air.

The light expanded, flooding the clearing.

Every seam of the machine came alive – lines of silver-blue racing across the plating, pulsing in rhythm with an unseen heartbeat. The sound deepened, a low, resonant hum that made the ground tremble.

The guardian’s frame convulsed once, hard enough that fragments of dust slid from its joints. Hard enough that Elynara was afraid it might just fall apart.

The glow intensified, forcing her to look through closed fingers.

And then the change began.

Elynara stepped back, shielding her face from the glare. She had seen and learned a lot during the repairs. Made a lot of magic happen. But this was neither simple magic, nor mere repair.

What occurred before her… was a transformation.

The light threaded through the guardian’s frame, spreading along every seam. Where metal had cracked, it healed. Where joints had rusted stiff, they softened and moved with new precision.

The rigid edges of its plating began to shift, their lines smoothing as though shaped by unseen hands. Parts of it gleamed with a mirror polish, others softened to matte, breathing hues that shifted between silver and pale blue.

The scent in the air changed too – ozone and grass, metal and rain, a sharp blend of life and circuitry intertwined.

Elynara’s pulse quickened. She could feel the energy flowing outward now, stretching beyond what her senses could normally touch. It wasn’t only mechanical anymore. The machine’s presence had joined the natural rhythm of the ruin, blending with the faint pulse of the earth’s mana.

Starpuff drifted closer, its glow pale beside the brilliance of the transformation. It chirped once – concern, awe, and confusion wrapped together.

“I know,” Elynara whispered. “I don’t understand it either.”

The light reached a peak, then settled. The humming faded to a soft vibration underfoot.

When her eyes adjusted, she found herself staring at something entirely new.

The guardian still resembled the machine she had repaired, but the difference was unmistakable.

Their limbs moved with quiet balance and its surface shimmered faintly in the firelight, no longer dull metal but something alive with mana.

The faint glow of veins pulsed under its plating, following patterns that looked almost organic.

For a long moment, it didn’t move.

Then its – her – eyes opened.

Not the cold mechanical lights she remembered, but something deeper – luminescent and clear, a soft shade of blue that rippled like water reflecting sky.

Elynara’s breath caught.

The head turned toward her.

“I…” The voice emerged halting, smooth, and nearly-organic sounding. Hesitant and unsure. “I can access the system now.”

The words hung in the air, sharp against the silence.

Elynara blinked.

“You–what?”

The guardian paused, looking down at its own hands, fingers curling with a precision that was almost human.

“Access… confirmed. System interface active.” The tone wavered, low and wondering. “Only the living should be able to.”

Elynara’s throat tightened. For a second she couldn’t speak. She felt the truth of it in her gut – felt it in the rhythm that pulsed faintly in the air around them.

Felt that she could no longer feel the spirit and machine. The two becoming one.

“Well,” she said finally, voice soft. “Guess that makes you living, doesn’t it?”

The guardian’s eyes flickered again, a shimmer of confusion that turned to something quieter.

Recognition.

It straightened slowly, testing the movement. The faint hum of energy beneath its frame steadied into a rhythm that matched her own pulse.

Starpuff spun excitedly around them, trilling high notes that echoed against the stone.

Elynara wiped her hands across her face, half laughing.

“I don’t even know what to call this. You’re not just a relic anymore.”

The guardian tilted its head slightly, the movement smooth and almost human.

“Designation,” it said. “Unknown.”

Elynara grinned.

“We’ll fix that too.”

The spirit’s glow now lived within the guardian – no separate light, no hovering presence. Yet she could feel it still, woven through the machine’s mana. It hadn’t disappeared. It had simply found a new form to exist in.

Though the melding of the two… it felt too perfect. Elynara was afraid the two would never separate.

Neither did the machine look the same as it did prior.

The two merged completely, reborn anew.

The guardian raised a hand, studying the fingers as though seeing them for the first time. The motion was clumsy, curious. And when its eyes met hers again, they shone with quiet awareness.

“Alive,” it said simply.

Elynara smiled until her cheeks hurt.

“Yeah. Alive.”

For the first time since entering the Deadzone, the air around her felt light. The ruin, long silent, seemed to exhale.

Starpuff trilled once more, circling both of them, scattering faint sparks into the dark.

Elynara reached out and rested a hand on the guardian’s newly-formed arm. The hum beneath her palm was warm. Steady.

“Welcome,” she whispered “To the world of the living.”

And in the glow of three quiet lights – spirit, spark, and girl – the world felt a little closer to healing.
