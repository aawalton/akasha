Callie stood frozen with her hand reaching out towards Mom. Mom stood frozen with her hand reaching out towards Callie. Captain Voss and Lysander stood frozen, their hands having stopped midway to their weapons. The blonde ranger was staring out the wagon with her eyes as wide as saucers. For a while, everyone just stood frozen and stared at what Callie had done.

A field of flowers had erupted around her, surrounding her in a perfect circle. Mom had been partially caught up in it, her shoes covered with flowers and greenery.

"Everything alright, Captain?" A low voice asked, and the large ranger crawled out of the tent. He too joined everyone else in staring at the clearly magical field of flowers. There were even motes of sparkling light settling down on it, looking especially beautiful in the early morning light.

"I'm so sorry…" Callie whimpered, looking at her Mom. "I felt so happy for the foxes, it just… burst out…"

Mom's mouth opened and closed, too shocked to really say anything.

"This certainly explains a few things…" Captain Voss noted, the first one to come out of the stupor. She walked up to the circle of flowers Callie had created, crouching down and inspecting them. "Magic."

All of a sudden, Mom lunged forward, grabbing Callie with one hand while with the other she withdrew her sword and pressed it dangerously close to Captain Voss's neck.

The other rangers reacted instantly, grabbing their own weapons and ready to rush forward. The blonde woman somehow had already nocked an arrow into a bow and had it aimed at Mom's head.

"NO!" Callie yelled.

Thankfully, Captain Voss had stopped the other rangers from doing anything by raising her hand in the air.

"Lower your sword," Captain Voss said calmly but firmly.

Mom remained stubbornly where she was, her sword still held close to Captain's neck. Would she really hurt another human?

"MOM!" Callie whimpered, her heart thundering in her chest with fear.

The flowers seemed to react to her emotion, their bright bloom drooping slightly to the ground.

"There's no reason to ruin this magical miracle, is there? We have no intention of hurting your daughter," Captain Voss assured.

"Nobody must know of this," Mom hissed.

"You think we would tell anyone of this? We're not fools, especially when you know our secret as well," Captain Voss said.

Mom pulled her sword back a little, but not fully. "Dragonbloods must never find out what happened here."

"You really think we have a friendly relationship with dragonbloods? If there is something we hate more than hunters, then it is dragonbloods. Although it does make me curious… is your daughter one? Are you perhaps one? Hiding away your true bloodline by living in some village in the middle of nowhere?"

"That is none of your business," Mom snarled and withdrew her sword.

The other rangers relaxed, lowering their weapons, but not fully removing their hands from them.

"Okay, let's all put our weapons away. We're clearly scaring the child. The flowers are starting to wilt. It would be a shame for something so beautiful to wilt, don't you all agree?" Captain Voss said.

There was a moment of silence, and Mom finally sheathed her sword. Her grip remained firmly on Callie, and her eyes shot nervously from one ranger to the next. They fully withdrew their weapons.

"Isn't that better? Now, let us establish that there will be no dragonbloods involved in this, no reports, no official record. We merely happened upon a patch of flowers, is that clear?" Captain Voss said firmly.

"Yes, Captain!" The three rangers replied in unison.

"Can I really trust that?" Mom asked.

"Just as much as I can trust you not to expose the true nature of our organization," the captain shrugged.

"Nobody would believe us even if we said anything," Mom countered.

"The same is true the other way around. Who would believe that a child not even ten years old can grow a field of flowers with a wave of her hand? Growing plants is no easy magic, you know," Captain Voss. "Now, why don't we clean this place up. This patch of greenery looks oddly out of place. It would be troublesome if somebody else noticed."

Mom nodded, and they all were soon picking up the flowers Callie had sprouted. She felt so bad. She wasn't able to control her magic. But the feeling had been so sudden and so powerful. Much stronger than any magic she had before. Could it be that another petal had appeared on her mark?

Callie wanted to look underneath her vambrace, but didn't dare. She would have to wait until they got home. Mom clearly believed that it was best not to explain the origins of Callie's magical power. Although Callie did wonder—would it be such a bad thing for the rangers to know about the dragon?

Any doubts that they might harbor ill will towards arcane beasts were completely gone. After seeing how gently Lysander had treated the thunderfox, how the Captain put the creature's life and safety above all else, and how the blonde ranger likely spent the whole night awake along with the thunderfoxes, had convinced Callie that these people truly and genuinely cared for arcane beasts.

But telling them about the dragon… Maybe it was better to remain cautious. Dad had said that knowledge that dragons were still alive could shake the world. Whatever that meant. Nothing good, Callie imagined.

In the end, they picked all the flowers in silence. The blonde ranger looked particularly pleased as there were medicinal herbs among the batch Callie grew—matricaria, dandilynx and even virthen which could be made into an antiseptic salve.

"These will replenish our stocks nicely," the blonde woman whispered, tucking the flowers and herbs into one of the many drawers of the wagon.

"Excuse me," Callie addressed the woman. She noticed that Mom tensed up while Captain Voss kept an eye on Callie.

"What is it?" The blonde ranger asked. She was speaking a bit strangely. With an accent, Callie realized after a moment. Was she from someplace far away? Mom had said that there were faraway places where people spoke a different language and looked much different. Perhaps blonde hair was normal there.

"What is your name?"

The woman blinked, then smiled gently. "My apologies. I never introduced myself. I'm Eliza Beaumont."

The way the woman pronounced her surname, Callie felt that she couldn't repeat it successfully. Could one's tongue even twist to make such a sound?

The large silent ranger walked up to Callie and pointed at himself. "Bram Moore."

"Nice to meet you, Eliza and Bram. I'm Calluna Aurelwynn," Callie said politely.

"You're full of surprises, kid," Lysander noted off-handedly.

Mom glowered at him.

"Tell me about it. But as we agreed, nobody will say anything about the matter. I do have to wonder… How do you intend to get your daughter's power under control? She clearly cannot control it at all." Captain Voss looked at Mom.

"We're working on it…" Mom growled.

"I suppose it's none of our business. It's a good thing you're living in such a remote village, but if you can't get this under control soon… Well, who knows who might find out about this next?"

Mom's fists clenched tightly. Callie couldn't help but feel like a burden. She would have to work hard on controlling her emotions. On tempering her heart. Clearly, that was a step in the right direction. She just had to keep working on it.

"Okay, enough! Let's head back to town for breakfast. If I'm not wrong, you two still have to travel all the way back to your village today."

Mom, Callie, and Eliza rode in the back of the wagon. Lysander sat at the bench to control the two horses pulling it, while Captain and Brem rode on their steeds alongside the wagon.

"Mom…" Callie dared to ask after a while. "Could we perhaps go to the library again?"

She wanted to check more of the old books. Perhaps one of them had more myths about the dragons, dragon magic, and how to control it. Because Captain Voss was right. Callie keeps losing control like this. It felt like more people already knew than was necessary. It was like she was one accident away from being discovered by the dragonbloods. And then…

"No, not after what happened. Besides, your brother and Silas must be worried sick about our absence," Mom said firmly.

Callie nodded in acceptance. Nothing could be done about it. She would just have to wait until spring.

Mom had been right about Callen being worried sick. When the rangers dropped them off at the inn, Callen had been pacing near the entrance, large bags under his eyes suggesting that he hadn't slept much.

"Mom, Callie! Where had you two disappeared?" He asked, pulling them both into a tight hug.

"Br-Brother! You're crushing me," Callie choked out.

"Maybe if I crush you, then you wouldn't escape like this with nothing more than a small note," Callen said, pulling on her cheeks. "This has something to do with you, I just know it."

"Ow, ow, ow!" Callie tried to pry herself free, but Callen was too strong. Mom made no effort to help Callie either.

"I see, you have quite the doting older brother. Keep your little sister safe! She needs you," Captain Voss said to Callen.

Callen stopped, regarding the woman suspiciously.

Without saying anything else, the rangers turned to leave.  But then Lysander turned his head, looking at Callie. The other rangers stopped too, looking at him. They shared glances, as if having some silent conversation. Finally, the captain nodded her head.

"Kid, should you ever need help. Seek us out. We'll help you, but in exchange, you will have to become one of us," Lysander said and pulled out a piece of paper from the inner pocket of his shirt.

Callie took it and read it. "Kallithera. Watch where magpies perch." There was also a sort of symbol drawn on it which looked like a magpie sitting on a sword.

"That will never happen!" Mom snapped, trying to reach for the paper, but Callie hurriedly tucked it into her tunic.

"It's just an offer," Captain Voss shrugged and left alongside the other rangers.

"Thank you!" Callie called after them, waving goodbye. The rangers, all as one, raised their hand and extended three of their fingers in a unified gesture. It kind of reminded her of a bird's wing.

"What exactly happened?" Callen grumbled, looking at the retreating back of the rangers. "I thought you hated them."

Mom sighed. "We'll explain at home. It's a very long story… Is Sylas ready to leave?"

"Yeah, he's waiting by the market," Callen nodded. "I swear, I thought we would have to leave without you."

Mom and Callen hurried towards the market, but Callie couldn't help but glance at the rangers for the last time. She really hoped to meet them again one day. Not just this group, either, but other rangers too. People who cared for arcane beasts.

This gave Callie hope. She wasn't alone. If she found other like-minded people and worked together with them, then perhaps they could make the world a kinder place for the arcane beasts.
