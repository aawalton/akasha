Chapter 12: Crossroads

I had been reforged in this training room. In my old life, I had no real need to fight anyone. There were a few scuffles I had, in my youth. A few confrontations that mostly involved yelling, maybe some swearing. The occasional shove in a bar when someone had too much to drink, or a heated argument at a job site when tempers flared under the summer sun. Nothing serious, nothing life-altering.

For the most part, I took pleasure in doing my job. In building houses that I knew memories would be made in. I concerned myself with only my family, and my personal responsibilities. There was a simplicity to that existence that I hadn't appreciated until it was gone. The predictable rhythm of days, the satisfaction of tangible accomplishment, the warmth of returning to those who loved me.

Funny, in a twisted ironic way, it was that very thing that led me down the path I now walked. My devotion to my family, my insistence on protecting them, had brought me here, to this moment. If I had been different, less attached, less concerned with their safety, perhaps none of this would have happened. But then, I wouldn't have been me.

The training room was stark and functional, like all Nyxen spaces. No decoration, no comfort, nothing that didn't serve a purpose. The walls absorbed sound, the floor provided just enough cushion to prevent serious injury from falls but not enough to be comfortable. The lighting was harsh, revealing every detail, leaving nowhere to hide.

Since the start of the nightmare, I had endured profound loss, and I had been beaten, healed, and beaten again. I had been pushed beyond physical limits, given little time to rest, before I was pushed once more. Vash's methods were brutal but effective. Pain was constant, failure expected, improvement demanded. There was no praise for success, only slightly less punishment.

I had drilled ten-chi-jin until my arms trembled, spasming to the point I needed a healing potion to continue. The forms had become second nature now, my body moving through them without conscious thought. Ten, the heavenly cut, descending from above like divine judgment. Chi, the earth cut, rising from below with inexorable force. Jin, the human cut, striking through the middle where vulnerability resided.

All of that, the months of training I had undergone, small moments with my daughter Elisa being the only thing I had to look forward to, had yielded me only a handful of levels. Progress was slow, painful, measured in incremental improvements rather than dramatic leaps. Each level represented countless hours of suffering, of pushing past limitations I hadn't known existed.

Now, I would face the greatest challenge yet.

Zorin stood across from me, his stance relaxed but alert. Unlike Vash, whose inhuman perfection always reminded me of her otherness, Zorin looked entirely human. Average height, broad shoulders, sturdy build. He could have been one of the contractors I worked with back on Earth, unremarkable except for the intensity in his eyes.

This was a man who had likely been through everything I had and then some. A man who knew the culture I found myself in, who had walked with pain for far longer than I had. His training under Vash would have been similar to mine, perhaps even more brutal given her apparent dissatisfaction with him.

His cavernous brown eyes regarded me, within their depths was a living hatred. His motivation was simple. Kill me to maintain his place. I was the usurper, the threat to his position, the reason his master looked at him with contempt. In his place, I might have felt the same.

I would learn that sometimes that was all the motivation one needed. Survival, position, pride—simple things, primal things, often drove people more powerfully than complicated ideologies or lofty goals.

Vash stood at the edge of the training area, watching with that predatory attention I had come to know so well. Her face revealed nothing, but there was a tension in her posture, an eagerness she couldn't entirely conceal. This was entertainment for her, perhaps, or an experiment. Two specimens pitted against each other to see which would prove stronger.

Several other Nyxen had gathered as well, forming a loose circle around the perimeter. Word of the contest had spread, drawing spectators eager to witness the outcome. Some watched with clinical detachment, others with barely concealed bloodlust. None showed sympathy or concern for either combatant.

"The rules are simple. Do not take the fights outside of this room. Survive. Now... begin." Vash's voice was anticipatory, a cold insidious excitement. The words were hardly necessary—the parameters had been clear from the moment she had announced the match. This was to the death, nothing less would satisfy.

Lt. Zorin wasted no time. His resonance blade, a simple rounded hilt, black wrapped handle, and a rounded pommel, sprang into life. It was a burning color like a rotting orange sitting in a pool of vomit. The hue was sickly, unpleasant, yet it hummed with lethal purpose as he brought it to guard position.

He came at me strong, starting with ten. The overhead strike descended with practiced precision, aimed to cleave me from crown to sternum. I blocked the blow, our blades meeting with that distinctive crystalline chime, but Zorin attempted to use his experience against me. Ten became jin, then ten again, followed by jin and chi.

The sequence was fluid, natural, his body moving through the forms with the ease of long familiarity. Where I had learned these cuts over weeks, he had refined them over years. The difference was evident in every movement, every transition.

It was a flurry that he began to enforce with Flux. His mouth was set in a grim line. Determination hardened his features, and I could sense the Flux flowing through him, augmenting his strikes, increasing their speed and power. Not dramatically—he wasn't as strong as Vash—but enough to make each impact jar my arms, enough to make each block more difficult than the last.

I moved, and parried, instantly put on the defensive. My white blade traced luminous arcs in the air as I fended off his attacks, giving ground steadily. The spectators shifted, following our movement, their expressions ranging from contempt to mild interest. I heard murmurs, assessments of my technique, predictions of my imminent defeat.

Tapping into the Flux within me became easier now, all I needed to do was remember the pain. The memories were always there, just beneath the surface, waiting to be called upon. I reached for them now, willingly, using them as Vash had taught me.

Lynn's dead eyes. The light gone from them, the warmth extinguished. The woman who had looked at me with love every day for years, reduced to an empty shell on our bedroom floor.

Jackson's open body. My son, my boy, torn apart by creatures that should not exist. His innocence, his future, his laughter, all ended in a moment of senseless violence.

Their deaths were the door that gave me access to the Flux within me. Power flooded my body. I could feel it spreading through my limbs, heightening my senses, sharpening my reflexes. The world seemed to slow slightly, giving me precious fractions of seconds to react to Zorin's attacks.

"His eyes. Amber-gold. Yes, there it is." I dimly heard Vash mutter. I had no time to wonder what she meant by that. The observation registered but couldn't be processed, not while I was fighting for my life against an opponent who clearly outmatched me in skill if not in raw connection to the Flux.

I attempted a combination of my own, my footwork precise, my strikes flowing. Ten to chi, chi to jin, jin back to ten. The sequence came naturally now, my body remembering what my conscious mind might forget under pressure. My white blade left trails in the air, its crimson core pulsing with each movement.

Zorin was better than I was, of that there was no doubt. He had been at this longer. His defense was tighter, his attacks more varied, his strategy more developed. Where I relied heavily on the basic forms Vash had taught me, he had clearly expanded his repertoire, incorporating techniques I had never seen.

In my life I have heard many talk about talent. Talent was good, necessary even. It covered a lot of gaps. But experience was no small thing. Experience could take even a small talent and turn it into something truly vast. The hours spent practicing, the mistakes made and corrected, the lessons learned through repetition and application—these things could not be replaced by natural aptitude alone.

Zorin hadn't reached that level, but the bottom line was he had more experience. He was better. It was a fact. His superiority was evident in every exchange, every parry, every riposte. I was keeping up, but barely, and only because my connection to the Flux was stronger than his.

He drove me back, his lips twisted in cruel satisfaction, eyes glinting like dark stalactites in a forgotten cavern. He sensed victory approaching, could taste it perhaps. Each successful attack pushed me further toward the edge of the training area, limiting my options, constricting my movement.

His hand whipped out, and I felt a push of Flux against me, pushing me back in a wave of invisible force kissed by the fire of anger. The telekinetic blast was similar to what I had used against him during our first encounter, though less powerful. It struck me square in the chest, driving the air from my lungs and sending me staggering backwards.

I gritted my teeth, trying to drop my weight. Lowering my center of gravity, bracing my feet against the floor, fighting to maintain my position rather than being thrown completely off balance. The pressure continued, a steady force seeking to topple me, to create an opening for a killing blow.

I knew then just how weak Zorin really was.

It wasn't a revelation born of arrogance. I couldn't claim superior skill or technique. But in that moment, as I struggled against his telekinetic onslaught, I understood something fundamental about him, about the Nyxen, about the path I had started down.

The Nyxen like to think themselves a complicated people. They live out the values of the Flux aspects they hold dear. Their culture, their hierarchy, their methods of training, all reflected their devotion to Primal and Void aspects. Pain, anger, shadow, emptiness—these were the currencies they traded in, the foundations they built upon.

Except there was a fundamental problem with that I began to grasp in the moment. It wasn't complete understanding, only its first breath. By cultivating pain, shadow, anger, and the void the way they did, they desensitized themselves to it. They became numb to the very sources of power they claimed to embrace.

How could one truly know pain if they had never known love? How can you truly understand shadow if you scorn the light? What is the void without the loss of life?

True power came not from rejecting one side of existence for another, but from experiencing both, from understanding the full spectrum of being. The Nyxen claimed to draw strength from darkness, but many of them had never truly known light. They spoke of pain but had never experienced joy. They embraced emptiness but had never known fullness.

The Nyxen wanted to know these things, or so they claimed. But they didn't want to actually feel them. Vash, I would learn, had truly set out on the path of mastering these aspects. She had done things I would think were horrific at the time. Necessary, but horrific.

No, they were necessary. For power. For survival. The distinction between horror and necessity blurred when existence itself was at stake.

Zorin was fighting because he didn't want to die. His anger, his pain, were paltry things. Sparks in a campfire that wouldn't catch. He hadn't lost what I had lost, hadn't felt what I had felt. His connection to these aspects was intellectual, cultivated, artificial. Mine was visceral, immediate, real.

Mine? It was an inferno. A meadow set ablaze. My pain was raw, my anger genuine, my understanding of emptiness born from having everything ripped away in a single night. I didn't need to manufacture these feelings as the Nyxen did; I lived them, breathed them, existed within them.

His Flux push was an annoyance, but it did little to me. Because my connection to the Resonance Flux was stronger. Because I had experienced what these aspects of the Flux truly meant. I had not only seen the heart of them, I had felt them. The loss of my family had opened a wound that would never truly heal, a void that could never be filled. But that same wound was the source of a power Zorin could not comprehend.

More than any of that, in that point of time, I was fighting not just for my survival, but for Elisa's. My daughter, the last remnant of the life I had built, the final thread connecting me to who I had been. Her future, her safety, depended on my victory here. Zorin fought for position; I fought for something far more precious.

Something I couldn't name detonated from me. The power wasn't visible. It was pure feeling. It was anger, pain, fear, shadow and the void made manifest, and yet it was more than even that. It transcended simple emotion, simple concept. It was raw will imposed upon reality, the assertion of my existence against all that would deny it.

It was authority.

Zorin froze in place, much like our first confrontation, he was unable to move. His body trembled as a physical weight descended upon him. As he felt my manifested authority. His blade wavered, its sickly orange glow flickering as his concentration faltered. His eyes widened in shock, then narrowed in desperate effort as he fought against the invisible force pinning him in place.

The spectators stirred, whispers rippling through their ranks. This was unexpected, interesting. The new initiate demonstrating an ability typically reserved for those of higher rank, higher connection. Even Vash's expression changed, showing something akin to satisfaction, to vindication.

Here now, was a crossroads.

I could allow Zorin to live. Refuse to kill him. Show mercy, compassion, humanity. Demonstrate that I had not fully embraced the Nyxen way, that I retained some connection to the man I had been. The builder, the father, the husband. The man who created rather than destroyed.

But Vash would see that as weakness. Though I couldn't see it in the moment, she was leaning forward. Her dark eyes flashing with the need to see Zorin die. To see the weakness purged. Her body was actually shaking, her mouth opened in a little 'O.' The anticipation was palpable, a hunger that could only be satisfied with blood.

Elisa would suffer if I didn't kill the man before me. The thought crystallized, providing justification, providing purpose. If I failed here, if I showed weakness, Vash would discard me. And my daughter, what would become of her then? Who would protect her in this cruel new reality?

Ha. It's strange, the lies we tell ourselves.

No, that was an excuse I told myself at the time. A weakness I indulged in. I was doing the right thing. The only thing I could do in the moment. I painted myself as the reluctant killer, the man forced by circumstance to commit acts against his nature. It was comforting, this fiction. It allowed me to maintain some illusion of moral superiority even as I descended further into darkness.

Pathetic.

I made the choice I did because I knew it would lead to power. That was the truth. Power would lead to me never feeling the type of pain that came with the loss of my loved ones. Power would ensure that no one could take from me again, that I would never again be helpless, vulnerable, broken. Power was the only certainty in an uncertain existence.

I moved in a blur, my resonance blade a flashing white with a touch of crimson. The movement was fluid, almost beautiful, a perfect execution of ten. The heavenly cut, descending from above like divine judgment. Zorin saw it coming but could not move, could not defend, could only watch as death approached.

Blood fountained. Flesh seared. The blade cut through armor, skin, muscle, bone, with equal ease. From crown to sternum, a perfect vertical split. The two halves of Zorin's face registered different expressions—shock on one side, resignation on the other—before the life left his eyes completely.

His body crumpled, the orange blade winking out of existence as his connection to the Flux severed. The room fell silent, all eyes on me, on the corpse at my feet, on the white blade still humming in my hand.

Power rushed into me. I could feel it flooding my system, a surge of Flux energy released by Zorin's death. Was this normal, this transfer of power? Or was it something unique to me, to my bloodline? I didn't know, had no context to understand what was happening. I only knew that I felt stronger, more connected, more alive than I had moments before.

I won.

The simplicity of the thought belied the complexity of the moment. Yes, I had won. I had survived. I had secured my position, ensured Elisa's safety, pleased my master. But I had also crossed a line that could never be uncrossed. I had taken a life not in the heat of battle against monsters, not in desperate defense of loved ones, but in cold calculation. A deliberate choice, a step toward a future I could not yet see clearly.

Vash approached, satisfaction evident in every line of her perfect face. She circled Zorin's body once, examining my handiwork with a professional eye, before turning her attention to me.

"Well done, initiate," she said, her voice carrying clearly in the silent room. "You have proven yourself worthy of further investment. Tomorrow we depart for Crucible, where the true test awaits."

The other Nyxen dispersed, their entertainment concluded. Some looked at me with new assessment, others with continued indifference. One or two with something that might have been respect, or at least acknowledgment of potential.

I deactivated my blade, the white energy dissipating like mist in morning sun. My hand shook slightly, not from exertion but from the enormity of what I had done. The path forward was clear now, unavoidable. I had made my choice, taken my step, committed to this journey.

As I left the training room, I caught a glimpse of my reflection in one of the polished panels. Amber-gold eyes stared back at me, a stranger's eyes in a familiar face. The eyes of a killer, of a Nyxen, of something I had never intended to become.

I turned away, focusing instead on the thought of seeing Elisa. Her innocent face, her trust, her love. The reason for everything, the justification for anything. The lie I told myself, and the truth I clung to.

The crossroads was behind me now. All that remained was the path I had chosen, stretching into a future I could not predict but would shape with my own hands, my own will, my own power.

No matter the cost.
