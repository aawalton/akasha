"Don't engage with the Doorkeeper," Fay said. "We state why we are here, we accept the time restraints and we continue on. Nothing more."

"I know," Alex said. "You told me earlier. Don't trust the Doorkeeper, don't say more than you need to, don't ask questions, don't engage. We're allowed to go visit them on Holding business, we aren't trying to do anything wrong."

Fay sighed before nodding. Alex wasn't wrong, they were allowed, even expected, to present themselves today to be allowed access to Scattered Creek to speak to Anya and Seb. The fact that every visit meant another encounter with the Doorkeeper, another interaction with it and another chance for something unplanned to occur was unfortunate. She wouldn't let it stop her though, they were family.

"Here on business or pleasure?" the thing asked as they entered the schoolhouse courtyard.

"Business," she said. "Holding Syl Seer and Arbiter for the monthly conference."

"Seer. A month ago you were here as family were you not? A repeat visitor so soon is unusual. Especially when the family tie is assumed and the Holders are junior."

She tried not to grit her teeth. Of course it remembered her, it likely remembered every Awakened to have ever crossed its path.

"If you remember me then you are aware of who I am, what my background is. You know I am authorized."

"Yes, yes. Authorized, entitled, permitted and granted passage. Both of you for three hours, not a second more or you'll be looked for."

"Thank you," Alex said.

"Don't thank me little Arbiter. The door is open, your access is permitted. I greet you not, merely send you on your way."

"You don't greet-"

"Alex!" Fay hissed and pointed to the school door that was the portal onward. "The timer has started."

Seb nudged Anya as Fay and Alex came over. She hadn't been sleeping, not really, and neither had he. They'd been relaxing, taking advantage of the moment to settle after their morning drills and time had gotten a bit ahead of them.

"Lady Anya, Lord Seb," Alex said, pausing a few steps from their table.

"Arbiter Alex," Anya said evenly. "Are we back to formalities so soon?"

"It has been months, most of a year if you include your time away following the wedding."

"Fay," Seb said. "Don't tell me you stopped having breakfasts and everyone is going to be all Lord this and Lady that."

"We still have breakfast, it isn't the same though. Sandra spends all her time complaining because no one puts the bacon in the right spot for her to steal."

"I didn't think she'd caught on," Anya said with a laugh.

"I think it never being there anymore finally made her realize," Alex said. "She's right though, it isn't the same, even if it is enjoyable."

"Everything else?" Seb asked.

"As expected," Fay said. "No surprises since I was last here, no change of plans needed. We aren't rushing anything."

"George is hoping you'll approve some directly affiliated or retained Divers," Alex said.

"She hasn't said anything," Fay said.

"Not directly. She's made mention of thinking of Diving again, but wouldn't want to with a group she didn't trust and know. Which means an established team that could benefit from her, and that will work around Holding requirements."

"I hadn't considered it in that light. We haven't opened up anything past level 9 yet though."

Seb felt Anya's unspoken question and let her feel his agreement. It made sense even if they hadn't actually discussed it. When they got back they'd make whatever was needed work. No reason to apply restrictions that didn't have any benefit.

"We aren't using it." Anya said. "If the Administration can handle it and George thinks they can handle the healing needs."

"Very well, should I let your mother know?" Fay asked.

"She's still looking?"

"From what Sandra grumbles she's being picky. Too picky."

"Go ahead," Anya said, her tone drooping slightly.

Seb flicked her side, her surprise spiking through the band and jolting him back from the edge of joining her in a yawn.

"They give you enough time to spar?" Fay asked playfully.

"Fay!" Anya said as she jolted upright.

Seb shook his head, not bothering to pretend he wasn't flushing slightly at her question. What was the point, Fay had realized long ago what they got up to, had told them outright that they weren't fooling her. If Alex hadn't figured it out, well he would at some point.

"Lord Percivus is training us," Seb said. "To the point of exhaustion is his idea of a warm up."

"It's barely past breakfast," Alex said.

"Two hours past. Three hours of regular lessons after lunch, three more hours of training, then dinner. Possibly more training after dinner, we won't know until then."

"Only half a day of training on the off day," Anya added. "The other six use up every hour."

Seb could see their surprise, could see that Alex had questions, suspected Fay was concerned. This wasn't like anything they'd described to her a month ago. This wasn't anything like what he'd considered a month ago.

"Lots of progress," he said. "He knows how to push, to find your limits and show you they aren't where you thought."

"He knocked us both out with Bladesong," Anya added. "One moment we were focused, the next the only thing I knew was that Seb existed somewhere near me."

"That's…" Fay began.

"More than I realized it could do," Seb finished. "More than I suspect I'll be able to anytime soon."

It had been an interesting experience. Seb still wasn't sure he could explain how it had happened, other than by Percivus being significantly more advanced than them. Which was obvious, but even then, the song hadn't disoriented him, hadn't made things easier or harder, it had struck the way a technique might, a blow that had no tangible form.

Focus, Anya sent.

I am.

On the world, not Bladesong.

"You're both exhausted," Fay said.

"We'll manage," Anya said. "We aren't relying on the meld yet."

"You…"

"We aren't losing ourselves," Seb said. "We aren't getting distracted the way we did that time. We're in sync, we're working together, stronger together."

"Be careful," Fay said. "Please?"

"We are." "Of course."

They both meant both answers, they had given both because they both applied. It wasn't a question of being careful, of taking risks. They were growing stronger, they'd deal with whatever came at them. Just because Anya was the one that challenged everything didn't mean Seb didn't feel just as driven. Just because he was the one who planned things, who looked for the patterns and flows that let them succeed, didn't mean Anya never saw the path they needed to follow.

It didn't matter for now though. They had time with their family, time they were supposed to spend planning and talking about Holding business. But nothing said they had to. They'd discussed what they needed, made the plans they needed. Alex and Fay would handle anything that came up, would make the decisions that needed to be made, delay those that could wait.

Three hours of business? They had needed thirty minutes. The rest of the time was spent relaxing formalities, finding the balance they'd started to lose.

"If you're going to doze off at least lay your heads down," Alex said. "Your blank stares are weird."

"Your watching us stare at you is weird," Anya said.

"A retainer should be attentive."

Less or more? Anya sent.

More, he sent back, chuckling at the mental image Anya had shared.

"Fay, make sure he gets extra bacon for his impudence."
