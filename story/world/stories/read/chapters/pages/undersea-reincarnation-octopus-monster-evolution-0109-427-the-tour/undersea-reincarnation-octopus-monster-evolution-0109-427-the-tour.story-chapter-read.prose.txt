Understandably, Velistraine passed out as soon as Henry made some cover for them and sealed out the exit. It hadn’t been easy. The mysterious matter on which she was now snoring had a strange habit of changing depending on who looked at it, which wasn’t something Henry was capable of mimicking. So he just opted to go for a dark, white-dotted camouflage that should help him blend a little with the background and well… he would keep an eye out. Or more accurately, a few hundreds of them.

“[Hey Henry? Do you think I’ll be able to consume my Merfolk fragment here? It feels… risky? You know? Velistraine made it seem like we really shouldn’t be found out here.]”

With six of his Octominds on meditation duty to replenish the prodigious amounts of mana and lifeblood he was expending on creating his Avatars, Henry had been considering that exact same issue as he popped the projections one after the other. By now, he was mostly creating Arcane ones. Arcane Avatars could handle the lack of air and cold just fine, while the flesh and blood ones struggled after thirty minutes or so. “[We’ll have to figure out something,]” he said after a beat. “[I’ve got enough to possibly upgrade my first Aspect to A-rank, and that’s probably noisy. I think… the best might be to raise hell somewhere else and just go for it. Or, we risk floating out in space and do it there. I’ve got enough water to last us for a bit, and we’d be able to hide a little better out there. But I worry we’d be sitting ducks. Let me try something first…]”

Henry handed Maurice to the tiny Octomind still working on Velistraine’s wound—which had now healed all the way up to her wrist—and took a deep breath. Henry took a second to arrange some of his clones in a line, just in case he failed to return to their hiding spot, then switched with the furthest Arcane clone he had.

Immediately, the frigid air of space made him shiver. Henry sharply inhaled, but there was nothing there to inhale.

Okay, this is unpleasant, he thought, limbs shivering. The thin layer of moisture on his arms immediately turned to ice, and he could feel how the dry air and the cold were trying to do the same to the deeper tissues, but his own cells refused to allow the foreign force in. With him being close to 180 points in Constitution, Henry could handle the vacuum of space decently well, but that didn’t mean this wasn’t a challenge.

Because if water was freezing this fast, how were they supposed to breathe any of it?

Well, that’s the next problem to deal with. First, I gotta find my way back to the road, he mused, sensing the Avatars he’d spread all around. He could still feel and see through the eyes of the Apostle he’d left with Maurice, but as expected, he couldn’t see the road anymore. It was gone. All around him, from where he was floating, there was nothing but the emptiness of space. Naturally, the hundred Avatars he’d created so far were invisible, but he could sense them all perfectly well. He could sense the Arcane Avatar he’d left in the hidden spot, so Henry once more activated his skill and attempted to switch places with it.

The soft snoring reached his ears once more, and Maurice jumped a little, his eyes roving over the quickly melting ice that coated Henry.

“[The good news is that we can hide out there, which should make not revealing our little base easy. The bad news is it’s cold as hell. Got any ideas?]”

***

With Velistraine still conked out of her gourd, Henry and Maurice explored and tested new ways to sustain their exploration of space, which, so far, wasn’t going so well.

“[Should have fought something that makes fire,]” Henry grumbled, annoyed at the damn cold that kept turning the water meant to allow them to breathe into ice. The cold didn’t care about his Control Water skill, and could bypass Trickster’s Domain as if it wasn’t even there. Of course, there were plenty more options to test and Henry knew he’d get one to work eventually, but right now, he was a little peeved at the weakness he’d just discovered. He had never really struggled, neither in water nor land. He could traverse and survive the environments he’d dealt with just fine. But this was a new one.

As soon as he got back, he was getting himself some skills to compensate. Something for fire. Something for light—even if he could see just fine in the dark.

“[We fought those lava ants, remember?]” Maurice chimed in, looking up from his own attempts. Unlike Henry, Maurice could apparently store some air in the back of his shell, so he could last for quite a few hours without oxygen, but it wasn’t infinite either.

Henry blinked, recalled those ants and the crystalline towers under which they hid. He’d sort of forgotten about them. Then again, Henry had only gotten fire resistance from those. Not a way to create his own, nor a way to control heat. Henry and Maurice were floating in the emptiness of space, trying one thing after the other. At first, he played around with changing the nature of his skin, turning it to bone or mimicking the property of stone. The bone did somewhat well in staving off the cold, but they still couldn’t breathe. Still, that gave him an idea that he was pretty sure would work. It just would need some effort.

“[Break for air,]” he told Maurice, then he brought them back next to Velistraine.

Outside, Henry pulled two clones aside and sent them far, as far as his range could allow, which could now be counted in miles. He sent one Lifeblood clone, and one Arcane clone, and once they were far enough, the Lifeblood made itself invisible, then it started inflating and as it did, Henry played a little with its shape. First, most of the hundreds of tons of mass went into its arms, which Henry lengthened, then flattened. Down their length, Henry shaped a spine-like structure, which he then connected all throughout the arms with rib-like bones, and as the minutes went by, the Lifeblood Avatar slowly took the form of a giant, hot-air balloon. Well. At least, it should look like it.

It was still invisible.

Henry continued working, pulling a couple of his Octominds to help as he altered things here and there, making improvements as he thought of them, and within an hour or so, he was done, and with Maurice in tow, he teleported them back, switching places with the Arcane Avatar he had left next to the improvised SS Henry, and before Blinking within the chamber hidden below the bone-encased head of the Lifeblood Avatar, he attached a handful of Arcane Avatars on the bony surface, to keep an eye out for trouble.

Then he Blinked inside and immediately, a chuckle escaped his throat at the wide-eyed stare Maurice was giving him.

“[Let me give you the tour,]” Henry said, grinning as he let saltwater pour out of his Maw to fill up the space. With how much mass he could create, the sphere encased between his Avatar’s arms was nearly two hundred yards in diameter. The fleshy insides were lined with luminescent organs, something simple he’d picked up from the electric krakens he’d fought in the past, which made the space almost look like a starry sky. Henry made sure to also create pockets of air between the flesh and bone separating them from the outside, both for insulation and to prevent leakage. Henry and Maurice floated about, moving away from the collecting water which Henry had moved using Control Water, as there was no gravity to collect it below.

Maurice tried to say something, but there was no air around still, just water and vacuum. “[This is great! How long do you think we can stay here? What about discovery? Could they detect us?]”

Henry hummed, quickly eyeing the volume of water still pouring out of his Maw. It was a good thing he’d stocked up on water, though filling this space up might stretch his reserve. He’d have to pack some of the water away after. “[For how long we can stay here, I’d say a while, especially if we make ourselves smaller. For discovery, the Avatar we’re within is actively cloaking its signature, and the Arcane ones are keeping an eye out. We’re also nearly ten miles away from the—road-thingy, and I’m in the process of making some more spots like this to hide in. I just gotta make sure we have multiple relays leading us back to the road so we don’t end up stranded.]”

“[So… safe enough to get started?]”

Henry grinned, noting that the water had finally expanded out and began touching the Avatar’s beak, which allowed it to take a deep breath. “[Safe enough to get started,]” Henry said, taking a moment to Shapeshift a flesh tunnel to channel the water out of the Avatar’s gills and back into the chamber they were all using to breathe. “[You go first, and I’ll wait and keep an eye on things,]” he said, and Maurice didn’t hesitate. The energies around the crab swelled as he consumed the Merfolk’s core, and within moments, the cocoon around Maurice formed. With the crab’s evolution under-way, Henry returned to his meditation and to his ever-growing swarm, spreading them further and further up and down and around the bridge.

It took less than an hour for him to find another sign of life.
