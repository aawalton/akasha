Half the settlement had survived the mythos attack. The other half had been razed to unrecognizable pieces of wood and mortar.

Kalsin slowly walked down a lane that once had narrowly divided a pair of buildings—both now piles that barely reached his waist. His eyes tracked the destruction with a familiar detachment that almost made him forget his sore arms. The beast's size had been exceptionally large. That one was obvious. Kalsin crouched, trailing a deep furrow in the smooth surface of a door half covered by debris. Claws. He could see similar marks here and there, could almost envision the mythos barreling down the road in blind rage. If he were to put money on its level of intelligence, he'd bet on bestial. Primal and instinctive and a step below having rational thought. Size or physical strength aside, a smart being would not risk alarming such a large portion of the human populace without good reason.

"Ulfhénn? Maybe. Maybe-maybe-maybe. Thought it might be an ahkinni or ala at first, but that wouldn't explain the claws..." Kalsin muttered.

He looked around then, the distance between him and Cinder washing over him like a bucket of cold water. He breathed in deeply. His sister wasn't here to give him the rundown, and he didn't hunt mythos for a living anymore. So why did he sneak away from hoarding rubble for a chance to investigate the wreckage by his lonesome?

The recruits had quickly, though not without complaint, started assisting the locals after the lieutenant had dropped the orders in their lap. They helped the settlers. One look at the dirty faces and blue-beaten bruises, and they would've helped even without the orders. The strongest recruits cleared the old and ruined, giving ground for the new and about-to-be. The smartest organized and handled the crowds with efficient hands. Others talked to the civilians, asked them where to take a load off their shoulders, and proceeded to do just that. Many tears had been shed by the locals, but borderlands-living made for people accustomed to hardships and setbacks. There was nothing to be done but turn a new side of the blade and hammer on.

The recruits helped, and the settlers showed gratitude in return. Gratitude, which Kalsin was intent on cashing in.

Now, there was no malice in these particular plans. It was a universal truth that people needed to unwind from time to time, said need constantly being multiplied by trudging through shit and misfortune. Kalsin figured both recruits and civilians had been excessively squeezed through the grinder as of late, so what better way to pump back a little color into their lives than with some festive drinking and music during the sunset? Carrying planks under his arms, stacking carts in the company of sweating men and women, Kalsin had chatted up the locals. In just a couple of hours he'd pushed past barriers and wormed his way into their confidence. It wasn't all that difficult, and again—his intentions were good and clear.

That's how a mutually beneficial deal had been struck; the recruits would give the strength of their arms to help with the village, and in return the locals would bring out tables, cork ale bottles, and try to put loss and destruction behind them for a single evening. Needless to say, the deal quickly found popularity among both groups.

So why the hell was Kalsin out here, walking through silent and abandoned lanes when they still had a good hour of work before dusk? Force of habit, nostalgia, or plain stupidity? He wished he knew.

Kalsin stopped and wrapped his arms around himself as a gust of cold wind passed through him.

"How do you do it, Jack? How are you so… assured?"

He remained like that, eyes glazing over until everything appeared as a blur or colors. He knew there was plenty to do, yet for some reason it felt like there was nothing to do. Kalsin knew for a fact he wasn't lazy. He'd swam through deeper and worse smelling sewage in the past. Even before his time as a hunter, he could follow orders just fine. For as long as he remembered he could always set a goal and push towards it with everything he had. Something grounded, something both selfish and selfless enough to be real.

And despite all that, lately he'd been feeling... gray.

A crunch of breaking wood came from his right, and Kalsin turned to see Ringtail walk toward him with a hostile scowl on her face. Kalsin cursed his luck of getting found, then cursed it twice for the person who found him.

"Why aren't you doing your job?" she nailed him with the question. He already knew she wasn't one for preamble.

"Why are you looking after me like it's your job?" Kalsin asked back, his arms already having shifted to a defiant fold from the vulnerable grasp.

Ringtail opened then closed her mouth, almost as if Kalsin's question was something she really didn’t want to confront, but a sneer quickly snuck in its place. "I already knew you were an insensitive pain in the ass, but an asshole who shirks his duty when it's needed? That's a new."

Kalsin laughed with disbelief. "Shirk my... look, there's a whole ocean of things you can do in your free time. I’ve got some coal and paper I can lend you, or ask Shoreline, she has a neat fishing set on hand. People call these things hobbies. Heard about them? Because stalking, for one, is fucking weird, and for two, it's not sustainable in the long term."

"Stop. Deflecting. Doesn't work on me during a fight, not gonna work here."

"Piss off."

Her palms formed a fist at her sides. "Of course. Would've been too good to be true. Ghost comes up with post-disaster party plans, and just when we're thinking, hey, he's not completely useless after all, he goes and walks away when the work actually needs doing."

"I was just taking a break," Kalsin said. He shouldered past Ringtail to rejoin the others and salvage what little he could. Anything but to endure this whining for a second longer.

"Don't you turn away from me!" she yelled at his back. "A break? A break? There's a boy who has not seen twelve summers yet, his fingers crushed, and with nobody to sit at his bedside!"

"And I'm going there now, ‘right?" he growled. "Thanks for looking out for me. Now, do me one more favor and shut the fuck up."

She didn't follow him, and that was that. At least, Kalsin thought so.

He'd barely been cleaning a basket of dirty clothes for more than five minutes when Littleguy approached him, saying Brownweather wanted to see him. Kalsin stood without a word and, after politely refusing the offer to be escorted, made his way toward one of the administrative buildings still standing.

He had no illusions about the topic of his summons, though it came as a mouthful of bitter surprise. He'd misstepped, gotten caught, and been snitched on. In hindsight, maybe he shouldn't have antagonized Ringtail as much as he did, but what point is striking up a fire in a snowstorm? He'd tried the patient approach with her in the beginning, and after all this time, she only managed to chew down his fuse until he would explode from the smallest of provocations.

By the time Kalsin found his way and pulled the door open, he reigned his emotions in order. There was nothing left but to hope the lieutenant was in a good mood.

The building wasn't large as far as offices were concerned, but a lack of furnishing and even bigger lack of personnel gave it an airy quality. Brownweather was seated behind an empty desk, steel-booted legs resting on the wooden surface that, probably not so long ago, had been the fine home of stacked paperwork. The scattered documents on the floor supported that claim with enough evidence. Unfortunately for Kalsin, the legal proceeding he was about to witness wouldn't be the one labeled ‘smelly underlimbs versus inked-down headaches'.

Ringtail stood in one corner, rigid as a pole and clearly not happy to be in present company—the loathing for their lieutenant was universal among the recruits. Her unblinking gaze found Kalsin the moment he stepped in, and her expression was about as apologetic as he would've expected; not at all. Kalsin raised an imaginary hat in her direction. Absolutely shameless, that one.

"For the love of—" Brownweather exclaimed, flinching. "Tűzmother's shiny arse, Ghosty, each time you make an entrance it's like a one man freak carnival came to town. Cover up or somethin'."

"Soon as I'm able to, sir. I hear the Veiled are bringing the trend westward."

"Ain't that the truth," Brownweather muttered, his attention having returned to a weathered piece of paper. He read in silence for nearly half a minute. "Nice of you to join us!" he said, looking up suddenly. He gave the paper a shake, which had the unintended effect of a few haphazard documents on the floor taking a brief flight. "This here rot-puke is a list of property damages the encampment suffered, and this," he glared down at the foot of his chair. "Eeeh, fucker's around here somewhere. Anyways, there's another list detailing the human damages caused by the mythos attack. Right? Right. Now, both lists are longer than a row of footmen who ate rancid soup the day before, 'front of a single latrine in a mile radius. And it's starting to smell like shit real quick in here."

Kalsin gave a small nod of acknowledgement, but remained silent otherwise.

"Ghosty!" Kalsin nearly jumped at Brownweather's screech. "I hear you've gone sightseeing! How was it?"

Kalsin didn't spare a glance at Ringtail, who could've made a falcon proud with her unashamed stare. "It was my mistake, sir," he said. He didn't have to fake the regretful tone of his voice. "I took a breather and lost track of time. It won't happen again."

"Mhm," Brownweather leaned back in his chair, massaging his temples. He spoke after a moment. "Haah. I really, honestly, not in the minusculest fuckingly care about this whole debacle." He waved at Ringtail with his left, though still addressing the roof. "But since this one reported insubordination... or wait, was it insubordination? Did I give explicit orders to—aw, who the shit cares. The point I'm trying to make is that, as long as I'm a leading officer and you're a bottom-feeder, you do exactly what I tell you to do. Or what I insinuate you to do. Understand me?"

"I understand you."

"In the army's chain of command there's no hidey-holes, no sneaky clauses written in tiny letters. You do what I say. And by you," he pointed at him, then drew a circle in the air, "I mean you as a collective, the whole lot. Funny thing is, if your mates also went along with your little break, there wouldn't have been any trouble. Did I specify a timeframe to finish? Don't think I did. The issue is that you, Ghosty, you stepped out of line. Everyone else is working like good little ants, and you, a singular you, is now an exception. Can't have that."

Kalsin spoke up, staring straight ahead. "Sir, we should have the work done by nightfall. Most of it, at least. I assume we're expected to assist only with the most pressing tasks, before we continue onward."

To Kalsin's surprise, Brownweather smiled at him. "Glad to hear!" Of course, it would've been too good to be true to leave it at that. "But I'm afraid you're still fucked lucked. What you did was insignificant, but still. Examples, you see. Gotta make them when you can."

The table creaked as Brownweather dragged his boots on the floor, adjusting in his seat so he could rummage through the pile around him somewhat effectively. Seconds trickled by, nothing but the sound of paper sliding on wood. He annoyedly swiped at a paper having flown in his face, then gave up with a curse. "It's not—fine. I faintly remember reading that report about human casualties? Pretty sure some people died, so, we're gonna need graves to bury the dead. You'll start on the digging."

"I, uh, believe we already took care of that, sir," Kalsin said.

Brownweather met Kalsin's eyes. "That's the neat thing about graves. There's never going to be a shortage of people dying. Working ahead of schedule has never been easier, hm?"

Kalsin nodded. Gravedigging shouldn't be an issue. He had a limited amount of practice in the past, though he had dug them to get rid of mythos corpses instead of human ones. "Just one?"

"Fuck if I know," Brownweather said. "Eh. I'd be surprised if you manage even that many before midnight."

From the corner of his eyes Kalsin saw Ringtail follow the conversation back and forth, chewing on the inside of her mouth. He couldn't decipher from her expression if she was happy with the outcome or not.

"Oh, and Ghosty," Brownweather said, once again leaning back in his chair, eyes closed as if getting ready for a nap. "Use your hands."

Kalsin's mouth popped open to protest, but a sharp 'dismissed' cut his breath before he could say a single word. He stared at the lieutenant, mind reeling. He made to speak again, but only empty air came when he realized he didn't know what to say. How the hell does one reason with an unreasonable request?

Then he realized—that was the whole point. This was punishment for punishment's sake.

But unlike flogging or beating—both of which would've required Brownweather to lift his lazy ass out from that chair—this punishment would instead be self-inflicted. Fingers against earth. See which one gave out quicker.

Kalsin looked away from the lieutenant. Spineless, sadistic fuck.

He saw from the corner of his eyes Ringtail take a startled step forward with a horrified expression, but Kalsin didn’t waste time on her as he strode out. She got what she wanted, and he...

He had a hole to dig.
