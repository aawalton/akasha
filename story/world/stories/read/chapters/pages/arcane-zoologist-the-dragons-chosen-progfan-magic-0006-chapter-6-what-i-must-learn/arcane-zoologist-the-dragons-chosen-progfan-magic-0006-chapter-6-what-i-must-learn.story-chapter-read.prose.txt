Callie hadn't met Mister Blackthorn many times. In fact, it couldn't have been more than five, but each time had left an unpleasant but lasting impression on her.

The first time, she was still very small, no older than five. She couldn't remember exactly what was going on during that day, except that she was left alone with her brother. They were going to the smithy to meet Dad after work when the hunters returned to the village while carrying Mister Blackthorn on a makeshift stretcher. His leg had been so terribly mangled that Callie began crying from fright.

Next time she met him two years later. Callen had been late returning from his practice, so Callie had gone to fetch him home. When she came onto the training grounds, her brother was battered and bruised, the old man staring down at him with a cold gaze, wooden training sword in hand.

Callie had once again started crying, inserting herself between the man and her brother, demanding that he stop hurting him. For some reason, Callen was apologizing profusely to him afterwards and dragging Callie home, saying that she couldn't act that way with his teacher.

Frankly, she was still worried whenever Callen left for his training, imagining all sorts of gruesome scenarios her precious brother could be put through. Even Callen's reassurances that he was completely fine did little to calm Callie down. He often came back from the training bruised and utterly exhausted. There was no way that was a good thing.

But thinking that she herself would have to be taught by this horrible man, made Callie shudder.

"M-Mom, can't you teach me?" Callie pleaded.

"I would if I had the time, but you know that I don't. I have to leave for another hunt tomorrow. There have been sightings of a glauxis nearby," Mom said.

"NO! Are you going to kill it?!" Callie shrieked in horror.

"Callie, I've told you this many times. It is the job of hunters to keep monsters away from the village. But it is not yet confirmed. It's more likely to be a regular lynx," Mom said.

"Arcane beasts!" Callie corrected. "Are you going to kill the lynx then?"

"Callie!" Mom growled.

Callie flinched back. This low tone was a warning from Mom. Drop the topic, or I will get mad. So Callie decided to drop the topic—for now—and turned to Dad instead. "Dad, could you teach me?"

"Callie, you know that there's never a shortage of work at the smithy. I simply don't have the time," Dad said, looking apologetic. "I promise, Mister Blackthorn is a good teacher. Callen has been learning with him for years."

"It'll be just fine, Callie. Mister Blackthorn would never force you to do more than you are able to," Callen said.

Callie narrowed her eyes. "Then why do you always return home with bruises?"

"It's part of the training," Called said, but he certainly looked more nervous than before.

"Will I have bruises too? Will he hit me?" Callie allowed her fears to flow freely on her tongue.

"Heavens, no!" Mom said, shaking her head. "You're going there for survival training. The training Callen receives is completely different."

That did calm Callie down. Only a little bit. She still feared the man and would much rather receive training from anyone else in the village. Except the village chief. He was even scarier.

"Okay…" She finally conceded. "If it means that I can go to the town with you next time you go."

"We'll see if you'll be ready by then. It's not that easy. I would be proud if you were ready by next spring," Mom said with a smile.

"Next spring?! No way. That's way too long," Callie said. She wanted to understand magic as soon as possible. And learn more about dragons. Waiting until next spring felt like an eternity.

"Maybe if you try hard you'll manage by late autumn, before snow settles down. Because once it starts snowing, any travel is out of the question," Dad said sternly.

"That's true," Mom nodded sagely.

"I'll learn everything I need to know before autumn!" Callie said resolutely.

Mom chuckled. "If you're so determined, then very well. I'll try to arrange for you to train with Mister Blackthorn three times a week. In the afternoons."

"What about my apprenticeship?" Callie asked.

"I'll inform Granny Ethel. I'm sure she can spare you three times a week," Mom said.

Callie wanted to protest, but stopped herself. Granny Ethel would understand her need for books. Besides, she could make it up to her by working harder other days.

"Okay!" Callie said, clenching her little fists. "Watch me learn survival faster than anyone ever before."

After dinner, Callie returned to her room. She was feeling nervous about her lessons with Mister Blackthorn, despite everyone's reassurance. Hopefully, everyone was right, and it was nothing more than unwarranted fear.

To ease her thoughts, Callie grabbed one of the blank pages and began drawing. After all, she had the most wonderful encounter earlier that day. While humming, Callie drew the mireling and wrote down its encyclopedia entry.

Name: Mireling

Location:

Flower meadow

Appearance:

Small ball of green slime with a red core

Abilities:

Dissolve with slime

Notes:

Loves eating flowers

Once done, she smiled proudly at the entry and pulled out the two pages that she had hidden underneath her bed. Looking at them side by side made Callie swell with pride. They looked really good.

"But Granny Ethel's encyclopedia had longer entries…" she murmured, seeing how little she actually knew about the creatures.

Perhaps, she could learn something more about them. Meeting the dragon and the glimmerhare would probably be difficult, but mirelings weren't uncommon, just incredibly shy. With the right approach, she might be able to learn more about them.

With trembling legs, Callie walked towards the training grounds which were located not too far from the healer's hut. Her mother had arranged everything first thing in the morning, right before leaving for her hunt.

It was good. Or so Callie tried to convince herself. After all, the sooner she started, the sooner she would be able to go to the library and understand the magic she received from the dragon. But going to Mister Blackthorn still terrified her.

As she arrived at the training grounds, she saw that the old hunter was far from her only worry. Other village girls were there. The very same that had been badmouthing her the day before. What were they even doing here?

"What is she doing here?" The oldest girl, Aurelia, snorted, throwing her long, black hair behind her back.

Callie found the gesture utterly annoying, but apparently Aurelia believed that being the village chief's daughter made her the most important person in the village. In reality, she was nothing more than a bully, thinking herself better than others.

"Calluna is going to be learning survival skills along with everyone else here," Mister Blackthorn said.

Callie nearly yelped when she saw the man hobbling closer to her, leaning heavily on his wooden cane. He was a large man, larger even than Dad, and most of his hair was gone, except for a few gray hairs on the side of his head, hanging on for dear life. His black eyes bore into Callie, his lips pursed into a frown.

Had she already done something wrong for him to look so angry?

"Her?! She's not even ten," Aurelia objected.

"It was her mother's request. Besides, one is never too young to learn survival skills. Come here, Calluna." Mister Blackthorn beckoned her to stand next to the other girls.

"Y-Yes," Callie managed to squeak out, her voice sounding way too shrill in her ears.

She wanted to be anywhere else but here. If it wasn't for the promise of books, she would have likely already darted away. But she wanted to know more about magic, even if it… even if it meant dealing with Aurelia and the other four village girls.

Aurelia shot her a nasty glare, and Callie stopped some distance away, not daring to step too close.

"Let's begin today's lesson. Since Callie has joined us, we'll go over the basics," Mister Blackthorn said.

The village girls groaned.

"Why do we have to start everything anew because of Calluna?" Aurelia protested.

"Think of it as a test. If you do everything correctly, then I'll allow you to go home for the day," the man said sternly. At this, the girls settled down. "Follow me."

Mister Blackthorn hobbled into the hut right next to the training grounds. Callie had never been there, so she looked around with interest. It looked mostly like a storage room with wooden weapons and leather armor on the side and several tables, which had a large variety of items laid on them.

Callie looked at them. They appeared completely random, and most didn't seem to be in any way related to physical training. Like a bundle of tinder, wool cloak, cooking pot, boots, blankets, among other things which in her mind made more sense—pocket knife, hatchet, and a small sword.

"I would like you all to pack a bag for travelling. Put in the items that you think are crucial. Leave behind anything you think you can do without. Go!" Mister Blackthorn said, and at once the other girls jumped to the task, grabbing the bags set down on the ground and sorting through items.

Callie blinked at the scene for a moment. Wasn't he going to explain anything more? She looked at the man, who just stared coldly at her. Afraid he would get mad if she didn't move, Callie hurriedly grabbed the bag, and began stuffing things she thought were useful.

Let me think… What would Mom take along on a hunt?

While she hated Mom going on hunts more than anything, Callie had seen her pack her bag more than a few times. She grabbed flint and the bundle of timber first. That was needed for making fire. Waterskin was a must. Strip of clean linen and millefolia leaves in case of injuries. Wooden cup, some dried meat, and a wool cloak.

Some items confused Callie. There was a pot which could be used for boiling water, but it was very large. It would take up too much space in the bag. The fishing line was probably useful, but Callie didn't know how to fish. The jar seemed useful for collecting stuff, but realistically who would carry a jar around with them when travelling?

In the end, she had been the last one to pack her bag, earning a fair few glares from the other girls. Thankfully, they didn't dare to say anything with Mister Blackthorn present, but who knew what they would do once they caught Callie alone again? She shuddered involuntarily at the thought.

Then Mister Blackthorn checked all the bags in turn, not saying much unless he found a mistake. One of the girls had packed the large pot, which turned out to be the wrong answer, as it wasn't essential. Aurelia's bag was apparently perfect, but he didn't express any joy about the accomplishment, beyond a brisk "good."

When the man walked up to Callie, she was scared. Just from seeing Aurelia's bag, she already knew she was missing a few crucial items. She watched with batted breath as he lifted each item.

"Nothing unnecessary, but you're missing some crucial things—knife and coiled twine," he said sternly.

Callie didn't dare to utter a word; his stare was so intense. She just nodded meekly.

"You're lucky Teacher Blackthorn is not sending us into the forest today. You wouldn't last until the morning," Aurelia said condescendingly.

Callie was horrified that they would be sent into the forest at night. Would Mister Blackthorn really do that? No, it must be one of Aurelia's many taunts. Mom would never let Callie stay in the woods at night. Not after what happened.

Mister Blackthorn shot Aurelia a silencing glare and began a lengthy lecture on why each item was crucial for survival. For some reason, the four girls were glaring daggers at her the whole time as if it had been her fault.

As soon as the lesson was over, Callie darted away straight to the flower meadow. She didn't want to risk running into Aurelia and the other girls on the way home. They were eyeing her with far too much anger for them to just let her pass by peacefully.

The girls had caught her on the way home before. They were faster than Callie after all. But by going to the meadow first, and staying there for a while, she would avoid them for sure. Dad might be a bit mad, but she could just say that the lesson with Mister Blackthorn took a long time.

There was another reason Callie wanted to go to the flower meadow. She was hoping to see the mireling again and learn more about it. It was an arcane beast, but what sort of magic could it do? She didn't know, but she desperately wanted to figure that out.

Unfortunately, when she arrived at the meadow, it was empty, save for bees eagerly buzzing around to collect flower nectar. With a sigh, she sat down and took in the peaceful sight, waiting for when it would be safe to go home.

The sun was still heating the air rather significantly, despite the evening hour, and Callie couldn't help but fold up the sleeves of her tunic. There was nobody around anyway, so she didn't have to worry about anyone discovering her mark.

Sitting in the warm sun, she dozed for a while. It was far too comfortable, and the pleasant chatter of birds lulled her to sleep. She woke up a short while later, after hearing some rustling nearby. Realizing that somebody might have found her, she hurried rolled down her sleeve.

But it wasn't a person. About a hand's length away, a mireling was gently wobbling closer to her.
