Skating through the forest on a trail of wood that crumbled to sawdust in its wake was a Pinaka clone with authority. It was exhausted now, having pushed its body at full strength the entire time.

“Hah!” it gasped as it reached Pinaka. Spotting the staff in his hands, it asked, “What did I miss?”

“You missed me becoming a magician!” Pinaka said, holding the staff close and giving it a quick twirl. He balanced it easily, like it weighed nothing.

Inside a narrow compartment running the length of the shaft was Sky Boulder powder. The amount had been carefully measured so the entire staff weighed less than a kilogram, making it light and effortless to carry.

“One day, I’m gonna swap Pronto’s digestive and perspiration systems with mine,” Pinaka muttered. “That way, maintenance would be easier.”

As a Human, Pronto needed a completely different diet than Pinaka. If his nutrition fell short, he’d suffer for it—meaning he couldn’t access his full powers. But with an Elf’s digestive system, he’d only need fruit.

Pinaka could conjure that with a flick of his hand. And the waste? Just sweat. It could be released from the tip of the staff in neat little droplets.

Way easier to clean than human shit.

“This is the Sun Stone I got from the messenger’s bike,” the clone said, handing over its bag before slumping to the side to rest. “The Warden won’t be alerted for the time being.”

Sun Stones were a rare resource. The Elven Prison had actually used a good number of them because of how crucial they were. They alone covered most of the Human Race’s organic needs—from food and Healing Potions to wood and clothes.

Each prison, like the grain prison where Pinaka lived, had its own Sun Stone. It was there to make sure the soldiers could keep working even when their Level 2 Officer had to leave the station for some reason.

Besides that, each Gate had three Sun Stones. Two were used on the bridge that stretched across the Lava Moat, and the third powered the messenger bike.

“Make a good job of hiding it,” Pinaka said as he watched his clones fill in the pit and press the soil down as tightly as possible. Then they covered it with ash and rolled a large cylindrical trunk over the spot until the section looked like the rest of the ash road.

The Sky Boulder was still inside the metal carriage. Pinaka didn’t have any use for it right now, so he buried it. He planned to come back for it later, but for now, it needed to stay hidden.

And under the ash road was the best place for that. Even if the Ogres ended up finding it, Pinaka wouldn’t mind. All he needed was enough of it to handle the Pronto Staff without having to deal with its weight.

Pronto’s raw power gave Pinaka the chance to build something like an aircraft. Of course, that wasn’t going to be easy. It would take a lot of experiments—and probably a ton of failures—before anything flew.

“What’s our plan now?” one of the clones asked.

“The Human Gate doesn’t have its officer,” Pinaka said with a grin. “So, we attack and replace them.”

He created two clones and passed his authority to both. One disguised itself as Pronto, and the other took the form of one of Pronto’s subordinate soldiers.

“Now, create an opportunity.”

The Human Gate

It stood on the road that led straight into Human territory. The soldiers stationed there were usually the most relaxed. Their only job was to carry out logistics checks on the carts heading toward the Human Kingdom.

If any other Race wanted to invade the Human Kingdom, there were plenty of other routes. No one needed to bother coming all the way to the Human Gate and risk crossing paths with the Elven Prison’s Warden—one of the most feared Level 3 existences on all of Gangnea.

—Yawn!

A soldier sitting on the fort attached to the gate couldn’t stop himself. He was bored out of his mind. “I want to take a nap.”

“Don’t spread your sloth to the rest of us,” his friend said, giving him a light slap on the back of the head.

“Oh, screw off, will you?”

Similar arguments echoed throughout the Human Gate, with some soldiers slacking off while others tried to keep things professional and uphold the dignity of their post.

Mornings were their busiest, with cargo being sent from the Elven Prison to the Human Kingdom. But by noon, all the work dried up, and every moment after lunch became a battle against boredom.

“Help!” came a soft shout.

Three soldiers jumped up, heads snapping toward the sound. They spotted two Humans staggering toward the gate, both clearly injured to different extents.

“Help!”

“What the hell? Injured people out here?” one of the soldiers on the wall muttered, frowning.

Another soldier suddenly leaned over the edge, eyes going wide as he recognized one of the figures below. “That’s Prince Pronto!”

“Quick! Get him!” he shouted. A team sprang into action, activating the bridge so it could be crossed. They rushed out, scooped up the two figures, and brought them into the fort.

“What happened?” one of the soldiers asked the injured man, who was supporting an unconscious Prince Pronto.

“The Dwarf made faulty repairs on our carriage,” the soldier growled, seething with frustration. “It exploded out of nowhere while we were on the way here.”

“Where are the rest?” the soldier asked. “Based on the letter we received, there were—”

“We’re all that’s left,” the soldier carrying Prince Pronto snapped, eyes dark with anger. “That damn Dwarf screwed us over!”

The two were rushed to a station inside the ward for treatment. Once there was an emergency, the soldiers moved like clockwork. A team started patching up the injuries, a messenger was sent to inform the Warden, and the rest tightened security.

Something was off. The Dwarf had always been obedient—so why would he suddenly pull something like this? The soldiers stayed on alert, cleaned their weapons, reinforced the fort, and prepared for the worst.

In the treatment room, Prince Pronto’s eyes suddenly flew open. He locked eyes with the second Pinaka clone, the one disguised as a soldier.

There were five Humans in the room: two within arm’s reach, two more within a single leap, and the last standing near the door. If they moved fast, they could take them all down before anyone raised an alarm. But there was no need to risk it—there were easier ways.

“Argh!” Prince Pronto let out a long, strained groan, his body starting to twitch violently.

“Quick, hold him still!” a nurse shouted.

All five Humans rushed to restrain him, grabbing onto his spasming body. That was enough.

“Heup!” Prince Pronto shouted as he launched a kick. The hands gripping his leg offered no resistance—his leg sliced through like it was moving through butter, slamming into a soldier’s chest. Using the man's body as a pivot, he spun, stretching his legs to their full length.

In one smooth, circular motion, a thick, buttery line traced across all five bodies. They dropped in pieces to the floor, not even having time to react. There was no blood; the sliced sections looked like rubber—clean, seamless, with an intact outer layer of skin.

“A—” One of the doctors, cut at the waist, had just enough life left to start a scream. But the second clone was ready. It plunged its hand into the doctor’s head, silencing him instantly.

“That was swift,” Prince Pronto said, already shifting his form into that of one of the doctors. The second clone did the same, changing into another doctor.

They stacked the corpses in a corner, then began spawning Pinaka clones—one every second. While the Spell of Pinaka could be cast instantly, the Creation Factor only allowed for one body per second.

The room was spacious enough to hold twenty clones, each armed with a short bow and a quiver of arrows.

“Count to 800, then start shooting,” the two clones with authority said in unison before rushing out.

“How’s Prince Pronto?” a nearby soldier asked when he spotted the two doctors leaving the ward.

“He’ll live,” the clone replied. As they reached a corner, the two split up—one heading toward the Human Gate, the other making for the bridge.

They planned to attack from both exits to make sure the Humans had no chance to escape.

“Why are you here?” a soldier suddenly barked. “You’re not supposed to leave the medical ward when a patient’s inside.”

“I came out for a stroll,” the Pinaka clone replied casually.

“Stroll?” The soldier’s expression shifted sharply. Without missing a beat, he drew his sword. “That’s not protocol! Speak! Who—”

“I messed up,” Pinaka muttered, wincing. A spear-shaped piece of bone shot out from his shoulder, aiming for the soldier’s forehead. But the man, already on alert, tilted his head just in time to dodge it.

“Monster!” he yelled, lunging with his sword—only to stab clean through a clone that had appeared out of nowhere.

“Ouch,” the clone grimaced as more of its kind materialized around the soldier. He went pale as one gagged him and another drove a wooden blade into his side.

“They’re exceptionally trained,” the Pinaka clone muttered. He didn’t waste another second—he broke into a sprint, spawning more clones with every passing second.

“Intruder!”

“Elves!”

Shouts rang out as soldiers poured into the corridors one after another. Within seconds, the hallways caught fire, casting everything in a flickering red glow and turning the already tense soldiers into something even more terrifying.

Oil grooves lined the walls, and once ignited, they spread flames through the entire fort like veins on fire.

The clones fought back, launching arrows at the Humans—but fire blasted at them from every direction, burning them faster than new ones could be spawned.

“Tch.” Pinaka, watching from a nearby spot, clicked his tongue as he sensed a shift. He had just regained a unit of Control Factor. ‘One of the clones I shared authority with is already dead.’

“They really aren’t easy to deal with.” Next to him stood a massive ballista, drawn all the way back.

[Spell: Pinaka Arrow]

He loaded the arrow onto the ballista and launched it toward the fort. Mid-flight, the arrow caught fire and began burning violently. But with most of Pinaka’s stats transferred into it, the arrow kept generating wood fast enough to avoid being consumed.

—Thud!

A flaming log smashed across the lava moat and rolled onto the fort. At that moment, the Pinaka clone inside used its last breath of authority to spawn another clone and passed the authority—just before being swallowed by the flames.

The new Pinaka clone immediately started creating a swarm of clones to lay siege to the fort. But with the entire place engulfed in flames, the Elves had lost their edge—especially without any authority left to back them up.

“It’ll fail at this rate,” Pinaka muttered, sprinting toward the bridge with the Pronto Staff in hand. As soon as he reached the Sun Stone that controlled the bridge’s heat, he made contact. “Activate it for me.”

The Sun Stone began sucking up all the heat from the bridge. Pinaka slipped on wood-formed shoes and started running.

There was still a dangerous amount of residual heat—enough to blister skin in seconds. But his shoes took the worst of it. They caught fire almost immediately, forcing him to constantly shed burning layers and grow new ones. Every tenth of a second counted. When he needed just a blink of time, he used the Pronto Staff like a cane to balance himself mid-stride.

The Sun Stone embedded in the staff absorbed the extra heat while Pinaka briefly hovered in the air. In that moment—just enough to lift his body—new shoes wrapped around his feet. Then he hit the ground running again.

“Heup!” He skidded to a stop in front of the fort and drove the Pronto Staff deep into the sea of flames. “Pronto, my dear,” he whispered.

“Absorb it all.”
