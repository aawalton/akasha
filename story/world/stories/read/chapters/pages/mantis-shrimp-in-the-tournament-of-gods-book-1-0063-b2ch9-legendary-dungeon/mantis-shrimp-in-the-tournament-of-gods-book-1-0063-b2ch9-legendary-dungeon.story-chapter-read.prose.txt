The dungeon looked exactly like I pictured it would look from the inside. A catacomb with moist, rocky walls all over, adorned with regalia and architecture from Middle Medieval Europe, or at least most of what I saw looked like that. There were some other strange things, like the glowing mushrooms I've become acquainted with on the first floor of the tournament already.

The map showed a forking path, diverging into four straight lines that we couldn't see into. After they made the first turn, the paths themselves were wide, however, at least wide enough to allow all four of us to get in.

"Any monsters yet?" I asked, looking around, mostly at Crudia. She had better senses forthis than I did.

"No, but I could go check," she offered, pointing to one of the paths.

"No, you are a good scout, Crudia, but we seriously want to stay together. Quite possibly any monster you see here will be level 60 or something around that and will easily dispatch you before you manage to get back." Mark shook his head, stopping her before she could argue more. "Don't think your invisibility protects you from monsters that are far stronger than you. They will smell you if they don't see you."

"Why would there be monsters that rely on smell in a catacomb? I'd say it's more likely there will be some undead." Lia smirked, walking to the second leftmost path already, Aura hurrying to follow suit.

"Where do you think you're going? Didn't we just agree that we'll party up?" Mark asked, irritated.

"Oh yeah, but we aren’t just leaving. We are setting the path. Now come on, what will we gain if we just stand around here like a bunch of herbivores waiting to get eaten?"

"She doesn't mean it, okay? But… I kind of agree," Aura offered a softer smile. "We'll just check if the monsters here are too high of a level and then go back. Easy, right? Then we can find your two friends-players, find a few others, and then we can come back here and clear it."

I seriously didn't understand how they thought this would be that easy. Not that I thought I didn't have the capacity to beat anything in front of me, of course, but after how the last floor ended up devolving, I wasn't quite so sure about the difficulty levels in this damn dungeon.

Mark said a level 60 monster would be here off-handedly, but I gave him more credit than I would have liked. Who knew what the gods were planning for us? Now that there were two top ten players in one place, I seriously didn't doubt it; they wouldn’t let us just breeze through whatever was in front of us. That wouldn’t be entertaining.

But with nobody offering a better plan, I had to step in front of the two avians and lead the way. It was really ungentlemanly to let them go first, after all, into a danger like this one.

I wondered if that was actually my personality or whether that was implanted into my skull as well, but it didn't matter. This was what made me happy now, and so I walked first.

The whole dungeon was beautiful. I hated to admit it, but there was so much water in such a place that was meant to be dry. The walls, all of them, were ornamented, but not in the overly gaudy way; instead, they looked just pristine. The only decorations were the carvings of the rock, which showed women, men, creatures I had no idea how to view, and other things like flowers and so on. Why would people who died even need such art all around them? I didn't know.

The path, however, continued being straight for the most part, except it was zigzagging after about every 50 feet. We didn't have much else to view except this new line. It took us about 20 minutes of just walking in a mostly straight line before the path started getting wider and wider. At the end, there was a drop and pure darkness below.

I raised my hand, summoning my shields, and got low as I went closer to the edge of the marble floor, which ended abruptly a few feet ahead.

"There's a drop off," I whispered. "Can't see much yet, though. Wait here!"

"No way you're just going alone. Wait up!" Crudia whispered, hurrying to follow behind me. The two birds stayed behind, though, watching me with keen interest in their eyes.

"Wait, let me go first. Nobody will see me if there's something," Mark offered, already zooming ahead. I followed, of course, paying attention to step softly. The cavern ahead was circular in shape, huge; and on our side it seemed that all paths led here, or at least some of them. There were three exits, so one must have looped somewhere else. At the other end, there seemed to be several dozen other exits, not as pristine as on our end, however.

While here it was a crypt with adorned walls and stone carvings, on the other side it was just bricks upon bricks upon bricks. Nothing else.

“Clear, no monsters here," Mark said, nodding, satisfied in a satisfied way.

"That's a good thing. We've been here for almost an hour, and we still have yet to see anything," I scoffed, and by anything I meant literally anything. There wasn't even a bug, despite how many spider webs I've seen.

"Hey, don't blame me for stuff like that. Besides, it's just the first day; we've got time still," Mark said, looking the whole cavern over. "Let's camp here, I guess, and try to see if anything strolls by."

"We've got limited time, Mark. You seriously want us to spend it just standing here looking at bricks for who knows how long?" I scoffed. That level 25 was SO CLOSE.

"You've got any better ideas? If we go down there, it'll be hard to get back up, and if something chases you, you are as good as dead."

"I can just dash up," I offered.

"Yeah? This high of a fall?" He actually seemed to think about it for a second. "Maybe. You probably could, but still it's far too risky."

"Jesus Christ, you two. Do you bicker this much all the time?" Lia scoffed, shaking her head. "I agree with the shrimp, by the way. We should go down there and see if there's anything."

"I am NOT going to die because you two don't know how to handle your egos," Mark shook his head in return.

"So pushy, dude. We can handle it. Both of us are in the top 10."

"Top 10 is good relative to the other players, but you're not even that much higher of a level or stronger. Well, you are, I guess. You're level 25 and have a class, but just no. Trust me on this; it is better to not push our luck."

"Quiet," Crudia suddenly exclaimed, putting a finger on her mouth.

"Crudia? Why?" Mark seemed genuinely confused by her order.

"Quiet, there's somebody… something approaching from way over there." She pointed towards the tunnels on the other side.

All of us stopped immediately, walking towards the edge and lying on the ground. The only things that would be visible from the bottom of the ravine would probably be our heads and maybe Crudia's ears, I supposed.

What followed sent a shiver down my spine. It wasn't a single creature walking down there. No, it was a whole legion.

How many people was a legion anyway? Well, there were a lot.

Skeletons walked, yet they weren't just the skellies I came to imagine after the mobs we fought on the first floor. What I saw was a humanoid skeleton, but they walked in formation. There were about twenty of them, separated into two neat lines, walking from one of the tunnels and heading towards the next. Their feet hit the ground in a synchronized motion, and all of them were adorned in the same armor.

Pure black, thicker than any metal, with no insignia, but instead weird lines that showed as if it was melted rock. They held huge tower shields in front of them and pointed towards the ceiling with spears, one half-time larger than they were. Their eyes burned with a blue fire.

At least they had good color choice, I thought. And they looked far better than any of the mobs we've seen on the first floor. Maybe these would be kind of fun to fight.

But who knew what they were capable of? I knew even before I used Analyze that we wouldn't be able to fight these piles of calcium.

You know, what I hate the most about the humans' video games and their portrayal of skeletons is the fact that they are, for some reason, utterly weak in 90% of the scenarios they appear in. Not only are they usually just the beginner mob that you either beat or the main character beats, depending on the medium you select, but they also just lack any creativity when it comes to describing these. And they also die if you hit them a few times? What's up with that?! They are just bones held together with magic. Is the magic just too weak or something? Make it make sense. I couldn't go full creative mode on these yet, at least for now. We do want at least some of you to survive this floor, after all, but do note that these are not the beginner mob, as shown by the fact that this is already the second floor.

These are the first line of defense of the master of this dungeon, and they will surely make sashimi if you dare approach them with too little caution. They were made magically, and hence, they are stronger than any human and definitely don't have any emotions that would make them run away in fear if you kill a few. If you start a fight with skellies, it's either you or the skelly that dies completely, no in between.

I know who I’d bet on for sure.

I didn't need to read it all to know that if we chose to fight them, we'd be dead in seconds. Level 40 would be manageable, since there were four of us, but 20 of them? No way.

We had some serious leveling up to do.

We watched as they disappeared in the other tunnel, walking ahead without even taking a look around them, and once the last one disappeared out of sight, several loud sighs came from somewhere around me.

"Yeah, okay. Guide guy was right, I guess," Lia smirked, standing up and dusting her robes off.

"Of course I was. I always am." He smirked, though the expression didn't quite reach his eyes.

"Anyway, I suggest we leave and get to our guild base and decide what we do afterwards." He sounded tired, as if he was the one that would have to fight those guys. But that was me, not him!

At least they didn't use magic or stuff like that; spears I could block sounded good enough. I stood up already, walking forwards—or, well, backwards. I had way too much to think about, and I didn't like that. I didn't like thinking, for that matter, but suddenly I had to not speak about some stuff. Keep a cryptic message in my mind to not forget, and also I discovered that the dungeon towards the next floor will be harder than anything we've been through yet. Level 40s, and they were just the outermost defense. I didn't miss that part.

I felt fear but… I also couldn't help the excitement rising within me.
