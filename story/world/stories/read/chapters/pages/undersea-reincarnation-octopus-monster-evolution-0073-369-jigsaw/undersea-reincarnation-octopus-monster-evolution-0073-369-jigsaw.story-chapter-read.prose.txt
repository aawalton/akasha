Ash took the news well. In fact, Henry was pretty sure he saw her eyes light up when Velistraine pulled her aside near the prow of the ship and told her that she was sending her to Thalis for a few days, and it wasn’t hard to understand why. Ash needed to get stronger to bring the Clydemars to justice, and what better way to reach that goal than through a plethora of sea monsters?

Thalis was in the middle of the ocean, bathed in the bolstering lights of the Current which they could see even from here, glowing in the distance like a thin thread connecting this planet to the one above. Which meant she could hunt and train to her heart’s content. She could spar with the folk over there, and if she was lucky, she might even get some of Zerathstra’s attention.

Plus, of course, it would be safer for her than staying here. So long as she didn’t venture too far or too deep. And so with that in mind, Henry got to work. Stretching his attention down the trail of hidden, camouflaged relays he’d left through their voyage, Henry’s awareness traveled down the line, occasionally pulling a clone closer or further, depending on the quality of its reception. But pretty quickly, Henry regained awareness of the tiny kraken in Arisia’s office.

There was another below the water, hidden in a crevice of the trees, but he was moving people around, so having a dry branch under their feet would be better. So, as he watched and listened to Ash animatedly talk with Fabian and Maurice, the clone pulled itself out of the aquarium under the raised brow of Arisia, who was staring at him, her pen frozen above an ink pot. Controlling the remote clone was a little challenging considering the distance. Henry could only field around three hundred clones, and each were close to their maximum range, so around two miles. That made the connection a little iffy. But it was still functional. And Henry was confident that with every level he gained his Aspect of Dancing Shadows, the network would get stronger and better.

Waving at the woman with a tiny arm, the clone lifted off the ground and began working on activating Maw of the Trickster while across the ocean, Maurice was urgently tapping him on the shoulder.

“Henry, what should I do? I want to go with them because Thalis is fun, but we barely explored the island! There’s so much stuff to do and see here. What do I do?”

Henry stared at the red-haired teen and blinked. Maybe it was the concentration it was taking him to maintain the connection, but he was having trouble understanding what the problem was and when he said as much, Maurice frowned. “Isn’t it going to be difficult to send her there? Last time you did this, you looked like you were in pain and it took you hours to recover.”

And then it clicked. Maurice was thinking of the time he’d taken them to see the porpoises. But back then—aside from not having the levels he had now—he’d been carrying Velistraine. Which made all the difference.

“It’ll take me minutes to take anyone from here to there and vice versa. It does take some effort, but it’s nothing like last time. Back then, I had to carry Velistraine, and she’s heavy—”

“Hey!” protested Velistraine, visibly offended as she looked up from a set of documents her quartermaster was showing her, but Henry ignored the interjection.

“—but it should be easy moving anyone else. Go with them if you want, and when you want to come back, just poke me and wait. I won’t be as responsive considering how much effort it takes to keep contact with the furthest clones, but we can make it work.”

With that matter settled, Henry popped the trio in his Maw, then spat them out the other side a few second later. He had to admit, he found the malicious stare Arisia had given them funny. It was as if he’d delivered her a new fun puzzle to solve.

Next he assigned an Octomind to keep an eye on this particular clone, created another clone-in-a-ball and gave it to Maurice, then finally pulled his attention back, confirming to Velistraine that the transfer was done.

“Lots of potential with that skill,” she mused as she leaned over the rail, facing the pillar of light all the way out at sea. “How far can you send ‘em now?”

Henry hummed for a moment, joining her to stare at the horizon. “With my current network? Around six hundred miles, so long as I have time to set up. The only limiting factor’s the number of relays and their range. Both increase with every Aspect level, so it should get better. Especially once the Aspect gets to A-rank.”

“Should be soon, if it’s already allowing you this much,” Velistraine said, giving him a sidelong glance, and checking his stats, he nodded.

Dancing Shadows of the Trickster (B) - Level 5

“It’s halfway there. So… soon. Especially with the work I plan on doing over the next couple days.”

Velistraine nodded. “Sounds good to me. Maybe we’ll even have a quick way out of the Reach, so consider saving some for that,” she added as she thoughtfully eyed a nearby floating clone for a couple of seconds before she straightened up. Slapping the weathered wood, Velistraine grinned at him. “Alright then. With the children tucked in, you ready to re-visit the creature? I’ve got a feeling this won’t be the last time we meet its kind, so we’d better be ready.”

Henry could only nod there, as he stared down into the water while he considered her suggestion. Ever since he’d experienced the odd madness that wafted off of the creature, he couldn’t shake off the feeling that he was going to see more of it. Was it maybe linked to that feeling of being watched? He really hopped not. That meant he was either losing it, or something he didn’t understand was taking place.

“I could not agree more,” he muttered as he peered at the teens through his faraway clone.

The next few days went by in a blur. Ferrying Maurice back and forth was easy enough, and together they spent time meditating around the creature, hoping to develop a resistance skill or at the least get used to the weird dissonant effect it had on them. When they weren’t in the basement, they moonlighted as hunters for one of the many monster-hunting guilds around the islands.

While Attal and his organizations were doing a fine job defending the coast from overtly curious beings, there was profit to be had by venturing a little out at sea. Sea monsters weren’t as common as they were in the Current, but that didn’t mean they were rare, either. Henry had the advantage of having his Shadows and Octominds to scout for him whenever they were out on a ship, and so he could always find low to mid B-rank aggressive enough to take a swing at him. A-ranks weren’t as easy to find though, unfortunately, but that made sense. They weren’t supposed to be that common, but even then, Henry’s pathfinding had picked up on a couple of signatures.

Still, it was impressive how easy it was to find powerful creatures in this world. Come to think of it, even his own ascent had been fast. And so was Maurice’s and Ash’s, which made the System’s first words to him flash into his mind.

“I take care of things,” it had said. “Help intelligent life strive to the top. Give them tools and skills to gain more power. Then I take a small percentage of that. It was supposed to go somewhere… but no one’s come to collect in a while.”

Back then, that was a curious tidbit to hear, but now, with everything Henry had picked up, he could see it click with everything else. The orbs. The isolation. The speed with which power could be acquired.

Back in Attal’s basement, Henry stared at the frozen creature, ignoring the barely audible whispers he felt crawling all over his soul, looking for cracks they couldn’t find to slither through.

It was like he’d been handed a blacked-out jigsaw puzzle and he’d been putting the pieces together one group at a time. Not knowing what the final picture looked like. But the more pieces clicked together, the more cohesive the image was becoming, and the System’s words clicked right in with the rest.

All that surplus is out here, Henry thought. Feeding anyone or anything that reached for it. That was why one could go from D-rank to B in months, probably. Anyone could gorge themselves on power until they popped, at which point either they made to A-rank, or they were the stepping stone for one who did. And then the System came to them with an offer. To disappear somewhere. A reserve of powerful beings, maybe. Setting them aside and waiting for more. There could always be more. The sea was crawling with powerful creatures, and all that potential was still available. Trapped so the things beyond the world can’t get through. But sooner or later…

Henry swallowed. Sooner or later, the ripe fruit would pop. Maybe that was why the System was taking A-ranks away?

“That makes a little too much sense,” Henry muttered to himself. It was one of the missing pieces of the jigsaw puzzle, but when you looked at everything you had already pieced together, it wasn’t too hard to make assumptions about the missing pieces. But it was better to be sure. There were a few bits that were still shrouded. The orbs and their roles. The System and why it refuses to say what was happening.

Henry hoped they’d get the final answer soon. But there was no denying the image was growing clearer, and the malicious hunger emanating from the frozen, bisected monster was making more and more sense.

“[Henry, our shift’s about to start!]” came the voice through one of his surface clones and with a stretch and a yawn, Henry set the worries aside and teleported up. Some sun and salt would do him good. He could worry some other time.
