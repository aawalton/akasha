Stella exhaled and closed her eyes.

It was like, even though she already knew what had happened, she really didn’t want Lexie to admit fault, especially with so little prompting.

“We talked about this," Stella said. “You agreed–”

“I didn’t agree,” Lexie said. “You weren’t listening to me.  You were putting everyone, including yourself, in danger by keeping him alive. I couldn’t convince you of that, so I took matters into my own hands.”

“That’s not how it works, Lexie!" Stella snapped, like a string stretched too tight. "You don’t get to take matters into your own hands. Even heroes don’t get to unilaterally decide whether someone lives or dies.”

Lexie shrugged. “Maybe they should."

Stella gasped.

“Do you even hear yourself?" she asked. "It's premeditated murder. Do you understand? This isn’t self-defense, as you’re claiming it to be. You murdered a man when he was powerless and in cold blood, and you don't even seem bothered about it. You're a child, you can't...and how did you even get into the pocket dimension in the first place?”

“I can get in anywhere I want,” Lexie said. “And if I could get in so easily, I'm sure Vulcan could get out, too. But he was waiting for something. Probably to escape with something or do something. Keeping him alive was giving him a chance to complete his plan.” She glanced at Uncle Max, who was staring at her measuredly. He didn’t exactly look pleased with her, but he didn’t look as incensed and frankly horrified as Stella appeared, either.

“Who are you?” Stella whispered as though seeing Lexie for the first time. It stung a little to hear that from her.

What stung even more was the look in her eyes, almost like she was staring at a monster.

Lexie swallowed the hurt. She tried to understand that the other woman had been raised with an honor code that Lexie's behavior went completely against. But she truly hadn't expected Stella to take it this so hard and be this furious about it. Vulcan was a terrible man who'd tried to kill so many people, including Stella's sons. Surely a part of her was relieved he was dead, and should appreciate that Lexie had taken that decision out of her hands.

On the other hand, perhaps Lexie had misjudged her.

Lexie guessed that her own blase attitude toward the murder didn’t help, but she didn’t want to pretend. She wasn’t going to pretend that she regretted killing Vulcan, nor was she going to pretend to go into a moral quandary about it. He needed to die. He died. Period.

Yet the more Stella stared at her, the more the human part of Lexie ached. And the ache grew into a piercing discomfort that made her want to lash out, too.

She controlled the urge.

“Are you going to take me in?” she asked Stella, deciding to focus on the reality rather than her emotions.

“No,” Max said instantly. “You’re not going anywhere.”

“I don’t have a choice,” Stella said. “You have no idea what you’ve done, Lexie, the problems you've caused. There were already things in place, the trial’s been set, announcements made, deals negotiated. Several governments are involved in this case. It’s bigger than the hero association now.”

“I don’t care about that,” Max said earnestly. “Just tell them that he offed himself in captivity and keep Lexie's name out of it.”

“They’re not going to buy that he killed himself. She put a hole in his head."

Max sent Lexie a sardonic look. “Couldn't you have been a little subtler?”

Lexie shrugged. In hindsight, she probably should have just poisoned him or something. Then they could pretend he drank it all on his own.

“Tell the association he got his hands on a weapon, and couldn’t take captivity anymore, so he killed himself," Max tried.

“He was restrained the whole time, and he did not indicate that he wanted to die. In fact, he was cooperating with us. It wasn’t just me who found his body, either. Jerry and Monty were with me, and I'm sure you know those two are pretty adept at ascertaining the mode of death.” She shook her head. “Monty suspected, but now you’re admitting to me that you killed him…The only reason they didn’t send a battalion of heroes to take you is because of the protective wards Aiden has around the house and because I told them I could bring you in peacefully.”

"Can't they let it go? It's my first offense, and I'm a kid."

"That doesn't work for premeditated murder."

"How would they know it's premeditated?"

"Because you just told me," Stella said in a hard tone, and Lexie could see that the other woman wasn't budging.

"You're doing all this to me because I killed the man who tried to kill your sons? Both of them? I saved their lives."

"This isn't just about Vulcan, Lexie," Stella responded, staring her down. "This is about you. Something is seriously wrong here, and we need to figure out what."

Lexie bit her lip. That sounded like a threat, though Stella didn't say it like it was one. She sounded more sad and worried than threatening.

Maybe it was because of Lexie's mom. Maybe she was panicking because of how Lara had ended up.

Lexie thought about how to make this better for Stella. She couldn’t stop the other woman from seeing her as a monster, but maybe she could correct the inconvenience factor.

“If it's his memories they’re worried about, I have them with me. I preserved them in one of my cards, and I can show you everything he had planned.”

“Monty was already working on that." She was frustrated. "We had everything handled. We didn't need you to do what you did."

“You weren't working fast enough, obviously.” Lexie was also getting frustrated. She didn’t know how the memory extraction thing worked for Monty, but she figured it was taking so long because Vulcan held onto a lot of memories, and they were probably all twisted and convoluted, considering the numerous lifetimes the man had lived. "It would have taken you forever to sort through all his memories, and Vulcan would have escaped. You know it, and I know it.”

“He was under guard,” Stella said. “Your father already neutralized him. There was no need to kill him, especially as you did.”

Lexie was quiet. “Difference of opinion.”

“This isn’t just a difference of opinion, Lexie!" Stella exploded. "A man’s dead. Yes, he was a villain, but he was a human being too!"

And I’m not. At least not entirely. Lexie could hear it implied, even though Stella had no way of knowing that Lexie was half Eldritch.

That is, if the Fae hadn’t told her.

"We don't kill people in cold blood," Stella said.

"Is that because you're so merciful? Or because you lack the courage to make the decision?"

Stella narrowed her eyes. "Because I'm not a god. I don't get to pick who lives or dies."

“I’m sure you think that, Stella, but you actually do. By keeping Vulcan alive, you were inadvertently picking who would die, who he would hurt next. And it probably would have been someone I love. It might have even been you." Lexie pressed her lips together, holding back the surge of words she wanted to say. She so desperately wanted to get her godmother to see things her way, so the other woman would stop looking at her like she was a villain. "The truth is that there was every need to kill him.” She stated it firmly, “You don’t know him. You don’t know what he’s capable of. He was planning something. I swear he was.”

“What’s going on?”

Lexie turned around to find both Xena and Dewie at the foot of the steps. Tate wasn’t too far behind either.

“Nothing,” Lexie said. “Just go back up. We’ll–”

“Did you kill someone?” Xena’s voice was quiet, her gaze showing that she’d heard most of what they were fighting about.  Her lips formed a line as she gripped the staircase, waiting for Lexie to tell her something different from what she'd already heard.

Lexie’s stomach twisted in knots.

She really wished Stella hadn’t chosen to do this right now. Xena would have probably found out anyway, but this wasn't how Lexie wanted to explain it to her.

To her credit, Stella attempted to rectify the situation. “No, we’re just having a conversation–”

“Yes,” Lexie admitted, because lying right now would only make things worse. “I killed Vulcan because he was going to kill everyone if I didn’t get rid of him.”

Xena stiffened. She paled. Lexie's hurt expanded.

“We’re leaving Lexie,” Stella said.

“Where?” Dewie asked, and at the same time, Uncle Max said, “She’s not going anywhere.”

“I’m not asking you, Max," Stella said.

“Neither am I.” In the blink of an eye, Max had his gun in his hand, and he had it pointed straight at Stella.

Stella reared back in shock, clearly not expecting that, but Max’s gaze gleamed like granite.

“Leave,” Max told her. “We can do this the easy way, or the hard way.”

Stella didn’t act scared, probably because of their difference in power levels. Stella was a double S-Rank, with physical and mana capabilities way beyond what Max had. She probably had a counter for the bullet Max was about to send her way.

Then again, Uncle Max was very unpredictable. Who knew what would come out of the barrel?

Stella tried to de-escalate the situation.

“This is already bad,” she told him. “Don’t make it worse.”

“Then don’t make me take the shot,” he said.

“It’s okay, Uncle Max,” Lexie said, keenly aware that both Xena and Dewie were standing behind her watching this disaster play out. “I’ll go with her.”

"No, the hell you won't.”

“Max, stand down.”

“Sorry, Stella.” The words came right as he fired a shot at her leg, but it was scorched mid-air. So fast. Lexie could have blinked and missed it, but Stella literally melted steel and simultaneously stopped its trajectory, leaving almost nothing in its wake. And she hadn’t even broken a sweat.

Lexie blinked. Even though she’d seen Stella in action before, Lexie didn’t think she’d had the concept of just how powerful the other woman was until now, especially given how controlled she was. After all, fire was, by nature, a large-scale ability, and it was much harder to give it specificity.

“Max,” Stella warned, but Max turned the dial of his gun and fired off more shots, this time, crystalline in form.

One of them, Stella caught with a heat-seeking tiny missile of her own, but then she erected a shield of fire that was somehow hardening around the bullets, trapping them in lava-like substance.

One of the bullets gave off a contained explosion, but it wasn't enough to escape its enclave.

“Stop it, Max,” Stella said, sounding more annoyed than angry.

“Max,” Lexie said. “I’m going with her.”

Max put down his gun. “Then I’m coming with you.”

“I’d rather you stay here with Xena and Dewie."

“No, I made a promise to your dad, and this…this is bringing back very bad memories.” He shook his head. “I’m not letting them take you alone.”

Lexie figured he was remembering when Aiden had been captured the first time. She’d never really considered how hard that must have been for Max. One of his best friends died, and the other one was now going to jail, possibly for life.

"It's not happening without me, Stella," Max reiterated.If you’re taking her in, I’m going with you.”

“What about Dewie and Xena?" Lexie whispered. "And the sleepover?" The sleepover was probably ruined at this point. Dewie looked worried, and Xena looked like she was about to cry. There was also anger in her eyes.

“Tate can take care of them," Max said.

Tate’s eyes flared as he pointed at his own chest. “Me?”

"Yeah. Just until Emma gets back from work. I’ll have her babysit.”

It clearly didn't matter to Max that Tate was the same age as the other two. He probably thought Tate was more mature because he acted more mature.

Tate looked like he wanted to protest, but on second thought decided not to. Dewie gave him a curious glance, but Xena didn’t even seem like she’d heard him; she just kept hitting Lexie with that betrayed look.

Lexie exhaled.

“Fine,” Stella sighed, the weariness stripping her face of anger. “Let’s go.”

Lexie went to take her hand, which Stella gripped tightly while still looking down at her with a mixture of disappointment and worry.

Lexie gave her the same look right back.

A minute later, Lexie sat before the hero’s association board in her new designer panda pajamas.

Uncle Max was by her side, giving all of them formidable looks. It didn’t matter that their collective power levels dwarfed his by miles. He wasn't going to let them take Lexie without a fight.

They hadn’t wanted him in here, but Stella had fought for it anyway, and Max hadn’t really given them a choice in the matter, regardless.

Jerry was at the head of the table, his piercing eyes enough to cut steel. Lexie wondered what his powers were. It was hard to think of him having powers at all because he had a very HR mid-level manager look to him. But she had the feeling it was something unexpected.

“You killed our prisoner,” Jerry said, as though he hadn’t already spent the last five minutes establishing facts.

“Yes,” Lexie said.

“You snuck into the pocket dimension and killed him in cold blood.”

“Well, I didn’t kill him in there, but yes, essentially. And I had a good reason why.”

"How do we know she snuck in?" Lady Thistleworm asked. "She could have easily planned this with her father."

"My father had nothing to do with this."

"Jerry, you must have expected this when you gave the villain the key to the cage," someone else muttered as though Lexie hadn't spoken. "I knew this was a bad idea."

"Yet on the day you voted yes," Lord Stillwater said.

"We didn't have a choice, did we?" he snapped.

“Enlighten us,” Kya was also looking at Lexie with a gleam in her eyes and an almost smile. Lexie wasn't sure if she was supporting Lexie’s murder of Vulcan or if she was happy because Lexie had proven her right, that the association shouldn’t have trusted Aiden to hold the Alchemist in the first place. Although it really wasn't Aiden's fault. "What was your reason for killing him?"

"Vulcan was planning to escape."

"And you know this how?"

Lexie didn't have any hard proof, but she was sure that if she went through his memories, she would find some. "Educated guess."

"How? Did Aiden not confess that he could see whatever Vulcan was planning?"

"Yes, but my dad is stranded, and probably can't communicate with us right now, even if he did see Vulcan's escape."

"You're telling me the all-powerful Archmage wouldn't be able to stop it?"

"I'm telling you my dad didn't know about this. Obviously."

"We can't say that for sure."

"Of course you can. I'm telling the truth. And if you really think about it, this is your fault." A few people raised eyebrows at her audacity, but she forged ahead anyway. "We had a deal that you would kill him within a few days. You didn’t keep up your end of the bargain, so I didn’t see it fit to keep up mine either."

They were stunned and silent for a few seconds as she stared at them.

"I think your voyage in the dungeon has made you a tad arrogant, Ms. Sparrowfoot," Lord Shadowsbane spoke. "You're mistaken in thinking that the Hero's Association answers to you."

"Well, you kinda have to work with me now," she said. "After all, I'm the only one who has Vulcan's memories."

"We don't have to work with you at all," Kya said. "We can make you hand over those memories."

"You think so?"

"I know so."

The tension in the room was thick enough to cut with a knife. Lexie felt the heaviness of the stares, and if she were still her old human self, she would be cowering by now.

As it were, she couldn't believe that they thought they could take her. She would almost welcome the fight.

"You’ll have to stay under guard," Jerry said. "While we figure out what to do with you. You'll need a tilling band and strict house arrest."

“No.”

“That's not a request,” Jerry said. “I’m not Vacek. I’m not going to let you break the rules and run amok just because you’re a Sparrowfoot and happen to have power. You’re showing yourself to be a threat, and it’s our duty to stop you.”

"That's fine," Lexie said. "But I'm also not my father. I'm not a hero, and if you think about it, I'm not really a citizen of Earth either. I don't have to comply with your orders, and if you want me to fight all of you, I will. And I don't have to keep any of you alive when I do it either. Except Stella." Despite their argument, she didn't want Stella hurt, although she hadn't looked at the woman throughout the whole meeting. Stella hadn't said anything to her either.

"You're threatening us?" Wavelost said.

"You threatened me first."

"Okay, this is going nowhere," Groundsmage interjected. "Obviously, what the child did was wrong, but there's no reason for us to do anything rash here."

"You of all people are worried about being rash?" Lord Shadowsbane raised an eyebrow.

"The cock crows once a day, does it not?"

Lexie had no idea what that meant.

"This too might work out for us in the long run," he continued. "Think about it, she got rid of a threat, and we have the memories, it's a win, isn't it?"

"That's not what this is about, Thor," Stella snapped.

"On the contrary, it seems like that's exactly what this is about."

"She's clearly unstable," Jerry said. "Uncontrollable. Dangerous."

Lexie said nothing because all those things were true.

"We could just as easily turn you over to the Fae," one person suggested. "If we assign her as a foreign threat and ask for their help, they'll take her."

"No," Stella said even as Jerry looked, considering.

"Fuck no," Max parroted.

"Absolutely not."

Jerry ignored the two of them. "It's certainly an option. They'll offer us a lot in return."

Lexie told him, "You do that, and it would be the biggest mistake of your life. Because once the Fae have me, nothing is stopping them from taking over Earth and forcing it to become a protectorate."

"You think so?" Kya asked.

"I know so." She turned her words back on her.

There was silence around the room for a few seconds, then, suddenly, defense came from an unexpected source.

"I believe her."

It was from Monty, who'd been standing silently at the back of the room for most of the conversation.

Jerry looked at him in confusion. "What exactly do you believe her about?"

"I think she might be–"

Jerry held up his hand to silence him, holding a finger to his ear.

Then he surged to his feet, startling everyone at the table.

"District 8 just attacked us," he announced. "They're dropping bombs in Indigo, Alberton."
