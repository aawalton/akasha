Five affinities. How, you ask? It was unknown, and everyone who asked—died.  — He who calls himself Chi You, The Completed Works by He Shulin, foremost scholar of the Outer Realms

Settling down in his cultivation chamber, Jianyi almost reflexively checked for the pills that would help him expel impurities, but according to the technique, those were not needed. There were no impurities to expel.

He sank into his lower dantian, the walls of the pathways leading to it shimmering softly. Over the last few days, he had diligently practiced the circulation technique needed for the procedure, and now was the time to begin.

With practiced ease, he separated two threads of qi from the small mass of motes that had formed and started to almost lazily cycle them through his pathways. For the lower dantian, they just needed to go down to the meridians on his feet and then back up. It was important not to go too fast, as otherwise controlling them in the dantian would be almost impossible.

As the two slivers of qi entered his dantian, instead of moving towards the next pathway, he directed them to and along the walls as if he was painting it with a palette knife, then back out, towards the opposite side exit from where they had entered.

The feeling was one of slight discomfort. A pressure that he felt in his spirit, similar to the feeling in his ears when he ascended the mountain. Easy.

Unfortunately, he was sure that it wouldn’t stay that way.

On and on he went, losing track of time, but it must have been hours that he had spent when he noticed that he had made it once around the dantian.

The changes were marginal. Maybe it had expanded a bit, but it was hard to tell. Possibly the color of the dantian walls had become slightly less translucent, but he didn’t know for sure.

He knew for sure, though, that he wasn’t done. Had hardly started.

So on he went, always keeping the two qi threads cycling. He briefly suspected that Elder Sun had given him this task not because it was helpful to his dantian, but because he wanted him to spend more time on improving his qi control. But he dismissed that bout of paranoia and lost himself in the work of expanding his dantian.

It was in the middle of the night, and even Jianyi had to admit that he should stop soon, when, on the third pass around his spiritual sea for a brief moment, he glimpsed the Celestial Core Card resting in his dantian.

It jolted him to full wakefulness again, and for a moment he thought he had just imagined it, but then he saw it again—so different from all the cards he had seen so far. Instead of jade green, it was pearlescent white and glowed from the inside out.

Again, it was gone, but it renewed his enthusiasm despite the ever-increasing pressure he was putting on his own spirit.

He found the energy to complete the third pass and then fell into bed, exhausted.

Probably, it was best to take a break for a day or so before continuing. But he would see on the next day whether he felt up to it.

Seeing that card, even if only for moments, had brought back the memories of the trial, something that, despite being less than four months ago, still felt almost like a dream.

Soon, he could unleash the power of the core card for real.

Jianyi hid under the bed in his old room in the city.

He was sweating and panting, having just run for days from those hunting him.

He could hear them. Could hear the barking of the bloodhounds following his tracks through the forest outside his window.

He was shivering in the cold wind that was blowing through his room unimpeded by the walls.

The one thing that gave him strength was the spear he was holding. His father’s spear.

And, deep within him, behind his navel, a warmth emanated.

A warmth against the cold. Against the hunters outside.

His room shook as purple light flickered by.

The warmth—his Celestial Core Card—pulsed.

The purple light that had just passed returned with a vengeance, turning his room into a hellscape of purple and shadow.

He knew that the light was reflecting off the piercing purple eyes that had hunted him for so long. Being seen would be his end.

He gripped his spear harder. He knew this was a dream. He just had to wake up. He pushed. Pushed hard.

The room shook. The walls rattled.

Push.

A radiant ray of blue light shot out from below, incinerating the floor and making him fall down, landing in a lake.

Instead of being shocked by the cold plunge, he felt at home.

The blue ray of light suffused the water. His will asserting itself.

He felt safe from anybody hunting him.

He opened his eyes. Sweating but calm, he found himself in his bed in Elder Sun’s compound.

Based on the darkness outside, it was still the middle of the night, only a few hours since he had ended his dantian-opening session.

A knock on his door made him fully wake up.

Elder Sun stood outside, staring at him intensely.

“Teacher?! How can I help you?”

The elder didn’t respond right away, instead continuing his examination of Jianyi, pursing his lips.

“What just happened?”

Jianyi scratched his head and blushed. “A nightmare. Just a nightmare. They have intensified again. I hope I didn’t cry out, waking you?”

For the first time, the elder cracked a smile. “I don’t sleep, Jianyi, and no, you didn’t cry out. That doesn’t mean that you didn’t call for attention—just maybe not in the way you meant to. Get dressed and find me in the kitchen.”

Jianyi’s gut was clenching nervously. What had the elder meant? What had made him come to Jianyi’s room? He quickly dressed in his robes and made his way to the kitchen.

He had never seen the elder there, but he was making tea with practiced movements as Jianyi entered the room. White Jasmin, a mild blend. Jianyi got the cups and sat down after the elder waved him to the bench.

The familiar ritual made Jianyi calm down again. Only after they had had a few sips did Elder Sun speak up. “Tell me about the dream.”

“I don’t think I have ever told you about the piercing purple eyes that haunt me in my dreams. Really have, as long as I can remember.” He looked at the elder, who shook his head, frowning. “It got better when I moved here, but in recent weeks, things got more intense again. Today was probably the most… visceral?” He searched the elder’s face for confirmation that the word made sense and got a nod in response.

He continued to explain what had transpired in his nightmare up to the moment that he had woken up.

All the way through, Elder Sun listened patiently, slightly frowning, but not giving any of his thoughts away.

“Teacher, what brought you to my door, though?”

Elder Sun blew on his tea and took a sip.

“Have you started opening your dantians?”

Jianyi forgot about the current troubles for a moment, smiling broadly at the elder. “Yes, I did. It worked really well—I even got to see my core card briefly.”

The elder nodded slowly. “Well done, my young friend, well done.” Sitting up a bit, the elder seemed to have come to a decision. “To answer your question, what brought me to your door was the feeling of a powerful presence pushing against my domain.”

Jianyi was appalled. “Me? You think my dream caused this?”

Snorting, the elder shook his head. “No, Jianyi. You certainly have a powerful soul, but it will take a while until you are strong enough to register. No, this was someone—or something—else.”

Purple eyes. That was the only explanation. It had always felt real—and now it turned out to be real. He shivered, goosebumps forming on his back.

“Who?” he asked, his voice forlorn.

“There are quite a few possibilities. But… do you remember our conversation on the day we met about the being scholars speculated to be behind the Celestial Confrontation?”

Jianyi remembered very well. He had also done a bit of research in the library. There was no confirmation at all, just speculation, but it had felt plausible. An entity steering the mostly mindless demonic beasts and the accompanying demons through the portals to their plane and scheming for the fall of the Empire and the end of Wuxing in balance.

He put his head in his hands, elbows on the table, his breathing quickening.

It was one thing for him to speculate. It was something else altogether for Elder Sun, somebody who could have been patriarch of the Fivefold Spear Sect, to share that speculation.

What was he meant to do? Did he need to run? Could he hide?

“Jianyi!”

He got pulled out of his tumbling thoughts, and with a few deep breaths, he felt the calmness of his spirit lake embracing him.

“I am sorry, teacher. I understand what you said.”

“Good. Because there is another thing that I noticed once I saw you.” Seeing Jianyi’s eyes widen, the elder raised his hand calmingly. “Nothing we cannot deal with, don’t worry. It seems that your… core card… is quite noticeable to anybody with the right senses, now that you have started to unlock your dantian.”

That was the worst-case scenario, as Elder Sun had always warned him not to let anybody know about the celestial-grade card in his spirit. But the calm that he had established earlier was not so easily broken.

“What can I do?”

The elder nodded.

“We differentiate between spirit and body cultivation. Those are indeed the only cultivation paths one can follow in the sense that you use qi to strengthen yourself. There is, however, another dimension, another resource that is available to a cultivator.” He paused for a moment.

“To anybody, theoretically. Your soul and the will that it empowers. It is not related to your strength as a cultivator, but the truth is most only learn to wield this resource as a shield and weapon later on their path. The reasons are that people are busy learning things that have a more direct impact on the frontlines—or duel rings.” The elder grinned at Jianyi, clearly having heard about his loss, despite Jianyi not having mentioned it.

“Another reason is that while the soul is immutable, will can grow over time as you channel your soul and understand your Dao better. Do you follow?”

They had touched upon this to some extent in the past, but not this exhaustively. He was keen to understand more and nodded.

“There are two domains to express your will, your soul’s strength. The inner and the outer domain. The latter will help you sense others within your domain and help you assess their strength and capabilities, just like I can sense your core card now—not in detail, but its strength. This domain will also allow you to affect the world around you and unleash your aura—sometimes called killing intent—on others.”

This was a whole new world opening up. He had spent months trying to get to the starting line of spirit cultivation—and the duel with Lu Shan had painfully made it clear how far behind he was—and now a new dimension opened up that felt enticing and overwhelming at the same time.

“What we need right now is the inner domain, though.” The elder squashed all thoughts of Jianyi defeating Lu Shan with the strength of his soul right away.

“The inner domain is about self-protection and resilience. It allows you to protect yourself from other people’s assessment, probing, and scrying. Also, it helps you protect yourself from emotional manipulation or soul attacks. As you can see, you are pitting your inner domain against other people’s outer domain. Given how strong your soul and will seem…” the elder paused, tapping his index finger on his chin repeatedly.

“Did you say that you have had these nightmares for as long as you can remember?”

Jianyi nodded, not sure what the elder was after.

“Mhh, it could be that your will is as strong as it is, as you had to resist the pressure from this entity in your dreams for so long. The potential of your soul was unlocked earlier, which we can use now.”

What irony, that years of nightmares had strengthened his will, which had allowed him to succeed in the Heavenly trial and the challenges since then.

“What I was trying to say is that given how strong your will is—and your will coming to your rescue in your dream is proof of that—you should be able to learn these techniques relatively quickly.”

Relief flooded through Jianyi. That sounded like there was a clear path forward. He would make this part of his routine, even if he had to deprioritize something else—sleep most likely. He grinned ruefully but was optimistic about his ability to fight whoever was searching for him.

“That sounds great. Do you want to start right now, or should I come to you after my classes tomorrow?"

The elder grimaced. “I am sorry, Jianyi, but you won’t be able to attend classes until you have reached a sufficient proficiency with the technique. You also can’t proceed with opening your dantians; that would make things even simpler for whoever is hunting you.”

He gritted his teeth. It felt like whenever he made the smallest bit of progress towards his ability to finally wield techniques, something set him back again. And not being able to attend classes also meant that he would be falling further behind.

Anyway. There was no benefit in crying over spilled milk. He would do his best to learn the technique as quickly as possible, hopefully be able to train with Minghao, and catch up on the classes once that was done.

He looked towards the elder, keen to learn his first will-based technique.

“Go to bed, Jianyi. Nothing will penetrate my domain tonight; tomorrow morning, we will start the process.”
