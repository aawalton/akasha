There are few things in the world more dangerous than the mad.

Where some speak of a rabid animal, or a cornered beast, I speak of madness. Pain, strife, fear, desperation- all of these can drive a creature to madness, where conventional logic ceases to function and what is left is a series of beliefs and choices and thoughts that make demands of the world in place of that logic.

‘I can’t die here’. ‘I’ll do anything’. ‘I can do this’. ‘I don’t care what it costs’. ‘This can’t be true’.

I have heard worse, but these are common. These are the sorts of thoughts that arise from the chaos of a broken body, a broken mind, a broken world. These are the sorts of thoughts that break reality along the reach of one’s fangs.

A cornered animal is not dangerous because it is cornered. It is dangerous because cornering an animal makes it go mad.

I will fight a hundred men at a higher realm than I with far more comfort and far more confidence than I would fight a single man who has lost his family, his eyes, and his mind. The former, at least, one can do one’s best to predict. I can understand them, for we are alike, even if only in the most superficial of ways. The latter is broken, and the jagged edges could carve shapes entirely new to me. The latter might very well find that they know better than all that is, bereft of the logic and sanity that held them to the world, and while these stars often burn for only a few moments, they burn brighter than any sun that might seek to eclipse them.

I can swordfight a hundred men without ever encountering something I do not understand. One man who has decided that a color is cancerous, on the other hand, is a bit harder to see coming, and much harder to counter. I am not so sane that a sword can kill me, but a color is another matter.

-Words of the Second Blade, Sword Saint Rin Zi, said to have killed his master with a sword made of his own birth

There are people in her temple.

That’s new.

So is the temple. That’s important. The temple is very new. It’s important to remember that because if she forgets, then…

People.

That’s new.

Not all the way new. There were people before. There are people now, maybe. It’s hard to tell. They’re not in the temple, she’s pretty sure. If they were, she would know them more. Maybe. It’s hard to tell.

But there are people in her temple. And they’re new.

“Who are you?”

The first person-people she sees is staring at her. He reeks of sharp edges and muddled flavors, too much blended into a container too small- and blended badly, too, so everything is out of balance and gross. He smells like he’s holding himself together by force, but he also smells like he doesn’t know that’s true, even though he’s acting like it is.

He has a piece of Her.

Not her her. Her. The one that she was, or that was her, or something of the sort. A piece of the thing that came before, made new and distinct and separate and real, as real as she is, growing out of the temple wall.

Anyways, he has a piece of Her, attached at his… hip. That’s the word for that part. Mostly.

He’s said something while she was distracted by remembering important things. She makes her eyes blink and stare at him, because that’s where a lot of the information comes from, and she’s pretty sure people respond when you look at them.

“Who are you?” she asks again.

“I am Geng He,” says the person in her temple. “Are you the spirit of this place?”

Spirit. An interesting word.

“I think so,” she says, because she does think, so it is so.

The person (Geng He, he said he was) hesitates, then bows at the waist to her.

“This junior is ready for whatever trials you have prepared. Whatever test is placed before me, this Geng He swears that he will pass it.”

He believes it, too. She can smell that. He smells like desperation and conviction melded together, sourness and umami and sweetness all made into an unbalanced, slimy mess, which tastes a bit like something has spoiled. It’s tasty.

“Ok,” she says.

He stares at her for a bit. Then a bit longer.

There’s a person in her temple. That’s new.

There’s people in her temple. That’s even more new.

Someone walks around the corner, wearing clothing and with a face (that’s how you know it’s a people, mostly). They see Geng He, and their smell briefly turns a little acidic and a little sour, but not terribly much of either, and they smile rather than growl.

“And here I feared you abandoned us for the temple depths!” says the new person. “A relief, to see we still have some time left as a team. Tell me, did you- “

The new person stops talking when he sees her.

She waves with the stump of her arm.

“Hello,” she says, because she forgot that last time, and greetings are important. “Who are you?”

The new person drops to his knees and bows, lowering his head to the floor.

“Bleeding Mother! Blessed spirit! Ancestral source and mistress of flesh, this lowly Yu Guanyu bows his head before you!”

“Ok.”

That’s a true statement the new person made. He did, in fact, bow his head.

He stays bowing for a while. He seems fine, so she doesn’t really see any reason to bother him.

“Honored spirit… the trials?”

Oh, the first person is talking again. That’s a thing she remembers. Those are words he’s used before.

“The trials,” she repeats.

“Yes, honored spirit. Is there any guidance you might offer? Or are we simply to march into the temple and face what comes?”

“I don’t think so. I don’t really know, though. That’s not really my job.”

Geng He shapes his face into the disappointment/sadness/frustration expression, and his smell matches frustration, so she got that right. She remembered it right. That’s good. She’s getting better at that. Probably.

“…if it is not too disrespectful to ask, honored spirit, what is your job?”

She shrugs, her lone shoulder rising and falling remarkably smoothly. She remembers how to do that, at least. Very useful movement.

“I mostly just build stuff.”

At this, the one that was kneeling raises his head, the smell of him filling with a mixture of lust, desire, and admiration.

“Honored spirit, you mean to say that your own hand has touched the works in this place?”

“No. I don’t have a hand.” She waves her stump to demonstrate. For some reason, this makes both of them flinch, like something heavy just went by right over their heads. They are very light, so maybe they’re just easily affected by movements. At least, they smell light. A lot less heavy than she is.

“Ah… I mean to say, honored spirit, that you had a personal investment, personal attention, in the creation of some of the constructs within this temple?”

She pauses. There were a lot of big words in that whole sentence. She remembers some of them, but most of them take a little bit to fully get back.

When she blinks, there’s two more people in her temple, which is so many people all at once. They’re waiting right next to the first two people, who have names that she can remember, and they’re all waiting for something.

Oh. Right. One of the people asked her a question. She remembers it, and what all the words mean in it. Which is good.

“Yes.”

The first person lets out a little growl, smelling of rage and insecurity and overwhelming anxiety, but the others seem much more calm about her answer. The second person just nods, bowing again, while the two new ones look at her, then at each other, then at her again.

That’s three out of four not angry. Pretty good result overall, probably.

“Why are you here?” she asks, because that seems like a good follow-up to the whole ‘who are you’ thing.

“Ah- apologies, great spirit! We did not seek to intrude upon your temple without permission, but can assure you that we are here with honest motives! Our group found your temple while wandering this hidden land, and seek to face down your trials, so as to properly be worthy of the rewards herein.”

She blinks. That’s a lot of words. She remembers most of them already, at least, but it’s hard to tell all the way, so she does a quick review.

“Ah!” she says after a few more seconds. “Alright. That makes sense. I think. I don’t have any tests, though. Or trials.”

A few blank stares. They mostly smell of confusion, a bit of befuddlement mixed in.

Befuddlement. That’s a fun word to remember.

“Then… what is this? What is the purpose of this place?”

“I live here.”

“...Perhaps the artifacts that you generated. Where are they now? Without a trial, perhaps we can simply take them, if you’re amenable, honored spirit?”

“Hmm.” She thinks about it for a bit. It feels a bit hard to do, because she spends most of her time making things or remembering things and then sometimes experiencing what happens if she remembers the wrong thing. Plus, most of her thinking happens far away. Very far away. Down and down and out and in and away, through all of the corners and pieces, winding out of everything and into itself and then back into her here. So many, many pieces, all thinking, all remembering, all having very mixed success with all of them and-

Oh. They’re still waiting for her to respond.

“No. It’s mine. You can’t have it unless I say so.”

“...Well? Do you say so?”

“But how can I know that you’re someone I want to give or make things for?”

“I… perhaps… if you designed a trial?”

She blinks. Then she smiles, bright and wide.

“That’s a good idea!” she says.

One of the people (the first one? His name… Geng He!) rolls his eyes, the smell of anger turning to violence wafting off of him like bad digestion.

“This is worthless. Whatever this thing is, it’s clearly missing half its mind. I won’t waste more time on this when we can simply head deeper into the temple and find whatever it’s hiding.”

That’s also a good idea.

She opens the floor beneath that Geng He fellow, and he gives out a yelp as he vanishes down a tunnel.

“We meant no-”

The other three, who all seem very nervous now, are even easier to catch. She just has to move the parts of her under and outside and around, and then they slip and tumble because they’re so small, and those larger pieces of her that are outside of everything but herself have swept them down one of the hallways.

This feels… right. Like a good idea. It’s making her feel a bit of a memory, maybe.

The angry one whose name is something or other falls and tumbles apart and comes back together, and he never really stopped being together if you’re really technical about it, until the tunnel opens up. As abruptly as it opened, it deposits him into another chamber, specially prepared.

He goes from moving faster than gravity would allow down a tube to tumbling in the open air towards a series of spikes.

They’re not her best work, seeing as she only built them just now, but even still, they’re fairly decent. When he swings his sword and uses that muddled mess of his Qi to carve apart the first wave, she inverts his direction to the other side, keeping his momentum constant and making every direction hold just as many spikes as the last. She does refine them, though- the second generation of spikes gains an additional layer of fat and muscle beneath the chitin-stone, dulling and slowing his cuts. The third generation has little lines of Qi running through it, and some of them explode if he cuts them too slowly or at the wrong spot.

Other chambers have formed and begun to multiply along where the others got launched too. They all smelled like they were better at not hating each other, so she decided to keep them together, especially since one of them was up in front of the others and speaking for the whole group, sort of. They land in a place she’s curved into a spiral, falling permanently into itself in both possible directions to travel. From the “center”, where they’re standing, a gush of ooze starts to bleed from the walls, drooling over the ground and sizzling on contact with the air.

They start running, which is exciting. They’ll be able to find the crabs sooner that way.

Very useful things, crabs. Leave pretty much anything growing in someplace long enough and it just turns into crab. Take that crab, put it someplace new, and it will just be a different crab after a while. All she has to do is edit the details.

One of the people of the group yells and sprays crimson as she fails to dodge a claw, serrated edges that cut through steel as easily as flesh proving their efficacy against her defenses.

On the other side of things, the angry one is yelling and waving his sword around, which is sort of funny, as any improvements he makes to his technique end up undercut by his own emotions. His Qi is moving as fast as she’s seen it yet, the organ of Her attached at his side slowly gaining toxicity as it’s forced to work harder than it’s supposed to. His sword flashes as he swings it, flavored by force primarily. He also has… not light. Bright. Brightness? That’s a hinky one. And dreams and bones, which…

Well, if it works, it’ll be pretty fun to see.

A wall becomes a ceiling becomes a floor as he tumbles through an ever-increasing cascade of architecture. It wouldn’t be fair if she just made the spikes unbreakable to him, so she spices things up by layering them, hiding traps through slight tricks of placement and perception. He fucks up the first time, and the second, failing to predict or react to the spike behind the ones he carves through, and one of his arms stops working as well as it should- but he gets smarter, some of his anger bleeding away to the smell of focus.

He… could be something. Maybe.

The others also aren’t bad. She might be biased, though. She hasn’t seen anyone in a while, not as this her, not as this part of her, so it’s just nice to see new things sometimes.

One of them has grown several sizes, using her added bulk to block off parts of the tunnels, while the one with cool water hair has drenched her and seems to be using the water as some sort of defensive measure to keep any of the crabs from cutting into her too badly. The last one, the talky one, has some sort of whip, which… yeah, that’s also a part of Her, which is neat. It arcs and slithers and carves through the shells of the swarm, even as the goop behind them continues to eat through her own body on its way down the spiral she has them in.

She tweaks the next generation of crabs, twisting their legs into being a little longer, giving their internal hydraulics a bit more torque, and adding a second set of sub-claws within the main ones. Then, just for fun, she gives them a redundant set of organs on their underside, mirroring them symmetrically so they exist on both sides of their own body.

Huh. She’s doing a lot, very fast. That’s kind of nice. She’s been really slow lately, mostly because remembering is really, really hard without touching any of the-

Ah! Like that! Almost touched one of the-

Evolution is a finicky thing. It’s like crabs. They’re shaped like that because generations of not-crabs did stuff until they realized crab was best, and found all the pieces to be crab.

That’s an encouraging thought. It’s one she holds onto.

The angry one (Geng He! She remembers that) starts using his full body to curve his cuts, relying on his core and legs even without being able to stand on the ground. He seems a bit too overwhelmed to really figure anything out, though- at this point he’s just reacting. He does seem to be expecting the worst, which is good, but he hasn’t figured out any way to land or anything.

He hasn’t given up, though, which is nice to see. He smells of a mix of fatalism and determination, mixed with a healthy dose of spite and barely-present hope. It’s just so very familiar and the memory tickles at her. Recognition crawls out of the back of a mind purposefully misshapen.

A Knife made of End stabs through her.

Far, far away, deep in a place that is outside anything but herself, a series of safeguards kick in. Sequentially, neurological tissue cascades across distances that make very little literal sense, transmitting orders from one part of her to another, until they arrive at the place where the body that just got stabbed used to be.

She’s gotten used to it. There are plans for this sort of thing.

Remembering is hard. All memory has context, after all, and when the context of the memory is yourself, and “yourself” is a thing that is trapped in a recursive death loop, remembering is also dangerous.

It took weeks before she could get more than a single coherent thought together. Since then, she’s had to install so many safeguards, so many mental blocks and interlaced mechanisms to prevent certain trains of thought. Even remembering herself as “she” took almost a month, maybe more, because it was so tied to other memories that are more than toxic- they’re oblivion.

A thousand different systems all activate, in sequences that interlock and sometimes overlap and sometimes even scrape against each other, and they make her be.

A new skeletal system wraps around a shell of gelatin and muscle-tissue that fills with neural mapping, bound to a pre-established format. Consciousness enters a mold prepared for it and shapes itself to the neurology built within it, pre-established thought patterns filling out a cranium with identity and self-awareness-

And then she bumps into herself.

This new part of her turns its head to stare at the old part of her, which stares back. Disconnecting from a node that has touched an oblivion-memory is standard practice, one of the easiest and quickest safeguards she established, so for the first time, she looks at herself with eyes that aren’t her own.

And they look back.

There is a Knife made of End emerging from the chest of the old her, which is the same as the new her. And yet, she did not End.

The knife opens. Just a bit. It starts to peel apart, like a flower, struggling to bloom.

And then it bends the wrong way, and twists, and falls into itself.

And then it’s gone.

And there is just two of this version of this part of her, staring at each other.

Twin mouths open wide and smile with too many fangs.
