If this had been a movie from April’s world, the sun would’ve been rising. In reality, it was still the dead of night, with a long six hours left until dawn.

The unknown soldiers were chasing the Eschal army while Neogon’s forces had decided to defend the townsfolk.

We hobbled back to town, battered and broken, but victorious. Shuffling along clutched in a half hug, it took us nearly twenty minutes to get back to the… drawbridge.

The people behind the castle walls were still exploring in awe, gazing at the new walls like they were something out of a story. I supposed they were.

The drawbridge lowered in staggered jolts. Whoever was running the lowering mechanism was clearly inexperienced. I was a little surprised the bridge had been lifted after half the army had ridden out after me, but apparently whoever Neogon had left in charge didn’t want to take any chances.

The moon actually provided decent light as it rose, shining down on the gleaming castle walls and the new moat that had filled in from the river. The castle loomed over the entire countryside. A part of the hill, but added to it.

“What under the heavens did you do Mera?” Mom asked, when she finally got ahold of herself.

“What I had to do. What about you? Where were you?”

Mom grimaced. “Prison. A wealthy peasant, buying up food in the quantities I was managing? Eventually the guard locked me up. I expected that, but I didn’t expect it to happen right when you needed me. Did you get the food alright? I was still planning to buy so much more, but I was only able to set those eight wagons aside before that happened. When you snapped the second rod I was still in mana cuffs. I’m… so sorry.”

Mana cuffs. I’d never heard of those. Wait, she’d expected to be locked up?

“How did you go from being in Prison to bringing an army of Denarlan City Guards?” I asked wearily.

She was about to answer, but I interrupted with a wince.

“Sorry. I… don’t think I’m in any state to hear what you’re saying. I’m just so fucking glad you’re here. Thank you for saving me. Again.”

She smiled, and gave me a hug. “It looks like there might not have been anything to save if it weren’t for you.”

I put that out of my mind as I walked across the damn drawbridge along with the rest of our limping group. Torchlights glittered along the walls and the town seemed surprisingly bright all things considered.

Soldiers led us in and flanked us on all sides. Some carried the wounded and dead on stretchers.

How many idiots had followed me out there on my suicidal attack? I had so many points. I thought… I thought with that much I could kill her and end this whole damn war.

No. That wasn’t doing it justice. I was blind. Stupid. I’d attacked out of rage and I’d paid the price for it.

I didn’t want to think about it. I was exhausted, and all I wanted was to lay in my bed and sleep. Maybe when I woke up this would all be a dream.

A cheer rose as we entered. Townsfolk were overjoyed as they saw us. I didn’t see Neogon in the torchlight, but I did see Berkenem. He looked tired, but healthy. He’d both survived his capture and managed to reclaim his sword on the battlefield… at night.

I was impressed but I was also aware that I’d pulled an entire fucking fortress out of the ground. If I could have secrets, so could he.

I saw Dobretin standing there. Unlike the cheering villagers, he had a frown on his face.

“Mera. Naska,” he said cordially, raising his voice a little to be heard over the revelry and cheers.

“Oppa,” I replied. He’d been avoiding me ever since I’d decided not to convince everyone to run. Helping where he could, but he hadn’t left with the evacuations, even though he’d clearly wanted to. I thought he probably stayed for me.

“You… I… dammit, I don’t know what to do here. That was you wasn’t it?” he said, gesturing to the wall, then back to the fortress behind him.

There wasn’t much point in trying to hide it now, but perhaps a thin shield of believability was all they needed.

“I may have lied a little about the strength of my talent. It’s Masterful,” I said.

He rolled his eyes.

“I’m not stupid, Mera, and neither is anyone else. You aren’t going to be safe if you stay here.”

“And where do you suggest I go?” I asked. “If I’m not safe here, around the people who owe me every last one of their lives, then where am I safe, Oppa?”

“I…” he seemed short on words.

“This doesn’t matter right now. Did you hear about…” my words guttered and died as I glanced up at the wall. She probably wasn’t still up there. Someone had probably carried her body down.

“Yes… I heard. Omoali seemed more enraged than you, and Mularet no less so. I thank the gods that Eysee wasn’t here to charge out there after the three of you.

Mularet followed me too. That made sense. He was a veteran, and had been on the wall shortly after the Summer of Bone. He didn’t ever really use it, but we all knew he’d been an assassin of some sort before hanging up the dagger to become a carpenter. His talent, letting him blend into the surroundings, was just too perfect for that sort of work. I hadn’t seen him on the battlefield but Mom might be dead if not for Omoali’s wall.

“Are they–?”

“Fine,” he said. “Omoali sprained her ankle and rather than stay out and fight in the fucking sky, Mularet grabbed her and came back to the wall. Got in before that idiot in charge closed up the drawbridge. Honestly! They were retreating! I think the stupid bastard just wanted to play with the new toy.”

I smiled, just a little. By all accounts, tonight had been a victory. If not for Elsee, I might feel that way too.

“That’s good.” I breathed a sigh of relief, happy that I hadn’t lost anyone else that I knew of. Not yet anyway.

“Mera needs sleep,” Mom said, cutting off the conversation. “Is anyone going to notice if we slip back to the house?”

Dobretin nodded. “Probably. Your house might be the first place they look, and I agree. There’s no getting out of here right now. I’ll do what I can but you know that will only stop people for so long. Once the morning comes and we see what she really did…”

He trailed off, and I nodded.

“Tom’s house, then,” Mom said. “I’m sure Irena would allow us to stay there for the night.”

“Might. Don’t know what’s still standing and what’s been pushed aside after that castle sprang up out of nowhere. The hunter’s lodge is a bit close to it.”

Part of me wanted to go to the castle. It was mine after all. I’d built the damn thing. Wouldn’t I be safest there? A bigger part just wanted to collapse right here. My frankly ridiculous mana regeneration was the only thing that had staved off Mana Exhaustion, and I felt nearly dead on our feet.

“Let’s go to Reid’s house,” I said.

Mom nodded and Dobretin stepped aside to let us pass.

Unfortunately, people noticed as we passed. I was limping from a hundred tiny cuts but I didn’t dare believe there was a healer with enough mana to heal a cut right now, let alone the mess that I was. I looked like a half dead wreck, but they didn’t care.

“Elmerina!” someone shouted.

“Bond Crafter!” another followed.

“Pemolar’s Castle!” came another cry as more and more people noticed Mom and I.

They didn’t crowd us. It seemed they could tell through moonlight and the numerous torches that I had no energy to engage with something like that. It also might’ve been that I was a bloody mess.

None of it mattered.

“Bond Crafter! Bond Crafter!”

“Did you see her set the fucking sky aflame?”

“Knew she wasn’t a fucking Elite. Knew it in my bones! Masterful! Right here in our own town!”

“Bond Crafter!”

“There are… so many people here. What did you get up to while I’ve been gone?” Mom asked.

“A lot,” I murmured as my eyes roamed the cheering soldiers and villagers, spotting the outliers. The ones who weren’t shouting. No one impeded our way as we left the wall and made our way into a new town. Walking by our own house, we stumbled away, hearing my name shouted alongside curses to the retreating army.

The street was filled with people. Even past the gates, there were people staring up at the castle.

“The food wasn’t enough,” I said. “We had to ration. Dobretin and I did what we could to get crossbows into everyone’s hands, but to do that I had to make sure they had money. I… got everyone working towards a common goal. They finished the entire wall! Then Neogon’s soldier’s came and–!”

“Neogon is here!?” she interrupted sharply.

I nodded. “Yeah… he was up on the wall last I saw him.”

“That explains why there were so many soldiers. So Neogon was pushed back through the Eastern Woods until he came here. And found you.”

I gave a sheepish half shrug. “He brought almost four thousand more mouths to feed. We won tonight but unless all those troops from up north came with literal tons of food, we’re all probably going to die anyway.”

She patted me on the head, and I felt like everything would be okay. It was stupid and false, but somehow, Mom could still bring out the little child in me.

“Fortunately, that’s exactly what we brought,” she said. “There was an attack on the Duchess. I… don’t really know the details. Hadra was involved somehow, and she got me out of prison before the city guard set off. I rode with them, bringing food as fast as we could.”

“How is Nemmikel?” I asked. “And Uraleka! Is she okay?”

“She… well. She didn’t come back with us. She elected to stay with Hadra for a while, but she’ll return soon. Those two love to hate each other almost as much as they love each other.”

I smiled through a yawn. “Sounds like Hadra.”

We found the Hunter’s lodge at the base of the tower, but it didn’t look the same. It seemed functional buildings that could exist within a castle had been morphed by it in the same way that the bar had. Now, it had stone walls and a floor, but the furniture and the doors were still… roughly where they had been. The only difference was that there was a door leading into the castle proper, and the new accommodations connected to the rest of the castle.

“I really hope Tom and Irena aren’t mad at me for this,” I said.

“They won’t care in the slightest, I’m sure,” she said as we both walked into Reid’s room. “I’ll make sure to get a healer for you as soon as I can find Akkiwa or Normuran. Or perhaps one of Neogon’s soldiers still has some mana left.”

“Thanks… Thanks, Mom,” I breathed, before slumping down onto Reid’s bed.

“Sleep well, you rotten onion. I have a feeling you’re going to need to be at your best in the morning.”

My eyes were already closed. My cheeks were wet, with renewed tears every time my mind drifted to Elsee. Seeing her corpse on that wall.

I pushed it out of my mind. Not now. Not while it was all still so raw. Instead, I focused on sleep. On recovering.

I knew I was getting blood all over Reid’s bed… but it smelled like him. Just a little.

For now, at least, I’d managed to keep this town alive.

Tomorrow… we’d find out if they were willing to suffer a legend.
