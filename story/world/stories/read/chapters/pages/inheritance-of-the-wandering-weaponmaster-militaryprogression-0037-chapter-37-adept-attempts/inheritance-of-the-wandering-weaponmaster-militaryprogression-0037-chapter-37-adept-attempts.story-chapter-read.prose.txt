Amy’s congratulations were immediate and genuine, and Petr’s nod of acknowledgement was curt, but without malice. Ezra swore he could see some measure of frustration there, and given what little he knew about the other man’s efforts in sensing aura to no avail, he didn’t blame him. The thought of being a career soldier, one who’d worked hard for years and seemed to have no ability to grow stronger other than to hone his body, wasn’t a bad idea to Ezra. At least, it wouldn’t have been a bad thought in his mind months ago, before he could see the path to Knighthood.

Now? If he’d been someone struggling like Petr for years, and then some snot nosed kid like him had wandered in and immediately left him in the dust, Ezra knew that he’d be pissed off, at the very least.

Instead of saying anything to Petr, he nodded as Georg’s second in command stepped out. Then, dragging Amy out as well, Ezra began to speak with the other soldier.

“Yes, it’s good, but I’m really damn tired,” he protested, falling back on an old companion that he hadn’t used in what  felt like years. With a calculated step, Ezra got closer to Amy, smiling as he offered an arm to her to escort her out. She smiled up at him and took the proffered elbow as he walked towards his tent.

“So how sure are you that you’re an Adept?” Amy asked. Her face was turned up to his as Ezra used his opposite hand to rub away the blood under his nostrils.

“My inner world’s changed pretty drastically,” he answered. “I’m not ready to promise anyone anything until I can talk with someone who’s at least an Adept themself, but I’d say I’m sure. It’s pretty different, and from what I know, I’ve done it.”

“Wow,” she smiled up at him, and Ezra relaxed a little as he continued on his path. He wasn’t often willing to lean on the good looks he’d been born with, but they were a tool he didn’t mind using when he needed to.

They continued on their path as Amy asked questions that Ezra wasn’t equipped to answer, though it was only a minute’s walk to the tent he shared with the other recruits. Before Amy pulled away towards her own tent, though, she pulled his head down so she could speak directly into his ear.

“Next time you try to trick someone with your looks,” she whispered, “you should do it when you’ve had an opportunity to wash in the past day or two. You smell like a soldier who’s been on the march for two weeks.”

Ezra’s mouth went dry at that, his self-satisfaction burning away with the heat of shame. He didn’t have anything to reply with, and she smiled as she flounced away, winking at him. With a sigh and a shake of his head, Ezra stepped back into the tent, his face burning with shame.

“Was that Amy?” Boyd asked.

“Yeah,” Ezra replied, keeping his tone as even as he could manage.

“What was up? Why did she rush off and then come back with you?” the damned lug insisted. Instead of rising to what he suspected might be bait, Ezra shrugged.

“I ascended to become an Adept, but made a bit of noise as I was doing it. She and Petr came to make sure I was alright.”

“You what?” Boyd demanded, his attention fully on Ezra and forgetting about Amy now. Despite his excitement to feel and see what had changed, Ezra welcomed that excitement since it pulled away from anyone potentially discovering his shame.

“So you can do those spear attacks whenever you want now?” Cord asked, sitting up on his sleeping roll.

“No,” Ezra shook his head. “I don’t know how much my inner world will hold once I’m able to refill it, but I know that I won’t have nearly enough to throw around like that.”

“Will you be able to fly?” Boyd grinned, the smile cracking nearly all the way to his ears.

Instead of answering with his words, Ezra couldn’t hold back the laugh at that question. Boyd’s own face reflected Ezra’s amusement, and he said, “I mean, maybe you could, right?”

“I guess,” Ezra shrugged. “But that’d be the realm of at least a Knight, I guess?”

“What other things can you do with the aura?” Leah asked. “Can you speed yourself? I saw what Artemis did with the water on her shield.”

“Did you think that I’ve had a whole lot of time to try?” Ezra replied. “It’s been a couple of minutes.

And so on the questions continued. Instead of familiarizing himself with every small tidbit of what he was now capable of, he spent the night answering questions from Boyd, Cord, and Leah. Even so, there really wasn’t much that he could say, so it boiled down to repeating the same things, most of which culminated in “I don’t know,” and “I haven’t been able to even try that.”

Before too long, though, they were all exhausted enough that they could get their rest, and Ezra took the time to meditate, if only a little. Now, different from before, the aura that flowed into his body moved easily, following paths into his lungs, through his nose, and even penetrating his skin. The feeling was different, more specific than before. What was before a general ability to sense the aura was now an immediate sense of what he was interacting with. Instead of stretching himself to begin to sense the aura that filled the air, he could not only feel where dense concentrations were but also the ambient varieties that floated by.

Inside the earth, he could feel the difference between stone and sand, soil and ore. That understanding only came after fully losing himself in his meditation, and it wasn’t long after that that unconsciousness claimed him.

Sleep was comfortable, and when the morning came, Ezra greeted his companions as he made his way out to eat his breakfast. As soon as he’d gotten his porridge, he had something very specific that he wanted. Fortunately, he could make a very easy excuse to Georg and his friends that allowed him to set out just a hundred meters or so away from camp without supervision. All he needed to say was: “I just want some privacy to see what’s changed.”

Finally, he had uninterrupted time with Jormun, and as Ezra stretched and warmed up his body, he had to say, “We need to figure out some way to talk when there’s nobody around. I bet that I’m going to get less and less time to myself, and I'd like to be able to actually learn from you when I’m not interacting with other people.”

True, Jormun grunted. You’re going to be surrounded with people before too long. Can you talk to me in your head?

Ezra shrugged, closed his eyes, and thought as “loudly” as he could, You’re a grumpy ghost that looks like a bear.

Jormun didn’t reply, and Ezra cracked his eyes open to look at the long-dead Master. Jormun’s face didn’t shift at all, and he glanced at Ezra.

I didn’t hear anything, he said, though that was obvious by his lack of reaction.

“We’ll have to keep working on it,” Ezra said, shrugging. “I guess I’d like to ask that you give me advice unprompted whenever it occurs to you, then. At least, whenever I’m surrounded by people and unable to speak with you.”

Jormun didn’t answer, just grunting as Ezra sighed and settled himself in a ready position. He was holding his spear and nothing else, and he spread his awareness into his inner world. As he meditated, the storm that he’d thought felt pretty complete in his inner world just the night before had proved to him how little he understood. As soon as he’d ascended to Adept, the swirling mass of wind aura had changed to become a cloud. More correctly, his now solidified inner world was a storm, as he’d thought last night.

Seeing it now, Ezra couldn’t help but laugh at his ignorance.

What had previously been a cloud with wind whipping through was a storm? It was now a raging tempest, winds constantly lashing and tearing the clouds in the center of his inner world to pieces as the storm fought to maintain its form. A constant battle was waged inside, neither side winning as the winds were slowed by the thick clouds, and the clouds were pulled out of a single clump by the winds. What he had ignorantly thought of as a storm only the night before was now a true cyclone, different aspects of aura whirling inside of it. Ezra could feel the wind as the primary, strongest presence, but the moisture that made the clouds was water, and there was another aura that flickered through the clouds too fast for him to be able to sense clearly. His hope was that it was lightning, but he had no frame of reference to tell if that was the case.

You can take as long as you want looking into yourself, Jormun said. I think that’s probably a waste of time, but it’s up to you. I thought you wanted to test what you could do with your aura now that you won’t hurt yourself trying to experiment. And, just in case you forget, I still think you’re a damn fool for pushing yourself so hard to ascend to Adept. Even so, you have the time now, so you better practice, and I’ll make sure that you’re safe.

Ezra could hear the hint of self-reproach in Jormun‘s words, his frustration in having withheld information to Ezra‘s detriment. He couldn’t help himself when he spoke to the ghost, “And why did you set me up to fail? You obviously expected me to.”

It’s something that we did for just about everyone who ascended to Adept, back in the day, Jormun grunted. People need to learn that, even though they may have reached the so-called checkpoint to ascend, they’re almost never actually ready. You need to be a lot more self-aware moving forward, because only you are the person who can tell what you might still be lacking. As it is, you learned the lesson I wanted you to, but it could have been a lot worse for you. I’ll make sure to be better in the future.

At that, the closest thing to an apology he thought he was going to get, the young man nodded, and started to call on the aura in his inner world. Instead of the somewhat reluctant sensation that it had had before, the winds happily obeyed him, and flowed into his body from where they’d raged before.

The difference between fortifying his body with aura as an Initiate versus as an Adept was immediately apparent. Before, it just strengthened him, helping him to recover better, to maybe push himself a little harder, a little longer. Now, the aura remained, where he willed it, swirling as a wind under his skin that demanded release. Without a thought, Ezra grinned, stabbed forward with his spear, and pushed aura into the weapon.

A burst of wind exploded out of the tip of his spear, maybe a meter across and ten times as long. Dust and gravel flew everywhere as leaves were shredded by the gale’s passage. Ezra smiled widely and looked up at Jormun, whose face remained impassive.

“You can’t even be excited for me when I’m learning?” Ezra scowled. “This is fun, isn’t it?”

Yes, experimenting with aura is very enjoyable, Jormun nodded. Now check your inner world’s reserves.

Ezra didn’t hesitate to do what the Master commanded, and he felt the excitement on his face drain away. Over half of his reserves were drained from that single display, and Ezra opened his eyes while looking up at Jormun.

“Did I use more aura than I expected for something so small?” Ezra asked, confused.

No, you’re unpracticed, and you just barely became an Adept, Jormun corrected. A newly ascended Adept can usually infuse aura two or three without fully exhausting themselves, and I’d guess that, since you hadn’t taken the time to refill your inner world completely, you won’t be able to use your aura again until you are able to meditate some more.

“But Helen and Artemis were doing so much,” he protested, though he immediately thought about how little they’d actually been using aura. Jormun said nothing, evidently allowing Ezra to come to that same conclusion himself.

“Most of what they were doing was because they’re well-trained, isn’t it?” Ezra groaned.

Exactly. Once somebody sends to become a Pseudo-Knight, they can shroud themselves which will allow them to defend themselves and make as many attacks as they want for as long as they can maintain the shroud. Until then, even the most accomplished Adept can only manage at most to make a bit over twenty aura-infused attacks or maneuvers before they exhaust themselves.

Disappointed, Ezra settled into a sitting position to start to meditate. After all, he wanted to try at least one more thing before he had to get back to marching. Even if he was a little disappointed, there was plenty he still had to learn, and Ezra didn’t mind if it took a long time—he would continue getting stronger and learn how to use aura.
