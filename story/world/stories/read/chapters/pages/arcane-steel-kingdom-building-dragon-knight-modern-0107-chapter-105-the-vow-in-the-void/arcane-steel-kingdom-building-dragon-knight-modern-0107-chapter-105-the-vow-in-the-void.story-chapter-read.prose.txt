We drifted up toward the top of the tower, Kaelus keeping his wingbeats slow and careful so we wouldn’t rattle the stones too much. The wind howled around the spire, carrying that sharp smell of rain about to fall and something older, like forgotten spells. We were basically ghosts in the half-light, sneaking up on a princess in her own tower like idiots.

Right when we drew level with the highest window, a scream sliced through the noise. Not scared—pure, pissed-off frustration.

“I don’t want to marry that fool!”

Kaelus flinched hard, head snapping back. Brother, he sent, sounding half-shocked, half-offended, did she just turn you down before you even opened your mouth? Also, rude. You're stubborn, yeah. Metal-obsessed, fine. But a fool? Come on.

I shook my head, trying not to make a face. Pretty sure that’s about Ignis, Kaelus.

He gave a slow, knowing nod. Ah. The shiny peacock. Yeah, that makes sense. Total moron.

I leaned in closer to the glass. The room wasn’t the stark, icy office I’d pictured for House Black’s scary heir. It was… a riot. Lace curtains fluttering everywhere, bed drowning in pink fluffy pillows. Looked way more like something Lyra would beg for than a spymaster’s hideout.

Kaelus squinted at the shelf crammed with stuffed animals. Brother, he said, voice dropping to grave-serious, you can marry her, but only if I get some ground rules. I’m a Dragon Prince. I deserve respect. No ribbons. No tea parties.

I reached up and gave his scales a quick pat—they were still warm from flying. You didn’t complain when Lyra stuck that bonnet on you last week.

He huffed out a tiny puff of stardust. She’s tiny. And her head scratches are unreal. Totally different.

We just… hung there. Window latched. No plan.

So, Kaelus prodded, you gonna yell up at her or what?

Yelling at a fifth-floor window already feels creepy enough, I shot back. I’m not adding lunatic shouting to the list.

Knock then. Classy.

Before I could decide whether knocking mid-air was dumber than running away entirely, things inside got worse. Nyxia grabbed a pink pillow and slammed her fist into it like she was training for war. Then she yelled my name.

“Alarion Wight!”

It came out broken, like it hurt to say. She hugged the pillow tight against her chest and slid down to the floor, shoulders shaking.

Kaelus swung his huge head around and gave me the most judgmental stare a dragon can manage. What did you do? She’s crying over you.

I didn’t do anything! Heat rushed up my neck. I haven’t even talked to her in weeks! I’m as lost as you are!

We should bail, he said, shifting uneasily. This feels wrong. Like we just opened someone’s diary.

Nyxia stood up, scrubbed at her face, and started walking straight toward the window. My pulse kicked into overdrive. We were seconds from getting caught like creeps.

Knock! I practically shoved the command at him. Now!

He did—stretched out one claw and tapped the glass. Soft. Knock. Knock.

She froze. Looked up. Tear tracks shining on her cheeks. Red eyes met mine through the pane.

Everything stopped for a second. She saw the silver hair, the crimson armor, the dragon hovering in the dark. I saw her—raw, clutching that ridiculous pink pillow like it could hide her from the world.

Then panic won. She didn’t scream. Didn’t run. She just dropped flat and yanked the pillow over her head like a kid hiding from a monster.

I cleared my throat. Felt like the biggest moron alive. Brain scrambling.

“Uh… could I maybe have a minute?” I called, letting the armor carry my voice through the glass. “I just… wanted to talk.”

A muffled squeak came from under the pillow. “Wait in the dungeon! I’ll be right down!”

Dungeon? I blinked. Was that code for parlor, or did House Black actually do guest receptions in the cells?

Good enough excuse to escape the second-hand embarrassment. I nudged Kaelus. Down. Now.

We spiraled into the courtyard. Torches everywhere. My landing didn’t go quiet.

Guards in black-and-silver rushed out, spears up, faces white. Scared stiff, but they didn’t budge. In their eyes, I caught something else—hope, buried under the fear. They knew my name. They knew what it could mean.

I slid off Kaelus. He shrank to cat-size in a blink and hopped onto my shoulder, looking around. Nice place, he said. Super pointy.

While we waited, I finally glanced at my HUD. Red alerts stacked like bricks.

[Message from: Mother] Alarion! Dinner’s cold! You can’t just vanish from family meals!

[Message from: Mother] If you are tinkering with another bomb, I swear…

[Message from: Lyra (via Patricia)] BROTHER! YOU MISSED THE STORY! The Citrus Dragon just got to the evil castle! What happens next? Also, where is Eggy? He owes me a tea party!

I winced. The Citrus Dragon? Cygnus was going to love that.

Then the serious ones.

[Message from: Patricia] My Lord, you’re inside hostile territory solo. This is not protocol.

[Message from: Bob] 70% war ready. Rescue fleet on standby? You okay?

I typed fast: Stand down. Diplomacy.

Right then, the keep doors creaked open.

Nyxia stepped out into the torchlight.

She’d changed completely. No frills, no tears. Midnight-blue gown that caught the light like deep water, clinging and flowing at the same time. Hair pinned up perfectly. Face calm—mostly. Eyes are still a little pink.

My heart gave an embarrassing thud. No more hiding behind forges and blueprints.

She walked over. Guards stepped aside. Stopped a couple of feet away, chin up, but her hands were locked together tight.

“You have awful timing, Wight,” she said. Voice steady. Fingers not so much.

“I know,” I said, and a small, real smile snuck out. “But maybe it’s the perfect kind of awful.”

I looked at her—really looked—and saw the girl who’d been bleeding in the shadows for her people while I hammered steel and pretended it was enough. For once, I wasn’t calculating an alliance.

I was making a promise.

. . .I cleared my throat—too loud, too rough in the sudden hush of the courtyard. “I’m here about that offer of yours. The marriage one.”

Nyxia’s head whipped toward me so fast I thought she might snap something. Her eyes flicked to the gawking guards, the whispering attendants, and ice snapped into her voice. “Out. All of you. Now. No one stays.”

They scattered like startled birds. In seconds, the courtyard was empty except for torchlight dancing on stone and the two of us staring at each other.

She drew a long breath, shoulders squaring, the Ice Queen mask sliding back into place—though the cracks were still there, raw and visible. “You mean the research materials and the Dungeon Core?” she asked, calm again, like we were back in the meeting room negotiating grain shipments. “Have you come to accept?”

After watching her sob into a pink pillow five minutes ago, the act felt paper-thin. Something reckless lit up inside me. I was done dancing around it.

“No,” I said, voice steady. “I’m here to make a counteroffer.”

Her lips parted—protest, negotiation, whatever—but I didn’t let her get a word out. I went straight for the kill.

“‘But can you expect a girl to make the first move?’” I quoted softly, perfectly mimicking her own voice from the tower window. “‘For a man who is such a genius… can’t he read between the lines?’”

The blush hit her like wildfire. Crimson raced up her neck, flooded her cheeks until she practically glowed against the midnight-blue gown. Her mouth opened and closed. No sound. The Ice Queen had melted.

“I’m reading between the lines now, Nyxia,” I said, stepping closer—close enough to see the pulse hammering in her throat. “And I’m taking the initiative.”

She tried to rally, tried to pull the mask back up, but it was shattered glass at this point. “So… what exactly is this counteroffer?” Her voice shook, just a little.

“Marry me.” The words came out simple, sure, like I’d been carrying them around for weeks. “Not for a core. Not for notes. Marry me, and I swear I’ll end the suffering here. I don’t need House Black’s granaries—I need your people standing with me so I can take the kingdom without turning it to ash.”

Another step. “I’ll give you your Dungeon Core. You’ll build the Mage Tower you’ve dreamed of. You’ll have every page of Vex’s research. But this isn’t a transaction. It’s a partnership. Equals.”

I met her eyes, let her see everything. “Politics, people, the thousand little things that keep a nation from collapsing—that’s your gift, not mine. I’ll handle the steel, the army, the war machines. You handle the rest. And—” I let a rueful half-smile slip out—“I’m going to need help with the… normal stuff. I can be dense. Blind. I know it. I need you to call me on it.”

Nyxia just stared, stunned. This wasn’t bargaining. It was me laying every card face-up, admitting I wasn’t enough on my own, asking her to be the part that made us whole.

I swallowed. Old-world instinct kicked in. Kneel? No—here that meant submission or knighthood. Tes?

[Procedure located. Take her hand. Ask if she accepts you as her husband.]

Simple. I held out my hand, palm up.

“Nyxia Black,” I said, voice low but rock-steady, “will you accept me as your husband?”

Her gaze dropped to my hand, then lifted to my face. Her fingers trembled as they slid into mine—cool skin, strong grip.

“I do,” she whispered.

The relief hit like a punch. Every muscle unclenched at once. But then she frowned, small and fierce.

“And you? Do you accept me as your wife?”

Tes?

[Mutual affirmation required. Say ‘I do’.]

“I do,” I answered.

The air changed.

Something locked into place—sharp, heavy, magical. Not the warm hum I felt with Kaelus. This was iron snapping shut, fate and power binding together in an unbreakable line. The torch flames flickered sideways for half a heartbeat. The night itself seemed to lean in and listen.

[Congratulations, Master,] Tes chimed brightly. [The World System has registered the Vow. Marriage contract sealed.]

Wait—what?

Sealed?

Congratulations! Kaelus yipped from my shoulder, tail flicking. Can we go now? I’m starving.

Nyxia’s eyes went huge. She felt it too—the weight settling over us like a crown neither of us had expected to wear tonight.

We hadn’t just gotten engaged.

We’d just gotten married.

Right here. In a torchlit courtyard. No priest. No guests. No dress fittings. No nothing.

My mother’s face flashed in my mind—calm, terrifying, murderous.

She was going to end me.

I’d come to stop a wedding. Instead, I’d skipped straight to committing to matrimony in the middle of crashing it. I was going to need to hold a proper ceremony later. A massive one. With a cake the size of a carriage and half the continent in attendance. Or else my mother would personally strangle me with a silk napkin.

“Did we just…?” Nyxia breathed, voice high and thin.

“I think we did,” I managed.

A velvet laugh rolled out of the shadows.

“Quite splendid, indeed.”

Morpheus Black stepped into the torchlight, eyes glittering with pure delight. “Though I strongly suggest you depart. Cinderfall officials have heard the dragon. They’re hammering at the gates, and they are not going to be pleased. They will consider this a very public slap.”

He chuckled again, low and pleased. “I have a council to inform. I recommend you fly fast.”

Kaelus didn’t wait for permission. He launched off my shoulder in a burst of starlight and wind, swelling to full size in seconds. The courtyard shook as he landed, wings half-spread, ready.

I turned to Nyxia. “Ready?”

She nodded—still dazed, still flushed—and I lifted her onto Kaelus’s back like she weighed nothing. I settled behind her, arms coming around to secure her against my chest. She was warm, trembling just a little, jasmine and old parchment filling my lungs.

“Hold on tight,” I murmured against her ear.

Kaelus sprang skyward.

We rocketed into the night, wind roaring past, torches shrinking to pinpricks below. Shouts faded. The castle, the kingdom, the scandal—all of it fell away.

Tes—full speed to the Vengeance. Tell them to meet us halfway. We need to figure this out before we land.

We flew hard toward the coast, toward The Aegis, a warlord and his brand-new accidental wife astride a dragon made of stars.

My pulse hammered with panic, exhilaration, terror, joy—all at once.

The war was still coming. My mother’s wrath was definitely coming. The entire political map had just been rewritten in one reckless minute.

But Nyxia leaned back against me, her heartbeat thundering against my arm, matching mine.

I tightened my hold.

The Age of Steel had begun. And I wasn't facing it alone.
