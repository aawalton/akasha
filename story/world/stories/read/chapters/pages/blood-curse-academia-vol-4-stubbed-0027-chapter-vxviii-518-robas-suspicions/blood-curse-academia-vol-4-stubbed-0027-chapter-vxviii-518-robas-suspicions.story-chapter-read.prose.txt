Chapter V.XVIII (5.18) - Roba’s Suspicions

Kizu fidgeted under Roba’s gaze. Her dark eyes bore into him, as it extracting his darkest secrets from the depths of his soul. He was keenly reminded of when this woman had wiped and altered the memories of Inari’s lackeys after their battle on Owl’s Respite. This was a mage trained in the forbidden art of mind magic.

Reminding himself to breathe, Kizu reassured himself that Roba was far too ethically decent to perform unprompted mind probes. After all, she was faculty of Shinzou Academy, a school with an exceedingly high reputation. No parent in their right mind would ever allow a full fledged mind mage to poke around in their children' s heads.

“How did you do it?” Roba asked.

“Do… what?” Kizu asked, playing dumb. He knew exactly what she wanted to know.

“Your scores,” Roba replied dryly.

“Just hard work and dedication. I’ve had a few friends helping tutor me in several subjects. They’ve really given me a leg up in my studies.”

“They certainly have.” Roba pushed forward a piece of parchment with his name and rankings listed. Kizu’s heart leaped as he scanned the numbers.

Combat 244, Astronomy 3, Divination 62, History 449, Politics 501, Rejuvenation and Restoration 712, Conjuring 800, Brewing 1, Numerology 515, Music 198 (Piano), Enchanting 143, Illusion 45, Elemental 132.

A smile cracked his lips as his eyes locked in on the one score he’d been hoping to see improvement in. One test, and suddenly he was one of the academy’s top 3 astronomy students. He’d felt confident after finishing the paper test earlier that day, but this confirmation was beyond his wildest hopes. And it was obviously what Roba was questioning him about.

“Did you cheat?” Roba asked plainly.

“No,” Kizu said. “There was a James golem there watching me the entire time. How could I cheat?”

“There are a few methods. They might be challenging, but you have a unique set of resources at your disposal that few at the academy can match or plan around.”

“What sort of methods?” Kizu asked, intrigued.

Roba met his eyes with a blank look. “Do you truly think me so dimwitted as to answer that question?”

“I promise that all tests I’ve completed at the academy have been based around my own knowledge and skills. I’ve used a lot of different resources to gather information, but it’s all mine now. Ask me any question on that test and I can give you the answer. Give me next week’s test at this very moment and I’ll score just as well as this week.”

Roba’s gnarled old hands clawed back the piece of parchment. She frowned down at it before depositing it in a drawer.

“A mind mage,” she said.

Kizu’s heart skipped a beat. “What do you—”

“That is one such way that everything you said could be truthful. A talented mind mage could insert specific memories. Take them from someone else and either transplant or copy them into a new host. You are young, barely having reached proper adulthood earlier this month, and with youth comes brash and idiotic decisions. Mind magic is an extremely complex and dangerous field of study. You, the subject, will be completely oblivious if the mage chose to insert other consents or memories into your sub conscious. You question reality and everything in your life. Mind mages drive their subjects mad. If you are using a mind mage to assist with your studies, I will help cleanse you and rectify your poor decision making. But you need to be honest with me and speak up.”

Kizu licked his dry lips. That was scarily close to what had happened with him and Zayne, save for it was their very souls involved. That… could be even more dangerous than a mind mage in some ways.

“I’m not using any sort of mind magic to help me in my studies,” Kizu answered honestly. “Though… I do suspect I have had my memories tampered with in the past. It was before my time at Shinzou Academy though.”

Roba leaned forward, her eyes losing a bit of their hardness. “You mean the witch who raised you. Yes, I absolutely believe that she altered and blocked your memories. I’ve suspected it ever since your arrival here.”

Kizu jolted upright, as if electrocuted. “You have? What do you mean? Why?”

Roba chortled. While his shock was genuine, his initial ploy to change the subject distract her from his sudden rise in rankings had clearly worked as well. The conversation had moved on.

“Kaga Kizu. I have met many witches from around the world. I’ve even been invited to join covens, if you can believe it.”

Kizu did believe it. Roba was a hag who’d fit right in with them. Though, admittedly, she was far more lenient and borderline kind to some students like Evie.

“When you arrived on campus, I expected a headache of hexed koi fish, brewing atrocities, and threats of cannibalism. And yet, when I met you, you appeared downright civilized. Ignorant? Certainly. But nothing like the savages I know from the coven in the Hon Basin. After living alongside the most brutal and dangerous woman in their number, you were almost undoubtedly altered mentally to allow you the sanity to fit amongst your peers.”

“If you… if you knew, why didn’t you ever say anything?”

“For what purpose?” Roba waved away his indignant look. “The only thing that would have done is cause you to question yourself and potentially seek out those missing memories. Nothing good would have come from it.”

“It was still my right to know! It’s my mind!”

“So it is.”

Kizu took a deep breath. “I’ve seen a version of myself with those memories and I don’t want to end up like that. I know the dangers of unlocking those memories and I have absolutely no plan to let them overwhelm my personality.”

“I am glad to hear that.”

“But… if someone attacks me mentally. They could easily uncover those. It’s an exploitable weakness. I need some way to counter that.”

Nodding slowly, Roba considered Kizu. “That is an understandable fear. Fine. I will teach you a mental skill. A way to section off your mind.”

Kizu blinked. That wasn’t at all the direction he expected this conversation to go when he first walked in for his meeting today. “You’ll teach me mental magic?”

“Hardly. Mostly this is a meditation technique that only involves the smallest splash of mental magic. It requires so little that even if you lack a decent affinity with that branch of magic you should still be capable to make up for it with raw willpower.”

Roba picked up a timepiece from on top of her desk. Kizu could hear it tick as she examined it. She then glanced over to the side at the glass wall. A purple jellyfish floated on the other side but the old woman’s eyes seemed to go beyond that.

“I am afraid our discussion went longer than initially planned. I will tutor you in the technique, but today I have pressing matters that need to be attended to. I also will need to decide how best to approach this topic. I will also assign you a useful book to study.”

“That’s completely fine,” Kizu said quickly. “I’d be incredibly grateful for any sort of mental counter.”

“Good. Now a bit more housekeeping to take care of.” A wave of her hand and a quill began scribbling notes on a scrap of parchment. “The headmaster believes that your dagger friend has successfully squashed the soul of its victim into mush.”

“Sojan has complete control of the Dragon?” Kizu leaned forward, alert. His eyes darted over to the note the quill wrote. It looked like directions.

“Yes. You have clearance to visit. But be warned that we set down certain safeguards in the area. Jumping to the location will send out alerts. As will any particularly powerful spells. Gates included. If you want to visit, you’ll need to rely on your own two feet to get you there.”

“What about leaving? Can I jump back to the academy?”

“Removing yourself from the site is perfectly safe. Headmaster Ballarfulur stops by every two or three days to check in as well. Should you fear traveling through the forest, he has offered himself as a guide.”

Kizu let out a deep sigh of relief. He’d finally get a chance to speak to Sojan again. Maybe the dagger could answer a few of his questions.

“3805.” Roba set down the timepiece and met his eyes once again. “That is your current total number when you add up all your rankings. Keep your secrets for now, but I promise you I will discover how you’ve achieved your current unfathomably high ranks. I believe the headmaster promised you information once you passed the 1000 mark? You still have a long way to go before you reach that objective. Especially should you decide to continue learning spatial magic. I look forward to many more meetings with you, Kaga Kizu.”

With that dismissal, Kizu stood and bowed to the old woman. Then he exited her office, his mind aflutter with new possibilities.
