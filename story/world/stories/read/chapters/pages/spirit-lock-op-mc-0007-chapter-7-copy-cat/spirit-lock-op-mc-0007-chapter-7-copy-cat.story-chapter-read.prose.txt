That was the fourth time Rosen’s spirit disappeared, and it was always at 7:00 PM—in the middle of his training time. It only lasted fifteen minutes, but he couldn't afford to waste any time.

Hayden was coming.

It sounded stupid to say it that way, but the melodrama was justified.

Hayden wasn't just talented: he was suddenly competent, dedicated, and charming when it suited him. He was suddenly popular, so people talked about him, and when people talked about him, they talked about his bully—Rosen—and spoke about suspicious rule changes. At any moment, his supporters could band together and demand an investigation.

The walls were closing in—and the stakes were too high.

If Rosen didn’t regain admittance to Junolyx, his father would disinherit him and give the family assets to his sister. It wasn’t a problem before; Rosen was trained in both martial and spirit arts. He had a C-rank spirit—a Rank 2 catalyst. He was overpowered at a remedial charity school—but now he could lose or be exposed at any moment.

Rosen needed to fix his situation—immediately.

He paused and wrote a group message to his “friends.” The message was humiliating, but he chose to send it anyway.

Twenty minutes later, Rosen sat on a training room table in front of the group.

“Hayden Reskco,” he said. “I want videos of his training. I want videos of him sleeping. I want videos of him…” He paused, realizing how creepy he sounded. “Conversing with people. I want to know everything about him, and I'll be rewarding you all with hard credits. Do you understand?”

Rosen felt like a low-time street boss ordering his friend group around, but he didn't have time to worry about his ego. So he paid them and sent them out to do his bidding.

Thankfully, students had already been recording videos of Hayden, so his group sent him some immediately. Or, perhaps, unthankfully was the right word. Because the first video he saw was disheartening.

“He runs at five am…” Rosen muttered with depleted eyes.

Rosen loved sleep. Yearned for it, even. Despite that, he went to bed, forced himself up—albeit at six—and ran the same distance as Hayden.

“What now?” Rosen asked between deep breaths, addressing the underling he paid to run with him.

“He…” The underling took a deep breath and bent his back. “Works out at… the gym.”

“Oh, thank God. Something I'm actually good at.”

“Yeah, but we should probably wait.”

“Why?”

“You haven't watched the videos?”

“No, I can only do so many POVs. They make me nauseous.”

“Well watch the two I sent you.”

Rosen reluctantly opened a video, seeing the world through another person’s eyes. He found himself in the gym, surrounded by people.

“Whoa, Hayden’s rockin’ plates!”

Hayden was at the bench press, pumping iron as spectators laughed.

“So?” Rosen asked. “They're plates. And he looks like he's struggling.”

“Yeah. That was two days ago. Watch the second one. It’s from yesterday.”

Rosen opened another video, and the POV switched to another day at the gym. It showed a man walking up to Hayden at the leg press. “I hear you can bench plates now.”

“I can bench two,” Hayden said.

“Yeah, that’s how it works.”

The onlookers laughed.

“No, I can bench double plates,” Hayden said. “Four in total.”

The gym fell silent as the man walked up to Hayden. He leaned in and said, “Bullshit. You were rocking single plates yesterday. Don't lie to me.”

“I don’t care if you believe me.”

“Yeah, but I do. ‘Cause I hate liars, and that’s a lie.”

Hayden left the leg press. “You know—there's nothing I hate more than people calling me a liar. If people want to do that...” He lifted his wrist and tapped his bracelet. “They have to pay for it. So how about a bet? If I can't bench double plates, I'll give you one hundred credits. The same is true in reverse.”

The man hesitated.

“What?” Hayden mocked. “Aren’t you confident? Or are you accustomed to arguing over nothing?”

Rosen already knew what was about to happen, but he watched the scene play out like an impending space shuttle collision. The man accepted the bet, and then a dozen people watched as Hayden put double plates on the bar—and then repped it ten times with ease.

“Bullshit,” Rosen said. “This has to be altered.”

“That’s what a lot of people thought,” the underling said. “So they made the same bet. I think three people’ve lost it already. Point is, I'm not sure if you want to go right now. I think Hayden’s there. That said, I don’t know if that matters. Just letting you know.”

Rosen ran his fingers through his sweaty hair. “Whatever. I’m way too hungry for this.” He stretched. “Let’s get something to eat.”

Rosen wasn't even hungry; he just didn't want to try benching double plates. So he made his way to the dining hall, unaware that Hayden had finished his workout early and was already there.

Hayden had actually been there for a while, replenishing his calories since he was burning a dangerous amount. It was meant to be a functional event, but it had turned into a spectacle.

As he bussed his latest tray and joined the line, he heard someone scream, “He’s going for fourths! I can’t believe it, he’s actually going for fourths!”

Another said: “This is his sixth. You showed up late!”

The server stared at him bug-eyed when Hayden returned.

“Is the dining hall still open?” he asked.

“Depends…” She looked at his scrawny body. “Are you going to be okay if it is?”

“I appreciate your concern, but I assure you—I’ll burn this off by lunch.”

“I can’t tell if you’re serious.”

“Neither can anyone else. That’s why I can afford this.”

She reluctantly let him tap his bracelet to pay for an extra serving.

“Thank you.” Hayden accepted the food and turned around, only to find himself face-to-face with Lina.

“Lina!” Hayden said with a sarcastically cheery smile. “I was just about to eat. Would you like to join me?”

His words caught her off guard, so she just stared dumbly. Then, she realized everyone was staring at her, and her cheeks turned rosy.

“With you?” Lina snapped.

“Oh come now. You act like I asked you out.”

“It’s pretty much the same thing. No!”

Lina pushed past him, leaving him face-to-face with Kayline. The two stared at each other blankly as onlookers in the cafeteria circled them like a roaming pack of hyenas.

“It seems you can smile,” Kayline said.

“It seems you can talk,” Hayden said. “I was starting to think there was a rule preventing you from speaking to students.”

“No. I was just watching.”

“Did you like what you saw?”

She shrugged playfully. “Not bad.”

“I'm glad. It was nice speaking to you.” He walked past her, so she whipped around to face him.

“Wait!” she said. “Aren’t you going to invite me to eat, too?”

Hayden turned back. “I don’t tease people who respect me.”

Kayline folded her arms. “Even if they want you to?”

Hayden smiled mysteriously and walked away.

Everything about Hayden's behavior left Rosen livid—and jealous. It made him wish he had never gone to the dining hall. In fact, the whole reason he went to the hall was to avoid Hayden. Instead, he had to watch Hayden embarrass Lina and flirt with Kayline. Assuming that was “flirting.” It could have been a genuine dismissal, and somehow, for reasons Rosen couldn't put into words, that was even worse.

Rosen was pissed—and he simply refused to keep copying Hayden for the day. He needed to cool off, so he bought individually wrapped food and went back to his room to play some video games. He'd hit the gym later, and everything would be fine.

It's not like it would be a huge loss, anyway.

Hayden worked out in the morning, but that was it for real training. After that, he just spent two hours throwing simple punches and kicks before taking his rat on a walk through the forest. He retired to his room after that and didn't come out.

It would be a cake walk.

Or not.

The next morning, Rosen ran at six. Hayden was still at the gym, so Rosen ate breakfast and then hit the gym. The next hour was a free period to train his spirit before simple punches and kicks. The day went just fine until the punches—that's when the massacre began.

Rosen thought it would be easy. He had been in martial arts off and on since he was a kid, and he was fit, so it couldn’t be too difficult to just punch for an hour. He was wrong. Thirty minutes later, his lungs burned, and his arms trembled.

He does this for an hour? Rosen silently cried.

Exhausted, he switched to kicks to recover.

That was worse. Far worse.

He did the first fifteen without issue, but his legs trembled, and he lost balance after that.

“What the fuck’s wrong with this guy?” he cried aloud.

There were seven more days of this routine—and Rosen hadn't even completed a full day. That fact left him with a lingering feeling of despair. The tournament was rapidly approaching, and Hayden was slowly becoming an indomitable figure in Rosen's mind. It shamed Rosen to admit it, but he was starting to dread Hayden, dread him like cultists dread the return of ancient deities.
