Graciously leaping from door to door as if they were frogs living inside a tale. Vela, not complaining once, kept up with Harry, his robe dancing to the wind.

Harry knew the mysterious man came from a monastery and, for an obscure reason, had an obsession with fire. Maybe not in a pyromaniac way, just a romantic one. Although, it all begins as a simple romantic interest, doesn't it? The Locksmith thought to himself as they once again approached an empty cabin.

No guards. No cultists. No prisoners. The young thief added inwardly they explored the abandoned, but cozy, cabin. The beds, with their uniform, green-striped mattresses, looked made. No sign of rushing or dirty stains; the place's organization was immaculate.

This was the fourth cabin they had gone to since Manes, and not a sign of the prisoners or of Brun and Prism. Had they gotten lost or been captured?

"Are you all right, Mr. Locksmith?" Vela asked as he slightly turned his head, "Don’t worry, I’m sure we’ll find them in the next one."

"That's not the only problem," The Locksmith frowned, "Why are they all empty? It seems that we're always arriving late to a party, except there was no sign of one whatsoever in the first place! I haven't seen this Shepherd or any supposed cultists, besides the few dancing around the fire. Since they're dominating this floor, it would make sense if there were at least a few guards in each cabin. Are they really this unconcerned about people using their floors or is this a trap?"

"Maybe they didn’t think that far," Vela countered, "Their motives and moral stance, for example, are still unknown. The only contact we had so far was with Manes, and he wasn't part of them. In my experience, cults are rarely aggressive or even sacrificial."

"But why kidnap Brun's father and Goldman?" Harry inquired. "Maybe we should just go to the boss man himself."

"...Do you mean heading to the church?"

"Yeah, why not?" He looked through the window, the mask staring right at him. "Brun and Prism said we should probably converge on the church, right?"

"Are you sure about that?" Vela replied, taking a step forward and touching Harry's shoulder. "We are in enemy territory, Mr. Locksmith. If anything, we should be going back and hoping that Brun and Prism weren't caught. I do not feel safe intruding."

"Don't think about it like intruding; we'll simply be unsolicited visitors." Harry grinned. "Now come on. I'm dying to meet them."

Going behind the church, Harry and Vela found a simple hatch, leading to an underground area, just like in the cabins. The stone slabs, adorned with extreme detail, compelled Harry to know more about the church's astonishing architecture. As if on purpose, the prayer palace was placed right in front of the stretching flame, the mask in front of it silently watching the dancing fire.

It felt abnormally cold, though, which made Harry wonder if there were any other materials in the mixing or if this was another one of the Tower's tricks.

"Unlocked?" Harry nudged the hatch lightly with his foot. "If they were here, they would have shown up by now..."

Vela, almost as if by a supernatural instinct, turned his head toward the campfire in the middle of the camp and saw a terrifying vision; all the hooded figures were staring right at them.

"Mr. Harry." Vela touched his shoulder while not taking his eyes off the fire.

"What is it..." When he turned to Vela, annoyed, he understood what the candle man was saying. That was a bad idea; it was time to live.

Before they could, however, they heard a sound coming from the basement; someone laughing. It was a man. No, it was a beast. The voice cackled like gravel sliding down a hill. When they got near the door and opened it, the laughter became even more hysterical. Suddenly, it stopped. The... thing heard them.

Thump! Vela's body collapsed onto the floor with his candelabrum. Before Harry had time to react, he was also drowned in the penumbra of the night. His eyes were drenched in the dark until he was no more.

Imagine being hangover whilst also being carried around by a high velocity horse inside a volcano; that's how the Locksmith felt. For an insignificant moment, Harry regained consciousness. That moment, however, was one of the worst of his life. His throat was scratchy, his cheeks felt hot, and his head felt... light? It seemed counter intuitive, considering the state of the rest of his body, but Harry soon realized what it meant.

In a surge of senses and emotions, Harry Bockart, with great effort, managed to open his eyes. The sound around him, however, was no longer muffled as before.

A strong sense of alcohol intruded his nose as he closed his eyes again, inhaling it as if he were out in a green field on a summer day. Fuckin' hell... I miss a beer. Like a cellar repurposed for a dungeon, several barrels of wine and booze were stored in the corner to his left. The cobblestone walls were dirty, and the arch they formed over Harry threatened to collapse at any moment.

This damn tower. There's always something... He thought as he involuntarily laughed aloud, his eyes consumed by the light like a moth.

"...He's awake!" Prism's voice could be recognized immediately after his laughter. "Harry, can you hear me?"

Two chairs were positioned diagonally to his left. Sitting in them were Prism and Brun, the latter clearly irritated about something. He shifted his wandering eyes toward Prism and felt the light shift with them onto her beautiful, radiant face. He had never noticed how magnificent the girl really looked. Her red hair seemed to reflect autumn itself. Like a drunk, he gave her a smile with his crooked glasses and messy hair.

"You're very pretty, you know?..." Hearing this, the laughter of another woman could be heard.

"W-Why are you saying that!? Don't make it weird!" The woman replied, making Harry feel confused. Although Prism was there, it wasn't the woman he glamorized.

Wait, Prism doesn't have red hair. Is that...?

"You imbecile!" Prism snapped, her arms pinned to the chair. "It was a fucking trap!"

"I told her we couldn't trust any retard who has an Emblem." Brun said, looking at a third girl sitting opposite her.

While they continued yelling at him, the girl in front of him only smiled. About his age, she had long red hair and wore a vest and a poet shirt like a waitress. When he looked closely at the woman, a rush of adrenaline ran through his body as he grimaced.

"...You!" He silenced the screams of the other two. His babbling voice made him sound like a drunkard. "You're stalking me, is that it? Even here, in the fucking Logos Tower? Damn it, I can't believe this..."

"You're the one following me, you creep!" The woman yelled with the same intensity, both of them writhing in their chairs so violently it was a miracle they hadn't broken them yet, "I've been here way longer! I am your elder. Deal with it."

"What?" For some reason, he was shocked, "I can't believe they threw you in here as well... I've always been the most wanted, it doesn't make sense to go after a weak thief like you."

"Weak?" She gasped and tilted her head slightly, "Just so you know, I was on the cover of the last issue of the Traveling Banker after the Carthos museum heist!"

"Oh, spare me..." He laughed shamelessly, "If a beggar trips in the street, he'll be on the front page of that newspaper anyway—"

"Wait," Prism said, "You two know each other?"

"No!" They both answered at the same time.

"She's an imitation, a cheap copy of me—" Harry said with a competitive smile.

"If the copy is one of us, then it's you! Hey, did you know I once caught him using one of his doors to pee? HAHAHA. He opened one in mid-air and kept opening and closing it to try and hit a bird like it was a game—"

"Shut up!" Brun finally proclaimed, only then did the two fall silent. "Damn it, I can't stand two of you... Harry, you've clearly already had contact with Miss Madeline, which saves us some time—"

"Miss? I've seen that woman eat horse meat!"

"It only happened once."

"Whatever," Brun dismissed with her hand, "Now that you're here, I need you to help us escape and rescue my father and Goldman. Vela was also captured, so we'll have to go get him."

"...They knew we were coming, didn't they?" Harry said, his tone serious.

"Yes." The older woman replied after sighing deeply, "But we wouldn't have been caught if Prism hadn't turned off her Emblem."

"I thought they wouldn't be able to see us! It's really exhausting to keep it, you know?" She said.

"I... I'm sorry."

She only wants to save her father. Harry thought to himself, still amused by the idea of a woman old enough to be his grandmother having a father younger than her.

The young thief didn't know exactly how to deal with a group. Seeing Madeline after so many months of isolation reminded him of his time stealing tomes and documents from royalty. Being alone had its perks, of course, such as having to report to no one and a sense of freedom most couples can't have. Friendship and love had to be overwhelming in times, wouldn't it? Having to maintain a social life was so draining, and that was coming from a capital known thief. Even so, a part of him enjoyed this... temporary group-up.

“Why don’t you try cutting the ropes by opening a door between them?” Prism suggested. “Didn’t you say you can decide the size of the doors?”

"He can't create passages within an already filled space," Madeline said without her mocking tone. When everyone looked at her, she frowned, "What? I've seen him screw up many times... It would be foolish of me not to see his obvious weaknesses."

Damn it, you were studying me? He added internally while smiling.

"She's right, unfortunately." Harry said, "But since you're so eager, I can also do this."

At that moment he threw himself backward with the chair as a door was created. Harry then appeared from the ceiling as he fell onto the floor with the chair. One girl was wide-eyed, one was laughing, and one closed her eyes and shook her head, disappointed.

“And what if they heard you?” Brun said calmly. “Did you think about that? You just made a hell of a racket breaking that chair.”

“It doesn’t matter.” Harry answered while untying his ropes. “If they tricked us earlier, it means they knew we were coming. If they put me here, they certainly knew how easy it would be for me to get out of the chair, even if I almost broke my ankle… By the way, is this the underground of the church? For how long have you been here?”

Harry then got up, and while waiting for their answers, began untying Madeline, Brun, and Prism.

Reaching towards Madeline's hands, he felt her pulse quicken when he touched her. The woman's palms were warm as if she had slept 20 hours straight. "Get on with it!" She replied, to which he quickened his pace and let her out. Fuckin' hell. I said she looked pretty... Well, it was only a compliment, right? No need to feel anxious...

They were... what was the word? "Frenemies"? Yes. That was what the Locksmith thought. But what about Harry? The young thief thought she was reckless, but also had self control. She was a fierce fighter, but also a skilled painter. Madeline was an artist, and Harry respected that. Maybe that was also what led to their mutual understanding. Maybe that was what led Harry to say she looked pretty. Maybe that was all his anxiety speaking.

"When we got here, Madeline was already tied up," Brun answered. "It's been a few hours. We woke up before you, but not by much. If I had to guess, I think we're near the end of another cycle. Maybe a few hours left? I don't know..."

"What are you doing here, anyway?" he asked Madeline while frowning. "Are you with them?"

"Not anymore." She replied. "I've been trying to find out the hatch to the third floor for a while now... I had to, unfortunately, mingle with the weirdos in their nest. Trust me when I say it; they are fucking crazy. Especially Conrad."

"Conrad?" Harry asked, vaguely remembering the name. "That's familiar..."

"He has two Emblems." Madeline blurted it out, promptly capturing the attention of everyone in the group.

"...Are you serious?" Prism was the first to say something. "How does one... How can it happen?"

"It comes with its sets of... problems, to say the least." Madeline continued. "I never interacted with him, personally, but if he's a third of what his followers say he is, then we're doomed. Conrad is big, okay? Like, from the shadows, he looks like a fucking golem."

"Let's get out of here first, what do you say?" Keeping the smile, he said to the girls while tidying up his tie and cleaning his glasses.

The Locksmith ran his hand carefully along his waist, feeling his journal. Thank God, it's here...

"You mean saving my dad, Goldman, and Vela first... right?" Brun raised an eyebrow.

Shit. Can't we just keep going? Harry sighed deeply. Well, maybe the commander was right; I'm going to need all the help I can get if I want to get to the end. Just hope it doesn't backfire.

"Of course." He replied with a smile. "So where are the booze-guzzlers?"
