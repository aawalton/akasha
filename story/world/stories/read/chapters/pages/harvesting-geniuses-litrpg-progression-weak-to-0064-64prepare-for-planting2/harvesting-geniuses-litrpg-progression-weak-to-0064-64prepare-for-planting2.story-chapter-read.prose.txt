Amid the veil of fog covering the cursed forest, a two-legged creature stood with branches sprouting above its head. Its frame was slender, lacking the thick muscles of other Eltharoks. Its eyes pierced through the milky white fog toward the village that had once stood abandoned and empty.

“The humans have begun to gather.”

A figure beneath a black cloak floated against gravity, nearly blending into the shadows of the great trees so completely that no one would notice it. The body was thin, with five-fingered hands suited for grasping. Nothing about it resembled a monster—except from the neck up, where there was a giant pumpkin head glowing green. It exhaled steaming breath, surrounded by a sinister aura so unnerving that even monsters avoided coming near it.

“The former Lord of the Mountain failed. Not only did he fail to complete the task His Majesty entrusted to him, he even blundered and offered up his life to make the Heavenly Demon stronger. That is unforgivable. He can atone only with his soul.”

“I already knew the former Lord of the Mountain was a worthless bastard.”

“Do not take him as your example. Do not underestimate the enemy. I will return to the inner forest. It seems an intruder has successfully entered the inner zone.”

“A human broke into the inner forest? Ridiculous.”

“And yet it happened. More than that, the Witch says it has found the sacrifice. I fear it may cause trouble and affect the progress of the ritual. I will find it quickly and cut off its head before it can contact the other humans. Humans are troublesome when they gather together.”

“Do not worry, Lord Jack. Leave it to me. I will restore the name of the Lord of the Mountain.”

The new Lord of the Mountain made his vow to Lord Jack, but when he looked up, Lord Jack was already gone. The new Lord of the Mountain leapt from branch to branch, drawing ever closer to the abandoned village.

It began surveying the surroundings, wanting to know where the humans were hunting. Normally, humans who fell into this place either starved to death or killed one another. Since the day it was born, it had never seen a human society living in such harmony. Why had they not starved yet? Why had they not turned on each other?

The Lord of the Mountain began searching for answers. At last, it climbed a tall tree and looked down. Less than two kilometers from the village lay a stretch of land about fifteen rai wide. A group of humans held the farming tools of the cursed villagers. They were digging the soil into ponds, plowing and dividing it into paddy rows.

“What does this mean, you humans? What are they doing? Leveling the soil? For what purpose?”

Suspicion took root in the Lord of the Mountain’s mind. It had to know. It needed to satisfy its curiosity. Though it could guess at many possibilities, it wanted the truth. Could it be connected to the way human behavior had changed?

The Lord of the Mountain kept watching. It was not only human behavior that had changed—even lowly creatures like Pumpkin Heads and Eltharoks had vanished from the surrounding area. Within a radius of forty kilometers, not a single monster wandered anywhere.

It was unnatural… the absence of Eltharoks would have been one thing, but the total lack of infected villagers was far too strange. In the end, it yielded to its curiosity.

“Damn it, why the hell has my life come to this?!” Peter wailed. He and the few companions who still followed him trudged along, searching for cursed fruit to keep themselves alive. It had been a disastrous decision. He had only slipped away to find something to eat rather than lie there waiting for death, but when he came back, the camp was gone.

The camp was empty. They wandered in the fog for ages before finding torchlight. Along the way, they had to fight monsters. Some were wounded, some died, until only a handful remained.

The survivor community in the village seemed willing to accept everyone, but it had one strict rule written plainly: cursed fruit was absolutely forbidden.

Any cursed fruit would be confiscated and exchanged for credit. With credit, people could trade for supplies later. Utter nonsense. Trade food you can eat now for something you do not even know when you will get to use? Completely absurd. Peter’s stomach growled. He did not know why, but even when he stuffed himself full of cursed fruit, he never truly felt full. Still, whatever. If he could not feel satisfied, that was fine—as long as he did not starve to death.

“Who the hell would go along with people like that, right? Even if we do not stay in the village, we can survive on our own. Right?” Peter shouted for agreement as always, but unfortunately, what followed was not what he wanted to hear.

“Peter, you’re not our leader. And I think splitting off is stupid. If they won’t let us keep the fruit, then fine, we won’t keep it. But we can still stay there. Work with them.”

“Hah! And what if we get caught eating it, you idiot? That village will collapse sooner or later. Once they run out of food, they’ll lose their minds and start killing each other. It has to happen. Those fools are staking their lives on empty hope. Why bother growing crops when there’s food everywhere here? Stay with them and you’ll just get caught in the fallout, get it?”

Peter barked at everyone following him. They all lowered their heads.

“What if that doctor guy actually succeeds in growing something?”

“So what if he does? That has nothing to do with us.”

“With us? No. It definitely doesn’t mean me. If I had a choice, I wouldn’t want to eat this stuff either.”

“Yeah, yeah. I’m eating it because I need to stay alive, not because I want to. This is the kind of stuff that kills you faster.”

“You idiots! Falling for some moronic scammer. If you get fooled by an idiot, that makes you even stupider than he is.” Peter’s eyes were bloodshot as he jabbed fingers into his followers’ foreheads, but no one resisted. Peter was the strongest power-user among them. No one knew why. Was it because he had eaten the most cursed fruit? Or because he had already had a good foundation of power from the beginning? No one knew. All they knew was that Peter had developed the most out of any of them.

They had to obey Peter. It was survival instinct.

“Hey, Peter, maybe we shouldn’t go deeper into the fog,” one follower warned.

“If we don’t go in, then how are we supposed to find fruit to eat? Those idiots have already taken all the fruit back to the village. Hah. Taking fruit that sells for a good price and trading it for that stupid credit no one even knows how to use. In the end, it’ll all just end up piled up with that whore Nephrin anyway.”

Peter led his followers into the white mist. It was dangerous, but areas with fog were especially rich in cursed fruit.

“Whoa! Look at this. It’s huge. It’s gotta be sweet as hell.”

“Don’t go too deep, you bastards. The deeper you go, the more likely you are to run into that deer demon. Even I won’t be able to protect you then,” Peter shouted at his followers.

But they either did not hear the warning, or pretended not to. They kept pushing deeper, heads down, harvesting cursed fruit without even looking up. Peter had no choice but to chase after them.

“What an interesting conversation. Pitiful lowborn human.”

The voice that intruded into Peter’s head made his scalp go numb. It was not a directionless sound. Peter slowly glanced toward the source.

“A deer?! Damn it, this is bad.” Peter reached for his axe, but then—

“Put down your weapon. It irritates me.”

The Lord of the Mountain’s voice boomed directly inside Peter’s skull, so loud he let go of the axe by reflex. Sweat poured down his body. It was smaller than every Eltharok he had ever met, but… why? Why did his instincts scream that this thing was far too dangerous?

“You… you can speak human language?”

“There is something I wish to ask. What exactly are you doing? Why do I see barren land leveled smooth? What does it mean?”

“You bastard!”

Peter poured Plana into the knife hidden inside his sleeve. The knife shot out and buried itself squarely in the Lord of the Mountain’s forehead. That was exactly what Peter wanted to see.

“Ptui.”

The Lord of the Mountain spat something from its mouth. It struck the knife Peter had loaded with all his Plana and knocked it spinning away into the trunk of a tree. Peter froze. He had used that knife to kill many monsters already, but it had been stopped so easily—by something so filthy.

Three heavy blows rang out.

Before he understood what had happened, both his arms and legs had been twisted into spirals. Peter spun through the air like a top. He had no idea what had happened. When awareness returned, all that remained was pain.

“Aaaagh!”

Peter screamed. A giant hand clamped down on his face and slammed him into the mud.

“I will give you one last chance. I hate repeating myself. What are the people in the village doing?”

“I—I don’t know.”

Wham!

A giant fist crashed into the center of his chest. Every rib in his body shattered instantly. Bone fragments sliced into his lungs and vital organs.

“Aaaagh!”

“You have eaten a great deal of Fruit of Bliss. Your body will heal and be injured again, over and over. So long as you are fed the Fruit of Bliss, you will not die. It would be wiser to answer my question.”

“I—I don’t know. They won’t let us collect the fruit, but they said if we work, we’ll get credit to exchange for food.”

“Food? So they truly are going to farm. In this cursed forest.”

“Hah… hahahaha… hahahaha…”

Tears mixed with mucus as Peter laughed in madness. He did not know why, but the unbearable pain in his limbs began fading into numbness. His mangled arms slowly began to heal, but at the same time drifted further and further away from anything human.

“My arms—my legs! Aaaagh!”

“I can heal you, if you agree to cooperate with me in a certain matter. Not only will your limbs recover, you will be allowed to enjoy as much Fruit of Bliss as you desire. You will never starve, and your life will be long. Is that not an excellent offer, human?”

“Hah… hahahaha… go fuck yourself. Who the hell would believe a monster’s words? Goodbye, you worthless piece of trash.”

Peter used all the strength left in his deformed legs and launched himself in one direction. The Lord of the Mountain’s eyes widened as the human’s Adam’s apple impaled itself on the knife lodged in the tree. Red blood sprayed out. His eyes bulged wide. Flesh spilled through the wound, and the bleeding showed no sign of stopping.

Cooperate with a monster?

No matter how much of society’s trash I may be, that is one thing I will never do. Those who sell their souls to monsters never meet a good end. Even if they survive, they can never return to living normally. Dying now is easier.

“You had no hesitation at all? You would rather die than become the servant of His Exalted Majesty? Rejecting pleasure and long life alike? How foolish. Or perhaps you’ve eaten so much Fruit of Bliss that your mind has rotted away. But no matter.”

The Lord of the Mountain flipped Peter onto his stomach. When its hand touched Peter’s forehead, a seed no bigger than a grain of rice bored through his skull. Something inside him began to grow at a terrifying speed.

“AAAAAGH!”

Peter screamed. He wanted to die, but his body refused to let him. Worse than that, his body was now brimming with life energy to the point of overflowing. No—no! Why won’t I die? That damned doctor really was lying. The more fruit you eat, the shorter your life gets.

I’ll kill all of you.

I’ll kill every last one of you!!

Jinyo slept soundly for eight full hours. With his special body, sleeping two hours normally equaled eight, but even so, he still had not fully recovered. Fighting monster hordes and collecting 36,000 hours of cultivation time within thirty-six hours had simply been too brutal.

A one-meter-deep pit had been dug according to his instructions. All the ingredients had been prepared by Butler James.

“It was no easy task, sir. Only the deer demons drop cores, and even then the cores are very small. Their blood is poor quality too—I had to filter it again and again.”

“I worked you hard, Mr. James. Thank you very much. This is enough. Everyone, please throw the ingredients in and stir them together.”

Jinyo began the ritual. Roughly made wooden furniture, high-level monster flesh, and organs gathered from the forest had been arranged into an altar.

All eyes were fixed on him. Jinyo began focusing his mind and performing a sword dance. The Hundred Blossom Bardic Sword Song began with Peach Blossom Dance and flowed into other forms. The scent of peach blossoms and a pink aura spread throughout the area, transforming the dark forest into a field of many-colored flowers.

“So beautiful,” Luciria murmured from afar. She had never seen a sword dance this graceful before.

No one knew why Jinyo was doing this. He had merely told everyone that it was a Southern Cross family ritual not meant for outsiders to witness. Rina had wanted to ask everyone to leave, but there was no stopping curiosity anyway, so she had let it go.

“How many hours does he have to keep doing that, Lady Rina?”

“At minimum twelve. At most fourteen. Until the mixture you all poured in is reduced to only a few small round pellets.”

Rina never took her eyes off Jinyo. She still held the Bardic Blade and had not returned it to him. Jinyo himself had never once asked for it back, even though having it during today’s ritual would likely have been better.

“Lady Rina, where are you going?” Nephrin asked when she saw Rina walking away from the crowd.

“I’m going to patrol the area. Naho, keep an eye on Gretel and Jinyo.”

“Yes, ma’am,” Naho answered, but before two breaths had passed, Rina had already vanished from everyone’s sight.

“There are no monsters left around here, right? Why is Lady Rina still going out on patrol?” Luciria voiced her doubt. No one bothered satisfying her curiosity.

“That woman has always been like this. She’s carried more responsibility than she should ever since childhood. That’s what it means to be Mother’s successor. A person like her would work herself to death and never stop until she was certain everyone was safe. Don’t mind her,” Naho replied.

But that did nothing to reassure anyone. What unsettled them was not Rina’s work ethic—it was the thought of what could make someone as strong as Rina so wary, so unable to rest easy, that made it hard for them to breathe.
