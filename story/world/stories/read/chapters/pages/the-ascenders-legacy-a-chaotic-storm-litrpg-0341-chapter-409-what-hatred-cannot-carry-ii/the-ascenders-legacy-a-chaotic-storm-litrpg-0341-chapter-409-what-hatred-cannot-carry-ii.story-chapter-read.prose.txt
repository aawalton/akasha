Within the ruined district of Alcatraz, Aodhán stood atop a floating platform, staring impassively at the destruction below.

It had been two days since they found Edrick Ghaltris's legacy seed. Two days since they had plunged an entire city of sleepers into chaos.

It had not recovered.

The flames had died down now, and the towering plumes of smoke that had once choked the sky were gone. Even the scent of sulfur and ozone lingering in the air had begun to subside. But the damage was still extensive.

After Vaymoz left for his palace, furious over what they had done to his daughter, they had all begun trying to put the city back together in whatever ways they could. Calamity Amoz had conjured a storm to douse the flames and cleanse the air. Calamity Rezin and Cephas established decrees to repair the tears in the fabric of space and reality, of which there were many due to the scarcity of essence in the air, while the Mythics had spread throughout the district, repairing the earth and sealing the fissures they had created.

Manipulating the earth was far beyond the capabilities of any storm cultivator, let alone a couple of Mythics, but somehow, using water, wind, and force aspects, they had made it work, albeit very crudely.

Aodhán, Sophran, and their two underlings had worked alongside the Mythics, searching wreckage and shattered buildings to pull out whatever bodies they could find.

They found many. But none were alive.

Those who hadn't died from the impact had choked on the smoke-filled air. And it was all their fault.

Aodhán managed to push through the first day, but after pulling out his hundredth body—its head crushed beneath a slab of concrete—he had decided, for his own sake, to work on something less... reminding, instead.

So, while everyone else focused on rebuilding Alcatraz on a grand scale—even Crixis, who insisted on continuing to recover the dead—Aodhán settled for carrying boulders, relocating debris, and clearing away rubble. He deliberately avoided the areas most likely to contain dead people, unwilling to look upon the faces of those he had helped kill.

Or worse, those he had failed to save.

Even so, he couldn't avoid it completely. Every so often, he came across another body, and the sight of each one felt like a dagger to his chest.

"You did your best, Aodhán," he tried to tell himself, whispering the words beneath his breath. But they rang hollow in his ears and false in his mind.

Still, they offered some comfort. And that comfort was what he'd held onto over the last two days.

Every time he found a dead body, he repeated the words. You did your best, Aodhán. You couldn't have saved everyone.

Every time the scent of death reached him, he said them again, trying to convince himself that those he had failed to save hadn't died in vain. Had he stopped to save them, he probably wouldn't have found the legacy seed in time.

But perhaps that would have been the better outcome.

Severian would have been defeated before he found the seed. Crixis would never have nearly died. Yelena would never have had her soul torn apart.

And most importantly… the seed would still be with him. Would still belong to his mother.

He grimaced.

Perhaps they had died in vain after all.

Sighing, he closed his eyes, forcing the thought away before opening them once more.

There was still hope, however little. Yelena Alcatraz could awaken a changed person. Perhaps she would see how many people had died, all that had been sacrificed for her sake, and simply forgive them.

Within him, Varéc chuffed.

Aodhán scowled.

The likelihood of that happening was so far from hope it bordered on delusional. But it was all they had at this point. The only thing they could potentially point to as the good to come out of this mission, because everything else was just… Ugh.

Shaking his head, he let his gaze drift across the things he had worked on over the last two days and let out a small smile.

It was impressive. If he said so himself.

Nearly every reusable slab of concrete had been gathered and arranged in a pile. Rubble, wood splinters, and debris had been reduced to ash, while metals had been heaped into a high pile for reuse.

And it wasn't just in a small area, but across the entire northern and eastern district.

However, as impressive as his efforts were, there was still so much to do before Alcatraz could resemble a city again. If they had possessed wood, earth, glass, or metal cultivators among them, much of it could have been rebuilt already.

Alas, every one of those affinities—save for wood and metal—belonged to sects that were sworn enemies of the Storm Sect.

That was the disadvantage of sects. And the beauty of Lutia's diversity.

Frowning, he returned his gaze to the pile of debris he had gathered over the past few hours and activated Lightning Beam—Black. The beam struck from above, and the rubble immediately began turning to ash.

Within seconds, the entire pile had disintegrated, and as the ash blew away in the wind, Aodhán dusted off his palms and turned toward an approaching core.

It was Sophran.

"Hey." Aodhán smiled. "How are you holding up?"

Sophran shook his head. "Not good. But it's almost over." He glanced at the pile of ash beneath Aodhán. "I see you're finishing up too."

"Yeah. The district will soon be clear of rubble for whoever ends up rebuilding it." Aodhán frowned. "Any idea who that might be?"

"Certainly not earth cultivators," Sophran snorted, drawing a broader smile from Aodhán.

They stood in silence for the next few minutes, gazing out over the city until Sophran finally broke it. He looked at Aodhán, his expression gentle.

"How are you holding up?"

Aodhán shrugged, knowing exactly what he was asking. "I'm fine. Not happy, but I'm fine."

Sophran nodded in understanding before turning his gaze toward the distance, where Calamity Amoz and Cephas stood talking quietly. Aodhán followed his gaze, his expression softening slightly.

"Do you think you can forgive him?" Sophran asked softly.

"I already have." Aodhán frowned. Then shook his head slightly. "What he did was wrong, but after the last two days, I kind of get it. The guilt that comes with seeing so many dead people." He let out a deep breath. "With his heightened senses, he must've sensed all that death all at once. It couldn't have been easy."

A smirk tugged at his lips. "Maybe Rezin and Selia are the weird ones for not carrying the guilt the way he does."

Sophran shrugged. "Or maybe they've simply learned to compartmentalize far better than he has."

"Either way, it's messed up," Aodhán said. "I can't hold it against him. Besides, he already promised to make it right."

"The High Elders will want to do something to apologize as well, if I know them." Sophran smiled. "Perhaps you'll end up with more than one seed after all. You could bring both your mother and father."

Aodhán chuckled, nudging Sophran playfully. "That'd be a dream come true. But I'd rather not get my hopes up. There are other people in the sect, after all."

"Yeah," Sophran nodded teasingly. "But none with an immunity to the twisted effect. I bet they're just waiting for you to understand it and eventually teach it to the whole sect." He snorted. "My point is, you're important to them."

Aodhán grinned. "We are all important to them."

"Indeed," Sophran nodded, not at all sarcastic. "But you've brought so much into the sect. It's only natural that they value you more. And it's nothing to be ashamed of."

Aodhán pressed his lips together. "I'm not ashamed. It's just… I have no idea how the immunity works. It's entirely possible that I'll ascend without understanding it."

Sophran chuckled. "Well, let's hope that's not the case, shall we? Some of us would also like to do away with the twisted effect. So if it's something that can be taught, we hope you figure it out."

Aodhán chuckled. "I hope so too."

Just then, one of the Mythics appeared above them with a smile on her face.

"She's waking up," she announced brightly, and though she never specified who was waking up, there was no need to. Aodhán could already sense the newly awakened core pulsing in the distance.

He grimaced. "She's going to hate us."

"You can't know that," Sophran said. "We have to be hopeful."

"Right." Aodhán sighed, already preparing himself for the worst.

They teleported to the rooftop where the cocoon lay, arriving only a second behind Calamity Amoz and Cephas. Remaris, Tianyu, and Crixis appeared a heartbeat later, and Crixis immediately came to stand beside him, his eyes heavy with exhaustion.

He bowed in greeting before turning his attention to the unraveling cocoon as strands of lightning peeled from its surface.

"Wow, that's beautiful," he whispered, and Tianyu nodded.

"Let's hope Yelena's reaction is just as beautiful."

Aodhán snorted instinctively, not bothering to waste his time hoping. Of everyone present here, he had spent the most time around Yelena. He had seen her hatred firsthand, and had witnessed just how far she was willing to go to put a cultivator down.

The cannons were proof enough. And Aodhán doubted any discussion, no matter how profound, could erase that much hatred.

Still, he kept quiet. It would be a joy to be wrong after all.

The cocoon broke apart to reveal Yelena a few minutes later, her raven-black hair now streaked with faint wisps of crimson. It was a bloodline manifestation—something she could only have gotten from a patron.

Something she must have accepted.

A collective sigh of relief passed through everyone present, and Sophran smiled.

"See, I told you to hope. If she accepted a soul seed, then perhaps she has accepted us too."

"I wouldn't go that far," Remaris grimaced. "But it's a good sign. It means she'd rather live than die, whatever her father suggested."

"Then it's a step in our favor," Calamity Cephas muttered, looking everyone in the eye. "Whatever we do now may very well cement or break what the Ascendant has done."

"So we should be nice?" Crixis asked.

"Yes," Cephas nodded, glancing at Calamity Amoz. "But firm too. Everyone be ready."

Everyone straightened, watching Yelena's sleeping form as she slowly came out of her awakening vision, looking so frail and gentle, Aodhán could have called her beautiful had he not known her true character.

Her eyes blinked open a minute later, pupils slowly adjusting to the light. And that was when the second change in her appearance was revealed.

Double rings.

Yelena had awakened into the Evolved Class, just as Aodhán had, and the implications weren't lost on anyone. To evolve during an awakening, Yelena must have undergone a tribulation—or a trial of some kind—and emerged victorious. That in turn meant she had likely gained an ability too, just like his core sense or Ayisha's foresight.

Aodhán frowned slightly, momentarily tempted to scan her core and discover what ability she had obtained. He restrained himself, however, deciding that the less he knew about Yelena's gains, the less regret he would feel over handing the seed to her.

"Are you alright?" Verity asked, a smile on her face as she leaned down to help Yelena up. Yelena's gaze focused on her, and for the briefest instant, she almost seemed happy to see Verity.

But then Verity's bloodline manifestations must have registered, and the happy expression froze into a cold mask of restrained hatred. She leaned back immediately, a sneer pulling at her lips before forcibly settling into a thin line.

"I'm alright," Yelena replied, her voice a harsh whisper. She looked away from Verity immediately, seemingly wanting to take in the rest of her surroundings, only to be confronted with the rest of them.

A dozen cultivators. All staring at her.

The sneer she had forcibly suppressed returned once more, fiercer than ever, and she immediately forced herself to her feet. She stared at all of them, cold hatred flashing in her eyes as she recognized them.

And then, her gaze landed on Aodhán.

And that cold hatred cracked into a fiery fury.

"Stay away from me, all of you!" she said immediately, taking several steps backward. "Just stay back."

No one moved, giving her space to do whatever she needed to do. Aodhán, however, grimaced in the realization that he was right. Whatever discussion Yelena had shared with Edrick Ghaltris, it hadn't quenched her hatred at all. Rather, it seemed to have sharpened it into something colder.

Something with purpose.

She didn't lash out at them as expected. For a long moment, she didn't even speak.

She simply glared at them, and then that fiery fury froze over once more—settling into a space between hatred, indifference, and disgust, the latter of which seemed to be directed at herself.

"Where is my father?" she gritted out a moment later, looking straight at Aodhán.

"He's alive," Calamity Amoz replied before he could. "He's in his palace. Would you like to see him?"

"Yes," Yelena replied, her gaze finally straying from Aodhán's to meet Amoz's. The chill radiating from her deepened. "I would appreciate it if one of you took me to him."

The cultivators exchanged glances. Then Verity asked, "Surely you must have some questions—"

"I do not have any questions," Yelena interrupted coldly, the steel in her voice unmistakable. "Edrick Ghaltris has explained to me all I need to know. What I would like now is to see my father."

Amoz studied her for a moment before giving a slow nod, his runic irises flashing. "If you ever need anything or have any questions, do not hesitate to ask any of us here. We are all willing to help."

Yelena sneered. "I'll keep that in mind for when next my soul is torn in half, Calamity. With my new life as a cultivator, I doubt it'll be very long."

Anger flashed in the gazes of many present, but Calamity Cephas raised a hand to calm everyone down. He stepped forward with a smile.

"You said you wanted to see your father, cultivator Alcatraz. Let Verity take you. In case you suddenly find yourself with a few questions."

Verity stepped forward immediately. She gave Yelena a small smile. "I know you hate us, child. We've certainly given you many reasons to. But it is my hope that you'll come to understand us one day and realize not all of us are as evil as you think."

Yelena's sneer tugged upward for a moment before she forced it back into a thin line of cold hate.

"The day I understand you, cultivator, is the day I finally lose myself." She smiled then, but it was far from amused. "I hope the day is far, far away."

Verity's eyes sparked with anger. Then she too smiled.

"Let's hope it's as far as you think, Yelena Alcatraz. For the time being," she conjured a lightning construct and gestured towards it, "let's go see your father."

Something in Yelena's gaze sharpened then, and her eyes grew even colder. She said nothing though, and after a moment of hesitation, she stepped onto the platform.

She glanced back at Aodhán then, cold hate gleaming in her eyes. Aodhán held her gaze unflinchingly, not entirely sure why she had singled him out as the anchor of her hatred.

But was he surprised? Not at all.

She knew him the most of everyone here. And telling her he was the reason she was now a cultivator didn't seem like a good idea. It most certainly wouldn't help matters at all.

The platform surged up to hold Yelena's feet steady, and once she was fully secure, Verity finally stepped onto it. She bowed to the calamities before immediately zooming off, the construct tearing through the air in a blur.

A few seconds later, they were nothing more than a dot on the horizon.

Sophran grimaced. "I'm not sure that speed is safe for a newly awakened cultivator."

Calamity Cephas sighed. "It's obvious she's trying to accept her new life, but her hatred is getting in the way."

"Indeed, it is," Rezin said, speaking up for the first time. "But whatever the Ascendant said to her, it has had an impact. She hates us all, but she's no longer being obstinate." He grimaced. "At least, less than expected."

"You think she might come around soon?" Remaris asked with a frown.

The three Calamities grimaced.

"I doubt soon is a word I'd use to describe this situation," Rezin muttered. "Maybe someday. But I doubt it'll be anytime soon. Or at all, for that matter."

"So she'll never come around?" Remaris's frown deepened.

"We cannot say," Amoz sighed. "We can only hope that as we all treat her nicely, she'll grow… warmer."

He glanced at the other Calamities, and after a moment, they all teleported, leaving the rest of them to stare at each other in silence.

Everyone scattered then, and Aodhán returned to his task of hauling boulders and disintegrating debris.

He honestly couldn't wait to return to the sect and put this whole Alcatraz arc behind him.

Only a few months ago, he'd hated the peace and quiet he'd forced upon himself after the war. Now, however, he realized just how ungrateful he had been.

That peace had been a blessing. One he feared he wouldn't get the chance to enjoy again anytime soon.

Vaymoz Alcatraz had been right.

The Storm Sect hadn't gained an ally with Yelena.

They had gained something—that much was certain. What it was, however, Aodhán doubted any of them would like.

Yelena Alcatraz closed her eyes, letting the wind wash over her face for a moment before opening them once more.

The cultivator had finally slowed down, much to her appreciation. She suspected it was to prevent her from seeing the extent of the damage they had wrought on the northern and eastern district, but Yelena refused to ask.

She would not give the infuriating cultivator that satisfaction.

Rather, she focused her attention on the energy humming beneath her skin like electricity. It was the energy of the world—storm essence, Edrick had called it. The very same thing storm cultivators manipulated to bring ruin and destruction to the world around them. The very same thing she was currently standing on.

Her gaze drifted to the lightning platform, and she scowled, disgust curling within her.

She had promised herself to do this, to accept this power as the weapon that it was. The Ascendant was right—hatred and defiance without power was nothing, and if she was to avenge herself, avenge Alcatraz, or even protect others from the fate she had suffered, she had to embrace this power and master it no matter how much she hated it.

But deciding to do something and actually doing it were two different things.

And as the power churned within her, Yelena couldn't help but hate herself for accepting it. For seeing the logic in Edrick Ghaltris's words. She now possessed the same power as those who had killed her mother.

What did that make her?

Sighing dejectedly, she shifted her attention away from the energy churning within her, and turned her gaze to the red-streaked screen blinking at the edge of her vision.

It was called a status screen.

And the moment she focused on it, the screen expanded to fill her sight.

STATUS

Name: Yelena Dominica Alcatraz

Class: Evolved Storm Cultivator: 1% (PENDING)

Tier: 1—4%

Techniques: Energy Weaving (Bloodline)

Skills (Innate): Storm Creation and Manipulation, Lightning Creation and Manipulation.

Bloodline: Guardian Weaver Ancestry (Epic)

Yelena stared at the screen for a long moment before shuddering, hating the sight of it even as childlike curiosity stirred within her. Edrick had taught her a lot of the ways of cultivation. He had told her the basics of her power. The do's and don'ts.

And when he'd mentioned the limit, Yelena had had half a mind to do exactly that—absorb so many unaligned cores and limit herself before tier ten, utterly wasting this prized "gift" the Storm Sect had given her.

Even now, the thought brought a smile to her face.

Oh, how furious they would be. How hateful they would become.

But then she had realized that doing so would cripple her too. She would still be a cultivator, only a weak one, subject to mockery and hate. How would she defy them if she was so weak? How would she protect anyone?

How would she spite them?

It was a bitter thought, and her lips twisted into a scowl as she contemplated the lack of good options.

She looked at the screen once more, reading through the technique, skills, and bloodline perks. No wonder cultivators carried themselves with such pride. No wonder they seemed so drunk on power. So convinced they stood above everyone else.

Why wouldn't they, with all of this at their fingertips?

Shaking her head, Yelena dismissed the screen altogether and turned her attention back to her surroundings. When she looked up, her father's palace stood in the distance.

She smiled… but felt the expression wane as her stomach began to churn nervously. How would her father react when he saw her? What would he think of her now that she had become the very thing they despised?

Drawing a slow breath, she raised herself to her full height, and clasped her hands behind her back, watching quietly as the palace grew nearer and nearer.

When they finally drew close enough, she whispered, "That's enough, cultivator. I can finish the rest on my feet."

The cultivator looked at her, resignation in her features. Then slowly, the platform lowered to the ground. It vanished an instant later, and Yelena let out a small sound as her feet touched the ground.

The cultivator flew back into the air, arms folded patiently.

She stared down at Yelena. Yelena glared up at her.

Then with a sharp breath, Yelena turned away and began walking, passing by deserted stalls and buildings as she made her way to the palace. She stared at each building, noting the complete silence and absence of any humans.

It seemed that despite being miles away from the worst damage, the citizens here had fled regardless. But not only them. Even the guards that should have lined the area were missing, having most likely deserted their posts at the prospect of death.

They had all fled at the prospect of death, leaving her father—leaving all of Alcatraz—unguarded.

But Yelena didn't blame them.

This wasn't their fault after all. It was the fault of those who had brought ruin upon their city.

And she had just become one of them.

How could she face her father like this? How could she tell him she was embracing this life rather than death? How could she tell him that, though her conversation with Edrick Ghaltris hadn't erased her hatred, it had left her with something else...

Something even she didn't fully understand yet?

Nervous, she continued walking, putting one foot in front of the other, until finally, she turned into the main avenue. The palace gate came into view, and finally, a handful of guards appeared.

There were three of them, each carrying a laser rifle her father had once called the ultimate weapon. At the time she had agreed with him. But now, as she walked toward it, a cultivator floating above her, Yelena realized just how useless it was against any cultivator of substance.

How useless it would be against her in a few months if she let herself cultivate this power.

She grimaced, straightening as the guards finally noticed them. They sputtered the moment they recognized her, their eyes widening in shock.

"Mistress Yelena!" one of them exclaimed, stepping forward instinctively. "We've been searching for—"

He stopped, his gaze straying to the red streaks in her hair and the rings in her eyes. His gaze darted quickly to the cultivator by her side, then back at her.

He raised his gun. The guards behind him did the same.

"Mistress Yelena," he began again, his expression wary. "You… you are a cultivator."

His words were like a slap on her face, and she swallowed. "I'm here to see my father."

For a moment, none of the guards moved. They glanced toward their leader, and though he made no visible gesture, they slowly parted, wariness evident on their features.

"You may go," the leader said briskly, his gaze straying from her to the cultivator by her side. "But this one stays."

Verity didn't argue. She simply clasped her hands behind her back and looked away, still floating.

Yelena drew in another deep breath, and after a brief moment of hesitation, she stepped into the house she had called home for the last fifteen years. Only this time, it welcomed her not as the mistress of the house, but as a cultivator capable of destroying everything in her vicinity with a sneeze.

Over three dozen soldiers stood within the main hall, and despite recognizing her, none lowered their weapons.

The implication was clear.

She was no longer the daughter of their master.

She was an enemy.
