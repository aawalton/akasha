After I cleaned myself off in the river, I returned to the tent and took apart the basin with my bare hands. It would’ve been a bit of a struggle before, but now, I could pull apart the boards and nails with no effort at all. For a brief moment, I changed into Sir Ricdine’s armour, then sat outside the front of my tent and burned the boards in a late night campfire.

The other in my squad waited inside the tent, hiding out of sight, but I knew they were there. I didn’t even give them a glance, not willing to risk it if someone was watching—and surely, someone still was. One of Hinlaw’s squires would be looking for me, and had been tasked to follow me around all the time.

It was a good thing, then, that I had my secret exit in the back of the tent.

As I burned the boards, I kept looking forward. I hooked the flap of chainmail over my mouth so no one could see my lips move if they were watching from a distance, then whispered, “That worked well. And if I can do it, then anyone—”

“Not anyone, Brother,” Brawl replied.

“Dupes will need a blessing from a Slumberer to make it work,” Sage said. “That was the only reason yours worked.”

I prodded the fire with a stick. “I suppose. But if there was a chance to get the rest of my squad a blessing, would you take it?”

“Of course,” Anchor said. “But I’m not sure if we truly could force such a thing.”

“There’s a way,” I said, remembering one of my conversations with Alis. “I just don’t really know how. Yet.”

We were silent for a few minutes. I looked up at the sky, watching Welkinmere and the other moons. It had been difficult to pick out stars before, with how bright the planet seemed in the sky, but the bodily enhancement affected all my stats—including Perception, it seemed. Hundreds of twinkling stars appeared overhead.

I wanted to grab my reading slate and check, but if I was truly under surveillance, they would see that. And no true Path-walker would actually use a reading slate. That was a sure-fire way to get myself caught.

“You seem glum, Brother,” Brawl said.

“Nah. Just…tired. And ready. It’s the calm before the storm.”

“It’s just a final joust,” Anchor said.

I resisted the urge to shake my head, and instead whispered, “Nah. There’s going to be more to it than that. We need to be ready, because things are going to go badly very quickly. Once it starts, I can’t guarantee what will happen.”

“You mean the orc attack?” Sage whispered.

“Yeah.”

“If they were coming, don’t you think Ceonwin’s scouts would’ve found them by now?” Anchor asked.

“Perhaps. I don’t know how well they’re hiding, or who Hinlaw has bribed to look the other way.”

“We’ll be ready, brother,” Brawl said. “We’ll keep your armour and spear nearby the joust, so you can change afterward.”

“Thanks,” I said. I took a long breath, though it wasn’t as satisfying as I hoped. The smoke of my fire, combined with the stench of the drainage ditch and the remains from my bodily enhancements overpowered any of the pleasant mid-summer smells. And with my newly-enhanced nostrils as sensitive as they were, I nearly gagged.

I didn’t have a perfect grasp on the date anymore, but I guessed it was about three or four months until the anniversary of our sapper training beginning. And almost a year since I’d arrived in this world.

Once I finished burning the boards, I snuck back inside the tent, then changed out of my armour and snuck out the back of the tent with my squad, then disappeared into the bushes. We raced back to the castle and returned to our rooms.

The first thing I did when I got back to my room was retrieve my reading slate and activate it.

Same tier, still. But I had to be close to reaching Silver. My new attributes sat at:

Vitality: 65

Agility: 71

Strength: 68

Perception: 47

Focus: 86

Presence: 91

It was a massive boost in pretty much every category. Even my Presence had increased. Yes, some of that was to do with my increased average Presence income over the past few weeks, but the bodily enhancement had also boosted my Presence greatly.

I had never had this big of a leap before. Tomorrow, in the Labyrinth, I was going to have to get used to that as much as anything.

Overall, I had three hundred and thirty-seven attributes, and ninety-one Presence—I was on the cusp of Silver. If I really pushed it in the Labyrinth, and in the coming week, I could hit the Silver threshold by the time I faced Hinfic. But just barely.

Reaching Silver wouldn’t just be a change in the name of my tier. An Advanced-tier Skill struggled to activate at its fullest potential when the user was too low of a tier. If I hit Silver in time, it should improve the effectiveness of my Art. I suspected it’d increase the general strength of the gravity well. It would help me match Hinfic.

And then there was the fact that few sappers ever reached Silver. That would have to increase my ranking to the top, further compounding my Presence income.

“Put the slate away, Levi,” Anchor whispered. “It’s late, and you need sleep, too. You can’t just stare at it all night.”

“Sorry,” I said, placing it down on the counter. I tucked into my cot and did my best to relax, no matter how bad the anticipation of the coming week gnawed at me.

“What is your report?” Hinlaw demanded. He met with Milsige behind the stables, late at night. It was midnight, but the squire had summoned him. He stepped cautiously in the dim light, dodging horse droppings and doing his very best to keep the ends of his cloak from brushing the ground.

There was no reason to keep his voice quiet. If anyone heard, there was nothing they could do about it.

“Nothing, sir,” Milsige replied. “That’s the problem. He’s suspicious, yes. But he hasn’t done anything obvious. No secrets about who he is. Nothing we can nail him on.”

Hinlaw turned and massaged his forehead.

“My lord, will he beat your son?” Milsige asked. “Is there anything we can do, sir, to simply defeat him on the lists?”

“I don’t just want to beat him. I want to make an example of him. People like him keep rising, subverting the natural way of things. They will bend every ancient rule and regulation to suit them. Eventually, they will rip apart the fabric of our nation.”

“Can your son kill him, then?”

“I certainly hope so. If not…well, our orcs shall make quick work of them. But we must humiliate him first. We cannot let him die a martyr.” Hinlaw turned back to his squire and grabbed the man’s shoulder tight, his grip unshakable. “I need details! Something, anything, that can bring him low.”

“There is one thing…”

“Yes?”

“Ricdine seemed unusually strong tonight. Like he just performed a main body enhancement. It doesn’t align with any of his feats I’ve seen before. He shouldn’t have been able to enhance himself again.”

Hinlaw narrowed his eyes. “When he entered the tournament, he was a Steel. He would’ve reforged his body to become a Titanium. And now you tell me he’s reforged his body again?”

“It would seem so, my lord.”

Hinlaw scowled. “Something isn’t lining up.” He paused. “Have you seen where he keeps his horse?”

“In his tent, sir.”

“Country thegns…” Hinlaw grumbled. “Sleeping in pigsties.” But still, he had nothing. There had to be a hole in Ricdine’s identity. He was too unusual. “Speak to the other squires. Put out a network. Check to see if anyone else has seen anything unusual about him. Someone must have something on him.”

“Yes, my lord.”

“And do not return to me empty-handed again.”

The next morning, I woke up as early as I could. I had one last point of curiosity that I needed to address: how did my enhanced body stack up against others, tier-wise? Sure, I didn’t need to ask, I didn’t need to figure it out, but someone would notice sooner than later that I was far stronger than I ought to be. I’d better let my actions be on the record.

So I found Master Thunric. He was speaking with a druid, and with Lord Ceonwin. Willaf and Foal stood nearby, watching.

I approached, then waited for a moment and said, “Master Thunric. Do you have a moment?”

“You?” the man asked. “What do you need? Did your squad’s druid do something ridiculous?”

“Actually, master, it might have been me this time.” I explained what I’d done last night, though I didn’t tell him where I’d done it.

A ripple washed over my skin, and a shiver ran down my spine. Thunric scanned me with his perception, then shook his head. “It is…average. It is Titanium-tier. Which is impressive. Without maegen control, your enhanced body should have been completely inconsequential garbage.”

“I’ll…take that as a compliment,” I said. “Can I do it again?”

“A Path-walker can only reforge their body like that once. They may perform smaller enhancements later in life, and their body will often improve in scale with their tier, assuming their maegen increases in purity…” He paused, then glanced at me. “Yours may end up scaling with your resonance nodes. They have, after all, become intertwined, and have received a greater connection now, with your reforging. They’re…closer to you, as far as I can see.”

“I’ll keep an eye on it, master,” I said. Did that mean that my stats were now going to keep increasing even faster?

“I should be furious with you, but you weren’t even under my command, and no one would have even thought to order you not to. But next time, request assistance first.”

I wouldn’t make any promises. Instead, I just nodded, then said, “Thanks, master. Now, uh, I’ve gotta run. Get breakfast before the labyrinth run and all.”
