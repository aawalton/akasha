Humans and Fae are so short-sighted.

A few centuries, a handful of millennia and they find themselves repeating the same sins, the same mistakes. They squabble, they face off against each other as though they weren't all the same at heart.

They inter-breed, and yet they believe themselves to be distinct. And they, the humans at least, even have the way to understand this fact, to see the truth about their near kin. But they ignore those who might delve into this knowledge because they are not Awakened.

They treat their petty differences as secrets, as a privilege rather than a gift. They condemn so many to the shortest of lifespans, just to feel special.

Yet some of our own youth find them appealing, find ways to explore the Ponds with them. Even we must admit that there was a time we watched more closely, where we considered such a merge. Many of us are guilty of such thoughts, few of us are willing to admit to them.

Much easier to deal swift retribution to those who seek us with violence, and to allow those who are polite insight into our hoarded knowledge.

His Eminence slept deeply, but poorly. Few would believe the sensitivity time had granted him, fewer still would believe he cared about those slightest of ripples. Yet the smallest ripples sometimes became the greatest of consequences. Once his actions had seemed insignificant, yet even now the ramifications were unsettled.

His Eminence did not dream, he had slipped too deep. He did not dwell on this fact, he did not consider the lack of dreams to be a hindrance. Why would he? Dreams were portents, portents required deciphering. To pass beyond dreams was to exist within the awareness of sleep. Awareness so few ever achieved.

His awareness swept across his descendants, too faintly for even the most perceptive to ever notice. This was not the awareness of a seeker, this was not attention to be noted, it was the ties that bound them all to one another, the web of blood and loyalty.

His Eminence noted the gaps, the tensions that came from strife and disagreement. He noted the dense clusters, the connections of love, of family, of trust. He noted the branches, those lineages that were seen as distinct, as unlike those of visible note. They were still family, still within his sight.

He saw chaos and calm, disorder and consistency. He foresaw upheaval, he predicted stability.

Beyond all else, his Eminence continued to sleep. That was his role, his intention. He would awaken in time, he would see what came of the events now unfolding. Those trusted few who remembered would ensure he did not awaken to strife, unless strife was inevitable.

Mahk picked up the list of scions on his desk and looked it over once more. He knew exactly what it said but it gave him a reason to delay looking at his Seneschal, delay the conversation that he felt obligated to have.

"Only Clyde?"

"Sir. The others are finding it difficult to-"

"I didn't ask whether they were having problems. I asked that you confirm that the only one to have made any sort of connection is Clyde."

"Yes, my Prince."

"I understand why he has succeeded. If he had failed then there was no chance anyone would succeed. The perfect pawn to attract attention and entice some foolish girl thinking she can find love, or some matriarch certain she'll gain an upper hand in a future negotiation."

"He was coached, he was taught-"

"They all were," Mahk snarled as he crumpled the report. "If a malleable fool is able to play the part, to seem desirable, how is it that the actual scions are failing at every turn? Are they idiots? Incompetent? Lazy?"

"My Lord," the Seneschal said. "Several have reportedly complained that their assigned sectors are, and I use their words, ugly and boring. They wish to return to their comforts instead of-"

"Re-align the Portal permissions. Access to all comfort Constructs, to all private Ponds is to be forbidden to the individuals on this list until they accomplish their roles."

"Are you certain?"

"How many years have you served me in this role?"

"Fifty three next month."

"And yet you question if I am certain?"

"I merely wish to ensure you have considered your actions, as you have frequently requested I do."

Mahk stared at the man. He could not fault his service, his willingness to put himself at risk to accomplish the specific duty he was given. Removing him would be more than an inconvenience, dismissing his statement would- No. In this case he was not acting rashly.

"The order stands. They may regain access once they show progress. Limited access based on performance, full access once the report no longer paints them as incompetent."

"As you say."

"If any still grumble and object, if they are unable to realize that they are expected to serve the Family, not their own whims… Let them spend an evening with my niece, at her discretion."

"Y-yes, my Prince."

"Another half dozen Guards have identified what they suspect to be their answer to crossing the Wall, assuming they are correct we will be-"

"Tomas?" Lyselle said, interrupting what felt like the hundredth report of the day.

"Yes, my Lady?"

"Is there anything important to cover?"

"Holding business is important. You asked me to ensure that advancement be monitored and reported on."

"Because I spent roughly fifty years without knowing the answer, without any progress. I spent the entire time focused on what was proper, what was right what was…"

She looked down at her desk and snarled as she saw webs of frost covering it, again.

"Why haven't you crossed the Wall?" she asked.

"I haven't had the opportunity to-"

"Bullshit. We both know that you could delegate enough duties to spend the time."

"My L- Elle," Tomas said as he sat across the desk from her. "What's wrong."

"Nothing. I'm advancing, my elite are advancing with me, the more talented guards are finding their own answers and doing so as well."

"You just swore. That is not you."

"That isn't Sovereign Lys. That isn't who the Court sees."

"Exactly. You swore at your Seneschal."

Lys could feel her Affinity roiling under her skin. Her daughter's Seer, Fay, had told her that she'd gain control over those manifestations as she incorporated it into her other techniques, as she grew used to it.

That was years ago now. She still had these… issues.

"Take the time you need," she said. "Find your answer and advance."

"The Holding…"

"Sovereign Lys need her Seneschal to advance. I need you to advance."

"Permission to seclude myself outside the Holding? If I am present I will find excuses."

"You can't stop yourself from working, can you."

"Can you stop yourself from doing what you crave?"

Lys said nothing, looking him in the eye until he broke contact. That wasn't fair of him, nor of her. She couldn't spend all her time Diving, or with friends. She couldn't push without limitations. She couldn't fret about her daughters, not openly.

"See if Holding Syl will give you appropriate quarters," she said.

"I intended to."

"And ask Fay if she can find out if Anya would be willing to have me visit. When possible."

"Elle?"

"I would like to see my daughter, if she is willing to see me."

"Of course. By your leave?"

She nodded and watched him exit her office. Once he was gone she looked back down at her hands, at the imprints in her desk that looked similar to burns. The frost had vanished, the chill had faded. She'd have to get Tomas to- No. She'd have to find a replacement, he had other things to do.
