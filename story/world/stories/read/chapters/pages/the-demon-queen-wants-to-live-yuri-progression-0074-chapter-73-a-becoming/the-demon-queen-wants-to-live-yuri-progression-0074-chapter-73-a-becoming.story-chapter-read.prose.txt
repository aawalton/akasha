Blue power poured out from the girl in a rush. It was Essence and it burst outward in a wave that threw the dust from the stone.

I raised both arms and set my weight against it, and still it drove me back. My heels carved two lines through the stone of the ring. The pressure pushed at me like a hand splayed across my whole body. Then it stopped and I lowered my arms.

Lena Eckhart stood at the center of the ring. The girl was staring down at her own hands, turning them over as if she herself could not recognize them. She clenched them into fists and looked back up at me. The girl smiled and whispered in a voice almost too low to hear: "Now this…this is what I should have done from the start!"

I saw black in the girl. I saw it in the blue Essence pouring from her. There were thin tendrils of it, snaking through the blue. They flickered in and out, in and out.

I knew that black. I had seen it in the wings of a phoenix that begged for an ending. I had seen it in a Great Elk that had once been divine. I had seen it, most recently of all, crawling across the shoulder of a general who should have been dust for a thousand years.

"Fool girl," I said. "What have you done?"

The girl's smile widened, and now it was for the crowd, and her voice went out over all of them, carried on Essence. "What's the matter, half-demon? Scared?" I did not answer her. The smile faltered just for a moment, and then she forced it wider to cover the falter. "You should be."

Lightning came off her mark and sheathed her whole body. It ran from her ankles to her shoulders, blue-white and crackling. The air near her bent with the heat of it. Her Tenet. I had just fought against it. Had just managed to outlast it. I thought I knew the mechanism behind it. I did not know what power the girl wore now. Because the black was in it. I saw one thread of it, coiling up through the blue like a vein of rot in living wood.

The girl rushed me, and she was faster than she had been just moments before. I had suspected she would be. I did not know by how much.

The girl swung, but by the time I saw the blow, it was already too late to answer it. It made me stagger back and half turn around. By the time I turned back and got my feet under me, a second blow struck my stomach and stole the air from my lungs. I almost folded and collapsed, my vision swimming white.

Then I threw myself back, firing Ruin as I made some distance between us, black fire pouring from my right hand. The girl rushed through my Ruin without slowing. She struck me with her shoulder, and that was enough to send me sailing back.

I was falling, but even before I had finished falling, I was pouring Bloom from my left hand across a burn smoking across my stomach. The skin there knit. I rolled as soon as I hit the ground. There was no time to recover, for the girl was charging at me again. This time I could react.

I twisted beneath a strike that would have snapped my neck. I blocked a blow that would have caved in my chest. The girl was moving faster than my eyes could follow, but her movements were crude. She was relying now on her speed and nothing else. To dodge her, I had to rely on instinct and nothing else. Some of the blows still managed to slip past my guard, but none were enough to put me down. I slowly gave ground to her, one step at a time. The Ember Core's hum was louder and more painful than it usually was.

Damn it. I had been careful with it. I had made sure not to use too much of the Requiem, spending it only when needed. I had thought that would be enough. The Core was grinding and telling me that it was not.

She kicked me under my ribs, hard enough that it flung me high into the air. My whole body lifted, and then I struck the earth, my right leg bending in a direction it was never meant to bend. It took all the willpower of a Queen not to scream. I hissed instead, forcing the Cradle into my leg until the joint snapped back into its socket. That brought with it its own pain, and I hissed again. Louder.

Why is no one stopping this?

The thought came to me, and I disliked it as soon as it did. The girl had done something to herself. Something that went beyond a simple duel. Anyone with eyes could see it. Someone should have ended this. That no one had was an offense. That I had waited for it at all was a greater one. I crushed the thought and rose.

The Archon stood at the ring's edge, his hand half-raised towards the ring. He slowly shook his head and lowered his arm. His choice, at least, was clear. Surely the man had the power to intervene in a contest such as this, but still he held his hand. Why?

Was it because of the vows we had taken before this duel? Had they bound us to see this battle through to the end without any outside interference? Nobody had explained those terms to me, and it seemed whatever this girl had done to herself did not count as 'interference'.

I considered all of this in a single sentence before Lena charged me again, right arm swinging.

I was keeping track of time between the blows. I had fought Ren's Tenet several times in the Sigil resonance chamber, and I knew just how much Essence it required, and it was no small amount. Ren could keep her Tenet up for a minute, perhaps two, before she was exhausted. I had outlasted Lena’s Tenet once before, and I planned to again.

I counted, and the count went past a minute, and past two. Her lightning did not run out. I looked at the girl's face beneath the crawling light. It had gone grey, in the way of all those in the old world who had been completely drained of their mana. This Essence must work the same. The color had drained from Lena’s skin entirely, and yet she still moved. Her Tenet still crackled across her skin.

I suspected why. I suspected it even as that grey skin turned into something thinner, almost sickly. This thing she was doing was feeding on something that went past mere Essence. I could not count on outlasting a flame that ate its own bearer for fuel. Not when I had to keep pace with the Ember Core. I had had a single plan, and now it was useless.

The black spread. What had been thin tendrils crawling through the lightning now spread fully through it like ink. And as it did, I saw more tendrils of black climbing up the girl's skin herself. They climbed up her throat first, then along the line of her jaw, thin dark lines spread across almost white skin. Her voice, when it came, came twisted. "Still standing?" she said, and there was less of Lena in it than there had been. "Why won't you just-" The words came apart in her mouth. I barely understood them at all.

But the girl’s stance was the same as it had been since the start of this fight. Everything else about her was slowly fading like a candle at the end of its wick, but her stance was the same. Out of everything else she had lost, this was the thing she would lose last.

Strangled screams and hushed murmurs began in the stands. Even the most shortsighted of humans could not be blind to this.

Lena came at me and I met her. I put my full weight and Essence into a blow to her face, through the lightning. Bone gave under my knuckles. I felt it break, even as my knuckles themselves cracked.

I just barely flinched from the pain of it. Lena did not, as if the broken thing in her face meant nothing to her at all. She hit me back, harder than before, and I staggered back under the weight of it before I went down to one knee. I sent Ruin through the broken hand, even as I healed it with my left. Where the Ruin met her Tenet, an impression came.

I saw a body, broad and tall and warm. I saw a back turned, walking away into an endless expanse of white. A hand reached out towards it, even knowing the reaching would do nothing.

I shook my head. I had not asked to see that. I did not know whose it was, and I did not want to. I looked up at the girl. One side of her face was caved in from my blow, and yet she still walked forward. I had fought to win. To thoroughly shatter the girl’s pride, such that she would never stand against me again. There was nothing left to shatter now.

Words came to me then, unwanted. Malcis fell to the void today. Zera had to slay him herself. She struck clean. It was a mercy to him.

I had given the phoenix that same mercy. The only mercy I, the last Demon Queen, could give anyone. Every road I knew ended in the same place.

The girl grabbed me. She spun, and the arena spun with her, and she flung me into the dirt at the ring's edge. I pushed myself up on my arms and coughed, and blood splattered on the stone. I healed, and the Cradle came slower now, and the core ground harder for the asking.

I met the girl's eyes, and in them I saw nothing that I recognized. This thing in front of me was not the girl named Lena who had challenged me in the Archon's office. She had not been for some time, I suspected. What she was instead, I did not know. The girl took one slow step after another. Her power -her Tenet- made her too fast for me to follow, and yet now she walked slowly.

Then she froze mid-step. The Tenet guttered around her, blue and black sputtering together, and the girl's whole body spasmed. The black surged. I saw it go up her collarbone, cover her throat, and encompass half her face in a single instant. She screamed.

It was a girl's scream. It cut through the roar of the stands and up into the high dark of the ceiling. Her body was bare and broken. There was no Tenet to guard her now. I saw my chance. I raised my right hand and began to gather the Ruin, more of it than I had allowed myself all fight, enough to end this-

The Ember Core screamed. A spasm went through me, and the Ruin died in my hand. This was a pain worse than the grinding had ever been. It almost felt like the core itself had cracked. It took some time for me to lift my head again, and by the time I did, the screaming had stopped.

It had stopped because there was nothing left of Lena that could scream. All of her was covered in that blackness now. The girl's Tenet of lightning still covered her, but even that had gone from a blue-white to a deep dark. She took a step toward me. Then another.

I looked away from the girl, to where Ash sat. The Hero had risen from her seat, and had one hand on the railing in front of her. One of her legs was over the railing. She had Sol clutched against her chest with her other arm, and I had no doubt that the tiny phoenix was screaming. The fool Hero was a heartbeat away from throwing herself down into the ring.

She met my gaze. Even from this distance, I felt her meet it. I shook my head, just enough that she would see. The Hero had made me swear a promise not so long ago. That if I ever planned to hurt myself, I would go to her first. There was no time to go talk to her now. This would have to serve. Her jaw set and her foot came down. The railing in front of her cracked and shattered under her hand.

The girl clad in black lightning staggered closer.

I reached inside my Essence Core, for whatever bits of my power remained to me. I found it, but what I found only came back wrapped in burning pain. The Ember Core was tearing itself apart and in the midst of that agony, a thought occurred to me.

The power of my Ruin, of the Requiem, could not defeat this. There would always be more of the Tenet beneath, and I might not have outlasted this girl before, let alone now. The Cradle could not touch her either. Ending and Beginning, my two weapons, had both failed me. But I had another. One that lived in the space between the two.

I brought my left and right hands together. I cupped them, like I had once cupped them around a black and gold egg. I reached inside the Ember Core, and this time I fired both Ruin and Bloom into each other. Black met white, and the world went very quiet.

Strange, that it be so silent. The arena was as full as it had been before, and those thousands of voices still shouted and roared. The silence was within me. The grinding pain I had felt since I had first knelt in a chamber of crystal was gone. There was no grinding or humming. There was only silence. Some part of me wanted to weep for it.

Time slowed, or I slowed, or the moment simply opened wide enough to walk around inside. The dust hung motionless in the air. The black lightning crawled across Lena’s body lethargically.

I looked back at the stands, or perhaps I only felt them, somehow, in a moment that stretched unto infinity. The Hero had a foot on what was left of the rail again. Solara was struggling against her.

And I saw Ren. Her mouth hung open. Edda and Corin stared at me with open astonishment. Alisa Wulfram, for once, had her chin down.

My gaze drifted away from them, to another section of the stands. It drifted to Malachor. Both of his heads had the exact same open-mouthed expression. It drifted downwards, to the Archon. The old man's eyes were wide. Lastly, my gaze flicked to the western seats, where a red skinned half-demon had leaned forward in her seat. And in her gaze I felt a hunger I had only known in myself, for something else.

I turned back to my own hands. To the Ruin and Bloom that poured from them. And where the black fire met the white, I saw it.

The thing that came from Ruin and Bloom clashing was neither Black nor White. It was not a color at all. Or rather, it was every single color, and in every single shade I had seen either in this life or the last. In my hands, I saw the red of the crimson sky I had known for more than a thousand years. I saw a blue, with faint grey at the edges. A blue I had only just come to know. I saw gold and black, neither color overpowering the other. It was a Hero, who stood tall in front of my throne and wept over a body in the dark. There were colors I had only seen once before, from a Hero who had burned all of herself and more to slay me. I saw all of them at once, pouring out of the seam where Ending met Beginning. Ruin ate Bloom. Bloom ate Ruin. There was a single second where I saw neither Beginning nor Ending, but the thing that lived between them: a Becoming.

And in that moment, when the world felt very slow and very beautiful and very alive all at once, I thought: Throughout Heaven and Earth I alone am-

I stopped. The railing had cracked under a Hero's hand for my sake. Alone was no longer the correct word. I smiled.

The attack left my hands in a single beam of all of those colors. Their light bloomed across the entirety of the ring. As the beam left, the air around it cracked, splintering in all directions like struck glass. It mended in the same breath. Cracked again. Mended again. The attack crossed the ring and reached Lena.

The world came back all at once, with the deed already done. The black lightning turned. It ended as one thing and began as another in the same instant. It broke away from her, becoming bright as it did. It reached the sky, and turned from something dark into the blue Lena's lightning had been before. That lightning fell apart into a thousand petals of pale blue-white that drifted down through the whole arena.

The girl took one confused step forward. Lena blinked, and for a single heartbeat, the black receded from her face, enough that I could see only the girl she had been. Lena took one more step, staggered, and fell backward. She did not rise. The grinding pain returned.

Of course it did. I had known it would. Even as I had hoped it would not. Still, the humming was back to the way it had been, before the duel. It was manageable. It was beneath a Queen.

The noise of the world crashed back over me. All those shouts. And then, as I began to move, it fell away again, and this time it was because the arena had gone silent, row by row, as I crossed the ring toward the girl. The only sound left was the chirping. And it was proud.

I reached her. Lena lay twitching on her back. Half her face was crisscrossed in fine black lines, spreading and receding, spreading and receding. I had seen this once before. Morrigan had worn a face like this.

I raised my right hand and ignored the pain that came with it. I gathered the Ruin, more of it than I ever had at any one time. The black grew in my palm in the form of a sphere, and it grew until it was large enough to swallow the girl whole and leave nothing at all behind.

Zera had struck her lover clean. I had given the phoenix a clean ending. Both were mercies. I could give one more. It was a familiar road. A single tear slid down the girl's right eye.

She spasmed. Her mouth opened, and a thin line of blood ran down over her lower lip. "Please," Lena whispered. "End it." There was more of her in the words than there had been before.

A trickle of Ruin slipped from my fingers and down into her, the barest thread of it. The impression came. The impression of a body on white sheets. Of closed eyes that would never open again. Of a tall man who had gone down, and would not stand again.

I shook the impressions away. I looked down at the girl who had goaded me, who had tested me. Who had earned my ire as none had in this new life. I made my choice. "This will hurt, girl," I said. The Ruin burned in my open hand, and its light lay black across her broken face. "For what you have done, you deserve worse."
