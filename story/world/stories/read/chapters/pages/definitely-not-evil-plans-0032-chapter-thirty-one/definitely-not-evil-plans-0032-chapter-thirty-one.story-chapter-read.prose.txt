Friday November, 11

Scott was waiting at the cafe next door to the bookshop when I arrived. I got you a pumpkin latte with almond like you had before. If that’s weird or not what you wanted, I can get you something else.”

“That’s not weird. It’s very nice.” I took the cup and wrapped my hands around its warmth. The November air was crisp and the warmth felt nice on my ungloved hands.  “You remembered my order?”

“I have an excellent memory for the coffee preference of the women I accidentally spill drinks on.”

“Women? Is this a common thing for you?”

“Well…” He said trailing off. “There were two of you. Coincidentally with the same order and…” His eyes darted to the side as he tried to find the words to say.

“It’s basic?”

“The definition of basic, I’m afraid.”

“What a shame, I guess I’ll have to find someone who finds my taste in beverages a little more interesting.” I turned as though I were going to walk the other direction.

“Not so fast,” he said, grabbing my wrist and spinning me back around. “I said it was basic, not that it wasn’t interesting. Plus who doesn’t like pumpkin spice?”

“Yeah but all the food that comes in pumpkin spice is way out of control.”

“Exactly. What’s the weirdest pumpkin spice flavored thing you’ve seen?”

“Easy, I said. Bacon. You?”

Scott’s mouth almost fell open. “Well that tops mine, who eats pumpkin spice bacon?”

“Someone I guess.” I shrugged. “What was yours?”

“Pumpkin spice dog treats.”

I laughed, “What? You’re joking.”

“Nope. And the most disappointing part was their lack of calling them ‘pupkin spice’. It was right there!”

“As someone who has spent a lot of time in marketing… I am actually surprised no one called them that.”

Scott nodded solemnly. “Well now that we’re properly depressed, we going to go look at books?”

“Yeah sure.”

The book store Scott knew of was the one I’d visited a few weeks prior, the Dusty Page. The moment the smell hit me, I was almost sad that I hadn’t been back since last time. Old paper, ink and cinnamon. Like happiness and proper escapism.

The bell chimed as we entered.

“Well, well. She returns.” Art commented as I entered and then he caught sight of Scott. “Oh, and I’ve seen you before too. Secret sci-fi lover, this one.”

“Art!” I exclaimed. “It’s so nice to see you.”

He shuffled around the corner of his checkout counter, a mug of coffee in one hand. He looked the same as he had the first time I’d seen him, crooked glasses, cardigan that would make have made Mr. Rogers proud, and a number two pencil perfectly sharpened behind his ear.

“Coffee?” He asked. “I just brewed a fresh pot.”

“Oh, we just got some next door.”

“Not to speak ill of my neighbors, but I don’t know if that even qualifies as coffee.” Art said in a stage whisper. “Well, if you change your mind, please do let me know.”

I chuckled and went to take a sip of my drink, without thinking I adjusted it to my perfect temperature and savored the warmth. It always seemed to comfort in a way that only a seasonal beverage could. Probably something like reminding one of easier times or what it was like to be a child when all there was to worry about was whether or not you had room after turkey for a slice of each flavor of pie without being ill.

Hemingway, who had been walking toward me stopped in his tracks, eyes wide. He took a wide path around us and placed himself between us and Art.

Art picked Hemingway up, “You seeing ghosts today old boy? Come on, let’s get you a snack.” He then turned to us, “Well you both know your way around, you know where to find me.”

“Alright,” Scott said. “I didn’t realize you’d been here already. I’m not doing too hot on dates.”

“I’ve only been here once, I’m plenty excited to be here again,” I reassured him.

He sighed. “So, what’s your guilty pleasure genre?”

“I don’t have guilty pleasures.”

“Don’t lie. Everyone has one.”

“No, no. I have pleasures–I’m just not guilty about them.”

“Diplomatic and yet evasive.”

“Oh yeah, well what’s your guilty pleasure then?”

“I really enjoy power fantasies,” he said simply. “You know, guy gets access to a system and all these powers and becomes just incredibly strong, and fights these huge monsters.”

“Huh, I have never tried any of those.”

He took me to the section in the store. “There aren’t a lot in print, but there are a few. He picked up one and handed it to me. This one is super fun. Tons of puns. Check it out some time.”

I read the synopsis on the back survival, fighting, growing and distant dimensions. It sounded interesting. An interface to keep track of the things you were good or bad at would be helpful.

I nodded and held onto it, maybe it’d give me some ideas.

“So, I showed you mine, now you show me yours.”

I took him down the aisle a little further. He pulled a book off of the shelf at random. “Let’s see. ‘The Vampire Lord’s Forbidden Bride.’ That sounds completely normal.”

“Those books are very popular.”

“I’m sure they are.” He reshelved the book and looked patiently at me.

“Throne of Light” I grabbed it off of the shelf.

Scott took it and examined the back, “Is this the one with the fae politics you mentioned at the arcade?”

“I mentioned fae politics at the arcade?”

“You were explaining how unfair one of the games was. Said it was…” he trailed off as he tried to remember my phrasing. “more corrupt than the seeping court.”

“Seelie court,” I corrected.  “But how did you put two and two together?”

“I googled the word when you went to the restroom. Obviously I forgot the word, but I remembered the thing about it being a fae court.”

He began reading the back of the book in a very dramatic voice, “Aurora never expected to be caught between two lines of royalty. Will her future fall in line with the incredibly handsome Careon, King of the Dawn court. Or, will her future be something far darker than even she could expect.”

“This sounds pretty dramatic.”  He continued in his normal voice.

“It is. The whole series is dramatic. There’s a prophecy and secret bloodlines, and this whole war between the courts. Aurora starts as an ordinary human but then her best friend turns out to be the princess of the Dawn and brings her into the court to be betrothed to her brother. Then she has to decide what it means to become fae and what her path holds and whether she will give into the prophecy or not.” I flushed. “Oh sorry, I’m rambling.”

“No, keep going.” Scott lowered himself to the floor and sat cross legged. “I want to hear everything. Sit.” He patted the floor. “Come on.”

“There’s zero chance you’re interested in faerie politics.”

“I’m interested in you and the stuff that makes you excited like that.” He patted the floor again. “Let’s go. From the beginning.”

So I sat on the remarkably clean floor of the Dusty Page, latte in hand and told Scott about Aurora. About how Aurora was supposedly plain and unremarkable but obviously was going to turn out to be special.  I talked about the Seelie and Unseelie courts, the differences between them. The Dawn and the Shadow bloodlines and the vast differences in creatures that inhabited each realm.

Scott listened, really truly listened, seemingly enraptured by my recounting of all of the lore. He asked questions like ‘If they’re in different realms why are they at war,’ and ‘So Seelie is good and Unseelie is evil.’ and seemingly took all of my responses as law.

“The fifth book comes out next month. I’ve been counting down. It’s supposed to be the big climax of the series.”

“And you listen to them on audiobook?”

“I do, I usually never had time to just sit and read so audiobooks made it easier to work while enjoying. The narrator does all of the voices. She’s incredible. Very dramatic for the romance scenes.”

“The romance scenes?” Scott raised an eyebrow. “How romantic are we talking.”

“These are known for being five chili peppers.”

He blinked. “I take it that’s a lot?”

“Very spicy.” I confirmed trying my damnedest to keep the embarrassment off of my face.

“Faerie porn!”

“It’s called romantasy.”

“It sounds like HBO fantasy.”

“Oh come on.”

He grinned. “Okay, I have a proposal. I’m going to buy this book and read it.” He held up the first book in the series. “And then we’re going to have a serious discussion on these politics and secret bloodlines.”

“You are absolutely not going to read that.”

“Oh I absolutely am. I need to understand what I’m dealing with here.” He stood and offered me his hand. “Come on. Let’s see what other treasures this place has.”

We wandered for an hour. Hemingway followed us but stayed out of arms reach, the entire time. Scott ended up buying the Throne of Light, plus a spy thriller that looked to be one of several dozen thin novels all by the same author. Kind of a whodunit mystery series. I picked up that power fantasy book, and a genderbent romance about a modern take on Frankenstein.

Art rang us up at the counter. Hemingway jumped up and observed from his throne on the counter. Art carefully wrapped each book in brown paper and tied it together with a string, which was so charming I couldn’t help but smile.

When he got to Scott’s selection he paused, “I see she talked you into it.”

“She certainly made a compelling case,” Scott smiled while he nodded.

“You won’t be disappointed in this one. The book really puts in work.” Art carefully wrapped and smoothed the paper down with patience and love, “Margaret read it first and she said, ‘Arthur, you need to sit down and read this immediately.’ when I told her it wasn’t my thing she said, ‘not yet. And then knowing just how to finish an argument she said, ‘and if you object there will be no dinner tonight.’”

He handed Scott his wrapped and tied package. “She was right. Come back and tell me what you think. I have opinions that I look forward to sharing.”

“You seem like you have opinions on a great many things Arthur.” I said with a smile.

“Art, please. And listen, at seventy-four years old all I have left is opinions.”

Hemingway yowled.

“Opinions and Hemingway. Anyway, we have a bookclub that meets the first Saturday of every month. Only three regulars show up and two of them are over seventy. We could use some fresh blood.”

“I’ll think about it,” I said with a wide smile.

His face lit up with such genuine excitement and hope that I felt a pang in my chest I wasn’t quite sure of.

“You two have a great day and come back soon. Hemingway gets lonely.”

Hemingway yawned and curled up on his bed, both eyes trained on me.

At the cafe next door we got a table by the window and the chair wobbled beneath me. I reached out for the code and found the short leg without even thinking about it before bringing it up to match the other three. The wobble stopped.

“Much better,” I sighed.

“Hm?” Scott looked up from his pastry.

“Oh nothing. Just getting comfortable.”

We talked until the cafe began to close and tear down for the night. We spoke about our families. His mom was a retired teacher in Ohio, his dad had been a tradesman and his sister was very into crossfit. He shared that he’d gone to school for computer science at a state school before working his way up in IT. Then he started his own consulting business and how nice it was to only have to work until the job was done. ‘No eight to five bullshit’ was how he put it.

We talked about what we wanted out of life. It was all very vague but came down to a lot of beating around the bush and not a lot of saying much. Just two people making noise at each other when they didn’t know what else to talk about but didn’t want the conversation to end.

“This has been really nice,” Scott said as we walked out into the night. “I know I keep saying that but it keeps being true.”

“It has been nice,” I agreed. I hugged my book parcel to my chest. “Thank you, for the coffee and the book store. And, for not making fun of my fae smut.”

“Excuse me,” He replied. “I think the proper terminology is “Romantasy.”

“Shut up.” I said laughing.

“Same time next week? I’ll have read at least half of the book by then. Probably.”

“Deal.”

This time when we got to my car he hugged me. A real hug, warm and lingering. I breathed in the smell of him, something woodsy and coffee. I tried not to think about the last hug I’d gotten and how much I missed her.

“Get home safe,” he said.

“You too.”

I watched him walk away and found myself looking forward to something other than watching Greg incessantly as I  slowly forced everyone around him away and left him alone and ready to face punishment for his crimes.

The only thing missing in my life was someone to share all of this with. I knew who that someone was, I just hoped that it wasn’t too far gone to save.
