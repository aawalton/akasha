With Vivi being taken care of by the Duke’s staff and Selene off chasing her own leads, Oswald walked alone now. Old March was waiting for him.

At first, nothing gave it away. Just the usual city noise, the dull murmur of voices, iron shop signs creaking in the wind. But then came the smell of rot, smoke, and something metallic clinging to the air. Looks like I'm close.

He followed the scent and the slow change in the stone beneath his feet. The paving grew uneven, fractured, with tufts of moss pushing through the gaps. Paint peeled from the shutters of buildings. Iron railings rusted where they curled around cracked stairs. A man sat slouched under a boarded archway, hood drawn low, hands tucked into his sleeves, watching.

Yet, Oswald kept walking until the river came into view, cutting across the heart of the city. Its waters moved sluggishly, grey and opaque, reflecting nothing but the overcast sky. Piles of trash floated along its banks.

A single stone bridge arched across it, hunched like a spine. Ivy clung to the sides in patches, and black stains streaked along the undersides like soot that rain couldn’t wash away. There weren't any guards. Just a few figures crossing now and then with their heads down. That’s it, Old March is just across that bridge.

Well, too late to turn back now. Oswald thought, stepping onto the bridge.

The bridge emptied into a tight corridor of alleys. Oswald moved through them like a shadow, boots brushing through loose gravel and rain-slick mud. The buildings here leaned over him, blotting out the sky with crooked timber and sagging rooftops. Broken lanterns swung overhead, their iron fixtures groaning in the wind.

He slipped into a narrower lane, the noise of the main street fading behind him. One hand dipped into his coat, pulling out the note Selene had left for him. Looks like she wants me to track down Senna Vale first. Apparently she is one of the top dogs in the Red Dragoons, right under Aldric Marr himself.

His eye scanned the lines again. Says she hangs around some arena… that's underground? How tam I supposed to figure that out? Says here that the duke's men has been tryin' to find the place for a long time, but they got nowhere.

He folded the paper and tucked it back into his coat. Hmm, you know what? Maybe I can make her find me instead. But if I want her to show up... I gotta give her a reason.

With every step deeper into Old March, the air grew heavier. The first thing Oswald noticed was the smell. It clung low to the ground, rising in damp waves. Maybe it was due to all the piss and puddles of other strange liquids, sour and stinging. This place’s rotting from the inside out.

Oswald pulled his cloak tighter as if that would guard him from the stench. It didn’t.

Further in, he saw lusters of people lining the broken streets and sagging stoops, leaning against stained brick walls. Most clutched small flasks or shallow glass vials filled with a what appears to be a strange silvery liquid.

Siren's Tears. The stuff glinted faintly in the low light, catching on their fingertips as they brought it to their lips.

One man swayed in place. His mouth hung slightly open, and drool had begun to thread down his chin. Another woman sat cross-legged in the mud, her fingers twitching erratically as she muttered half-formed words under her breath, staring into a crack in the wall.

Others weren’t still at all, jittery limbs, darting glances, sudden flinches from nothing. Some twitched violently when touched, recoiling as though burned. Wow, it's everywhere. They're drowning in this stuff.

His boots crunched against broken glass as he moved through the haze of the addicted, his gaze catching on every glint of silver. There had to be dozens just on this one block. And this was only a sliver of Old March. He didn't need to look far to understand that Siren’s Tears ruled the place.

Then, a sudden weight crashed into him. Oswald staggered a step back as a man lunged forward, grabbing him by the front of his cloak. The man’s eyes bulged with wild hunger, bloodshot and yellowed.

“D’you got the Tears? You got it? I’ll pay, I swear please, just a taste, just a drop—”

“Get off me!” Oswald shouted, shoving him hard.

The man stumbled backward, arms flailing, then turned and sprinted away down the alley, still muttering to himself. His bare feet slapped against the cobbles, the sound fading quickly into the filthy hush of the street.

Oswald stood still for a moment, heart thudding. Whatever that stuff is… it’s gotta be some strong shit, twisting people up like that.

He clenched his jaw and moved on. Much as the situation in Old March disgruntled him, Oswald had a mission to do.

Oswald clenched his jaw and kept moving. The more time he spent in Old March, the worse it sat in his gut.

He turned down another narrow street and caught sight of a woman slumped against a crumbling wall, her shoulders trembling with each breath. Wet coughing rattled from her chest, and a stained cloth covered her mouth, but it didn’t catch the dark smear spreading along its edge. Her eyes were glassy, sunken deep into the sockets.

Just beyond her, two more figures sat in the shade of a warped awning, one rocking slowly, the other too still. A man passed Oswald on the other side of the street, dragging a cart with a bundled shape in it, limbs tucked awkwardly beneath a canvas sheet. No one looked twice.

Maiden’s Bane, Oswald thought, his eye narrowing. Looks a lot like the stuff I saw back in Brightmarch. Same damn cough. But back in the proper city, it was a few people. Looks like it's all over the streets here.

His fingers twitched near his belt, but he didn’t stop. His boots crunched over loose stone and broken glass as he pressed forward. I wanna help them. But what can I do right now? Can’t fix this on my own.

The best move, the only move, really, was to dig deeper into the Red Dragoons. Selene’s note had pointed him toward that. If the Dragoons were tied to the Siren’s Tears, they’d know more than they let on. And if they weren’t? Maybe they still knew something about that progenitor freak the Duke had mentioned.

Either way, he wasn’t getting answers standing around. Eventually, the street opened slightly. Up ahead, wedged between two leaning warehouses, sat a wide, sagging building. Its walls slumped like shoulders under too much weight. The roof bowed in the middle, with missing tiles that left large gaps.

Yet, from inside, sound poured out. Light leaked through the filthy windows in fractured streaks, like the building couldn’t hold onto it. Empty bottles littered the steps, glinting in the gutter where a man lay face-down, half-covered by his own tattered cloak, snoring into the grime. Yeah… if I were trouble, this’d be my tavern of choice.

The closer Oswald got, the louder the chaos grew. Music pounded like a racing heartbeat from somewhere inside, muffled by the walls but thick enough to feel. Smoke poured from a side window, curling up the stone like choking vines. The whole building gave off a heat, like it was sweating booze and bad decisions.

Three men stood near the door, shifting as he approached. Broad-shouldered, scarred, all wearing the look of men who liked hurting things. Their coats were stained, loose over grimy shirts marked with grease and chain scuffs. One held a half-empty bottle, swinging it lazily at his side.

The man in the middle stepped forward, rolling his neck with a crack like splitting wood. “Oi, kid. Ain’t free to walk in.”

The second, lanky and rat-faced, grinned with yellow teeth. “You want in, you pay the fee. Twenty coin.”

The third said nothing. Square-jawed, arms crossed, eyes locked on Oswald like he was counting how many ribs he could break before someone screamed.

Oswald stopped a few paces out. “I don’t feel like payin’."

The first thug snorted. “Then you don’t feel like drinkin’. Off you go, runt.”

Oswald tilted his head slightly, eyes narrowing. “I ain’t goin’ anywhere. What I am gonna do… is walk through that door. So unless you’re real keen on coughin’ up teeth, I’d get outta the way.”

The second man barked a laugh and stepped in close, jabbing a dirty finger at Oswald’s chest. “Listen to this rat, you think you can talk big in Old March? You got no idea who you’re dealin’ with."

“Relax, I'm actually lookin’ out for you. Just get out of the way, and nobody has to get hurt."

“You little—!”

The thug swung. But Oswald caught the man’s wrist before it reached him. Bone shifted under his grip, followed by a sharp pop as joints snapped under pressure. The man’s mouth opened to scream, but the sound never made it out.

Oswald drove his other hand into the thug’s ribs. The breath whooshed out of him as his feet left the ground. He slammed into the wall with a thud that shook the boards, leaving behind a smear of blood and a groan that dropped into silence.

The second thug lunged, bottle raised, but Oswald moved first, faster than most people had a right to move. His foot snapped out low, sweeping the man’s legs out from under him. The thug hit the ground with a smack that echoed across the square, wind punched from his lungs.

The third one hesitated for a few seconds. But it was enough.

Oswald was on him in two steps, driving a shoulder into his gut and shoving him back. The man’s feet left the ground as Oswald lifted and threw him, clean across the narrow alley and into the wall of the neighboring building. The thud shook loose dust from the windowsills.

That should be enough of a statement.  Oswald thought, flexing one hand.

He turned back, stepping over the groaning pile of men, his breath even, Oswald adjusted his collar and looked toward the door. One of the hinges creaked under the weight of the impact, but it still held. That oughta get someone's attention.

Then, without another word, he pushed it open and stepped inside. Sweat, smoke, and alcohol hung in the air, clinging to the skin like oil. Light came from crooked chandeliers with half their candles melted into waxy stalactites, their glow dimmed by a fog of pipe smoke that drifted beneath the ceiling beams.

The place was crowded with men and women hunched over drinks and dice. Some laughed loud and bitter, others murmured quiet deals through clenched teeth. Coins clinked, mugs slammed, chairs scraped. Boots stomped to some half-drunken rhythm thudding out from a back corner where a blind fiddler played with more rage than skill.

A woman with a scarred jaw threw her head back and cackled at something a man whispered into her ear. Another guy coughed hard into his sleeve, then lit a pipe with a hand that wouldn’t stop shaking. One man had iron studs hammered into his shoulder plates, and a woman passed him wearing rings on every finger.

Every eye in the bar turned to Oswald. He didn’t say a word. Just moved across the warped floorboards and dropped himself onto a rickety stool at the bar. It creaked under his weight but didn’t give way. He leaned his elbows on the counter, pulling his hood down.

The bald bartender looked over with a dry expression. He kept wiping out the inside of a chipped mug with a cloth that might’ve once been white. “What’ll it be?”

“Cider,” Oswald said.

The bartender squinted at him. “You look a bit young for drinkin’, boy.”

“You look a bit old for smart talk, but I ain’t pointin’ fingers. Just do your damn job. Or else.” Oswald said.

The man stared at him for a long second, then let out a snort and a crooked smile. “Tough little rat. Alright.”

He shuffled off down the bar, muttering under his breath.nOswald let his hand dip into his coat. Fingers brushed parchment again. He slipped the note out and opened it just enough to read. "So this dump’s called the Rat’s Nest, huh… can’t say it’s a bad name, all things considered."

He folded it back quickly and tucked it into an inner pocket. The cider came in a dull tin mug, still cold, rim bent in one place. Oswald took a sip, it was sour and a little bitter, but clean enough to pass.

At the same time, he let his gaze drift. The smoke and noise made it hard to focus, but Oswald watched the room carefully.

He checked for ink, the kind that tells you who someone bleeds for. Sure enough he spotted many people with tattoos of a red horse, the symbol of the Red Dragoons.

A few of them were already watching him. Not openly, just glances over shoulders, sideways looks. And when he met their eyes, they dropped their gazes fast. Word’s travelin’.

He reached for another sip when the bartender slid something across the counter toward him. It was a glass filled with a strange liquid. It was frosted, chipped, and filled with something deep red, thick like syrup, with little flecks of crushed ice floating on top. It smelled faintly sweet, but sharp underneath.

The bartender didn’t look at him. “On the house, a gift.”

Oswald frowned. “No thanks.”

“If you know what’s good for you, boy... you’ll drink.”

Oswald didn’t touch the glass. Not yet.

Condensation clung to the rim, droplets sliding down the curve and pooling at the base like sweat under pressure. The liquid inside shimmered under the bar’s hanging lanterns, catching fractured light that did nothing to make it look more inviting.

His eyes drifted sideways. More of them were watching now, Red Dragoons leaning on banisters, posted against doorways, half-lost in shadows. Their stares weren’t casual. They were waiting.

He turned back to the bartender. “Who’s buyin’?”

The man just gave a slight twitch of the chin toward the crowd, barely more than a gesture. “One of them.

Oswald’s jaw shifted slightly. Right. Ain’t just a drink, it’s a leash. And they wanna see if I wear it.

He picked up the glass. Not to drink it, just to make a point.

Then he stood. Conversation at nearby tables faltered as he moved, and boots scuffed the floor in a slow ripple as men leaned away, as if clearing space for a storm they didn’t fully understand.

Oswald crossed the tavern with the glass still in hand. The smoke in the air thickened as he neared the back, curling around the low lights like it had been waiting for something to stir.

He stopped in front of the table where the stares were strongest. Half a dozen Dragoons sat there, rough-faced and draped in patched coats, the red horse insignia visible on more than one arm. Knives tucked into belts. Bottles half-empty. Eyes tracking him with the slow calculation of people used to getting their way.

“Which one of you’s buyin’?” Oswald asked.

One of them leaned forward. Gold glinted between cracked lips, a single tooth catching the lamplight as he smiled. “The boss wants a word, but you don’t get to see him unless you drink.”

Oswald’s fingers tightened slightly around the glass.. Didn’t mention a 'she'... so probably not Senna Vale. Doesn’t mean the one runnin’ things ain’t worth meetin’. Could be someone with answers.

He placed the glass on the edge of their table, the base clinking softly against the rough wood.“Not drinkin’ it, but I’d still like to meet your boss.”

Immediately after, one of the Dragoons pushed back from his chair, hand sliding to rest on the hilt of a short sword at his side. Another dragged his boot along the floor, shifting a chair back with a loud scrape.

But Oswald didn’t move. “Really You dipshits wanna do this now?”
