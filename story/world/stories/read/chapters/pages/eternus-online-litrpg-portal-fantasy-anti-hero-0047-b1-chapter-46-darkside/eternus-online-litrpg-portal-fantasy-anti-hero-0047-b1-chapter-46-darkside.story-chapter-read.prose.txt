“This place is huge!”

Kate and Romulus turned toward Maya at her words and then followed her gaze to the rest of the throne room as they emerged from the transit chamber. The throne room spread around them in all its glory, lit by bright blue lichfire torches. The vaulted chamber was, Kate conceded, incredibly impressive—enough so that she still found herself staring at it now and then. Platinum veins crossed the black marble beneath their feet, reflecting the Lichfire in pale lines of shimmering precious metal all the way toward the distant main entrance and the various doorways and stairwells lining the chamber.

Old banners covered the walls, preserved for long-departed orders, and the distant balcony overlooking the city from the heart of the Necropolis lurked above them—unused by Romulus and Kate thus far, but no less awesome for the lack of their exploration.

“All of this is yours?” Nate asked while they made their way slowly toward the throne dais.

“All of it,” Romulus confirmed with a grin that made Kate’s heart jump. “At least, everything up to the citadel’s outer bailey. The city itself is more complicated, like I told you before we tethered, but I’ll get back to that in a bit.”

“I get the feeling we have a lot to cover,” Cora said, her arm still in Kate’s while Maya detached herself to investigate the floor, bending near the dais to trace her hand over a platinum vein with quiet wonder.

“Before that,” Nate said while following Romulus up the steps, “you mentioned something earlier that I’d like to ask Mortarius to clarify, if he’s willing, Dee.”

Romulus raised an eyebrow as he settled into the throne, while Maya and Corva joined Nate near the throne, and Kate settled herself comfortably on the armrest, letting her feet hang from the height of it. Her balance wavered for a split second, and she adjusted quickly, but not before Romulus’s armored arm slid around her waist, holding her steady by gripping her belt near her hip. Her heart stuttered again at the swiftness of his movement, and she forcibly reminded herself she’d been steadied by him dozens of times in the past.

That didn’t stop her stomach from fluttering, nor did it stop the meaningful glances Maya and Cora sent her way, which she opted to firmly ignore.

“You can ask whatever you want, brother,” Romulus said while gesturing at Mortarius. “I can’t promise he’ll answer, though.”

“You are learning,” Mortarius said in response while giving Romulus a pleased look, and then turned to Nate. “You may ask, Nathanius.”

“Thanks,” Nate said, setting his shield down against the floor upright and leaning on it while he considered the specter. “Dee and Kay have given us some basic insight, but I wanted to know what being a Crowned Power actually entails. As in, what does that actually mean? Information on it in our world is kind of vague.”

Kate felt her own curiosity rise despite having heard something of it from Mortarius, and she felt Romulus’s attention sharpen as well. Neither of them interrupted as Mortarius considered the question.

“A Power, as you may already know, is someone who has transcended the limits of normal mortal advancement. On Eternus, that means reaching Paragon, at level three hundred, and then discerning the knowledge necessary to advance further into a new tier of existence. From there, one becomes a nascent Power.”

“Is that like a Demi-God?” Cora asked while spreading her robes and settling down on the dais’s top stair.

“Not quite,” Mortarius said in reply. “That is not wholly inaccurate, but it would be better to phrase a nascent Power as a being who has transcended age but has not acquired true Dominion.”

“Dominion with a capital ‘D’?” Cora asked immediately.

“Yes,” Mortarius answered, and Cora gave a satisfied nod.

Maya raised her hand next, and Kate saw Romulus grin out of the corner of her eye while Mortarius turned to Maya.

“Yes, Mayarella?” the specter asked with faint amusement.

“Are there levels to being a Power? Like how we have Novice, Initiate, Adept, so on? I assume so, but our information is shaky at best, and I’ve done a lot of research.”

“There are,” Mortarius confirmed while inclining his head in approval. “After ascending to become a nascent Power—the lowest rank after Paragon’s rank thirty—the pathway to apotheosis begins. You may consider the hierarchy to correlate to levels of attainment relating to Dominions. None are as powerful as Divinarchs, and even the most accomplished Power remains a pale imitation of a Divinarch, but there are ranks that follow Nascent Power, starting from Minor Power to Major Power, Major Power to Supreme Power, and Supreme Power to Crowned Power. Above a Crowned Power is the Divinarch to whom that Power owes allegiance.”

Their friends nodded as Mortarius spoke, and Nate folded his arms with a thoughtful look. “So, Dominions, then. What does that mean? We know that Divinarchs have Dominions, but what does that really mean practically?”

“A good question, and one too few ask,” Mortarius said with approval before looking across them all. “A Dominion is not simply a thing a Divinarch claims authority over; it is best described as a force of reality. These forces are not always inherently equal, nor are they always congruent with what one might expect. Sylvaine, the Evergreen Mother, for example, holds Dominion over Nature, Fertility, Beasts, Renewal, and Instinct—while Pyranthos, the Crucible Lord, holds Dominion over Fire, Forging, Transformation, Passion, and Destruction.”

“I get it,” Kate added abruptly. “Instinct and Passion. There’s immediate overlap there.”

“There is,” Mortarius agreed, “yet they remain distinct. Dominions are not fixed concepts inasmuch as they are amalgamated ideations of core precepts of reality. The True Dominions are the seven Affinities: Light, Nature, Fire, Air, Earth, Water, and Darkness. All other Dominions are aspects of reality that either predate Eternus or were manifested through its people.”

“So something could be motivated by either Instinct or Passion,” Maya said thoughtfully, “but they’re still fundamentally different motivations, despite creating a similar or even near-identical result, and the Dominions remain distinct.”

“That is accurate,” Mortarius confirmed. “Dominions may often overlap. That does not mean they function identically.”

“Then how do Powers fit in? You were a Crowned Power, so does that have a tie-in to Divinarchs?” Nate asked, leaning forward on his shield with interest. “Dee told us you were ancient and warned us to be polite, but I’m realizing he may not have fully covered your actual badassery.”

Romulus gave Nathanius a betrayed look from the throne, as if his best friend had thrown him under the bus, and Kate laughed while settling her hand on the Revenant-King’s shoulder, squeezing his neck gently in support while Mortarius allowed the ghost of a smile to touch his spectral features.

“Romulus’s measurement of my former capabilities may have been ignorant by omission, not malice, though I appreciate your enthusiasm,” Mortarius said, causing Kate to laugh again while her best friend groaned into a palm in betrayal. “The correlation between a Crowned Power and a Dominion is one of stewardship, in the simplest sense. Every Power must understand and resonate with a Dominion to ascend. A Crowned Power must embody one with their entire being. As the Crowned Power of Death, I was entrusted by the Dark Lady with the governance of the Cycle—not in Her place, but at Her side. I could stop life with a glance, hold age and death in abeyance, and command the unliving to return in service once more.”

The specter’s clarification settled over the group without fanfare, and yet Kate felt her own eyes widen slightly at the former Autarch’s words. Nate became still at the revelation, Corva let out a low whistle, and Maya’s gaze became more attentive.

In gaming terms, Mortarius was essentially a late end-game raid boss on steroids, one that could have wiped an entire group of players by looking at them, while simultaneously summoning undead to aid him in the fight. The implied power, even with the minimal understanding she possessed, was staggering.

“When you say end life with a glance or hold death in abeyance, what does that mean exactly?” Cora asked, her ears twitching unconsciously as she half-raised her hand. “I mean, I sort of get it, but I’m also still a bit confused. You mean you could just stop someone from dying or end their life like”—she snapped her fingers—“that?”

“In practice, yes. In more detail, not that simply, no. It would be more accurate to say that I could extend my authority to dictate their relationship to the Cycle. Holding death in abeyance was a matter of simply willing my influence upon reality to invert the natural erosion of life force in a chosen mortal, permitting them to enjoy a set amount of extra vitality that would defy the natural procession toward their death.”

“So, in short, you were literally Death walking?” Nate asked in a subdued tone.

“In a manner of speaking,” Mortarius agreed.

“And how long did it take you to become a Crowned Power?” Romulus asked finally, settling his hands on Lightsbane as he watched his predecessor, while Kate gently massaged the back of his neck and listened as well. “I’m asking for definitely unrelated reasons to my own survival.”

Kate and the others smirked as Mortarius turned to Romulus and regarded him calmly.

“It took me over a century to achieve my status,” the specter stated steadily, “and that was only after the time it took me to become a nascent Power, which spanned decades in a world where opportunities for the Dark Faith to test itself were far less perilous than those you are faced with.”

Romulus deflated at the news and sighed. “Okay, fine, no becoming a Demi-God for me any time soon.”

“Thank God…” Nate murmured. “You’re bad enough already, brother.”

“Traitor!” Romulus groused, while Kate, Maya, and Cora laughed.

“You need not fear too much, Romulus,” Mortarius said after they settled. “After all, you have an advantage I lacked.”

“Oh?” Romulus asked with immediate interest while raising an eyebrow.

“Yes,” Mortarius continued. “I did not become Autarch until I was already a Death Knight and Sanguine Revenant, two centuries after my birth. You are unique, Romulus. No Autarch in Eternian history has ever been as young or as underdeveloped as you are now. That means your challenges will be greater, yes—it also means your growth will be far more explosive. You are beginning from a low enough rank that your ability to increase your Traits and Skills will not suffer from an experience bottleneck.”

“What’s a Death Knight?” Nate asked the moment Mortarius finished. “Please enlighten us, I mean,” Nate added respectfully. “You can’t mention that and then not tell us.”

The former Autarch favored Nate with a wry smile before inclining his head to the request.

“Death Knights are warriors whose service in life earns them continuance past death, and who are raised to serve the Dark Lady beyond the constraints of their mortal life,” Mortarius explained while Nate’s eyes brightened in a way Kate knew meant he’d found a new obsession. “Death Knights are gifted Revenant Runeblades, such as Romulus’s Lightsbane”—every eye turned to the sword, which vibrated visibly enough that Romulus grinned—“and sworn to the service of the Dark Lady, after which they are charged with the protection of the Faithful. It is not something earned simply by dying at the first possible chance. It is earned through genuine, required, and chosen sacrifice.”

“And I take it that isn’t something I’m qualified for yet?” Nate asked with faint disappointment.

“Not as of yet,” Mortarius said almost gently. “There will be time to learn more in the future.”

“Thanks,” Nate said sincerely. “Sorry for interrupting.”

Mortarius inclined his head, though Kate noticed he seemed unbothered by the interjection.

“Um. What about us, Mortarius?” Maya asked after a moment. “Does that mean we’re out of luck? We were hoping to help him with his whole Revenant-King thing.”

Nate and Cora nodded, but Kate kept her eyes on Mortarius, who smiled at them mildly.

“Not at all,” the specter said. “Allow me to tell you about why you are so lucky to be brought here, and precisely what awaits you on the other side of your [Dark Benediction].”

Kate grinned as she remembered her own audience with the Dark Lady.

This, she thought to herself, is going to be good.
