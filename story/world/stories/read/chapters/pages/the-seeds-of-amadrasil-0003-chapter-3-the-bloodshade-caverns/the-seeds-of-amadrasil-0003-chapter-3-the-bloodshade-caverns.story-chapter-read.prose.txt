Hunters call them Betrayer’s Gates. That’s sentimental nonsense. A portal never promised to let you come home.

When the entrance turns to silver and disappears, you are alone. No reinforcements. No rescue. You can wait for the portal to mature, tear itself open, and spill its monsters into Valmyra—or you can finish the delve.

Stay together, find the anchor, and break it.

Worldshield gives you a fifteen-percent chance of coming home. I suggest you improve the odds.

- Darius “Cinder” Okoro, S-ranked Guildmaster of Dark Phoenix, address to the 24 FPE graduating class of Worldshield’s Monster Hunter Academy

The first sign that something was wrong came the same moment he entered the portal. Kal’s mana exposure badge chirped in sudden alarm as he pushed his way into the shimmering blue veil of energy and mana dense enough to prickle under his skin. The surface bent and warped around him like a viscous, stretchy soap film, pulling against his insides as he passed through the threshold. Even with several portals under his belt, he still hadn’t gotten used to the disconcerting sensation of pushing himself through a tactile mana boundary, but this was far more intense. His vision filled with the blue radiance, punctuated with disturbing blotches of crimson and wisps of black that made him shiver whenever they got close.

An instant later, the clinging veil slid past, releasing him, and he found himself choking in the suddenly cloying humid air, dense with a metallic scent that seemed to seep in through every pore. He staggered, suddenly hit with a wave of dizziness.

“You ok?” Erin whispered, steadying his arm.

“Yeah, just dizzy,” he muttered. “What’s up with that portal?” He glanced back at it to find the edges were undulating sinuously, as if diaphanous curtains were becoming detached from the sides.

She raised an eyebrow and glanced at his mana exposure badge, but, to his relief, she changed the subject. “I’m not sure—Brannic said it’s normal variance. Stick with me, alright? We made a good team last time.”

“Ok,” he said, offering a quick smile of gratitude. He hauled his cart the rest of the way through the portal veil and moved off to the side to allow the others to enter and then glanced around.

Brannic had the E-rank hunters already forming a competent perimeter around the portal, guarding the broad ledge on which they emerged.

The cavern roof soared far above, glinting with the sparkling light of bioluminescent fungi that pierced the oppressive gloom like constellations of stars. A constant patter of dripping sounded like rain as water fell onto unnaturally slender metallic stalagmites and filtered down into softly glowing crimson pools that stank of rust. Kal peered over the edge, trying to pierce the dense shadows nearly fifteen feet below, but the darkness seemed to pool among the sharp spikes, shifting and waving with restless energy.

Somewhere in the darkness, something screeched.

I guess Rigel nailed it. Again. This is no goblin world.

Kal glanced to his left as someone joined him at the ledge—one of the fresh faces. She pulled out her phone and held it up to take a picture, and immediately, it sizzled and sparked, and the screen went dark. “Shit,” she cursed.

“Didn’t they tell you at orientation? Tech always goes screwy inside portal worlds,” Tess said, her words a little too rushed as she played with her flames. “It’s the mana. You quickly learn to leave your phone in the lockers.”

Some simple electronics worked, but even with shielding, it was often touch-and-go. The more complex the device, the more sensitive it was to mana interference. Kal had learned that the hard way, just like this new girl. Expensive lesson. That was a new phone. Good model, too.

“So, just where the fuck are we?” Drex demanded. “I thought we were supposed to have trees and animals and shit.”

Something sparked, and the light dimmed. Kal snapped his head around. With a strangely distant tearing sound, the remaining edges of the portal detached, and it collapsed in on itself and vanished, leaving behind a reality-bending ripple through which nothing more than a shimmering reflection could be seen.

“Mirror portal,” Sarn said, biting his words off sharply.

“A Betrayer’s Gate? Fuck!” Drex swore.

“Obviously not the world we were sold,” Brannic said, drily. “You need to be prepared for anything when you enter a portal.”

Drex frowned, clearly not happy with being lectured.

Kal stared at the shimmering remnants of the portal and their only way back to Valmyra with a sinking feeling in the pit of his stomach. Now what? How do I get back? Mira’s going to be so worried!

“Sarn, your profile says you have a stealth skill already,” Brannic said, ignoring the grumpy fighter. “Go scout up ahead and see what we’re dealing with.”

“Right,” he said. The masked rogue turned away and, to Kal’s surprise, faded into the shadows as he walked toward a dark tunnel entrance leading off to the right.

That was Stealth! Actually Stealth… There were many weaker ‘knock off’ skills available to rogues, assassins, and those who wished to move without being noticed—but almost all of them were based on the common Sneak variants found predominantly in the many goblin portal worlds. He must have earned that skill from an Elf or something even more exotic.

“It’s demons,” Kal said quietly, his eyes still lingering on the tunnel, trying to catch even a glimpse of the vanished hunter.

“What was that?” Brannic said, turning immediately.

“My friend outside is a tech. He said we’re probably looking at demons. Bloodshade demons, if this cavern is any indication.” Kal had never delved into a demon portal, but he had done his homework. While relatively uncommon, there were several variants of demonic realms that opened on Valmyra, and each had its quirks. The unnaturally dark shadows and the crimson pools were often called out as characteristic of Bloodshade demon realms.

“Nobody cares about what your friend said,” Drex snapped, obviously annoyed.

Kal frowned at the bruiser’s bluster but chose not to engage. He sounded more and more like an idiot, but he had a mana seed, and they would need his power if they were to have any chance of escaping this world.

“Smells like demons,” the white-haired Lycan observed, sniffing the air delicately.

“Fuck,” Tess exclaimed. “We have the devil’s luck.”

“Quiet down,” Brannic ordered as murmurs and mutters swept the harvesters. “Let’s not attract unwanted attention. We’ll find out soon enough. Wait here for the scout.”

Kal took a seat on his cart to wait, watching how the gleaming violet eyes of their resident Lycan fixed on Brannic with an uncanny focus every time he spoke. Yes, he’s the strongest, Kal thought, guessing what the Lycan was thinking. Brannic certainly cut an imposing figure in his dark armor. The Arkanis logos glowed brightly in the shadowy darkness, inadvertently confusing the eyes and making him hard to really focus on.

Warrior Seed, definitely. I wonder if he’s any good with that? Kal’s eyes slid again to the intriguing, curved sword that hung at his waist. It was sheathed in a red lacquered sheath, a couple of inches longer than standard, and the grip was wrapped in interlocking diagonal bands of black leather, leaving a line of red diamond patterns exposed, but it was the guard that drew Kal’s eye. It appeared to be red hot and about to burst into flames. Could it be… no. Artifacts are far too rare and valuable. It must be a trick of the light or something.

Unfortunately, neither the sword nor the armor gave him any clues; he would need to observe Brannic in action to guess which skills the D-rank fighter had taken. Swords and spears were by far the most common weapons for warriors due to the predominance of mana seed skills that favored them, and his father had ensured Kal was competent in both weapons well before he reached high school. While many relied on the skills provided by their implanted mana seed to cover for them, Gareth Wolfe had never been one of them. He had always insisted that Kal learn to fight, so that when he earned his seed, the skills it provided would be tools he could already wield, amplifying a solid foundation.

Lucien had, of course, taken that same philosophy to the extreme, insisting Kal learn to fight with every weapon available at the gym, and even learn to hold his own unarmed. Thus, having spent more hours than he could count sparring with both sword masters, he was curious to see what Brannic was made of and what his sword style was like. Something about it told him there was more to his blade than mere steel. Sadly, it would have to wait.

Almost ten minutes passed according to his old-fashioned analog kinetic watch. The screeches and strange, eerie moaning whispers had most of the harvesters and even a few of the Pale Fang hunters on edge, so, when the shadows shimmered and Sarn Drell reappeared, more than one hastily swallowed yelp greeted him.

“He was right,” the scout announced, flicking a calculating glance in Kal’s direction. “Bloodshade demons. I’ve marked the way forward.”

“We should abort,” Tess said. “Demons are too dangerous. Some of them even have regeneration and stealth.”

“I’m inclined to agree,” Nirelle said, thoughtfully tapping her staff on the ground. “A high-ranking team would be a safer pick for this job.”

“I can’t believe you guys. In case you hadn’t noticed, the portal is gone!” Drex exclaimed, his voice rising toward the end as he gestured to the vaguely shimmery patch of empty space behind them.

“I’m aware of that, you ignoramus. I do have eyes, you know?” Nirelle snapped back. “Worldshield doctrine for handling a mirror portal is to wait it out. We sit here and let the portal world break. They’ll already know it’s a mirror portal outside and will have a high-ranking team waiting to clear the monsters when they spill out. All we have to do is survive.”

“For how long?” the newbie who’d destroyed her phone asked.

“Two weeks,” Tess said. “Then we’re home free.”

“Two… weeks?” she gasped, going pale.

Kal swallowed. It was far worse than that. Ordinarily, it took anywhere from two to four weeks for a portal to progress to the portal break stage, depending on rank, size, and several other factors. Two weeks subjected to mana this dense would put most of the harvesting team into a coma. None of them had a mana seed, nor the crucible needed to process mana—the mana sickness would be excruciating.

For him, though… he was already suffering from mana sickness. He faced death or permanent injury if he stayed in here that long, and the mop-up team would claim all the bounty from a portal break. He would be out for the count, with no way to pay the bills at best.

“We don’t have food for two weeks,” Erin said brusquely. “Besides… are you seriously suggesting intentionally letting a portal world break and spawn a demonic realm inclusion?”

“A mirror portal is an extenuating circumstance,” Nirelle said, raising her chin and leveling a challenging glare at Erin. “An extreme one, at that.”

“I guess then you don’t mind taking point when it’s time to explain to General Blackwell why we sat on our asses and let several dozen square miles of Demon Central Park insert itself into the middle of Ravenna Mercado?” Erin demanded. “What do you think will happen to the surrounding neighborhoods from all that spatial warping? The mana surge alone would make half of downtown uninhabitable to anyone without a mana seed. Worldshield would have our heads.”

Nirelle sniffed and glanced away, shifting uncomfortably.

“If we have heads left,” someone muttered.

“I say we kill the bastard demons,” Drex said, bashing his gauntlets together.

Kal glanced uncomfortably at Drex, not relishing being in the position of agreeing with the rude monk, but he needed to step in before Nirelle rallied. Before the others could argue Drex down, he said, “Waiting out a mirror portal is Worldshield’s plan of last resort. Standard policy is to clear the portal world, if possible, and we have a D-rank with us, thank heavens.” Kal nodded toward Brannic. “Besides, Xenova pays premium prices for intact Bloodshade demon corpses. We might still be able to turn a profit on this job.”

Every one of the hunters turned to look at him, and Drex spat on the ground.

“Right,” Brannic said. “We will continue the delve with this team, unless we run into something we definitely cannot handle.”

“But…” Tess said.

“In a demon portal, I require immediate obedience. Anything less will get everyone killed,” Brannic snapped, glaring at her for a moment until she gulped and nodded. Taking in his team’s sullen looks, he added, “Demon portals are dangerous, but I outrank this one, and I’m more than adequately equipped.” He banged the heavy chest plate with his fist. “Follow my orders, and you will be safe. Unfortunately, all the mana seeds in here will be red; however, there is typically a wealth of uncommon or rare resources and monsters to harvest in worlds like this one, and I expect to make a nice, tidy profit from this run.” He paused and fixed Erin with a level gaze. “And we avoid intentionally creating Demon Central Park.”

“Thank you, sir,” Erin said, nodding.

“Heck yes!” Drex said, pumping a gauntleted fist. “Music to my ears.”

“Oh, God save us from the murderhobo,” Kal muttered, keeping his voice low enough so that only Erin heard.

Brannic paused for a few seconds and then straightened up. “Alright. Let’s move out. I’ll take point.” He spun on his heel and strode off into the dark tunnel without hesitation. “Follow me.”

Alright. Kal grabbed the handle of his cart and followed along with the group as they entered the tunnel. Glad he’s flush with confidence—just hope he’s got the skills to back it up.

“That was ballsy,” Erin whispered from beside him.

“You, too,” he said.

“Why’d you kick it off?” Erin asked.

“I need this job,” Kal muttered. He left out the fact that they had likely averted two weeks of misery for all the non-seeds and staved off a run at death for himself. Erin was sharp; there was no way she would have missed that, nor the fact that it had been her who had begged him to come despite his condition. She couldn’t have known, and I chose it knowing the risk. He offered her a grim smile; he was not about to hold it against her. Besides, the fact that she had immediately jumped in to back him up with a stronger argument was clear evidence that she understood implicitly.

He didn’t know Erin’s full story, but she and her family had fled Northreach during the horrors of the Hollow Purge. Whether it was from that experience, or just came built into her nature, Erin was a survivor. She could hustle better than anyone he’d ever met, and she always found a way to get the job done.

Kal shifted his gear, hoisting his pack higher on his shoulder, and headed down the tunnel, following along at a healthy distance behind the hunters breaking the trail. There were ripples in the basalt floor of the tunnel, and a vein of what seemed to be wickedly sharp obsidian jutted out from the left-hand side of the wall.

As they plunged into the dark shadows, Nirelle raised her staff, and the tip began to shine with a bright, emerald-green radiance. Most likely, she’d had an Aether crystal embedded in there for it to react to her mana like that. Closer to the front, Tess fed a little more mana into the flames dancing along her fingers, and the bright orange glow helped drive back the shadows.

In the strangely dancing light produced by the implanted mana seeds of the two powerful magic users and Erin’s bobbing helmet-mounted spotlight, the group wound its way through the dark tunnel to the sound of thumping footsteps, clanking tools, and creaking carts. The craggy passage slowly curved to the left, a fact that Kal immediately noticed had them skirting the perimeter of the giant cavern near the now-missing portal where they’re entered, instead of leading toward where he imagined the final chamber might be. As the minutes marched onward, his headache began to grow. Mana sickness had never been fun, but he had put himself through worse before. He gritted his teeth and hauled steadily on his cart. Have to pace myself. I’ve got this.

Sometime later, a shift in the light drew Kal’s gaze, and he glanced upward. A soft, violet glow seemed to be emanating from around an upcoming bend, and he sensed an unusual energy in the air—a tang that reminded him of the uncanny darkness when the power went out in the middle of the night, and all the electronics and softly humming fans went out at the same time.

“Crystals!” someone murmured from up ahead. “Shadestone crystals!”

“Payday!” Drex gloated, rubbing his hands together with a metallic rasp of heavy, armored gloves. “I knew it!”

Kal stepped past the threshold and entered a surreal, hauntingly beautiful cavern. The walls were heavily encrusted with crystalline growths that radiated captured light in ghostly crimson and soft violet hues. A quiet hush filled the cavern, and even the dripping of water into the metallic-smelling crimson pools that dotted the ground sounded muted and quiet.

“Wow,” Erin whispered.

“This is perfect,” Kal said. And it was. Even judging by his inexpert eye, he could hazard a few guesses at the impressive wealth this cave alone held. The size and purity of the colors would be enough of a haul to set him up for several months, even with a harvester’s share, and would go a long way to offsetting the loss of all those goblin mana seeds they’d been hoping for—hopes that had been dashed by the Mirror Portal and the demonic world. It would take more work on the part of the harvest crew, much more, but he could already see the pile of pink bills and final demands for payment evaporating in his mind.

“We’ll pause here,” Brannic said. “Miners, get to work. Pale Fang, let’s set up a perimeter. I want two sets of eyes on both exits. Stay sharp.”

A sudden frenzy of activity rippled through the group as everyone got to work.

“Come,” Erin said, producing a pickaxe and rushing for the crystal-studded walls with an excited grin on her face. “Follow me.”

Kal hauled his cart, relying on the independently sprung bubble wheels to handle the uneven terrain, and followed Erin as she sprinted across the damp rock. As soon as she reached the cavern wall, she swung her pick with expert precision and a surprising level of gentleness until she dislodged the first crystal without shattering it. As soon as it dropped, Kal caught it. It was exquisite, almost as large as his fist, and it radiated a soft, violet aura that sent goosebumps skittering down his arm.

“This color will fetch a good price,” Kal said and quickly stowed it in the cart, packing some storage paper around it before reaching for the next one. Even if the only people who want it are the jewelers.

“Yes,” Erin said, beaming in the ethereal light.

To Kal’s surprise, the Lycan girl popped up beside him and peered at a crystal jutting out from the rock. “This is good?” she asked, bending down to sniff it. Then she licked it and immediately curled her lips back and growled. “Doesn’t taste good.”

“No, hon. It’s not food,” Erin said. “But we can sell it for money and buy a lot of food. Put it in the cart.”

“Ok.” The Lycan reached out and pulled the crystal out of the rock with her bare hand and gently placed it in the cart beside Kal’s one. “I’m hungry,” she said, gazing longingly at Kal, as if he were about to magically conjure up something delicious.

Wouldn’t that be a useful skill? “We usually stop for lunch after a few hours,” Kal offered, a little puzzled by the Lycan’s antics.

A flicker in the corner of his eye caught his attention, and a subtle shifting in the shadows above sent Kal diving for cover, his father’s Chekhov springing to his hand in an instant. Something moved in the shadows between the strangely burnished, metallic stalactites hanging down from the roof. The Lycan growled low in her throat, somehow already down on her haunches beside him, sniffing at the air, but as Kal scoured the shadows for any signs of monsters, an incandescent flare of light bloomed like a red-orange flower from among the hunter team, and a blazing bolt of fire shot upward.

Kal’s eyes widened, and he shouted, “Get down!” He grabbed Erin by the back of her utility belt and dove behind the cover of the bulky steel cart.

A sudden detonation rocked the cave and left his ears ringing as the fireball struck the roof. Stalactites snapped like twigs and came crashing down among the harvesters and the miners like blazing meteoric spears, sending the slow and unlucky diving and screaming. Kal winced as one crashed into the ground nearby, unleashing sharp, high-speed splinters flying in all directions.

“Aah!” he gasped, grabbing his calf muscle and feeling warm blood spilling through the tough gray fabric of his overalls. Wincing, he dug a splinter the size of his finger out of his leg and clamped down on it to staunch the bleeding.

A small black… creature… fell from the roof, hitting the Lycan squarely on the top of her head with a distinct splat. She instantly leapt back, growling, and, in a feat of inhuman dexterity, snatched it out of the air with her teeth before it could hit the ground. She bit down fiercely, shaking her catch back and forth as it twitched, spilling unnaturally tangible shadows down her front like a toddler with soup.

As relative silence returned to the cave, Kal poked his head up and kicked another twitching creature with his foot. Shadows leaked from it like treacle, but from the sense he got, it was a furry creature, maybe about the size of a house cat. Mira would have, no doubt, adored the ugly things and wanted one for a pet regardless of the stringent ecological restrictions on bringing live portal world wildlife back to Valmyra. Still, there’d been enough failed portals leading to inclusions that there were now stable colonies of Veilbats and similar creatures in a few of the darker forests up north.

Kal grimaced, feeling blood pooling into his sock. He had half a vial of Chrysalin wound repair gel left, but that was for emergencies. Using it would no doubt trigger so much additional mana exposure that he would need to be carried the rest of the way.

The Lycan spat out her improvised meal with an angry snarl. “Yucky!” She kicked it, revealing the leathery wings as the shadows faded.

“Bah, Veilbats,” Brannic spat. “You blew up the cave for Veilbats?”

“They’re bloody F-ranks. They don’t even have mana seeds,” Sarn muttered.

“I… I didn’t know,” Tess stuttered.

An inexperienced hunter? New E-rank? Either way, it seemed Tess hadn’t known that the Veilbat was merely a mana-adapted, but otherwise normal creature, no particular threat. The spell she had thrown was most likely Fire Blast—a versatile fire magic skill which formed the backbone of almost every Fire Mage’s build. If Kal had to say, it was probably her only skill. It seemed unlikely that she had filled her second E-rank Aether Capacity with anything yet.

“Next time, hold your fire till you do,” Brannic said, dismissing the destructive Fire Mage with a wave. “Nirelle, see to the injured.”

“Already on it, sir,” she said, strolling among the recovering people and anointing the few who’d been unlucky enough to be hit by falling debris or flames. Kal didn’t miss that she’d left a hint of a pause before the ‘sir’—just enough to be noticeable. Brannic didn’t miss it either, judging by the fresh scowl that followed the healer on her rounds.

Kal rose cautiously to his feet, glancing around. The sight that greeted his eyes was far worse than he’d feared. There were not too many injured, and Nirelle was already making her rounds with her green-glowing staff, but the cavern had been wrecked. Shadestone crystals were fragile, and the sudden shockwave of the fireball had shattered most of the beautiful crystals, leaving sparkling dust sprinkled across the ground and leaking a slowly fading violet glow. He stepped toward the wall to see what might be salvaged, but the instant he placed weight on his foot, his ankle buckled from the pain in his ripped calf. He sucked in a breath.

“Here, let me see that,” Nirelle said, appearing right beside him.

If he hadn’t already held his breath, he might have given himself away with a gasp. Lucien would have handed him a week of extra drills for a lapse of awareness that egregious, no matter how much pain he was in.

But the druidic healer barely glanced at his face, instead kneeling beside him to peel up the fabric around his ankle and inspect the wound. If it hadn’t been so dark, he would have missed the faint gleam of light that briefly shone from her eyes. She laid her fingertips around his wound and summoned a soft green glow. Immediately, his skin and muscles eased under a soothing warmth, and the deep cut began to stitch itself closed, until all that remained was the blood on his clothes.

He sucked in his breath, but it had nothing to do with pain. While there was a world of difference between an S-rank and any E-rank, her spell was so similar to his mother’s magic that his heart clenched painfully in his chest. Nature-affinity healing. Most likely Mend. And a perception skill of some sort. Exactly the kind of seed Mira dreamed of getting one day. She was also steadier than Tessin, which likely meant she was a veteran who’d earned her skills the hard way, rather than a sponsored bought-build from a guild with deep pockets.

Unfortunately, such seeds were so rare that there wasn’t even a reliable market price. They were the kinds of seeds that required bodyguards or strong hunter allies to protect, as the people who had them attracted the worst denizens from the criminal underworld, Seed Hunters and assassins, people who had no compunction about killing other people to take their seeds.

Nirelle stood up, leveling a glare at him. “You have mana sickness.” It was not a question, but it was low-voiced enough that none of the others would overhear.

“A little,” he admitted—it would be impossible to hide the fact from a healer. Especially if his guess at her second skill was accurate.

“Tsk. That’s more than a little,” she said, turning away and then brusquely added, “You’d better not hold us back.”

Kal let out his breath in a sigh of relief as the healer wandered off to tend the rest of the injured, instead of immediately reporting him to Brannic Vohl and having him kicked out. Well, it wasn’t like there was anywhere he could be kicked out to, right now.

At that moment, he sensed someone coming up behind him and whirled around.

“Ooh, scary. What’re you going to do with that little toy, boy?” Drex said, flicking the barrel of the pistol he still held and chuckling at him, but the powerful monk just walked on by without waiting for his answer. “This is a portal world. Guns are obsolete, or hadn’t you heard?”

Kal twisted his mouth into a grimace aimed at the man’s broad back. He knew better. Everyone knew better. Perhaps in the goblin world they were supposed to have found, he might have killed an E-rank monster with a pistol, but here? Against a Bloodshade demon? From everything he’d read, putting a fifty-caliber round into one of the denizens lurking down here would only serve to earn him an angrier demon and a swifter trip to whatever hell they called home.

He just didn’t have anything else. Not like some people with their potent mana seed, granting them mana and the skills to power those steel gauntlets. He didn’t have an international organization backing him, spending millions of credits to buy him artifacts like an Obsidian Aegis powered armor prototype and an enchanted sword, or a wealthy family to buy him a Fire Mage seed on the open market. His only chance had been the peerless reputation and S-ranked power of his parents, and their goal of hunting seeds for him and Mira—and that dream had died with them somewhere deep in the heart of the fungal hellscape the Mycelith had inflicted on San Alvero. Losing half their S-ranks to the Third Raid five years ago had dealt Veritania a blow from which it would likely never recover, but he really cared less about that. It had left him and Mira without their parents and nearly destitute. They had come so close to losing everything, and even now, they were struggling to pay for university tuition and rent. Saving up for a warrior seed was a pipe dream, let alone the insane cost of securing a seed like Nirelle’s for Mira—that was an impossibility. He may as well try to catch a star with his bare hands.

No, if he had a mana seed—any seed—he would not be scraping his way through this portal world with a worthless pistol and a cart, nursing a mana-induced headache and stumbling on trembling legs. He turned back to his cart and the wall, stowing his dad’s Chekhov Custom .50 in the holster at his hip.

If only some twitchy fool hadn’t blown up the crystals…

The annoying thing was that the rude monk was right—against any threats in these Bloodshade caverns more dangerous than Veilbats, his weapon and skills would likely be as good at fending off an assailant as a spaghetti noodle. Not even one cooked al dente; an overcooked, soggy noodle slowly disintegrating in a puddle of warm water. That was, after all, the point of his dad’s silly joke. The gun should sit on the mantle and never be fired. It was a blunt and biting reminder that all of Valmyra’s advanced technology amounted to nothing in the face of the overwhelming magical power that spilled from the portals. If not even the nuke Veritania had dropped on San Alvero thirty years ago had so much as slowed the higher-ranked Mycelith invaders, what could he ever hope to achieve with a mere pistol or a practice sword?

At least the bats are worth almost twenty credits each to Xenova Labs. Maybe I can afford my lunch, after all. He bent over and picked up one of the least burnt small leathery bodies, stashing it on the cart on his way to scouring the walls for any intact Shadestone Crystals that had escaped being shattered by the blast.
