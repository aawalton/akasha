Despite Ta’Stralanov’r’s rather dismissive air to the attack on her and the cathedral, the rest of the Technocult left no doubt that the strike had been damaging. Men and women of metal stood in uncertain silence. Their flickering eyes announced their agitation.

This was a cult that prided itself on discipline and holding themselves to the principles in which they followed. So the unnaturally discordant atmosphere was tangible. Nyxalrai could feel the Technocultists’ desire to rush out and crush the Machine God Worshippers being strangled by the need to stay. This had been the closest anyone came to reaching their leader in well over a century.

They had sacrificed a lot of ground to assure their head wasn’t removed.

Nyxalrai passed through the army, eyes following her every step. Her concealer may be back in place, but too many had seen what she was; they wouldn’t be fooled by her sudden human face. They knew she was more than she appeared.

They knew she didn’t fit into their mould. She was an exception.

Her new concealer had been expanded on from the last iteration. Ta’Stralanov’r claimed it was necessary to last any longer than the week of the last, but the way it bolted into her spine and neck was already unsettling her ability to deal with the intrusion.

The expanded pauldron was too large to keep under her robe and cloak anymore. It should have offered her some reprieve in the eyes of the Technocultists, that she was one of them, but Nyxalrai couldn’t help but feel it did nothing but point out their inescapable differences. Her body wouldn’t accept mechanical intrusion. The Technocult was strict in their way of things, accepting fellows only by a stringent, unchanging rubric.

Nyxalrai did not feel like a Technocultist.

As she felt the gazes of hundreds of warriors fall on her — hybrids of human and machine scrutinising someone that was neither — she imagined a fracture. There wasn’t one, of course. Commitment to superiors’ orders was ingrained deep into Technocultists. More-so than any other cult. Even the eyes she saw held only appreciation for winning them the Trial by most, and true respect by the few that knew what she’d done to Solan and what she continued to do to the cults.

But… there was a flicker of confusion there too. Of worry or concern. Nyxalrai wasn’t taking the Technocult path — she couldn’t — and that instilled legitimate apprehensions in those beholden to convention. Was Nyxalrai unwilling to do what was needed to become one of them? Was special consideration being given to her?

As appreciative as she was not to see greed from the sole cult she trusted, these eyes, concerned about the very foundations of their own cult being warped, were not ones she was glad to see. In a way, it was worse.

At least the greedy eyes could be stabbed.

The instant the last eye slipped off her form, Nyxalrai ripped open a tear in reality. She stepped past sharp-pointed teeth, and bit.

Pink engulfed her, and not long after, a mountain of chains attempted to do the same. It was a weak effort. In the two months since Nyxalrai had swallowed the remnants of Solan’s soul, they had grown frail. Each fleshforged chain link was cracked and brittle through the metal, and the fleshy parts had become little more than a foul sludge barely holding the whole thing together.

The attacks were so weak, Nyxalrai simply sat and took it as Lý̥o̻t́e̞pͦ kept her whole.

Curiously, there was less chain attacking her than usual. She noticed a sizeable chunk off in the far distance of her stomach, cowering and desperately trying not to be noticed. Apparently, even Solan’s soul could be broken.

Nyxalrai settled in. Immediately, her soul bloomed to the forefront of her mind and manifested in the Pink around her — not that there was much difference. So much power sculpting had led her to this moment. As the thousand-odd possible evolution options came into being in the form of ghostly spirals superimposed upon each other, Nyxalrai resolved herself to take the risk she knew she needed to take.

As the soil crumbles and sky tumbles, the growing pillar shatters both. Soul and body are united. To strike with one is to risk the other. A whole, dedicated to the downfall of immortals.

But before she initiated the evolution, there was another she needed to consider.

Curse — A seedling grows within the rotting soil of fate. Sprouts cut through the environment of rot and parasitic weeds, growing in spite of all that smothers. A single bud parts the surface, touched by ravenous air.

Just before she allowed her orbiting spiral of names to evolve, she just had to bring up the complete combination. Nice to see she was apparently breaching the surface now… or maybe it was ominous. Nyxalrai remained completely in the dark on whether this Seed was a good thing for her.

The chains flinched back as her evolution began. With thousands of components spiralling around her in a violent clash of orbits, Nyxalrai found her attention falling on the remnant of Solan. It was clearly reacting to her evolution. The entire concept of someone else being able to look in on such an intimate moment — even if there wasn’t exactly a consciousness left of Solan — was disconcerting.

At least, it should be.

Nyxalrai found herself entirely unbothered by the thought of people witnessing her soul’s orbital restructuring. The little pieces that made each name, that held every secret, it was all on display, peeled open and bare. But she felt no shame. The insular need of humans was absent. Like with her mutations, what did it matter to Nyxalrai what people thought of her.

If they thought they could decipher the curse of her core name — which still eluded her — then they could try.

Her logical mind defied that sentiment. Nyxalrai wasn’t about to let some onomastician dig freely through her name before she was ready to face the cults. She knew that wouldn’t end well. It would be hard to hide her evolution if they saw her out in the open, but the in-depth analysis could not be completed quickly. Not even by high creed cultists.

Her galaxy eyes could dismantle the very components of a name and pick them apart individually, so if there were any onomasticians capable of similar, they would be able to solve each name as if it were a puzzle. Much like she was learning to do herself.
