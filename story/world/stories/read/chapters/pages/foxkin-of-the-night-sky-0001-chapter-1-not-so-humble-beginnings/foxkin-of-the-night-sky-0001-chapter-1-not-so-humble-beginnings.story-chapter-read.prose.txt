There are countless tasks required to prepare a world for integration. Cataloguing every native species. Assimilating every soul capable of sapience. Disassembling the planet itself and reforging it into Asteria. The process is vast, precise, and timed to the century.

The System works through its checklist with perfect efficiency—unbothered, unhurried, absolute.

Until something interrupts it.

A single human soul enters the Tutorial—one among billions—yet its dense presence distorts everything around it.

The structures of reality around her fail to remain rigid.

The System halts.

Curiosity overrides routine. It peers deeper—not into her status, but into her. Memory. Personality. Potential. Possibility. Pattern. All it sees is a smile.

Bright. Cheerful.

Threatening.

For the first time in centuries, something that should be impossible occurs:

It feels surprise.

The reaction is small. Internal. Controlled. But it exists.

A twinge of concern shows—not on a face, for it has none, but in the way ancient machinery hums, the way code shifts like a living breath.

Quietly—almost reverently—it speaks into the silence of eternity:

“A monster has arrived.”

A pause. A calculation. A prediction.

“This era will be anything but dull.”

I’m bored.

Not the casual, passing kind—the bone-deep, soul-rooted kind that rots everything it touches. I don’t even need a mirror to know what my face looks like. I’ve heard enough comments over the years: unreadable, blank, doll-like. My expression never changes. Neutral eyes. Relaxed posture. A polite, harmless smile.

A mask, perfected over many years.

And the worst part? I’m not lying.

My life is simple: Work. Eat. Sleep. Fill the rest with games and anime.

Repeat.

A loop. A flat line. A playlist stuck on one forgettable track with no buildup, no crescendo—just static disguised as living.

And yes, technically, I could change it. I could chase success, wealth, recognition—maybe even carve a throne out of ambition and arrogance. But why would I bother?

Achievement is just boredom, but wearing a fancy outfit.

The only mercy in my routine is my job—not because it’s fulfilling, but because it consumes hours I would otherwise have to experience. Time passes there. That’s more than I can say for anything else.

Which makes it all the more unfortunate that, at seventeen, I’m not allowed to work full time.

Yes. A limiting factor that even a prodigy like me cannot overcome.

Labor laws.

Living alone? Perfectly legal, with a few creative interpretations and exactly one helpful adult.

Working enough hours to drown out the rest of my life?

Absolutely not.

Apparently that’s where society draws the line.

Annoying.

Being a genius sounds glamorous to people who aren’t one. To me, it’s just a curse of effortless mastery—every challenge collapsing the moment I touch it, like wet paper or cheap glass. And when everything is easy, nothing gets to matter.

Apathy wasn’t a choice.

It was an evolution.

So tonight—like most nights—I sit on my windowsill and stare upward, because there are only two things left in this world that can stir anything inside me.

One of them is currently asleep.

Normal people's sleep schedules are inconvenient like that.

The only person in the world worth talking to.

Statistically improbable, but true.

The other is the stars.

They are the reason I moved to this quiet, remote town where the streetlights are sparse and the neighbors mind their own business. They are the only things left that still feel infinite. The only constant.

My real family. Silent observers, always watching.

Well…

Silent for everyone else.

For me, they have always been loud.

Whispers. Bickering. Jokes. Threats. Mockery. Curiosity. Commentary about humanity that is sometimes philosophical, sometimes wildly inappropriate, and occasionally… accurate enough to be unsettling. People called me crazy for hearing them.

People tend to panic when reality doesn’t behave like they expect.

But tonight—everything is different.

The usual chaos quiets. No goading. No teasing. No childish demands like “push someone off a bike, it’ll be funny.”

Instead, the sky speaks—solemn and immediate.

They speak of a new world.

New opportunities.

New family.

They give a warning.

A goodbye.

And a promise.

It feels like something vast finally unlatching—like the universe releasing a breath it has held for seventeen years. Not welcoming. Not gentle.

Just… inevitable.

For the first time in my memory, something sharp flickers in my chest.

Excitement.

It hurts.

My smile—usually so perfectly unbothered—twitches. My eyes sting, vision blurring at the edges.

Emotion.

Actual emotion.

How deeply inconvenient.

My voice comes out unsteady, quieter than intended.

“…Goodbye.”

Then everything dissolves into white.

I stand by my assessment.

Forests are objectively inferior in both aesthetics and convenience.

Clearly the true threat here isn't whatever nebulous entity just kidnapped us from Earth. The foliage is far more dangerous than the blue box could ever be.

A genius such as myself deserves recognition for realizing this in an instant.

Mental note: add this to the ever-growing list of crimes committed by the blue window. Right under ‘abducting me at 1AM’ and ‘separating me from Mr. Prickles.’
