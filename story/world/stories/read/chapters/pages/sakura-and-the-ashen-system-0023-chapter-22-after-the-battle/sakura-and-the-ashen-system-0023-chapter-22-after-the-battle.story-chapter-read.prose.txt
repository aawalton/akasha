Sakura was still staring at the place above the river where the mist figure had been hanging just moments ago.

She was breathing fast and shallow, as if after a long run, though her legs could barely hold her up. Her fingers still did not fully obey her. There was no relief inside her, no joy that they had survived.

Pampli and Gilbert stood just as tense beside her. Pampli was growling low in warning, never taking his eyes off the empty space above the river. His body, enlarged after the fight, still looked alien and unsettling. Gilbert still had not lowered his sword, as if he did not believe it was really over.

“What is it?” Sakura repeated, quieter this time.

Gilbert frowned harder.

“No idea,” he replied after a short pause. “But there was enough power in it.”

At last he lowered the blade, but he did not relax. If anything, he looked even more focused now.

“And that doesn’t promise anything good,” he added.

Those words hit Sakura unexpectedly hard. Something inside her tightened painfully. Everything that had happened in the last few minutes had already felt like a nightmare, but now that nightmare had been confirmed by someone else.

Sakura sucked in a sharp breath.

“This is all because of me, isn’t it?” she blurted out quickly. “The creatures came for me. Not for you and not for Pampli. For me. And that thing too... it came for me. I saw it. They all came for me. Somehow I’m drawing them in. Or they feel something in me. Or this magic. Or the mark. Or...”

She broke off for only a second, then immediately kept going, faster now, slipping into panicked, rushed words.

“And if this keeps happening, then I’m just going to drag death after me, that’s all. Wherever we go. Whoever ends up near me. I don’t even understand what’s happening to me. I don’t understand why it said it had been waiting for me. I don’t understand any of this.”

Her voice was shaking. Sakura hated sounding like this, completely lost, like a little girl.

Gilbert stepped closer and put a hand on her shoulder. The gesture was not very confident. Not the kind people make when they actually know how to comfort someone. It was more like an attempt to ground her, to keep her from falling all the way into panic.

“I suspected it,” he said.

Sakura jerked her gaze up to him.

“What?”

For one sharp second, Sakura almost asked how he could have known. Then she understood. He had seen Pampli after the arena. He had seen the mark on her wrist and heard enough about her class to guess that obsidian magic did not simply sit inside her quietly. It answered her. Maybe other things answered too.

“I suspected the forest might react to you. That the creatures might too. That’s why I took you outside.”

For a second, she even forgot the fear. All that remained was shock.

“You... what?”

She took half a step back, staring at him in near outrage.

“You took me out of the cave on purpose? After everything you already saw? After the arena? You knew this could happen, and you still dragged us out there?”

Gilbert met her stare calmly.

“Yes.”

“Gilbert, we almost got killed!”

“But we didn’t.”

Sakura let out a disbelieving breath. From exhaustion, fear, and anger, her thoughts became deafening for a second.

“But we could have, damn it! They could have done it!”

“I never said it was safe,” he answered dryly. “I wanted to see it and make sure it would happen exactly like that. So I would know what to expect later, when you and I leave Saorex’s caves.”

“And how was it?! Did you like it?!”

“You handled it.”

He said it without condescension and without trying to soften the words.

Sakura fell silent.

“If you want to be angry, be angry later,” Gilbert told her. “Right now, it’s better to get back to the cave.”

That was hard to argue with. Sakura only nodded. Pampli growled more quietly, though he was still on edge, and moved back toward the forest first, as if he had decided to cover their retreat. Gilbert walked beside Sakura, silent and tense. Sakura stayed silent too.

When they made it back to Saorex’s cave and found themselves in the familiar chamber again, Sakura felt herself loosen up from the simple fact that there were walls around her. It did not make things better or safer, but at least she could breathe.

Gilbert sat down directly on a stone by the wall, pulled a vial from his Inventory, and drank it in several swallows without ceremony. It was obvious from his face that the fight had cost him more than he wanted to show.

Sakura lowered herself across from him, stretched out her arm, and stared for a long time at the mark with its ashen petals. There were more of them now. The patterns on her skin were spreading slowly but steadily, growing more intricate. Sakura ran a finger along the edge of one of the petals without touching the skin itself.

“I keep trying to piece all of this into something whole,” she said after a long pause. “But I can’t. At all. Everything just keeps getting worse and stranger.”

Gilbert, already looking a little better after the potion, lifted his gaze to her.

“That’s normal.”

Sakura gave a short, humorless laugh.

“No, actually, none of this is normal.”

She looked at the mark again.

“It’s dangerous for you to keep going with me.”

Gilbert visibly frowned, as if that was not the conclusion he had wanted to hear.

“I need to.”

Sakura blinked in surprise.

“What?”

“To keep going with you. To figure this out.” He shifted one shoulder a little, as if he did not particularly enjoy explaining himself too directly, but he still continued. “If Crowlis became a key to chaos, then maybe you can become a key to something else. Maybe to fixing what he did. Maybe to changing the way this world itself works.”

Sakura just stared at him for several seconds.

“You say that like I’m not falling apart after every second attempt to use my magic.”

“One doesn’t cancel out the other.”

Sakura would have snorted if she had not been so tired.

Gilbert leaned forward slightly.

“We’ll learn more about it. About your class. About why the creatures react to you and what that shadow at the river was. I’ll help you.”

The words were simple, but they still made something inside her calmer. Sakura looked away and said quietly,

“Thank you.”

“Don’t,” he answered at once. “We have common interests.”

This time she gave a real, if faint, smile.

“That sounds very romantic.”

Gilbert did not give the joke a full response, but the corner of his mouth still twitched.

Sakura looked at her hand again.

“My skills are really limited,” she said. “They keep throwing restrictions at me. Every time I start to understand something, the system immediately says no, too early, limit reached, restricted, or some other kind of bullshit. Is that normal?”

“Yes,” Gilbert answered calmly. “Quite. The lower the caster’s level, the more restrictions there are on using skills. Especially if the class is heavy or poorly compatible with the bearer’s body. You need to level up.”

“By using magic, right?”

“By interacting with it often,” he corrected. “Using it. Learning to hold it. Understanding how it responds. The more practice you have, the more stable the connection between you and your skills becomes.”

Sakura nodded slowly. It sounded like a hassle, but it made sense.

“And do you know very little about your skills too?” she asked a second later.

Gilbert narrowed his eyes slightly.

“What do you mean?”

“The system didn’t explain one of my skills at all.” Sakura frowned, trying to remember. “A blank window came up when I tried to get more information. There was nothing there. That seemed strange to me.”

This time Gilbert did not rush to answer. He was clearly thinking.

“No,” he said at last. “That shouldn’t happen. Every skill I have comes with a clear description. Purpose, restrictions, sometimes even recommendations for use. There shouldn’t be blank windows.”

Sakura felt another stab of anxiety.

“So that’s bad?”

“It’s... abnormal,” he admitted. “Maybe the Obsidian Hierophant class itself has a flaw. Or maybe the system doesn’t know how to process something that rare correctly. As far as I understand it, aside from Crowlis, there were no other bearers of this class. And now there’s you.”

“Wonderful,” Sakura muttered. “So I’m not just the owner of horrifying magic. I’m also a walking system malfunction.”

“For now, that’s only a guess, so calm down. There’s no point panicking.”

“If this kind of hell isn’t the moment to panic, then when is?”

Gilbert laughed at the question, but offered no answer. After that, the conversation fell quiet again for a while.

The silence was broken by a familiar dry clicking.

Saorex entered the chamber, followed by several spiderlings. They were not moving the way they usually did, without their busy little scurrying or their curious twitching. There was nervousness in their movements.

Saorex stopped, looking first at Sakura, then at Gilbert.

“The fores-st has-s grown very unrestful,” he said in his even, rustling voice. “The s-spiders are anxious-s. They s-saw the shadow that has awakened...”

Sakura felt a chill run down her back again.

“I didn’t...” she said quietly, but she was not allowed to finish.

“Shadows-s that s-sleep for a long time rarely awaken without reas-son. And when they do awaken, they bring nothing but ill omens-s and new mis-sfortune. You need to leave. As-s soon as-s possible.”

One of the spiderlings climbed up one of his side legs and then crawled back down again, as if it could not settle.

Gilbert let out a heavy breath and nodded.

“We’ll go. We’ll rest for a few hours, sleep, and then leave.”

Saorex looked at him carefully, weighing the answer.

“That is-s reas-sonable. Tired fugitives rarely run for long.”

“True, Saorex. Thank you for the wisdom,” Gilbert said dryly.

“I do not s-say it for wisdom,” Saorex answered calmly. “I s-say it becaus-se I want you to s-survive. There is no time to delay.”

Sakura looked at Saorex and felt grateful to him. For everything. Even if he was hurrying them now, that did not undo the creature’s kindness. His strange, warped kindness.

Saorex shifted his gaze to her once more.

“And do not think that s-shadows come only for the weak,” he said unexpectedly softly. “S-sometimes they come for thos-se in whom power s-sleeps.”

With those words, he turned and went back into the depths of the cave as soundlessly as before. The spiderlings hurried after him.

Sakura sat there staring into the empty space ahead of her and felt the exhaustion finally begin to take over.

Gilbert rose first.

“Sleep,” he said. “Tomorrow, or more likely already today, we’ll have to move again.”

Sakura nodded in silence.
