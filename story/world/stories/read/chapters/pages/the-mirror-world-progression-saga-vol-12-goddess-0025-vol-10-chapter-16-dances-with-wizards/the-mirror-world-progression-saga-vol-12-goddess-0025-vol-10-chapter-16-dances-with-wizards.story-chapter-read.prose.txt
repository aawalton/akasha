Here we go…

A sense of dread blanketed the room, thickening the air. Before Samantha’s eyes, a lich materialized next to the sarcophagus. He was draped in heavy, strangely colorful robes of yellow and blue, with intricate designs in different layers, though it was spoiled by half of the material being stained and soiled. He wore a tall, bejeweled hat and was otherwise absolutely stacked in mismatched jewelry. What bits of skin could be seen between his extensive clothing were shriveled and black, and his face was cast in a menacing scowl. His eyes could not really be seen for the intensity of the red glow. In one hand, he held a stylus; in the other, a golden staff that formed into a small book at the top.

Samantha grinned. "Hello there, Consolidator. Shall we dance? Oh-"

Two Contingency spells triggered instantly upon catching sight of her, both of them AoEs centered on her. The lich snarled at her even as they materialized.

A high-level Dispel effect flared over everyone, stripping out some of the protection layers, and then a destructive blast of necrotic magic flared. Samantha wasn’t ambitious that she could counter them, nor was she inclined to sacrifice actions, as opposed to just soaking it. There was no way shit bound in a Contingency like that was anything approaching the best he could do. Such preparations were not typically known for their potency.

To Samantha, this was bait, and she was far too seasoned to bite.

The Dispel seemed to target physical protections, and so it stripped her wound negations and armor reinforcement right off. On the other hand, her own Minor Contingency fired, keyed to losing wound negations, popping her with the classic Mystic Armor and Three Small Fortunes spells, the latter of which included a wound negation.

Unsurprisingly, the AoE that came after was something that ate right through wound negations, but it at least weakened the intensity, and her protections flared with a bright aural interlace to resist the weakened effect. It… wasn’t good enough to wound. That was a really good thing. On the other hand, her armor didn’t do so well, deteriorating extremely, a metal plate on one foot falling off to the floor, and one shoulder guard breaking some strapping to fall and hang loose at her back. Still technically functional, at least.

Samantha also noticed there was some component of the spell that conducted an Unsummon effect, but that was irrelevant. Gabriel might’ve been summoned, but she wasn’t ‘a’ summon, per se. It was more like she’d been teleported, effectively.

Azure only grunted from the scathing blast, her crystalline form more than up to the task of resisting. Gabriel cried on in pain and stumbled, her golden armor turned to blackened shreds, but she was still on her feet.

Meanwhile, the attack did nothing to the undead.

How nice of the lich lord to think of them.

Samantha could see the lich already moving to continue casting, but she was much faster than him. She took a moment for the faintest glance at his protections. Not many, but potent. No wound negations, thankfully — that was too modern for this guy. Instead, he had something even more annoying: an amulet that reduced a total number of additional wounds, basically making him like a damned [Knight].

That amulet was technically not of an insurmountable level if she could interact with it. Naturally, AoE Dispels and such didn’t touch item enchantments. She had a few ways of potentially dealing with it. Some of them might require auto-success, which she didn’t exactly want to burn through willy-nilly. She had one idea that might work without it.

Moreover, he had a damn Reflect Spell effect. Of course! It was kind of annoying… she could just not use spells on him to save her action economy — which was almost certainly very, very important against a lich. She could use her best thing, [Spellfyr: Unleash], on him, but she’d risk incinerating him and his gear. All she needed was his skull. Just aim correctly?

From experience, she knew it was a waste of time trying to utilize [Mirror Maze] on an undead. For that matter, she couldn’t use [Remove], either, because he was already gone-pecan. This was just an animated, driven-insane husk.

She had only a moment to deliberate, even with her superspeedy mental speed. Abundance of caution, precision attacks with goddess lasers, perhaps? Or trust her ability to control Spellfyr and incinerate him from the neck down?

She made her choice.

First of all, she had to lay the groundwork to counter his damned item-effect. She began her spellcasting. [Alter Spell: Prepare] dropped an instant AoE Dispel, much like the lich’s Contingency, as she hoped to get something relevant…

She got absolutely nothing. No protections were stripped. Damn- wait! No, she got something on the sarcophagus! She had zero clue what it was, as she had only just barely noticed succeeding at all. Whatever it was, the spell had been hidden from casual detection.

And that’s why AoE dispels are gold, I guess.

She utilized [Auto-Cantrip] almost as an afterthought, refreshing Three Small Fortunes for the wound negation added back on. The look on the lich’s face showed he understood on some level what she’d done, and was definitely not happy about it.

I’m at least as annoying as you as a spellcaster, buddy!

Samantha utilized [Alter Spell: Double] for her main action focus. Instead of actually casting twice, though, she immediately committed the second spellcasting into countering whatever the hell the lich was doing, via [Deific Counterspell]. After all, she had time to spare, and nothing good for her was coming from a damned lich.

Her actual spell was [Wire Jammer], utilizing [Alter Spell: Amp] for the convenient enhanced physical effects.

Their spells more or less fell simultaneously. As she was dual attempting to identify and counter the lich’s spell, a great mass of metallic wire spawned in a cone of effect that ensnared the space of the lich, imminently intent on ensnaring and attacking him.

In the meantime, however, Samantha was dealing with the lich’s spell. The first faint licks of its magic were as some horrible, reality-altering howls from the netherworld.

How lovely.

The spell was called Dirge of the Pitiless King, Time. Obscenely high level — of course — and evidently designed to cause a nasty combination of Haste on himself, while causing Slow, Terror, and Deterioration on everyone else in a massive radius. They were probably fine against Terror, and she was immune to Slow, but by no means did she want the lich Hastened, nor did she want to find out what Deterioration did to her or her allies.

Against most, this opener would be devastating, setting up an immediate follow-up, perhaps a full-power AoE that could be lethal, and almost certainly would be crippling, putting him firmly in control with his opponents likely stunned or needing to go on the defense.

Not liking her chances against the spell, Samantha went for the ‘go ahead’ effect that was [Alter Fate: Spell Witching], which worked fine as [Deific Counterspell] always had to sacrifice a spellcasting of some sort. It would force failure, and thus counter the spell, but if she succeeded without it, it wouldn’t be expended.

Samantha had one hand gesturing outward, still materializing the metallic wire out from her fingers for her other spell, as she thrust her staff, and a heavenly chime resounded through the chamber, vibrating the floor, the walls, and all within, with a strumming straight from Heaven itself. The chamber glowed golden, and music played from nowhere, unavoidable, consuming all sound, yet strangely calm and gentle. The dirge was drowned out, becoming a mere buzz.

And then Samantha added her voice, singing in multiple layers over layers, words of the arcane tongue, never so beautifully uttered as in that moment, as suddenly touched by the divine.

Against this song, against this witching of reality, the magic of the dirge died completely. The ghouls suddenly stopped throwing themselves on Azure’s protective blades and shirked away, pained and terrified by the glorious sound. The lich howled like a banshee, shaking with rage.

It was clear to Samantha from all this that she’d had to expend [Spell Witching].

Quite worth it!

While this was going on, two additional undead giants entered the room. One was an even bigger lizardman with a mace and shield, but the other was barely taller than the other ghouls, yet much wider, and no lizard at all. It was some species that looked like a cross between a man and a gorilla. But that was barely accurate for the strange shape of its head. It was hard to say exactly what it was, considering it was a ghoul with half its face torn off and showing a blackened skull on the top. Whatever fur it might’ve had was long gone, and all it wore was a ragged chain skirt ready to fall apart at any moment.

Deathsting, having thoroughly wounded the first lizardman giant, quickly moved on to take the apeman, almost instantly scoring a slash across its still-meaty chest with a sharp tail, spewing black gore and causing the charge of the creature to divert off and fizzle into a staggering, shambling motion. Its mouth opened as if to scream, but only a squeak came out, its vocal capacity apparently damaged. It tried a smash with both hands on the wyvern, but Deathsting dodged this rather disdainfully.

Gabriel fought defensively against the new giant, barely engaging with a feint and falling back, to keep an eye on the rest of the battle. She cast some sort of self-buff on herself to prepare for the inevitable duel versus the giant lizardman that she now might not get help with.

Azure, sensing that there were likely more coming, and seeing that the expected Dispel had already propagated, opted to stop that. She cast a Wall of Force over the hallway entrance, blocking further entrants from muddying the waters of the battle any further. Almost instantly, figures could be seen through the semi-transparent wall, banging on it. Probably a good call, and more or less doing as Samantha had asked when it came to her expected focus for the challenge.

Samantha noted it all with a glance as she turned her attention back to the lich, now trying to physically dance away from the metal wires and bat it away with his staff. This last thing was surprisingly effective, as the touch of the ‘book’ at the head — which fluttered its ridiculous gold pages frantically until stopping right before impact — caused an entire section of the flowing wire ‘beast’ to disintegrate into nothing.

But the wires weren’t so easily rebuffed, and they more or less surrounded the undead maestro. The mass surrounded him, and the true strike came from a section of mass on the flank, almost from behind, revealing the other attack as a feint the lich did not read well.

Samantha smiled. The real strike was about to drop. She prepared to take advantage, already in motion…
