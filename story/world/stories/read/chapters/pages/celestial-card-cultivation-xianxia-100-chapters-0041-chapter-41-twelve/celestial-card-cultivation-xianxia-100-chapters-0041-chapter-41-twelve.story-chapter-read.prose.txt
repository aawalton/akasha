He had opened ten out of twelve meridian gates already. According to Elder Sun, this was sufficient to become a powerful cultivator. According to the ‘Heavens’ Steps’ challenge during the test, his natural talent had been eight and increased to nine open gates thanks to the Ancestral Pathway Preservation Pill.

But, as Elder Gao had said, the talent was not a hard barrier, but something that could be overcome with the right will.

Jianyi settled down in his cultivation chamber. He did have the will. Even before he had started to unlock more and more of his soul-empowered will, he would not have hesitated to take the next step.

He was very, very painfully aware of the price of failure, and he was quite sure that there would not be another rare pill in his future should he mess this attempt up… However, he felt he owed it to the Celestial Core Card, to his father, to himself, to open up the path to become as strong as possible, as fast as possible.

That’s why he had prepared diligently. Innumerable test runs as the paths he would have to take with the two slivers of qi were the most difficult yet. The final yellow pills to push out the impurities from his body after he had cleared his gates stood beside him in the small pill bottle. He had disrobed. He was focused.

This was the most dangerous attempt; his heart and stomach meridians needed to be opened. He was well aware that a misstep could mean his death and, given his limited talent, the impurities blocking those gates were thick and required a powerful attack to open. There were no half-measures here.

Jianyi focused inward and separated two sizeable threads of qi from the small mass that had collected in his lower dantian.

He decided not to do another practice run; he had done plenty, and he felt confident.

The first sliver of qi needed to go down all the way to his right foot, while the other needed to flow to his left hand. From there, they needed to move down the opposite paths, meaning right arm and left leg, then both needed to repeat the circuit, and then ascend to his neck meridian before they could start on the final stretch.

The smooth feeling of the qi moving through his pathways was incomparable to what it had been before he had taken the Ancestral Pathway Preservation Pill.

He remained focused and guided the two threads at an ever-increasing pace down from the neck meridian on their separate paths to the stomach and the heart.

He could see the gates and the large, rock-like impurities blocking them.

He was centered in his broadened pathways. The qi was sufficiently large. His speed was as intended.

He held his breath.

The qi connected.

Pain.

But manageable.

He had hit the impurities dead on, with not much bruising on the areas around them. The impurities were shattered, and he quickly reached for the yellow pills.

As he swallowed them and took a couple of weakened steps towards his bathroom, he felt enormous pressure falling off his shoulders that he hadn’t even known had been there. Anxiety that he had controlled with his will but could not completely prevent.

He had done it. Twelve open gates, the maximum possible.

The path towards becoming a powerful cultivator was open, literally.

“Congratulations, my young friend, this is a great achievement!”

“Thank you, Elder. I am glad to be done with this.”

Jianyi had brought the elder his breakfast and used the opportunity to inform him about his breakthrough from the previous night.

“I imagine. However, there is one constant in being a cultivator: it never gets easier.” The elder chuckled quietly. “How are the classes?”

Jianyi thought for a moment whether he should mention Elder Fang’s behavior, but decided against it. For now, he had overcome the challenge; if things were to get worse, he could still mention it, but dealing with these things on his own felt better than asking his teacher to solve them for him. “Very interesting, teacher. I learn a lot; in particular, the classes on natural treasures and beasts of the Empire offer lots of insights. I sometimes wonder how life would be if one could focus on those things exclusively and not have to worry about the Demonic Tide.”

The elder nodded slowly. “Yes, that was a very different time. But even today, many cultivators do that. Not everyone is fighting on the frontlines all the time—and for good reasons, we need the materials, ingredients, and cards that those cultivators provide to our crafters.”

Elder Sun was much more controlled than he had been the last time that Jianyi had seen him, and he decided that it would be best not to pry for now.

“Very well, unfortunately, I don’t have time right now, but once you have fully recovered from the gate opening, come and see me so that we can discuss opening your dantians.”

“Some of you have already opened your dantians; all of you have taken steps towards that target. Once achieved, you are a Qi Gathering cultivator.” Elder Zhang, as always meticulously dressed, paused to look into the audience, conveying the gravity of the achievement.

“There are many responsibilities and opportunities that come along with that first step on the path of a cultivator, and I am sure we will have time to discuss many of them. Today, however, I want to talk about one of the first and most important things to consider if you want to walk down that path further. To be a cultivator, you need to cultivate.” When she paused this time, a slight smile played on her lips, well aware of the triviality she had just uttered.

“To cultivate means to draw qi into your body or spirit to make it available for whatever use you have. Every one of you who has opened even a single gate knows that qi enters you automatically, unbidden. Everyone, whether they are a mortal or a cultivator, benefits. Whether they manage to shake off a disease quicker, grow that tiny bit stronger, or live a few years longer, having qi enter their body shapes and helps them.

“However, the amount of qi you gather naturally is… negligible. You likely have no way to put this in perspective but the simplest of techniques…,” she made a small motion away from her body, four translucent cards appeared before her, and with another motion one of those cards turned into a blade of water that hovered before her, before she dismissed it with a wave of her hand to the excited murmur of the disciples in the room, “… costs more qi than you gather in days.” Another pause, giving her audience the chance to chatter with their neighbors about the casual demonstration they had just witnessed.

“Now,” her glance demanded silence, “if you don’t gather sufficient qi naturally, that means you need to do it intentionally, which brings us to today’s topic: cultivation techniques.”

Jianyi leaned forward; that was a topic that he had not discussed with Elder Sun at all, and now that he was so close to having completed this first step as a cultivator, it seemed very relevant to his near future.

“Cultivation techniques are ways to cycle qi through your body in a way that draws in qi from outside your body through one or more of your gates. The most common way to do so is with the help of breathing techniques, which leverage the lung meridian disproportionately, but more complex techniques are manifold.

“One of the most important tasks ahead of you is to find the right technique for you, as there are hundreds of them out there. Just like with gate openings, there is, however, an easy answer: the Imperial Cycling Art is available for the first three cultivation realms, works for nearly every cultivator, and is a most solid choice.”

At this point, Jianyi felt the elder’s gaze on him, and he remembered her reaction in the tent with the four other elders to him using the Martial Meridian Art and not the Imperial Gate Opening Technique.

“If you do want to select a more individual technique, there are three dimensions that determine which of them fit your personal needs best. First, your Body and Spirit composition. This encompasses the number of meridians you have opened, the quality of your pathways, your breathing pattern compatibility, and your yin-yang body balance.”

Those mostly made sense to him, but he was unsure what his yin-yang body balance was.

“Second, your strength of will, or simply said, your ability to endure pressure and pain.”

He hadn't known that when people talked about the path of a cultivator being one of pain, it was so literal. For him, this was a question of discipline, and that he could do.

“Lastly, the state of your cultivation. Meaning, which realm are you in, which, if any, qi cards you use, do you only cultivate your spirit or also your body, and finally, which techniques have you used before? The last point is important, as many manuals have built-in progression over the different realms, and it is easier to follow that progression than to switch to completely different techniques after every breakthrough to another realm.

“Now, let me explain this in a bit more detail…”

Liu Jun and his cousin Hong walked beside him as they left the lecture hall.

“You are missing out by not living in the dormitories,” Jun said, grinning.

Jianyi raised an eyebrow. “How so?”

“There is a bunch of young mortal servants going around offering... You know.”

He raised another eyebrow, looking at the broad-shouldered boy.

Jun laughed. “Don’t look at me like that. I haven’t engaged in anything, but apparently, they are quite persistent. I heard one girl complaining, though, that they seem to be interested in boys only.”

Jianyi stopped for a moment, shook his head, then continued walking. He was glad to be living with Elder Sun; this sounded like complications that he didn’t need in his life right now.

“How is your spear training coming along?” he asked Hong.

“It is a bit cumbersome that we have to run to the central peak for our training as the disciples responsible for our training live there, but otherwise it is good. It was never a big focus for us, so learning from the ground up does us good.”

“We will challenge you for Lapis soon!” Jun said, laughing.

“Good.” He hesitated for a moment; his conviction that friends were a distraction was unbroken; however, he had not forgotten that running in a group had felt infinitesimally easier. “A couple of fellow disciples wanted to convince me to spar with them. I don’t have time for it, but in case it ever happens, I will let you know if you want to join.”

Jun grinned enthusiastically, and Hong nodded as well.

“Very well, I will let you know.”

As he walked away, he thought about the exchange some more. Strictly speaking, it had been a waste of his time—but it also hadn’t been exactly unpleasant to talk with the cousins, and it hadn’t really cost him anything.

Regarding spear training, he was pretty sure that an hour with Minghao would help him more, but maybe the difference wasn’t enormous given how proficient Shuhua and Miyao were, and if he could help the cousins improve and they were to join Lapis, there might be the opportunity for another conversation like they just had—of course only as long as it wasn’t a waste of time.

He wouldn’t initiate this, but if the women insisted, he would maybe not outright decline.
