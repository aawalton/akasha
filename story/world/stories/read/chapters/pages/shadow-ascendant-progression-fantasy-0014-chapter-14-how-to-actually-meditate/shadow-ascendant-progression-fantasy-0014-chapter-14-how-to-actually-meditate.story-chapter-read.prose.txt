“Oh, don’t be a nitpick,” Instructor Mirayel waved his right hand dismissively. “Anyway, to the point, would you prefer I sit perched on the table, staring at you while you train? Or,” he drifted toward his bookshelf, fingers brushing along the cover as he plucked them out.

“Should I leave and give you some alone time?”

“I am absolutely not hoping for a half day,” he added, shaking his head with five books in hand.

Liar. Though honestly, I’d rather not have him boring holes into me while I’m trying to close my eyes… Wait! Was that supposed to be a threat?

“I’d prefer the second option,” I replied flatly.

“Well then, see you later, Arnos.” He cut me off mid breath, walking toward the door. He didn’t even bother closing it behind him.

I sighed, walked over, and locked it myself. The office was mine now. His office. My gaze slid over the shelves, lined with volumes of dark magic, all staring back at me.

My eyes squinted, frustration spiked. He didn’t even hide it with a curtain or anything. I mean, who leaves a student with such enticing books? It’s like leaving a lion with a bunny! Totally dumb.

As a responsible student, I turned my gaze, avoiding the books’ temptation!

Instead, I was now drawn to the Iceclure, “ahhhh!” I sighed. Everything in this office was drawing me towards itself.

I walked towards it. The surrounding air was frosty.

In his room, the Icelure was in a small glass box with a blue crystal, unlike the ones in the hall. The box had a movable lid on top with ten small holes.

Instructor Mirayel had set it at four holes. I adjusted it to ten, then returned to the armchair he had set for me.

I plopped onto the brown chair. It was comfy, the soft wool… or is it cotton? Well, whatever it was, it was ideal for sleeping.

“Sleep?”

I snapped out of my daydream, not blaming it on the Minotaur fight again. I leaned forward, holding the staff just below the centre in my right hand.

Just need to focus on spreading it horizontally, I reminded myself, closing my eyes shut.

I felt the light of the office hit my eyelids, so I shut my eyes even tighter until all I could see was darkness.

Everything around me was dark. There was nothing—no person, no furniture and no light. I focused my mind on my breathing, keeping it steady.

It was the time I began imagining my body.

In the darkness I sat, with a black body that had no eyes to see, no mouth to speak, no nose to smell. All I had was a core within myself, a small core somewhere near the centre of my chest, and five smaller mana nodes near the mana channels.

The core was glowing brilliantly. It was giving out bright light just like the sun, though I didn’t have eyes right now, so it didn’t blind me, no reason for me to throw out slurs, haha.

Don’t get distracted, Idiot! I yelled in my mind, focusing again on the breathing.

I watched as mana coursed through every part of my body—slow, intricate, and immense, though most of the vessels converged around my limbs and forehead.

I tightened my grip on the staff, imagining it to be an extension of my body. It is my hand, it’s just my hand, I said to myself, until the sculpture inside my mind got a staff of himself.

The mana from my body flowed to the staff, slowly and steadily. I wasn’t even consciously trying to flow the mana; it just happened.

I had to spread the mana out. I knew I tried it… with my fingers twitching and the meditation breaking.

My eyes were still closed, but everything around me vanished. “ahhh!” I sighed, leaning back on the armchair.

The first time imagining something was always troublesome. I knew it already. I looked at my right-hand index finger. The thought that I could touch the mana flow probably caused the twitching.

Something I could do—that is, if I was physically manifesting it to the degree Instruction Mirayel did, something that wasn’t needed, unimportant.

The room was colder now; Icelure was working wonders.

Should I turn it down? I questioned myself, thinking otherwise.

Since who in his right mind would want to get up from their comfy seat for a task? Certainly not me.

I turned my head backward, stretching it a bit to relax the muscles, and then I leaned forward again.

This time, imagining my body with a staff was easier. It took me two or maybe three seconds, and I was back in the same state.

Don’t twitch your finger! I reminded myself. Looking at the flow of the bright energy into the staff.

I gulped, unsure how to start the exercise. Maybe spreading the mana just beside my palm could help? I thought, and that’s what I did.

The mana flow into the staff rippled as I tried to control it… the flow got erratic. I tried to spread my mana, but mistakenly put in more, something I wasn’t supposed to do.

I paused, and took a quick breath, leaning back on the chair. I think I knew the basics of how to imagine myself with a staff in hand; I could do it while sleeping.

But that wasn’t the problem… it was never a problem, how do I spread it? I questioned myself. Gazing at my right arm with my eyes closed, I could still let the mana flow freely.

Wait! Can’t I cover my fingers with mana!? The thought crossed by mind.

Yes, I can’t physically manifest the mana like Instructor Mirayel, but to touch mana, all we need in a mana dense environment, it shouldn’t matter how.

I leaned back forward, the third time, my palm tightened around the staff, a smile spreading on my lips. I was excited.

I turned my eyes away from the staff, looking at the left hand, covering my index finger and thumb with a constant efflux of mana. My mana output for each finger equating the amount the staff sucked from me.

I placed my other hand slightly away from my right hand. The wood was rough around the edges and definitely cold.

I gulped, pressing my fingers more at the surface of the staff, till the mana of all three sources, my finger, thumb and the one flowing in the staff. Met, tangled and converged.

I looked as a tiny orb of condensed mana formed between the stream of the energy. It might work! I mentally exclaimed.

Slowly and carefully, I widened my fingers. While I broke the tiny mana sphere I had formed, I also split the tiny stream of energy into two. I could interact with my mana.

The mana spread apart. Of course, I wasn’t able to spread it as far as instructor Mirayel, but still it spread sideways, forming a quadrilateral of sorts between my palm, crystal and the two fingers.

I kept the mana flowing, attempting to keep my hand steady. I just had to adapt to the feeling of my mana going through an alternative path.

Keeping the fingers steady was harder than making mana flow through a different pathway. I knew my hands were shaky, but this shaky? I had no idea.

I waited a few more seconds. Maybe I have gotten accustomed by now? No, no, I shouldn’t take any chances!

Then, a few more minutes, and I finally stopped the flow of mana from my left hand. I didn’t move my fingers immediately, but slowly.

The mana I had spread? It stayed the same; I had done it! I learnt to spread mana flow while using a staff.

It worked! It freaking worked! I yelled inwardly, smiling.

But then the mana collapsed, moving back to the straight line. “No, don’t!” I yelled, grinding my teeth, pretty sure I had a vein or two popping on my face.

But it didn’t listen; the spread mana slowly but surely moved back towards the staff. It felt like it had suddenly stopped listening to me.

But that’s when I saw it twitch.

The mana that was moving back into the staff moved outward one last time, and that means? My experiment worked, not completely, sure, but partially.

I just had to influence mana from a closer range before increasing the range. I just had to put in more practice.

Following the revelation, I opened my eyes, my head snapping to the clock. It read 11:30, meaning I had roughly an hour and a half before school ended. Though training for that much time would have certainly left me unconscious.

And so I decided to call it quits by 12. I mean, I also had to go to the dungeon with Mabel, right? Without mana, I’d probably die by a slime.

I shut my lids, then leaned backward. My back was throbbing; maybe old age was approaching. I mean, 14 is practically an adult, right?

The next thirty minutes went without a hitch. I practiced steadily and productively. I let the mana flow and spread, all with a smile.

I mean, the training was nothing for me; it was easy, like really, really easy. I just screamed four times out of frustration and asked mana particles to die like six times.

Since I was practically meditating, my mind was rather calm; maybe that’s the reason my reaction was this mild to the idiotic mana flow. I mean, does it not understand when I want it to flow outward? Huh!?

“Ahhhh,” I sighed, my training was over… I mean I ended it an hour early, but I am sure instructor Mirayel would understand, maybe he’d also do a little dance from happiness that he is getting off easy?

“Hehehe,” I laughed, imagining him dancing like those street performers, “idiot!” I yelled, realising I was laughing at my professor!
