We walked after the man who introduced himself as Yusuf, from Indonesia, and talked on the way.

He was friendly and eager — in a way that immediately reminded me of Arjun. Not a great first impression.

“Samar,” he turned to her, smiling warmly. “You are Muslim too, right?”

“Yeah…” she said.

“That’s great! Eight of our group are Muslim. We organize group prayers, especially on Fridays. One of us, Muhammad, is really well learned in the Quran, and he holds casual teaching sessions. They’re really interesting… But he’s currently on a mission with a few others. He would love to meet you when he returns!”

Muhammad was actually lost in the woods trying to find his way back. And he probably wouldn’t love meeting her at all.

“They also have a Christian group,” he continued, “they do prayers together. Though I’m not sure if Protestant or Catholic.”

“I’m actually Jewish,” I added flatly. This guy talked way too much.

“Ah…” Yusuf blinked. “I don’t think we have another Jewish…”

I was far more interested in magic books than the Bible or praying. You know — real magic stuff.

“That sounds nice,” Samar said politely, but her voice had a soft ache to it. She knew we were only pretending to join them. and I could hear her longing for a piece of the old life.

We took a long, complicated route to the keep. I didn’t miss how careful Yusuf was to avoid populated areas and watch for signs of city guards.

I managed to get Duchess for a quick, quiet word while we were at a safe distance from Yusuf.

“Listen,” I whispered. “We’re pretending to be friends with these people. But we’re not. So we need you to pretend to be a nice tamed monster. Better if you don’t talk at all. Can you do that? Can you pretend?”

“Do I look like a Bumrag-fizzleclod?” she whispered back. “I know what’s going on without you telling me.”

“Good. Just be quiet.”

Eventually, we reached the keep, and through a hidden tunnel and alley, we slipped past the wall and into the courtyard.

Sneaking into your own base? Lame.

The courtyard had a stable, a well, and a few shops, but they all looked deserted. Quiet. Lifeless. Then we entered the Keep Entrance, down a long corridor, and finally reached the Great Hall.

It was... ugly.

About ten people were scattered around — eating, playing cards, reading books. There was garbage in the corners, dirty plates, and someone had spilled stew on the stone floor and not cleaned it. The walls had dark smudges. It looked a lot more like a college dorm than a majestic keep.

A single NPC servant shuffled slowly around, trying to keep things in order. She had a sad expression on her face, and just looking at her was enough to leave you uneasy.

Our arrival drew attention. Everyone stopped what they were doing and came to greet us — smiling, friendly, extremely annoying.

Two guys stood out. Everyone else gave them more space. From the names Arjun had mentioned, and from their physical appearance, I figured this was Farhad, the leader, and the other, probably Miguel, his second and the mage.

Farhad held some kind of history book in his hands and spoke in a calm, educated voice.

“Welcome,” he said. “It’s so good to meet other players. Please — tell us about yourself. Where you came from.”

So I told him the same short version I’d already given Arjun. And then he told us more or less the same story we’d already heard — their history, their rise to power, the problems with the guards, and, of course, their grand solution: conquering our town, Kamatouu.

It was literally the same conversation, just with fewer “blessings from the gods” and a bit more self-reflection.Still annoying. Still gave me déjà vu.

But Farhad wasn’t exactly like Arjun. He was... less arrogant.

“I’ve made many mistakes,” he admitted. “The attack on the keep was probably one of them. We lost three good men there — losses that could have been avoided.”

He looked thoughtful. “And how we’ve handled the NPCs here… some of our people treat them badly. And even if they are not truly people… it isn’t the Muslim way. We intend to do better. I will correct those mistakes… when we take Kamatouu.”

It wasn’t nearly enough to earn our sympathy. Not even close. We still had to fake being nice and enthusiastic, and I’m not sure we did a good job.

I was probably doing fine — nodding, smiling, saying more or less the right things. Juliette was mostly quiet, polite, smiling from time to time. But Samar looked pissed, and I don’t think she was even trying to hide it.

“We’ll gladly help you,” I said finally, ceremoniously. “We can do some shopping for you and tell you everything you need to know about Kamatouu. When you want to raid the town, I’ll accompany you as a guide. I know the way and the forest creatures in the area. You could be there in two days easily.”

“Kamatouu is nothing like this place,” I added. “It’s so small in comparison. I think I saw, what… ten guards patrolling regularly? Maybe four more stationed at the Baron’s manor. That’s it.

Total bullshit, of course. Kamatouu had at least three times that number.

“This is great news!” Farhad said, delighted. “Exactly what we needed! In fact, we already sent five people on a mission to your village to gather this same information.” He laughed lightly. “When they return, they won’t be thrilled they made the trip for nothing!”

“I’m sure they’ll still learn valuable things,” I offered.

“You’ve helped us so much,” Farhad said warmly. “Is there anything we can do for you?”

Hell yes.

“We came to Kuriolis to learn about magic,” I said. “We’re really interested in that stuff. Do you… maybe have someone who knows anything about it?”I tried to sound shy, even a little bashful. “We don’t know much — just thought it would be cool.”

Farhad smiled. “I happen to have a wizard… right here.” He put a hand on Miguel’s shoulder. “Please show them around. Answer their questions. They deserve our gratitude.”

Miguel nodded. “I’ll be happy to. Please, follow me.”

We walked after him, up one of the towers, climbing a spiral stairway that was... honestly very cool. I liked that stairway a lot.

But because the tower stairway had so much buildup in my head, I was all the more disappointed when we finally entered the wizard’s room.There were no colorful potions, no glowing symbols, no eyeballs in jars, not even a single skull. What kind of wizard doesn’t have a skull?

But he did have books. Lots of books. And they were probably what I was really looking for.

“Please, sit,” said Miguel, and offered us a table.Duchess went “Chuuu” in annoyance at being expected to just stand in the corner like a tamed pet.

“Despite what my friend Farhad said,” Miguel began, “I’m really just a novice. This art probably requires a lifetime of experience, and I’ve only had a few weeks. So I’m not just being modest — I really know very little. But I’m happy to discuss what I’ve learned. Watch this.”

Miguel raised his hands. “Jw’aawwilll,” he said, and a glowing magic circle appeared in the air, vibrating and flickering.

He concentrated hard, moving his fingers with careful precision. The lines and triangles inside the circle shifted — then with a crackling sound, a thin bolt of lightning shot from the circle and struck the stone wall.

We all gaped.

“Holy shit!” I said.

“Wow!” Samar said.“I know it looks cool,” Miguel replied, wiping sweat from his forehead. “But it’s useless in battle — probably hurts less than a standard police shocker.”

Maybe useless… but it still looked pretty awesome.

“And even that,” he continued, “was only possible after I leveled up twice and put both my skill points into magic. So let me give you some advice: when you level up, put it into strength like everybody else. It’ll keep you alive.”

“So how did you even get that far?” I prompted. I wanted to learn.

“The most important part,” Miguel said, “is stabilizing the magic circle. It’s really hard to form the exact shapes and symbols and keep them stable long enough for the effect.”

He pointed at the flickering symbol still hanging faintly in the air.

“The next thing is: be ultra-specific. Unlike any video game you’ve played, the magic system here is divided into hundreds — maybe thousands — of subtypes. There are more than 15 subtypes just for what call – ‘dielectric breakdown’, and more than 100 for electricity in general. You need to choose exactly what you’re doing and build the right circle for it. Most of the hard work is just deciphering the endless books — which use scientific ideas, but with weird medieval terms, often badly translated by the system.”

“So by conquering the keep,” I said, “you got access to a huge collection of magic books?”

“Yes!” Miguel’s eyes lit up. “It took me days and nights to organize them. See those two bookcases? All about chemistry magic. That shelf — just about gas magic. I thought about making fire magic by igniting gas… but that path turned out to be a dead end. Let me tell you — creating a proper fireball in this world could take years. It’s nothing like games.”

“I see.” I wasn’t deterred at all. The complexity made it even more fascinating.

“And lastly,” Miguel said, “you need levels. Honestly, I don’t think it’s even possible without skill boosts.”

That was... the problematic part. We weren’t planning to murder hordes of forest creatures for XP. And apparently, that was the only way to get it.

“But there’s another way to do magic around here,” Miguel said.

“Magic devices!” I blurted out.

“Exactly,” he grinned. “There’s a shop here in Kuriolis called The Arcane House of Zerathis & Malovar. Unlike other shops that sell useless junk, they have the real stuff. Powerful gear — swords, armor, devices. Just 10 gold coins, and you can get something that makes a real difference in combat.”

“Ten gold?!” I coughed. That was a thousand silver. If those were the prices, we were severely miscalculating.

“Well, if you earn 2 silver coins a day, it’s a bit much,” Miguel smirked. “But if you rob a city keep, like we did, it’s not an issue. In fact, Farhad has a list of items he wants you to buy from there. It would be a huge help.”

“Sure,” I said. “But you did already get to buy some magical devices?”

“Yeah.” He answered, “We had three days with a new player before the system marked him as a criminal. We got some cool stuff.”

Miguel opened a closet and pulled out a silver plate and a glass stick.

“This is like a phone,” he explained. “Someone far away has the paired plate. Only through this side can you start communication. We use it twice a day to contact any party on a mission.”

“Oh, that’s cool,” I said — then I saw Juliette’s face go completely pale. Ah?

“Actually,” Miguel continued, “this is a great opportunity. Let me contact Arjun and his group. Maybe he’ll have some questions you can help with, about the routes to Kamatouu.”

Now I went pale too.

We… made a huge mistake. We even joked about magic phones. How the hell did we not think of this possibility?We were so stupid. And now we were about to be caught.

My brain raced for an excuse to delay the call. I found nothing.Nothing.I blanked out in panic.

Then Juliette drove her knife into Miguel’s throat.

He gagged for a few seconds, blood bubbling from the wound and his mouth. Then he went limp.

I slapped my hand over my mouth to stop myself from shrieking. I wasn’t expecting this — least of all from Juliette.

Samar made a sharp squeak but managed to stop without being too loud.

Juliette stood there, knife trembling in her hand, tears already filling her eyes.

“Look, I didn’t want to do it,” she said in a weak voice, dropping to her knees. “Didn’t want to do it. But I agreed to be the leader, okay? And that’s what I think needed to be done. So I fucking did it.”

I put a hand on her shoulder. “I know…” I managed, still in shock. “You… you didn’t have a choice.”

“I feel fucking sick,” she said, and turned and vomited.

I felt sick too.

We had just murdered a human being. And possibly… a nice one.

At least we wouldn’t need to hide the body, I thought grimly — as I watched it disappear into thin air, clothes and all.
