Chapter 93

Riley looked at Tarin across the table for a long moment before she answered. Then she leaned forward on her hands.

"He is supposed to be the king of this realm, correct?"

Tarin nodded once.

"Then explain something to me. Why does he let raiders kill and steal on his own roads? Why does he let the Clawborn enslave whole villages like the Hoshin? I have walked into a village where every adult had their tongue cut out for being too loud at a tribute count. I have watched a child see her father executed for paying tribute that had been doubled overnight on the whim of a Clawborn thug. If your king holds the southern border, what exactly has he been holding it for."

Tarin did not flinch, and he did not look down. He had heard versions of this question before, and the answer he had ready, was the answer he had already given other people in other rooms.

"Grey Ridge could defeat the Clawborn in an all-out war," he said. "My king believes this. More importantly, most of his council believes this. It would cost him tens of thousands of soldiers and the wealth of a generation. The faith of the great Houses, several of which would refuse the levy or split from him outright if he called it. He would likely lose his throne in the process even if his army won the field. To a king with that calculation in front of him, raiders on a southern road are a small matter. A border village whose tongues were cut is a small matter. A Clawborn doubling tribute on a whim is, again, a small matter. The world looks different from where he sits."

"Maybe the king's throne is a little too tall. The world looks different from where I sit too."

"Yes." Tarin held her eyes. "And that is why he sent me."

Riley did not answer that. She let the answer he had given her stay where it was on the table between them. Her hands were still flat. Thorne had not moved. The soldiers at the door had not moved. The room had the particular quiet of people who were all waiting for the same thing without saying so.

Then she shifted, and the conversation shifted with her.

"Why did you come through my gate openly today. You could have arranged a quiet meeting and didn't, why?"

Tarin took the breath of a man who had been waiting for the question.

"Because by walking through your gate today as my king's man, I have ended my life as Tarin the tea merchant. I cannot return to Rivermark, that cover is finished. Whoever I am from this point forward, I am a known agent of Grey Ridge. There is no version of tomorrow where I sell tea at the corner of the market square and listen to a boy from Stonehaven complain about the price of cinnamon. That man is gone."

"Was that your choice?"

He paused; it was the only time he paused in the conversation that was not a courtesy pause to let her speak.

"No," he said. "It was my king's judgement and the weight of the message I carry, he thought tipped the scales."

Riley looked at him for a long moment.

She saw a man who had been called to a duty years ago and which gave him a new identity. She saw a man who had served well and been spent without consultation. She saw the small bend in the shoulder that came from carrying that for the length of this journey he had not chosen. She did not let any of what she thought show on her face, and somewhere in the part of her that was still the woman who had made Mali a doll out of scraps, she filed it away and said nothing.

"Is Tarin even your real name?" Riley asked.

"Does it matter?"

Riley sat back in her chair and took a breath through her nose that rose her chest.

"All right," she said.

There was a knock at the door. Nikola let himself in, turned, looked at the group, and stopped.

"Apologies," he said, his eyes moving to Riley, who gave him a single nod. "You requested this as soon as possible. All resources are at critical points. Production has stopped and—"

"Stop." Thorne's voice came from behind Riley's left shoulder, flat and carrying. He did not shout. He did not need to. His eyes were on Tarin, whose head had tilted a fraction and whose eyes had narrowed just slightly at the edges. "This can wait."

The room held its silence for a beat.

"That will be all," Riley said.

Nikola said nothing more and gave a nod, stepped back twice, turned, and pulled the door closed behind him. His footsteps faded into the sound of the yard outside and the two soldiers breathing at the door.

Riley looked back at Tarin, stared at him for a moment looked slightly up with only her eyes then reestablished eye contact.

"You are going back to your king today, before the light fails. You came as a Hoshin and you will leave as one. Hooded, with two of my men. They will take you far enough south that we will know you have truly left our walls. Understand, if you attempted to penetrate these walls again or are found around them without making your presence known, you won't leave here. Ever."

Tarin did not protest. He had understood, the moment he passed through her gate, that the terms of his return were going to be hers to set.

"Yes, my lady."

"Warden." Riley corrected.

"Yes, Warden."

"One question first."

"What does Darius want from me?"

Tarin looked at her for a moment before he answered, and when he did his voice was quieter than it had been for the whole meeting.

"He doesn't know yet. He wants to see what you become before he decides."

Riley tilted her head and looked up into the distance. Then she took her hands off the table and folded them in her lap and gave him her message.

"Tell your king I have heard him. Tell him I am not a game piece to evaluate. When I am ready to speak with him, I will do so, and I will choose the terms of that exchange. Until then, tell him he would be better to concern himself with the small matters of his kingdom, before someone else does."

Tarin's lips moved and his eyes widened.

"He will receive it exactly as you spoke it." Tarin held her eyes. "Something this plainly spoken doesn't need interpretation."

Riley nodded once at the soldiers at the door and they came forward. Tarin stood without being told. He looked at her one last time before the hood went over his head, and there was nothing in the look that asked her for anything. Then the cloth came down and the soldiers turned him toward the corridor and walked him out. The door closed behind them.

Riley sat at the head of the table with her hands in her lap and Thorne behind her left shoulder and the room very quiet. After a little while she lifted her hands back to the table.

"Warden, we are not low on resources, we are very much the opposite." Thorne said.

"Yes, we have had spies in our midst for some time looking for information, we just gave them some. Maybe it will buy us some time. I am not ready for the King of the south. Yet."

She thought through the change to the situation now in front of her. Corvessa eyes, watching her in the south. The Clawborn, lion share of their army in the north to fight some unknown threat. Grey Ridge, watching her through a king who was patient, thorough and willing to spend a multi year asset on an afternoon's message. Two of them knew her name. One of them had been measuring her for months. One of them had just put its army into a valley nobody understood and might or might not be coming back from.

She had an open window, with an unknown time limit to its close.

She stood and left Thorne, heading upstairs without a word. She looked out her window and the escort detail was just visible going through the north gate, two guards on horseback and a third horse with a hooded man on it, and the dust they kicked up hung pale in the light for a few seconds before the wind took it. She watched until the gate closed behind them and they disappeared into the treeline.
