​

“I’m telling you this is a bad idea.”  Denderra hissed.

“I don’t care.”

In spite of his words, Alley knew that what he was about to do was going to feel awful at best, and be actively devastating to his health at worst. It didn’t matter; Alley had made his choice, and he wasn’t going to allow himself to lose here.

Taking a deep, steadying breath, he shot one final look at the Cards in his Hand.

He had redrawn at the start of his turn to replace Soul Exchange and pulled Endless Horde. So his Hand was ‘Endless Horde, Mouldering Mage, Shadowed Eater, Wraith Master and…’

“Hunter’s Descent!”

​

In spite of what the Card said, it cost Alley a Vial because his Deck did not possess any of the Resonances listed on the Card. He spent the Vial, Discarded Mouldering Mage, Sacrificed Brutish Zombie, and then paid the true price of playing a Card that should never have been put in his Library.

First came the pain as Resonance that was completely alien to Alley’s internal system flowed through him.  This was different to when Clataire Astley had poured centuries' worth of Undeath Resonance into him, or when the power of The Cursed King had circulated through his Resonance system. Those had been overwhelming amounts of Resonance that had bent and split his Resonance channels out of shape, yet at least the Resonance had been of a type that his body was used to.

This might be orders of magnitude less than either of those experiences, but the energy itself felt corrosive. Alley’s Resonance channels were still raw from the brief time he had spent with the strange spirit that was made of both his own soul and that of Anaten in his body, so the burning of a Resonance he wasn’t accustomed to was even more painful than Alley had anticipated. He winced and managed to stay standing straight, even though his body was begging him to either bend double or sink to a knee.

The Cards Alley had selected as his sacrifices to play Hunter’s Descent were converted into blue light, which shot up above the Challenge Arena to fuse together into what was an obvious, if ethereal, rendition of Darius riding the huge duck Amelio. Both the man and the feathered beast were just a little bit more dynamic looking than they were in life, with sharper features and a mystical-seeming glow about them.

Tracking the flight of the Spell Card’s creation with his head, Vern looked upwards.

“I respect the urge to win kid, but -” the rest of his words were cut off as the image of Darius and Amelio suddenly slammed into Vern’s side of the field with a shockingly fast dive.  The force of the impact was greater than the real moment had been as well, where Darius had merely ended up with a broken arm and a Demanifested Duck. This was an explosion of dramatic force that sent a shockwave through the air that was strong enough that Alley's cloak whipped out behind him, and he had to hold an arm in front of his face against the brief gale.

At the same time, every last Terracotta Warrior exploded into blue light and vanished into Vern’s crypt.

“Sorry,” Alley called with his classic shit-eating grin. “I didn’t catch that.”

The large man from the Shattered Peninsula shook his head ruefully before he replied.

“I can already see you are sweating and shaking.” The white-bearded man called. “ Keep playing like that, and you will kill yourself.”

As much as he would have liked to argue, Alley had to admit the man had a point. He could feel his nausea rising, and his body being shot through with intense bouts of hot and cold, along with an ache that had blossomed in his knees and elbows.

Wild-eyed and still grinning, Alley nodded.

“I know, but I’ll be winning today.” With that, he Manifested Shadowed Eater. The monster was, in a word, horrifying. It wasn’t a unique, so it Manifested in a flash of blue light without any fanfare. Still, the Creature that emerged was an androgynous mass of shadowy cloth strips with nothing but blackness and a pair of bright, pupilless eyes where its face should have been.

​

​

Alley continued to grin as he moved on from his Creation Phase to his War Phase, though it was more like an emotional armor to keep himself upright against the awful sensations running through his body than a true expression of his feelings. Doing his best to disguise how hard he was breathing and how much his body was demanding he go lie down, Alley sent Necrotic Fiend to attack his now defenseless opponent.

Well, almost defenseless. The older man might not have any creatures left on his side of the field, but he did have his Crystaline Barrier.

The whitebearded man sacrificed the Relic, and the attack was negated. Alley considered the pair of Cards he had redrawn.

‘Shade Step, Shadeling’.

‘Well, I’m drawing a theme at the moment I guess.’ Alley thought, though he wasn’t expecting to play either of these. In fact, outside of the Wraith Master , he wasn’t expecting to play anything else at all.

His left hand clenched, and his eyes still wild from the fever that had swamped him Alley went on to end his turn.

“Alright,”  Vern said quietly and then spent a solid thirty seconds looking at his Cards before he began to actually play things. First, the muscular man  had no choice but to choose one of Alley’s creatures as the target for Hall of the Underlord.

Shadowed Eater was selected as the target and grew by five hundred power thanks to the sole Relic still on Vern’s side of the field. Next, he activated a familiar spell in the form of Strike It Rich.

​

Vern’s eyes glowed blue for a few seconds, and then he selected something with a little smile.

“On to plan B.” He said. Alley had seen the other man play several times throughout the tournament and was fairly sure he knew what was coming. It wasn’t quite the same level of threat as the swarm of Terracotta Warriors, but it still represented a legitimate problem. Or would, if Shadowed Eater and Wraith Master weren’t sitting in Alley’s hand and on the field, ready to end the game.

“Sage of the Stone Palace!”

The ape dressed like a wizard that appeared in the flash of blue light cost Vern another Vial, but the old man didn’t even seem to notice. It was enough to make Alley jealous. He might have gained more ranks than could reasonably be expected in the last six months, but he still felt so far behind the sheer amount of Life Essence everyone he faced seemed to have built up.

“Next will be the hammer.” Alley mused, as he continued to fight back the sense of nausea and delirium that threatened to overwhelm him even further.

“Tectonic Hammer!”
