Explosion after explosion rang out as Veronica launched more spells at the metal knights. For some reason, their armor seemed almost unbreakable. No matter what she threw at them, they kept getting back up. Even the seemingly normal weapons they wielded, regular iron blades and metal shields, held their form under heavy damage.

What exactly had caused this room to appear? What happened to the monotonous, boring-looking rooms that looped around to each other?

Veronica threw another spell—a compressed burst of wind straight at the metal knights. The air exploded in front of them, knocking each of them off their feet and sending them flying back into the armoury, where they crashed against the far wall.

Although she couldn’t damage them, it appeared that she could still move and restrict them.

“What the hell is going on?” she murmured.

Before the metal knights could recover, she quickly turned around and pushed open the doors she had originally come through, curious as to what would appear behind her. Maybe she could leave without fighting them.

As the doors opened, she gritted her teeth.

The room in front of her, which she expected to be one of the mundane rooms with multiple doors, had now become another armoury. Sets of weapons and armor appeared in front of her, with each metal knight reset to its starting position. The same central double doors appeared at the far end.

Just like before, she heard the screech of metal, and a second later, the metal knights began to move toward her.

So every new room is now one of those knight rooms?

She was in a new predicament.

Sage. What are the chances that going through those doors at the end will open into another armoury room with more of these knights?

[The chances are high. It seems something has caused a shift in the illusion’s rules. If you go through that door, you may be stuck in an endless loop of dealing with armored knights ahead and behind.]

She glanced behind her. The doors to the other armoury she had entered were now closed. The rule that only two rooms were accessible at a time was still in effect.

She looked back ahead, noticing the metal knights reaching for the weapon racks. She had to make a decision soon.

Until now, there had only been one recognizable room pattern: an identical template. She mentally labeled it the base room: four white walls, four identical double doors. For the past hour, she had been going through copies of them over and over again. But now, the castle had switched room templates. A new one had been added.

The armoury room.

She was standing in the center of a base room, which connected to an armoury room. If she decided to go through the door at the end of the armoury, the base room would disappear. And if her hunch was correct, she might step into another armoury room, leaving her stuck between two of them, with twelve sets of indestructible knights to deal with.

The knights in front of her had now grabbed their weapons, leaving her no more time to think. They rushed at her all at once.

Veronica raised a barrier several meters away from herself, forming a thick wall between her and the knights. They immediately stopped and slashed at it, but their weapons bounced off.

Veronica studied them closer.

They kept swinging at her barrier, but she didn’t lose much mana in the process. Although they were nearly invulnerable, they weren’t that strong. The swords they wielded were just regular iron or steel blades. With that alone, they wouldn’t break through her barrier anytime soon.

The problem was that they were tireless. She couldn’t maintain a barrier forever.

Sage. I’m just going to head through those doors. Maybe if I run through enough, I’ll see another room template.

[Very well. I will keep track of any changes.]

Veronica nodded and used her mana to push the knights aside as she walked through unharmed. Once she reached the other side, she pushed open the doors.

As expected, and definitely not what she wanted to see—she entered another armoury room, with its own set of metal knights. Now, she had to deal with twelve of them attacking from both sides.

“This is so annoying…!” she grunted, immediately rushing forward to the next set of doors.

This went on for half an hour. Every new room was another armoury room, with no sign of changing to anything else. She just wanted to leave and see something new, but nothing changed.

Eventually, she slowed down to take a break. She maintained her barrier, keeping all twelve metal knights at bay in front of and behind her.

“Got anything useful yet, Sage?” she asked.

[There isn’t enough data yet for me to come to a conclusion on how to break out of this illusion.]

She clicked her tongue.

[However, it seems your body may be getting hungry.]

Veronica raised a brow. “Hungry?” She frowned. Maybe… maybe she was? She didn’t feel that hungry, but perhaps her nerves were masking it.

[Let us keep going. If you’ve entered an armoury, perhaps there are other rooms from the castle you can enter, like a kitchen.]

She was mildly confused. Whatever Sage was telling her didn’t quite make sense, as if it were hinting at something. Instead of asking, she simply stood up and walked forward; her barrier pushed the metal knights out of the way until they were pressed against the walls.

A kitchen stocked with food wouldn’t be too bad to see.

As Veronica placed her hands on the door, she tried to push them open, but then she paused.

The door wouldn’t budge.

“What?” she murmured.

Again, she tried pushing, but the doors wouldn’t move.

“Alright, then… How about…” Her words trailed off as she accelerated her mana rings. Mana flooded her body, her hair lighting in separated strands as her eyes glowed.

[Exalted Body has been activated.]

With her strength enhanced, she pressed her hands firmly against the door. And in one go—she pushed.

The moment the doors burst open, her instincts screamed at her. Not like she needed them to, because the danger immediately revealed itself.

A torrent of water crashed against her, immediately soaking her clothes. It was relentless, pouring into the armoury as if she were at the bottom of an ocean.

She grunted and rapidly formed a dome-shaped barrier, preventing more of the water from overwhelming her.

“What the hell?” she said, trying to hold her ground against the rushing torrent. “Why is this room full of water?”

She couldn’t see past the doors. There was only a solid rush of blue water and white foam.

[Veronica. I believe I have found the trick to the illusion.]

Veronica focused on maintaining her barrier and holding her ground. “Great. How about you tell me before I drown in here?”

[You need to turn around and exit through the front of the armoury before the water pressure gets too strong.]

Veronica nodded, not needing to be told twice. She immediately turned, leaping backward. The water in the room was already up to her chest, and it didn’t seem to be stopping anytime soon.

[The next room will be another water room. You’ll need to enter it as quickly as possible. The trick to the illusion will be inside.]

“Alright,” Veronica said out loud. Once she reached the front of the armoury, she pressed her palms against the door and pushed as hard as possible. If it was another water room, she’d need to brute-force her way in.

The doors exploded from their hinges with little resistance, much to Veronica’s surprise. What Sage had said would be a room submerged in water was anything but. Instead, it was what she had mentioned earlier: a kitchen. A large counter stood in the center with several cooking ingredients, like bags of flour, sugar, fruits, and vegetables. Along the walls were shelves and cabinets, with more food waiting on the counters. At the far end was another set of doors.

“What?” Veronica said aloud, bursting into the new room.

[Do not stop. Run to the other end as quickly as possible. The next room will contain an enemy you’ll have to defeat. According to my calculations, it should be a large male warrior, heavily armored, physically powerful but slow. He’ll likely use a large melee weapon, so keep your distance.]

Veronica’s brows furrowed as she ran to the other end.

That was oddly specific, but the giant wave of water was still behind her. The water room had since closed, but the water that had flooded into the armoury was still rushing after her. It wasn’t enough to harm or drown her now, considering it had another room to spread into, but it was still enough to impede her movement.

“You said this was supposed to be a water room. How do you know for sure that—”

[Trust me. You need to be careful in the next room. Think about how you’ll defeat the enemy first.]

Veronica wasn’t given much time to argue. She rushed across the kitchen and slammed both palms against the doors.

In her mind, she was already preparing for what Sage had described.

If it was going to be a tough enemy, she’d have to try and beat them in an instant. She poured mana into her palm, ready to blow up whatever appeared in front of her.

She burst through the doors and came face-to-face with a base room once again. But in front of her was nothing—

Veronica’s instincts screamed.

She felt movement above her.

In that instant, something dropped onto her back before she could turn, driving her hard against the floor. Veronica twisted as she fell, landing on her back and throwing up one arm while mana surged into her palm. If there really was an enemy here, she was prepared to detonate the entire space, even if the rest of her party was somewhere in the same illusion.

That plan was cut short.

Cold metal pressed against her throat.

Veronica froze.

She looked up.

The person straddling her stomach froze as well.

Sitting on top of her, blade pressed against her throat, wasn’t some heavily armored warrior. No, it was her party member. Her friend and equal lover.

“Nasha?”

Staring down at her was Nasha, looking every bit as surprised. She slowly lifted the blade away from Veronica’s throat.

“Veronica?”
