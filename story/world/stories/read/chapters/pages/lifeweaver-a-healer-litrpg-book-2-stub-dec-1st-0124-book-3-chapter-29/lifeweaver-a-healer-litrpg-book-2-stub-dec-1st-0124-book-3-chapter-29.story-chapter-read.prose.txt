Walking through the portal and emerging back in New York, the shift in temperature was slight but noticeable. Going from daytime to night with a single step was still a little weird, even after experiencing it so many times. The others waited nearby, except for Curtis and Ray, who must have gone back to the apartment to watch TV. There was no sign of the other group who'd entered the dungeon before being shoved back through by the demons. No blood was a good sign, at least.

"Nice to meet you, Luke!" Nick shouted over from where they'd formed a tight circle together. "You're welcome to fight with Phoenix Phalanx at any time!"

Luke waved to them as Hannah appeared behind him. "Sure! Let's hope for something a little simpler next time!"

"It's never simple," Ariel said with a snort, before the whole group turned and walked off, Nick speaking in a hushed but harsh voice to Clementine, who kept looking back over her shoulder, staring daggers at the portal.

"Ahh!"

The cry made Luke turn and remember he was far from done for the day. Lemira had emerged and was down on her knees, hands to her head. Weaver's Perception told him what was happening to her, and it wasn't good. Not unlike with radiation and the death energy, her body was just, for lack of a better explanation, falling apart, the very cells dying.

Reaching in with threads of mana, Luke got to the task of healing her. While her physiology was a little different from a human's, it didn't differ much. Unlike her father, she had actual mana channels, a meta-heart, as well as a skeleton, the right set of organs, nerves, et cetera. All of it was there, just a little different. To his relief, dealing with the nerve bundle in her neck removed most of the pain she felt. Otherwise, she would've been in excruciating pain for the whole ordeal, and it didn't seem like it would be a quick fix, either. The act of being on Earth was killing her at a rapid rate. If Ezezal thought this was slow, he wondered what he'd gone through coming through the portal.

"Can you stay, Hanna?" he asked as he concentrated. "I'll need a lot of mana before we're done here."

"Of course," she answered, getting down to sit next to Lemira, stroking her hair as the half-demon sobbed.

The portal closed, leaving them in the dark in the middle of the road. Even while healing her, Luke picked up his patient with as much gentleness as he could and placed her on a wooden bench on the sidewalk. It wouldn't do to get run over by a car in the middle of healing. Hannah followed and ended up sitting with the other woman's head in her lap, patting her as Luke worked.

Adding Hannah to their small weave added a little to his own vast store of mana, and Luke even pulled from Lemira herself, using the woman's own mana to heal, since what he was doing required so damn much. With this sort of damage, there was nothing to fix per se, at least no injury to stitch back together, or illness to flush out. Her body was ripping itself apart, and Luke raced through her body with threads of mana, applying healing mana to the worst spots, keeping the mana spend as low as possible, since he didn't know how long this would last. Hearing Ezezal, it sounded like his daughter would acclimatize to Earth. Luke saw nothing like that happening, and he worried they were doing nothing more than buying time for her, extending her suffering.

As if that wasn't enough, the differences between her body and that of a human made things even more difficult. Her mana wasn't quite right. Luke's and Hannah's mana felt more like cool water, or soft wind racing through their mana channels, while hers was warm to Luke's senses, almost too hot to touch. It made navigating her body, both physical and metaphysical, difficult, since it resisted him at every turn. Her meta-heart was the same, shaped like a human's but with something akin to the demon's sludge pumping through it, almost-burning and slow-moving while holding so much more than even Luke's. When he added mana to it, as well as healing mana, it swirled around with her own, as if unsure what to do before Luke pushed, forcing all of it to bow down to his will.

Boon of Potential grants you an attribute point (+1 Willpower).

His expertise in directing her mana increased, and combining it with his own meant he could use it for healing, but it was a damn chore. Her heart threatened to give out at several points, forcing Luke to keep a thread at the ready at all times to massage it back into sinus rhythm. That wasn't all. Lemira's lungs shut down at random as well, burning from the inside, as if the very air she breathed was toxic. Choking, her body struggled before he was able to fix her, spending more mana than he could afford to get his patient up and running again. It hit the nerves as well, which would have been torture for her if Luke had not first numbed them, then severed them at the neck to give Lemira some peace. Different parts of the young woman shut down again and again, and Luke was losing hope as he kept a close eye on the available mana.

Even so, he kept healing her. A full hour later, he got the first inkling that the damage might be slowing down. Lemira's sobbing had subsided somewhat, and it appeared as if she drifted in and out of sleep, her mind no longer able to keep itself going due to the damage.

"I can't do much more, Luke," Hannah said, her voice gentle.

He nodded and removed the thread of mana from her. She'd already done more than he would've expected, and her mana was low at that point.

"Thank you," he said without opening his eyes. "Stick around?"

"Yeah. I'm not going back to Curtis's. Don't want to be a squatter. Also, I never liked Scrubs much. Big fan of Cougar Town, though."

Luke chuckled and wiped sweat trickling down the side of his face. "Worst Bill Lawrence take I've ever heard. You should check out Shrinking. Guess we won't get that third season now, though, not with society falling apart and anything."

"You never know," Hannah said.

They sat in companionable silence after that, while Luke worked to keep Lemira alive. Little by little, the decay stopped ravaging her body, easing the burden on his mana pool, but it still wasn't over, and he was running low. Surprisingly, Lemira's own mana regenerated at an incredible rate. Using that to fuel her healing, Luke was further forced into a learning by doing situation, dealing with her strange mana and manipulating it into doing what he wanted. Healing mana based on her own worked better with her body, which was a welcome surprise, even if he should've expected it.

A numbness spread as he struggled to keep going after hours of monotonous work, and when his interface dinged, he started, having been half-asleep.

Boon of Potential Grants You an Attribute Point (+1 Focus).

"Huh," he grunted, sitting up.

At that point, dawn had come, the sky turning from purple to gray. A shiver ran through Luke, who felt a pang of hunger, like he hadn't eaten in forever. Come to think of it, he wasn't sure when he'd even eaten last. His vision blurred and mind spinning, Luke blinked and shuddered, trying to focus. His mana was low enough for the threads of The Greater System to appear, like a weave beyond the weave of his own system.

Lemira was still sleeping. Hannah too. He wasn't sure when it had stopped, but his patient's body was no longer falling apart, and he must've healed the nerves at some point, too, because they were no longer severed. She was fine.

"Hey," he said, nudging the two.

Neither moved.

"Hey!"

Hannah blinked and sat up. "Wha?"

"It's done," Luke said.

"Hey there," Hannah said, leaning forward to brush Lemira's hair from the half-demon's face. "Wakey, wakey."

When she didn't get a response, she looked up at Luke, who shrugged. "She's been through a lot. Maybe she needs sleep."

"I need more sleep, too," Hannah said, shuddering under her Skeletal Archon robe. She glanced around and a second later, her Shelter appeared right there on the sidewalk. Frowning, she looked down at the still sleeping woman in her lap. "I don't really have room for her."

"She's my responsibility," Luke said, putting up his own Shelter right next to Hannah's. It was a little bigger, but not by much. "She can take my bed. I'm too wired to sleep."

Hannah gave him a look.

"Relax," he said with a sigh. "I'm not going to share a bed with her. I'll add another bedroom if she wants to keep staying with me."

"Good. I'm not so sure Dot would be too pleased otherwise."

He let out a tired chuckle and supported Lemira's head, allowing Hannah to get up from the bench. "That's over."

"You don't know that."

Luke gave her a look. "She saw me kill Alan and then laugh about it, and that's after killing Leslie, that Solarian."

"Maybe you should stop killing people?" Hannah asked, raising an eyebrow.

It was his turn to give her a look, and she raised her hands in surrender. "Not that I blame you. I would've done the same. But don't worry, Dot will see reason."

"She wanted to talk today."

"That's good!"

He shook his head. "Over message. Didn't want to see me."

Hannah's face fell a little, but she did her best trying to wipe the pity off her expression. "Well... Stay strong!"

Luke couldn't help but laugh, despite it all. "Thanks. See you later?"

"Maybe. We'll see. I've got practice."

"Practice?" Luke asked as he stood, Lemira in his arms. She was snoring now, some drool running out of the side of her mouth.

"Always wanted to play an instrument. Figure it's now or never, while we still have electricity."

"Makes sense, I guess," Luke said. "What do you play?"

"Guitar. Found a group playing out in Seattle, so I'll be heading over there later today."

"That's far."

"Traveler, remember? I go where I please."

"Right! Cool, cool. Let me know when you're playing somewhere. I'll be there."

She laughed. "We just started. I barely know how to hold the damn thing."

"Still."

She opened the door to her Shelter and gave him a thumbs-up. "Yeah, sure. Of course!"

With that, she was gone, having disappeared into the small cube that was her new home.

"Guitar, huh?" Luke asked, looking down at his still-sleeping patient. "Pretty cool, if you ask me. Now let's get you to bed. I've got a tough conversation waiting for me."
