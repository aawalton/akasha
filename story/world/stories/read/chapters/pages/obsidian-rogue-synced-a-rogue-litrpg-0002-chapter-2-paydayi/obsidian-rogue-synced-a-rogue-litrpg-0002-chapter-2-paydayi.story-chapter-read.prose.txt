"You've got my attention," Levi said. “What job?"

"He's just a level one synced," the rogue beside him said, his tone dismissive.

Levi was startled by the voice of the man who'd been silent since he'd stepped into the room.

"So?" Killian asked.

"He can't do it, and I can't jeopardize a mission like this on someone who's only been synced for two minutes—"

"It's actually been about four hours," he corrected. "Besides, I've been working for Killian for years. He knows I can work whatever job you have lined up."

"You can't," the rogue said matter-of-factly.

A tense silence dominated the room. The three men quietly stared at each other. Well, Levi tried to stare, but his gaze kept drifting off the rogue's visage. It was almost like his brain didn't want to register the rogue. It was certainly an unsettling experience for him, but at the same time, it made him more confident in his decision to pick the rogue class. Off the top of his head, he could imagine a few use cases for such a skill.

"Well, that's unfortunate...For you," Killian said to the dismissive rogue. "He's your only option, especially with the time limit you set. You could decide to wait and see if any of my other associates will be willing to take a job with so little notice, but I highly doubt that. Which leaves you with him, your best option, your only option."

What are these two waffling about?

Levi tried to focus on the face of the rogue—at least he tried to—but in the space where a face was supposed to be, he only saw darkness. Nothing more, nothing less.

"Fine."

"Excellent," Killian said, a triumphant expression on his face as he turned to Levi. "Now, my boy, the job that we want to offer you requires a certain, how shall I say this ... Finesse. In other words, we need a clean job, in and out as fast as possible."

"That won't be a problem for me. I haven't been caught—"

"Yet," Killian corrected. "Listen, you getting synced might make you powerful, but it also makes you easily identifiable, especially at earlier levels when you don't have protection against the basic analyze skill."

Levi tried to object, but the taskmaster raised a hand, indicating that he wasn't done talking. "That aside, you aren't in the little leagues anymore, lifting items off unsuspecting merchants and …well, easy marks in general."

"So what? I doubt there's anything or anyone in this town that I can't steal from. Not much of a difference if you ask me," Levi said, his voice full with confidence, earning him an appraising grunt from the rogue.

"The difference is that these people fully expect to be robbed. In fact, I believe that they're counting on it," Killian said. "And at the very least, individually, they have the power to crush you like a bug if you're caught. Which is why finesse is required."

The way Killian worded it made the job seem more like suicide. Unfortunately, that didn't do much to deter Levi. The newly synced rogue couldn't help but wonder what exactly they were after, or more importantly, who it belonged to.

"What's the job?" Levi asked again. Killian had masterfully dropped the bait by subtly claiming there was something he shouldn't… no, couldn't steal, and now all he wanted to do was steal it.

"Well, I did warn you," Killian said, stroking his white beard as he prepared his following words. "We have some guests coming this evening. They've already booked the entire Sapphire Inn for today and tomorrow, which means you both have limited time to get in, steal what you need to, and get out."

"So I take it the job is happening sometime tonight?" Levi asked. "We hit them when they're tired and take the mark. That brings me to my next question: what is the mark?"

This time, the rogue answered him. "The obsidian stone."

"The what?" he asked. For something that was apparently so valuable, it was the first time he was hearing about it.

"Information on the obsidian stone is well guarded, so don’t think too much about it. All you need to know is that it's a black gemstone the size of a fist," the rogue said. "There are a lot of rooms in the Sapphire Inn, which means a lot of places to hide the stone; that's exactly why I need a partner for this job."

"Okay, that takes care of the what and why," Levi said. "Anything else I need to know?"

"The party coming in tonight are expected to be at least D-ranked, with two C-ranked confirmed."

Grak this.

Levi whistled in annoyance, his current ranking coming to mind. He'd been ranked F by Cerpital, which made him totally outclassed if any different confrontation should occur. On the other hand, Levi rarely got caught, which was a significant factor in how he'd been able to negotiate a ticket off of Killian without signing a contract.

"What's the pay?"

"One gold,"

"I want five."

"My boy, it doesn't work like that. I'm afraid my hands are tied, I can't—"

"Yes, your hands are tied because it looks like you need me to get this deal done on time, so I say five."

"Best I can do is two."

"Make it three or I walk away."

"You drive a hard bargain, boy. Three gold coins it is," Killian said. "I won't be giving you a single copper till the job is done. You know it's a big job, plus I can't have you running away with half of the coin now, can I?”

“This is the first time you're refusing to pay me half up front, but I'll take the job anyway,” Levi grumbled.

That should take my stash to about seven gold coins.

It might not have looked like it, but he didn't have much of a choice in the matter. The amount of gold offered for the one job was a lot more than he'd made in the last year working for Killian. He'd gone off on a hunch that the rogue was rich, judging by whatever fancy skill he was using to keep Levi from focusing on him, and his hunch had led to him negotiating a lot more than he'd expected.

With coin like that, he might finally get his chance to leave Gantus, reasonably prepared. A part of him still wanted to press Killian a bit more, just to see if he'd break on the matter of giving him some coin upfront, but he ultimately decided against it. It wouldn't look good to press Killian for his coin right in front of the client.

"Now that you both are done, let's get to planning. We only have a few hours before the sun goes down," the rogue said, with both Levi and Killian nodding in agreement.

Planning the heist was the easy part; the execution, on the other hand, seemed like it would be a hassle. Nothing Levi couldn't handle, but still, it seemed he'd have to go through a hail of fire for his big payday. It felt like it'd take everything he had to get out of this one unscathed, and he didn't like it. Too much at stake, too many uncertainties, but the money he'd get from this job alone was close to half his savings. A significant sum for a night's work. He'd be able to pack up and leave Gantus confidently, knowing that Silas and Bree were well equipped for his departure.

It's worth it, it has to be.

At least that was what Levi repeated to himself as he crept closer to the sapphire Inn. The shrubbery that surrounded the inn was something that made the place stand out. Ornamentally oriented as it was, it would allow him a hiding place, especially in the dead of the night. The building was eerily quiet courtesy of its newest inhabitants.

Whoever the visitors were, they were certainly competent. There were two people at each entrance, one person on the roof and another patrolling the building, and those were just the easy-to-spot defences. Unfortunately for Levi, that was already too much for him to handle ... Alone, which was why they'd agreed that the other rogue would go ahead and clear a path for him to follow.

Speak of the Vavnir.

The rogue was currently scaling the wall, and he was doing it at a pace that was three times that of Levi's, which caused him to whistle internally. Going that fast up a wall should've been impossible, but the rogue had done it, which meant another skill was in play. Another skill that had him salivating. So far, he'd only witnessed two skills in action, and both skills had made the rogue much more efficient.

"I guess I'm up," Levi whispered.

The rogue had successfully scaled the wall, and he'd overpowered the person on the roof, quickly. Which meant it was time for Levi to get moving. With the grace of a thief, he moved forward. Exiting the shrubbery to the side of the building, which was currently unguarded. He started climbing immediately, knowing he didn't have time to waste, especially since someone was still on patrol.

"You're slower than expected," the rogue whispered as Levi came onto the roof.

"My bad," Levi said, his eyes scanning until he saw the body of the roof patrol, a few feet away from the door on the roof. The patrol was probably a ranger, judging by bylin's glow, which outlined what they wore. “Dead?"

"Unconscious.”

“Impressive," Levi said. The rogue had scaled the wall and knocked out the patrol in quick time, and he hadn't made any noise as well.

A grunt was the only thing Levi heard from the rogue before he moved to the door on the roof, opening it and motioning for him to follow. Levi gingerly stepped over the unconscious body and followed the rogue into the inn. Bright yellow light flooded his vision as he stepped into the inn, causing him to blink twice to clear the black spots in his vision.

"Alright, stick to the plan. I take the ground floor, you take this one. If you find the stone, you take it, find me, and we'll escape together."

"Noted."

A subtle nod was the only sign Levi got that the rogue had heard him before he dashed downstairs. His footsteps were so faint that Levi barely heard them, even though the rogue had been right in front of him seconds ago. Shaking his head at the oddity, he got to work.

"Easy money," he whispered.

A glance confirmed that there were only six doors along the hall of his floor, which made his job seem more manageable. The fact that it was upstairs, rather than downstairs — where it was further away from the guards at the front and back doors of the inn — made it safer. Well, as safe as it could be considering his line of work.

Shrugging his shoulders, he got to work. The first door was to his right, so he gingerly put an ear to it for five seconds. Listening for signs of life, satisfied with the fact that he hadn't heard anything suspicious. He reached for the door, cracked it open, and neatly slid in, closing it behind him. A habit he'd picked up after almost having his right eye poked out in one of his past jobs. He'd succeeded that day and had also gotten a free lesson in what not to do.

I guess no knowledge is actually wasted.

For the best Inn in Gantus, it was modest, to say the least, at least that was what Levi thought as he carefully canvassed the room. There were two drawers beside the bed, a two-person bed in the corner of the room, and a nightstand. The room was untouched and bereft of any runes — at least any that he could easily spot. Regardless of what he did or didn't see, he played it safe. Being fast was only good if you didn't get caught.

Slow and steady.

Rushing in this case would most certainly get him caught or, worse, killed, and that depended on how lenient the people he was trying to rob were. Fortunately, Levi had no interest in getting caught, and he absolutely hated having his life dictated by anyone else.

Closing the last of the drawers, he sighed, aiming for the exit. This room didn't contain anything interesting. He hadn't even glimpsed a traveler's bag or anything of the sort to suggest that anyone had settled into the room at all. If anything, Levi's best guess was that the entire floor was unused. It was just a hunch, but he wasn't getting paid a lot of money just to speculate, which meant he had to get moving to the next room.

Levi thoroughly searched the drawers, beds, nightstands, and even the space underneath the beds for the stone. That process was repeated for the next four rooms. Each came up surprisingly clean. This increased Levi's begrudging respect for the visitors, and he couldn't help but tip his hood off to their fore planning as well. He'd been brought in just in case there was actually something up here. Five rooms in, and the entire floor looked unused. The lack of runes on the doors was a giveaway, but he always played it smart, which was why he was going to check the last room on the floor, even though the others had been a waste of time.

Who am I to complain? It is free money.

Chuckling softly, he made his way to the last room, which was arranged the same as all the other rooms on the floor. He went through his checklist. Confirming that the stone was absent from the room, he prepared to exit, but he'd barely completed a step towards the door when all hell broke loose.
