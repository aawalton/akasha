Ekon’s heart seized in his chest as the mutated giant waded toward the shore, each movement sending massive waves rolling across the watery surface of the Folded Chamber.

A moment earlier, he had seen the Sovereign—no, the corpse the Sovereign had been possessing—drop from the sky like a stone cast from a cliff. How that happened, he had no idea. Still, something like that was hardly a minor setback for a great being like the Sovereign. Surely, he had foreseen the possibility. Surely, it was part of the plan.

But Ekon wished such a plan hadn’t left him alone here with a mountain of muscle and claws and teeth.

What now?

The thought flashed through his mind as he watched the giant climb onto the land, water pouring from folds of swollen flesh and cascading down its limbs in sheets, its immense body rising higher and higher until it seemed to block his entire field of vision.

The vines reacted immediately. Green tendrils burst forward in thick writhing masses, coiling around the giant’s ankles. More followed. They crept up, wrapping around its calves and knees and thighs, layer upon layer of vegetation tightening with enough force to crush armored men.

The giant didn’t seem to notice. It took a step, and the vines ripped like wet parchment.

What should I do?

Running back toward the bridge would be suicide, but trying to hide in the building didn’t look like a good option either. After all, he still didn’t know what exactly was inside.

As he pondered, the giant got closer.

Much closer.

Its shadow engulfed him as one massive foot descended like a mountain falling from the sky. He threw himself sideways at the last possible moment, rolling across dirt and broken vines as the impact struck the ground where he had stood, sending a shockwave rippling through his very bones.

He barely had time to scramble to his feet before the giant moved again—this time not a stomp, but a kick. The motion was casual, almost lazy, as though the creature were simply clearing space rather than trying to kill anything in particular, and Ekon sprinted out of its path just as the catastrophic blow smashed into the corner of the stone building. Chunks of stone flew outward, dust and debris raining down around him as the entire structure trembled, as if it might collapse under its own crushing weight.

This is... manageable, actually.

A ridiculous thought to have while being hunted across a tiny island by a nightmare from the depths. Yet it was true. The giant was enormous and terrifying, but it was also slow and clumsy. As long as he kept moving, he could avoid being crushed under those mountain-sized limbs. Even though he had no way to defeat the thing himself, survival no longer felt impossible. Besides, the giant was every bit as dangerous to the enemies hiding inside the building as it was to him, and that was something he could use to his advantage.

One of the vines burst through a shattered window and emerged dragging a masked man behind it. The poor bastard thrashed wildly, kicking and twisting as the green coils tightened around his body, holding him high above the ground like a child showing off an insect they had caught.

Without giving it much of a thought, Ekon willed the vine to throw the man at the giant. An instant later, the screaming captive shot through the air, straight at the hulking monster’s misshapen face.

The creature’s hand came up in reflex, and with a single swat that echoed like a thunderclap, it sent the masked man flying, his body tumbling end over end before splashing into the water far from the island. He didn’t resurface.

For several seconds, the giant stood still.

It stared at the place where the man had vanished under the waves, and Ekon could swear the creature looked confused. As though it had only just realized what it had done.

Then, to his surprise, it began to retreat.

Step by step, it walked back into the water. Now it stood waist-deep offshore, looming above the surface like a floating, grotesque fortress.

Ekon’s brief optimism vanished.

The giant was no longer trying to chase him. Instead, it plunged both hands into the tangled mass of vines that was trying to reform the barricade. Great fistfuls of greenery vanished in its grip as roots, branches, and tendrils were ripped from the earth. Sections of the barrier were destroyed within seconds.

The creature had changed its tactics. Whether such an idea had come from its own limited mind or from someone giving it instructions, Ekon couldn’t tell, but it had apparently concluded that personally hunting him was inefficient. So, rather than continue lumbering after a target it struggled to catch, it had shifted to a support role. It would let the smaller monsters do the killing while it cleared the way.

Through the gaps in the living wall, figures surged forward. They were larger than the previous creatures, broad-shouldered humanoids with gray-blue skin stretched over powerful muscles. Their heads resembled those of sharks, complete with rows of serrated teeth and black, lifeless eyes. Spears, tridents, and harpoons gleamed in their hands as they advanced in a snarling mass.

Ekon exhaled. Then, he drew the whip from his belt, feeling the familiar weight settle into his grip. He muttered the words under his breath, and fire answered. Thin at first, then steady, it crawled up the length of the coiled leather.

He cracked it out in a wide blazing arc, forcing the nearest shark-men to recoil as sparks and embers scattered across their damp skin. He spun it again, keeping the fiery weapon moving in wide circles around his body, creating a barrier of flame to keep them at bay. This wouldn’t hold for long, he knew that much. But every second he remained alive was another second closer to the arrival of reinforcements.

“Ekon!”

He heard the voice before he could fully process the two shapes that split the sky above the battlefield. When he looked up and recognized who they were, the relief that hit him was so sharp it almost hurt.

But that relief lasted only for a heartbeat.

The giant’s gaze swept across the air, and his friends suddenly faltered mid-flight as though the world itself had decided they were no longer allowed to remain aloft.

Then, they fell.

Ekon reacted before he could properly think, driving what remained of the vines around him to shoot upward in a desperate arc. Fortunately, they were not too far away, so the green tendrils reached for the falling figures, wrapped around them just in time to catch their descent, and lowered them toward the ground as gently as the situation allowed.

Before her feet even touched the earth, Mandra pulled out two of her vials and smashed them down. The instant they shattered against the soil, new vines erupted in thick, overlapping waves that spread outward to reinforce the fragile defensive perimeter. At the same time, Renee rolled cleanly out of the descending tendrils and came up in one smooth motion, her staff ready in hand. She pivoted toward the nearest shark-faced creature and brought it down in a single brutal strike.

Mandra walked to his side, her expression tightening as she scanned the surroundings.

“What the hell just happened?”

“I think,” Ekon said, glancing at the looming behemoth still anchored at the island’s edge, “it can shut down someone’s wind manipulation just by looking at them.”

“That’s ridiculous,” Renee shouted from nearby as she cracked yet another skull. “It means I’ll be completely useless here.”

Mandra’s eyes narrowed. “Where is he?”

“Well... he got caught by the giant’s gaze, so he... dropped from the sky and sank.”

“What?”

“It’s just the puppet. His real body was not affected at all.”

“And where is that real body?”

“Nearby... I think,” Ekon replied. “He’s coming. Trust me, he’ll be here within minutes.”

Mandra didn’t look reassured in the slightest, and he could hardly blame her. Because even he had begun to doubt. He had always told himself never to take anything for granted, but for some reason, he had come to believe—no, to have faith—that as long as the Sovereign was with them, they could win this fight effortlessly and without paying any real price.

He let out a weary sigh. Either way, he was the one who had dragged Mandra and Renee into this mess, the one who had assured them that everything would be alright. So it was his responsibility to keep them out of harm’s way.

Better said than done, though.

The vines were still holding, but only because Mandra had concentrated them here, near the building, and away from the shoreline, since anything extended too far would be immediately ripped apart by the giant. Meanwhile, more and more shark-men were pouring out of the water as though the sea itself had decided to empty its contents onto their position.

Mandra muttered a curse, then she ordered the vines to retreat even further, tightening into a dense, layered barrier that enclosed them in a perimeter roughly fifteen paces wide. She even had them seal off the nearby openings of the building, as she feared the creatures might enter through another entrance and attack them from within.

The three of them stood within the enclosed space, watching every movement beyond the living wall. Renee gripped her staff, ready to strike anything that managed to slip through, though for the moment the barricade stood firm enough. Perhaps, it would buy them just enough time for help to arrive.

That was when Ekon saw the giant lift its hand.

And with it, came a boulder. Easily the size of a wagon, cradled in its palm as though it weighed nothing at all.

Where the hell did it pull that from? From the seabed, or from the island itself? Well, it didn’t matter. What mattered was the simple, horrifying fact: it was about to use that rock. It was going to throw it—at them.

We are... trapped.

The thought arrived with uncomfortable clarity. They were confined inside their own hastily constructed barrier, and beyond it the swarm of shark-faced monsters pressed in from every direction. There was nowhere to run.

He glanced at Mandra. Her face had gone pale. She had seen it too, the inevitability.

From the edge of the island, the giant drew its arm back in a slow, deliberate motion, muscles tightening across its enormous frame as it prepared the throw.

“I’m... sorry,” Ekon said.

Mandra didn’t say anything. Instead, she reached out and took his hand, her fingers locking around his in a grip so tight it was almost painful. He hesitated for a heartbeat, then squeezed her hand back.

The boulder was coming. A dark sun blotted against the sky.

Ekon closed his eyes. This is how I die, huh?

He could hear it all. The roar of something massive carving through the air. The screams of shark-men, crushed out of existence as it tore through them. The sound of vines being shredded in its path.

And then—

Nothing.

No impact. No pain.

Huh?

Had his death been so quick and painless that he wasn’t even aware of it?

No, wait.

How could he even be thinking this if he was already dead?

Slowly, he opened his eyes, and he gaped.

Because the boulder was right there. It was like... two paces away from Renee. Yet, it was not moving forward at all.

Between them and that impossible stillness, there was a figure. A man made of shadows. He stood with one hand raised, his left forearm casually resting against the surface of the rock.

The Sovereign.

He had arrived.

And...

Did he just stop that boulder dead in its tracks with his bare hand!?

Renee’s voice broke the silence. “...How?”

The Sovereign gave no answer. He angled his face past the rock and toward the edge of the island, where the giant still loomed above the water like a malformed monument. Ekon followed his gaze, and only then did he notice it. Something was climbing over the giant’s body.

Was it... a snake made of water?

He had seen it once before, when the Sovereign summoned it to massacre the masked men during the battle in the ruins. Now, the very same living construct was slithering up the giant’s torso, coiling around its throat before flowing across its face. The surface of it spread like poured glass, sealing over mouth and nostrils and eyes. The giant reeled back, hands scraping frantically as it tried to tear it away.

Suddenly, ice bloomed. The crystallization raced along the serpentine body in branching veins, and the water construct locked into place, hardening as it clung to the giant’s face. The frost spiderwebbed outward from the point of contact, crawling over skin, petrifying flesh.

Then, the ice shattered.

In the blink of an eye, the front half of the giant’s head was gone.

The enormous body toppled backward, the force of its fall sending a tremor throughout the entire Folded Chamber. A massive wall of water surged into the sky, expanding outward in every direction.

“Up,” the Sovereign said, then he rose.

Ah yes, Ekon thought. With the giant dead, aeromancy should function again. After a brief delay, Renee lifted him and Mandra into the air as well, just before the flood could reach them.

Looking down, Ekon watched the unfolding aftermath. Massive waves rolled from the fallen giant, crashing into the island and washing away clusters of shark-men like they were little more than debris. And where the hulking creature had once stood, there was nothing now but widening rings that dissolved into ripples.

Only then did Mandra speak at last.

“The fuck just happened?”
