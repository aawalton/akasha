[Spacefold] activated, and reality shrunk around me as I suddenly appeared on the wing of the skeleton pilot's F-35. The jet was already positioned on the catapult rail, engine spooled up, and if I’d mistimed it even a little, I was about to have a very bad day.

The skeleton turned its skull toward me, probably wondering what the hell someone was doing standing on his wing.

He never got the chance to ask, because I ripped the canopy release lever, and the glass bubble hissed open. Before the pilot could react, I grabbed it by the collar of its flight suit and hauled it out of the cockpit with every ounce of strength my Grace suit could muster. Which wasn’t much, but my rings did enhance my other stats enough to make the task doable.

The skeleton's bones clattered against the tarmac surprisingly far below, and I didn’t bother to see if it got back up. I threw myself into the pilot's seat and fumbled for the harness straps as quickly as I could.

Here, my Grace suit did help me, letting my fingers fly faster than any human possibly could, loosening the straps and clicking the five point harness less than a half second before the catapult fired.

On the bright side, my tactic had worked, and I’d made my move late enough to prevent the skeletons from aborting the launch.

On the down side… I was so not ready for the onslaught of acceleration that slammed me back into the seat. The air flew out of my lungs and for an instant, I couldn’t breathe as the F-35 rocketed from zero to 150 knots in less than two seconds, the catapult flinging us into the air like a slingshot. Which, I guess it kinda was.

But then I was airborne, and the insane G’s suddenly ended.

They were replaced by warning sirens, which immediately started blaring. Red lights flashed across every display I could see, and I realized with growing horror that I'd never closed the canopy. Or done any of the dozen other things I was probably supposed to do for a safe launch.

Turned out canopies weren’t designed to stand up to 150 knots plus, and the thing sheared off, flying off into the distance.

The wind hit me like a sledgehammer, buffeting the cockpit with enough force to make the jet shudder. At 200 knots and climbing, the airflow was trying to tear my head off. Without my armor's helmet and its integrated visor, I'd have been completely blind, eyes full of tears and unable to see shit.

Luckily, I had the big noise canceling headphones on from my previous plane-jacking attempt, so at least I wasn’t deafened. Even then, I barely managed it.

The stick felt loose in my hand, the jet wobbling as the aerodynamics fought against the missing canopy. I jerked the stick right in pure panic, trying to correct, and the F-35 immediately rolled inverted.

“Shit, shit, shit!” The sky and the ground traded places and every instinct screamed at me that I was going to die and that I should let go of the controls and cover my face, like that would somehow help.

I fought it down and forced my hands to move. Opposite stick input, gentle pressure on the rudder pedals, and the jet slowly rolled back upright. My heart was pounding away madly and I thought I’d throw up, but at least I was flying straight and level again.

Well, more or less.

I at least managed to find the landing gear lever and yanked it up. The wheels came up, silencing one of the several alarms blaring at me. The flaps were next, getting them configured for cruise flight.

Except by the time I managed all that, we weren't anywhere near Asakusa anymore. In fact, I wasn’t even sure we were in Tokyo at all.

Forget the Skytree or the Imperial Palace—I didn’t see a single landmark I recognized, and the surroundings had gotten distinctly suburban.

“Holy shit this thing is fast.”

The jet had taken me halfway across the greater Tokyo area in the time it took me to stop panicking and get the damn thing under control.

The airspeed indicator read 450 knots and climbing. That was over 500 miles per hour, and I was still accelerating. Suddenly, all those hours in the sims felt utterly useless. Nothing could prepare you for this.

Still, I supposed they weren’t completely useless, because I was pretty sure only the reflexes I'd honed from hundreds of hours in the sims kept the plane in the air.

It was only then that I had the thought to reduce the throttle, which was currently maxed out. I definitely didn’t need to be traveling at these absurdly stupid speeds, so I pulled it back to a more sane setting that wouldn't get me killed. Or tear a canopy-less plane apart.

Even then, buildings were blurry streaks and it was almost impossible trying to find a highway, let alone try to follow one back.

Well, turning the thing around seemed like a good start. I could figure things out from there.

I knew from experience that to turn a plane like this with wings that generated almost no lift, I’d have to bank 90 degrees and pull back on the stick.

The F-35 responded instantly, banking so hard I felt the G-forces pressing me into the seat. The jet was insanely maneuverable, way beyond anything I'd experienced in the sims, and holy shit the forces it could pull. Three Gs. Four. My vision started to tunnel as blood rushed away from my brain.

Yeah, maybe I shouldn’t be turning that hard.

My Grace suit wasn't doing a damn thing to help me here. Enhanced Agility meant jack shit when the problem was blood pooling in my legs and my brain getting starved of oxygen. I was pretty sure the only thing keeping me conscious was the enhanced Vigor from my rings.

Even then, I was barely hanging on. If I'd been in my Vigor suit instead of Grace, I'd have been able to handle ten times these forces without breaking a sweat. But I'd needed the mobility to get here, and now I was paying the price.

I eased off, leveling out, and couldn't help the grin spreading across my face despite the absolute chaos of it all.

I was actually flying a fighter jet. A real, honest to god cutting edge fighter worth more money than I’d ever see in ten lifetimes.

Then a blaring warning started screaming at me, and the grin disappeared.

The radar display showed three contacts behind me. Enemy fighters. And I already had warning tone blaring at me, and even a civilian like me knew exactly what that meant.

“Countermeasures, countermeasures—where the fuck are the countermeasures!” My hands flew across the console, stabbing at buttons, trying to remember which switch deployed flares and chaff.

Nothing happened, and I stopped when I realized mashing random buttons midflight might not be the brightest idea.

I threw the jet into a spiraling downward turn, bleeding altitude and speed, trying to break the lock through sheer maneuverability the way they did in the Top Gun movies.

It was probably stupid.

No, scratch that. It was definitely stupid because a second later, the F-35’s flares shot out automatically, sounding like a muted machine gun. The missile lock tone cut off, replaced by the distant sound of explosions as the enemy missiles chased the flares instead of me.

“Okay,” I panted, leveling out. “Good. That's good. Auto-countermeasures. Nice.”

The plane might have saved my ass, but I couldn't afford to screw around. Those enemy fighters were still on my tail, and more were probably scrambling to intercept. I needed to take out the core and get the fucking dungeon to collapse.

I pulled up, gaining altitude to just below cloud level, and finally got a clear view of my target.

The dungeon core floated above Asakusa like the disgusting eldritch ball of flesh that it was, pulsating just like a heart.

And surrounding it, those cargo plane deployments had already linked up into a lattice of metal framework, some kind of emergency shielding being hoisted into place by hover-capable modules.

Still, it was a big target. A big, stationary target. I could work with that.

I put the jet into a shallow dive, lining up my shot, and pulled the trigger on the stick.

The Gatling Cannon roared to life, slinging rounds at such an absurd speed, it sounded more like a swarm of mosquitos than a machine gun.

I held the trigger for maybe two seconds before letting off, and the entire jet shuddered from the recoil.

My eyes briefly registered a ‘94’ on one of the screens before I did a double take.

Seriously? 94 rounds remaining?

“Are you shitting me?”

I'd started with 180. 180! A two-second burst had eaten 86 rounds. Which meant I had maybe two more seconds of cannon fire left.

“What idiot thought it would be a good idea to give this thing such a pathetically tiny loadout?”

Not that any more would’ve done much good. Most of the rounds hit the core dead-on, but the temporary shielding did its job. They just bounced off, sparking harmlessly against some invisible barrier that ran between the metal latticework.

“Son of a bitch.”

The computer responded by beeping at me incessantly.

More missile locks. I banked hard left, then right, going to max throttle and throwing the plane into a dive.

The countermeasures deployed automatically again. Flares fired behind me and I heard the explosions, but this time a red alert flashed on the icon for the countermeasures on the primary display, Japanese text blinking rapidly next to a big fat 0.

I couldn't read it, but I didn’t need to guess what it meant.

Countermeasures were empty. Another missile and I was screwed.

At least I'd figured out how to arm my own missiles by then. All four AIM-120 missiles were loaded and ready to go.

The only problem? The targeting system was too smart for its own good.

The display automatically identified targets and assigned threat values to them, and of course, there were no targets… because it treated the other fighters as friendly.

As for the pulsating dungeon core? The damn thing didn't even register. The system simply didn’t consider it a target at all.

“Come on, lock the fucking core!” I desperately cycled through targeting modes, trying to manually pick the target, but nothing worked. The system wanted to shoot other aircraft, and it wasn't giving me the option to override.

“No, I do not want to target the fucking F-35s! I want to shoot the gigantic floating sphere!”

The missiles just sat there, waiting for a target lock that I couldn't give them.

More enemies were converging on my position. I had maybe thirty seconds before this got significantly worse.

“Fuck it.” I mashed the weapons release, firing all four missiles.

They dropped from the internal bay and immediately veered off in different directions, their seekers locking onto whatever the jet's computer had decided was worth killing. Two went after enemy fighters. One tracked a gunship. The last curved toward one of the shield modules.

Maybe that would actually do something useful, but I wasn't holding my breath. This was not how I had envisioned things going.

But then, I didn’t need guns and missiles, did I? Not when there was the most reliable missile of them all.

“Time to kamikaze this motherfucker.”

I eased the stick back, pointing the nose at the dungeon core, and shoved the throttle to maximum. The F-35 surged forward, slamming me into the seat with raw acceleration, and I braced for impact.

The jet had mass. It had a shit ton of velocity. And in about ten seconds, I’d see just how powerful those temporary shields were. I somehow guessed they wouldn’t be up to the task. Not when my JDAM bombs sat armed and ready to blow.

Which was a very good thing, because the incessant siren told me that another volley of missiles was headed my way, and I was fresh out of countermeasures.
