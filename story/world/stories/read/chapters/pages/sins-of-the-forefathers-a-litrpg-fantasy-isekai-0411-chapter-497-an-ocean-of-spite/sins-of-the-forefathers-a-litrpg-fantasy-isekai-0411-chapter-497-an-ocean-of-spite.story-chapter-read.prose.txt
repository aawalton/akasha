In some ways, it reminded me of the lake basin that was far above us, here deep beneath the surface of Vereden. There was, in some way, a lake of blood in the center of the cavern that opened up before us, though on a much smaller scale. There was, in some way, a number of different ‘spires’ arranged both around and in the center of the space. I could tell that it was meant to be a smaller reflection of the greater, in terms of scale, ritual that existed in the mountain hollow of Karag Korvok. That at least told me this had to be a ritual as well, of some kind.

But the similarities were only skin deep, for all of the lack of skin on display.

In terms of size, the ‘lake’ was more like a large pond…and I’m not one hundred percent certain that was blood. Within the bounds of it was an oddly thick liquid that reminded me of blood in some way, but instead of a deep, rich crimson, it was black instead. It swayed and swelled in a breeze that didn’t exist within this confined space, small waves skittering across the surface and lapping at the gravel shores. There was a smell to it as well, something that wasn’t reminiscent of the above lake either. There was no ever-present tinge of iron in the air. For some reason…

It smelled of salt instead. I was struck, suddenly, with an intense longing for the ocean, for that is what it reminded me of. I deeply wished I were elsewhere from this miserable place, sailing upon the seas I had grown to love, skies unclouded by calamity stretching off far away into the distance.

For some reason, the odd black water of the pond was familiar to me, for reasons beyond the salt-born longing in my heart. I swear I had seen it before, but as high-strung as I was right now, the memory wouldn’t come.

I instead focused on what else was in the cavern.

Instead of rising from the center of the pond as the Heartspire had, there was instead an oddly disturbing, almost pyramidally shaped stone hanging from the ceiling. Formed, or perhaps created, from impenetrably dark onyx stone, the great inverted mountain was impossibly precise. Four perfectly flat planes on each face terminated in a wickedly sharp point that hovered only a handful of feet above the surface of the pond. In a perfect circle upon that surface, the black water was completely calm, with none of the waves or ripples entering into it. They almost seemed to break upon an unseen barrier there in the center of the pond.

As if from a distance, through my shock at the sight before me, I heard Fade’s voice from a distance. “Nate, something feels off here…”

That had to be the center point of whatever ritual was being performed here, and yet the mirrored one above had other components to it. That must be what my Familiar was sensing.

And that was where all of the many, many captives Korvoks had taken came into play.

Arranged all around the circumference of the pond were seven spires of, presumably, stone, not unlike the ones we had toppled, if not in a smaller, more grotesque form. But these towers were different from the ones we had unintentionally destroyed, yet similar to another kind. These were covered in the odd, pulsating flesh that we had seen on the greater bulk of Karag Korvok, when we had fought through the streets of the Hold. They were all spaced equidistantly from each other, and for good reason.

Each of them was surrounded by pens. Pens not unlike those you would hold livestock within, all of them neatly divided and walled off from each other, centered around the pulsating spires and forged from crude iron. The result was the closest thing I had seen to classic chain-link wiring from back on Terra.

And in those pens were the missing captives we had fought so hard to find. Hundreds and hundreds of them, verging well into the thousands. It was impossible to count all of them, simply because of how packed into their prisons they were. The Korvoks had shoved them in so tightly that all of the myriad different Dwarves were standing shoulder to shoulder in abject, torturous misery.

This was far, far more than would have been abducted from Karag Varla alone. I don’t think that Ul-Korveth had been bluffing when he said that his madmen had raided more than just their sister Hold for ‘sacrifices’.

There was a low, continuous moan of anguish and despair that filled the air, coming from thousands of throats. Dry, hopeless sobbing, wails of helplessness, and pleas for deliverance echoed off the walls of the cavern we found ourselves outside of. At first…at first I didn’t understand why. There was no obvious cause, beyond their imprisonment and the abominable circumstances they had found themselves in.

And then I looked closer with both my eyes and my mystical senses. The people were oddly pale, even for those who dwelled beneath the earth…because their very Aether was being drained. With every pulse of the pseudo-flesh upon the spires they were forced into proximity with, a small sip of their vital energies was forcibly torn from their spirits. A wave of flinches would pulse through the masses of the prisoners every time it happened, followed by fresh wails from the victims. By itself, the amount of Aether stolen from them was small…but together, taken from every single soul imprisoned within this place, the combined wave of energy was massive.

There were far, far more of the armored Korvokian warriors in here, as well. The walls of every single pen were surrounded by dozens and dozens of the Immaculata. To my further fury, their job seemed to be to manage the prisoners who were most desperate to escape. It wasn’t uncommon for desperate Dwarves to try to climb the fence, or even to try and reach beyond it, begging for mercy, even in the scant few minutes we had watched this atrocity. Every time, they would be beaten down or beaten back by the Immaculata jailers, often sent back into the pen with broken bones or fresh gashes from the halberds used to do it. The Korvoks doing it didn’t even seem to care. These were by far the most dispassionate of their kind I had yet to encounter, but that didn’t matter.

This was monstrous. This was a violation far beyond even the physical mutilations that the Korvoks inflicted upon their sacrifices. It was one thing to defile the flesh.

It was something else to defile the soul.

In an instant, I had drawn the laser pistol my hand had been almost constantly hovering near, and I leveled it at the back of Ul-Korveth's disgusting head. I lightly brushed the trigger, and the barrel lit up in a glowing, searing-hot heat, and I dug it into the maroon flesh there. The faint sizzling and scent of burning muscle filled the air, joining the disturbingly out-of-place sea smell, but the Korvokhan before me didn’t even flinch.

“Oh?” I could hear the smile in Ul-Korveth’s voice as he spoke, as if he wasn’t concerned in the slightest. “Is that a weapon, Nathaniel? My, my. Are you already choosing the path of most resistance?”

I ignored his taunting, as blades all around me were bared by my soldiers. I wasn’t the only one who had made the connection about what was happening to the captives, and I certainly wasn’t the only one affronted by it.

Strangely enough, the armed and armored Immaculata didn’t seem to care that I was holding their leader at gunpoint. The spectacle got a few disinterested turns of the head, but other than that?

No reaction.

It didn’t matter. They weren’t going to be my problem unless they made themselves my problem.

“Give me one good reason why I shouldn’t blow your head off right now,” I said lowly.

“Nate, something is WRONG!” Fade nearly shouted across our bond. I chanced a brief glance down at my side to see that the Spirit Wolf was crouched in a lunging position, growling not at any of the Immaculata, or even Ul-Korveth himself.

Instead, he seemed to be growling at the pond and the pyramid that hovered above it.

Ul-Korveth must have sensed the movement, because he chuckled. “A reason, hmm? Well, one reason is, perhaps, because of the consequences should you do so.” He turned his head slightly, uncaring how the heated barrel of the gun carved a line in the back of his head from the movement. The Korvok stared at me with one mad, amused eye as the corner of his mouth quirked. “At the moment of my death, my Immaculata will cease treating our guests with utmost gentleness. You are strong indeed, Nathaniel. I can tell. But not one of you and yours is quick enough to save all of our sacrifices before they are scythed through like wheat. Some, yes…but not all, and then you shall need to deal with all that the wretched, relic of a who caretaker led you through.”

Caretaker? Was…he talking about Korvakh? I hadn’t thought about the steward much since we had entered into the Aorta, but I hadn’t seen him leave either. If I strained my senses, I could tell that he was trailing to the back of our formation, but it was like he was almost blending in with our surroundings. Perfectly still.

Didn’t matter. Ul-Korveth’s threat did.

I narrowed my eyes, even though he couldn’t see it. “Maybe we just take you captive yourself, then,” I growled, digging the barrel deeper into his skull. “Rescue everyone down here, slaughter all of your puppets, and then once we’re free and clear…”

I let the implicit threat hang in the air as I raised my free hand and motioned forward with it to my people behind.

“I like that idea,” Bleddyn chuckled maliciously, as he stalked forward past me. In his wake trailed dozens and dozens of our own warriors, each of them more than ready to finally, finally put an end to this far.

The moment that Bleddyn’s foot crossed the threshold into the chamber proper, something changed. A previously hidden sense of awareness within the air around us, that had been blending into the background, suddenly snapped into focus. I don’t think I was the only one aware of the sensation of eyes that fell upon everyone present, then, and they were not friendly eyes.

There was a distinct sensation of malicious glee.

Fade howled in pure, sudden panic, the sound echoing in an oddly sinister manner throughout the chamber. “NATE, IT’S HER! WE HAVE TO G-”

He froze. I froze. Bleddyn froze.

In fact, everything froze. Every soldier under my command, every Immaculata both in the chamber and around me, and every single prisoner within the chamber before me froze. Time still passed, and yet…I didn’t have control of my body any longer. I could still breathe; I could still move my eyes, but that was the extent of it. I certainly couldn’t speak. Absolutely everyone within range of the odd pool in the center chamber had lost complete control of their bodies.

I had to fight down a sudden, intense surge of panic. This was a worst-case scenario on a level I had never even considered. How was this possible? Even Ul-Korveth himself had abruptly stilled. Neither he nor his people had been spared from whatever phenomena this was.

What the Hell was happening?!

I soon got an answer.

A low, malicious feminine chuckle abruptly rolled throughout the chamber, cutting through the last remnants of Fade’s howl. There was no source that I could pinpoint, no speaker. It was like the voice was coming from nowhere and everywhere all at once.

And then, a light bloomed in the chamber. In the space between the pond and the point of the pyramid, a malevolent red star of crimson energy oozed into being, as if it were crawling between the gaps in reality. Languidly, it sank beneath the surface of the black water, disappearing from sight.

For a moment, even the residual Aether in the air of this abominable chamber seemed to hold its breath…

A pulse, echoing out from deep beneath the suddenly still surface of the pond, rippled outward. First, through the pond, sending waves lapping out onto the blackened gravel shores, and then through the very air itself. When it reached me, I wanted to shiver in horror as I felt the very blood in my veins ripple within my…body…

My blood. Suddenly, I, and perhaps I alone, realized what was happening. Through my blood sense, I could feel it.

The blood of every single person within my range had stilled. I could feel hearts all around me fluttering in very real peril, as they suddenly found themselves incapable of pumping anything at all, as their lifeblood itself was frozen unnaturally. Even I was starting to notice the effects, and only seconds had passed since this effect had begun.

Slowly, sluggishly, the blood in mine and, from what I could tell, everyone around me, started to flow once more. But it was as if there was the feeling of a hand carefully hovering over my entire circulatory system, and it was only flowing at the owner's behest. Still, we were all held at the mercy of this grip, incapable of moving. At the very least, though, we were not in danger of sudden death.

Or perhaps not.

The top of a head breached the surface of the pond, slowly revealing itself to the air of the chamber. The owner, whoever they were, was almost slinking out of the black blood of the pool. Every feature on their face was obscured by the liquid, and yet I could see something distinctly feminine about the shape of their body, as it sauntered up onto the shore of the pond. It wore no coverings nor clothes, and yet there was not the sense that it was bare to the world. Rather, it was as if the ebon fluid of the pool was enough for their purposes. There was nothing lavicious about this thing’s form, and yet there was a posture of supreme confidence within the stance they took, as they stopped mere feet upon the beach.

Gradually, as they continued to be shrouded by the blackness still clinging to them, I became aware of something. There wasn’t even a body beneath all of it.

That form was comprised entirely of the black liquid.

Upon its nearly featureless face…two bright red, crimson orbs appeared in the hollows where eyes should have been. They roved casually across the cavern, first over the prisoners and their Immaculata jailers, pausing briefly on the still form of Ul-Korveth…

Before stopping on me.

And a voice that I had heard but once in my life, that I had yet to forget, opened from the darkened facsimile of a mouth that appeared on this thing’s face.

“You.” The single word dripped enough contempt to drown the world in its callousness. “I should not be surprised that you have managed to worm yourself into even this hallowed nexus, Precursor. But…perhaps…you can be of some use, in the end.”

I abruptly regained control over my face, and I licked my lips in nervous anxiety. Almost unbidden, a word escaped them.

“Neris…”

The chosen avatar of the Great Spirit of the Sea merely cocked its head at me…

Before a smile that bore an ocean of spite crawled over its blackened face.
