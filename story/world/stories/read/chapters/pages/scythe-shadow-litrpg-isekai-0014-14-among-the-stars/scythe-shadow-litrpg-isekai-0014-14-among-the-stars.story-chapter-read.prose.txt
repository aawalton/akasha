I stood, surrounded by a globe of stars, as if I floated out in the center of the galaxy. They twinkled brilliantly, infinite in number.

Seconds ago I'd been at the pillar, writhing in pain. Now I was here. I felt nothing—not the fall of clothes on my skin, not the pressure of boots on my feet, no warmth or cold. Almost as if my body didn't exist at all.

My feet rested on solid ground, a small transparent platform with a slight teal hue. Instead of a scythe, sitting in my palm was the solid Amber sphere of the Will I'd harvested. It was weightless.

The red and blue bars that I'd become familiar with atop my vision were gone, making me feel surprisingly naked.

My attention was drawn above, where specks of light began to move and realign. A few at first, then dozens. Hundreds. In a circle in the sky around me, they arranged into recognizable symbols. A shield, flame, snowflake, flower, lightning bolt, eight-point star, heart… And skull. As the shapes solidified, they glowed white at varying intensities. Two, however, glowed Amber. The star and the skull.

These are the various affinities. I didn't know the names for all the affinities, but it was clear that the two Amber symbols above represented Astral and Death—my assigned affinities. I held up the orb, observing the flame symbol etched into it. And the interface said this Will is a Zeal affinity.

I rotated until I found the flame symbol in the galactic sky, holding the orb up. A perfect match.

Then the orb cracked, just like it had when my scythe first pierced it. This time, though, the light that escaped was white instead of orange. It drifted up like mist, drawn to the flame above, seeping from the shell until it was empty. The mist joined the glow of the flame, making it ever so slightly brighter.

The remnants of the orb disintegrated into small sparks, then disappeared.

[Will Successfully Relinquished

Rank: Amber

Affinity: Zeal]

[Initializing corresponding augmentation to:

Volition

Strength]

Physical sensation returned like a train blasting through a concrete wall. The weight of my body, the itch of fabric on my skin, even a slight headache I didn't know I had.

My veins suddenly burned orange—no Amber, light searing through my skin in a euphoric release. I expected pain to follow. Instead, an unfathomable relief of tension throughout my entire body.

[Augmentation complete.]

The stars around me began to spin like I was being sucked into a black hole. The transparent floor vanished beneath my feet. I abruptly experienced the sensation of falling, like I'd left my organs behind as I dropped.

Then I was in my body again with a jolt, standing at the pillar, gripping my scythe like my life depended on it.

I… What!?

The veins in the pillar pulsed white, then Amber. Alternating gusts of hot and icy air crashing against me. Burning and freezing. Melting and searing. The Amber light left the pillar and shot up into the sky, riding the black beacon already there, then dissipated back to 'normal.'

My grip loosened, finally released from whatever force held it to the scythe. I dropped to a knee, blades of frozen grass cracking beneath.

What… the hell… was that?

Relinquishing the Will had given me some sort of enhancement. I stood, feeling a little more power in my thighs, a little more strength in my fingers as I balled them into fists.

So, this is what Calliope meant about other ways to gain strength besides increasing your rank.

It felt… Incredible.

And then it didn't.

As I turned to head away from the pillar, my vision swam. Like my body, Will, and mind were entirely disconnected from one another. The world twirled around me as I stumbled to my hands and knees.

I vomited into the grass.

The freezing air burned my throat as I inhaled. Then, I vomited some more. Flecks of bile and spit instantly turned to ice on my lips.

Once my stomach was completely empty, I dry heaved on my hands and knees for an agonizing thirty seconds.

Hands gripped under my armpits and helped me to my feet.

“It’s alright,” Calliope whispered as she guided me away from the pillar. "I've got you."

She leaned me against the back of the tavern. Nuzzle sat on my knee wagging his tail, eyes shining bright, oblivious, and licked the tears and spittle from my face as they thawed. I allowed my head to fall back and clunk against the wall. My breathing slowly settled, eyelids closed. I could still sense my orientation off, spinning despite my seated position, but it gradually steadied.

I felt Calliope sit next to me. “Sorry,” she said, her tone genuine. “I didn’t remember how disorienting it was the first time. Happens to everyone. I should've warned you.”

I looked into her eyes. One black and Amber, the other white and green with that golden twinkle.

"I can handle it," I said, though my stomach was still recovering.

“Advancing to the next rank is gonna be even worse, or so I hear,” Calliope said. “You have to actually bond a Will to your own. Not something I’m interested in doing. Ever.”

“What’s so bad about it?”

“I don’t know the details really, but even Jericho could only take it for so long. That’s why he stopped advancing, or so he says.”

I nodded. Guess I’ll have to ask him for more details.

"What affinity was the Will you relinquished?" Calliope asked.

"Uhh, Zeal I think it said. What are the other ones?"

"The Affinities are Aegis, Zeal, Glacial, Lithe, Vigor, Astral, Death, and Life," Calliope recited as if she'd memorized a textbook. "Most have a pair of associated stats they boost, such as Health, Volition, Strength, Defense, Agility, Stamina. It's really hard to pick and choose which Sins you want to battle to selectively increase stats, so don't worry about it too much. I mean some people do it. Volition is probably the most important for now so you can do more skills and not have to scramble to replenish constantly. You should've got a boost to Volition from a Zeal Will."

My attention shifted to the blue bar in my vision. It had completely replenished and increased in length a short amount.

So, the blue bar is Volition then, I surmised. It went down when I used my ability, but refilled from relinquishing the Will. The augmentation must have increased my Volition capacity. So it was like mana to fuel skills, which meant I couldn't keep using Aero Shift endlessly in a fight. It'd eventually run out until I harvested and relinquished a Will.

"Good thing too," I chuckled. "It was getting pretty low."

"Your Volition?" Calliope asked, concerned. "How low?"

"Unsure. Maybe had about 10% left."

She grimaced.

"What? Is that bad?"

"That's a lot closer than I thought. Why the hell were you using Aero Shift if it was that low?" Her tone became accusatory, which immediately put me on the defensive.

"To stay alive, obviously. I was being tossed around by a giant rhino beast, remember?"

She shook her head. "Idiot… Didn't Jericho tell you?"

"Tell me what?"

Calliope put her hand to her face and sighed. "Fucking Jericho… I wouldn't have phased you back to Vitria if I'd known."

"What the hell are you talking about?"

"Zephyr," she said, using the name she'd helped give me. "If your Volition hits zero… you die. Like. For real die. Not pop back to Vitria for a visit."

I stared at the circle of frozen grass before me. The words started to sink in. Fuck… I was that close to dying. One or two more Aero Shifts would've been the end.

"Volition fuels our Wills," Calliope explained. I forced myself to listen as I dealt with the existential crisis of almost-death. "Without Volition the Will seals up and erodes. Using skills costs Volition. As does transitioning to Vitria and back, whether by intentional or… unintentional means. And then it just slowly drains over time too, the price of you consciousness being present in Penumbra. Relinquishing Wills replenishes it. That's why we have to keep killing these bastards."

"Well, it's a good thing we didn't dilly-dally on the way back then I guess."

"Heh…" Calliope chuckled softly. "Yeah. Good thing."

Confronting your own mortality was exhausting work. Then again, I'd already thought I was going to die several times today. What's one more?

At least there was one thing I knew now to keep me going.

I can go home.
