I was on my way back to Louis' pizzeria when the world lurched. One blink later, I found myself standing atop the observatory tower in the Sunless Reach.

Right... I had banked a return wish for an hour before dusk.

Since I was already here, I might as well make use of the time. I reached into my Bag of Holding and pulled out the Campfire Aura hose I'd bought earlier that afternoon.

Following the demonstration Shina had given me, I wrapped it around my chaise lounge and connected the ends. The cores embedded within the braided cord shimmered faintly before an invisible bubble of warmth settled around the chair.

Immediately, the chill mountain breeze vanished.

"Oh... that's nice."

The temperature wasn't hot enough to make me sweat, nor cool enough to leave me wanting a blanket. It simply felt... comfortable. Like sitting beside a fireplace without the smoke, sparks, or the need to constantly feed it wood.

Worth every silver.

I stretched out on the chair, planted my spear beside me, and began recharging its Star mana.

My senses drifted toward the heavens as I patiently waited for shooting stars to streak across the sky.

After nearly thirty minutes, both my spear and I were fully recharged, and as another bright streak crossed the heavens, I made a wish.

"I wish to teleport beside Louis."

The night sky vanished, and darkness greeted me.

For a brief moment, I thought I'd miscalculated the destination before my eyes adjusted.

I was standing inside the kitchen of Louis' pizzeria.

The ovens were cold, the dining room empty, and the only light came from the far windows near the front door.

Louis and Silnar were, rather enthusiastically, occupied with each other. Louis was practically glued to her fiancé, standing on her tiptoes as she pulled him down into a passionate kiss. Both of her arms were wrapped around his neck while Silnar held her tightly around the waist.

Unlike my freelancer teammates, who had developed enough aura control to notice the disturbance caused by one of my teleports, the two civilians remained blissfully unaware that I'd suddenly appeared in the middle of the room.

When one of Louis' legs slowly lifted, beginning to hook itself around Silnar's, I decided it was time to intervene.

"Down, girl!"

Both of them jumped apart. Louis nearly stumbled backward, while Silnar looked ready to fight whoever had somehow sneaked into the restaurant.

Their expressions shifted to embarrassment the moment they recognized me.

"Alice, since when were you here?" Louis squeaked.

"I just teleported in," I replied, narrowing my eyes at her. "What exactly was that about?"

Louis let out a defeated groan and buried her face in both hands.

"I've been having trouble holding back lately." Her voice came out muffled through her fingers. "Ever since we officially became fiancés..."

She peeked at me through the gaps between her fingers.

"We really need to move up the wedding date. Otherwise I might... do something I'll regret."

I slowly turned toward Silnar.

"And you."

The poor man visibly shrank, despite being taller and broader than me.

"I know she's your fiancée, and I know you love her. But you also know she'll have to wait until marriage, right?" I said in my best chastising voice. "Think of it this way. Would you shrink half of her Dream Sanctuary just because you couldn't be patient?"

"She might hate you for doing that."

The boy absolutely recoiled at my words.

"Don't scold him," Louis said, sounding even more embarrassed as she rubbed the back of her neck.

"I'm the one who keeps jumping on him whenever we're alone," she admitted. "He's actually had to stop me multiple times..."

"This is why I'm not fit to be a cleric..." She sighed dramatically.

I raised an eyebrow. I figured there was a reason for that. She had been defensive about it when I asked on the first day we met.

"I have always been too interested in boys," she admitted. "It started when I was twelve. I used to sneak into the Freelancer Guild just to watch the muscular men spar."

That... actually explained a lot. She did have a wandering eye. I think I noticed her checking out Yon when she met him.

"Now that I have a hunk of a man who is all mine," she said, hugging Silnar's arm, "I'm having trouble holding back."

My eyes drifted toward Silnar. The cook had shoulders broader than Edmund the knight's and arms that looked as though they belonged to a blacksmith rather than someone who made pastries for a living.

Had he noticed that Louis liked muscular men and started training just to catch her attention?

That was... honestly rather sweet. All those years of effort just so she'd look his way.

"From now on," I declared, pointing a finger at both of them, "you are not allowed to be alone together without a chaperone."

Louis let out a tiny whine.

"I've seen what happens when a Mythic person loses their class," I continued. "I'm not letting that happen to you, Louis."

"If moving the wedding forward is a money problem, I'll help pay for it," I added. "You are far too close to the finish line to do something reckless now."

I walked between the two of them toward Nina's workshop, crossing the Portal Hub to travel quickly from the Guild District to the Merchant District.

The streets had begun to quiet as evening approached. Merchants were packing away their stalls, apprentices swept storefronts, and the smell of fresh bread drifted from a nearby bakery. It was strange how quickly Hano had started feeling familiar.

The moment we stepped into Nina's workshop, the Dreamer nearly tackled me.

"Alice! Come here and help me test it!"

She practically dragged me across the room before I even had time to greet her.

On the workbench rested two paper cones. Each cone was tightly wrapped with coiled copper wire, the seams sealed with a pale coating. Both ends of the wire extended in long parallel lines until they reached the other cone, where they coiled once more and completed the circuit.

Nina thrust one into my hands while keeping the other for herself.

"I finished it!"

I turned the cone over curiously.

Spirals of copper reinforced the paper, and the entire wire seemed to have been carefully insulated.

I rubbed the smooth coating with my thumb.

"Is that wax?"

"Yes," Nina replied proudly.

"Brilliant." I nodded approvingly. "It'll be more fragile than rubber, but it should work well."

"Rubber is too expensive," she said matter-of-factly. "Maybe in the final project I'll use it."

"Fair enough."

I stepped to the opposite side of the room before lifting the cone toward my ear.

"Okay, you speak first," I shouted.

Nina placed her mouth close to the other cone.

"Alice..." Her voice emerged from the paper cone in my hand. "...you are my Muse."

My eyes widened.

"It worked!" I couldn't help grinning. "The sound definitely came from the cone."

I looked down the length of the copper wire stretched across the workshop.

"It's a little quiet," I admitted. "I suppose you still haven't figured out amplification."

"Not yet." She shook her head. "I wanted to recreate what you showed me in the video before I started improving it."

That... honestly wasn't a bad research philosophy.

Reproduce the experiment first. Then you can consider innovating.

"My turn!" she declared.

She eagerly swapped the cone to her ear.

I leaned toward mine.

"Nina..." I paused dramatically. "...you're a genius."

The reaction was immediate. Her wings puffed out, and they actually twitched in what was almost a shiver.

"It worked!" she squealed. "Thank you, my Muse!"

Out of the corner of my eye, I noticed Silnar quietly nudging Louis.

He leaned over and whispered something into her ear.

"You think so?" Louis whispered back.

"Can't you see it?" Silnar replied.

Louis frowned thoughtfully.

"I'm Holy. I have blind spots when it comes to... that sort of dynamic."

She glanced between Nina and me again.

"But now that you've pointed it out..." Her eyes slowly widened. "I can definitely see it."

"What are you two whispering about?" I called across the workshop.

Both of them looked guilty.

"Nothing!" they answered so perfectly in unison that I didn't believe them for even a second.

"Sure." I narrowed my eyes suspiciously.

We spent another fifteen minutes discussing Nina's newest invention. Without any enchantments whatsoever, she'd recreated the basic principle of a telephone.

The current prototype was little more than two paper speakers connected by a stretched copper wire, but the concept itself was sound.

"So..." I said. "If we could somehow amplify the vibrations, we could make the wire much longer. Then we'd have a communication device we call a telephone."

"I see it!" Nina nodded enthusiastically. "But for a longer wire, I might need insulation that's better than wax and cheaper than rubber."

By the time the discussion finally wound down, the blue sun had almost disappeared beyond the rooftops.

"Okay," Nina said happily. "Let's head up to my nest."

We climbed the single flight of stairs leading to her bedroom.

As always, we left our shoes in a basket by the door.

Nina stepped over the pile of softness she called a room. Despite technically being a single bed, there was enough room for ten people to sleep comfortably and enough pillows to bury a goblin horde.

Nina crouched beside a small cabinet and pulled out a vial filled with orange liquid.

"You are a Dreamer, aren't you?" she asked Silnar. "You won't need this."

"Half Dreamer," he answered. "But yes. I can probably reach your sanctuary on my own."

"Good." She handed the potion to Louis instead. "Drink."

"What exactly is this?" Louis accepted it cautiously.

"A Potion of Paradoxical Dreams," Nina explained. "It forces you into a dream state that's easy for me to find and pull into my sanctuary."

Louis frowned at the bottle.

"Will the dream be scary?"

"No." Nina tilted her head. She thought for a moment before giving an example.

"It is more confusing than frightening. Like dreaming you're a frog..." She paused. "And living a perfectly happy frog life."

Louis stared at her, horrified. Despite her obvious reservations, she uncorked the bottle and drank it in one gulp.

"Good." Nina clapped her hands. "Now lie down, both of you."

Both Louis and Silnar settled onto the mountain of blankets.

I immediately narrowed my eyes. The two of them instinctively scooted closer together.

Absolutely not.

I grabbed several nearby pillows and began constructing a barrier between them.

"No funny business."

Louis turned bright red.

Silnar merely chuckled, having already accepted my self-imposed role as chaperone.

Nina ignored the entire exchange and began to gently shake her wings.

Two feathers drifted free, floating lazily through the air before settling upon Louis' and Silnar's foreheads.

The moment they touched, both closed their eyes. Within seconds, they were asleep.

"Our turn," Nina whispered.

We lay down, and I instinctively crawled beneath her wings. They were soft, and they smelled nice and comforting. Before I even had time to think about anything...

Darkness claimed me.

I found myself walking across my old university campus.

Faceless students drifted between buildings with backpacks slung over their shoulders, coffee cups in hand, laughing about assignments and complaining about deadlines. The familiar brick paths, the rows of maple trees, and even the faint smell of roasted coffee coming from the student café all felt so vivid that I forgot I had ever left Earth.

I spotted Zuri sitting at one of the outdoor tables with two thick medical textbooks spread before her. Dark circles hung beneath her large circular glasses as she scribbled frantic notes into the margins.

"I swear they expect us to memorize everything," she groaned without even looking up. "If I fail these licensing exams, I am starting an OnlyFans."

"You've been saying that since first year," I said with a grin. "And yet you always end up at the top of your class."

She tapped the thick stack of books.

"That doesn't change the fact that this is excessive."

"That's why I steered clear of medicine despite my mother's constant nagging," I laughed, drinking coffee from a paper cup I didn't remember picking up.

The scene shifted so naturally that I barely noticed it.

The university buildings dissolved into another familiar corner of campus, where a young man sat on the edge of a stone planter, his face buried in one hand.

"I don't even know what to say anymore," he sighed. "Jenny joined the military."

This felt more like a memory than a dream as I watched my archaeologist friend Mark going through a rough time after his girlfriend enlisted.

"I understand why she did it," he sighed. "She wants to help people, and she's always dreamed of being part of the UN peacekeepers." He rubbed his face with both hands. "But joining the military doesn't even guarantee that. What if she moves away from me only to be miserable?"

"Did she break up with you?" I asked.

"No. We're trying long distance."

"You'll make things work."

"I hope so."

The dream rippled.

The campus melted away as though someone had poured watercolor across the entire world. Concrete paths became rolling hills, buildings crumbled into drifting pollen, and within seconds I found myself standing in the middle of an endless green field blooming with yellow wood sorrel flowers stretching all the way to the horizon.

Mark stood several dozen meters away.

Except... he wasn't wearing jeans anymore.

Leather armor covered his chest, a weathered spear rested in his hands, and his expression held none of the uncertainty I'd known from university.

Across from him crouched a monster straight out of mythology.

It had the body of a lion with a jet-black mane like an Atlas lion, leathery bat wings folded against its back, and a scorpion's tail swaying lazily behind it. Every instinct I possessed screamed that the creature was a manticore, though one slightly different from the versions depicted in medieval artwork.

The beast lunged.

Mark reacted instantly.

He rolled beneath a swipe of its claws, twisted around the snapping jaws, then narrowly avoided the venomous tail as it slammed into the ground where he'd stood a heartbeat earlier. His footwork wasn't perfect, at least compared to Edmond or Katar, but it was practiced enough that he clearly wasn't a complete beginner.

To my astonishment, he actually landed a hit. The spear drove into the creature's shoulder with enough force to bury half the blade inside.

Then everything went wrong.

The weapon lodged in the thick shoulder muscles. Mark tried to yank it free, but the monster was already upon him.

Its jaws closed around his torso, and there was a wet crunch as blood sprayed across the flowers.

I froze, my eyes wide in horror.

The world dissolved again, and a heartbeat later the field returned.

Mark stood at the starting position once more, spear already in hand, as though nothing had happened.

He charged. He fought. He died.

The cycle repeated over and over before my horrified eyes. Sometimes he survived longer. Sometimes he managed another wound. Once, he even severed the creature's tail before it tore his arm off and ripped out his throat anyway.

Every failure simply reset the dream.

"What is this?"

I turned to find Nina standing beside me, staring at the impossible battlefield with wide eyes.

"Nina? What are you doing here?"

"I couldn't find your sanctuary," she said, still watching the fight. "The door never showed up, so I came to find you... I didn't expect you to be Dream Hopping on your own."

"Dream Hopping?" I frowned. "I can do that?"

She looked at me as though I'd asked if the sky was blue.

"What did you think you were doing every time you visited my sanctuary?"

"I thought that was a Dreamer thing," I said. "I'm pretty sure Mark isn't a Dreamer. Well, no. He's probably as much of a Dreamer as I am."

"It is easier for Dreamers," she corrected, "but everyone's dreams can be visited. Some are simply much harder to reach than others."

She studied Mark in silence for a few moments before tilting her head.

"You must have been very close to him," she said hesitantly. "That's why you managed to accidentally find your way here."

"To Mark?"

She nodded. She looked at him, then back at me, with an expression I couldn't quite read.

"Was he your boyfriend?"

"Not a chance." I couldn't help snorting.

"No?"

"I was just as good friends with his girlfriend as I was with him." I watched Mark duck beneath another claw before taking a venomous stinger through the shoulder. "At least... before she enrolled in the military and we gradually lost contact."

"Oh."

She looked strangely pleased by that answer.

She watched another brutal death play out.

"This dream is strange."

"You're telling me."

"No." Her expression became serious. "I mean genuinely strange."

She pointed toward the battlefield.

"It feels... deliberate."

I frowned.

"What do you mean?"

"Ordinary dreams don't repeat with this much structure. This is closer to a Dream Sanctuary." She hesitated before quietly adding, "I think he can actually feel the pain."

"That's impossible. There isn't magic like this on Earth."

She gave me a skeptical look.

"You were able to leave Earth, weren't you?"

The words struck harder than I expected.

I slowly turned back toward Mark. He was a worshipper of Hestia. Not only that, he was descended from a lineage of temple priests who had kept the old faith alive in secret despite the rise of Christianity in Europe.

I'd met Hecate once. Or at least, I thought I had. It could have been just a dream.

Still, if one Greek goddess existed... then why not another?

A cold knot formed in my stomach as Mark died yet again, only to stand back up moments later with another spear already in his hands.

"What if..." I whispered. "What if he was in danger?"

Nina didn't answer at first.

She simply watched the endless cycle with the same troubled expression I was beginning to feel.

"He should be fine,” she finally murmured. “Though, that is one brutal training dream,"

"What?"

"You can find similar dreams in the Great Dream. They sharpen your instincts without harming your real body."

She watched another cycle begin.

"But somehow..." She frowned. "I feel like this one is stronger."

I nodded slowly.

"Yeah..." There was something familiar about it.

Something.

"It feels..." I searched for the word. "Mythic. Like the aura around Saint Sarah's corpse."

Though I couldn't tell whether this aura was a blessing or a curse.

Mark rose once again and faced the monster without a hint of hesitation.

"We should go." Nina gently tugged on my sleeve. "Louis and Silnar are waiting."

Then she tilted her head.

"And you're also bleeding Inspiration."

I instinctively reached for my phone and checked the battery indicator.

It was at fifty percent.

"This will take me a few days to fill up," I winced.

"Or one new experience," Nina said with a smile.

"Okay." I nodded. "Let's go."

I glanced back at Mark one last time. My former classmate, who used to share his class notes with me, was fighting toe to toe with a creature of myth. I wondered if our positions were reversed and he got to see me fight; would he be as surprised as I am?

“Goddess Hestia,” I lowered my head in prayer just in case, “if you can hear me, please take good care of him."

“And tell him I am doing fine,” I added as an afterthought.

I felt a chunk of Inspiration be consumed.

“We better leave now before I end up with another case of dreamer crash”

I grabbed Nina's hand, and the field of yellow flowers dissolved into countless drifting fragments of light.

A heartbeat later, we stood once again within her floating sanctuary, surrounded by the familiar islands suspended beneath an endless blue sky.
