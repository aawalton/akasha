Ferris and Jade approached the walls of the fort, light still blazing from each of its four corner towers. He looked up at the oath statue, hanging on a hook suspended from a frame above the center of the fort. It was cracked and beat up, white chains hanging down beneath it. There were no demons visible, so they carefully climbed up one of the many chains hanging on the side of the fort.

Carefully lifting himself and Jade onto the walkway atop the wall, Ferris looked inside. The last he’d seen inside the fort had been in the midst of battle. Ferris had jumped down into the courtyard at the lanky chain demon, holding it down while Jade killed it, only for them both to be surrounded and killed by four fat chain demons.

Ferris was shocked to see the lanky demon upright beneath the oath statue. He quickly pulled up the bone spear, preparing for a fight… but then realized it was just a headless corpse.

The white chains hanging down from the oath statue had latched onto the corpse, and hoisted its headless body up in a macabre display beneath it.

More chains had grown from the oath statue: they reached and dug into the floor, stretched out along the ground, and wrapped around the base of the frame holding it. What had been a prison was being converted into the statues’ foundation, although it still hung suspended on its hook.

No living demons were present inside the fort, either scared off by the displayed corpse, wary of the oath statue, or missing for some other reason. The corpse was a surprise, as anything left unattended away from Ferris’ Heartfire had always disappeared. Looking around, Ferris saw no sight of his own body or the weapons he had dropped on his death.

Ferris stood atop the wall for a few minutes, simply taking in the sight. Jade wandered around nearby, but stayed relatively close at his request.

Part of him was being careful, looking for a trap. This is where the lanky chain demon had surrounded him, cutting off his retreat, after all.

But a larger part of him just wanted to observe. To think. The oath statue—which looked exactly like him—hanging from a hook. The statue had him dramatically posed, reaching up for a severed leg. White chains bound his form, the leg, and then fell down to wrap around the lanky chain demon’s corpse.

This, Ferris eventually thought, this reminds me of Assimilate Object.

The information from Assimilate Object’s fruit specified to build a frame around the ability seed. The frame would hold ropes, which would form a connection between the ability’s base and a spiritual construct representing the artificial organ. Pulling on those ropes in the soul would manifest as manipulation of the object in the real world.

He looked at the fort surrounding him. It was sort of like the frame the ability required, although much sturdier.

The statue in the middle, wrapped in chains, was like the artificial organ the ability was supposed to assimilate.

I wish this was all in the slimespace, he thought, then I could develop the fort into Assimilate Object.

He sighed. He had no idea how to break the fort down and move it, or how long it would take. Months? Years?

It would be better to start simpler. Build something he had the means to accomplish. Improve it over time.

Yet, something about the concept of the fort around him… felt right. He liked the idea, like it supported what Ferris wanted from the ability.

Ferris had been wondering if Assimilate Object could hold against an attack, but maybe that was the wrong way to think about it. Instead of asking if the ability was strong, he needed to be looking for and testing ways to make the ability strong.

The fort’s walls were a solid structure to bear the weight of an attack. Ferris wanted Assimilate Object to hold as a shield protecting Jade’s core from the world. The simple frame from the fruit’s instructions would support moving the object, but he could do better for stability. Building the ability as a fortress in the soul could turn it into Jade’s bulwark against all enemies.

It felt… right to construct heavy stone walls instead of a simple frame, but that was only half of his inspiration. The oath statue hanging in the middle of the fort showed Ferris, dramatically reaching for a severed leg just out of reach above him. Chains wrapped around his stone body and the leg, representing the oath he had allowed to bind him… and why.

Ever since the statue had appeared, it had put out chains. It attempted to dig deep and bind itself to his soul. Ferris had been warned that if he broke his oath, the statue would cause a backlash in his cultivation. On the flip side, while he kept his word, it could be a source of strength.

While Ferris didn’t intend to break his oath or the statue, he had resolved to control how and where it dug into him. He didn’t like the idea that the oath, or the bond to Jade, could control him.

Assimilate Object, in essence, took an object and controlled it. Looking up at the oath statue, Ferris felt there was a symmetry there. The statue was making chains to control Ferris, like Assimilate Object’s ropes would control an artificial organ. What would happen if he fed the white chains from the oath statue into Assimilate Object’s artificial organ?

Would the two concepts support each other, or fight?

It was exactly what he wanted: a way to use the statue—and his retainer contract to Hollowfield—as a source of strength. Each day Ferris worked for the clan would strengthen the statue, and thus himself.

Even better, the white chains wouldn’t be digging directly into Ferris’ soulspace. Redirecting them into an artificial organ felt… safer.

There was at least one downside. Assimilate Object was designed to move and manipulate what it held. It was a representation of controlling the organ in the real world. If Ferris bound that construct in the oath statue’s white chains, would that hamper the ability’s manipulation?

Additionally, it tied the oath statue to the ability, for better or worse. If one day Ferris broke his oath, the backlash could also break the ability.

There was also a danger in modifying the ability’s construction. The changes felt right, which mattered in the soul, but Ferris had been advised to build abilities to work as intended first. Then, experiment from a working basis.

Although… there wasn’t any reason Ferris had to do everything from the start. He could build Assimilate Object from the instructions, test it, get it working, and then add improvements on top. It wasn’t as exciting as immediately going for all the extra features, but it would be smarter.

It was a plan, though. Ferris had been on the edge between focusing resources on Grow, Assimilate Object, or waiting. Now, with the potential to both solve and benefit from the oath statue, he decided to prioritize Assimilate Object—at least until a new option appeared or his situation changed.

Excited and inspired by the ideas, he still needed to secure the area. Ferris and Jade explored the fort, although they didn’t find any demons lurking about. There were no internal rooms, just four walls, four towers, an entrance, and a whole lot of chains. The chains covered each wall and hung between the towers. Examining them, they appeared to be larger versions of the chains wrapping around the demons. Ferris hoped to use them in qi crafting like the demon corpses… once he figured out how to transport them all back to his Heartfire.

If he could use the chains… could he also use the stone? The slabs that made up the walls were too big to move. Maybe I could break them down to rubble, then use them to create something new? Is the stone worth anything, or are there easier ways to get similar material?

It would be a lot of work, but he was getting better with a pickaxe from the spirit gem refinement. The stone rubble would be even harder to transport than the chains, it made him miss the magical bag from the refinement device. Collecting the stone was at least an option, if he needed it.

Speaking of magical, Ferris was excited to finally get a close look at the towers. Ever since discovering the fort, they had blazed with a bright light. He climbed up the side of one and looked over the top. There was a large stone basin, with a bonfire blazing inside. The fire burned brightly enough that he had trouble looking directly at it, but after his eyes adjusted a bit, he could see inside. The sides of the basin were blackened, with a single large rock glowing white-hot at the bottom. It was like a large chunk of coal—the size of a small log—was fueling the bonfire.

These towers had been lit for days. Had the demons replaced the fuel, or had the same chunk been burning the entire time?

Ferris had no idea what it was, how to collect it, or what he could do with it… but he knew he wanted it. Should he try to douse it? Smother it in dirt? He tentatively held out a hand, feeling the heat. Could Jade eat it? Should Jade eat it? If he put it out, could he relight it? How could he learn more?

Regretfully, Ferris left the coal alone. Uncle Winton would be visiting soon, and he would likely know more.

Ferris heart tightened a bit at the thought of his uncle. So much had happened over the last two weeks, where would he even begin? He was bonded now, and could hardly imagine a life without the slime next to him. But Uncle Winton had never even met Jade. Neither had his mother, father, or sisters…

After climbing back down to the ground, Ferris inspected the lanky demon’s corpse. The head was missing, melted away when Jade killed it. It was wrapped in chains—both the white chains from the oath statue and its own. One chain in particular stood out. It was a large, pitch-black chain, more than twice as thick around as the rest. This was the one the demon had used to hit the statue from the ground, and had wrapped around Ferris’ neck before Jade saved him.

Looking up at the oath statue, Ferris saw himself carved from stone. The statue was beaten up and cracked, but was otherwise intact. He needed some kind of cart to get it back to the Heartfire.

The white chains had grown down, wrapped around the demon, and then dug into the ground. It had only been four days since the demon had died, so it shouldn’t be hard to cut them yet.

Ferris didn’t have his axe, but he did have Jade. The slime was happy to eat through the white chains, separating them from both the ground and releasing the demon corpse to fall down. Like before, the oath statue’s white chains faded away like mist after losing connection to the statue.

Once the chains were cut back, Ferris was left with the unpleasant task of carrying the tall demon’s corpse back to his Heartfire.

The demon was taller than its fat cousins, but thankfully weighed about the same. The oversized black chain, however, doubled the weight Ferris had to carry. He wasn’t willing to leave it behind, as he feared it would be stolen now that it was cut free from the white chains of the oath statue. He was able to bear the weight, but it was a struggle. Returning took three times as long as it should have.

After finally dropping his prize at the base of his Heartfire, Ferris and Jade left the soulspace. It was late, and Ferris had stayed up once again, but he didn’t regret it. He’d finally cleared the fort, and had a plan for what to do next. With a smile, he quickly fell asleep.
