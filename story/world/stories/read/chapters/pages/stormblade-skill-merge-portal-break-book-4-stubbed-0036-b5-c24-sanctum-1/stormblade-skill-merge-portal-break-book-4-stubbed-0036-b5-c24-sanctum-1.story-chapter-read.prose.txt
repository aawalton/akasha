The portal was a trap.

It would obviously have been one even without the existence of trap portals—and with the revelation, after Yalerox’s world, that Paragon portals could be trapped, too. But even without that, the placement, the intentionality of it, screamed trap.

My every instinct hammered at my being, urging me to enter it. Step into the portal. Cut down the monsters inside. Bring a storm of wrath and fury to bear on the Placid King, right through his front door. If this was truly a gate to his world, it’d be easy to walk through. The Storm’s Blade, my rank, and Ellen’s too, all gave me me a shocking amount of confidence.

But I’d also seen the Placid King’s tendrils squeeze an S-Rank boss to death.

Ellen watched me, eyes intense. “We’re not going in there, right?”

“No. That’s bait for us. We can’t beat whatever’s on the other side—not in its home territory. Let’s get out of here.” I waved my hand at the Desert Wind building, and the two of us retreated into its scorched, unstable facade.

Then I turned, watching the parking structure.

I didn’t have to wait for long. The ground shook below us, and part of the wall separating the gym from the library collapsed nearby. I ignored it. Only one thing mattered.

The Placid King: SS-Plus Rank

He didn’t enter Earth. Not fully. A pair of massive tentacles pushed through the golden portal, thrashing around the parking structure as they searched for something to grab onto. Black, with glowing yellow-orange eyes, they did two passes of the space as I counted down from one hundred twenty. It took forty seconds before they withdrew and the portal closed behind them.

Ellen started to move, but I waited, hand around her wrist. I kept counting. After a good three minutes, I cleared my throat. “I think we’re okay. I’m not sure why the Placid King didn’t just come through. He’s powerful enough to overwhelm this world and break it altogether, but he spent only enough time to…”

“Kade, pay attention,” Ellen said.

Then I looked around. The Desert Wind building had continued to collapse, the damage from my fight with the Placid King’s assassin and his temporary presence on Earth too much for it to withstand. All around it, buildings sported brand new cracks—vertical ones—and some had even started to physically split into pieces. Even the 303 Wall wasn’t intact; sunlight poured through the new gaps in the once-imposing structure.

Only forty seconds of the SS-Plus-Ranked Paragon had done this?

I shook my head to clear it. Whatever the reason, the Placid King hadn’t stayed on Earth. He’d left—and he’d left knowing that I was close to his trap. That meant something, but I wasn’t sure what.

All I knew was that Ellen and I couldn’t stay here. It was far too dangerous—for the world, and for everyone we cared about.

I pointed into the wreckage of our guild’s headquarters, where a portal was still open—a portal shrouded in green-black lightning. The other portal, which would normally lead to the Crone’s Ghostmarket, was gone. I ignored that as Ellen nodded. “I’m right there with you,” she said.

It was time to see what Tanat had cooked up.

Tanat looked somewhere beyond exhausted, but not quite comatose.

The swordswoman sat curled up on the Black Tower’s throne—though I supposed the Black Tower was the wrong name for what she’d created. It was hardly a proper tower anymore; every scrap of the Dark Citadel world’s brick had been pulled off of it and been stacked into pillars a good twenty to thirty feet high and thicker than I was tall. Atop every pillar sat a spike of portal metal, each reaching a uniform fifty or so feet into the air.

Thunder boomed around us, and electricity poured into the lightning farm Tanat had built, only to feed back upward through the tower at its center in a self-feeding loop.

“Tanat, you madwoman,” I said.

Her eyes cracked open. They were bloodshot—even through the blackness, I could tell that much—and narrow, but when she rubbed them, it was almost cute. Ellen snorted, and the pair of jet-black eyes narrowed even more. “I have been hard at work.”

“I can see that. Is it ready?” I asked. There wasn’t time for messing around.

“Does it need to be?”

I nodded. “Yes. Now, if not sooner.”

Tanat’s eyes closed, and something shifted in the cage of portal metal all around us. The structure, which had been a single, dagger-like tower yesterday, clicked and screeched as metal ground on metal. The sounds went on and on, louder and louder, and I found myself wincing with every floor-shaking thud and crash.

“I am reconfiguring the Lightning Tower. It has been building slowly until now. That is the most sustainable way. If the storm builds too quickly, its structure will be unsupported, and it will collapse. Is it time, then?” Tanat asked.

“It is. The Placid King is making his move. He won’t stop at one failed assault,” Ellen said quickly. She shook her head. “We don’t have the Traynor-Overholz Cannons—the ones we’re supposed to deliver to the Crone or the ones to defend your world. Can we win this?”

Ellen raised a good point. I knew, without a doubt, that the combined power of Phoenix couldn’t defeat either the God of Thunder or the Placid King. But at the same time, I doubted either could conquer the entire city in two minutes. It’d be the sheer damage their presence caused that ended Earth’s resistance, not anything they actively did. And here, in my portal world, we had a few advantages. The biggest was that the world wouldn’t buckle under a single SS-Plus-Ranked Paragon’s weight.

All around us, the Eye of the Storm slowly began to open as I cleared my throat. “I think we can, but we’re going to need an absolutely outstanding battle plan.”

“Then we should get to work,” Tanat said. She unfolded her too-thin, too-long limbs and stood, and the three of us put our heads together. The Placid King would be coming for me. It was time to make him pay for that.

Jessie’s fist clenched around the keystone to the Ghostmarket’s portal as the truck Yasmin had hotwired rocked. The quakes had hit only a few hundred yards from the garage, but she wasn’t stupid; the last time Phoenix had faced quakes like these had been when the God of Thunder entered Earth with Ellen’s injured body. He’d only been present for a handful of seconds, but even that time had been plenty.

Her brother was facing off against a being just as powerful, and she needed to help—in whatever way she could.

But how?

She could…

Talk to the Governing Council? That was where they were headed, with Yasmin flooring it as quickly as the beater could go, Jeff and Jessie jammed into the cab, and the rest of the Desert Wind Guild’s low-ranking delvers packed into the bed like sardines in a can. The Governing Council would mobilize what delvers they could, but it had been less than a day since the so-called guild war had erupted and, just as quickly, fizzled out. They wouldn’t be able to rally enough delvers, quickly enough.

The Portal Tyrants were an option, too—but that’d only compound the problem. The Tyrant himself might be able to last a handful of seconds against the monster assaulting Phoenix, but that wouldn’t make a difference. Still, his castle was rumored to be the strongest of the guilds’ headquarters. If anywhere was safe, it’d be there.

But Jessie didn’t want ‘safe.’

Her guild had been attacked. Not Phoenix. Not just Kade. Her guild. The Desert Wind’s headquarters had been wiped off the map, and she had to do something about it.

She had only one play.

“Go to Peoria!” she yelled point-blank at Yasmin.

“Are you out of your god-damn mind? That’s the wrong way!” Yasmin shouted back. The truck’s windows were all busted out—and had been since the Stormbreak—so they had to do a lot of yelling. “I’m not turning this truck around until—“

The quaking stopped.

“God dammit,” Yasmin said ‘under her breath,’ but still loudly enough to be heard over the seventy mile an hour wind.

“Peoria! Now!”

Yasmin nodded. Fine. But if this gets us all killed, it’s on you!”

Jessie was pretty confident it wouldn’t get them all killed. In fact, it might be the only way to avoid Kade and Ellen’s deaths—and she’d do anything she could to keep her maniac of a brother alive, no matter what trouble he was getting into.

The Placid King didn’t give us much time.

But we used every second of it.

When his gold-and-midnight blue portal opened, it opened on the far side of Ellen’s garden, almost as far from the Lightning Tower as it was possible to get. The portal vomited out a swarm of S-Rank monsters, along with a tidal wave of cold, fetid-smelling water. Even from the far side of the portal world, the stench was…impressive. I was ready for it, though. This wasn’t my first time in a portal world being invaded by the Placid King’s minions.

And, unlike the Carlsbad Portal and the Workshop within it, the Lightning Tower and Void Garden was built to handle it.

Even so, watching the tidal wave bear down on the Lightning Tower, and feeling the ground shake below it, was nerve-wracking. I squeezed the Storm’s Blade in my grip, knuckles white. The wall of water passed over the rows of shadow hedges, hardly seeming to notice as it did. I wasn’t fooled, though. Ellen had planted those hedgerows deliberately, and they were doing their job.

By the time the wave reached the Lightning Tower, it was a fraction of its former self. A large fraction, to be clear—but even so, the individual hedges had broken the wave’s strength. Even better, it had scattered the S-Rank monsters it carried all through the maze.

“I guess it’s my turn, right?” Ellen asked.

“Yeah. Do your thing,” I said. “I’m heading out to protect as many lightning rods as I can. The longer they do their thing, the better.”

“I’ll try not to hit you too much.”

“Thanks. Love ya too.” I jumped from the Lightning Tower and took off, heading for the battle. Stormrunner carried me through the suddenly-humid skies, and thunder boomed around me.

And behind me, a wall of darkness grew over the flooded hedgerows as Ellen unleashed her Tides of Darkness aura.

She’d planned the Void Garden meticulously. Every hedge was arrayed in a series of concentric circles, with the Lightning Tower at its core—and as the Eye of the Storm slowly formed over its now-forked pinnacle, the constantly-flashing sky threw shadows across almost the entire portal world. Every one of those shadows joined with Ellen’s attack, only to be refreshed as the strobing light overhead cast a brand-new one.

The shadow tide washed over the deep water below me in an almost uncanny mirror of the Placid King’s initial assault. Ellen had used Tides of Darkness before, but now it had the strength of an S-Ranker behind it. Now, she could cast it safely, even at a truly massive scale.

It wasn’t as much force as the Placid King’s wave. But unlike his attack, which had felt methodical and emotionless, hers carried a weight with it.

Something roared in agony below me as the Tides of Darkness washed across it, and I took a look. An SS-Ranked monster. It would survive Ellen’s most powerful ability easily, and if it could do that, it’d be a serious threat.

I dove toward the four-armed, brass-suited monster—with just enough time to note that it reminded me of the Craftsman’s armies—and shifted stances to Thunderlord.

It was time to defend the Lightning Tower and wait for the Eye of the Storm to finish opening.

Mind Maiden Enolda had returned home as the Placid King’s assault began.

The Crone understood, even if she disapproved of the hunk of junk’s choice. The puppet was nothing if not loyal, and if another SS-Plus-Ranked Paragon was on the move, she’d feel it was her place to be by her mistress’s side.

Still…

“I told you to keep close to that girl, you pile of splinters,” the Crone said. She wasn’t angry. Far from it. Just annoyed. Ever since her conversation with Kade Noelstra, her eye had been on that lunatic of a swordsman.

He wasn’t as strong as he thought. The gulf between S-Rank and SS-Plus was almost impossible to even understand. The God of Thunder and Placid King were two of the three most powerful beings out there, and that Stormblade was a fresh S-Ranker.

No, he wasn’t as strong as he thought.

But that didn’t matter.

At this exact moment, from inside her tent near the center of the Ghostmarket, the Crone had exactly two weapons she could bring to bear. One of them would cost her an incredible amount of power—the equivalent of a dozen S-Rank boss cores—to even brandish, much less deploy. If she used it to stop the Placid King, it would clear the way for the God of Thunder to attempt his own ascension, and she’d be…not powerless, but weakened.

That was unacceptable. The crux of her problem was that both her most powerful rivals were attempting to ascend at close to the same time. She could easily stop one, but not both. Not easily. She needed her full power to do that.

Which left her with a single weapon.

“That Kade Noelstra had better know what he’s doing,” the Crone muttered.

“Oh, he does,” a voice said.

The Crone started. Her eyes locked on the girl in the soaked-through pajamas, and the handful of impressively low-ranked delvers standing around her. She pulled herself together in record time, but even so, she’d slipped. How had Jessie Gerald of all people sneaked up on her?

“Crone, I’m here to bargain for your assistance with the Placid King,” she said.

The Crone laughed, as much to cover her own surprise as for any other reason. “Oh? And where, pray tell, are my cannons?”

Jessie’s eyes brightened, and the Crone realized her mistake. She, somehow, had played right into the unawakened, unranked human’s hands.
