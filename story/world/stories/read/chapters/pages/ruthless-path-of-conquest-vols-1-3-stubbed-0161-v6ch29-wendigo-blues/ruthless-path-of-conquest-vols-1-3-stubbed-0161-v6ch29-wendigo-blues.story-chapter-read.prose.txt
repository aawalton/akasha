“Begone, foul demon!” shouted the Holy King, hurling some balls of light in Cara’s direction.

The Wendigo rolled her eyes and dodged, only for the energy attacks to follow her.

Well, at least that poses a little more of a challenge than he seemed to at first, she thought. Assuming those attacks can actually kill me if they connect.

This place was very low stakes, ever since the System announcer had stated that death here was not permanent. That was why Cara could afford to make a deal with someone she didn’t really know, like the Fisher Emperor, in exchange for competing in the melee suboptimally.

She sidestepped the balls of light again, noticed out of the corner of her eye that the Holy King she was fighting was actually directing them, and then fired ice projectiles out of her shoulders. Cara could guide her projectiles too.

The Holy King fired a dozen more balls of light that joined the cascade of energy attacks that seemed to be multiplying around Cara. She continued dodging, and the Holy King sprinted away from her ice spears in turn.

The two fighters were thrown into a game of repeatedly evading each other’s attacks, weaving around the trees and little random bits of elevated ground around them, and continuing to add more projectiles when they could, all while trying to keep an eye on the other to continue guiding their own attacks.

The Holy King broke first. He gave up on dodging in favor of securing a hit.

Cara saw it out of the corner of her eye when he paused and focused completely on her.

At almost the same moment that he stopped, the icicles that were chasing him stabbed him through his shoulders and thighs, drilling deep into his flesh and piercing all the way through to the bone, until the air was filled with the sounds of ice and bone grinding against each other.

The Holy King winced as this unfolded, but he retained his focus.

A second later, Cara realized she was completely surrounded. Somehow, dodging the last three balls of light had placed her in a position where she floated in a sort of midair minefield, dozens of orbs of energy hovering all around.

I can’t dodge this, she assessed. Just have to… minimize the damage.

She twisted and curled her body and threw herself down at a diagonal. Arms wrapped around her head, head tucked into her abdomen, she sacrificed her legs and back to the brunt of the attack. There was a silent explosion. The only cues that something was happening were the flash of blindingly bright light and the searing agony that ripped through her back and scorched her legs.

Fight through the pain, Cara thought. Get the enemy. Earn the reward.

She kept her body moving even as the explosion scorched her. A shadowy, conjured hand pushed off of the nearest solid surface—a tree or a mound of earth or something, she couldn’t see anything—and threw her headfirst into the dirt.

Cara lay there, still and silent, until the explosion stopped and the flesh on her back began to heal—painfully, but at a tangible speed. The Wendigo couldn’t feel her legs at all. She tried to kick out with them and didn’t notice any motion or even pain.

She pushed herself off of the ground and looked down at her lower body. The limbs were just gone. She still had thighs, but they ended abruptly in scorched stumps.

She poked at the meat just above the stumps, and it still twitched and felt sensation in response to her touch. She jabbed her finger hard into the stubby ends of the limbs that looked almost like they had been melted off with a blow torch. She felt nothing.

Fucking Wendigo body. I don’t even understand how it works.

She poured Mana into her hand and sharpened her claws, then hacked the stumps open until she felt the pleasant relief of pain, and her strange blue-gray blood flowed out in thick rivulets.

Only when she could see her flesh twitching and bleeding and at least trying to repair itself did Cara return her attention to her opponent.

The Holy King was quietly crying in pain. The icicle spears Cara had hurled his way were still drilling through him, and she could see they had pinned him by his shoulders and thighs to a stone cliff face, which they were trying, slowly but surely, to bore into.

A dark smile danced over the monster’s features.

It would be funny to just leave him there, but I did promise the Fisher Emperor to get this fool back to him. She thought of Mina. A part of the Wendigo wondered if the other woman would be pleased with her for helping James with his mysterious mission. It was something the Fisher Emperor had apparently been unable to do on his own and that he considered essential to their family’s safety.

Perhaps that would wash away some of Cara’s… less than savory actions in the Empress’s mind.

“Please…” the Holy King’s whining broke Cara’s train of thought.

“What is it?” she growled, pushing herself up and beginning to walk towards him, using her inhumanly long arms like a pair of legs.

“Please put me out of my misery, for God’s sake!” the Holy King exclaimed. “You won the fight, go find your next opponent, ahh!”

His plea turned to a cry of pain as Cara hit him over the head.

Not hard enough.

She struck him twice more before she correctly calibrated the amount of strength she should use to knock the man out without actually killing him. It had been some time since she had an intentionally nonlethal fight.

Then she hoisted him onto her shoulders. Her legs were growing back slowly, but she didn’t actually need them to get around. She could fly.

Cara took off, a big, toothy smile spreading across her face.

I come bearing gifts…

—

The Wendigo descended from out of the sky before the Taiwanese warriors had departed again. James was still visible, and he couldn’t hide that he was slightly impressed.

That guy had the strongest aura out of the group, he recalled, looking at the unconscious figure draped over Cara’s back. I wasn’t overly impressed with any of the Holy Kings… but she’s kind of a beast, huh?

“I brought one of the targets,” the Wendigo said as she came close to landing. She didn’t quite set down, as James noted her legs were mostly missing. The stumps slowly dripped a strangely colored fluid onto the ground that he guessed was her blood. Cara looked from side to side. “Guess I’m the first one?”

“Not quite,” said Zhang Chāo. He raised a hand halfway to his mouth as soon as the words were out, as if he had realized midbreath exactly what he’d just said—or rather, the implications of it.

“Well, where are the other Holy Kings?” she asked, twisting her head around as if she thought they might be hidden somewhere—or perhaps just trying to crack her neck.

“I destroyed them,” James said firmly, not looking at Zhang Chāo, very deliberately not glaring at him for this slight fuck-up.

“Destroyed? I killed something on the way to fighting this guy, and the body didn’t disappear…” James saw the moment when it clicked on Cara’s face and then in her body language. “Soul magic?”

He nodded.

“So, this was never about just getting you an advantage in the melee. Well, of course it wasn’t. I knew that much. Mina made it seem like a matter of life and death. Which it was, I guess.” She looked slightly amused. “For these guys, at least.” She jerked on the leg of the Holy King on her back, eliciting a tiny, pained cry.

“It’s life and death for me as well,” James said. “These people put out a hit on me, and they’re not above coming after my family either.”

“Now why would they go and do a thing like that?” Cara asked, her teeth all bared in a cynical grin. “Don’t bother telling me. It’s written all over your face that you’ve done something to them.”

James raised an eyebrow. My perfect control over my expressions is back. There’s no way she’s reading anything off of my face.

“Sounds like projection,” he said.

“I bet this is a matter of life and death for more than just you—and more than just the Holy Kings, too, eh?” There was that mocking, fake Canadian accent again. Or maybe she had simply consumed enough Canadians that talking that way came naturally to her. You were what you ate, after all. “I’d imagine that the Holy Kings and all their followers are coming after everyone who helps you fight them here, given that you’re destroying them permanently, not just targeting them in the competition to show them that you’d beat them outside of the melee or something.”

“If you’re worried that the Holy Kings could come after you for your involvement, you’re welcome to join my Empire,” James said. “I’ll of course be available to protect you either way, in keeping with our deal, but it’s always easier when the people we need to work with are close, in a connected territory.”

“You’d like that, wouldn’t you?” Cara scoffed.

“It seemed convenient enough for Count Aleph,” Han Jianguo ventured.

Cara stiffened, and her expression grew cold.

I need you guys to shut the hell up! James thought fiercely. She doesn’t need extra information! He again refrained from showing any displeasure with his expressions or body language, though. The alliance with the Taiwanese was still young enough, fragile enough, that it could be broken in a fit of ill temper.

“Listen, Mina chose you as someone we might be able to trust,” James said carefully. “It would be a shame to leave you outside of our circle when there’s a good reason for you to join us.”

“Mina did choose me,” Cara agreed. “And she chose you too. We have something in common. Great. You’re her husband. I’m the monster who didn’t eat her. Both trusted. But I didn’t choose you. I chose myself. Independence. Freedom. I will not serve. That’s not what I gave my heart and soul to achieve. I bet that’s not what Count Aleph wanted, either, but you roped him into your Empire anyway. Thanks partially to me. I regret ever listening to you.”

In her proud stance and wish for independence, she reminded James for a moment of Dean. He had to remind himself that this was, underneath her very human-sounding values, behind her attempt to seize the moral high ground, still a monstrous Wendigo who had slaughtered hundreds of people.

“Well, I’ll still keep my end of the deal, of course,” James said after a moment. “And it’s not like the Holy Kings are really likely to go after you up in Canada before they come for me anyway, if they survive today.”

“It’s quite something to turn a threat to your family into an opportunity to expand your own power base,” the Wendigo said in a tone of disapproval. She shook her head. “Our deal is off. I do not need to seek the protection of someone like you.”

She dumped the Holy King’s body to the ground, turned, and raced off.

James thought about giving chase—what if she told someone else what she knew about James’s plans?—but then thought better of it.

Anyone might have informed the Holy Kings of what was going on already. Silencing her would just be a waste of time. Her Race was supposed to be difficult to kill by conventional means, and he absolutely did not want to permanently kill her with Roscuro. Cara had helped him, after all. She hadn’t run off with the Holy King. She had left his body there. It was a freebie.

“She was right, in a way, about the way you’ve handled the situation,” Zhang Chāo said quietly.

James gave him a sideways look.

“I’ll have a word with him,” Zhang Chāo mouthed, as if reading James’s mind about Han Jianguo’s little interruption. “Sorry.”

James just nodded. “Is it true what they say about ‘crisis’ having the same character as ‘opportunity’ in your language?” he asked after a moment.

Zhang Chāo shook his head. “A Western distortion.”

“That’s a shame. It makes for such a pithy quote.”

“Most of our best sayings are rather awkward when translated into English, I’ve found,” Zhang Chāo said. “If you don’t need me or Jianguo, we’ll go now. More work to do, right? More Holy Kings to find. And maybe a conversation about discretion somewhere along the way…”
