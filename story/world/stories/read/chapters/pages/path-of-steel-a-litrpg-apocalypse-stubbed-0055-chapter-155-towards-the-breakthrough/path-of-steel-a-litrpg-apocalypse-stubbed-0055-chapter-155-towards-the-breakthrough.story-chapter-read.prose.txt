Gary sat cross-legged on the grass. No matter what came next, or what monsters lay in wait, the best response was always getting stronger.

An item resembling a light bulb floated over his head, as if he just had an idea. This was the Bulb of Englightenment—an invaluable treasure that Gary had gotten for ranking second in the tournament. It slightly increased his comprehension—his ability to understand vibrations. He hadn’t meditated much since then, but when he refocused on his vibration, the benefits would be felt over time.

Bulb of Enlightenment

This is the good stuff! Not only does it slightly increase your comprehension—a magnificent bonus—but it also makes you seem bright! Get it? Because it’s a light bulb.

Comprehension-enhancing treasures were beyond rare. Gary had already gotten dirty stares from a bunch of climbers, but he didn’t think they’d try anything.

Swiping his space ring—a motion he was finally used to—he removed a handful of dirt, very careful not to let the smallest grain slip. A faint glow permeated it, and it felt light, almost weightless.

This was Active Dirt—the astral resource which cost him as much as four beef boy mercenaries.

“Let’s see,” he muttered, spreading it on his lap. He took a deep breath and dove into meditation.

His body was a lattice of ever-moving energy. It flowed up and down, broke into streams and fused into rivers, accelerating with every beat of his heart. Tiny hints of astral energy swam in that river, so little they eluded his perception.

Gary looked deeper. Just outside the lattice of his body lay a heap of astral energy. It was dreamy, a sort of whimsical, fairy tale aura. It spoke of far-off lands, of novel creatures, of the vastness of space and dreams.

Bit by bit, Gary pushed it into his energy circulation. The dirt itself never moved—only its aura did, seeping into Gary’s body to flow along his bloodstream. He could feel himself growing stronger, slowly but measurably.

As the absorption neared its end, however, he noticed another change. A tug in his veins. The astral energy was integrated into his bloodstream, but it often disagreed with how the blood flowed. It bumped into walls and cut corners as if trying to carve new pathways through Gary’s body.

The astral disagreement in his blood was a slight sensation, but an annoying one. He knew it would become more pronounced as he absorbed even more astral energy. When his bloodstream was saturated, he’d let the astral rearrange his veins into a constellation matching the patterns of the starry sky, and then he’d gain the power to absorb energy directly from starlight. Only then could he be called a true student of the Starvein Immortal Body Manual.

Gary opened his eyes. The active dirt had saturated his blood by about three percent. As the coach had said, it was more efficient than the star grass. There was still a long way to go, but if he increased his merit point income and got the Star-Forged Steel, completing this stage might be closer than expected…

Even without the completion, however, he already felt stronger. He opened his status screen.

Gary Steel #00301, Level 25

Floor: 4

Clan: Heaven Reaching

Strength: 55

Speed: 54

Vitality: 106

Fortitude: 7

Wisdom: 5

Aura Volume: 25

Aura Control: 28

Vibrations: Black Lightning, Seed of Eternal Flesh (U)

Each of his stats had increased by two. It wasn’t a massive change, but then again, he’d only just gotten started.

Stats weren’t the only change, though. His blood felt livelier. As if dreams flowed in his veins, enhancing his every breath, step, and strike with the power of imagination. Or space destruction. Something like that.

Gary cracked his neck and looked around. The sun was slightly lower on the horizon—he must have spent an hour or so absorbing the astral energy. Since they had three days until the next round of missions, he closed his eyes and returned to meditation.

Gary had fought Core Formation opponents for the first time during this mission. The bear which struck him on the river, the high-level octopus, the chimera… This far-off realm was taking shape in his mind, and it was no longer the unattainable tyranny it once was. He could already match the weakest Core Formations in combat. How far could his own breakthrough be?

The thought filled Gary with anticipation. If I break through, my merits will multiply… Every Core Formation kill is at least a hundred points. I can get the Star-Forged Steel and advance my body cultivation, allowing me to kill stronger enemies and advance even faster…

First things first. I need to break through.

That wasn’t something he could do on a whim, and it wasn’t something he could do yet. But he was close. The constant training and battles of the tournament had pushed his aura control to rapid growth. He’d been Level 25 for a while. Now, his Aura Control stat had reached 28, close to the 30 limit that Master Colorful had set for Gary’s breakthrough. His aura field was seven wide—more than most Birth of Aura cultivators. Oracle Jay, who specialized in his aura field, had it at eight feet. Feng Shen had been at eight feet, too. The maximum was nine.

In fact, Gary was pretty sure Kyle had once mentioned he could perceive everything within nine feet of his body.

That was a pipe dream, however. Not because he lacked in talent—which he did—but because it took a lot of time.

Most cultivators stayed in the Birth of Aura realm for a few months to a year. In that time, they could reach six or seven feet width in their aura fields, maybe eight if they were lucky. That’s when progress slowed down. Pushing for nine feet had small benefits that were a waste of a cultivator’s golden years. The only people who did it were the scions of great families who started training early and showed potential—like Kyle. These people spent their entire childhood and teenage years in Birth of Aura, practicing their battle techniques, aura control, and the overall fundamentals of cultivation.

When they were finally of age to start climbing, they had foundations incomparably more solid than everyone else, and their expanded aura field would give small but meaningful advantages throughout their lives.

Gary wasn’t that kind of person. He’d started cultivating way later than most. A seven- or eight-foot aura field was more than enough to move on. In fact, the thirty points in aura control that Master Colorful had demanded was already a challenging threshold.

Shooing the thoughts away, Gary focused inward. He forgot about the bloodstream and saw himself as a mass of aura. It pulsed not with his heart, but with the singing spear, the cultivation manual Kyle had taught him at the Floor of Trials. Spikes followed by lulls, then a spike again.

When he’d first broken through, his body parts had been out of sync, their spikes neutralized by the neighboring lulls. Through hard work and constant effort, Gary had managed to synchronize his various body parts. The spikes now came together, amplifying each other, and the lulls made his aura withdraw as if almost vanishing. That was the main principle behind his widening aura field.

Not everything was in tune, however. Gary’s internal organs pulsed to their own tempo, conflicting with his skin and flesh, and the extremities of his body were also harder to control, since they were heavily influenced by the outside aura. Those were the details only child cultivators would bother fixing. Gary set to the final frontier separating him from his breakthrough—his bones.

Unlike his skin and flesh, bones were less “alive.” They were made of hard materials, producing a steadier, deeper vibration that served as the backbone of his entire body. That made them hard to adjust, but it also meant that, once he adjusted them, everything else would be standing on a solid foundation.

He’d focused on the nine songs before. This was the first time he faced his bones.

Stilling his breath, Gary deepened his meditation until the world around him disappeared. He floated alone in a void dimly lit by starlight. His vibration echoed in his head like drums. He could sense himself shaking, violently, intensely.

How do I not feel this constantly? he wondered.

In this violent vibration, like drums deep from the jungle, some notes were discordant. The deepest ones. The drums beat together, but those who should be leading the rest played a different tune—one that carried little order, little meaning. An undisciplined vibration which contradicted itself.

It rang, and Gary imposed a change on it. It resisted. It rang. He imposed himself again. Pulse after pulse, Gary met the bones’ vibration with his own, slowly carving it to his rhythm.

Strangely, he felt a slightly greater clarity than usual. This must have been the Bulb of Enlightenment at work—a boon that already filled Gary with glee.

Hours passed in the outside world. People ran around him, sparred, trained alone or in groups. The sun dipped below the horizon, moonlight showing those who trained hardest, until the sun rose again as new dawn. Everyone around him had changed by now, but Gary sat alone, still as a rock. His vibration leaked to his surroundings, a song of sudden ruin which gradually flattened the grass, leaving him in a seven-foot circle of black dirt.

Though many cultivated, few were as devoted as Gary. That drew attention. People pointed at him and whispered, while his beef boys proudly proclaimed he was their boss. The pro boxers stared with new eyes. And the coach, watching from the entrance of his gym, nodded slightly.

Gary himself didn’t know all this. He’d lost track of time, as he’d learned to do while practicing the spear with Kyle, or while training the songs. He only awoke when he sensed a disturbance around him. The first thing he noticed was that his bones were more in tune than before. His aura control had risen to 29, and his aura field was approaching seven and a half feet in width. Just by one day of meditation, he’d reached halfway to his breakthrough.

“Huh,” he said.

Soon, however, his attention was arrested by his surroundings. People discussed in excited tones, pointing at the gym. No beef boys trained on the grass. A crowd had formed at the gym entrance.

“Shit!” Gary said, bolting upright. He grabbed a random person. “Hey, excuse me! Did I miss the missions?”

“What? No, man, there’s still half a day,” his victim replied, shaking off Gary’s hand. Both were surprised when the man who turned around was the Lord of Pot. Gary hadn’t recognized him from the back, since his signature jives hadn’t regrown yet.

“It’s you,” Gary said.

“Yeah, fuck you too,” the Lord replied.

“What’s going on?”

“Why should I tell you?” the Lord said. A moment later, he shook his head. “Whatever, man. Feng Shen just beat the boxing champ.”

“Really?”

“Yeah. He’s really strong. He just announced he’s heading to Satan Gym to challenge Satan Jr.”

“Satan Jr.?”

“You know. The Gymnasium floor boss. For God’s sake, man, I know you’re a drafter, but look things up.”

“Satan Jr. is the floor boss?”

“Yeah, and you can challenge him if you have all belts. That’s impossible now though, so they let us challenge him with just one belt.”

Gary was lost for words. Quirk! he roared in his head.

Quirk: What’s up, buddy?

Gary: You want me to tickle the balls of the floor boss!

Quirk: Yeah?

Gary: That’s ridiculous! I’ll die!

Quirk: See, buddy, there’s your problem. The question mark is missing from your messages. That makes it hard to do my job, which is answering your questions.

Gary: How do you expect me to complete this quest??

Quirk: Quick learner! I’ll tell you exactly how. You get below him, raise your hand, and wiggle your fingers.

Gary: Quirk!

Quirk: Come on, buddy, I’m kidding. What are you so afraid of? It’s easier now that you’re allies, since he might not kill you. That Branch Elder you slapped across the face was much stronger than Satan Jr.

Gary: I had allies then.

Quirk: You have allies now. Get off my case, buddy, I’m busy.

Gary crossed his arms. “This is ridiculous,” he said.

“I agree. That belt doesn’t suit him at all,” the Lord of Pot said.

Gary heard the cheers and realized Feng Shen had just left the gym, uninjured, with a massive golden belt strapped around his waist. It read, “Boxing Champion, GBA.”

“GBA?” Gary said.

“Gymnasium Boxing Association,” the Lord explained. “I wonder if he can beat the floor boss.”

“Wait, he’s going now?”

“Duh. Why did you think there was a crowd?”

Gary’s eyes widened. He saw Feng Shen jog out of the grass field, followed by dozens of enthusiastic climbers and hyper-enthusiastic beef boys. Pocketing his Bulb, Gary joined the group. Satan was waiting.
