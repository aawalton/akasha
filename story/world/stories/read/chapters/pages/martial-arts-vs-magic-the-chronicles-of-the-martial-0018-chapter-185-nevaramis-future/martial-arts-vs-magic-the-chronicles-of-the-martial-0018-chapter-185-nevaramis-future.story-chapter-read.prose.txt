The Bifrost brought me home alone.

White-gold light peeled away from my body, leaving me standing in Nevaramis' primary teleportation chamber with dried blood on my clothes. I didn’t bear any wounds, for the fading three-colored flame on my forehead declared I was beyond mortal wounds. Even so, I had enough political trouble trailing me that Stratos' spatial filters had every right to resign.

“Iskandaar.”

The chamber was crowded.

Amelia stood near the front with her arms crossed and her face locked into that calm, professor-like expression she used whenever she was close to losing her temper. I found Ralian standing beside a medical stretcher where Lilian lay wrapped in a dozen different healing spells. Those weren’t really needed, but okay. Vargathrian loomed behind them, her silence pressing the chamber smaller.

Munera Obsidian was present too, dressed as if the day had involved afternoon tea rather than watching demigods die through Nevaramis' observation link. Nebula stood beside her, pale and quiet, with one hand resting on the back of Valeria's chair.

That last thing made me pause.

Valeria was sitting wrapped in a dark blanket, weak and managing to look offended by it. Her violet eyes found mine first, then moved to Lilian's stretcher.

"Is she going to be okay?"

Her voice was thin. I looked at the sleeping wolf.

"If we’re talking about pure physicality, Lilian is perhaps the strongest among us now. She’ll be fine."

Valeria's fingers loosened on the blanket. She looked away. Of course, she’d sacrificed her life for Lilian. She wanted to make sure the little wolf would be fine. Nebula saw it too. Her hand stayed on the chair, but her fingers never once looked ready to become claws. I saw conflict in Nebula’s expression.

Solara stood on the other side of Lilian's stretcher, her wings tucked in so hard they almost looked painful. Her face had that stiff, too-bright look I had seen once before, after the Phoenix Essence nearly ate her alive.

She had lost her old family. She had us now – me, Lilian, and the others – but today she thought she had watched this new one collapse right in front of her. It must have been really scary.

Lailah stood near Senna, bronze skin a little paler than usual. Her silver eyes moved over me, studying the blood and posture before anything else. She was checking the damage before deciding how to feel about it. I had always liked that about her.

Stratos waited at the center console, hands folded before her. Her golden eyes met mine.

"Welcome back, my liege,” she didn’t stop there. “I’d calculated today’s fight to be the end of your reign, but… Truly, it’s an exhilarating feeling to be wrong.”

I looked around the room. I needed a moment to gather my thoughts. Only then could I speak, "The Maharani left for Nagadipa with Shayura and her surviving people," I said before anyone could ask. "Bagha is alive, and Vanga is bleeding. But the Lucent Air, the Stillness of Aryavarta, is gone, at least for now. The Arcane King has invited me to Amravati in twenty-one days to hear her final decision."

Nobody spoke for a few seconds. A lot had happened.

Ralian stepped forward first. Her red eyes were dry, which made them worse. A screaming mother I knew how to stand in front of. This was the other kind.

"Iskandaar. I am glad you survived, but… you brought my daughter back half-dead."

“Unfortunately.” I didn't look away.

Her claws slid out. "Did you give her that technique?"

"It wasn’t me."

Her nostrils flared.

"But… haah, yes technically it was me. I’d fallen berserk."

That answer landed worse than a denial would have. I knew it. So did Ralian. On one hand, I felt a little irritated that she was confronting me like this, as if I’d hurt Lilian myself. On the other hand, I could distinctly read her emotional threads and so understood a mother’s irrational emotions.

"If she dies because of a technique carrying your shadow," Ralian said, "I will not care if you were berserk or not. Do you understand?!"

I just sighed.

"She chose to use it." Her voice tightened. "I saw that too. That makes it worse."

"It does."

Behind her, Vargathrian's ears twitched as she sighed. The pressure in the chamber eased by a fraction. “My daughter’s feeling a little emotional. I apologize for the commotion,” the Beast Hero said, moving her tail to pat Ralian’s head.

Ralian’s fingers curled, and she lowered her anger with visible effort.

I decided to look away from her, my eyes falling on Nebula. She did not approach me first. She was still looking at Valeria. “Is anything wrong, Nebula?” I asked.

She looked at me, paused, and then looked at Valeria. There was a brief silence. Then she said, "She bought her place at the table, I guess."

Valeria's fingers tightened around the blanket again. Nebula watched her. "Hey, don’t relax too much. Don’t mistake my words for trust."

"Oh, okay..."

Nebula looked flustered at the reply. "No, not like that. I meant… The Demon King may have contingencies, yes? You were his servant for a century, after all. Oaths, hooks, old commands, or like hidden triggers. I will check all of them."

Valeria looked up at her. A terrible smile spread across her lips, “Awh. I thought you were just hard to get along with, but now… Do you have a crush on me or something?”

Nebula froze, and her brows tightened immediately. The aura around her shifted. "You shifty demoness, if I find something weird hidden in you, don’t blame me for being ruthless."

Valeria giggled, covering her mouth with a hand, as the crowd, wearing different expressions, watched. It was obvious she found Nebula adorable. "Alright."

That was it. A vampire deciding the demon spy had become a problem to manage rather than a rat to kill. In this flying city, that counted as something.

A groan came from the stretcher.

Every head turned.

Lilian's nose twitched first. Then her eyes opened, red and irritated, and she stared at the ceiling like it had insulted her. There was a pause, then her head snapped to look around, finally locking on mine.

"...Did you kill the shiny queen?"

Ralian let out a relieved breath while Solara made a broken sound, crouching down to grab Lilian's wrist. Nebula's face went still.

I crossed the space in two steps and leaned over the stretcher. Lilian's eyes tracked mine perfectly, despite my speed.

"If you didn't," she rasped, "give me meat and a minute. I'll go finish her."

My chest loosened.

Stupid girl.

Ralian bent over her daughter and pressed her forehead to Lilian's hair. Lilian's face went red instantly. "Mother, stop! I survived. You're embarrassing me in front of everyone!"

Ralian did not move. Lilian looked at me over her mother's shoulder. Her eyes softened for half a second, then narrowed.

"You look terrible. What’s up with all that blood?"

"Nice to see you too."

"Don't get cocky."

"Stratos, bring her some snacks."

"Already prepared," Stratos said. "Three roasted boars, six bowls of marrow stew, two trays of dumplings, and pudding."

Could that be considered snacks…? Anyways.

Lilian's eyes sharpened. "Hey, bring the pudding first."

"Absolutely not," Ralian said into her hair. “What do I always say? You need meat to recover properly after a big fight.”

Lilian clicked her tongue. Solara turned away when Lilian started complaining about medical cruelty and pudding rights. At first, I thought she was laughing.

Then I saw her shoulders. Lilian noticed too. She yanked her by the wrist, giving her a tight hug, ruffling her hair. "Heyyy, don't cry, Solara. I might not be able to regenerate like you, but I'm far harder to kill than that."

Solara wiped her face with the heel of her palm and turned back with wet eyes.

"Say that again after you stop needing spells to keep you alive."

"I don't need it. They put it on me."

Ralian shot her a look.

Lilian shut up.

Truly, she was alive. For now, that was what mattered.

****

The medical wing ate the next hour. I had to submit myself to Amelia's examination because refusing would have turned her into a dragon indoors.

Ralian stayed with Lilian. Solara refused to leave until Lilian had eaten half a pudding and fallen asleep again. Nebula took Valeria to a sealed side room with Munera, Senna, and Stratos to start checking for hidden demonic hooks. That sounded a bit invasive… Anyway, Valeria agreed to it without complaint.

Later, I sat on the edge of a healing platform while Amelia ran golden mana through my chest. The hole where my heart had been ripped out was gone, but my body still remembered the abuse. Apparently organs could hold grudges. Who knew?

"You're quieter than usual."

"I had a busy day, you see."

"Right. You fought an Arcane King, opened a complete Worldforge, revived a demoness, broke a continental mind-affecting field, and negotiated with a woman older than some religions."

"See? Super busy."

Her mouth tightened. I raised both hands.

"Sorry, I'm not trying to make light of it."

"Yeah? I think you are."

"Okay, maybe a little. There’s a reason though,” I said. “Because if I don't joke about it, I'll have to think about it properly. Because… then I’ll have to think about how the fight against Emperor Shengzong will go.”

Amelia's mana paused for a second, then continued.

"Check your Status."

"Sure, why not."

I opened it.

Well that’s a lot… I didn’t realize that I’d gained about twenty Levels, out of the blue. The System notifications hadn’t bothered to flash before me during the intense battle.

I wasn’t surprised by the level gain. Sure I hadn’t killed any big shots but technically I drove an Arcane King to stalemate. If not that, what else was Experience Points?

What shocked me was that at Level 117, I had 33,700 Qi. Now? Just twenty levels ahead, I had over 60,000 Qi!! I read the next notifications.

“Can you show me what you’re reading?”

“Why?” I asked, receiving silence in reply. “Uh, sure.”

I snapped my finger, allowing the notifications to form before us. Amelia read it over my shoulder. Her face did not change much, but her hand tightened on my shoulder.

"Level 137… You know, even I don’t have that much Mana even at Level 180?"

"Surviving an Arcane King apparently counts for a lot. Plus, what’s a man if he’s not stronger than his woman?” I said, receiving a smack on the back of my head.

"Right, Iskandaar Romani, who’s always been weaker than me until recently. Are you trying to piss me off today?"

"Aw, that hurt."

"Good."

I closed the Status. Honestly, I had expected a boost. Fighting a Level 310 Arcane King, surviving, forcing her to stop, awakening the Third Flower, completing Event Horizon… Of course, how could the System not reward such a spectacle?

Amelia tapped the side of my head with two fingers.

"How’s your brain, by the way? Be serious."

"Mostly mine."

"Are you certain? My Dragon’s Eye noticed that this thread technique of yours is pretty draining to the mind.”

"You’re reading it right. The Third Flower is dangerous if I keep it fully open, but I think I can train myself."

"I hope so.”

I gave her a look. She stared.

I sighed, closing my eyes. Using my Demonic Sphere, I decided to try something. Before, the Demonic Sphere merely mapped the area, but now it was a limited-area Omniscience kind of thing. Within range, I could see everything.

So I checked the others out of habit.

[Lilian Lunewolf, Moon's Warden, Level 112]

[Nebula Carlstein, Witch of Crimson, Level 103]

[Solara Fenixia, the Last Human-Phoenix, Level 107]

Not bad, everyone was a few levels higher. Good for them, even if the way they'd gotten there was horrifying.

On Lilian’s body, flesh had grown where flesh should not have grown. I remembered that sight, as goosebumps spread across my body. My Sovereign's Gaze had seen the technique hungrily drinking tiny bits of her body heat, then returning them in rough pulses. The rhythm had been wrong but stable, which was the worst kind of stable.

She had not learned the Eight Shackles properly. But she had survived. Which, by itself, was impressive. Amelia noticed my expression.

"Are you thinking about Lilian?"

"Yes. I’ll have to fix the technique she learned, it's all messed up," I said. "Later though. If I think about that now, Ralian might really attack me. I’ll also have to talk to Valeria… she wasn’t simply revived; I’m certain she also received a couple of boons. I need to figure out which. Otherwise we can’t trust her."

"No, I don’t mean it like that. Valeria herself is trustworthy but… The Demon King isn’t. I dived into Valeria’s memories, she showed me around, and I met the Demon King there. He’s more of a… God of Demons, I don’t know. In the memory, he’d simply flicked his hand at Dhumravarna, the four-armed Rakshasa, and the demon was sent flying. If the Demon King’s got a power like that, I won’t be surprised if he has any mind-controlling traps hidden inside Valeria, to maybe make her betray others if she ever switches teams.”

A tremor moved through the room. Heavy feet in the hall, not an attack.

Vargathrian entered without knocking because ancient wolf sovereigns did not believe in doors as a political concept. She looked at Amelia, then at me.

"Can you stand?"

"I can do a lot more, grandmother."

She heaved a breath hearing my tone. "Then come. I thought you two had gotten sweaty, given how long you were taking. Everyone is waiting outside."

I sighed, feeling a little tired. "Can I at least put on a shirt first?"

Vargathrian looked at the bloodied cloth hanging from my shoulder.

"No."

Amelia smiled faintly.

Traitors. All of them.

A replay. The council members wanted to see a reply to everything to study a conflict of this level properly. It was a great decision, since this was pure data and data was always useful. The replay happened in the council chamber. Stratos projected the battlefield above the central table, reconstructed from Nevaramis' link through my senses.

I showed them snippets leading up to the big fight, and when the vision reached that point, I slowed down. The image showed Vanga's ruined sky, Bagha's broken body, the Maharani's Crown, and then the moment Event Horizon swallowed the world.

Nobody spoke through that part.

Even Lilian, who had been carried in on a larger bed after threatening to bite the poor Obsidian Vampire nurse, watched in silence with a bowl of stew in her lap.

The replay skipped forward.

The Maharani stood after Event Horizon, tears on her face, while Shayura held her hand. Then came the negotiation, the twenty-one days, the laughter.

Then the projection showed the last exchange. I quickly tried to close it or skip it, but the controls slipped past me for a moment. Which wasn’t normal.

"Say, Iskandaar Romani."

"...What?"

"You're quite young but... Have you thought about becoming a father yet?"

Stratos stopped the replay there. So it was her!

The chamber went silent.

Lilian's ears slowly rose. "Stratos."

"Yes, Lady Lilian?"

"Rewind."

"Hey guys, let’s move to the next part,” I said.

"No, Stratos, rewind to that part!"

Stratos rewound the scene.

"Have you thought about becoming a father yet?"

The projection paused again.

Several faces turned toward me.

Lilian growled from her bed. "Just so you know, according to tribal tradition, while I don't care if you have other mates, I WANT a son first!"

Ralian closed her eyes.

From the sidelines, Munera's smile sharpened. "How charming. The Obsidian line has suffered severe losses, and Iskandaar bears some responsibility for our current situation. It would be proper for him to help restore it through Nebula, first of all."

Nebula slowly turned toward her mother.

"Mother."

Munera ignored her with the ease of someone who had been doing it for centuries. "Bloodline stability matters."

Lilian pushed herself up on one elbow. "You're making it sound like farming, dirty bloodsucker."

"For vampires, it is at least more elegant than your tribe's approach."

"My tribe's approach works, Munera,” Ralian replied.

Solara's face had gone the color of her hair. "Can we maybe not decide his children in a council meeting? A-and if we are, I want to revive House Fenixia too!"

Lailah was covering her face on the side, steam coming off it somehow. Valeria, sitting under three layers of anti-possession wards, finally stirred. "Hey, uh, I died to be here. I feel like that should earn at least a place somewhere high on the list."

Nebula's head turned slowly.

Valeria shrank into her blanket. "I was joking. Mostly."

The argument had reached its third simultaneous conversation when Stratos raised one hand. "I can act as an incubator."

The chamber went quiet.

Her face was completely flat. The face of someone filing a budget report.

"As you are all aware," she continued, in the same tone she used to announce weather conditions and supply chain updates, "I already hold the essence of every council member."

A transition rippled through her, as if to make a point. In rapid, seamless succession, a silver-furred wolf form that made Lilian blink, a pale sharp-featured vampire that made Nebula's eye twitch, something scaled and magnificent that Amelia scowled at, a horned silhouette that Lailah and Senna found personally offensive, a blazing phoenix which made Solara freeze, and several others that defied easy categorization.

Stratos returned to her default appearance and shrugged, the gesture carrying the energy of someone who had just shown a mildly interesting spreadsheet.

"So theoretically, if each of you were to contribute your life essence as raw material, I could synthesize a compatible vessel and–"

"No."

It came from approximately six directions at once. Lilian, Nebula, Lailah, Amelia, Solara, and even Valeria who had no biological stake in the matter whatsoever but felt strongly nonetheless.

Lilian, from her stretcher, pointed a spoon at Stratos without looking up from her pudding. "No."

Stratos accepted this with a small nod, already pulling up the next item on her agenda. "Understood. I have logged the proposal as declined. Moving on."

I put my face in my hands. Nobody said anything for a moment.

"Stratos," I said finally.

"Yes, my liege?"

"Where did that idea even come from."

"Approximately four hundred and twelve years ago, when I was considering logistical solutions to dynastic succession problems," she said pleasantly. "This ranked in the top three for operational efficiency."

"And what were the other two…?"

"You don't want to know."

She was right. I didn't. I rubbed my face with both hands. "There's a reason kings used heirs as political weapons. At some point you just surrender."

"Enough," Vargathrian said. Everyone quieted. The old wolf stood at the far side of the chamber, arms folded, eyes on me. "Before we continue discussing, I want to note something. You refused to let us come."

Amelia's face shifted, eyes hardening on me. Ralian looked down at Lilian. I lowered my hands. "Yes."

"Even though you were facing an Arcane King?"

"Yes."

"You had allies here strong enough to matter, you’re aware?"

"I don’t know. Strong enough to die, sure," I said. "Not strong enough to change the shape of that fight."

Amelia's eyes narrowed, but she did not interrupt.

I stood, finally a bit too annoyed to just take it. The room felt different when I did that now. I could feel it in the threads, people adjusting without realizing, bodies angling toward whoever held the most weight in the space. I hadn't asked for that, and I wasn't sure what to do with it yet.

"I had cards left," I said. "You didn't know them. The Maharani didn't know them. Even I didn't fully know them until they opened in my hand. If I called you all into that field knowing your presence wouldn't truly change the outcome, I would have risked Nevaramis' spine for comfort."

Vargathrian watched me.

I continued.

"I didn't enjoy it. It wasn't clean. Lilian nearly died. Valeria really did die. I had my heart ripped out. But as Sovereign of Nevaramis, my duty was to use what I had to keep myself alive and therefore keep all of you alive as well."

A moment of silence later, I added. "And if the same board appeared again, with the same pieces, I would make the same kind of choice."

Lilian stopped eating.

Ralian looked at me like she'd been reaching for an argument and found the shelf empty. Vargathrian's mouth curved. It was not quite a smile, more like a wolf deciding something had started to earn its ground.

"Hmm."

"That's all?"

"Did you want praise?" she asked, making me shrug. A throaty laughter left the beast. Then she said, "Your Worldforge."

"What about it?"

"It was rough."

"Thank you."

"No, I mean it should be impossible."

"Again, thank you."

"It was irritatingly alive." Her eyes sharpened. "I want to study it. Would you let me see? I think I can improve my own Worldforge if you let me."

Is Event Horizon really that big of a deal? I was a little surprised hearing this from a hero of legends. “If it was someone else, I'd tell them to submit a research request and wait three to five business months."

Amelia coughed into her hand.

I looked at Vargathrian.

"But you're my people. An elder in my council. I'll let you take a look later, under controlled conditions."

Vargathrian laughed again. The sound made the windows shake. “Perhaps that is why I’d chosen to serve, despite all my greatness.”

Perhaps because she was mostly chill, I forgot how arrogant thousand year old deities could get. Her words reminded me of her status properly. I only felt proud, for indeed, Vargathrian the Beast Hero was a part of my council.

The strongest of Nevaramis.

Twenty-one days passed quickly.

Too quickly, considering most of them involved rebuilding diplomatic bridges I had burned, repairing the emotional damage from Vanga's awakening, and dealing with Valeria's new place in our city’s structure.

She remained under watch. She accepted it.

The old Valeria would have turned the surveillance into a game. This one was the same, there was no change. Still, she managed to sit through Nebula's inspections, answered Lailah's questions about demonic contracts, and let Solara burn a thread of suspicious dream residue out of her shoulder without making a single obscene comment. She was incredibly annoying to work with, according to the reports, but she cooperated in the end.

Progress, I suppose.

Nebula and Lailah found no active Demon King compulsion inside Valeria. She found old scars where such things might have been. It didn't make anyone comfortable. But it was the better kind of unsettling, something survivable rather than something live.

"I still don't trust her. Her situation, I mean," Nebula told me one evening, after Valeria had gone to sleep under six wards and one very smug owl sitting outside her door. Vyrn had reportedly come to great help in this Valeria check-up case.

“It’s okay, I’ll do a check on her myself,” I told her, making her raise an eyebrow.

“Well to satiate your curiosity. Now that you’ve done your check and found nothing, I’ll do a final check up on her before declaring she’s clean.”

“Right. And do you, perhaps, need to be alone with her during this procedure of yours?” she asked, and her tone carried a strange accusatory tone.

“Haha, I have no idea what you’re talking about.”

Lilian recovered faster than anyone expected. The Eight Shackles had left marks on her body that did not fade, thin black-red lines along her spine and shoulders like someone had carved restraints into her flesh. She pretended not to care. Ralian cared enough for both of them. So much so that I had to go tell her that I’ll return her daughter to pristine condition, and better, if she gave me a few weeks.

Solara spent the last three weeks training in the Nevaramis dungeons, growing stronger as a 7th Ascension. Since she’d been the weakest among us four in this journey, she was trying to make up for it. She found it easy to cross Nebula and Lilian’s levels, given both of those girls were too busy in their own ways to level up.

Although…

Stratos framed that last one in the council chamber without asking me.

Bagha sent no apology for nearly killing me during our first meeting, nothing about kidnapping Shayura from his prison, or well, for being Bagha Hamid.  Instead, a striped fang the size of my forearm arrived in a blackwood case. One sentence had been carved into the bone.

If the Baolian dog comes east, you know which Tiger to call.

Vargathrian laughed for a full minute when she saw it.

I couldn’t stop grinning.
