I could feel it.

The joviality I tried so hard to grasp when talking to Joey was draining, leaving only a bone-deep cold behind. It was strange to feel my moral compass disintegrating in real time. I wished I were angry or scared, but the only thing left behind came in the form of instinct.

I sat on my couch, leaning forward, elbows on knees, as I stared at the phone in Joey's hand. My friend didn't notice. Too absorbed in the conversation.

Maerin knew more about the Dream than the other Survivors. Knee more than me, most likely. How likely was it that she had done something to Joey's actual girlfriend? I didn't know, but if I found out any of the Survivors did anything, I was killing them all. I didn't invite these people into my world so they could start fucking with the people I cared about.

Joey continued talking to Maerin, his voice tinged with concern. I couldn't tell if it was for me, him, or Maerin.

"I know, I know babe. It'll be a bit longer. I need to talk to him about some stuff. It's important. I don't know if he'd be comfortable with that. It's the touchy subject. Not more than ten minutes I promise." I couldn't hear what Maerin said on the other side, just Joey's portion as he tried to reassure the woman.

I cut into the conversation. "Tell her to come up," it came out as detached, as if I projected my voice rather than speaking directly. I felt something shift, and the world moved a hundred miles a minute and simultaneously at a snail's pace.

"What?" Joey asked, clearly confused.

While I couldn't hear Maerin, I could pick up on context clues, "She wants to come up, right? Tell her she can."

He muted the phone and turned to me, practically scowling, "What's gotten into you all the sudden man?"

"Joey," I added as much finality into my voice as I could, "stop fucking with me and tell her to come up."

He met my gaze and didn't waver. "No," he said.

"No?" I questioned.

"No," he shook his head, "You're acting strange and pulled more spider silk than I've ever seen from thin air. We have stuff to talk about and honestly I don't trust you right now."

"You don't trust me. Really. Really?" I laughed in his face and stood up. I wish I had Peeler with me.

Joey sighed, "That's not what I meant. I do trust you, that's why I'm here, but I trust you with me."

"She's obviously scared about something Joey. Are you just going to leave her down there all alone?" I pushed.

"Not going to work on me, Dorian. Something's going on, and until you and I talk about it she's not coming up and we're not going anywhere." Joey was a stone wall. He always could see through me.

"Do you think I'm going to hurt her?" I changed tacts.

"Yes," he said, and I was genuinely surprised.

"Why?" I asked.

"Because I've seen you like this one other time, and you put someone in the hospital." The memory came to mind. A house party, someone had done donuts in the street, drunk off their ass, and crashed into a tree.

"He deserved it," I spat, remembering the anger I felt as I dragged the other boy from the car and wailed on him.

Joey stayed calm and resolute. "He was wrong for it. He should've been arrested, but you went too far Dorian. You always go too far."

We stared each other down for a moment, unmoving, and then Maerin's shaking voice whispered over the phone, barely audible in the silence. "I-I'm scared."

Just like that, whatever had taken hold of me broke. In the Instance, Maerin never sounded scared. Angry, overwhelmed, yeah, but not scared. She was no good at lying either. That fear was real. It sounded like someone who could see giant man eating spiders.

I deflated further and crashed back into the couch, "I won't do anything. Promise. Worst case she can wait in the hall."

Joey gave me a long look, searching for something. He found it and unmuted the phone, "I'll be right down. Hang tight."

And so I was left alone in my apartment—and it had never felt so empty. It gave me time to walk into my room and retrieve the box I had spent years hiding underneath my bed.

I had been waiting for this moment for so long. Ever since I was pulled into the Dream in the cemetery. I had waited for … two days. After everything, it had only been two days. More time had passed in the Dream, of course. At least double, but … but still four days.

My hands brushed against one of the journal's edges. I imagined Rowan descending into the Dream, waking up, and writing down all his findings. The image of him flying in space and raining down abominations onto another world broke me from those sweet thoughts. I really didn't know a single thing about him. How many people did he kill and why? I turned to the tapes, sure I would get my answers there. Sure, more than anything, that it wouldn't just be static anymore.

I found the tape that started it all. J4-P123 and slotted it into my old VHS player.

It crackled to life.

Static at first, but the image quickly came into colored focus. It was of Rowan's old apartment building, it was pointed at the bed and his stupid lime colored sheets, there was a chair and a whiteboard beside it. A figure walked around the camera.

My brother, Rowan.

His face was in full frame. He looked intensely frustrated as he tapped an open marker against the whiteboard. We looked similar, with light brown skin and scraggly hair. He took a seat and started speaking.

"Alright," his voice was deep, smooth, and had a lilt to it that spoke of absolute confidence. "House on the Hill. How do we fell 'bout that? I feel like it's a little too difficult. Visitors wouldn't come, not even for the rewards."

I frowned, pressing myself closer to the screen. The voice that sounded next dropped my legs from under me.

The voice was feminine, but more importantly, static on the screen formed with the words.

"I'm one for efficiency. High risk, high reward. I'll never understand the Instances with low risk." Rowan replied.

My throat closed.

"Your method lacks creativity."

"It's sort of a Trojan horse ain't it. Who are we sending it to?"

No. Please no.

Rowan began using a marker to draw up plans. Blueprints. Started outlining Restrictions and rewards.

My fist slammed into the TV. It sprawled backwards, crashing and shattering on the ground.

I stumbled backwards, my hand bled onto the floor. I fell against my bed. It was all so loud. I gripped the side of my head.

Who the hell were you, Rowan? I gathered the notebooks and tapes in a daze.

From the corner of my eye, just through the open door frame, I could see the upper half of a desk-sized spider phase through the bottom edge of the floor. It stayed there, with half of its body visible, occasionally pivoting in sharp, jerky motions. Its legs were a whirlwind of disorienting motion for the second it took the spider to turn.

This was familiar ground. There was danger here. I slowly tiptoed to shut the door between us. It was my first mistake. The movement triggered some predator instinct in the creature. One moment it was on the other side of my apartment, the next it was darting towards me.

I slammed the door shut, stepped back, and saw it leap through the door directly at me as if it wasn't there.

My hands shot out in front of me, and I felt prickly, needle-like skin dig into my hands. Simultaneously, I saw the spider split and continue phase through me. A migraine racked behind my eye, and I wasn't sure if darkness or static took my vision as I almost let go of the spider's two front legs—the only thing keeping its mouth from my neck.

I screamed at the monster. The sheer volume of movement was hard to keep track of. It wasn't just pushing away two legs and fangs.
