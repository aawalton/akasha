It took Levi more time than he would've liked to cut off the head of the krupi, but he eventually got to it. Blood dripped from the severed head of the monster as Levi walked towards the other part of its head that had been severed using his void tear.

To tell the truth, it was a bit unnerving for Levii to be doing such a thing, but he had realized that he’d have to get his hands bloody if he planned to go far in this world, especially since his stint in the dungeon. With a grunt, he quickly assembled the Krupi’s head and began his trek towards the village.

He preferred to walk, rather than put some of his newly gotten points into his mana and just void tear all his way back there. It wasn’t optimal, but it’d be the best thing he could do for the future him.

If he distributed those points in a smarter way later, then he’d end up ensuring that they were useful later on for him, and not just useful for his current issue.

Levi’s thoughts jumbled about as he walked, and Levi was left considering the fact that the system hadn’t warned him about how close he’d been to death, or maybe it had, and it was just in his system logs. Still, he had to wonder if he had to check his system logs every time he was in a fight.

It seemed like a ridiculous thing to do—having to check his system log or status screen every time he was in a fight, just to confirm how much health he had left— because he knew the risks that came with fighting monsters.

The high-level ones that he’d clashed with so far hadn’t given him much time to think, at least not enough to make much of a change to his situation at those times. They’d been brash and vicious. Attacking without a shred of honor or empathy. Of course, he’d have to be a fool to expect that from those beasts. Their sole mission was to kill, and he was their target.

It was simply the way the world worked.

Levi also knew that if he was the one who’d been stronger in those fights, he’d not have had to waste time strategizing how to take them down. He’d simply brute force his way out of every fight.

His mind flashed with images of him ending the krupi with a single strike, and he couldn’t help but grin. It wasn’t as far-fetched as he imagined, especially since he knew that there were actual synced who could easily pull off such feats, and all they needed was the right class. The warrior class.

It wasn’t like he hated his rogue class; on the contrary, he liked it. That was the only reason he’d chosen it. And now he was in a mess that required him to be a mage, priest, warrior, rogue, and ranger just to survive.

Xara had confirmed what he already knew when she’d told him that Ghost wouldn’t be the only one coming for the stone. If anything, it had forced him to analyze himself and try to figure out how exactly he planned to handle multiple threats at once.

No matter how long Levi thought about it, he couldn’t help but come to the same conclusion. He needed to meet with the Black Baron at Planthrit, and he’d have to do it as soon as possible.

The obsidian stone that he’d absorbed was the pride of many, and if he couldn’t grow strong enough to protect it himself, he’d have no other choice but to depend on others, like Xara and the Black Baron, to do so for him.

The facts are all there, but still I hesitate. Why? Levi couldn’t figure it out. Meeting with the black baron was his best course of action, but he was still two minded about the whole thing.

It wasn’t far-fetched to say that he didn’t trust his life with someone nicknamed the Butcher of Talpha.

That was one of the reasons why he’d stuck to Xara, preferring to waste time fighting the Krupi for the mage, even at the risk of his own life, rather than find a way to escape, hit the next dungeon, and use the way to continue going west.

His position as one of the first would allow him unlimited use of the way. A loophole that he wouldn’t be able to enjoy with Xara by his side. Nonetheless, he’d refrained from running away because of his trust issues.

It was beyond obvious to Levi that he was now growing attached to the mage. He’d put his reluctance to leave her down to logic, but knew it was more than that. There was something growing for her, but he wasn’t ready to confront whatever it was.

He couldn’t think or dream about it because there were more important things at stake, and the faster he could get his priorities checked, the faster he could confront whatever his soul was trying to tell him about the mage. For now, he’d have to get the head to Meron and take the mage with him…or maybe he’d leave her there.By all accounts, they were even now. She’d paid for his gear, and he’d risked his life for hers.

Regardless of how it looked, Levi wouldn’t mind calling it an even exchange. After all, it wouldn’t be the first time he’d been on the end of a bad exchange just to get what he wanted.

Levi sighed at the thought, his tired legs moving on their own accord as the thoughts in his mind rumbled about.

He’d had an eventful week. His syncing had become one of the best and worst decisions Levi had ever made. He’d gotten the skills he’d dreamed about, as well as the cloak, daggers, and adventure, but it hadn’t panned out the way he’d planned.

Rather than being a normal rogue in the big city, he was now the First, on the run from multiple threats that he didn't even know, with a Noble by his side.

This was just like the first time he’d met Killian all over again. There was no mistaking it. His entire life was some kind of poorly written script that had led him here, to indescribable power, and all he had to do was stay alive long enough to enjoy it.

And therein lies the problem. I’m not strong enough to keep it. Not yet. A tired chuckle escaped Levi’s lips at the thought. It was so funny to think about from another person’s perspective.

He’d been made an offer way too good to be true, and he’d have to be prepared for the shoe to drop. If he were lucky, the Black Baron would be the solution, and if he wasn’t, he’d be too dead to worry about it.

Levi cleared his mind and focused on the path up ahead.

He’d gotten into range, judging that the few villagers who now trailed him from atop the trees. There was no doubt in his mind that he could take them on now. With the amount of points he had available to him, but he didn’t try to attack, as that would be counterintuitive, especially since he’d succeeded in his quest to kill the krupi.

Even though he kept his head straight, he made sure to scan his surroundings.

It seemed that night was about to fall, and even though he’d have preferred not to return to the village this late, he couldn’t afford to take unnecessary risks by sleeping in the forest, as there was no telling what kind of monster the blood of the severed head of the krupi would bring to him. So he would rather take his chances with the villagers, and if push came to shove, he could void step out of the place.

“You survived,” Gazun grunted, surprise and disappointment coating his words.

“I did, now take me to Meron,” Levi said to the guard stationed at the gate.

“It’s Chief Meron to you,” the guard huffed, but ultimately moved to open the gates and lead him into the village, once Levi refused to take the bait.

The sight of the bleeding decapitated head seemed to be enough to knock the disrespectful tone out of the guard's tongue. In fact, it seemed to have made Gazun a bit fidgety.

Levi could see it from the way that the guard constantly glanced over his shoulder as they walked. Gazun’s grip on his spear was a lot tighter than it had been earlier. Levi could tell from those two patterns that the guard was obviously scared, and why wouldn’t he be?

According to the village chief, many villagers had been sent to battle the beast, but none had returned. So, to them, Levi had most likely done the impossible by taking down a monster that so many of theirs had failed to slay, and after his battle with the beast he could understand why it had been so hard for them to kill it.

The Krupi’s armored scales would’ve rendered all their weapons useless just as it had done his, and with their biggest means of attack nullified, they’d have been nothing but easy targets for the Krupi’s claws and fangs.

They’d have been ripped to shreds within minutes. A terrible but otherwise expected outcome. It was almost unheard of for an unsynced to stand up to any synced regardless of the synced rank. It was a power gap that only got wider with each rank the synced achieved.

Even between synced, that gap existed, and so, with the recent increase in the rate of dungeon leaks, it’d be up to these villagers to decide what they valued more, their lives or their beliefs. Levi knew that it was cruel of him to paint everything black and white, but that was the reality of the situation. As they were, the villagers were doomed, and the death of the Krupi wouldn’t change that fact.

The only thing that could change their reality was if they could swallow their pride and sync. If they could not do that, then there was nothing else in store for them other than death. A gruesome one, by the fangs or claws of the next monster that decided to escape a dungeon close to them.

It was an inevitable outcome to anyone with an open mind, and Levi guessed that the Chief had come to the same conclusion.

In fact, Meron had told him to his face that he feared for his people, and Levi could see why. These villagers were held back by decades of being shut out from the outside world. Their ancestors had believed that by staying away from the rest of Rinesta, they’d be safe from the problems of the outside world, but they weren’t.

The monsters were now escaping dungeons, and they did not care one bit about who was synced or unsynced. They’d eat on flesh regardless of who was what. That was what Meron had realized, and that was why he’d offered Levi the chance to save Xara’s life by slaying the Krupi.

If Levi was successful, then Meron would have a proven example as to why the rest of the villagers should sync, and the logic would be sound.

If one synced could take on the Krupi and slay it, then why should they send many men to their deaths in hopes that one of them would be able to kill it? Levi tightened his grip on the severed head of the Krupi.

His eyes briefly met those of the many villagers who had begun to gather around him and Gazun. Some had tears streaming from their faces, and Levi could only imagine how difficult it was for them to set their eyes on the head of the creature that had most likely caused them to lose someone close to them.

The silent procession carried on, with more and more villagers joining in until they finally reached the building that belonged to the village chief, where Meron stood waiting. His face was blank, but Levi saw something in his eyes. Hope.

“You’ve returned with the head of the Krupi.” Meron nodded. “For that, you have my thanks.”
