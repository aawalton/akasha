It wasn’t easy to maintain one’s calm, staring at two Champion rank demons of incomprehensible scale, bearing down on the Fable Train. Ethan watched them through the monitors with more than a little apprehension, telling himself the plan would work, and these wouldn’t be the last moments of an admittedly exciting life.

The serpent was still a considerable distance out to see, though it was difficult to judge the distance given the creature’s size. Maybe two minutes away, Ethan considered, and knowing that everything would fall apart if it reached them before Allyara did.

In the other direction, the silver Champion was just managing to keep ahead of the gargantuan shark she’d led here. It was still invisible beneath the grey waters, only the towering fin and massive wake showing its position.

This was what they’d been waiting for, and what had taken several weeks to find. Time that Ethan still couldn’t be certain he had.

The first element was the serpent. It needed specific physical characteristics, not the least of which was the ability to travel on land. Steelbinder postulated that the only reason The Throne hadn’t been taken already was because of that one simple fact: most water demons couldn’t make it far enough inland to be a threat.

Some could, of course, and they’d been Allyara’s focus for the last few years. She’d gotten quite good at facing them, really, though she rarely went for the kill. Champion rank demons regenerated at ludicrous speeds, and it was far more effective to weaken them, then let the other predators finish them off.

This part of the plan should be relatively safe for that reason. Allyara was an expert at drawing Champions together, and letting them fight it out. Thankfully, while she may not have her full power at the moment, her speed appeared to be more than enough.

It was the very essence of working smarter, not harder, at least on the surface. Allyara had admitted that getting trapped between two warring Champions was how she’d lost her arm, and severed her most recently lost Bond.

No system was perfect.

Finally, the shark showed itself, and everyone crowded around the nearest monitor. Fable obligingly created a larger one along one wall, and Ethan and his duplicates watched in stunned silence as the enormous beast rose above the water.

“It’s more…shark-like than I’d have expected,” Apollo said.

He wasn’t wrong. Ethan had seen much the same on Nexum, and figured that there simply wasn’t much more that a shark could need. The ones he’d seen were effectively just enormous mouths with several tons of muscle propelling them toward their prey, and apparently the Champion rank version wasn’t far off.

The demon’s head was split by a cavernous maw, revealing rows of teeth that disappeared into the dark reaches of its throat. Two sets of black eyes gazed at the silver Champion like death itself, and its dark flesh had only the barest hint of red to it.

Otherwise, it appeared to have a few more fins than its Earth counterparts, but was unmistakably a preposterously large shark.

“Big shark,” Zakiel said, nodding sagely.

“I don’t think sharks need anything more than they have back home,” Ethan began, planning to share his observations.

Just then, an enormous tongue shot from the depths of its gargantuan jaws, like that of a frog. The sinewy length of twisting flesh went directly for Allyara, the Champion dodging at the last moment.

“Never mind,” Ethan said. Apparently there was something that could make a shark more effective.

He turned his attention back to the serpent, grateful to see that it had noticed the other demon approaching, and was turning to meet it. That was good. They needed it weakened.

But… Ethan couldn’t help but stare at that long, twisting neck. They needed this creature alive, and that was a surprisingly obvious weakness against the shark. Surely the serpent would be hard to catch, but one good bite…

“Shark wins,” Zakiel said, beating Ethan to the unfortunate conclusion. “Snake versus shark,” he said with a shrug. “Shark wins. Science.”

Ethan sighed. The assassin might be right.

The two colossal creatures were close now, only the tiny dot of silver light between them, and Ethan found himself gripping Steelbinder’s chambers. The metal was bending beneath his fingers, but all he could do was watch.

At the last moment, Allyara pivoted directly upward, blasting into the sky even more quickly than she’d moved up to this point. The shark didn’t even appear to watch her leave, both sets of black eyes already focused on its new opponent.

Ethan ground his teeth, looking back at the serpent as it rose even higher from the water. All it seemed to be doing was revealing more vulnerable neck, and his mind began working on some new mad plan, some way of distracting the shark to keep the serpent safe.

Well, not safe, but only just injured enough.

But right as the shark opened its titanic jaws wider, and it tilted to bite the other demon, something else burst from the depths.

Tentacles. Enormous, twisting tentacles, dozens of them, the same shade of deep blue as the serpent. They latched onto the charging demon one after another, suction cups and barbed blades piercing into the shark’s thick hide.

The momentum of the charging demon seemed like it would allow it to reach the serpent anyway, but it didn’t happen. Instead, the serpent was lifted from the water, revealing the divide where long neck split into a thick forest of tentacles, holding the other demon back.

The shark wasn’t completely out-matched, however, and its enormous jaws clamped down as it thrashed and writhed in the water, sending waves outward. It was biting through dozens of the binding limbs, and the true fight was on.

Ethan let out a sigh of relief.

“It’s working. That means it’s almost time–our window will be narrow, and not easy to predict.”

He moved back to where he’d planted Rothavaro’s gifted blade into the floor, ignoring the monitors as he reached down to touch the exposed hilt.

“This part will hurt,” Apollo warned, but Ethan was committed.

Sending his awareness into the sword and Fable both, he began the next phase of the plan. Glistening Razor disappeared beneath his fingertips, as if digested by the train, and he felt its power all around him. That done, he reached out to his spirit, giving it the command it had been waiting for.

“All of it,” he whispered, “every last drop of power.”

Allyara Silverbane hovered above the battlefield, watching the fight between enraged Champions with detached wonder.

“This is the craziest thing I’ve ever been a part of,” she whispered.

A thousand feet below, bladed tentacles were ripping apart the shark, as it tried to eat its prey faster than it was killed. This part of the plan didn’t overly concern her. She’d pitted enough demons against one another to know they almost always retreated before they were killed.

Beasts this ancient had a sort of cunning, and an appreciation for their own lives. There’d be no Champions left beneath the waves if they always fought to the death. No, easy victories were what they liked, devouring anything small enough to be easily caught.

Ethan would have the chance he sought.

She shook her head at that, and her thoughts turned back to that day, years earlier, when she’d seen a poor, doomed fool about to be eaten by a demon. The decision to save him had been an easy one, she liked saving people, simple as that.

But who could have predicted how far that man would come?

She’d known she was potentially dropping a dangerous catalyst into the boiling cauldron that was Nexum. A doctor, in a world that outlawed healing. But for him to come this far.

Seeing multiple versions of the man had only cemented her appreciation for just how unusual the man was. Multiple potential realities in which he became something far beyond a simple threat. Allyara had thought she’d known what she had sent through that rift, but she hadn’t known what it meant to be a Bishop.

Conversations with the man’s allies had given her insights into who he was. What he was. It was strange to have such faith in anyone anymore. Let alone a simple Twilight. Yet Ethan Bishop…

Allyara’s thoughts were interrupted by a sensation of terrible power vibrating through the Astral, and she turned to look out to sea. There, she saw the last thing they needed.

Another Champion.

It was lost beneath the waves, but she could feel its power, and knew its hunger. The sea was red with demon blood for miles, and this newcomer saw a chance at two easy meals.

Her eyes flashed back to the battle. It was progressing as they’d hoped, but it wasn’t time yet. The serpent was still too strong.

Allyara looked at the Fable Train, feeling the power gathering within it. That man’s ridiculous Spirit was being completely drained, and it was like a dam had burst, unleashing titanic energies, gathered across untold miles of Potentia.

He needed time.

Allyara sighed, knowing she’d already made her decision. She hadn’t told Ethan how bad her body and spirit truly were. She was barely holding herself together.

If she had a hundred years of isolation in perfect safety, perhaps she could have cobbled back together the ragged fragments of her spirit, and her Bonds. But she’d never have that now.

Reaching down within herself, Allyara felt the warmth of her three broken Familiars, each as far gone as she. Yet they were always within reach, always with her, as they had been for centuries.

She barely needed to ask to know they agreed. They were one being, after all, and they’d been together so long.

This sacrifice was no different.

Allyara felt tears running down her cheeks as her friends burnt themselves away for her, empowering her ragged body one final time.

Mana rushed through her body, and she felt herself cracking open, unable to contain the wild energies. She shot downward with the force of a hundred thousand bolts of lightning.

She moved so fast that she didn’t even feel it as she pierced through the shark’s head, explosions erupting behind her. The demon howled in pain, and the serpent’s tentacle’s released in surprise.

The shark followed her as Allyara shot away, directly toward the third champion. She could see three gargantuan heads rising from the water, like those of a sea turtle, and forced even more speed from her deteriorating form.

She feared that the Serpent might follow as well, and risked turning to check. The immense creature was looking at her, but was being held back by untold thousands of steel wires, all fired forth from the Fable Train.

They wrapped around its gaping jaws, pulling the serpent backward as Allyara flew further out to sea. She looked at the train, fascinated by the countless points of light appearing all around it.

They tiny shards of glass, she knew, the transformed blade that Ethan had produced. Artificially raised to Champion rank strength thanks to Steelbinder, and replicated a million times over thanks to the power of Ethan’s spirit.

The Fable Train was becoming an enormous sword, point directed at the towering serpent.

With a blast of energy, the blade exploded into the air, the wires pulling it toward the most absurd of targets–the demon’s roaring mouth.

Allyara heard herself laughing as the shark crashed into her from behind, and the turtle from the front. As the last of her power erupted from her, she held her smile as long as she could.

Ethan bloody Bishop. Go win, you absolute madman.

Allyara Silverbane disappeared in a blaze of burning glory.
