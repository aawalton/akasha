CHAPTER 87

The yard did not stop for her.

Riley had worked hard to build something that would continue without her in its day to day. The settlement moved the way it always moved, carts and soldiers and the distant ring of the forge, work that did not stop to acknowledge her return because work needed to get done. Riley finished her scan and her face softened.

She dismounted and handed the reins to the nearest soldier without looking at him. Thorne was already down, already moving to the horse that carried the chest. He lifted it without being asked and stood waiting for instructions. Riley was looking in another direction.

Across the yard, near the bunkhouse wall, Nami stood watching her. Riley met her eyes and raised one hand, palm out. Not yet. Nami gave a small nod and stayed where she was.

Nikola was already walking toward her.

"The soldiers for Hoshin, first," she said before he could speak.

He turned without argument and led her to the line.

The group of soldiers waited. She walked the line the same as Valrik had walked it a dozen times, slowly, without rushing. She checked their uniforms and weapons, then she asked three questions and listened to the answers and corrected one without making it a lesson.

At the end of the line she stopped and looked at them for a moment.

"You are going to Hoshin," she said. "The people there are leaving everything they had to come here. You need to blend in and allow the rest of the Hoshin the time they need to move here. Collect resources and stay clear of the Clawborn unless ordered."

She stepped back.

The gate opened. The soldiers filed through in a clean double line, boots striking the packed earth in a rhythm that carried back through the gate as it closed behind them.

Nikola stood beside her watching them go. The yard settled into a quieter register without the line in it. A cart passed. The forge rang once and went silent.

He turned to her and she looked at him and waited.

"The soldiers arrived safely," he said. "The bodies are in the hospital. The timer is running." He paused. "There is something else."

"Tell me."

He told her. The glow moving through the room. Landryn at the bedside. The way Valrik's face changed before he woke, the jaw tightening, the eyes moving beneath the lids. Landryn already on his feet. Valrik sitting upright, and the name that came out of him before he went back under.

Riley stood very still.

"Is he still out?" she asked.

"Yes," Nikola said.

She nodded once. She looked at the gate the soldiers had just passed through, as it closed, and then she looked at the hospital building across the yard.

"Thank you," she said.

She moved toward the hospital alone.

***

Thorne and Nikola did not follow. The yard moved around her and she walked through it and nobody stopped her or spoke to her and she was grateful for that same courtesy.

The hospital door was not fully closed. It rested against the frame, a finger's width of darkness showing along the edge. She put her hand on the wood and held it there for a moment before she pushed it open far enough to see inside.

The lanterns were low and amber, casting the same unsteady warmth across the two rows of beds. The room smelled of pine and sterility.

She found Valrik's bed from the doorway.

She stayed there watching the slow movement of Valrik's blanket, the rise and the fall of his chest. His face was slack, turned slightly to one side, the lines around his jaw softened. She just stood there, watching, taking in the scene.

She did not go in.

She stepped back and pulled the door to its original position, the finger's width of darkness restored. She stood with her back against the wall beside it and looked at the yard in front of her without seeing any of it. Valrik had called her name while recovering. Nikola had told Riley this, but the detail had landed differently. She had thought of soldiers as just soldiers but now that ideas were beginning to blur. She pressed the back of her head against the wall and looked up at the pale sky and let the moment wash over her. Then she pushed off the wall and went to find Nami.

***

Nami saw her coming and walked toward her, which saved Riley the trouble of searching. They met near the edge of the yard where the bunkhouses cast a long shadow across the ground, the sound of the forge and the carts muffled here, the air a little quieter.

Nami did not wait for Riley to speak first.

"Nikola told me to find you," she began. "I have done the work he assigned. I have not complained. But mending and kitchen work is not enough. Not for me." She held Riley's gaze without flinching. "I want to help in a more meaningful way."

Riley looked at her. Really looked, pushing away the distraction of the dozens of other details waiting.

Nami's hands were still at her sides but her shoulders were raised. She had rehearsed this, and this was landing as intended.

"You're right," Riley said. "There is a difference."

Nami blinked once and a space crept between her lips.

"I need you to head up a new project. You will need a few of your people to help with this. But I cannot give you all the details yet."

Nami was listening carefully.

"I can tell you that your group will be processing minerals from the mine, not ore or gold, something else. This could be one of the most important projects for the future of our community."

Nami was quiet for a moment, then straightened, eyes widening.

"That is a real thing," she said.

"Yes," Riley said. "A very serious and vital thing."

Nami looked at her steadily, her body shifted as she smiled.

"I can do that, what's next?" Nami said.

"We will meet soon and I will lay it all out for you."

With a nod Riley turned and walked towards the tower, leaving Nami with a bigger smile, bent arms and tightened fists.

***

The tower stairs were quiet.

Thorne had left the chest just inside the door without a word, set squarely in the middle of the floor where she could not miss it. She picked it up and set it on the table.

She took out the history book and set it beside the chest. Then she reached into her coat and took out the Corvessa scroll and set it on the other side and sat down.

The HUD pulsed softly at the edge of her vision. Timers she already knew. Numbers she had checked before. The settlement running its quiet arithmetic in the background, patient and indifferent and waiting for her next decision.

Her hand rested on the edge of the table without touching any of them. The tower was quiet in a way that nothing had been quiet since the compound, no orders to give, no timers to check, no one waiting for her to make a decision.

She sat back.

The battle had worked, mostly, and she was glad for that, but watching her archers handle the tar fire mixture had made her stomach tighten in a way she hadn't had time to process until now. It was sticky and slow to apply, and if any of it caught on a sleeve or a boot during a real fight the soldier wearing it would become the problem rather than the solution. What she needed was the mixture inside a clay pot with a wick, something a soldier could throw at a structure or a siege weapon from a safe distance and walk away from cleanly.

The glass bottles from the compound basement were a different opportunity entirely. Small enough to throw with accuracy, and if she could get the mixture right, a single bottle landing on a high-level enemy soldier could begin to narrow a gap that nothing else in her current arsenal could touch. Her soldiers were lower tier and the Clawborn were not, and that gap needed real solutions well before the army currently marching north reached her walls.

Clay pots she could probably produce herself with the right setup, but glass would need to be purchased and some of the gold recovered from the basement could cover that. She needed to sit down with Nikola and map out the next steps properly, not on the fly between tasks but with a real plan and enough time to think it through.

She looked up at the ceiling and exhaled slowly, "Can I clone myself?" she said.

The silence did not answer.

She looked at the three things on the table, the chest, the scroll, the history book, all of them just waiting.

The chest that Thorne had picked up with something shifting in his face. The scroll with Corvessa's seal sitting unbroken in the wax. The history book with its dense small script and its passage about a kingdom that had stood where something else now stood.

She reached forward.
