Last time Alex was at Delver’s Association HQ, he’d emptied his bank account and maxed out his credit card for two pretty low-tier items.

Hopefully this time’s a little different.

He was pretty excited about actually getting a full set of half-decent gear. Hopefully a little better than half-decent; he’d already decided he was reinvesting basically all his earnings into gear. It wasn’t the time to penny-pinch; he was going for pure returns right now, and he was turning around a few ideas of what kind of gear he’d want in his mind.

Before very long he was dropped off, and he went directly to the 11th floor. A few minutes after that he was reaching into his Interspatial Ring.

When he’d first picked it up, he’d had to wriggle the giant sword in carefully; it wasn’t easy placing it into his 4x4x4, 64 cubic feet ring. It barely fit along the longest diagonal.

“Treasures, please?” said the Exchange rep, giving a polite smile.

The rep, a guy with glasses in his mid-twenties who Alex was told was also an F-rank Awakened, seemed unfazed as Alex placed a giant, sparkling icy blade on the counter; he probably sees this all day.

Alex proceeded to deposit the rest of the ring’s contents, including several light-blue shards, six mana-conductive steel ingots, six essence stones, a bracer, a circlet, a series of rift creature drops including slime-balls, several Iceling eggs, Ice Warg fangs, and, of course, a Skill Shard.

“Wow,” said the rep, laughing a bit. “Was this all a single rift? I’ll get all this appraised and priced, please wait here.”

After half an hour, he returned with an itemized list priced at market rates.

Reflect Skill Shard  — Common — 11 Credits.

Which is like, $15,000, by itself... It felt a little surreal.

The glacier sword, was an Uncommon rarity item, and went for 5 Credits—seems like price and rarity grade are related, but price also depends a lot on the item type.

Another revelation, the strange blue shards he’d picked up in the rift were Awakening Stone shards, like he’d assumed, and went for 2 Credits. The six essence stones were of course a Credit each. The rest of the items were all individually worth less than a Credit.

All in all, the items added up to a tidy 26.5 Credits.

Which kind of shocked him; he wasn’t sure what he’d been expecting, but this was, what, $37,000-ish? He couldn’t seem to stop converting everything into prices he was familiar with, because earning that in a morning felt absurd.

Alex was grinning; fuck yeah.

And this is at F-rank?

He knew Awakened were rich, but—why aren’t even the F-ranks insanely rich? I mean, this is…

Well—okay. You can’t delve that often, you’d have to rest at least a day, probably, between delves, for willpower recovery.

Of course you also have to split it 5 ways if you’re in a party, he recalled. And then, they can also cost quite a bit to purchase. And the treasure figures you get are pretty varied per-rift, I think, like this was a lucky rift. And you can lose money on certain rifts.

Today’s rift essentially side-stepped almost all the costs and resource-splitting it’d take to run a rift. Realistically, 26.5 Credits is going to be a pretty big outlier for single-rift profits for a little while.

Getting his gear choices right felt all the more important.

Alex’s items were also changed, in that they were all nicely identifiable. But they didn’t tell Alex much that was new.

Finesteel Bracers [Common]

Bracers forged of Finesteel, rift-originated. This item is mana conductive and decently resistant to foreign mana. Requirements: Level 3

Emerald Circlet [Common]

A circlet that grants a small boost to the wielder’s Intelligence, which primarily affects processing speed and the aspects of willpower related to Skill usage.

+3 Intelligence

These two he decided he’d keep; they weren’t included in the final profit calculation.

“Okay,” said Alex, taking a breath. Ideas were swirling around in his mind. “I can try gear on before buying it, right?” Surely you can?

“Certainly, we have trial rooms for precisely that reason. I will be required to accompany you, however. It’s association policy.”

“That’s fine,” said Alex. “How many items can I trial at once?”

“Well,” said the rep, hesitating. “Forty, at maximum—it’s a security issue. But we usually narrow the focus before we select items to trial.”

“Here’s the thing,” said Alex. “It’s my first time buying gear, and I’m missing a lot of gear.”

“Ah.” The rep smiled. It seemed they were on a wavelength. “You’ll want to see how all the various new pieces are, as a set.”

“Yes, but also—I’m unsure what I want. Which is why I’d want a little variety.”

“Which pieces, might I ask?” the rep tilted his head. “Weapon, ring?”

“Chestpiece, leggings, feet, a pendant, a ring, and a mana-conductive sword,” Alex listed. “A few kinds of each.”

He wasn’t sure if he was asking a bit much, but the rep simply laughed. “We’ll get you a bigger ring.”

***

What followed was an hour in which Alex felt flooded with possibilities.

Ten gleaming rings were presented to Alex—steel, gold, tempered silver, woven steel threads, glass with mana flowing slowly within, or laden with gemstones—after a few indecisive minutes Alex selected a lapis-studded ring, a ring with a blood-red ruby, and the glass ring for trial. The rep was graciously patient, and quite professional.

The 11th floor of the Exchange held rings, pendants, and swords, and the rep would bring out around ten of each, of which Alex would narrow to three-ish. Narrowing down swords was especially agonizing; Alex went with five to trial. Afterward they went to the 12th floor, where Alex selected the rest of his potential gear.

As they went, Alex saw a few Guilders in bodysuits with the O2 symbol, the cloud in a circle, as well as a few crossed swords symbols of the Institute, two Guilders with the ArchKnights’ shield, along with dozens of logos of boutique Guilds he wasn’t as familiar with. I thought Guilds provided gear? I guess you might be here spending pocket money, getting an upgrade over a Guild item.

Or maybe they’re just here selling?

Alex glanced around, curious. The folks here still mostly seem to be independents.

Bits of relaxed conversation floated around; topics included this weekend’s upcoming 5v5 Paris World Cup, featuring all the best Guilds; the recent appearance of two 3-star D-rank rifts somewhere in the mid-Atlantic—“It’s 167 3-stars this year, we’re in June and we’re already at last year’s count,” an O2 Guilder was gushing—as well as an upcoming auction by the luxury auction house Exeter’s in New York City, which included among its items an instance of an incredibly rare ‘Blink’ Skill Shard, one of less than 50 in circulation.

It piqued Alex’s interest; Blink allowed for short-range teleportation at a high mana price. It was also maybe the Skill Shard Alex wanted most—at least, he thought it was absolutely sick after seeing it in action on the dueling circuit.

But if a decent Class Card’s already this far out of reach… Blink was an instance tower item, a reward for the completion of a specific high-tier instance dungeon, and a rare reward at that. He winced just imagining the price; it’s not even a dream at this point.

Let’s focus on what I have.

He was plenty excited about several of the items in this increasingly full trial ring—and especially his vision of how he wanted to execute his gear setup.

They walked into a well-lit, all-white hallway, with doors almost seemed carved into the walls; the aesthetic was pretty similar to the v-dueling stations below.

“If that’s all,” said the rep, giving him the ring. “I’ll be waiting. You have an hour. Best of luck!”

“Thank you.”

The rep opened the door by injecting a little mana, which closed behind Alex as he stepped in. A stopwatch on the wall was already counting down—59:52.

Alex went straight into v-space, which featured a single standard v-station, and tapped his ring.

And a wealth of treasures splashed out in front of him.

Gleaming rings, pendants, swords, chestplates, leggings, a shield and a helm, even, several enchanted and gem-studded—Alex felt a grin across his face.

Let’s play around a little, shall we?

What Alex wanted, at least at first, was very simple.

First, he bonded a teardrop azure pendant, which featured +4 Wis, as well as a minor enchantment which slightly increased the effect of the int stat. What followed was a lapis-studded ring, for +3 Wis.

After which he added the Manasilk Gossamer Robes, a set of extremely thin, very pleasant-feeling silken robes, which from the description was spun from the silk of a certain ‘Manasilk Spider,’ and soaked in mana-dense liquids. +5 Wis, +5 Int.

A certain theme’s starting to emerge, mused Alex.

He wasn’t finished, since next up were Acolyte’s Slacks, featuring a nice +4 Int, +2 Wis.

And finally Dew Lapis Slippers, +3 Wis.

He felt a sudden sense of clarity as he bonded every item, a feeling like his soul was buoyed with new energies; the sensations quickly faded.

Alex glanced up.

Above him, two Mana Spears formed—full-on Mana Spears, with Swordforce slicked at the tips, and Force mana held at the ends.

It really isn’t that bad, he thought wonderingly. It was near his limit prior to all this shameless stat stacking.

A third, and he was starting to feel resistance.

Four spears at once was significantly harder; Alex didn’t try five.

Still—he was thinking about the upcoming Cup, which was all one-versus-ones; four of these Rare rarity spears at once, against a single enemy?

Could be pretty freaking nasty.

“Okay,” he said, laughing. “I can’t.”

He realized this was kind of ridiculous—the items offered almost zero protection. He could feel the floor through the slippers; admittedly quite comfortable slippers.

I also look kind of ridiculous in all this, but that’s by the by.

His core hypothesis felt pretty reasonable. Since Alex was so mana construct-dependent, playing to his strengths only made sense, right? And with his new Force augmentations, he could kind of subsidizing his lack of physical stats with mana as well.

Except, it was also the case that Force augmentations were like a % increase to a physique; he couldn’t just neglect his physique. At a certain point, the mana reservation Force augmentations required would also get onerous, restricting his pool of mana for the rest of his Skills.

Certain physique passives also required high physical stats; there were various balancing reasons why only going for mana wasn’t recommended.

But prioritizing it pretty heavily, especially since he did have this Force-body subsidy, seemed fine? Unless I’m missing something?

From his perspective more mana plain felt better, but he knew Warrior-types weren’t primarily Wis- and Int-users. He was pretty certain Warrior-specific Passive Skills scaled efficacy off of Constitution and Strength as well, facilitated by mana—which was fine for them, but it wasn’t really relevant to his situation.

Okay, regardless, the core idea of int/wis-stacking might work for me? Well, I like it, anyway, but this getup specifically...

It felt so glass-cannon it was kind of hilarious.

Then again, he’d been fine as a glass cannon all rift, but he’d also had to fight pretty cautiously that entire time. And he’d used a lot of mana on shielding minor attacks. Surely there’s a balance here?

But while I’m here, and as offensively maxed-out as I can be…

Alex walked over to where he’d scattered five swords on the v-station grounds.

These swords were quite special, in that they were by far the most expensive items in the ring.

Every single one was mana-conductive.

The least expensive was a glassy onyx gladius, at 8 Credits. The most expensive, at 12 Credits was the only unenchanted blade he’d chosen.

Unlike the other blades, this one was quite simple; it was a gleaming silver blade, five feet long, and unlike the rest it didn’t have any selling points. There was a single reason it was this expensive.

Its material was supposed to be very mana-conductive, and Alex could feel its shine as he picked it up.

This entire time, I was really supposed to be a spellsword.

He bonded it. He channeled a little mana in, as with the bracer, and was surprised how easily the sword received his mana; the blade seemed to hum with the Swordforce, within—and he realized it could take a lot more than if it were merely on the surface of the blade.

So Alex poured mana in, second after second, to the brim, until he could see the fast-flowing Force cresting the edge of the blade. An almost fifteen-second process.

And then, curiously, he slashed.

He saw the arc that emerged from the sword.

A second.

“Holy fucking shit,” he said, blinking down at the sword.

Is this what I’ve been missing?!

What the fuck?
