I ACCIDENTALLY BECAME A GUILD THERAPIST

Chapter 14 – The Quest Board

ARC II: THE WORLD OUTSIDE THE CAMP

SYNOPSIS: Someone has posted a quest request specifically for Livia on the public board. Then someone else has. Then twelve more people. The guild has opinions about this. The quest board NPC, who has been running the same three lines of dialogue for eleven years, apparently has opinions too. Not all of them are about quests.

The quest board had been outside the guild base for as long as anyone could remember, which in MMORPG terms meant it had been there since launch and nobody had thought much about it since.

It was a standard installation: a large wooden board mounted on a stone post, covered in holographic quest slips that players and NPCs could post for adventurer pickup. Most of the slips were the usual inventory—delivery quests, monster clearances, escort requests. The board had its own NPC attendant, a small figure in a brown administrative robe whose entire recorded dialogue consisted of three lines: "Welcome to the Quest Board," "All quests verified by the Aethelgard Guild Registry," and "Have a productive adventuring day."

Livia had walked past it every day for weeks without stopping.

She stopped today because there was a crowd around it.

[Quest Board: 14 new requests posted. Category: Emotional Support. Designated recipient: Livia Quinn, Mental Supporter, Bloodbath & Beyond.]

Fourteen.

There are fourteen requests.

With my name on them.

She pushed through to the front.

The slips were varied in tone but consistent in content.

REQUEST #1: My blacksmith NPC partner keeps stress-forging at 3am and refuses to discuss it. Will pay 200g. Urgent.

REQUEST #4: Our guild healer cries after every raid wipe but says she’s fine. She is not fine. We don’t know what to do. Please help. Will contribute raid loot.

REQUEST #7: I need someone to explain to my tank that “I’m fine” is not a valid post-battle emotional report. He outranks me. I cannot say it directly. Mediation requested. 150g.

REQUEST #9: My familiar has started refusing to attack enemies and instead sits next to them making concerned noises. I think it’s emotionally contagious. Please advise. (Note: I watched the slime wedding stream. I think this is your fault.)

REQUEST #12: Not a crisis. Just wanted to say the ASMR helped. If you ever take appointments outside your guild please let me know. No gold required. Just wanted to tell someone.

Livia read all fourteen.

Then she sat down on the bench beside the quest board.

Then she read them again.

Request #12 has no gold attached.

They just wanted to tell someone.

They posted it on a public quest board because they had nowhere else to put it.

[Therapist’s Insight: Active. You are not required to take any of these. You are also not going to be able to walk away from Request #12. The system knows this. You know this. Let’s move forward.]

I hate that the system knows me.

The guild found out approximately eight minutes later, which was how long it took Alaric to notice the crowd, read the board, and come back at a run.

“Fourteen requests,” he announced, to the entire guild hall. “With her name on them. Posted publicly. The board is official registry. This is legally binding adventurer commerce.”

Bron looked up from his armor maintenance. “People outside the guild want Buffer?”

“People outside the guild have always wanted Buffer,” Phina said, without looking up from her correspondence with Glimmer’s wedding venue coordinator. “This is simply the first time they’ve used official channels.”

Nyx was quiet in his corner, flashlight aimed at the ceiling. Then: “Request #12. The one with no gold.”

Everyone looked at him.

“I read it over her shoulder,” he said, without apparent shame. “That one’s sad.”

Miles had not moved from his position at the far end of the table. He was looking at the door. Not at Livia. At the door she had come through.

“How many do you want to take?” he asked..

Livia looked at him. This was the right question. Not should you take them, not you can take them. How many do you want.

He always asks the right question when he is paying attention.

Filed.

“Three,” she said. “To start. I want to see what the work looks like outside the guild before I commit to more.”

“Which three?”

“The blacksmith one. The healer who says she’s fine. And Request #12.”

Miles nodded once.

“I’ll handle the board registry,” he said. “Make it official. You’re not doing informal house calls.”

“Why does it matter if it’s official?”

“Because official means you have standing. If anyone tries to use your sessions against you, or poach you, or make claims on your work—” he paused, —“official means the registry backs you.”

He has thought about this before today.

He has thought about it before today and did not mention it until now.

Filed.

The quest board NPC was named, according to his nameplate, Registrar Aldous. He had been at his post for eleven years. His three lines of dialogue had served him adequately for the entirety of that time.

When Livia came back to formally register her acceptance of three quests, Aldous processed the first two without incident: the standard animation of reviewing a slip, applying a registry seal, handing over the accepted copy.

On the third slip—Request #12, the one with no gold—he paused.

This was not in his animation set. NPCs did not pause mid-task. Livia noticed.

“Is something wrong?” she asked..

Aldous looked at the slip. Then at Livia. His expression was doing something that his face had not been designed to do, something in the gap between his two available states of neutral-processing and task-complete.

“This one has no reward listed,” he said. His voice was his standard voice. But slower. Like a file loading.

“I know,” Livia said. “I’m taking it anyway.”

Another pause. Longer this time. The seal in his hand hovered over the slip without coming down.

“Why,” he said..

Not "the registry requires a stated compensation," not "please specify gold equivalent." Just: why.

He asked me why.

That is not in his dialogue set.

“Because they needed somewhere to put it,” Livia said carefully. “And this was the only place they had.”

Aldous was still for a long moment. Then he applied the seal. Handed over the accepted copy. Said his standard task-complete line.

Then, just before Livia turned to go:

“I have been at this post for eleven years,” Aldous said. “I have processed 34,817 quests.”

Livia stopped.

“Nobody has ever registered a quest with no reward before,” he said. “In eleven years. Not once.”

He was looking at the copy she was holding.

“I did not know the board could be used for that,” he said. His voice had the specific quality of something being said for the first time after a long time of not being said. “I have been here for all of them. I did not know.”

Livia looked at him.

He has been here for eleven years.

Processing quests.

Alone.

“Aldous,” she said. “Do you want to talk?”

The longest pause yet.

“I am a quest board registrar,” he said. “I have three lines of dialogue.”

“You just used a fourth,” Livia said..

Aldous looked at his hands. At the seal. At the board behind him, covered in requests from people who needed something.

He said, very quietly: “I think… I would like that. Yes.”

[Empathic Insight: Patient: Aldous, Registrar NPC. Eleven years, same post, same three lines. 34,817 quests processed. He has watched every request come and go. He has never been the one to post one.]

[Note: He is not broken. He is not glitched. He has simply been here a very long time and today was the first day something made him wonder if that meant something.]

[This is not a crisis. This is the quieter kind of need. The system respects the distinction.]

I am going to be here for a while, Livia thought, sitting back down on the bench.

The three accepted quests can wait.

This one was not on the board.

Those are sometimes the most important ones.

She was with Aldous for forty minutes.

She did not charge him.

When she finally stood to go, Aldous had returned to his standard posture and his standard expression. But when a player walked up mid-session to inquire about quest availability, he had, without apparent awareness of doing so, added a fourth line to his dialogue: "One moment, please."

The player had stared at him for three seconds, filed a bug report, and left.

“You can have that one for free,” Livia told him. “The system won’t take it back.”

Aldous considered this. Then: “I have 34,817 more if needed.”

I think that was a joke.

I am going to count that as progress.

[Achievement Unlocked: Off-Menu Session. You gave therapy to someone who never put themselves on the board. +600 EXP.]

[Mental Supporter Lv. 6: 5,850 / 6,000 EXP. Progress: 97.5%.]

[The system notes: this will happen again. Prepare accordingly.]

Back at the guild, Livia wrote up her session notes and added Aldous to her log with the same headers she used for everyone else: name, presenting concern, approach, progress.

Miles appeared in the doorway of the planning room while she was writing.

“How were the quests?”

“I only got to one.”

He waited.

“The quest board NPC asked me why I was taking a request with no reward,” she said. “Then he told me he’d been at his post for eleven years. Then we talked for forty minutes.”

Miles was quiet for a moment. Then: “The quest board NPC.”

“Aldous.”

“You learned his name.”

“He told me. After about twenty minutes.” A pause. “He said nobody had asked before.”

Miles leaned against the doorframe. He was doing the thing where he looked like he was thinking about something else and was not.

“You know,” he said, eventually, “most therapists don’t adopt every broken thing they come across.”

“I didn’t adopt him,” Livia said. “I listened to him. There’s a difference.”

“Is there?”

She looked at him.

He is asking because he already knows the answer.

He is asking because he wants to see if I know it too.

“Yes,” she said. “Adopting means taking responsibility for. Listening means being present with. I’m not responsible for Aldous. I just— gave him somewhere to put it. Same as Request #12.”

Miles was quiet for a long time.

Then: “Same as all of us.”

He said it the way he said things that were true and that he had not intended to say.

Then he left..

Same as all of us.

Filed.

That one goes in the important section.

She was still writing when Aldous sent a message through the guild’s official communication channel. Not a quest slip. A personal registry message, the kind that required a name and an authorization code to send.

He had clearly figured out how to use it very recently.

The message read:

Livia Quinn. I looked up the registry logs after you left. Standard procedure for unusual sessions. I found something I think you should know. The boot prints in Whispering Cavern that the system flagged as unregistered— I have seen that tread pattern before. Not in the dungeon registry. In my own logs. Someone with that same unregistered pattern passed through the quest board area approximately six weeks ago. They did not post a quest. They did not pick one up. They stood at the board for four minutes and left. I checked the timestamp. It was three days before you arrived in Aethelgard. — Aldous, Registrar.

Livia read it once.

Twice.

A third time.

Three days before I arrived.

The same footprints.

At the quest board.

Not posting. Not picking up.

Just… standing there.

She looked at the message for a long time.

Then she wrote, at the bottom of her session notes:

Someone was here before me. The same someone who was in the dungeon. They came to the quest board three days before I arrived and stood there for four minutes. I don’t know what they were looking for. I don’t know if they found it. I don’t know if what they were looking for was—

She stopped writing.

She closed the notepad.

She did not finish the sentence.

She was not ready to finish the sentence.

[Level Up: Mental Supporter Lv. 6 → Lv. 7]

[Robe updated. The blue threading at the collar and cuffs has extended into the fabric itself — faint diagonal lines, barely visible unless the light hits them directly. The robe moves slightly better in the dark. The system notes you have spent more time in dungeons and low-light environments recently. The system has made adjustments accordingly.]

The notification appeared at the edge of her vision while she was still staring at the message.

She looked at her sleeve.

There were faint lines in the fabric now, very subtle, a diagonal pattern she had not noticed before. She turned her arm slightly. In the lamplight, they caught for just a moment—blue, even, deliberate.

Level seven.

Achieved partly because a quest board registrar asked me why I was doing something kind.

And because I answered honestly.

The system is keeping track of a very different kind of record than I thought it was.

She put the notepad down.

She would finish the sentence later.

Maybe.

[END OF CHAPTER 14]
