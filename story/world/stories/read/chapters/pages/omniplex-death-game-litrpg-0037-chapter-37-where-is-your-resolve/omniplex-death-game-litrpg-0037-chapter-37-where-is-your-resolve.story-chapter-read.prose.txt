Luka let out a groan as he slowly got up from the ground. He looked around in panic, his immediate surroundings hadn’t changed, but further in the distance lay some sort of wall. A translucent blue of energy that flickered slightly, even the long shadow it cast trembled.

What the hell did that bastard do? Luka shook his head.

“Ivo, Birk! Where are you?” he shouted.

“We’re here,” a voice replied weakly to his side.

He turned to find the twins sprawled on top of each other, rocks and sediments decorating their long blonde hair. Luka pulled Birk off of his brother. “We need to move closer to that blue wall.”

Ivo got up from the ground, picking out pieces of rock from his hair. “Looks more like a dome.” He craned his neck, looking towards the blue mass.

“It doesn’t matter.” Luka clenched his fists. His knuckle dusters sparked with electricity, responding to his hunger for battle. “We need to make sure Ciro survives.” He ran forward, heading towards the area where he was blown back.

“He’ll be fine. I don’t know why you’re worried. He’s Ciro, the dude is seriously strong,” said Birk, running close behind, his skin red and splotchy from trying to keep up with the arduous physical work.

Luka knew he was right. Ciro was strong and capable. He needed to have faith, but that duel he accepted wasn’t the problem. It was the rest of those gray alien bastards. I killed one before. I can kill a hundred more. His eyes lit up with rage. As he ran towards the dome, he didn’t know what compelled him to do it, but he threw a deadly punch at the wall. Electricity sparked and trailed from the sheer force his fist flew at.

There was no sound. Not even a returning force. The wall absorbed his blow, the electrical residue from his knuckles dissipating into the air.

“Uh boss, why did you do that?” asked Birk, both twins looking at him, confused.

“I’m just frustrated.”

He looked to the wall; he brought his hand, a palm full of calluses and scars, and placed it against the wall. A slight prickling sensation tickled his hand. The wall quivered under his touch. “You better win Ciro dammit! Because once you’re out, I’ll be the one to kill you.”

“Boss look!”

Ivo tapped his shoulder furiously. Luka turned. Ivo pointed to the side of the dome. The group of Xyphon’s staring at the wall intently. They stood in silence. Waiting.

Luka snapped his attention to the child that stood by their side. The kid’s legs were wobbling and bruises covered his body. There was no time to worry about Ciro. He needed to save that boy. “Let’s go!” he bellowed, urging the twins to follow behind him.

The twins were unarmed. It would be a difficult battle without Ciro, but Luka was going to come out on top. He had never once lost a fight. And he planned on keeping that way.

The aliens noticed their presence and shifted their attention to them, raising their spears in response. Luka and the twins halted their advance. Both parties eyed each other, daring the other to make the first move.

A Xyphon was the first to break the silence. “What’s the matter, Earthlings, worried that your man will lose the Hakasha?”

“We’re not worried.” Ivo stared back at the aliens, his lips curling and fists clenched. “It’s your boy who should be worried.”

The Xyphon’s laughed amongst themselves. “You think that pathetic creature can beat Tenryubis? You’re truly mad. After Kaisu and Muriel, he is the strongest of our cohort.”

“Wanna bet?” Birk piped up, lifting his head up high.

The Xyphon’s looked towards each other, sharing sarcastic smiles. “If you’re so confident, why do you face us now?”

The Xyphon holding the chain to the child, pointed a finger at them. “Go back and wait patiently. We’ll fight once the Hakasha is complete.”

Luka spoke up finally, sick of the back and forth. “We all know that’s not going to be fair.” He pointed towards the dome. “Only one person is coming out of that thing alive. You already have the numbers advantage.”

Luka titled his head up to the sky, shaking his head. “I don’t understand why you’re spewing all this shit about honor this and honor that. You’ve been crowding together, clinging onto your strength in numbers.”

“You’re all weak.” Luka spat out.

The Xyphons roared in anger, stepping forward and pointing their spears at him. Luka didn’t flinch. He pounded his fists together; the metal creating sparks of electricity. “I’ll take you all on!”

His words didn’t leave the effect he was hoping for. The Xyphons instead were focused behind him, looking at the dome. Luka furrowed his brow.

“Shit,” hissed Ivo. “Do you see that behind us, boss?”

Luka turned his head to see the dome. The blue walls were moving, tightening, and shrinking. “What the hell is going on?”

“It has begun. Your friend’s death is imminent.”

Luka turned to see the sinister smiles of the Xyphon’s. He scoffed, “you really think he’ll live, huh? He’s going to die, but you won’t be alive long enough to see it.”

Within a single stride, his muscles exploding with power, he broke the distance that separated him from the aliens. The bastards didn’t see it coming. He threw out both his arms, clotheslining all six of the Xyphons. His biceps rammed into each of their necks, sending them careening away.

“Boss, you’re gonna hurt the kid!”

Luka grunted, biting his lip. He realized the kid got sent flying back too. With the aliens slowly getting back up, he dashed towards the kid, who rolled on the ground in pain. He yanked him by the collar, throwing him towards the twins.

“Keep him safe!”

“Got him!” The twins both yelled.

The Xyphons circled around Luka tightly, their spears only inches away from meeting his flesh.

“You’ll regret doing that!” One of them shrieked.

“I highly doubt that.” He clenched his knuckle dusters tightly, electricity exploded across his fist in response. The Xyphon’s didn’t need another warning. They thrust their spears at the same time, trying to impale him from all angles.

With a sweeping hook, his fist whistled, meeting each pointed tip. His knuckles burned through each of the spears. The sound of metal snapping reverberated through the broken city.

Not letting up, his open palm planted into the ground with a heavy thud, his body tilting its weight onto it, with a push of his feet he generated momentum, movement coiled by lightning, he swept his enemies legs, some dodged, some didn’t, as the kick finished its full circle, he flipped onto his other side, and with the weight of all of his torso, he cracked his other fist into the ground, sending out a rippling bubble of electric webs that scorched and stunned all that surrounded him

With the space opening, Luka lunged towards one of the Xyphon’s on the floor. He struck his neck, a sledgehammer blow. The Xyphon desperately punched at him, but he didn’t yield. He twisted his fist, the grooves of his knuckle dusters grinding deep into his skin, puncturing the alien’s airways.

The Xyphon’s last breaths were drowned out by Luka’s yell, fueled by pure adrenaline. He felt arms clamping onto his body as Xyphon wrapped their arms around his shoulders, trying to pull him away.

“Get off me.” Luka grabbed the Xyphon’s arm, releasing his grip on him, before throwing him over his shoulder. But just like the tide, the Xyphon’s came back. Two more charged at him from the front. He kept his stance loose, keeping on his toes. As the two Xyphon’s advanced, he felt someone sneaking in from behind. He quickly pivoted, twisting to the side.

His three attackers bumped into each other, collapsing in a heap. The Xyphon he had thrown returned. He tried helping them up.

In that split second, Luka lifted his fist, charging as much electricity as possible, before pounding his knuckles into the ground. The electricity traveled along the wet soil, snaking towards the three aliens. Just as he thought the electricity would disperse, it struck the four Xyphon’s. They screamed in pain, their skeletons pulsating from the electrocution.

Their bodies convulsed, froth building at their mouths. Once the attack had subsided, one of them collapsed onto their ground. Their gray skin charred and burned. The smell of bleach wafted through the air.

The four Xyphon’s stood before Luka, stunned. The sound of thunder raged and a melancholic rainfall followed. Luka slowly walked towards them. Tears dripped down the faces of one of them.

“So you can cry? That’s the funniest shit,” Luka chucked, but there was no smile on his face, only cold resolve.

The alien’s eyes shook at his words, taking glances at the bodies of his dead friends. His legs quivered in fear. “You won’t live for much longer!” he shrieked. “You’ll see! Once Tenryubis is released from his Hakasha, you’ll pay for what you’ve done.”

With delirious eyes, he charged at Luka.

“You weak, pathetic hum—”

Before he could blabber on, Luka placed his hand over his mouth, clamping on his jaw. He lifted the Xyphon off the ground. He scratched and clawed at his hand, but Luka simply stared at him without any care. Ignoring the alien in his hand, he peered at the other three, who stood there in silence. Was it awe or fear on their faces? Luka couldn’t quite tell.

“Watch this,” he called out to them. He lifted the Xyphon higher into the air, adjusting his grip, holding head by the side. He probed his thumb into his mouth, holding onto his lower jaw. As he lifted his other fist, he could sense the aliens knew what he was going to do.

He stared at them, daring them to make a move. He lifted his fist, electricity sparking alive, dancing between his knuckles.

The alien shrieked, a gurgling, high-pitched warble.

Luka ignored it. He brought his fist down. No hesitation. No mercy.

His knuckle dusters planted into the Xyphon’s skull. Like glass breaking, his skull cracked, folding inward with a wet crunch. His fist rose once, then again, and then again.

Each impact wetter than the last, until the bone collapsed.

His shrieks died mid-sound.

Luka continued his barrage.

The fourth punch split the eye socket.

The fifth caved in the crown.

The sixth tore through the jaw completely, tearing it from the throat.

By the time he was done, there was no head left, just a waterfall of green blood, shattered bone and twitching tendons dripping down Luka’s fingers.

His grip loosened, the corpse fell onto the wet soil with a splatter, his feet twitching, before going still.

Luka stood there unphased. His hands trembled only slightly, both of them dripping with alien blood down to his wrists. He looked towards the other Xyphon’s his eyes wild, hidden beneath streaks of blood and gore.

“Who’s next?”

To his frustration, he didn’t get a reaction from them. Not even fighting words. Instead, they were more focused on something behind him.

What is it now? thought Luka as he turned his head. His eyes widened at what lay ahead. The blue dome had become the size of a telephone booth. The rain completely subsided, and a ray of light showered onto the dome.

The twins and the young boy slowly stepped backwards towards

“Ciro…” Luka whispered under his breath.

The dome responded to his words. The blue light shook and trembled. Small cracks formed, growing like ink on a page. The ray of light radiated through the thick, distorted cracks. As if from a cocoon, something would emerge.

But was it friend or foe?

Neither the twins nor the Xyphons behind them spoke. They just watched and waited. For when the dome opened, the victor of the battle was already decided.

Finally, after what felt like hours, the dome shuddered under the weight of all the cracks that had formed. In a way, Luka thought it was beautiful, a design a human couldn’t even comprehend. And then, with a groan, the dome finally crumbled. The blue walls disintegrated into dust, shining particles rippled in the air before fading away.

And what lay inside left Luka’s breath stuck in his throat. Was he even the same man? With a single arm, he sat in a pool of two separate liquids. It must’ve been blood, if not for the way it merged and repelled each other, looking more like a sick science experiment than any known liquid. He sat with his knees bent, one of his legs bent in the opposite direction.

And in front of him lay a corpse.

The Xyphon.

Scars everywhere across its body. Stab wounds deep enough to kiss bone. By its neck, only a quarter of its flesh remained, just bones and cartilage poking out.

This wasn’t a fight or a duel. It was the finale of two prey trying desperately to survive.

When his shock subsided, a sense of relief washed over Luka, knowing Ciro had survived. Barely.

The Xyphons behind him let out audible gasps. “Tenryubis died!”

“Run. Retreat!”

The remaining Xyphon didn’t even bother to gather their dead, as they bolted away from them. Luka’s attacks rendering them slow and lethargic. Luka shook his head, not even bothering to chase. They were pathetic. His friend needed his help.

“Don’t.” A voice called out that made Luka’s body go cold. It was quiet, barely a whisper, but it had such a tone where you couldn’t ignore it.

His eyes met with Ciro’s. His eyes were as dark as the void itself, holding such intensity that it made even Luka nervous.

“Don’t let them escape.”

Luka clenched his fist, his heart pounding, as he turned to see the Xyphon’s still running. Why did he even think of showing them mercy? Was this all his resolve amounted to? Was Ciro stronger than him?

“Kill them all.”

“Kill every single one of those bastards!”

Ciro let out a scream. Within Luka’s core, he felt a rush of power.

With a final nod, he went to chase. For a demon had released him.
