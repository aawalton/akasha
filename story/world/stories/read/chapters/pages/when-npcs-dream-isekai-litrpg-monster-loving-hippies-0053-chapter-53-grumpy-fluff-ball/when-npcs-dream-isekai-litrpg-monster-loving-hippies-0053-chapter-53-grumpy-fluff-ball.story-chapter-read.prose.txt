We ran through the forest—hasty, fearful—like hunted animals.

That naked Nula: small, soft, pink. No claw, no horn. But when she roared, she felt like a demon; every segment of my carapace shivered.

I remember looking at Uul. It was the only time I’d ever seen him utterly, completely terrified—right there in his eyes. It felt good, in a small way, to see him scared. To shutter the image of his almighty confidence he tried so hard to portray.

Uul was also injured, and I didn’t like that. I never had great love for him — but seeing him bleeding, broken, in pain…It didn’t sit right with me.

We reached the forest clearing where we had left the wagon and the four-horned snakes. They were sprawled out, lazily asleep in the tall grass.

Freya immediately laid Uul down and climbed into the wagon, probably searching for bandages. I just stood there, useless.

I looked at injured Uul. With his wing broken he was even more vulnerable than usual; he’d have to be carried around like a baby. I could probably kill him quickly now, while Freya rummaged for bandages in the wagon.

If I did it, maybe I’d even be doing good—maybe saving the continent from an invading, mind-controlling species.

But I won’t kill him, no, I was on Uul’s side; I was his helper.

Why was I helping him? Because Uul had saved my life from those two assholes in town? Because I hoped for a reward when the controllers won? No. Those weren’t the reasons. I didn’t feel grateful to Uul—and, from the look of things today, his side’s chances of winning weren’t great anyway.

I was helping him because he scared me. I was helping him because I was a coward. It was that simple.

“What are you looking at?” Uul snapped at me. I averted my gaze.

Uul was typically calm, cold, and calculated. Not today. This was a bad day for Uul, and he didn’t take it well.

“I’m… I’m broken!” he said to no one in particular. “The plan, my mission… it’s broken too!”

“It’s that damn John Smith!” Uul raged. “All the continent was expecting my master invasion—maybe for months. And if—if you believe that Geshem naked Nula—well, we can’t build the airship without the Order of the Phoenix knowing about it. Hell, if that arrow-shooting demon subdues Geshem, in about five days his body will clear the control fungus and he’ll immediately contact the Order of the Phoenix—probably any other player groups he knows of—and give them the precise details of our plan!”

Freya then climbed out of the wagon.

She walk over to Uul, and began wrapping cloth around the bleeding wound in his side.

“That HURTS, dammit!” he shouted as she worked.

“It’s necessary,” Freya explained, continuing. “Now… the wing?”

“It’s broken,” Uul said. “But it’s cartilage. Just stretch it, splint it, wrap it in cloth. In a week, I may be able to fly again. Just… get me a piece of wood to bite before you do it. This… is going to hurt.”

She gave him the wood. He grabbed it with his tail and stuffed it in his mouth.

“Ready?”

“Jush doo ih ahwready!”

CRACK.

“AHHAA!” Uul screamed and spat the wood from his mouth as Freya tied a splint to the broken wing.

“That damn naked Nula demon!” Uul continued. “How could she find us? Why didn’t Geshem warn us about her? He… I don’t think he knew. He put himself in the line of fire to protect me—he even got injured himself. No… he was loyal.”

“Oh, I know who she is,” Freya said, smiling. “It must be Lori. I’ve heard of her—she’s Geshem’s girlfriend. She’s an NPC and a badass. I thought the stories were exaggerated, but apparently… not.”

“An NPC?” Uul asked. “How can that be? Her behavior—the magic—doesn’t fit at all. And we can’t control NPCs! That Lori is just another nail in my mission’s coffin!”

“Ah… if I may speak a thought?” I began, voice trembling. “Perhaps… perhaps this continent—grand as it is—does not harmonize with the Master’s grand plan. Maybe… maybe the vision lies elsewhere, and perhaps we might simply… remain here… in peace?”

Uul turned to look at me slowly. His face was blank.

“We will fulfill our mission,” he said softly. “We will control builder communities. We will kill. Or die. But we will find a way to bring my Masters here. And until then, dear Shooya, you may want to keep those… refined thoughts… to your damn self!”

“Yes Yes! You definitely shouldn’t give up!” a new, cheery voice said behind me.

I jumped, startled, and looked around for the source.

It was another Uul! Or another creepy flying fluff ball.

It hovered above us without our noticing. This one was a bluer shade of pink, and the system translated enough for me to understand she was a female, of whatever species Uul was from.

“Lovely to meet you!” she sang, circling above us. “My name is Zylil. And who might you be?”

“Stop buzzing around like a damn mosquito,” Uul growled.

“Ooooh, cranky, are we?” she smirked. Then turned to Freya and me.“Must be the broken wing? Or is he always like this?”

“We had a rough day,” Freya said brightly and offered a hand to shake Zylil’s tail. “I’m Freya, the shy bug girl is Shooya, and the grumpy fluff ball is Uul.”

“How did you find us?” Uul asked, ignoring the pleasantries.

“We have the ability to sense spores emitted by the fungus embedded in us by our master,” Zylil said. “You should’ve known that—it’s part of our master’s teaching.”

“I knew that!” he growled. “It’s just that I hadn’t sensed anyone.”

“Well, it’s not that easy,” she explained. “You need to look. I’ve been tracking you for some time.”

Then we heard a rattle in the plant thicket nearby. The three of us jumped, imagining Lori there to hunt us down. But it wasn’t her.

Instead, a huge zebra-like beast emerged with two bird-people riding on her back.

Two of our large horned snakes lifted their heads to peek at the new beast, but they seemed to sense no danger and went back to their lazy sprawl.

“These are my friends,” Zylil gestured at the bird people—“Nhilmesh and Vru’th”—then at us: “Shooya, Freya, and Uul.”

One of the birdfolk — the male — went to join Zylil. The other, Nhilmesh, remained by the zebra beast’s side.

“So why have you tracked me, Zylil?” Uul asked, showing no real interest in the newcomers.

“Because we need to work together,” Zylil said. “Or did you come to another conclusion?”

“Well, we did seem to have encountered a minor setback.” Started Uul, and then began to elaborate in a long, dismissive tone.

But I wasn’t listening. I was wholly focused on the huge, beautiful zebra beast—multiple horns, black-and-white stripes of fluffy fur. I wanted to pet her… badly.

I walked to it slowly and reached out my hand.

“Can I?” I asked the bird girl who stand by the beast.

“Sure,” she answered smiling. “I named her Liuasha, She’s a good girl.”

Liuasha sniffed my hand with its huge nostrils — it tickled. I reached out and petted her striped, fluffy forehead.

Then she sniffed me all over — antenna, arms, legs — and finally gave me a long, wet lick across the face.

“Looks like she’s really taken to you,” Nhilmesh said.

“I… like her too,” I said. “But… she is… um—”

“You mean controlled?” Nhilmesh asked. “Well, yes. She’s calmer because of it, but she’s usually suspicious of strangers. Not with you, though. Somehow you made her trust you.”

“Th—thank you,” I mumbled. “That’s… kind of you to say so.”

“Can I ask you something?” Nhilmesh asked.

“Sure.”

“Zylil explained to us that they can’t control your kind,” she said, lowering her voice. “So why… are you, you know… helping him?”

I lowered my head. It was something I should feel ashamed about—and I really did. Freya and the bird people served the controller species because they had to. But me? I had no excuse.

“It’s because… I’m a coward,” I said, small and simple. I trembled a little when I said it. It felt awful.

Nhilmesh touched my cheek gently, brushing her soft, bright-green fur against my carapace. “Don’t think badly of yourself,” she said softly. “You’re in a difficult situation. This fluff ball is a powerful being, with or without control spores. Looking at you, I don’t think you’re a coward. You’ll find your strength and bravery, I’m sure of it.”

I started to cry softly. This was probably the nicest thing anyone had ever told me.

“Thank you,” I mumbled.

“Listen,” she whispered, lowering her voice even more. “I’ve heard from a good source that, despite the rumors, the Stormcrows are kind. Fair. Accept anyone, no matter your race — as long as you’re not violent. This could be your chance, Shooya. When we start marching in line, hang back. Take the rear. And when no one’s looking… slip away. Head to the Stormcrows.Uul won’t be able to touch you there.This is your chance to be free.”

I froze, slowly digesting what Nhilmesh had just said.

“But how…” I asked. “How can you suggest this? Your… control?”

“Oh, it’s fine,” she said casually. “Zylil’s control leaves a lot of autonomy. And anyway, it’s not like I’m betraying my boss. Just annoying Uul. And honestly? From what I’ve seen, he’s kind of a jerk.”

“He… is,” I said. “Thank you.”

The problem with Nhilmesh’s idea was that it required courage. Not much, granted, but courage was in very short supply for me.

Another issue: I wasn’t sure I wanted to leave. I liked Freya a lot, and even with Uul, I felt part of some group. Irrational, maybe, but still a tie. And Lori—if she thought I was her enemy, she could have me arrested or put an arrow through my carapace.

So I didn’t know what to do. I would probably do nothing; indecision and cowardice had me frozen.

After some time it looked like Zylil and Uul finished their arguing. “Shooya, Freya, come here,” Uul ordered. We walked over.

“Listen,” he began, “this is what’s going to happen: Zylil is part of a group of six agents of my species, working together. And although I have many… reservations about the way they operate, it appears that cooperation is necessary…”

He sighed, as if the entire situation offended him.

“The construction of an airship is proving to be a far greater endeavor than I originally estimated.And the naked Nula — they are more united, more knowledgeable, and considerably more dangerous than anticipated.”

He turned to look at each of us in turn.

“Though the bird people are inferior builders and less resourceful, they hold political advantages.The two bird kingdoms are locked in war — and not just among NPCs. The players who arrived here brought their old world factions and hatreds with them.The Order of the Phoenix is aligned with the southern bird kingdom. They hold little sway in the north.So the plan is simple: we will try to make contact with the twenty or so agents that arrived here, combine our resources, and effectively control the northern bird kingdom—Sin-Irsh.

We’ll use their war to our advantage, manipulate politics, and maintain armed forces with local NPCs to deter the Order, all while constructing the airships. Two of Zylil’s allies are already there and have taken control of two small towns.”

“Will we get the amount of cloth we need for the airship envelope?” Freya asked.

“With the combine resources of the kingdom, maybe” Uul said. “. Zylil also thinks we should manage trade with the bug kingdom in the north to hasten the process.”

“So basically we need to control Sin-Irsh,” Freya said, “and hold it just long enough to build a ship.”

“Yes,” Uul said. “After I updated Zylil with Geshem manufacture estimation, we think we need one month of production—once Sin-Irsh is fully secured.”

It seemed Uul had finished explaining the plan, and it looked like we were about to head out.

“What about Geshem?” I ask. Lori had him, but he was still under Uul’s control.

“There’s a chance he escaped that demon NPC,” Uul said. “He’s smart—maybe he got away and is searching for us. But if we wait for him, there’s an equal chance Lori or more Stormcrow soldiers will find us. As much as it hurts to lose Geshem, we can’t stay here.”

I liked Geshem. He was soft and cute. A clever naked Nula. Even Uul respected him.

I was sad to lose him. But also... happy, in a way.He was important. The acting leader of the Stormcrows. A powerful mage.It was better if he was with the good guys. Yes. I was rooting for the other team.

“Get ready to move,” Uul instructed. “Freya, prepare the wagon and arrange a comfortable seat for me. Shooya, prepare the snakes for the ride. We’ll join Zylil and head straight to Sin-Irsh.”

I went over to the snakes — Nilgulini and Tingulini were still stretched out lazily in the grass.

They’d be pulling wagon. The other two — Nulgi and Tulgi — would  take the next shift.

I tightened the leather straps around their collars. They let me handle them calmly, smart and gentle creatures as they were. I guided them into position without Uul needing to say any controller command.

Zylil’s party began moving ahead.

Freya set Uul into the cushioned corner of the wagon and climbed in beside him.

I preferred to walk beside the wagon as much as I could. The road wasn’t smooth enough for speed anyway, and I disliked the jostling.

I also liked the never-ending change of view and scenery as we crossed the land.

Shortly after we started, a small wind blew my way and my antennae picked up something.

What? Did you think those were just for show?

People and animals controlled by Uul or Zylil gave off a distinct chemical trace — something fungal, maybe.Right now, I sensed that exact trace drifting from the forest nearby.

Someone controlled was out there. Close.

It could be another agent like Uul with animals or people—or it could be Geshem.

Most likely… Geshem.

He must’ve tricked Lori and escaped. And if he was close but silent, it probably meant she was nearby too — chasing him.

Uul was right. We had to move quickly. More Stormcrows could be on our trail.

But I was the only one who knew Geshem was here.

That meant... decision time.

I hated decisions.

If I just kept walking after the wagon, we’d put distance between us and Geshem.

Or maybe he’d pick up our trail and follow us.

I could tell Uul.

I could go get Geshem.

I could...

I hated this. I hated choosing.

What should I do? What did I want? I didn’t even know what the cowardly thing to do was anymore!

So I did nothing.

Literally. Nothing.

I stopped walking. Just stood there. Like an idiot.

Uul and Freya were in the wagon; they wouldn’t notice my absence for some time. I sighed. I felt like I should use my brain for such an important choice, but apparently I hadn’t brought it.

My antennae pinpointed the chemical trace faster now; It was easy tract him, he was really close.

I walked over to him.

“Oh, hi Shooya!” Geshem said brightly. “I was actually looking for you guys!”

“Hi…” I murmured. “Umm… Lori? Did you...?”

“What, hurt her?” he said, surprised. “No way! She’s my girlfriend. I love her. I just made her sleep.”

“Oh…”

“I just needed to come back to Uul,” Geshem said. “He’s my boss now, you know, with that controller stuff.” He gave a short chuckle, like it was funny. “Was Uul sent you to look for me? You know… I think Lori is searching for me… maybe she’s not far.”

“I will… take you…” I started.

He was close to me now. I saw his pink, soft cuteness. Part of his face was swollen and bruised—the result of a hard punch. He was soft and injured and impossibly cute.

So I punched him in the face.
