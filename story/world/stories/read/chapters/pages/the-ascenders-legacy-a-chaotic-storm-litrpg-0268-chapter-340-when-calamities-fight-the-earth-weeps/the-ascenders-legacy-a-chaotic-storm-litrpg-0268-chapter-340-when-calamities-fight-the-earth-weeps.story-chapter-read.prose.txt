Three hours was supposed to be a long time. At the very least, it should have been a while. Yet the last three hours had passed in a blur of armor fittings, more tactical briefings, a brief yet memorable visit from Zarxan, stolen glances at Nymera, and a trip to the war camp.

That was all that had happened in the last three hours, and Aodhán couldn't help but think they had missed a chunk of valuable time.

They had arrived at the viewing deck of the war camp about ten minutes ago, and while the battle renovations in the sect itself had been extensive, the war camp was a marvel of storm artillery and battle-ready disciples rushing to arrange themselves in formation, hundreds of thousands of Mythics painting the western valley in a myriad of colors.

The noise of war machines and barked orders filled the air, fear and anticipation so thick it was nearly tangible. But within the viewing deck, it was worse.

Several elders, including Elder Ghaltris, had gathered with them, their gazes fixed on an array of flickering screens showing the war. Aodhán and the other specials didn't stand with them. Instead, they stood facing one of the largest screens a fair distance away from the elders, their gazes intent and jaws tight with anticipation.

Aodhán himself watched the battlefield with a lump in his throat, aware that any minute now, their enemies would arrive on the eastern flank, murderous intent billowing from them in waves.

His mind raced with worry for the disciples on the frontlines, those who would take the heat of the first clash, and for those waiting to be deployed. Yet despite all he was seeing and feeling, Aodhán felt his gaze stray once more to Nymera.

She stood calmly by his side, the frills of her cloth armor billowing around her like smoke tendrils. Unlike Ashscar and Mirelle, her gaze was serene, as if she'd fought a million wars before.

And perhaps she had. He would never know if he didn't ask.

Exhaling softly, he was about to turn his gaze back to the screen when Nymera suddenly spoke. "Is there something you would like to say to me, Aodhán Brystion?"

Aodhán glanced back at her, surprised she'd noticed his staring. His gaze flicked to Ashscar and Mirelle, who were now staring at him, and he shook his head. "No. Not… this isn't the time or place."

She stared at him for a moment before shaking her head. "You're wrong, you know. This is exactly the right time and place. Didn't you hear Elder Ghaltris? We could die out there."

"Please, Mera." Mirelle raked her fingers through her hair, barely managing to hide a shudder. "No one's dying."

Nymera glanced at the double inheritor for a moment before nodding. "You're right. I shouldn't be negative." She glanced back at the screen, snow-white hair spilling down her shoulders like the purest of silk. Aodhán's breath hitched.

He glanced away immediately, his gaze meeting Ashscar's, who frowned in disapproval. Before Ashscar could say anything, though, Nymera did. "Stay alive, Aodhán. I have a lot of questions for you, and I can't ask them if you're not alive."

Aodhán shifted his gaze away from Ashscar and turned to face the screen. "I intend—"

The rest of his words were drowned out as an alarm suddenly blared, ripping through the air with an intensity that sent a violent shudder down his spine. The screens glitched once, twice, and then their enemies appeared—hundreds of thousands of them stretched across the eastern valley like a sea of killing intent.

Space churned violently above them, purple and crimson twisting like a soup of blood and death.

For a tense heartbeat, neither side moved.

They didn't even seem to breathe.

And then, in an instant, all hell broke loose.

The sky cracked and darkened as the most powerful storm Aodhán had ever seen tore into existence, its surface seething with apocalyptic lightning. All of it struck down on the eastern valley in a single, blinding instant—only to crash against a colossal shield of rising earth that erupted from the ground like the fist of a buried titan.

They clashed with a sound more deafening than thunder and a light more blinding than a thousand suns.

The earth screamed.

The world stopped.

And then, almost as one, hundreds of Calamities spoke, the very air shattering under the weight of their rules and decrees.

Reality screamed beneath the strain, fissures spreading across the sky like cracks in glass. The fabric of the world tore open, and through those wounds, a living corruption of nothingness bled in, pouring through the tears of reality like ink bleeding through parchment.

The earth roared in agony. The winds shrieked in terror. Lightning didn't just strike—it rewrote the sky, carving destruction into the heavens with every bolt.

And then, with a sound like the death scream of the world itself, the entire battlefield was plunged into a depth of annihilation so ruinous the entire world went black.

A wave of absolute destruction erupted outward, obliterating everything in its path until it slammed against the barriers of the war camp with enough force to shake the entire structure. Runes blazed across the transparent walls, flaring white-hot as they absorbed the impact.

But they did nothing for the sheer, overwhelming pressure the attacks of Calamities carried.

And that pressure crashed against Aodhán's spirit like a physical weight.

Dread flooded his body, cold and suffocating. His body stumbled backward—not a decision, but pure instinct overriding conscious thought, as if that could somehow save him from the catastrophe he was witnessing.

He'd taken nearly a dozen steps back before his mind caught up with his body.

His legs were shaking. His breath came in short, sharp gasps. And his hands—when had he raised them? —were held up defensively as if they could ward off annihilation.

But he wasn't alone in his terror.

Beside him, Nymera had gone perfectly still, her serene expression finally cracked by something that looked almost like fear. Ashscar's was no different, his jaw clenched so tightly Aodhán could see the muscles jumping in his cheek. And Mirelle—Mirelle seemed to have gone comatose with fear, her eyes wide and unblinking as she stared at the glitching screens.

They'd all stepped back. All of them. Without realizing it. And as one, they realized how foolish they'd been for thinking they could have fought on the frontlines of this war. How arrogant.

None of them could face a Calamity and live. They couldn't even face a Mythic of significant strength and live to tell the tale.

Yet they had thought to fight this war.

That was arrogance at its peak.

And as Aodhán stared at the aftermath of that single exchange—a smoking crater hundreds of miles across covered in blood—he remembered the words of the elder guarding the rift's verge.

Arrogance is the curse of your kind.

He'd never learned the elder's name, but those words had been carved into his mind.

Aodhán hadn't believed it then. He'd thought himself the very picture of humility.

And yet, what else could he call this?

Shakily, he turned to stare at the others, a cold certainty rising within him.

They couldn't fight in this war like it was any normal war. Their power would have no impact, and even serving as bait was courting death.

There was only one thing they could do. And that was to sit this war out and—

"Aodhán, Ashscar!" Elder Ghaltris called out sharply. "You two are together."

Aodhán's eyes widened. "What! No. We can't continue with the bait plan. We'll die."

"No, you will not." Elder Ghaltris frowned at him. "You'll just have to be careful. That was the plan."

"Yes, it was, until we saw that!" Ashscar spoke, pointing to the devastation on the screen. "Did you see that?"

Elder Ghaltris glared at him. "Yes, Ashscar, I did see it, and I assure you the power of Calamities was well accounted for before presenting this idea to you. Surely, you didn't think I thought you could outwit Calamities and Mythics without some help?"

Ashscar stuttered, and Aodhán closed his eyes in shame because that was exactly what he had thought.

"I… no, Elder Ghaltris," Ashscar eventually responded.

"Good." Elder Ghaltris smiled. "Because High-Elder Kaelthorn will be here soon to bless your paths with his. Just a little something to give you all a fighting chance."

Aodhán nodded somberly at that and glanced back at the screen, swallowing nervously as he watched an ocean of blood suddenly rise into the sky to clash against the roiling storm.

A fighting chance wasn't much, but it was something. And Aodhán wasn't stupid enough to think it meant anything more than that.

A chance. It was no certainty.

And the same went for those Mythics on the frontlines as they battled like ants between warring titans.

Their survival was no certainty either. And they all knew that.

***

Back on the battlefield, only meters away from the hundred-mile crater, Elder Vasmos clawed her way out of a pile of broken bodies and severed flesh.

Her marble-white skin was stained a deep crimson, and for a brief, humiliating moment, the world rang hollow.

Her ears screamed, a high, thin whine that swallowed all other sound. Pressure crushed her chest, not from injury but from the residual authority of Calamities still grinding the atmosphere like millstones. Vasmos dragged herself upright, one trembling hand pressing into the churned earth as her vision swam.

This wasn't her first war.

She had fought in three Balance Wars in her lifetime. She had seen sects burn. She had watched oceans boil and had buried friends whose names the world no longer allowed her to remember.

But this—

This was different.

These Calamities weren't just fighting for balance. They were angry. Desperate. Scared.

Vasmos couldn't blame them. The expansion of the Origin Plane this time was anything but normal. Even now, she could still feel it expanding, though significantly slowed from the death of so many Mythics.

She grimaced and barely managed to activate {White Storm Embodiment} before another cataclysmic attack struck, an ocean of blood clashing with the storm above to rip it to pieces.

For a heartbeat, it succeeded, an ocean of blood replacing the roiling clouds to rain down an indiscriminate plague of blood spears in their thousands.

Elder Vasmos reacted with a swift activation of {Harmonic Load Interdict}, and the air rang—not with sound, but with resistance—as it folded inward, forming a vast shield of force above her.

Willpower and chaos churned within her as the Aspect of Force hummed through her spirit, guiding, leading—funneling power into the construct with ruthless precision.

But despite all her power, the attacks of Calamities weren't so easily brushed aside.

Three spears punched through her defenses regardless, tearing through storm light and ordered force alike. One pierced her shoulder. One punched cleanly through her abdomen. The last grazed her side and detonated inside her storm form.

Her body flickered, trying desperately to hold onto its intangibility—and failed.

Her body solidified. Her wounds became real. And Vasmos choked as she spat out blood.

Dying screams erupted around her as Mythics fell like ants. All around her, thousands of Mythics fought in formations to blunt the attack. Wind Mythics unleashed colossal gales, warping trajectories and reality itself to redirect the spears. Water Mythics raised a surging ocean to drown both the falling blood and the enemy Mythics alike. Shadow Mythics vanished into darkness, unbothered by the storm of death as they struck with silent, lethal precision.

They were assassins, after all. Yet even they fought in formations. That was the only way they could stand a chance against entities as powerful as Calamities. That was the way anyone could.

But Vasmos needed no formations to unleash her fury.

She was a cultivator of the Ordered Storm—an engine of order amongst chaos, of stability in destruction.

She was her own formation.

And with a glare of furious agony, Vasmos summoned Thryx from her spirit space. The massive gryphon erupted out of her spirit with a piercing shriek, lightning racing across its feathers like living veins.

Bloodthirsty growls and roars echoed from the sky as other familiars dove toward her in response, claws and fangs gleaming. Vasmos watched them draw closer with a cold detachment, and with a pained smile, she stroked Thryx's fur. "Protect me, will you?"

Thryx turned to stare at her, twin orbs of molten gold searching hers for a moment, then with a growl, she nodded, spread her wings wide, and shrieked again, hundreds of intricate constructs materializing to revolve around her.

The constructs surged forward, order slicing through the fabric of reality like a knife through butter. They clashed against chaos-born bodies of the other familiars a moment later and simply sliced through, decapitating several familiars and dicing them to pieces.

It wouldn't kill them. Not while their bonds remained alive.

But it would buy Vasmos enough time to do something drastic and perhaps turn this whole war on its head.

Grunting in pain, she lifted her gaze to the sky, her smile widening as the storm regained its supremacy with a righteous fury.

Another judgment of lightning descended before their enemies could snuff it out, and the world was once more plunged into the depths of annihilation, another crater several miles wide appearing within enemy ranks.

The scent of blood and ozone filled the air. Cries of hatred and desperation echoed like symphonies of destruction. Colors filled the skies as Calamities fought to overpower each other, the pressure of their will pressing down on the world beneath like a mountain of tempest. Reality strained from the force of it all as Calamities battled like titans of destruction.

And at the center of all that destruction, temporarily overlooked, Elder Vasmos raised her arms to the churning storm above and activated her most powerful skill.

{Grand Ordinal Engine}

There was no boom of thunder in response or groan from the earth. Her path wasn't one of chaos, after all. It was of structure and stability. Of creation and eventual annihilation.

But first, creation.

And in response, the storm directly above her suddenly stilled, lightning arresting mid-strike as if seized by an unseen hand. Pressure folded upon itself in concentric layers, clouds compressing into rotating bands of white and silver.

The surrounding storm continued to rage, lightning striking down wildly as several Mythics pulled from and fed its power with their own—but the white storm remained unchanged, unmoving as if made from glass.

Quintessence, chaos, and willpower drained from her core like a river of energy, so overwhelming it burned white-hot, and her spirit groaned in effort.

Lightning snapped into rigid geometries around its edges, arcing along invisible frameworks. Thunder became rhythm. Wind became torque. And from the White Storm, a warframe—an ordinal engine—began to descend: a vast intricate structure of solid white storm, layered lightning, and condensed storm matter.

Silently, it descended from the storm, but it was too vast—too powerful—to go unnoticed.

For a heartbeat, the entire war stilled as they all turned to take in the otherworldly structure, eyes narrowed in caution as it descended. Segments separated along its underside, concentric rings rotating in opposite directions as seams of white lightning split open.

And from those openings, shapes dropped to the earth like falling stars and landed with a sound like continents colliding.

The earth shook, and the war restarted, but it was already too late.

Colossal mechanical constructs of storm and force rose from the impact sites, towering forms wrought from layered lightning plates and pressure-bound frameworks. Their limbs were massive and angular, each movement accompanied by shockwaves that crushed the earth flat beneath them.

Four colossi rose around Vasmos. Half a dozen others rose scattered among their ranks. While the rest—over a hundred—rose among enemy ranks, each one radiating power on par with Vasmos herself.

And yet more came, descending from the engine like an army descending from the heart of the storm itself.

They landed in kneeling positions, limbs locking into place as impact shockwaves flattened the earth outward and crushed Mythics with the force of their descent. As one, they rose, sleek storm machines of bladed limbs and harmonic cores, eyes burning with white lightning as targeting lattices activated.

And then they began to fire.

Enemy Mythics barely had time to register what they were facing before massive beams of annihilating force tore their formations apart. Walls of light scythed through ranks, vaporizing bodies mid-motion. Automatons surged forward without hesitation or fear, trampling defenses, smashing Mythics beneath titanic feet, and firing disciplined volleys of condensed lightning at point-blank range.

Despite their immense size, they were terrifyingly swift, evading attacks as Mythics gathered to destroy them. Blood-bending washed over them uselessly, crimson waves dispersing against bodies that had no flesh to command. Illusions shattered on contact, finding no mind to deceive.

That left only the Serenity, Glass, and Earth sects.

And they were brutally effective.

But for every construct that fell, more descended.

They arrived in scattered clusters, bodies phasing through space itself, blades screaming as weaponized sound reaped souls from flesh.

Around Vasmos herself, the war had grown even more furious. Enemies and allies surrounded her, the former attacking, while the latter defended, urging her to keep the automatons coming.

Vasmos was more than happy to oblige them.

Standing at the heart of annihilation, her body blazed with stormlight, marble skin gleaming like polished glass beneath a burning sun. Within her, the Aspects of Force and Sound hummed in perfect resonance, folding into storm and order alike to fuel the Ordinal Engine.

But around the engine itself was the war most chaotic, as Calamities fought desperately to bring it down. Rules and laws ripped reality apart as Calamities from both sides fought either to defend or destroy the engine.

But in so much chaos, even Calamities couldn't fully protect such a vast structure from destruction.

And then—with a sound like breaking glass—a mountain-sized boulder slammed against the engine with the force of a falling star.

Space collapsed in on itself. Reality buckled, and with a crack of thunder, the Ordinal Engine shattered.

Its rings exploded outward into fragments of lightning and storm-matter, debris blotting out the sky as annihilation washed across the battlefield and—

And then it snapped back, its fragments glitching through space as if reality itself had failed to reject it. Rings reformed. Lightning surged. The engine stabilized. And from its belly, more automatons descended.

Shock rippled across the ranks of Calamities, even her allies. The Ordinal Engine was a construct. It shouldn't be reforming.

But the unexpected barely delayed them for more than a second before another attack struck—this time a massive blood scythe that cleaved the vast engine in two.

The Engine shattered once more, then reformed.

A third attack. The Engine shattered, then reformed again, its core flaring brighter with each destruction.

This time, hesitation spread, and for a moment, they wondered if a time Calamity had snuck into their war and was now playing tricks on them. But then their gazes strayed to the four colossal automatons standing directly beneath the engine, and the blazing woman at their center.

That was when they realized that the engine was no ordinary skill. Just like a familiar, the constructs of ordered storm never died as long as their creator remained alive.

Fortunately, it wasn't hard at all to find the engine's creator.

And as one, nearly all the enemy Calamities turned toward Vasmos with one aim.

To kill her.

But none of the allied Calamities would allow that—not after how devastating she had just proved herself to be. They rushed after their opponents immediately, and the air screamed as authority layered upon authority, intent sharpening to a singular point to slow their enemies down. The descending pressure alone sent thousands of Mythics to their knees, and dozens to their deaths.

The automatons fired massive beams upward to deter the descending Calamities. Thryx unleashed attack after attack, hundreds of razor-sharp feathers surging upward in defiance.

But it was all for naught.

Her automatons were strong, and with Thryx, they were most certainly a force to be reckoned with. But against Calamities? She might as well have been attempting to dam an ocean with pebbles.

Vasmos felt it then—the weight of inevitability.

Her heart thundered in her chest, not with panic, but with the cold realization that she had reached the edge of what she could endure. She kept her eyes open, wide and burning, terrified that the moment she blinked would be the moment she died.

Her entire being shuddered as she resigned herself to a swift, unavoidable end.

And then she felt it—a warm splattering of liquid against her cheek. Crimson liquid.

It forced her to blink.

And the moment she did, the world exploded in a supernova of energy so dense and overflowing it bathed the sky in light so blinding it washed the rest of the world out, turning day into screaming radiance.

The explosion was silent.

Not because it lacked force, but because sound itself was erased.

A wave of annihilation followed a fraction of a second later, reality slamming back into existence as the shockwave tore across the sky like a blade dragged through the firmament itself. The Ordinal Engine shattered into oblivion. The storm was ripped apart. And her automatons disintegrated mid-battle, their ordered forms unraveling under the backlash of a Calamity's death.

Mythics in the air ceased to exist by the thousands.

Even Calamities were hurled backward, their Authorities flaring instinctively as they struggled to protect as many of their people as they could. But they couldn't protect them all.

Thousands died, far more than anyone could count. Far more than even battle-hardened veterans could stomach.

And in the silence that followed, the Calamity who died was identified.

She'd belonged to the Serenity Sect.

Shocked silence filled the ranks of their enemies as the storm reformed above them, giving them no time to mourn. Lightning struck down an instant later, vaporizing thousands of disciples to bloody mist.

Their enemies let out agonized growls of hatred as they attacked once more, their desperation rising to a fever pitch.

Vasmos watched it all with numb relief, just glad that she'd made it out alive.

This war was different. Far different from any she'd ever fought before. But perhaps, with the way things were going, they could survive it.

Back in the viewing center, Aodhán and the other specials stood shell-shocked as they watched the devastating battle and the number of lives each attack claimed.

Their foreheads tingled with the blessing. High-Elder Kaelthorn had bestowed upon them, but in the face of such devastation and loss of life, they all felt less than ready for the plan they had agreed to.

Elder Ghaltris, however, seemed to think otherwise.

In the silence that followed the Calamity's death, he nodded and turned to glance at them. "Our opportunity is here. Are you ready?"

Aodhán glanced at the others, noting the fear on all their faces, even Nymera's, who had looked perfectly calm before. And when it became obvious none of them would speak, Aodhán nodded. "We are ready."

He turned his gaze to the screen and whispered, "Storm save me!"
