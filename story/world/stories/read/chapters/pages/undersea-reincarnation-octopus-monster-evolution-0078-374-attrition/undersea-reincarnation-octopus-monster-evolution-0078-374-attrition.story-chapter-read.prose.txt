It was like he’d wrung himself dry, and the first few drops of fresh water had finally reached his soul. Henry had to admit, he might have overdone things by spending such a prodigious amount of mana in barely seconds by re-summoning the bulk of his clones, but the rush of mana and vitality seeping out of the pulsing parasite and into his soul quickly washed away his worries, trickling back into his barely strained channels.

He was pretty sure if it hadn’t been for his recent tempering, he might have injured himself there, and for the first time since this encounter had started, he felt the first sparkle of hope in his chest.

But he still felt like a candle in the storm.

The parasite so far had shrugged most of what they’d thrown at it. The simple Toxic Rays were almost as inoffensive as pointing a laser pointer at it and as for its flesh, even his Octominds had barely broken the skin when they’d used the empowered Bite, and he could tell the venom wasn’t sapping as much as it could have been. It was decent enough for him and it’d keep him going, but he could tell instinctively that he could have been drawing a lot more if it wasn’t for the thing’s innate resistance.

But it’s not that physically resistant, in the end. Too bad I can’t easily capitalize on that, Henry thought, the faraway shouts of the survivors barely audible over the waves. The Octominds hadn’t done that much physical damage, but they also weren’t as powerful as he was. Then again, that point was moot. Henry couldn’t afford to get within arm’s reach of it. He was pretty sure it’d be a lot harder to shove off its influence if he allowed it to get in arm’s reach of him. Just like him, it was likely more effective in proximity, so he had to stick to ranged attacks.

Usually, Henry would attach to something that outlevelled him. He’d find a little nook, make himself tiny, then keep pumping mana out and toxins in until his target was weakened enough to finish off. But getting close wasn’t an option here. And this fight wouldn’t be quick, no matter how much he wanted to be, so resources were the priority.

“[It’s probably the strongest thing we’ve had to face,” Henry said, eyeing the creature as it raised its arcane, manifested arm to intercept a wave of exploding bubbles. At the same time, Henry sent three Octominds down toward the starfish.“[Much worse than the seahorse.]”

“[Agreed]” Maurice sent back, using the volley of Toxic Rays to disguise a particularly empowered black bubble, which he empowered with his own, weaker Rupture Casting. Henry watched as the parasite waited until the last second to float out of the way of Maurice’s attack, swatting it down with an arcane arm, only for both the rays and the arm to disappear into a dark, light-twisting implosion.

The parasite was unaffected, and it turned its attention back at them, which was still as unnerving as it had been when the creature had shown up.

“[Well… one of those will hit eventually,]” Henry muttered.

A series of detonations sent multiple plumes of water up, the work of Maurice, which made Henry consider a future combination now that he had managed to eat through the parasite’s defenses with full-army blast of Toxic Rays.

The next time he has some downtime, he should work on tying down Spiteful Detonation to his venomous abilities. Exploding, toxic clones would be pretty handy right about now. Especially if he could control when they became toxic and when they stayed normal.

A project for another day, he decided. For now, his best and most viable angle was a war of attrition. A hit-and-run tactic until the parasite either ran or died. Most importantly though, he needed to make sure he always had enough juice and room to get the hell away from the creature at the drop of a hat and for that, he needed more batteries.

It was time for step two.

While his hundreds of clones continued pelting the only marginally weakened shell of the parasite, Henry turned his attention to the three Octominds he’d sent down to the starfish, one of which he was ready to swap place with. The starfish was the brawn of this duo, so filling its system with his own venomous cocktail would ensure he wouldn’t run out of mana and health any time soon. And once he’d gotten enough venom within its massive body, he’d spare a look through the Octomind flying down the elevator shaft.

He hadn’t forgotten about it. It was just not an immediate priority. Then again, it had barely been ten seconds since he had sent it down the shaft.

Dear God. It hasn’t even been two minutes this thing showed up, he thought as he spared another section of the battlefield a half-second of his attention.

With a grumble and a spark of worry that he immediately smothered, Henry scooped up every sailor he had located through his clones. He had hoped the survivors would pull their colleagues along with them, but they were too busy getting the hell out of Dodge rather than rescue those that had been knocked out. Only a couple—Rafa included—seemed to have picked up some survivors, but even the leader was limited by how many he could carry.

Henry didn’t really have time to play the blame game, so he just gave each a pulse of Life Surge, paid for by the generous parasite, before he chucked them into his Maw and hoped for the best.

He was under no illusion that all of them survived. Especially considering the holes the starfish’s spines had left in their bodies.

Henry shook his head, shoving the worry aside. They were all hardy sailors. None below C-rank. Most would make it. As for the runners… well. Who wouldn’t run away from two wild, hostile A-ranks when they were barely B-ranks themselves?

At least it frees us up to let loose, he thought, turning his attention down to the three Octominds approaching the core of the starfish, which had somehow gotten close to their general location.

“[Maurice, we’re going under for a second,]” Henry warned as the magic rippled around him, while Maurice immediately stopped his assault and raised his hands. “[Got it,]” said the crab, and then Henry pulled the trigger. One moment the two of them were bombarding the glowing, starfish-encased parasite, and the next they were floating above a star-shaped field of thorns stretching out in five direction, and it only took half a second to realize the massive field was moving, kicking up a storm of sand below it, but not high enough that the massive limb were hidden. If anything, it made finding the giant starfish easier.

Henry didn’t waste time. Shedding his disguise, he spread his eight arms out and shot down toward the core of the massive starfish, his two empowered Octominds at his side while still keeping an eye on the parasite above. He didn’t like how it shifted its attention and looked down, but the literal hundreds of simultaneous Toxic Rays made sure to keep the parasite busy while Henry channeled nearly all the Predation he had left into his beak.

But before that, he had to prepare the injection spot.

Bone surrounded four of his arms in barely a second, then Henry wailing on the patch of spiny flesh, right above the core tenderizing its flesh and shattering the protective spines. The starfish squirmed, and the rest of its spines glowed, but Henry wasn’t here to start fighting it.

He just had to deliver the venom.

Biting down into the bloody flesh, his beak crunched through the remaining spines and through the thick skin, and when Henry tasted blood, his venomous gland squeezed, channeling the burning magical toxins into the creature, his own venom slipping into the massive starfish’s system.

A few paces away, his two other Octominds did the same though at the same time, he sensed something building up below him. The starfish’s response. But he’d made his delivery, so he was good to go.

Activating his teleport, Henry switched places with a clone, leaving his third Octomind on the surface just in time to escape being pin-cushioned as all of sudden, all the spines in a hundred foot radius surged up toward the exact spot where he’d been, breaking against each other as they released a cloud of dark toxin Henry made a point of sampling later.

The sun shone on his black, yellow dotted, while the parasite glared eyelessly at him, its attention baleful. Still, Henry didn’t really sense any wariness from it. It was just pissed and annoyed at the flies that refused to fall under its control.

Letting out a slow breath, Henry began charging another powerful Toxic Ray, one that’ll use the generous amounts of mana both the parasite and the starfish were funneling to him, and finally, he looked through his Octomind on the island.

Henry did not like what he saw there.
