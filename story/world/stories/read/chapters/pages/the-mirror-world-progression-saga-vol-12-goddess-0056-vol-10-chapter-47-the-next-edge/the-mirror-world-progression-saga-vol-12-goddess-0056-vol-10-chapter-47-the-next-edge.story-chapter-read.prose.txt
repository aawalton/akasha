The undead giants had all been destroyed, but they’d had a toll of casualties for the privilege, including among the Radiancers, who’d had little choice but to spread out into smaller pockets to escape the charge of the gradually dying giants. Fourteen of them were gone, along with over two thousand soldiers, the bulk of that simply in the initial, explosive appearance, before they’d adjusted.

Before all the giants had fallen, but their numbers had thinned, half of the elites — composed of those deemed less effective in delaying giants — had moved beyond them to hold the ranks back as the enemy made routes through the burning roots, brambles, and corpses.

With Samantha blitzing the wizard groups, the enemy finally gave up on supporting the giants and retreated. The lip the giants had exploded out from was taken and passed, and information specialists confirmed there weren’t any further surprises lying within.

After obtaining repairs to her armor and, more importantly, quickened repair of her Battle Dress, Samantha returned to her position among the oversight members of the Counter Crew. The army as a whole advanced, now facing a reduced total output magic-wise, though dangers remained. Countering the tower spells would only get harder as time wore on, as they got more and more in range of greater spells, numbers, and intensity.

The bleakness of the sky and the land itself only increased as they got deeper within, and closer to the walls of Matrazere. It was as if making a transition into a whole other, far more awful world. A feeling of corruption, of pollution, was palpable, and in front of them was a horde only tightening up in power, and the damage they’d done so far seemed utterly irrelevant, as if they’d cut off the fingers of a hand, but then found the creature had more hands than they could count.

Sensing the hesitation and worried mood of her army, Samantha spoke, direct to minds and hearts, channeling all that she was as the amalgamated product of many goddesses. “This enemy remains a coward,” She declared derisively, angrily. “With every step, we see this truth more. Their only weapon is despair. To stab your hope and cause you to know their fear. Keep the core of courage within yourself and march forward always to our horizon. They are nothing more than spineless, bloated worms in our way.” Her voice rose as she thrust her staff, pointing directly at their towers. “Under our boots, false sages! Be crushed and join the bed you made in the dust!”

Hyped-up cheers answered her as they charged forward anew, but all too soon there was only the perpetual, horrid sound of war — men cutting into the dead with increasing hatred, and the dead, without passion, returning the favor.

The attack waves of the elites, now weaving in wider wheels to prevent envelopment, slowed from a combination of gradual fatigue and expended resources, as well as the sheer toughness of what they faced. They were blunted against the stiffer resistance of the empowered undead, who were drawing strength from the environment they were so attuned with, as if it anchored them supernaturally. They were not even as easily moved as before.

Almost the entirety of their cavalry and smaller groups of elite skirmishers, including flyers and minor summoned entities, guarded their flanks and rear as they advanced, while suffering continual attrition. Little could be done about it against an enemy without morale. It was simply a churn.

On the other hand, whatever ground they took could make use of spontaneously generated fortifications by small specialist teams dedicated to the purpose. The vast favorites were walls, as they were low-level and required wasted effort to deal with, even if you could dispel them. Some walls were simply fast-assembled from parts, the magic used and expended in moving and fusing them. There was nothing to dispel. The enemy had to blow them up, and it was a big waste of energy, so these walls mostly stayed.

It was a clever innovation that ended up saving lives by blocking flanking avenues. Funneling enemy forces and protecting the backlines.

All-in-all, the alliance advance was a great, coordinated dance on the part of Dax, her sub-generals such as Linnore, the Brain Trust, and the communication network of Jeeves and the Resemblants, further aided by the networks of their deific allies. More and more, orderly processes held instead of the rampant, chaotic destruction first waged. Two coordinated waves here, waves on the flanks there, a boosted advance of the core infantry forward. Cycles, rotations, in-and-out mobile strikes, planted defensive lines, screening volleys of ranged fire, arrays of walls and other deterrents, so on and so forth, all with pre-imagined timing.

The ‘machine’ as a whole was a psychic reading the enemy’s local strategic plays before they acted. Tightening up and preventing any avoidable catastrophes of loss by mere tactical trickery. Without question, their side had the advantage there.

The Radiancers bloomed with occasional volleys, wiping out groupings of undead with more impunity than anyone by now. Thanks to them, the witch flyers stayed away, conserving their numbers lest the precision undead slayers focus on zapping them out of the sky. But the elves had to conserve a portion of their mana for greater threats that might rise, so they were only firing intermittently.

Samantha’s proximity to the core of their mages allowed them the privilege of some recent regeneration, thanks to her unique [Mana Font] ability and its 110-meter radius, bumping the entire mana level and doubling regeneration. This alone would make it beneficial for her to limit her frontlines fighting, much as it ached not to be there with them. But she remained too valuable simply blocking spells and thus casualties, as the enemy continually tried to break through and damage their highest value targets, some of which they could afford to lose even one of, including the Antimages.

No matter how efficient they were, men died. Attrition continued.

Boxed in; losing men continually. But their ‘losses’ continue to be much higher. Progress.

The enemy couldn’t pull necromantic reinforcement — or at least they’d taken every precaution. A simple alchemical preparation prevented it for days. Whatever soaked into people being near Matrazere wasn’t good, and certainly had something to do with why there was a mass killing and conversion to undead, but it could hardly matter for transformation in the timespan they’d be there.

She hoped it was true, anyway.

The grueling battle continued. It had gone on long enough to strain their shorter-duration buffs. In many cases, they simply ran out of feasible castings. Performance dropped as movements slowed, as overpowering extra numbers tacked on to their elites’ totals fell off, as spells and effects lost a bit of their oomph. It was not great in combination with a strengthening enemy and intensifying fire from the walls and towers. Momentum loss? Oh yes.

Okay, then. Time for the next edge.

Samantha gave the word to the leader of the Radiancers, Leiande, and the whole army for the strategic shift to come. New buffs bloomed, and all were made ready for a key resurgence.

The elites fell into formation, and everyone tightened up centrally. Leiande, hovering over the ground, approached the frontlines and revealed her artifact with a flourish. The Second Sun was brandished, and the sphere opened.

Unstoppable warm light spilled out in a wide radius, turning the gloom to gold. As soon as the light touched the vanguard undead, they disintegrated, their arms falling to the ground — a hundred or more all at once. Hundreds beyond them were touched by the light and seized, as their skin burned. Most staggered, dropped their weapons, or fell. Not quite screams came from their dry lips. Too guttural, too choked-off. Growls and muted howls of disturbance.

Leiande charged forward swiftly, and those all around her were ready, charging and unleashing waiting ability or artifact activations, saved spells, and otherwise going for broke to not let her advance be slowed.

Above, Samantha and the Counter Crew, together with key cleromages, flew overhead as oversight and protection, surrounded by Angels, griffin riders, demons of many stripes, and — of course — Azure atop Deathsting, leading them all, primed to prevent any aerial interference.

The Second Sun, flanked by a second storm of elite martial and magical destruction, drove deeper than ever into the ranks, disintegrating undead with impunity. She curved her progress instead of making for the walls of Matrazere, leaving a fat trail of undead dust at first diagonal, and then curving back on one flank. Against that charge, the enemy could do nothing. Thousands went up in smoke.

She crisscrossed back centrally, making a loop that pulled her flanking masses of elite supports gut out the core of it in the center, leaving a massive voided space for the army to raise a cry and fill up.

All the while, interrupting spells made a flurry, detonating fire and lightning and force magic, along with darker magics, but to the last, they fizzled, giving but a taste of the disruption they intended. Antimages, countermages, and Samantha herself blocked it all, dooming the undead below.

She wasn’t done. On the other side, she crisscrossed the flank similarly, in what Samantha realized with a coughed-out laugh was an extremely long figure-eight. An infinity symbol.

It was oddly quite effective at making a void out of the undead. Their ashes made a dust storm rising into the air, and the battlefield left in the wake looked like some solemn offering of arms and armor to the dirt. Ten, twenty, thirty thousand suits paired with this or that weapon, almost pristine amidst the collection of dispersing dust.

Leiande, carrying the Second Sun out before her, panting with effort and glistening with sweat, floated back to the center point. Wild eyes of glee flashed out to the armies behind her, and she smiled resplendently, holding the artifact higher. “The elves of yesteryear have come to remember… and we refuse to be forgotten!”

Many elves cheered in response, in fury, ancient pain, and exultation for a glory long coming. The whole army soon responded.

Samantha felt a tug on her heart from this, but her eyes remained ahead. Out across the gap, the enemy had not moved forward at all to fill any space. They waited, now close enough to the walls that the still vast and extensive army could be said to be guarding it. It was a denser formation, now. Even the spells from the tower ceased, as if poised for a new stage in the battle.

Samantha’s eyes narrowed. Something truly darker there, inside the outskirts.

The real battle was about to begin.
