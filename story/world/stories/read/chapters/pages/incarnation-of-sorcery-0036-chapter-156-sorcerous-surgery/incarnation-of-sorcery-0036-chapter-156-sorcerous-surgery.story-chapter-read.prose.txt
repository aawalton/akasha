If nothing else, Ryland had to admit that the buildup to the meeting received a suitably significant payoff with the Demiurge’s announcement.

The banquet hall was filled to the brim with students, parents, Academs, administration officers, and all other personnel associated with the institute and the various visiting academies. That all these people had been shocked into silence proved the magnitude of Mavar’s decision. Ryland had to admit that he was impressed.

He couldn’t tell what exactly the others had expected. Maybe reluctance from Mavar to admit that his Soul Glyph had indeed been Inscribed on people’s manasouls. Maybe belligerence on the fact that it was something needed, something that was necessary for Rhellians.

What they surely hadn’t expected was for him to blatantly admit that the Inscription was real. That he truly did control, to a great extent, the actions and choices of everyone born on Rhel.

And then he had gone further to turn the whole thing on its head. He had rolled the ball into his opposition’s court. Instead of leaving it to them, Mavar himself had come up with the most drastic solution to the issue that others had discovered, thereby taking control of the situation.

Ryland wasn’t quite certain what his angle was, but it was evident that the Demiurge possessed not an inkling of doubt.

Belief. He had sincerely talked about having faith. Was it really going to pay off now?

Of course, once everyone had regained their faculties, things broke down into quite a bit of chaos. So much so that Ryland was terribly glad that none of this was really his headache.

It took a good long while, and Ryland wasn’t particularly interested in the particulars of the process, but they all eventually settled on a course of action. Mavar couldn’t just remove the Soul Glyphs willy-nilly. There was a process involved.

Since it wouldn’t be easy at all to remove an Inscribed Soul Glyph from the hundreds of millions of people inhabiting an entire planet in one day, they would start small and expand outwards. First, the Demiurge would complete the Rhellian Institute. Then he would take care of the city of Vezion they were all residing in. Then the cities beyond.

In this way, they would slowly expand the operation, bringing in people from the entirety of Rhel and carrying out the operation.

Ryland was once again surprised at the revelation of the sheer scope of the undertaking. Mavar had built up his society, the way of life he had instated on Rhel, over centuries. To simply throw it all away because of the demands of foreigners seemed very difficult to believe.

Unless he was confident that his real goals weren’t changing through this operation. Or perhaps he was sure that things would go back to normal.

Maybe this was all an elaborate deception. A ruse to fool everyone into thinking that he had complied, when in truth, he hadn’t. Of course, Arcoryx, Jure, and Eviliyen had all thought about it too and had decided to come up with their own methods of verification. All three contingents were going to send out their own mages to check the Rhellian manasouls.

Speaking of whom, a great many of them were highly opposed to the idea. Even after their own children had revealed their determined wish to not be affected by the Soul Glyph, they were insisting that it was their way of life. That this was a violation of their very culture.

Of course, since the issue was one of proving that the Demiurge’s acts weren’t basically instilling mind-control over an entire Realm, and since they all still had the Soul Glyph of Unity Inscribed on their manasouls, their opinions weren’t given much thought. Everyone was just waiting to see how their tunes changed once the Glyph was gone.

And then, of course, Ryland didn’t fail to throw a wrench into proceedings.

“Please remember,” he said. “The ones who brought you this news, which isn’t untrue at all, are being influenced by the Flourinus Order. They were freed through the machinations of a group whose true goal is the ascension of new Calamities across all the Realms.”

Everyone was staring at him like he had said something even harder to believe than the fact that a single mage was more or less mind-controlling an entire Realm. Thankfully, despite the missing students’ protests, there were many witnesses to the Order’s shenanigans from multiple involved parties, so Ryland didn’t have to struggle with making them trust his words.

For all that the students were clearly angry at him for undermining their efforts, the truth did need to be said.

“Otherwise,” he pointed out. “You’re simply being hypocrites, are you not?”

To that, they didn’t have an answer. The truth wasn’t out there to simply benefit their point of view, no matter how righteous it might seem on the surface.

However, the real problem from Ryland muddying the waters with his truthful addition was that there was renewed resistance from the Rhellians who fully trusted their Demiurge. They had no reason to comply with the demands of the people from other Realms. In fact, several brazenly proclaimed they’d rather have all the foreigners leave instead of complying.

Thankfully, Mavar didn’t use it as some sort of cudgel to beat down the foreigners’ point. He couldn’t. Not when all his subjects’ opinions were highly suspect.

It was a lengthy process, so it wasn’t going to be resolved any time soon. But the progress was real. They all saw it. Even Ryland. Close inspection after the first day revealed that everyone at the institute who was checked were found to no longer possess a Soul Glyph of Unity Inscribed on their manasouls. Mavar was keeping his word.

Unfortunately, the removal process was rather intensive on the subjects. It left practically everyone incapacitated after the procedure, so the institute felt devastatingly empty after that day.

Ryland didn’t concern himself with it at first. He had decided to focus on finding just where the Order mages had gone and finish the job that Mavar had failed to complete. At this point, the Flourinus Order was an existential threat to multiple Realms. He still didn’t understand just what Mavar had been planning, but whatever it was, he may have finally seen reason.

At some point, Ryland did intend to interrogate the fellow and figure out what was going on. Regardless of what the rest of Rhel might think about their leader being forced to answer.

For now, however, he was genuinely concerned about the process that Mavar was using to remove his own Soul Glyphs from the Rhellians.

By the second day, most of the incapacitated hadn’t recovered. This meant the institute was rapidly running out of space in which to keep all the new patients they were accruing. It seemed that Mavar hadn’t quite foreseen this exact scenario.

“You think he’ll let you help?” High Academ Tyra asked Ryland as they headed towards the central chamber where the council running the affairs of the institute usually met up. Except, this time, it would be occupied by the person running this entire operation—Kindly Mavar.

Ryland shrugged a bit. “I am offering assistance out of concern. If I am barred from helping, then it will not affect me either way. I have no skin in the game, you see.” He eyed Tyra. “What about you, though, High Academ?”

“What about me?”

“Have you made enquiries as to why you weren’t informed about the Soul Glyph of Unity beforehand?” Ryland asked.

Tyra scowled a little like she didn’t appreciate being reminded of the fact she had been kept in the dark about it all. “Didn’t you answer the question yourself that day? It’s too sensitive a matter to be revealed too blithely?”

She did have him there. But the real answer was that she hadn’t been given a proper answer to the issue. It seemed even Arcoryx administration was cagey about everything they had discussed with the Demiurge before agreeing to send their students and Academs over to Rhel. Something that reasonably frustrated High Academ Tyra.

“Perhaps I can find it out,” Ryland said. “After all, I will be discussing things with the source of it all.”

Tyra looked unconvinced. “Good luck. For once, you might just need it.”

She might have had a point there too. Ryland nodded and went ahead to find Mavar.

The main location of the procedure they were undergoing was in the same great hall where they had held that big meeting with everyone. It had been remodelled to house beds for the patients, screens for privacy, tools for performing the procedure, and so on.

A lot of Healer Mages were present as well, all of them looking rather tired after the days-long operation. Inscriptions were trying to keep them all energized and sated, but there wasn’t much anyone could do about the mental toll this whole procedure was taking. Just another way the hastiness of it all was affecting things negatively.

They didn’t let Ryland approach the Demiurge at first. He was “too busy”. A short demonstration of how Ryland could simply take himself there with a rift had the guards reconsidering their stance rather quickly.

Mavar was in the middle of the procedure when Ryland reached him. He had his hands raised over an older woman, palms aimed downwards, a spell circle glowing in front of each hand. “This is possibly the worst time for you to interrupt me, Ryland.”

“I thought the time I caught you pilfering a guava from a hapless fruit-seller was the worst time?” Ryland said.

Mavar almost winced before shooting a glare at Ryland. “Enough. Spit out what you came here for.”

“I came here to rectify whatever process you’re using to reverse the magic you yourself implanted into all these people.”

That had Mavar stopping his cast. He turned to Ryland with a frown. “What makes you think your interference here is welcome?”

Ryland looked around a little exaggeratedly. “Seeing as how you appear to have caused yourself a great deal of trouble, I thought I would alleviate some of the pressure. Because I suspect that you’re going about this in an incorrect fashion, or you wouldn’t be swamped as you currently are.”

“None of that answers my question.”

Ryland inspected the body Mavar was currently freeing from the clutches of his own Soul Glyph, one eye submerged in [Omniscience]. “You appear to be hollowing out the manasoul, essentially gouging out the region where you Inscribed the Soul Glyph of Unity, thereby technically reverting them to a state without the Inscription.”

“But you and your brilliant mind spotted the flaw in the process and came up with a solution.”

“Essentially, yes. The way you appear to be going about it is causing the manasoul to lose the level of cohesion needed to function. This leads to it becoming inert, at least until it has recovered to a more functional state. I assume that, at first, you considered this to be an acceptable price to achieve your goal. But now…”

Mavar scowled at Ryland’s lead, following his gaze aimed at all the occupied beds and unmoving people. And these weren’t even the majority.

“I am yet to hear about how you would handle this,” Mavar said.

Ryland inspected the poor woman again. “It’s simple. I will just dissolve the Inscription.”

Several nearby mages assisting Mavar all froze in surprise at the statement. Even the Demiurge himself slowly turned around to face Ryland as though he had said something incredulous.

“You know how to dissolve Inscriptions?” Mavar asked. “By yourself? Without the help of any Relics or Glyphtech or anything like that?”

Ryland smiled. “Indeed. Neither of our magic has remained stagnant since the last time we met, Mavar. Now, allow me access. Swallow your pride and let me see what I can do. You know full well I don’t intend to let any of them come to harm.”

The other Rhellians made it very clear what they thought about that idea, though they all appeared to leave it up to their Demiurge to decide. To be fair, their Soul Glyphs of Unity hadn’t been removed yet.

Mavar took his time deciding. Maybe he was leaving Ryland hanging on purpose, though Ryland was far too patient to be offended by the pettiness of it, especially since he had handled this sort of thing before. Eventually, Mavar sighed and stepped away for a moment, indicating Ryland to advance.

“Go on, then,” he said. “I’m only allowing this because your process sounds worth it.”

Ryland ignored the actual wording and focused on his task ahead. Admittedly, it had been a while since he had tried the spell. Longer still since he had attempted it on an Inscription on an actual person.

But he forged on regardless. It took about a minute for Ryland to refamiliarize himself with the process. There was no rushing this. It wasn’t just selfishness stopping Mavar from simply handing over the reins of the procedure to Ryland. Like most surgeries, there was always the chance that things could get botched during the operation.

Ryland brought out his Soul Glyph of Genesis. Manipulating the right Subglyphs into place finally got his spell into the shape he wanted. All the while, the growing spell circle’s glow made all the other Healer Mages stare and gawk, unable to take their eyes off the spell.

Once he was done, the spell’s effect sought out his target—the poor old woman lying on the gurney—and began to take effect. A small waterfall of mana entered the woman’s manasoul, flooding her spirit with Ryland’s magic. Since he had added a Subglyph of Tranquillity, her manasoul didn’t reject it as some sort of foreign invasion.

Ryland could proceed. “[Inscribed Renewal].”

Even with the spell circle active, he had to guide the mana. He had to make sure that all the Subglyphs were in the right spot. The last thing he wished to enact was some sort of complication for the poor woman.

Thankfully, things went without a hitch. Ryland’s mana healed the woman’s manasoul, the coating slowly dissolving the Soul Glyph of Unity until the manasoul was back to normal.

[ Achievement Recognized: Inscription Bane

You’ve removed a Tier [28] Inscription despite being merely Titan-ranked.

Rank up from Titan [13] to Titan [19].

Reward: 360 Glyph Prints. ]

“There,” Ryland said. He wasn’t quite sure what to do with all those Glyph Prints, but he’d think about it later. “All done.”

Everyone else was looking on with slightly wide eyes. As ever, Mavar was the one to voice their feelings on the matter.

“You’re done?” he asked. “Already?”

Ryland didn’t need to answer. What better reply was there than the woman slowly awakening, blinking as she gently sat upright, wondering at them all with a tremendously blank expression. There were hints of fear on her face, a confusion that reminded him of those who woke up from a long coma.

“This is the first fully successful case, yes?” Ryland asked. He eyed the patients who were yet to awaken.

Mavar held himself together for a moment longer before finally sighing. “Yes. The others have not taken as well to my procedure as this woman has taken to yours.”

“Procedure…?” the woman asked in confusion.

Ryland exchanged a quick look with Mavar. There was almost a shade of that old familiarity between them, infected with the memories of their time when they had wrought this whole world together. When Rhel had been born anew, and they had been the ones to create it, guide it, and mould it.

When they had protected this little haven against the brutality of the Realmbreaker Wars.

The moment passed. The new Mavar, the real Demiurge, reasserted himself. His eyes, momentarily hopeful, grew cold and calculating. “We have a great deal of Rhellians left.”

“Indeed,” Ryland said. “Please bring them forward. The faster I finish with this, the faster I can deal with the real problem.”

“Which is?”

“The mages from the Flourinus Order, of course.”

Kindly Mavar looked at the woman whose confusion was ever so slowly growing into actual alarm. “That leaves me to deal with a very different problem for the time being.”
