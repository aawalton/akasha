I stormed back up the stairs toward Valciferous' balcony, leaving Jericho, Calliope, and Nuzzle behind.

Eugene followed halfway up. "You can't just go in there," he said nervously.

I ignored him, grabbing the handle to the ornate white door and twisting. It was locked, obviously. I knocked hard with my full carapaced forearm. The door shook.

Without my shadow I was cut off from everything that mattered. From my real life. From Avery.

"Hold your undead horses," a voice said from within.

This was not what we'd agreed to.

A few seconds later, Trix opened the door. "Well, if it isn't Zephyr, our new golden boy."

I grabbed her by the cloak and spun her around. Same symbol on it as mine. But her feet. She still had a shadow.

Fuck that!

I stormed past her into the anteroom.

"Where is he?" There was no one else here. I pointed aggressively to the door out to the balcony. "Is he out there?"

Trix rolled her eyes and lounged back on the couch. "He's a god, darling. He doesn't have time to lay about at the arena all day."

"Unlike you, apparently," I spat.

"I'm here because he knew you'd come running back, furious as a poked lion. And look! Here you are."

"This isn't some joke. My shadow is… is gone."

"Mmm. Yes. I see that."

"We never agreed to this!"

She sat forward. "Actually, you did. You swore to act as an agent and symbol of the god Valciferous, and all that entails. Perhaps you should've asked for the fine print."

My fingernails bit into my palms from squeezing them so tight. "You can't just trick me like this? You can't expect loyalty after this. You can't expect—"

"He doesn't expect it, Zephyr. He demands it. And you'll pay the dues. Everyone does eventually. As the scythe of Valciferous active in Penumbra you need to be a constant presence for all to see. And a Death Reaper to boot? You need to become stronger, progress faster than anyone else. Without the distraction of Vitria, you have more time than anyone. I suggest you accept your situation and get to work. After all, the sooner you complete your end of the bargain, the sooner he completes his."

"My end of the bargain…"

My end of the bargain involved infiltrating a ring of disgruntled Reapers—Shepherds—and discovering the source of the wave of Unchained that had been consuming the land. That could take weeks. Months. Longer.

And that was just the beginning.

Reapers had been trying to destroy Sin for eternity for all I knew. And what? I was going to just waltz in, wave my scythe, and be done with it?

I was probably looking at years of servitude.

All that time I'd be trapped here. What'll happen to my real body if I don't return to it? I knew… I'd be asleep. In a coma. And Avery would have to live with that. What emotional toll would that take? How could I let her know I'd be back? How…

"This is unfair. It's cruel to the ones in Vitria who rely on me."

Trix waved her finger in the air. "Now now. You Ambers are always thinking too small. Your actions here are for the greater good. You have the power to unite this realm, to push the Sins of humanity to the brink of existence. To make a better world for your loved ones. And you think that cruel?"

I growled. This holier than thou, righteous justification was annoying as all fuck, but difficult to argue against. Because it was true… There was so much shit in the world. All caused by humans.

"Right now, out there…" Trix pointed in an arbitrary direction. "Kids are being raised in untenable situations. Entire countries are starving while others toss boatloads of excess into landfills. Billionaires hoard wealth instead of building up communities. Murderers with guns slaughter innocents daily. Rapists avoid conviction on semantics. And countless more. You could have the power to end all that. And you'd deny the world that grace? If so, you're the one who is cruel. Selfish."

She was right. There was so much sin.

I always tried to ignore it. Because what else can you do to stay sane? What else can you do to forget the sins you bear?

But if I was going to create a family. To have children of my own. What wouldn't I give for them to live in a safe and happy world?

Anything.

Everything.

And these Shepherds wanted to perpetuate that pain by joining with the Sins? Stand between my family and happiness? I could prevent all of it.

The tension seeped from my body.

Valciferous had been untrustworthy in the way he'd recruited me to his cause. But Trix was right. In the end, it was a price I was willing to pay. And it wasn't just a burden.

People crave the ability to make a difference. To mean something to the world.

And that opportunity was being handed to me on a silver platter. No one in Vitria wold know my sacrifice. But that was okay.

I sagged onto the sofa opposite Trix and sighed. "Can you at least give me some advice on where to start?"

"Ah, there he is. Our great Death Reaper. I thought you'd never ask."

I sauntered back down the stairs after finishing my conversation with Trix, partially defeated, partially rejuvenated with purpose. It was an odd juxtaposition of emotions.

Calliope and Jericho were leaned against the registrar's counter talking with Eugene and Sibyl as I arrived back into the colosseum lobby. I didn't see Nuzzle, but he was probably curled up in the nape of someone's cloak, knowing him.

They hadn't noticed me, so I pulled my hood up, kept to the far wall, and headed to the exit. I couldn't bring them into this. They'd been here so long, just trying to live their lives and make the most of it.

They had their reasons for not advancing. It wasn't my place to interfere with that. To ask them to change the way they'd chosen to exist here and put them in potential danger.

So I'd go it alone.

Trix hadn't given me much to work with. I had two tasks.

First, to persuade the Shepherds to give up their crusade, and if that failed… kill their leader. Another human. I really hoped the persuading worked, but for that I was going to need respect. A reputation. Valciferous had helped me take the first step there, introducing me to the Reapers of the Meadow as the first Death Affinity in generations. Word would spread fast. I'd gain reverence, but also resentment, Trix had warned.

Second, to deal with the Unchained scourge, figure out why they'd become more prevalent and aggressive recently, and deal with the source. It seemed none of the gods understood what was going on with them—at least so they claimed.

I still didn't understand why Valciferous needed me to do these things. Why couldn't Trix do this? Or some other high ranking Reaper under his employ? I was missing something crucial. I suspected it had something to do with my Death Affinity. That's the only thing that made me unique. But I didn't know how that connected with these seemingly independent issues.

I stepped out onto the street. Reapers and Penumbrans bustled about. Claustrophobia struck among the congestion of people and buildings. I spotted Calliope's white stallion, Allium, among the chaos. He trotted along lazily, untethered, chewing on bits of grass and weeds that stuck through the cobblestones.

Damn, she trained that creature well.

I needed a place to think, to just be alone and feel peace for a moment.

I remembered the ceiling mural inside the Meridian Tavern. I remembered where I wished I'd been that fateful night during my bachelor party instead of camping.

The sea.

There was one here too. Everyone ignored it because there was nothing there.

I'd go to the sea.

I eyed Calliope’s horse, plodding over to a new tuft of grass.

And I knew just how I’d get there.
