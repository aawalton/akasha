The heli-jet burned through the night sky. Elion peered out of a small window. Black shapes and shadows rushed past, whether mountains or clouds, Elion couldn’t tell.

His head hurt. Keyla, Selna, Tuft, and Yaret all seemed to be sleeping. Despite his burning eyes and heavy lids, Elion remained awake. He couldn’t sleep.

Zev and Isalyn sat up in the cockpit, muttering occasionally on their headsets.

Elion was ready to go home. Back to earth, where he didn’t have to worry about laser guns, or cyborgs, or Aurelian oaths. He missed playing video games. He hoped that whatever Zev had planned for breaking Liora out would be fast and smooth.

Zev would zap them all back home. Their house would be repaired, and Snickers would come back. Everything would be normal again.

Keyla would be sad. Maybe she would come with them? They’d have to work on fixing her memories first.

But Elion wouldn’t be able to look at Snickers the same way again, knowing that the cat was really Tuft in disguise.

He’d get a new cat. A real one.

Elion jerked awake. Light shone through the windows on the right hand side of the craft. A kaleidoscope of color filled the sky; the broken shards of the celestial sphere reflecting the morning light.

Zev stepped into the small room and took a seat beside Elion. He raised his voice to be heard over the noise of the engines.

“We were heading to a safe staging area on the outskirts of Erod,” Zev said. “Our forces were preparing for a full scale assault on the Palace. Scheduled for a few weeks for now.”

“Were preparing?” Elion asked. “What happened?”

“Tephalians,” Zev said. “We’ve been monitoring the increase in probing attacks, and today a sensor array in deep space detected an incoming warship. They’ve decided that Praxis is too weak to deflect them and they’re coming in for the kill.”

“More Tephalians?” Elion shook his head, then rested it in his hands. “What does that mean for us? We’re out of time to escape back to Earth? You’re talking about leaving Liora behind?”

Zev rubbed his brow. “Elion,” he said, looking concerned. “We need to get you to the Golden Throne. That’s the only power that can stop the Tephalian invasion.

A sinking feeling swelled in Elion’s gut. “I thought only the true heir could operate the power of the throne,” he said. “That’s not me. It’s Liora.”

Zev sighed. “That’s right. But should something happen to her, it would be you. We want you both there. Besides, your destiny is here, on Kylios. We need to defend this planet—”

“I’m from Earth,” Elion said. “All I’ve wanted, all along, has just been to find Liora and go home. I hate this place.” As he spoke he glanced around at the Kylians filling the hold. People who’d helped him, fought alongside him. People that he didn’t just want to abandon.

Then he thought of Tharnen dying, blood leaking from his neck. Of Domas, breaking apart as he desperately drove the bomb to the crystal. His hands still felt the crack of bashing Brynna’s head on the ground.

He what he’d been forced to do here.

“This planet is falling apart,” Elion muttered. “Dorian, Tephalians… I miss the time when my biggest concern was Liora staying out too late. Can’t we just get her and go home?”

Zev pursed his lips. “Your mother always intended to bring you back here, when you were old enough.”

Elion shook his head. “I’m tired of all this. Please Zev. Leave me out of it. I’ll abdicate my right to the throne. You can have it. This is your planet.”

“Fine. But that doesn’t change anything. We still need to go to the Golden Throne. It’s the only way to open a portal that can send you back to Earth.”

Elion sighed heavily.

“There’s one more problem,” Zev said. “Liora might already be using the throne.”

“What? Why is that a problem?” Elion asked. “That’s great!”

“It’s bad because it means Dorian thinks she’s going to do what he asks her to. Otherwise he’d never let her sit on it. She might not want to go home.”

Elion’s face darkened. He chewed on his thumbnail, not liking what Zev suggested.

The jet shook, and Zev grabbed a seat belt, strapping himself in.

“A bit of turbulance!” Isalyn called from the cockpit. “Hold on!”

Everyone straightened. Keyla’s knuckles whitened as she gripped the edge of her seat. The jet bounced around aggressively for a few more moments, then smoothed out.

“Sorry about that,” Isalyn shouted. “That came outta nowhere!”

“We’re going to drop your friends off at the staging area,” Zev said. “We’ll pick up a team and head into Erod. We’ve already set off several distractions, and we should be able to make it to the palace quickly.”

Zev fished around in a pocket and produced a pouch. He pushed it into Elion’s hands.

“These are ascendency Stones. We’ll stop in at an altar en route to the palace, and you can level up. We need all the strength we can get, because Dorian will be amassing his Black Wall warlocks at the palace.”

Elion shifted uncomfortably in his seat. “I don’t know how much more I can do,” he said. “I was suffering from ascendency fatigue pretty badly last night.”

“You’ll be fine,” Zev said, clapping a hand on his shoulder. “You’ve had plenty of time to rest and recover.”

Elion looked at the pouch of stones in his hands. He took a deep breath, fighting back disappointment. He’d been so relieved to see Zev. But, his work here wasn’t done yet.

“How long until we get to the rest area?”

“Less than an hour,” Zev said. “We’re skirting the southern edge of Erod now. We’re not going to disembark. We’ll drop off your friends and go, so say your goodbyes now.”

Zev rose, heading back toward the cockpit. The jet shook slightly, then dropped suddenly. Zev fell to the ground, slamming down hard as they shook in aggressive turbulence.

“What the—” Zev shouted, but was cut off as the vehicle rocked violently. Metal groaned.

Elion’s heart raced.

“I don’t know what that was!” Isalyn shouted as the shaking stopped. “That wasn’t normal turbulence.”

Zev scrambled to a seat and secured himself. “What do you mean?” he shouted at the pilot.

“All my days,” Isalyn exclaimed. “We've been picked up. We’ve got incoming fire!”

Elion clutched the pouch of ascendency stones in his hands and gritted his teeth. He could do this. Now that Zev was here; he wouldn’t have to try and make every decision himself. They could get Liora, and get home.

He wasn’t going to be swayed by any of Zev’s attempts to convince him to stay here.

Elion secured the stones in a pocket, and looked out the window. Zev and Isalyn continued shouting to each other, calling out technical details that didn’t mean anything to Elion.

Through the window the twilight city sparkled. Lights shown in buildings, roads stretching out to the horizon. Erod was massive.

As Elion watched, bright flashes of light bloomed from several points in the city, followed shortly by loud pops around the get.

“Anti Aircraft fire!” Isalyn shouted, and Zev swore as he pulled open an emergency storage container. He distributed brown canvas bags to everyone, and demonstrated how to correctly wear the device like a backpack and secure the harness.

“Parachutes?” Elion yelled as more explosions rocked the craft.

“I hope we don’t have to use them!” Yaret called.

“Just a precaution!” Zev said. “We’re not going to make it to the save zone, now that they’ve clocked us. We’re rerouting to a new destination and calling for an evac!”

Elion pulled the straps of the parachute on over his shoulders, securing the buckles around his legs and across his chest.

“How did they find us?” Selna asked.

Zev shook his head. “Someone’s operating the Golden Throne,” he said.

“Someone?” Elion exclaimed. “You mean Liora!”
