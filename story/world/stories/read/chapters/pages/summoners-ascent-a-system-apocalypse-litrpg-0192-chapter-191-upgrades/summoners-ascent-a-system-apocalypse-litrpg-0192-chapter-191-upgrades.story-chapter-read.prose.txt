When some time passed and Ashton could feel the warmth on his face and the smell of morning dew couldn't be ignored anymore, he faced the stove. This was the last thing that Ashton hadn't linked to the tent and upgraded.

Though, beside the bed, the 'upgrades' were a little lacklustre. The chest was a little larger, the pillows more comfortable to sit on, and the wooden table increased in size a bit, too.

The tent itself was a little larger than it had been before as well, though only from the inside. It wasn't a huge difference either. Enough for someone to notice that things might be a little off when walking inside, but probably not enough to be able to pinpoint what that 'off' thing was.

After connecting the stove to the Living Abode, Ashton tried to look at the 'potential' that it had. And actually, there was something here that Ashton didn't quite expect, but welcomed thoroughly.

It was a potential developed even before the Living Abode became a thing, when the salamander was sleeping within the stove, it had become developed as a sort of 'nest'. A place for the salamander to rest, while their heat was used to warm the tent. There was another great benefit of a 'nest', though.

While an elemental was resting within it, their active summoning period was vastly extended. It could still run out sooner or later, but it could effectively stretch hours worth of summoning time into days worth.

Of course, it always still depended a bit on the quality of the nest and how much the elemental liked it, but either way, this would be great. Plus, if Ashton could develop other similar potential, that might come in useful, too.

A nest for the undine could allow something like plumbing or water filtering, the sylph would purify the air even in conditions when Ashton had to camp out in toxic conditions. The gnome might be able to help out in some kind of indoor garden, where Ashton could plant things for potion-making.

Ashton smiled lightly. The process of making this a 'nest' was a little more complicated than just upgrading other things, but it would mostly require some special ingredients, loads more mana and a little bit of interference and control from Ashton himself. Though the first thing that Ashton did was 'repair' the stove, trying to get rid of all the rust that had built up on the surface, filling out the scratches and smoothing out the bumps. It took a bit of the scrap metal that Ashton had collected, but otherwise it was fine.

As for turning it into a nest, that was a little more complicated. The Living Abode was asking Ashton for some sort of magical fuel as one of the central ingredients. There were a few different options, though the simplest one was making charcoal using fire with high concentration of magic.

It was actually simple enough. He could starve a metal container of air using the sylph, and concentrate the salamander's fire to a high enough heat. Though the problem was that Ashton would have to keep at it and fuel that fire with his mana for some time.

Then again, he did also have some flame magic stones that contained powerful magical flames within them, which he got from the mixed bag of magic stones he received as a reward when reaching level 10.

He could use one of those, but he had so few of them that it felt almost like a waste. At least one of them was required to help the 'lesser Salamander' advance to just a 'Salamander'. Preferably more. It wasn't easy to capture such a magical flame and create this type of magic stone, and he probably wouldn't get to a point where he could do it before the Salamander reached level 20.

Well, whichever it was, for the time being Ashton couldn't upgrade this yet. Which didn't mean that it wasn't a great place for the Salamander to take a nap while Ashton cooked some breakfast and continued sorting out a few more things.

The past few hours were mostly spent organising things in the the Backpacker's storage using the spirit's new ability. All the potions formerly in multiple bottles were now in one each, and the magic stones and other materials gathered from monsters were sorted into the pouches.

As for other items, they were mostly kept loosely within the bag or, in case of items with big groupings like all the potion-making equipment, were put into the chests.

During this process, Ashton learned a few things.

The maximum volume of each storage item was 1 cubic foot. Again, the actual dimensions of the objects didn't matter, just the actual 'volume'. Once Ashton created ten storage items, he had to wait until this ability recharged, which took about half an hour for one new storage container.

Now, there were also two different 'modes' Ashton could create when it came to these items. One was the 'permanent storage' mode. In this case, the storage containers were tied to the Backpacker, and with that, Ashton's channelling range. That meant that this way, items couldn't be handed out.

However, there was the 'one-time storage' mode. In this case, it worked the same as the chest did before. The items could be taken out once, nothing could be put back in, and once everything was taken out of the storage container it disappeared in a puff of magic.

These containers, however, took twice as much magic to create as the permanent ones, probably to account for the fact that they had to be taken out of the channelling range.

Whichever the case, this ability was still a massive boost in what could be stored in the Backpacker's bag, and allowed Ashton to take a lot more materials than what he had been able to up to this point.

But this new ability of the Backpacker wasn't the only great improvement that had been made. The new ability that the Pen of Insight gained was great, too.

The innate ability that the Pen of Insight possessed was called 'Heavenly Scripture'. It was a fancy name, but it essentially was supposed to mean that the Pen received information on the target from the 'heavens' to then write it down.

The new ability followed that theme, and was called 'Earthly Depiction'. Instead of drawing forth the hidden information that couldn't be seen directly, this new ability allowed for the opposite. It let the Pen of Insight depict the world exactly and truly as it was.

Ashton grabbed a piece of paper on the table next to him. On it was a simple drawing of the Salamander, which Ashton had made the Pen draw as a test. All of Ashton's summons, particularly the elementals, appeared quite clearly in his mind. Their forms, the stances they took; it was all there in immense detail in his head, so this was a good test.

And Ashton had to admit that this drawing was quite magnificent. It really showed the Salamander sleeping peacefully within the stove, as the fire crackled around it. You could almost see the yawn that the Salamander was suppressing, and if the elemental weren't resting not even a step away, Ashton would think it was about to crawl from the page.

Since it was drawn with magical ink, Ashton could see it perfectly fine, too. This might be a good way to try and figure out the state of town at a distance, now that Ashton's vision was so limited.

Though in that line of thought, there was something else that the pen could do now. Ashton took a brief walk around the schoolgrounds earlier, effectively trying to take in every part of this place at least briefly, while linked to the Pen of Insight.

And when he came back to the tent, he asked the Pen to interpret all that Ashton 'saw' through the wisp-sight in a particular way. What came out of that interpretation was what he was looking at now.

It was a map of the schoolgrounds. Due to the Pen's other ability, it was able to even mark down points of interest, such as the gate, certain storage areas, Rory's shed, and the Living Abode itself.

Of course, technically, Ashton had everything in his head already and he wouldn't necessarily need these drawings or maps, but this was one of the most amazing things he could ask for to hand out to other people.

Now that this ability was a thing, he could hand out drawings of important magical plants en-masse, and if this new ability had access to all the information the Pen had already gathered, as long as he prepared all the ink, Ashton could immediately upgrade all the info available to the people of Drakewood.

Plus, the maps that Ashton could create with absolutely irrefutably precise information would help people move from camp to camp safely while not having to deal with certain types of monsters, as they stuck to particular areas of town in their own hunting grounds.

This was a great way for the people of this town to develop information further using magical knowledge they couldn't themselves access.

However, there was one more thing that, while maybe a little wasteful at times, was something that helped Ashton in his current situation quite a lot. He could practically have the Pen copy down every image or text that Ashton wanted it to, allowing him to see it through the mana in its ink.

Essentially, this meant one big thing... Ashton could finally read the posts in the community on his own again!

The community was a great tool for this world, so losing out on being able to properly access it 90% of the time was utterly terrible news.

Sure, the Backpacker was keeping track of it anyway, but having others read the posts out to him all the time when he was actually back at a camp was annoying.

For now, Ashton used this to catch up on a lot of things in the community. The highlight reel that the Backpacker had picked out for him. He decided to read through all of that while finishing up the breakfast.

By the time the almost alluring scent of sausages, eggs, and toast filled the tent, he felt pretty caught up on a lot of things.

Particularly on the fact that things weren't really looking great right now. Market Street and the University camp were fairly neutral, but there were people affiliated with them that seemed to cause quite a lot of trouble with barely any consequences.

The camp at the outskirts of town that Ashton hadn't visited yet was staying small and keeping mostly out of things, not accepting anyone from the outside anymore.

As for the ones that are the absolute worst news, it was the Police camp and the Cathedral camp.

The police were basically forcing people under their protection whenever they could, and then didn't allow them to effectively level up. Only the members of the police force, or those that were considered their 'trainees' were allowed to take part in not just any sort of combat, but use any skill whatsoever.

And on the other hand, the Cathedral camp... they started holding daily sermons. They rung the church's bells every single day, and then prayed to this new power this world had received through the system.

They were apocalyptic cultists.
