Fiona returned a minute later with two bottles in hand: one for Norbert, and one for the other guards who had slowly made their way to the table at the insistence of the senior dragon.

To say the mood was tense was an understatement. Fiona opened the bottle slowly and smelled the cork a moment later. “Oh, that is good, Miss Brightstone. Where did you acquire this one?”

“It reminded me of an energetic elf I knew,” she replied coyly. “That one is…let me see, from a farm the Bar’dathi run on the plains, just next to the Sylvan Forest. Gorgeous place.”

Karlin grunted. “I think I know the area. My brother used to–” he sucked in a sharp breath, and realized he’d said too much. “Never mind.” His gaze returned to his father. “I thought you were in the Arkantines, doing who-knows-what.”

“I was. Then I heard about what was happening in Fiefdala and Vale.” Norbert gently accepted the glass of wine from Fiona, swirling it gently before tipping it back carefully with his snout. The slight recess on the glass for those with muzzles or snouts really was a convenience for many on Cepalune.

He then set down the glass gently after taking a sip. “Oh, this is good.” Fiona, in the meantime, distributed the other bottle–the one that Bonnie had subtly enchanted to spur a quick reaction for them to need to vacate the premises. She just hoped they got the timing right. “Now, given our esteemed guest, I’ll delay my…conversation with my son. Apologies if there was any unpleasantness.”

Darla shrugged softly while preparing a small slab of seasoned Rorganth steak. “Suzie’s got the right idea. Sometimes people need to unwind over a good meal. Do tell, how do you prefer your steak, Mister Fierkraag?”

“Medium rare. I think my son still prefers his wolf raw, yes?” Norbert seemed to smile softly at the noncommittal grunt from Karlin.

But after a few seconds, he relented. “Medium rare,” Karlin finally answered. “Douglas used to like his wolf raw. Not sure if he still does.”

Hey, watch it, mister. You got a stay of execution; don’t push it. Fiona wasn’t the only one agitated by his comment, as Missy gave him a stern look, if brief.

The mood had eased over the table. Somewhat. She needed to keep the talk going somewhere, figure out Norbert’s angle , and how it related to him trying to take over the retreat by legalese from Douglas.

There it was. “I notice you speak of Douglas in the past tense. Surely he isn’t–”

Karlin's hand clenched into the armrest. “No, I don’t want to talk about him further.”

“No?” Norbert equally gripped his armrest, and Fiona heard the strain and creak of wood being pushed to near its breaking point. “You’re not going to talk about how you two used to get along so well? You were egg brothers and friends.”

“Not anymore.” Karlin didn’t even look his father’s way as he said that. “You know this, father. And I–”

Darla flipped a delicious appetizer onto his plate without hesitation, interrupting him. “So I had to take a guess, but how does this look?”

Karlin raised a brow. “Underlune trout. Crispy, salty, a touch of acid with the glaze, the tiniest bit of sweet…” The dragon looked amazed. “This is…really good.”

Missy also stole a bite off his plate, much to his disappointment. She apparently wasn’t asking for permission anymore, much to Fiona’s delight. She, too, was amazed by the light, fluffy fish. “It’s enchanted, Miss Brightstone. Fish with just a hint of crisps.” She also tried the chipped tubers, a bright orange crisp with just a hint of browning, and it made a satisfying crunch. “Oh, I’m stealing this, Karlin.”

“But I–”

Fiona smiled internally as Missy took the plate out from underneath the dragon, nearly twice her height, and looked like a puppy scolded by its owner. Fiona saw a hint of amusement crease the elder Fierkraag’s face, but he said nothing. “Miss Brightstone, I must know…this seems to be a fusion dish, is it not?”

“Oh, the wild tubers are from above ground, but the Underlune trout is hard to come by. They hide in the little brashish pools you find all over, and they use little wing fins to jump from puddle to puddle. It’s utterly fascinating. And tough to catch them,” she added with a light purr. “I knew this person from Earth who got summoned and talked about fish and chips. The chips were something like our tubers, but plainer, really bland, so they’d fry them up. It was a dish of royalty.”

Fiona remembered getting smacked in the face by a tail while they were getting the Rorganth steaks. Best not to remember that part.

Missy blinked in surprise. “Who…was this person? This sounds like something from…home.”

Darla let out a soft smile. “Oh, she was a warrior, this one. Tall, fearless, but with a soft spot for good food and good friends. High energy, and I mean that in a good way.”

Darla, you are so daring to get us this close to being busted. Fiona cleared her throat as she helped Darla plate the meals. “Appropriating meals from across the cosmos now?”

“With my own spin. The truffle oil I use on them is phenomenal.” She then distributed the plates across the table, acting cool, calm, and natural like this wasn’t a high-stakes dinner. “The stuffed crawfish, though, that would be my father’s dish. He taught me that one.”

A minute later, the meal hit home, with everyone complimenting it. Even the two dragons had set aside their differences. For now. She wondered if it would last, and she had her doubts. The truce, as implied, would last until dinner’s end.

Which meant she needed to get the ball rolling. “So Mister Fierkraag–”

“Call me Norbert. Please,” he emphasized with a slight bow. “You seem a curiosity. You don’t act like just a server, Miss Suzie.”

“I’m a personality,” she responded politely. “Plus, I’ve worked with Miss Brightstone for some time now.”

“She does liven up the conversation,” Darla answered, with a playful swish of her tail. “Anyway, what do you think, Norbert?”

The dragon spent a long time cogitating on that answer before smiling. “I think your father would be proud that his daughter is just about as good as he is. I presume you’ve kept in touch?”

“Was supposed to meet up with him in the coming week, during the holidays. I happened to be in the area,” she answered in an assuring tone. “I suppose he misses me a bit since I live topside now. But we do get together every year for a week or two. Usually right around now.”

Norbert cogitated on this. “So why the pop-up cafe?”

She shrugged. “Spur of the moment. Got a few volunteers and we went to town. The place we visited was in dire need of smiles and filled bellies.”

Karlin nodded at that, strangely. Fiona wondered if this whole evil dragon thing was a facade. He still was on the hook for many crimes, and would likely be spending decades in a Fiefdalian prison. “I meant to ask; I saw the artwork. Is this yours, Norbert?” she inquired.

The dragon nodded. “A…collection from the years. I see my son kept it in good standing. Including the one in the other room?

Fiona wondered if it was the mind-affecting tablet, because Karlin glanced at it. “I’m surprised you kept it. All things considered. You threw out all your other beliefs–”

Fiona heard the crack of the glass in Norbert’s hand, fortunately empty, and saw a deeply furrowed brow. That is not a step in the right direction; need to redirect this, quickly!  “Oh, oopsie, let me take care of that–”

“Are you really going to say that, now, after I just sat down to extend a fig branch?” Norbert bristled. “Son…I have not always said the right thing at the right time, and I drove your mother nuts with some of those things. But to suggest I didn’t value her words or her beliefs…especially after what happened…”

He cleared his throat. “Apologies, Suzie, Miss Brightstone. Would you excuse my son and me for a few minutes? We need to clear the air.” He gave a wicked gaze at Karlin, who shoved away from the table.

“The main course will be finished in a few minutes.” Darla, calm under any level of pressure, kept on cooking with the flames on the stove on high, and she slowly did her magic, along with Bonnie passing items from the kitchen from time to time.

Fiona fumed internally. Great. How am I supposed to sneak away again? Stubborn dragons. Before she could come up with a plan, Missy stood up abruptly from the table, stopping by to tap Fiona. “Hey, I need your help on something. Take a few minutes with me?”

“Huh? Oh, uh, sure.” Fiona followed Missy, who took her hand gently and guided her back to the library. Okay, is this an opportunity, or am I about to get busted? I think she knows something’s up, based on the looks from the table. If it’s an opportunity, I try not to take advantage of her. If we’re busted, I…well, I hope I don’t have to face that scenario.

As soon as she was through the doorway, Missy locked it shut. She let out a frustrated groan, running her fingers through her hair. “I knew it, I knew it, I knew it. I knew this was a bust. It was a last-ditch effort. There is no getting that damn dragon to admit fault or when he’s wrong.”

“Uh…context, Missy. Look, I know we just met, and you barely know me–”

“Of course, I know you. How could I not?” Missy’s gaze grew forlorn. “I just…I think I let it slide because…I know what it’s like.” She leaned against the door, arms folded, her posture anything but composed or confident.

Fiona blinked. Crap. I’m busted. Maybe. “What do you mean?”

Missy smiled faintly, laughing bitterly. “You’re from Earth. Aren’t you? Or you were, until they dragged you down to this hell. This place.” She gestured around the room.

She knows I’m from Earth. She doesn’t know who I am. Okay, play this cool. “So, what gave it away?” she asked with a touch of reluctance. “It was the book, wasn’t it? Alice in Wonderland. A girl lost in an imaginary land, unwilling to face growing up. The illusion replaces reality because the truth is hard to face. And I think I know why you know that, too.”

Fiona took a quiet breath. “How long have you been on Cepalune?”

The answer was reluctant. A gaze down and to the right indicated as such, or the clench of her fingernails into the hand across her chest. “A long time, Suzie. Too long. Do you know how I got here? On my way home from school, suburban Windsor, Connecticut. I was eleven. Backpack in hand. Linkin Park's first album was all the rage…then…it was gone.”

She snapped her fingers for emphasis. “Goodbye, mom, dad. I doubt anyone ever understood how a girl could disappear off the face of the planet as I did. Next thing I knew, someone had a collar ready for me. And a mission.”

She rubbed uncomfortably at the tight collar. One that had not grown,even though she did. “I did nothing wrong, and was brought to a hell that I wouldn’t wish on anyone, Suzie. You’ve never known what it's like to be a piece of property. To not have rights. Where your very life hinges on the whim or the amusement of those in power. But I vowed they’d never break me.”

She tapped the collar again. “Memento, for me. See, there was one utterly fatal flaw with a collar like this that keeps me alive, protects me against acts of…creative solutions.” She swallowed uncomfortably. “It kept me alive. It also got me thinking. I have fire-based mark powers. So, knowing that, I waited until they got together for one of their galas in Aegortin…the bunch of pricks tried to turn me into an agent saboteur, like they do lots of other times, then send us to the corners of the world to soften their adversaries. I vowed I’d rather die than help them. And preferably, take a lot of them with me.”

That sounds eerily familiar.

Missy laughed bitterly. “Kidnap a kid, make her a trained killer, try to make her forget her world and be nothing more than a tool? No. They made a mistake giving me the knowledge they did. Aegortin’s one weakness is its arrogance, if anything. I was supposed to destroy Fiefdala from the inside out, destroy supply lines, and sabotage them. Well, they trained me well for that.” She wore a grim smile after that.

“Then I dropped an inferno at my feet, watched them burn, and ran. I made it all the way to Vale before I got busted for something different. They never discovered who I was or what I did, because Karlin bought my contract. Then he destroyed it.”

She let out a soft sigh. “Deep down, Karlin is a good person. But it is so, so hard to see sometimes. He gave me a second lease on life. One that…I’m not sure I deserve.”

Missy unfolded her arms. “Your turn. Who are you, really?”

Fiona took a soft breath. Well, here’s to an idea I never thought I’d try.  “Someone who wants to smash a hammer into his skull, repeatedly. Someone hurt by his antics,” Fiona answered. “But that doesn’t fix anything. As for who I am… I’m someone who wants to bring some semblance of a fortune of heart back to Karlin’s broken family–him, Norbert, and Doug.”

Missy gasped. “Hang on. Are you…who I think you are?”

“Yep. I’m the Merchant of Fortune.”
