Despite Sedrick informing them of the lich’s rough location, the team still had to settle in for the long haul to actually get to the lich. The heart of the forest was a hundred and forty miles farther into the forest than the druid’s cabin, which meant they had their work cut out for them.

On average, the team was capable of hiking roughly thirty miles through the dense forest on a good day. But after factoring in the spread of deadly monsters that continued trying to ambush them seemingly at every turn, that slowed them down a little bit. It was enough that Asher was tempted to roll the dice and just have Samantha fly them straight to the heart of the forest, but they all agreed the risk simply wasn’t worth it. It would be another story if they had an exact location for their target, but ‘the heart of the forest’ was still a massive area to cover. If the lich spotted them coming through the air, he would have ample time to run away while they searched the center. And if Jormon didn’t leave anything for Rulfar to track him with, there would be no finding him a second time.

So it was that Asher found himself wiping the seemingly acidic blood off Crescent four days later, sighing as he kicked at the latest monster to have tried to do them in. This one looked like some sort of bulbous penguin with snakes growing out of its sides, each one with acid dripping from its open mouth. While the days of frequent combat were solid training, his skills were high enough at this point that he’d only received a single level up in Identify and a decent chunk of shards for their trouble. None of the monsters had really threatened him in any way, and it seemed systematically scything through them wasn’t doing him any real favors.

As the group began making their way toward the heart of the forest once more without even needing to share a word, Asher couldn’t help but think about his two new blended skills. While certainly useful, each one was a pain to level, and unlike most of his other skills, he was going to have to actually devote some time to specifically work on improving them one of these days. The biggest issue of course being that he rarely had downtime to work with in the first place. When he did, he preferred spending it practicing with his far-more-exciting skills instead of wandering the markets looking for objects with meaning imbued into them, or purposefully lopping body parts off.

It wasn’t long before his gaze shifted slightly farther down his interface toward his three evolved resistances. It had been quite some time since he’d really bothered to think about them, largely due to the fact that the cost had grown so extreme. Purchasing a new resistance or evolving an already purchased one both cost ten times as many shards as the previous purchase. The very first buy at only a single shard had been easy enough, and evolving it for ten was a no brainer. But after having made six purchases, that meant his next resistance was going to cost him a million shards, and then evolving it would cost an additional ten million. His next resistance purchase would most likely be his final one, and Asher was still hesitant to pull the trigger.

While he didn’t exactly regret snagging his evolved truth resistance, as it had given him the power to resist truthseekers and artifacts that could detect the truth, he probably wouldn’t have made the same choice a second time. However, Scrying and Darkness were still picks that he would absolutely make again if he had to do it all over, as each one had seen near-constant use over the last couple of months.

“Hey guys,” Asher started, nodding his thanks toward Rulfar as the bloodhound held a large branch to the side so it wouldn’t swing back and smack the rest of them in the face. “Out of curiosity, what do you think my fourth resistance should be? I asked Rosh this ages ago for my very first one, and he had some valuable insight. I’ll admit I’m curious what you all think.”

“That’s a tricky question, seeing as you already have Complete Resilience in your back pocket,” Rulfar admitted. “At its current level, it seems as though the skill is either as strong or even stronger than most evolved resistances on their own. Not to even mention your ridiculous purge and immunity effects. With that in mind, selecting a resistance solely for the evolved effect is probably what you should focus on.”

“Carrot is right,” Moxy said, grinning as she ducked underneath the branch Rulfar let go to snap back into her face. “For example, I grabbed an evolved blood resistance just to make sure Knick or any other Blood-element users wouldn’t be able to try anything. That way I don’t need to worry about leaving my blood all over the place when I’m fighting. It’s not a complete immunity, but it’s pretty thorough, and the evolved form directs me toward anyone bold enough to try such a thing.”

“Make sense,” Asher muttered, wondering if he shouldn’t do the same. Thanks to having Eternal Regeneration to rely on, Asher ended up spilling a lot more blood than Moxy ever did. And while he could probably just switch his immunity to Blood and call it a day, he’d never be able to shift it to another element unless he figured out who was trying to do him in. There was a chance Remote Control might end up fixing that problem after it was evolved once or twice, however, so he was a little hesitant moving forward with that line of thinking. Being able to Recall his own spilled blood back to him like he did his leg against The Lost Lady would be quite convenient.

“What about focusing on the few things that could still potentially kill you?” Samantha asked. “I’ve never regretted my evolved disease resistance, even before heading into this forest.”

“I don’t know… Eternal Regeneration is a hairsbreadth from hitting level 40 and reaching its third tier. I have no idea what the newest effect will be, but I’m hoping it will make me even more impossible to kill.”

“Want me to speed things up for you?” Moxy offered, raising an eyebrow as she mimed punching him in the face. “Bet I could tip that over in a couple of minutes.”

“Honestly, it’s a tempting offer,” Asher admitted. “I know I’ve gotten better at not taking on horrific wounds lately, but it’s a little frustrating that it’s still at level 39.”

“The evolution will come in time. Don’t try to force it,” Rulfar said, his eyes looking slightly glazed over as he no doubt checked his own interface. “Damn, I was hoping there would be a void resistance, but it seems the system doesn’t offer one. Just our luck.”

“That sort of makes sense at least. Void seems to just… erase whatever it touches,” Asher pointed out, laying a protective hand on the hilt of his own void dagger. “I’m not sure how one would become resistant to that.”

“Kroult, what do you think?” Samantha asked, giving the silent lich a small smile as she glanced back at him. “You spent decades wandering around remote villages before getting roped up with all the Council stuff. Do you have any resistance recommendations?”

“Disease is never a bad pick, as you yourself already stated. Though Rulfar has a point. Asher’s Complete Resilience and Eternal Regeneration largely negate most of the general resistance selections I would recommend for most people,” Kroult said. “In my opinion, it might be wise to go with a more obscure choice. My first suggestion would have been truth, but seeing as he already has that one… what about time?”

“A time resistance?” Asher asked, frowning as he pulled up the list and skimmed through it. Sure enough, the list that seemed to have a resistance for everything under the sun beyond void did have time as an option. “Why time?”

“Time is one of the few elements that can still truly ‘hurt’ you in a way,” Kroult explained. “While you may not receive physical damage, it can snatch away memories as though they had never happened. And unlike someone with a Mind element who would still have to fight past your Complete Resilience, it seems Time works a bit differently.”

“That’s not a bad idea at all…” Asher muttered as he turned it over in his head. “However, I think it would end up being really annoying as well. I mean, Donvath uses Timeline pretty frequently from what I’ve seen, rewinding time just a few seconds here and there to stop himself from putting his foot in his mouth during conversations. If I was suddenly dragged along for the ride anytime he undid time, that would get old very quickly. Not to mention if I got dragged along anytime anyone with a Time element did so. There have to be others across the world with the element, even if it’s almost impossible to get. I wouldn’t say no to an artifact to grant such a resistance, but a resistance? There’s no way to turn those off, so I don’t think I’d want one for time.”

“So similar to what evolving the scrying resistance does,” Asher said, deciding that was an excellent idea. Doubling up on resisting any sort of identification skill wouldn’t be a bad idea. I was never a fan of the idea that certain people could look at me and just know all about my skills. We’re only going to be going up against more powerful people from this point forward, so keeping what I can do hidden sounds like an excellent idea.”
