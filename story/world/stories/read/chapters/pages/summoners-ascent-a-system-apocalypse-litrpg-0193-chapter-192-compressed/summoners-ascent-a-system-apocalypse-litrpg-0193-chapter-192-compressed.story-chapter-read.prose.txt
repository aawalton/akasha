As the smell of freshly-made breakfast filled the tent, Millie slowly twisted and turned in the bed. She pushed herself up and leaned against the headboard, rubbing the sleep out of her eyes.

"Hm?" she let out, just as Ashton put down a plate for her on the table.

"Morning. Get yourself cleaned up, eat something, and then we'll take care of some last business here before heading out," Ashton said, crunching up a piece of toast between his teeth.

Millie slowly nodded, clearly still half-asleep, as she dragged her feet out from under the blanket. She wobbled over to the table and plopped down on the soft pillow in front of it.

"Ah, wait, brush your teeth first," Ashton said, clicking his finger. He had put a jug of water into the corner of the room, and used the undine to have some stream out into a cup that he quickly placed in front of Millie, together with a fresh toothbrush and -paste.

She grumbled lightly and grabbed the toothbrush, just staring down at the tube of minty toothpaste with a sense of immediate defeat.

Ashton reached over and opened it for her, squeezing a small bit of it onto the bristles. Silently, Millie began to brush her teeth.

"Urgh... shpishy..." A discomforted grimace twisted her face, but Millie still kept going.

'Hm, guess I'll have to pick up some other toothpaste for her," Ashton thought. There really were a lot of parts that he didn't consider much. Back in Lumia, the only way Ashton had to brush his teeth was by chewing on some particularly bristly roots for a while.

When Millie was done, she set down the brush and took a swig of water from the cup, though she just ended up holding it in for a while, not sure what to do.

"Just spit it back out into the cup, I'll take care of it," Ashton said, and Millie slowly nodded.

That was probably something Ashton should prepare sooner rather than later. Basically, the moment the Living Abode gained the ability to create separate rooms, Ashton would create a proper bathroom. That would resolve a lot of discomfort in numerous ways.

Finding a bush or squatting behind some rubbish bins while hoping nothing was about to jump at you from below wasn't exactly how Ashton wanted to deal with these things forever.

Ashton shivered at the bed memories creeping up his back, as Millie handed him the cup. And with a quick, calculated motion... he just splashed the water outside the front of the tent. Didn't even use the undine to help with that.

He put the cup over to the side, into a small wooden crate where he was trying to keep the dirty dishes until he had time to clean them. And now, finally, Millie could eat.

Her sausages were already cut up into bite-sized pieces, as she couldn't do so herself, though Ashton soon learned that this was mostly unnecessary.

The way she was eating, just like last night, reminded him of the wild feeding of a hungry beast. Or the way Ashton was eating when he freshly came back to earth.

"Calm down, you don't want to choke," Ashton said, pouring her a cup of water just in case, which came in quickly during the coughing fit that followed.

But even that didn't make her slow down. Instead, it was like she wanted to drown the toast in the water that was now puffing up her cheeks.

She ate so fast that she actually finished before Ashton did. Only then did she slow down for a moment, though her attention was almost wholly focused on the rest of the sausages in the cooling pan behind Ashton. He had originally prepared them for the wisp, since the meat would otherwise go bad soon, but waiting until he knew if Millie was still hungry felt like a good idea.

He got up and smiled, giving her another few of the small breakfast sausages. "Want some eggs, too? More bread?"

"Yes, please," she replied, eagerly watching as Ashton cut the sausages up. If he read that look on her eyes correctly, she would clearly prefer to just grab them with her hands and bite into them just like that.

Truly, seeing Millie like this was nothing but gut-wrenching. Sure, kids didn't always have the best table manners, though this wasn't what was going on. Millie ate like she didn't know when she would have this chance again. Ashton specifically talked to Rory about the food situation here just the other day, and he knew that it shouldn't be too bad.

Were other kids stealing her food? Or was this just a habit that had developed even before this all began?

Whichever it was... Ashton wanted her to not have to wolf down her food like that.

Once Millie was done, and her whole face was covered in grease and breadcrumbs, Ashton got up and grabbed the girl's plate before she could lick it clean.

He clicked his fingers and moved some water from the jug in the corner over into a bowl for Millie. He had also grabbed a small mirror for himself before his eyes were taken, so he put it down in front of Millie together with it. "Clean yourself up, and then get changed. If you need help, let me know."

"You don't need to tell me everything, you know? I know how to get ready on my own," Millie grumbled, her voice only muffled by the splashes of water she threw in her face.

"Yeah, yeah," Ashton stepped to the entrance with the dirty dishes in hand, and quickly started to scrub them clean. With the ability to control water, it was actually pretty damn easy, to the point where Ashton was getting annoyed at how much time he would spend on chores like this in the past.

It was all just so much easier with magic. All the dust could be swept away with air magic, grime was easily washed away with water magic. Plus, once Ashton developed himself further in spellcasting, the things he would be able to do were almost ridiculous.

Hell, with the right spell, you could get dressed at the click of a finger. Not that Ashton knew that sort of spell, or would be able to construct it without the help of a circle.

When the dishes and the pan were clean again, Ashton put them away next to the stove. He had been trying his best to collect things that could be useful once he contracted the Living Abode, and things were coming together pretty well already.

And of course, there was a massive benefit to this entire thing anyway. It was technically inferior to the Backpacker's storage just in nature already, but the space within the Living Abode always stayed the same.

Any object within there was fully contained once the Living Abode was rolled up. Living beings couldn't stay within it of course, with the exception of plants beside a certain few types of outliers, but for the time being Ashton could probably grow small plants in there without issues. Not that there was all that much space for much more than that anyway.

The two did work completely differently though, just inherently. While the Living Abode suppressed and contained the space within its 'walls', in this case its white canvas sheets, the Backpacker's bag contained a pocket dimension.

The benefit of the former was simple. It allowed for particular arrangements and for the space within to exist exactly as it did when you last left it. And of course, it was an incredible means of storage, but most of all, it was meant to be a central base.

As for the benefit of a pocket dimension; the Backpacker's bag has not yet earned the opportunity to shine, though it was already showing it. The spirit's storage was perfectly efficient without 'dead space' taken up by unshapely objects, and it was possible to access whatever you wanted at basically a moment's notice.

But in the future, there were more ways that a pocket dimension could change. Time dilation, specific environmental conditions matched to each and every individual object, and numerous other ways that the space within the pocket dimension could alter from the space outside of it. Especially considering that the Backpacker was currently only a Lowest-Grade spirit, this ability would continue to evolve in almost unimaginable ways.

And especially once Ashton gained abilities to let the spirits wander away from his limited range, the Backpacker would come in extra useful. They were not just a 'carrier', but a 'traveller' in every sense of the word.

Plus, beyond all that, there was just the matter of convenience out in the field. The Backpacker could just throw everything into the bag and pull something out in seconds, and that obviously wasn't possible with the Living Abode. Setting it up and taking it down required a fair amount of mana and concentration, plus a bit of time that made it very inconvenient when out and about.

So essentially, the Backpacker was best for immediate and emergency storage, while the Living Abode was better for long-term, large-scale storage and complex setups.

Either way, as soon as Millie got out of the tent, changed into her regular clothes, Ashton was excited to give this a go. He had seen this happen numerous times from an outsider's perspective, but it was of course the first time Ashton actually initiated this process on his own.

He slowly closed his open palm into a fist, and with each bit that his fingers approached his palm, the tent began to fall apart.

The stakes holding the tent's ropes down were pulled from the ground as the the tent collapsed in on itself. The ropes spun around the canvas, tying it up and practically compressing it into a smaller and smaller bunch.

Just around half a minute later, the large bell tent, with all the things inside of it, had turned into a canvas cube half a foot on each side.

Millie looked back and forth between Ashton and the compressed Living Abode.

"What did you do to Abby?" she asked, startled, and Ashton smiled at her.

"Don't worry, they're just resting." He pointed at his chest. "I'm sure you can see them, right?"

"Oh..." Millie leaned forward, staring deeply at Ashton. It was weirdly an uncomfortable sensation, knowing that she was basically looking directly at his soul right now. "I think I can see Abby? Maybe? And some others... are they okay in there?"

"Of course. They're in a space called my 'vessel'. It's basically the nucleus of your soul."

"What was that again?"

"A nucleus? It's usually the centre of something, the densest part that holds most of the important bits. Like, hm... your brain. That's like the nucleus of your body, since it's the most important part," Ashton explained. He wasn't perfectly sure how to explain things like this. He wasn't exactly a teacher.

Ashton picked up the Living Abode and handed it to the Backpacker. Even if constructs couldn't enter the Backpack's pocket dimension, now being technically living, albeit inorganic, beings with a fully intact soul, that pocket dimension wasn't the only way for them to carry things.

With the help of the ropes holding it together, the small, boxed-up Living Abode could easily be attached to the side of the bag.

While Millie was still staring at Ashton, apparently trying to see where the 'nucleus' started and ended, he walked past her and petted her head.

"Come on, let's make our rounds before we head out."
