The creature towered over James, forcing him to look up to make eye contact.

As he eyed her thicket of antlers, James’s mind automatically went to the question of whether he could kill her if it became necessary. He recalled that destroying the Heart of Ice was a step in the process, though he wasn’t certain if it was located in the same place as a biological heart, or even if it had to be enclosed within the monster’s body.

Is that even enough? Or just a step in the process? He wasn’t entirely confident.

But, of course, it was a moot point. Violence would not be necessary. Not just because they were at the Summit, where murder was impossible, barred by System restrictions. Cara Dahlhaus was here as a friend.

“Hello, Fisher Emperor,” the Wendigo said. Her voice was a winter’s breeze, cold and cutting. James could swear he felt the temperature drop noticeably as she stood near him. But that was impossible with Han Jianguo still around and nullifying everyone’s Skills.

“Hello… Ms. Dahlhaus,” James replied.

“Mina asked for me,” Cara said. “So I came.” She lowered her voice. “Is it true that you need my help?”

“For an underhanded plan,” James said. “But I can’t think of what else to do.”

“Underhanded doesn’t bother me too much,” Cara replied with a dry smile that showed far too many teeth. “There are worse things than being a bit sly. You’re protecting your family, right?”

He nodded, not trusting himself to speak.

“I figured as much. You have at least one kid, I know.”

“I have you to thank for that, don’t I?” James recalled that Cara had rescued a very pregnant Mina from possibly losing their son in an Orientation labyrinth.

Cara shrugged. “I did what felt right to me at the time. That’s what being a Ruler means to me. We act with the freedom that power grants. Don’t think of it as something you have to thank me for. You don’t owe me a thing. I wanted to do it.”

“But I will owe you for this,” James said quietly. It wasn’t a question.

“A nice young man like you.” Cara smiled again, and James forced himself not to look too closely at her teeth. He thought he could see a bit of flesh stuck between two of them, and he wasn’t sure if it was human or beast. “You shouldn’t get into debt with monsters like me. But here we are. It doesn’t seem like you have much of a choice. I’m happy to do you the favor.”

“What favor do you want in return?” James asked.

“Does it need to be defined now?”

“Won’t you be worried about whether I’ll keep my word if you wait into the indefinite future? I’m sure there’s something you want now. Right?”

“If Mina trusts you, you’re already better than most men in my book. Plus, half the room thinks you’re the closest thing to a god among us.” She looked him up and down. “I can sort of see it. You’ve got the aura. You’re not as tall up close as you were on the stage. But you seem formidable. I understand where you’re coming from, though. You don’t want to give me a chit to be cashed in at some time in the future if you can avoid it. Maybe I’d show up and embarrass you with a request you couldn’t grant. Or ask for something unreasonable once you’re in a more advantageous position. I get it. You don’t want to trust the big, scary Wendigo…”

James sighed. “Look—”

“It’s fine,” Cara said, cutting him off. “I do have some things I want now, but since you’re making me lay my terms out at a time of your choosing, they’re slightly more demanding than they would probably be otherwise. Got that?”

He nodded and waited.

“All right. You’re conquering the good old United States, from what I hear?”

“Yeah, slowly but surely.”

“Well, that shit stops at the old Canadian border, got it? You agree to leave my territory to me. Forever. That’s all of what used to be Canada, plus Alaska. I won’t bother you in the warm, sunny places, and you won’t enter my frozen wastes without an invite.”

“Deal—”

“I wasn’t done. I also want you to pledge that if someone else comes to exterminate me, and I request your help, you will come to my defense with haste.”

“Are you done now?”

“One last thing. Do whatever you can, if possible, to help me win the Victors’ Tournament.” She chuckled. “It might not be within your powers, but if we win the melee, you’ll be able to define the rules. Do your best to give me an advantage.”

“And if we come against each other in the Tournament proper, I will lose to you on purpose.”

“Convincingly, please,” Cara said, sounding amused. “That will convince the world to leave Canada alone, eh?” She spoke the latter sentence in a mocking facsimile of a Canadian accent.

James forced himself to smile and nodded.

“Mina never mentioned what a hard bargainer you are, but if those are all your terms, I accept.”

“I was nice to Mina, and you’re getting the friends and family discount on my services, too, as far as I’m concerned.” Her voice became serious. “We’ll beat these people, James. Should we fail, and they come after Mina and your baby, I’ll consider myself honor bound to come and help you, too.”

He looked up into the cold, hard, monstrous eyes of the Wendigo and smiled more genuinely this time. It was strange to be knowingly and semi-voluntarily working with someone he thought of as evil.

But if she was this determined to defend his wife and family, Cara couldn’t be all bad.

“Thank you,” he said.

“If you’re willing to offer similar terms to another, I met a Ruler from Europe who I think might also be interested. He wanted to approach you himself, but he was a little intimidated.”

“The more, the merrier,” James said, chuckling quietly.

I was thinking I’d be outnumbered, but at the rate things are going, we’ll have them beaten in both quality and quantity. He had already sent his nonhuman Council members off to try and recruit other nonhumans to help him, after Mina left and Rotter finished giving him intelligence on the other relevant Rulers. Hopefully James would end up with a small army. He had the advantage of already being known as a Ruler who would work with nonhumans, and he was willing to give out a lot of IOUs.

“Excellent. Count Aleph will be pleased to hear it.”

She sauntered off.

It was while he was standing alone, waiting for the next monster to approach, that Zhang Chāo stepped back into James’s part of the room. The Fisher Emperor waited for his new ally to draw close before speaking.

“Any new news?” James asked.

Zhang Chāo lowered his voice to a whisper. “The Holy Kings got wind. They’re trying… something.”

“How much can they even do?” He shook his head, shrugged, and smiled. “Not only do they have a sort of human-centric, anti-monster stance—I knew that from my encounter with another of their people, who they claim to be avenging, and they haven’t changed it—but even if they could try to come after me in the melee, they don’t have…”

He let his voice trail off, both because he was close to giving something away and because three nonhumans had entered the zone of the room that James had decided was his territory.

The conversation paused for several minutes while the Pixie Queen, two toad monster Rulers, and then a stream of additional monsters approached James. Most of them wanted to make a favorable impression on him. A few of them had already more or less directly said they wanted to come under his authority.

But even more interesting was the large number of nonhumans who were interested in making common cause against the Holy Kings.

These were not the strongest of the monsters. They were the most practical. Human-like in intellect and disposition, it deeply troubled them to know how many humans thought like the Holy Kings or like Pú Tiě Shān—thought of the nonhumans in this new world uniformly as a threat to be purged.

On their side, there was no such unity. There were some monstrous Rulers who wished to wipe out whatever humans they could find. But most were happy to just eat whoever was nearby, whether monster or human, and still others were focused primarily on not ending up as food.

So it happened that despite being the one who wanted help, James ended up in the dominant position in many of his negotiations with the creatures. They wanted to do him a favor, because they felt they needed the return favor more than he needed them. And all most of the creatures wanted in return was assurance of a firm defensive alliance against the Holy Kings, which James was happy to give.

He was rebuilding NATO, or something like it, one ally at a time. They weren’t all human. In fact, most of them were not. Some of the allies were stronger than others. Indeed, almost half hadn’t even made the cut for the Victors Tournament despite being Rulers.

But they all had followers, and that made each one of them a potentially valuable ally in the case of a larger war. They had just needed a good boogieman to scare them into seeking out his strength. The world was a frightening place now, with people like the Holy Kings and Pú Tiě Shān around.

Thank goodness someone like James was there to offer some stability in those trying times.

Hi, I’m the Fisher Emperor, and I don’t want to purge all your kind from my planet, James thought, imagining giving his speech from earlier again, only targeted at the monsters.

He had never thought of them as vulnerable, but of course, they could get scared as easily as humans. Most of them were far more isolated than a human Ruler, too.

Even Count Aleph, when he approached, hemmed and hawed and finally negotiated a milder version of the deal that Cara had gotten. The Vampire Count wanted assurances that James would not come and hunt him for experience or take his territory—a ludicrous idea, considering that the Count resided in Europe—and he wanted James to come to his aid twice if the Count’s life were threatened. That was the real ask, and James agreed to it easily enough.

But one question lingered, a point that Cara had insisted upon but which the Count had not brought up at all.

“What if we happen to meet head to head in the Tournament later?” James asked.

The pale man across from him licked his lips, grimaced, and said, “Then I will immediately surrender. My kind have intelligence networks just as you humans do. I am familiar with one of your Skills that is likely to be… quite deadly for one of my kind. To tempt fate with a battle in the hopes of surviving it or dodging what ought naturally be a fatal move would be the height of hubris.”

He had already walked away by the time James realized Count Aleph had meant Solar Ray.

I wouldn’t aim that at an ally who would be instantly killed by the sun, he thought. Then again, I don’t know if death in the Tournament is permanent outside of the melee. Since it’s not meant to be permanent in the melee, maybe the rest of the tournament is also designed to be non-lethal.

“The Holy Kings might not be able to do anything,” Zhang Chāo said, resuming his previous line of thought during a lull between monstrous negotiations. “They’ve focused a lot of time and energy on investigating what it is you’re trying to do.”

“Yeah, and they won’t find the answer,” James replied confidently, an amused grin tugging at the corners of his lips.

It pays sometimes to keep secrets.

Zhang Chāo visibly relaxed. “When we were talking earlier, there was a brief period when I didn’t think you knew the value of discretion, but I’m relieved to see that when it counts, you’re playing this so close to the vest. I’ll look forward to learning the truth of your plan when it’s appropriate.”

He turned and walked away with a carefree stride.

James smiled grimly as he waited for the next potential ally to approach. His plan was nothing complicated, though it was ambitious. He intended nothing short of the cruelest form of murder he knew of.

As he thought of that, the timer that had accompanied the wait for the System to teleport them to the Summit reappeared in the corner of his vision.
