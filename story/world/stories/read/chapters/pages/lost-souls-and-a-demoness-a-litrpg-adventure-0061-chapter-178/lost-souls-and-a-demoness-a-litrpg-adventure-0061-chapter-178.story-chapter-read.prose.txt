"Yo. How're you holdin' up?"

"Huh?" Jade shook herself out of the meditative trance she'd slipped into. The soft rumbling hum of the airship continued unabated, and Jade checked the time readout on the far wall. They still had a ways to go. Luis was beside her, now, and he'd placed a gentle hand on her shoulder. She opened her mouth to reply with an automatic "I'm fine, you?" but the words wouldn't come out. It wasn't true, nor was her friend looking for a canned response. Jade could feel genuine concern emanating from him.

"I'm… worried." She finally replied. It wasn't easy to admit, even in the back corner of the airship where the others couldn't overhear their conversation.

"About?"

Jade gave him a strange look.

"What do you think? We're flying straight towards what's probably the most powerful person on this world, and the plan is to fight him."

"I thought the whole point of the plan was to not fight him." Luis said, raising an eyebrow.

"Yeah, but since when do our plans ever work?"

"We've had a few good ones. And even the ones that fell apart halfway through gave us a good start."

Jade shook her head, a flicker of amusement dancing in her eyes.

"How do you do it, Luis?"

"Do what?" Now it was his turn to give her an odd look.

"Stay so positive all the time. The weight of the world, of more than one world, is crushing down on us, and you don't even seem to feel it."

"It's because I'm not fighting for all of that."

"Huh?" His response genuinely confused Jade. "What, you don't want to protect them?"

"Of course I do." Luis made a dismissive gesture. "But that's not what's driving me. Not really.""Then what is?"

"That would be you." Luis jabbed a finger into her chest. Or rather, he tried to. Her armor's defensive wards stopped it cold an inch or so before it could make contact. "And Naomi, and Tara, and Fenrin, and everyone else in our little group. You lot, plus my family back home. I started out by fighting for all of you, and I'm going to see this through doing the same. Helping everyone else is great, I'm glad we're doing that, but you can't let some abstract idea be your strength. It isn't solid enough. That has to be the people you surround yourself with. People you can rely on, and people you love."

The people you're with, was it? He made it sound so simple.

"I wish it was that easy."

"Seeing the big picture is a good thing, amiga. Just don't be so focused on the forest that you can't see the trees."

"…I think you have that metaphor backwards."

He waved his hand again.

"Ah, that's probably just the Labyrinth's translation power messing up."

Jade blinked.

"Translation? Luis, you speak English just fine."

"Sure I do." He smiled impishly. "But I could have been speaking Spanish for our entire time in the Labyrinth and you wouldn't know the difference."

"Wait, have you really been doing that?"

Jade sat up straight, looking at him in surprise. Luis just laughed.

"Nah, I'm just messing with you."

"Oh, you little…"

Jade laughed too, punching him on the shoulder and promptly bruising her knuckles on the unyielding metal of his armor. That just made him laugh harder.

"Oh, shut up." Jade said, rubbing her hand gingerly. "And… thanks. I'll give that a try."

Luis was the heart of their team. He never failed to keep their spirits up, and she couldn't imagine a more reliable companion to have at her back. Beneath the levity and good cheer was a steely core of unyielding willpower that had never let them down.

"We wouldn't have made it this far without you. Thanks for sticking with me through it all."

"That's what friends are for." Luis's grin softened. Then his eyes lit up with familiar amusement. "But don't say things like that, Jade. That got pretty close to a death flag there, and I won't stand for that."

"Oh, for the love of…" Jade rolled her eyes. "Luis, the world isn't a video game."

He raised an eyebrow at that.

"We just spent the last six months in a world that runs on levels, experience, and literal magic, Jade. I wouldn't be so sure.""The Labyrinth just contextualized it that way for us so we would have an easy time understanding its systems." Jade countered. "Siora, Fenrin, and Tara all see a completely different interface.""Actually, Tara's is a lot like ours. But either way, it's alright." Luis clapped her on the shoulder again. "Pointing out a death flag negates it, everyone knows that. I've got your back."

Jade just snorted in amusement. Tara slid over a moment later, asking Luis what "death flags" were. Jade was amusing herself by listening to his explanation when she felt a twinge of somber emotion coming from the rear of the compartment. That caught her attention, as it stood out against the general air of anticipation, anxiety, and tense excitement emanating from her companions. Looking up, she saw Fenrin standing next to the closed back ramp, his gaze distant as he stared through the cabin's only window. Jade hesitated for a moment and then stood up. She wasn't about to leave the responsibility of group morale entirely to Luis. She'd wasted enough time worrying about things she couldn't control, and it was time to do her part.

"What's wrong?"

Jade didn't waste time dancing around the topic. She didn't have Luis or Naomi's way with people, unless you counted the ability to charm others into submission. Which, if she was honest, was probably only serving to further stunt her social aptitude. So, best to be direct.

"Hmm? Oh." Fenrin started slightly, as if jerked free from deep contemplation. Then he shrugged. "It's nothing, really. I've just realized something that I should have figured out a while ago.""Oh?" That wasn't the sort of thing one wanted to hear right before executing a carefully crafted plan. "What's that?"

Her sudden concern must have shown on her face, because he quickly continued.

"Nothing that affects what we're here to do."

That was a relief. The last thing they needed was a last-minute complication.

"Then what is it?"

"Remember when I told you about how my father went missing on an expedition to the fourth floor?""I do. He was a merchant, right? Going to meet the tek'kalla?"

Fenrin had mentioned the story to them a couple of times, although Jade didn't know all the details. It was clearly a sensitive topic, and she'd never felt like it was the right time to ask him about it.

"That's right. He never shared the prejudices others held against them. At least, not against the ones who were here for exploration rather than raiding the lower floors. It was a long time ago now, but visiting Tara's home reminded me of his last trip. And… that valley where those portals appeared aren't too far from the route he always took."

It took Jade a moment to realize where Fenrin was going with this. She inhaled sharply.

"And you think he ran into a group of demons?"

"Maybe." Fenrin shrugged, still gazing out the rear window as the shifting landscape passed beneath them. "We know they've been sneaking around the Labyrinth for years, now. He knew how to get around the higher floors safely, and he was quite a high level for a non-Wayfarer. I've always had a hard time believing that he fell to a wandering monster. But if his path took him too close to one of the demons' early hiding spots…"

"They could have ambushed him, and no one would ever know." Jade finished solemnly. Fenrin simply nodded. His face was stoic, devoid of emotion, but that didn't stop Jade from tasting the bitter anger smoldering in his spirit. She laid a gentle hand on his shoulder.

"I'm sorry, Fenrin." She said, squeezing his shoulder softly. "I know you wanted to look for him once we made it up there.""Even if there wasn't a war going on, I doubt there'd be much point to it now." He said softly. Then he balled his hands into fists. "Just how much are they going to take from me? First father, then Mimi-"

He cut himself off with a firm shake of his head. Then, finally, he turned to face Jade, a sad smile touching his lips.

"Sorry, Jade. I shouldn't think like that. It's not like I'm the only one they've hurt.""Screw that." Jade said. "You're allowed to be angry, Fenrin. Anyone would be. Don't keep it all bottled up. Trust me, it doesn't help."

"Neither does letting it all out on the battlefield." He pointed out.

Jade almost flinched. That hit a little too close to home for her liking."Fair point." She admitted wryly. "But, after this is done, if you… well, ever need anyone to talk to…"

Why was she always so damn awkward at times like this? She was a literal empath, finding the right words shouldn't be this hard. If this was her ability now, Jade didn't want to think about how bad she must've been back in her old life.

"Thanks, Jade." Fenrin saved her from having to finish her sentence with another smile, this one tinged with both gratitude and a little amusement. "I may take you up on that."

If we all make it out of this. A dark corner of Jade's mind whispered. No! She couldn't think like that. They'd made it out of more dire situations than she could count, and they'd make it through this one, too.

She took another look around the compartment. Luis and Tara were still together, speaking softly. She could feel his reassuring presence easing the tek'kalla's nerves, although she doubted Tara would have admitted to them. Then there was Siora, who was carefully slipping brightly colored potion vials into slots on her belt. The elf's new Wayfarer mark burned bright on the back of her hand, a symbol of her commitment both to the protection of her people, and to her adventuring companions. Both she and Naomi had come a long way from their timid first steps into a world of adventure and danger, and both had blossomed into stalwart friends and brave warriors. For her part, Naomi was sitting with her eyes closed, practicing one of the breathing exercises Aylin had taught them to expand their mana control. She had always been the mage's most diligent student, and Jade marveled at her ability to focus even as each second drew them closer to confronting a powerful enemy.

"Get ready." Seris called back through the cockpit door. "I think we're getting close. There's a large plateau with a big fortress on top of it. Looks like the source of this dark cloud."

Jade was the first to reach the door. She slid into the copilot seat as the others crowded behind them, forced to awkwardly perch on the edge of the chair due to the size of her wings. Sure enough, they were approaching a large, crumbling structure situated high above the rugged wasteland. A plume of dark smoke erupted from a tall tower, spreading outward to cover the sky in a cloud of roiling darkness that plunged the world below into shadow. They crossed that boundary as she settled in, entering a land of darkness and oppressive gloom.

Great. That definitely wasn't an omen of any kind.

"Where should we set down?" Seris asked. He didn't address the question to anyone in particular, but Jade felt the group's eyes shift to her. She thought over the options and then pointed ahead.

"Find a spot at the base of the plateau. This airship is quiet, but not silent. I doubt we could land on the fortress itself without anyone noticing.""Besides," Luis said, "they probably have defenses.""Defenses that will target us if we get out of the ship." Naomi pointed out."We can heal ourselves with magic. We can't fix the ship."

"…I see your point."

"Seris, stay inside and keep everything ready for us, okay?" Tara said, snapping her visor down over her eyes. "When something inevitably goes wrong, we'll be in a rush to get out of here."

"You don't need to tell me twice." Seris grunted, easing off the throttle as they descended towards a relatively flat section near what appeared to be a ramp that rose towards the base of the fortress. "I'll keep the engines hot."

Jade reflected that his expression was probably a liberal translation on the part of the Labyrinth as the airship wasn't propelled by anything so mundane as combustion engines. She pushed the thought aside, mentally chiding herself for her lack of focus. This was it. There was no room for distractions.

They set down with a soft thump, the airship rocking twice on its skids before settling on the sandy ground.

"Alright." Jade said after a moment of tense silence. She got up, leading the way to the rear hatch. A brief sweep with her magical senses revealed nothing of note, and the only thing visible out the window was shadowed desert dimly lit by what little of the crimson sky bled through the clouds above. The place seemed to be as deserted as the goddess had told them it probably would be. "We're off to a good start."

She opened the hatch and, mere moments later, regretted having opened her mouth.
