Emperor Gallow surveyed what remained of his army, considering how the losses were offset by his new prize. The towering, Master rank serpent had been entirely converted now, though its unusual mind had required more direct intervention to subdue. Still, with what it had done to his forces, it had been more than worth the effort.

The air was thick with the dust of those Twilights ravaged by petrification. Gallow amused himself by imagining the same devastation taking place among the enemy army, but the image faltered.

Replacing it was the thought of a Champion rank beast appearing on the horizon, then destroying them all with ease.

“They have Champions,” he said, needing to speak the words aloud, needing to confront the truth of them.

Bek appeared, having wisely remained hidden in the pavilion during the attack. He’d been examining the serpent, but rushed to his Emperor’s side at the words. Gallow kept his voice calm, but his fingers twitched his irritation, speaking volumes for each word he uttered.

“At least one, my Emperor,” Bek agreed. “Nearly destroyed, by the look of things, but Champions are difficult to kill.”

“There is no way of knowing how many they may have, or how soon that one might be healed,” Gallow said, staring into the horizon where their enemy had disappeared.

“You are wise, Emperor,” Bek said. “It’s possible this was merely the nearest available, yet I must wonder why we haven’t seen their influence before.”

Gallow considered. “Judging by the beast’s condition, the Champion may have been engaged in battle elsewhere. Regardless, we must act as if they have more; we cannot proceed as we have been.”

Bek’s fingers twitched in agreement. “A prudent course. Yet we cannot ignore the Chosen’s forces. If you’re to ascend The Throne, you must accumulate the required energy, and reach Dusk’s power before he does. You can still feel it?”

Gallow closed his eyes, sensing the corruption flowing through him, and resonating with his demonic army. He’d lost many, however… “I still feel it. Somewhere to the north. But it won’t be enough to simply march in that direction, we cannot risk the Chosen gathering a larger force, or eradicating our forces before we arrive.”

“I hesitate to send more scouts, my Emperor, there are too few of The People remaining. Harrying them would be ideal, but we’re not in a position to do so. That’s without even considering the likely intervention of one more Champion.”

“If only our own hadn’t fallen to Noctem,” Gallow whispered. He hadn’t meant to vocalize the thought, but the simple truth was that he missed his fallen brothers. Zatem, Liadrin, Kalten, Alekk, and so many more. He’d known them for countless millennia, had set out from their home together, so long ago.

Now they were all dead. Nearly everyone was dead.

Gallow felt himself growing warmer as rage surged through him. They needed a new plan, something spectacular. There would be risk, but all he had left was the chances he took, and he had acquired a significant advantage in the serpent’s terrible gaze.

With a thought, he summoned Conquest to him. The reluctant weapon rushed to his sides, hands held close to his twin blades, ready to be sent to destroy once more. But destruction wasn’t what Gallow needed at the moment.

“Tell me, Conquest,” he said, “what does the Chosen fear most?”

Ethan’s power quested through Cara Fletcher’s body, while she stared down at him hopefully. Their reunion had been brief, with her immediately gesturing furiously at her throat. Selina had already prepared him for the challenge, of course, so he’d been content to begin treatment immediately.

This was a patient he wouldn’t keep waiting.

As he investigated the changes that Potentia had wrought upon the lithe elf, Ethan struggled not to be distracted by the audience they’d acquired. Walking through Bastion had caused something of a stir. Not only was he another stranger, but the word “healer” had echoed through the streets.

Ethan’s translation rune hadn’t needed to be updated the way Selina’s had, and so he hadn’t missed the longing in those voices. Pleading, really. He hoped he could help them all, but Cara’s condition rapidly squashed that hope.

Of course, the hundred or so Nator gathered around the small building they’d entered for privacy weren’t here to see Ethan. Searching for a way to distract them, he’d manifested Tomo, knowing his Familiar would be aching to see his people again–however they’d changed.

He’d been right, and the nine-foot, four armed demon had proudly walked beside him, eyes wide with wonder and disbelief at the sight of his people–and a true Nator city. But neither of them had been prepared for the reaction he received.

“Fated One. Fated One. Fated One.” The low chant had been repeating for a quarter of an hour now, and Ethan still didn’t know what to make of it. Allyara had gone to fetch the two elders who ruled the city, and they could hopefully provide some explanation for the title.

Tomo, himself, had no idea how to react. He’d simply sat upon a chair that was brought for him, and allowed his people to gather around him. Many reached out to touch him as they waited, still chanting, and the Familiar handled it with grace.

Ethan turned his full attention back to Cara, already knowing what he had to do, but frustrated by it.

“Can you help her, Ethan?” Selina asked.

He sighed. “I can, but not in the way I’d hoped.” Noticing Cara’s concerned expression, he hastily explained. “You’re not injured; that’s the problem. I’ve examined your larynx, and it’s…different. Changed in the same as those horns, and a few more subtle aspects to your internal organs.”

“There are problems with her…insides?” Selina asked, making a face. Cara glared in return.

Ethan shook his head. It was easy to forget how rudimentary medical science had been in Nexum. “Nothing serious–but only because Cara is Dusk rank. The magical part of her constitution is keeping her healthy, but a normal elf would be feeling sick and not understanding why. There’s no cause for concern, Cara, I can fix it all.”

“Not the horns!” Selina insisted.

“I’m not just going to leave her with horns…” Ethan began, but raised an eyebrow at Cara’s shrug. “Okay fine, I am going to leave her with horns. Anyway, that’s not the problem. The issue is that her body… This is hard to describe. It’s as if her body ‘thinks’ it’s healthy. That this is her natural state. You could argue that it is, in some ways.”

“And magical healing is about restoring the body to its proper state,” Selina said. This time her voice held much more confidence. She was a healer, herself, after all, but her abilities required little understanding of the subtleties of the body.

“Exactly. I can restore Cara, but only because I’ve healed her before. My rune has already seen how she’s meant to be, and I can return her to that state.”

Selina frowned. “But the other Nator…” she began.

Ethan nodded. “This is how their bodies belong. They’ve been changed, and I have no way to easily change them. I think that–given time–I can use Selina’s physiology as something of a baseline to develop a treatment plan, but that will likely take months, and a great deal of experimentation.”

“Time that we don’t have,” Selina said in a sad voice.

Ethan nodded, then turned back to his patient. “There’s more to say, but let’s include Cara in the conversation.”

His power reached out, finding the hundred tiny changes, and the several larger ones compared to the last time he’d healed the archer. She closed her eyes as magic rushed through body, then began coughing violently.

Ethan stood up, and backed off. “You need to get it all out,” he said.

Cara held up a hand, nodding the acknowledgement as she continued to cough, thick, phlegmy blood spattering to the floor. It continued for nearly a minute, before she finally managed to speak in a gravelly voice. “What kind of half-assed healing involves this much bleeding?” she demanded, but she smiled as she said it.

“You’re back!” Selina said, rushing forward and gathering the smaller woman in a tight hug.

“I’m myself again,” Cara said, voice muffled against Selina’s robes.

“Better than before!” Selina said, pushing the archer back, then gently tapping her larger, sweeping horns against the tiny buds on Cara’s forehead.

Cara chuckled. “We’ll see if I keep them. I tried bangs once, so these aren’t so bad.”

“Nothing can heal bangs,” Ethan said gravely, and Cara laughed, before surprising him with a hug as well.

The three friends sat for some time after that, listening to Cara’s story now that she could explain in her own words.

“Valanor is here?” Ethan said in shock.

“I don’t know,” Cara said. “We were terribly outnumbered, and this was some time ago. He reached Twilight rank, so it’s possible that he was able to survive where the rest of us weren't, but if he was killed…”

“Then there’s a chance a slightly more demonic version of him is out there somewhere,” Ethan finished.

“We’ll find him,” Selina insisted, “one way or another.”

Ethan sighed. “Another thing we don’t have time for right now. Honestly, I shouldn’t have even spent this much sitting down. No offense, Cara, healing you was worth it, but I have to reach our army and tell them what’s coming. Maybe we can hide…hide a few hundred Twilight demons. Damn it, I’m not even sure what to tell Apollo.”

“That there was a threat, but it has passed for now.” The voice was Allyara’s, who now stood in the doorway.

“What do you mean?” Ethan asked, standing up and looking past her for the absent elders.

“The Follower army is moving north,” the Champion reported. “Come, you can see for yourself. It’s time you meet Steelbinder.”
