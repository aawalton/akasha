Chapter 214 / B3-59:  Fighting Fire

Joe found himself standing in a field of flames.  Everything was burning.  The blaze was both around him and inside him.  Joe was in fire, on fire, and was the fire all at the same time.  There was heat and some pain, but for the most part Joe felt strangely at home buried in the raging inferno.  He felt the fire working inside his body, burning out small flaws, leaving behind something stronger and more resilient.

The agony on his arm was gone, yet Joe knew that battle was still taking place somewhere nearby.  He tried to see through the conflagration, but he was completely blinded by the towering sheets of flame.  Everything was just more snapping red, orange, and yellow.

Then a patch of fire shifted and slid up to Joe.  A lupine face resolved itself from the blaze and rumbled at Joe.  It was the wildness.  The last time he had seen this representation of his feral side was when Myllo had to magically sedate him during the Lexeroth’s demon-summoning incident.  That time the area they had stood in had been a great grassy sward, not an all-encompassing firestorm.

Thanks to his battle with the wraith of Sougath in the Spirit Realm, Joe had some idea how this plane worked.  Desire and conviction were the coins of this realm.  The Felix avatar had said something like, ‘You have to want it more than the other thing wants something else.’

This meant Joe had to assert his authority if he wanted the world around him to behave. Laying a hand across the back of the burning wild avatar, Joe pushed against the rest of the flames surrounding them. With a Spirit in the twenties and [Jequa's Verve] to boost it, resolve was not something Joe struggled with. Even so, he felt a powerful resistance at first, as if the flames thought they could deny him. Joe bore down harder, driving his determination against the raging fires. With almost a pop, an expanding ring blossomed around his feet, rapidly replacing the inferno with green grass and blue sky.

The abstract statue of Azbekt was revealed to Joe’s left, though he was no longer alone.  Several other carved effigies had joined the dwarven champion.  A slim archer with a light lattice of emerald vines stood next to a lanky bugbear coated in shaggy moss.  A tiny gnome snuck behind a massive swordsman with blue blossoms around his feet.  There were others as well.  Joe guessed that everyone he had gained a trait from with [Anyone] was part of the Statue Garden.

The next avatar to emerge from the retreating flames was something new.  A massive bright sapphire lily stood higher than the tall grasses. It released an intoxicating scent, pulling at Joe, trying to draw him over to it.

“I’ll get to you in a minute,” Joe said to the bloom, which now resembled a royal blue iris, pushing the inferno back even farther. The flames peeled back to expose what Joe was looking for: the furious avatar of the phoenix bloodline and the black banner of the Mark of Death.

The burning raptor was a fraction of Phyrros's size; still, it was far bigger than any eagle on Earth, roughly the size of a Saint Bernard, but it was presenting the non-stop ferocity of a honey badger.  The raging avian swooped through the air, flaming talons extended to rend the hanging pennant.  Yet as it closed to within striking distance, the battle changed.  As the flames pulled back, Joe watched the two avatars instantly switch places.  The firebird wheeled around, only to be blown off course almost immediately by an errant blast of wind.

On its next attack, it was intercepted.  Three canine figures interposed themselves between the frenzied raptor and the ebon banner.  Bayu's bark was more resonant here, and Baru was huge, larger than Joe.  Ranu was already harassing the phoenix entity, nipping and zipping around the flaming creature.  Since the spirit pack was soulbound to him, it made sense that they’d be here in the Spirit Realm’s representation of Joe’s soul space.

“Knock it off!” Joe shouted at the phoenix.  “What is the matter with you?”

‘That thing sits upon my perch. I govern death and renewal. Rid yourself of that canker and embrace the Fires of Life.’

A pronounced aura filled the air, and for the second time Joe heard the voice of the Mark of Death. The sepulchral voice was somehow both deep and hollow at the same time.  “Two sides.  Same coin.  Choose.”

Instinctively, in response to the Mark’s directive, Joe weighed his feelings about each of these two forces and which he’d pick if he were forced to decide between one or the other.  Oddly, Joe felt a deeper bond to the newcomer.  The phoenix was being an ass at the moment, but phoenix fire represented more of who Joe wanted to be than the enigmatic omen.  Fire was active, something that would push Joe to shed his more passive habits.  Its focus was life and rebirth.  It didn't carry the ‘chosen-one’ connotation that might drag Joe deeper into some world-altering conflict.

Yet, choosing between them was not really an option at the moment.  Joe had made a pledge to do everything he could to break the curse tormenting Finn and the rest of the chimeras.  He was also going to have to deal with the noble fey in the very near future.  Dorian had all but stated that the phoenix bloodline would be a huge asset in that confrontation, and Joe was all but certain that he was going to need the Mark to free the chimeric outcasts.

Joe suspected the Mark was well aware of this logic. His gut was saying that the Omen wasn't demanding he choose between it and the bloodline. If Joe had to guess, it was suggesting he choose a path that brought about the change he wanted.

Another aspect of the Spirit Realm was that reality was often depicted by manifested metaphors.  The wildness was made of fire, yet so was the phoenix, and yet they felt completely different to Joe.  He was completely aware of the wildness; he could sense its presence and its feelings.

To a lesser degree, it was the same with the bloom, banner, and pack.  The blue flower of the Aspirant of Spring, which was currently a lapis-hued rose, wanted Joe's attention and was a bit miffed it wasn’t getting it.  The hounds wanted to do what they could to serve Joe. Since they knew he wanted the fighting to stop, they were doing what they could to make that happen.

The Mark of Death was waiting, waiting for Joe to make a choice.  The prophetic omen had already established that it was not going to solve Joe’s problem for him.  It only seemed to nudge events when Joe brought them to the edge of a turning point.  Then its shifts were small things: tweaking powers, giving him a sliver of time when he needed it. Joe wasn’t sure what it would do in this situation, but he knew the answer would remain ‘nothing’ until Joe chose the path forward.

The soul constructs that felt unlike these were the stone figures in the Statue Garden and the avatar of the phoenix bloodline.  The stone carvings didn’t have active presences outside the Spirit Realm.  They were memories, or perhaps Joe’s way of honoring those who had gifted him with traits and helped him grow into the man he was becoming.  They were already integrated into Joe.  They weren't allies like the hounds or the powers behind his tattoos.  They weren't a fragment of his own personality, like the wildness, either.

He looked at the raging raptor and realized it was neither of those things either.   It was more than just another trait he had gotten from [Anyone].  Joe still wasn’t 100% sure what a bloodline was, but it meant fundamentally altering Joe on some level; not just adding a new ability.   It would redefine what Joe was from here on, yet like the rest of the racial traits he had absorbed, it did not need to be a presence in Joe’s already crowded headspace.

With that realization, Joe called on [Pack Master] and fortified himself within the skill. Technicalities like the fact that the bloodline was not in the form of a dog or wolf really didn't matter much here, in this realm where desires trumped definitions.

Joe asserted his dominance over the disparate aspects of his soul. The hounds and wildness came to heel instantly, their wills falling into alignment with his own. The blue blossom drew back, its petals folding inward as it sensed the change in Joe. The pitch-black banner radiated a hint of amusement before dipping in a small nod of acknowledgment.

The firebird turned to face Joe. It still emitted a strong sense of resentment toward the pennant of the Thirteenth Omen, but it too acquiesced to Joe's authority.

“Alright.  Enough of this.  Phoenix?  You have a purpose?  Correct?”

‘Rebirth through the Fire of Life.’

“Phyrros didn't give me your fire so you could fight the Mark of Death. He gave it to me so I could become something more. That's your job here. Right?

‘This is true, but …’

“No buts,” Joe asserted.  “Do what you were tasked with.  We can work out the rest afterward.”

The scornful avian threw one last scowl at the hovering flag before launching itself skyward.  It shot a hundred feet upward before folding over on itself and dropping like a missile. Joe's instincts almost kicked in, but he held himself still as the burning comet smashed into him, igniting the world around him once again. This time, Joe knew the blaze was folded just around his body, not his whole soul space.

The heat inside him doubled and redoubled again.  It scoured its way through every tiny bit of him, mind, body, and soul.  Joe had thought he was healthy beforehand, especially after he had been auto-healing himself for weeks, ever since he reached Fort Coral.   Yet the phoenix fire found countless cracks throughout his body and spirit.  The broken bones in his arms, legs, and ribs which Joe had mended were reforged and toughened.  His muscles, skin, organs … everything … was recast and fortified into something more durable.

It went beyond molding what he was now into something better.  Joe sensed new paths opening before him.   A stream of visions swept through Joe's mind, much like when he faced the Gwyllgi.  This time, instead of seeing his death and doom, Joe witnessed scenes of triumph.   He saw himself rising from defeat, ropes of fire reconstructing his mangled body into something stronger.  The next vision showed him battling through legions of undead encased in armor of flames.  The lesser decaying warriors burned away at his approach as Joe drove toward towering undead juggernauts in the distance.  This was followed by an image of Joe tending the Shrine to Murceee in Cliffside, having become Mercy Suku’s successor. Instead of the saint’s continuous healing aura, countless butterfly-sized phoenixes spread wellness through the district.

These and more possible futures opened to Joe as the flames burnished his body into something greater. By the time the process reached his crown, fingers, and toes, Joe felt like a new being.

Hawking confirmed it.

{Body Tempering} has been completed.  This process has improved your attribute efficiency by 17%.

You have obtained the Phoenix bloodline.  This has altered your race from Changeling to Phoenix-Kindled Changeling.

Joe really was a whole new person.
