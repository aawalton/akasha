Myka P.O.V.

Myka had been observing the Lillow for a while and had noticed something about its regenerative ability. He’d spoken his thoughts about taking advantage of the quirk aloud, and apparently Sombra’s Perception was high enough to notice.

“What do you need?” Sombra asked immediately, watching as Kain once more intercepted a vine striking towards Dani’s back before dashing over to help Raj as she was forced to defend herself with her hammer instead of actually striking a blow.

“I just – I need Raj to isolate it. Not stop it from going somewhere, but isolate the dirt around it so it can’t keep burrowing itself further. We’ll wait for it to uproot itself and slam down again, and then I’ll have her harden the earth there.”

“Do you think she can do it?”

“We can ask.”

Sombra grabbed an innocuous-looking radio on his lapel, one that Myka did not have.

“Scope to Hammer, Scope to Hammer. Do you read? Over.”

He saw Raj juke back from the battlefield and click the radio already on her own lapel. Myka looked over and saw both Dani and Kain had one. He just shook his head and shrugged.

“Is this really the time for that?” A voice crackled through the receiver. “What do you need?”

There was a pause before Sombra sighed. “Scope to Hammer. Next time the Lillow jumps, are you able to–” He turned to look at Myka, who leaned uncomfortably close to Sombra’s chest. Uncomfortably for Sombra, at least.

“Isolate it from the ground. Or maybe harden the ground around it, or something like that. Um, over,” Myka said, his voice coming through the radio.

“What?” came Raj’s voice.

Sombra pinched his lips together before unclipping the radio mouthpiece and handing it over to Myka.

“Yeah, this is...” Myka looked at the focus in his hand, thoughts spinning. “This is Wand to Hammer?”

At that, both Raj and Sombra chuckled, relieving a bit of the tension.

Myka shook his head. “Whatever, it’s Myka speaking. Can you keep it from burrowing down? Not keep it off the ground, but just keep it from burrowing down?” he asked.

“I’ll have to step back from the battlefield,” Raj replied.

“What are you doing?” Kain’s voice cut across the channel, interrupting Myka’s transmission.

“This confusion is why we follow protocol,” Sombra murmured.

“Just – I have an idea. I think it’ll work,” Myka replied.

“What we’re doing is working,” Kain said.

“It is,” Myka agreed. “But it’s not gonna wear out before we do. I–”

“Hey, some help, please!” Dani’s voice rang out from the radio.

“On it!” Kain replied, his voice distant as he shouted across the battlefield before returning to the radio. “Do whatever, just–”

Kain’s voice cut off as he once more darted in and cleanly sliced through one of the limbs that had been hounding Dani now that fewer of them were paying it attention.

Another two townspeople had gone down during their short stretch of conversation. This time, only one had returned to replace them.

“Okay, so next time it goes up, just before it slams back down, just keep the earth beneath it hard. Try to keep it from digging in. Do you think you can do it?”

“I’ll certainly try.”

“Okay. Um. Over.”

Myka handed the mouthpiece back to Sombra as he focused on the ongoing battle.

It didn’t take long before the beast screeched and raised itself up on the two longer limbs on its back, uprooting the four that had tendrils grounded in the earth before slamming itself back down.

Only this time, instead of automatically re-rooting itself into the ground, the creature found its attempt stymied.

Raj had turned the small area where it came down into reinforced stone, preventing it from easily getting its roots back into the earth and regenerating.

“Okay,” Myka mumbled.

It was obvious how much strain the effort was putting on Raj. She buckled, both knees hitting the ground at the same time the creature impacted the earth. She screamed, hammer abandoned and arms straining as if she were holding up the mana construct with her bare hands.

“Raj! Raj!” Dani shouted, abandoning her own post along with Kain as they moved to defend her.

That left Chainsaw Guy alone. He was forced to make a few risky manoeuvres and took an injury to keep himself out of the line of fire.

Myka watched it all play out with a sort of detachment he’d last felt when his Skill Heal had upgraded to Overheal.

Myka’s mind was steady as he began to cast.

Overheal.

Unlike the Heal Skill, Overheal had no fixed mana cost. Instead, the new pattern allowed the caster to continue pumping as much life mana as they wanted into the final construct.

For the first little while, it looked like Myka was actually helping the Lillow to regenerate. The whole town watched as its wounds closed and its body healed.

“What the hell, dude?” Kain screamed, his bellow carrying across the battlefield.

Myka didn’t pay him any mind. He was only half present and entirely focused on the target, on the flow of mana. On pushing and pushing and pushing until, slowly, the Lillow that had been basking in the healing energy began to shudder.

The limbs that had regenerated were beginning to grow overly long. Then, instead of the neat, corded, muscle-like vines, nodules and masses began to appear. Then they became tumours. Those nodules broke and burst into smaller, thinner vines, some leafy and others entirely too smooth. Then the larger nodules exploded into thick, tangled masses that themselves couldn’t stop drooping and growing.

Myka kept pushing and pushing and pushing, its natural regeneration forced far beyond its limits by Myka’s Overheal.

It didn’t take long for the two-storey-tall beast to end up crushed under its own mass. Weighed down and crippled by its own excess.

“Now,” Myka gritted out.

“What?” Sombra turned from the horrific display to face the innocuous-looking young man who’d caused it. “You–”

“Now. Tell them to kill it, now!”

Sombra snapped back to himself before saying something into his radio that Myka missed, too concentrated on moving healing and reinforcement away from the mana heart.

Overheal was a single-target Skill; its pattern didn’t allow for directed healing. Myka wondered if he’d need to get a new healing or manipulation Skill if he planned to use this technique in the future. As it was, he was intentionally leaving weakened areas around its now-unmoving core and mana heart by multicasting Overheal across the surrounding tissue while excluding those weak-points, watching as the beast mutated into a horrific, wooden, knotted, viney mass.

The Lillow shrieked as both Kain and Dani climbed onto its back and slammed their attacks down simultaneously, trying to expose the mana heart. Meanwhile, Chainsaw Guy burned through the few feeble vines still actively attacking the fighters as Sombra coordinated the attack over his radio.

With a final, empowered slam, Skillit smashed through a thin membrane and straight into the mana heart.

The creature let out one last horrifying, shrieking cry, and then it was dead.

For one moment, all was quiet. The only sound was the fading echo of the beast’s dying wail.

Then there were cheers.

Heads poked up from behind the barricades. Echoing cries of victory rang out from observers and resting defenders alike.

While the cheering went on, what appeared to be a dedicated team came in to begin harvesting the beast.

Never one to dismiss an interesting reagent, Myka himself hopped down from the building he’d been stationed on.

Pockets of wind mana slowed his fall just enough to cushion the jump. Then he pulled out his own kit – a System reward – and made to join the harvesters.

That’s when they heard it.

The sound of familiar bestial shrieking.

The defenders looked up to see not one but two more Lillows charging from both the north and the east.

The one in the north would enter from the clearly arranged kill corridor. However, the eastern beast would arrive behind the biggest barricade.

“Come on, really?” Dani cried as Raj’s Skill identified the eastern one as a Level 6 and the northern as a Level 8.

Inwardly, Myka concurred.

The group was spent. Raj was mostly tapped out, both stamina and mana nearly entirely depleted. Dani was raring to go, but the obvious tremors in her hands and legs made it clear just how much the battle had taken out of her. Kain hadn’t taken any wounds, but he was panting hard and leaning on his sword. Myka himself had used so much mana that just using the wind to cushion his jump had caused a pounding headache to bloom in the back of his skull.

Looking up, Sombra appeared to be the only one of their group even remotely ready for another fight. That said nothing about the town’s defenders, who had been fighting since before they even arrived.

The question of where the beasts had come from didn’t even cross his mind.

“All right, everyone. Form up,” Kain called.

With no other choice, the defenders braced for the coming fight.
