Zax woke up to a dry, lifeless chuckle and someone kicking Zax’s tail back and forth like it were a child’s game. "Zaxius, or 'Zax' is it these days? Haven't seen you in a while."

Zax turned to look up at the intruder, flicking his tail out of reach.

The man casually batting the tip of Zax's tail between his feet stood about three times as tall as the humans, his limbs pale grey flesh drawn tight over bone and muscle that bulged out oddly around the joints before thinning to almost nothing. His robe drifted around him spiderweb-thin strands woven in loose crimson diamonds. The usual connections were not needed for one such as him, but he enjoyed playing with the form of those he ruled, and today he had chosen a humanoid shape.

Zax nodded in acknowledgment. "Ifnal. I thought you were dead."

He laughed. "I've been dead many times. That has never been enough to drive me away yet." The crown he wore was a simple band of black metal, though the power it emitted was enough to level most cities if applied correctly.

Zax shrugged and rose, checking himself over for damage. He'd expected at least one more attack from the Ascendants before the local creatures overwhelmed them. He’d half expected to die in the process of his gambit, and was pleasantly surprised to find he hadn't been more than trivially injured in the whole affair. Most of the damage had been self-inflicted when he tore pieces out of his chained spirit to escape. The merely physical damage was inconvenient and briefly painful, nothing more.

"What brings you to my doorstep, old one?"

"A few unruly children, if I'm honest." Zax walked over to the edge of the pillar and stared down into the free-for-all melee below.

The Void Huntress was binding groups of monsters into her domain and picking them off a few at a time, directed by her Lunar Prophet ally.

"Your doing?" There was no way his brief flare had been enough to attract quite this much.

Ifnal shrugged. "You seemed to be in a bit of trouble, I would be remiss as a host if I allowed you to be so trivially destroyed."

"I seem to recall you doing your best to kill me last time we met."

"I did my best to push you outside your limitations. Killing fellow seekers has never been my style." His dry voice sounded almost disappointed. "I thought you knew me better than that."

"We all change with time." Zax wasn't about to admit how much of his past had been sacrificed in his attempts to break free of the restrictions he'd been deceived into accepting so long ago, or how much of who he'd once been no longer existed. His pride was as strong as ever.

"Have you seen the state of Ascendancy these days?" Ifnal asked conversationally, joining Zax in looking down at the chaos below. "They have no sense of decorum."

"Subtlety is a lost art," Zax agreed. He nodded in polite acknowledgement, rather than admit aloud that he should have been dead and Ifnal had gone out of his way to protect him.

"All three cults have sent their agents," Ifnal commented. "What have you been doing to upset all our neighbors, little dragon?"

"Befriended an anomaly. The boy needs guidance and protection, but his potential outstrips us all. This kind of potential evokes fear in the weak."

"This from you, little dragon?" The lich king's voice turned to amusement. "You who refuses to touch the deeper powers would call these ambitious children weak?"

"Ambition needs guidance or it will become patterns that lead to entrapment and its own destruction."

They stared down at the brawl a while longer, watching as the Crimson Cataclysm tore his way through whole swaths of monsters that would give Zax at least a little bit of a challenge and the Void Huntress continued picking them off a little at a time.

"That boy might be worth something someday," Zax commented, pointing out the Cataclysm. "He's got the right spark, even if he's reckless."

Ifnal gave a dry chuckle. "I have missed you."

"Surely you haven't even thought of me in centuries," Zax retorted.

"There are not enough people ready to accept their own faults. Of all the things that you are, blind is not one of them."

Zax chuckled and shifted his form to something more appropriate for the conversation, crossing his front legs over each other as he relaxed. "I'm going to need to get rid of them."

"Don’t trouble yourself over it." The lich king grinned, eagerness seeping into his voice. "I told you, I'm a good host. But first…" The ancient lich extended a bony hand toward Zax’s neck, then paused. “May I?”

Zax inclined his head in agreement.

The lich ran his hand through Zax’s aura, gathering it between his fingers until it formed a glowing gauntlet around his entire hand and forearm, then he reached into Zax’s soul and ran the hand over the torn edges where he’d shattered his bindings in too much of a rush.

Zax suppressed a shudder as the coldness of the lich’s touch bled through into his soul even through the protective layer. To anyone else, it would only appear he stood still while the lich king leaned down to press one finger to his throat, but inwardly there was a lot more going on.

Ifnal took his time, feeling over every frayed edge, every missing piece, before he fell still. “I see. I had wondered what could have left you in this state.”

“My own pride and foolishness. Nothing more.”

Ifnal chuckled and shook his head. “A lot more. And a lot less.” His hand became a fist and he withdrew it, something bright held within, though Zax felt no lessening of himself. The lich brought his other hand up and pressed them together, sandwiching the glow between them.

Zax watched curiously without interrupting.

For several minutes nothing appeared to be happening, then the glow began to subside. As it faded away entirely, the Lich King opened his cupped hands and held them out to the dragon.

Inside them lay a delicate chain, bone-ivory links held in loops by four solid black bands. The chain itself was thin enough to be mistaken for jewelry, though Zax recognized the suppressive force within it.

He took a step back. “I am done with being restrained.”

“They are not for you.”

Zax’s eyes narrowed. “Arm and wing bands, with a soul array between them. Built for a dragon.”

“You are not the only dragon in the Valley.” Ifnal turned and looked out into the distance, toward the mountains dividing them from the Middle Depths. A flash of darkness and light flared atop the peak of the highest one in the range, blue and red tinged with black so hungry it made Zax’s soul flinch. “I suggest you hurry.”

Zax grabbed the chain without a word and threw himself into the air, shifting into his messenger form as he sped toward the distant point at top speed. What have you done now?

Sev faltered mid-strike, paralyzed as something else glowed from the Vessel’s eyes. He’d imagined himself prepared, he’d assumed they had time. They were holding two of the shards, the dead god couldn’t possibly have revived itself.

Sev wanted to run. He had no words. His domain claimed supremacy, this creature had no presence worth speaking of. Yet its aspect glowed with a solidity that Sev had failed to attain even after centuries. Its aura remained strong, but not abnormal for an overcharged B-ranker, certainly nothing to compete with Sev’s own.

But the aspect!

If he threw himself against that, it would be his own person that shattered.

This was nothing like before. He remembered all too clearly how it felt to have one of his paths ripped away, torn out of him, but whatever lesser form the woman had carried, this was ten times worse.

He turned to flee and the monster was already there. He threw himself upward, and a spear met him. The puny weapon failed to even scratch him, but the weight of its existence was enough that Sev bounced off it and had to reclaim his dominance over the air below him before he fell.

Everywhere he turned, that smile was waiting. Every direction he tried to flee, that spear was ready to slice and stab. His clothing slowly began to come apart under the relentless assault, though Sev’s skin was stronger than the C-grade weapon could hope to puncture. The ghostly dragon claws had been doing more damage, and even that he’d healed in seconds.

But even though every logical piece of Sev’s mind insisted that he was fine, that he could sit down or take a nap and the monster would still fail to penetrate his defences, logic failed to override the primal panic. Terror pervaded Sev’s aura and sent his soul cringing away from each contact, and he had no Regression to shield him, no Negation to fight back with.

He was driven back again and again, forced to fly lower and lower until he was barely higher than the mountaintops.

“Running away is such a boring method, don’t you think?” the monster asked conversationally, and for a moment the terror receded as the young man’s friendly voice echoed from a hundred directions.

It had to be a trap. A false promise luring him to his demise.
