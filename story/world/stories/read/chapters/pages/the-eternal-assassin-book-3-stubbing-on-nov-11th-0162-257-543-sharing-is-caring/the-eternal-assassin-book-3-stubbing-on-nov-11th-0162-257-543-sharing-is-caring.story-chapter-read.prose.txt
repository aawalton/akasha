Rather than trying to stab the monster again, Asher returned to the material realm a few feet away, watching Smiles’ carefully as he did so. The undead man frowned and opened his eyes when he realized Asher wasn’t trying to attack him any more, and he stomped a foot on the ground.

“Come on, we were playing a game! You can’t just stop because you’re not winning, that’s no fun!”

“Sorry, Smiles, I had an idea for a different game we could play,” Asher suggested, deciding he might as well play into whatever weird personality the undead creature seemed to have.

“Oh, a new game?! You should have said so earlier!” Smiles said, laughing and jumping up and down as his bloodshot eyes stared at him in excitement. “How do we play?”

“What if the two of us took turns attacking the other?” Asher ventured, hoping beyond hope the monster would go for this first plan. “I attack you, then you attack me, and we keep going until one of us gives up!”

“I don’t like that game,” Smiles said, frowning as he shook his head. “Both of us rely on speed or dodging to survive. Either one of us could kill the other in a single blow if we just stood there and took it.”

Of course, now the monster decides to show logic and reasoning. But rather than roll his eyes, Asher simply continued smiling, keeping his annoyance off his face. “That’s fine, I’ve got other games we could play as well! What about sword-swap? It’s where we take turns trying to fight with the other person’s weapon and see who’s better with the other person’s blade!”

“I don’t like that game either,” Smiles said, beginning to get a bit frustrated as he pointed his short sword at Asher’s living wood dagger. “That dagger smells bad. I don’t like it, and I don’t want to touch it.”

“That’s fine, I’ve got other daggers!” Asher said hurriedly, pulling the living wood dagger back into his rift and bringing out one of his old steel ones. “Better?”

“Hmmm…” Smiles said, tapping the flat of his blade against his head as he thought. “…Nah, I don’t like that game either. The King gave me this sword personally, and he told me to make sure nothing happened to it. What if you tried to steal it when we traded weapons?”

“What if I promised I’d give it back?” Asher ventured, wondering just how naïve the monster actually was. Surprisingly, Smiles actually seemed to consider the suggestion for a moment, before shaking his head vigorously.

“No, too risky! Do you have any other games? So far, all your ideas are bad.”

“Well excuse me, I don’t hear you coming up with any suggestions,” Asher shot back as he struggled to come up with another idea to either get Smiles to give him a free shot or give up his sword.

“How about screams? It’s a game where I try to make you scream as loud as you can, and you try to keep from screaming?” Smiles offered, nodding as if this was an excellent idea. “It’s one of my favorites, but I can’t play it with the other Servants because none of us feel pain. Stabbing each other with our weapons doesn’t do anything.”

Smiles’ own words gave Asher an idea, and he paused, going over it in his head. It was risky, seeing as he didn’t know how the monster would react, but it was worth a shot. He’d actually seen a similar technique used on a greater demon once before, by Audrey, a truthseeker with a Guilt element. And if it could work on a demon, he could only hope it would work on an undead monster as well.

“Smiles…” Asher began, choosing his words carefully. Against anyone else, this would undoubtedly come off as a direct threat. But he had a feeling Smiles wouldn’t see it that way. “Do you want to feel pain?”

“Of course I want to! I want to feel all the different things you feel when you’re alive!” Smiles said, looking downtrodden as he hung his head. “But the King said stuff like that is pointless, so I’ll never know what it feels like…”

Like a hunter waiting for its moment, Asher leapt at the opportunity he’d been waiting for. “What if I could help you experience it? Not just pain, but everything people feel? Hunger, fatigue, warmth, all those things. Does that sound fun?”

“Yes…” Smiles said, watching him carefully. “But I am incapable of feeling those things. The other Servants never cared about all that like I do, but they still did their best to help me experience the sensations of the living. But no matter how hard they tried, it didn’t matter.”

“I have a skill that lets me share my own memories with others,” Asher said, silently praying that this would work. “If you’re willing to let me touch your forehead and accept the skill, I can share with you whatever memories you want. Memories of me eating a big meal when I was starving… Memories of me slipping under the warmth of my covers on a cold winter’s night… Hell, you seem to like the idea of pain, I’ll even share with you the most painful memories I have. Ever been ripped in half before?”

“Yes. It was most inconvenient,” Smiles said offhandedly, looking like he was fighting a war with himself. “I don’t know… The King told us we shouldn’t let people lay their hands on us… Apparently there are all sorts of nasty skills people can use that require touch.”

“This isn’t a bad one though, it’s just memories,” Asher said, realizing Smiles was actually thinking about his suggestion. “What if I let you stab me with your sword again? That prevents most skills from working, right? That means the odds of me using any sort of bad skill on you would be even less!”

“Yeah… yeah, that makes sense,” Smiles said, nodding as he began to come around to the idea. “Though you’re the first person to willingly let me stab them. Most people seem pretty scared of the idea.”

“I’m not like most people,” Asher said, silently thanking whatever Gods were watching them that Smiles was so strangely innocent and easily swayed. “So like I said, you just need to—”

Asher gasped, blinking at the sword that was once again stuck straight through his stomach. Only this time, Smiles was standing immediately before him, beaming at him with a child-like grin as if he hadn't just run Asher through with his sword. Yet again, Smiles shocked him with his impossible speed. If Asher hadn’t been able to see the dregs of energy drifting around the realm of ages from his Lesser Realm Sight skill he still had Permanence on, he would have assumed Smiles was copying the greater demons.

He had no idea how the King of Death had created a minion with such power, but if the other Servants were just as dangerous, getting to the center of the Deadlands might be tougher than he first thought.

“I should warn you, even if this is some sort of trick, you can’t actually kill me anyway! We Servants draw our strength from the King of Death, and even if you were to chop off my head, it would be little more than an annoyance. In fact, I sometimes take my head off just for fun. Trying to direct my body around without sight is an entertaining way to pass the time!”

“I don’t doubt it,” Asher hissed through clenched teeth, trying to look unbothered by having been skewered through twice in only a few minutes. This time, he was pretty certain Smiles had unintentionally cut through something more important than just intestines, and he suddenly felt a lot less bad about tricking the undead monster. Child-like innocence or not. “Okay, I’m going to place my hand on your forehead now. Remember, you have to be willing for this to work.”

“I know, I know,” Smiles drawled, grinning at him. “Come on, I want to feel!”

“You did ask for it,” Asher muttered, placing a hand on the pale man’s forehead. As he’d expected, Smiles’ skin wasn’t just cold and clammy, it was nearly freezing to the touch. Doing his best not to shudder at the sensation of essentially patting a sentient zombie, Asher focused on the most powerful memories he could think of.

The utter relief of waking up in Samantha’s cabin after finishing off Loratta and realizing he’d survived having a few metric tons of rock dropped on his head after already accepting his death. The mind-shattering pain of Eight’s mold spores digging and ripping their way through his body from the inside out. The pleasure and excitement of finally evolving the last of his skills and feeling the energy within his body solidify, turning him into something more as he progressed through the stages of elemental advancement. Asher focused on the best and the worst memories he’d ever experienced. The most intense, most powerful sensations he’d ever lived through.

And then he used the second tier of Incorporate to shove them straight into Smiles’ head.

Incorporate - Lvl 28

The undead monster that had never truly felt a thing in his life staggered backwards, letting go of his sword as he clutched his hands to his head.
