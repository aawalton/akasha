“So, uh, that doesn’t look normal,” Stephen said uncertainly as he looked over the valley from beside me.

We’d found a good vantage point near the ocean, where a chain of mountains met a jungle, separated only by a deep canyon. Sorenguard had been on the opposite side of the mountains from the jungle, and though the map had clearly shown the jungle existed, seeing it still took me by surprise.

The shift between environments was just so abrupt that it almost felt like patchwork instead of something that had developed naturally.

Even more unnatural was the angry red fissure in the distance, partially hidden by what appeared to be blurry fortifications.

“Is that an incursion breach?” I asked. “It matches what they showed us, but the demons aren’t behaving like Veridia described at all.”

“Yeah. It kind of looks like they’re building something, but everything is unnaturally blurry. Do you think they’re building some kind of fortifications? I’ve never heard of demons doing something like that,” Stephen agreed. “What do you think is going on?”

“I have no idea. We should probably reach out to guild headquarters and let them know what we’ve found.”

We’d been directed to report anything of interest to headquarters instead of going through the Sorenguard branch of the Porter Guild for such things. And while the IRT wasn’t technically a part of the guild, the fact that it relied so heavily on the guild to function meant it was only logical to report such things through guild channels.

“Yes. We can see the breach, but it is partially concealed behind something we can’t quite see. It looks like they’re using a concealment array or something.” Stephen listened for a moment before responding, “No, we can’t tell for sure, though my gut says it’s some kind of fortifications. I can shapeshift and get a better look if that will help. You can track our location through our communicators, right?”

Even inside dimensional storage, the communicators could be tracked. That was also how we were able to use them while inside my demesne.

It was a bit of an invasion of privacy, and for that reason, Porters were only required to carry the upgraded devices when they were on official guild business. But since neither of us had anything to hide when it came to our location, neither of us cared very much about being tracked.

It did help that only senior guild members could access such information, though.

After another pause, Stephen said, “Yeah. We’re both new. We just finished training, and it included stealth. We were also both issued equipment enchanted for stealth.” Another pause. “Okay. We’ll try to get a closer look and report back. Got it.”

He put away the communicator with a frown. “I’m actually surprised the clerk wanted us to get closer. I would have expected them to tell us to leave immediately.”

“Which clerk did you talk to?”

“I didn’t recognize the name.”

“Maybe they didn’t realize we’re just bronze rankers?” I theorized. “Regardless, I wouldn’t mind getting a closer look. We should be able to teleport from here. As long as we keep our distance from the breach and maintain stealth, we should be fine.”

“I can just check things out in falcon form,” Stephen offered. “There’s no reason for you to put yourself at risk for something like this.”

“I don’t think it’s much of a risk. Even if it is, I can always just teleport away or enter the sanctuary if things get too dangerous. In fact, I’ll set a Dimensional Anchor here so I can immediately return if any of the demons see through my stealth. In fact, it may be safer for me to scout things since I can practically guarantee my own escape.”

Shaking his head, Stephen said, “I figured you’d say something like that. Just make sure you use the cloak so I can keep track of your location. I’ll circle around a bit, then come to you.”

We’d already discovered that the evolved form of my Telekinesis skill could handle the weight of my body and Stephen’s mirror falcon, though it was right on the edge of what I could handle.

“That sounds good. I’ll pull us into the sanctuary after we get a good look at things. We can just report from there.”

“Sounds like a plan,” Stephen said before shifting into his much smaller mirror falcon form.

Without waiting for a response, the shifter took flight, heading straight for the angry-looking tear in reality.

“That thing really has to be huge to see it all the way from here,” I muttered to myself.

Of course, I’d seen pictures of incursion breaches before, but this was the first time I was actually seeing one for myself.

It looked nothing like the rifts I’d seen in my old realm, nor did it resemble the blurs in space that marked a dungeon’s entrance. Instead, the breaches looked like jagged tears in reality, with edges of red making it look like space was literally bleeding from a distance.

It was still a bit oval-like, similar to a portal, but the edges were far too rough. The sight actually made the hair on the back of my neck stand at attention. It was just wrong on so many levels; it was hard to put into words.

Seeing that Stephen had already crossed half of the distance between me and the breach, I took a moment to manually cast Stealth before equipping the cloak I’d been given as part of my IRT kit.

Targeting a space high above the breach to ensure I remained well outside the distance mandated by our instructors, I crossed the space in a blink.

I fell for less than a second before Telekinesis slowed me to a stop, keeping me well outside the danger area. Once my position stabilized, I shifted my attention to the movement below.

By all appearances, it seemed as if the demons were building a fortress in the middle of the jungle. Since such things ran completely counter to anything we’d been taught about demonic incursions, I found myself drifting closer in curiosity.

A few minutes later, I felt Stephen’s familiar presence approaching from above. My body automatically straightened to provide a larger landing area, which he made use of just a moment later.

Because he was so close, I could see the blur marking his body’s position. His talons suddenly gripped my shoulder painfully, and I worried that something might be wrong.

However, before I could pull us inside my demesne, I felt a shift in reality. It was like up suddenly became down, and my senses had gone haywire.

Acting on instinct, I tried to shift us both into my soulbound dimensional space, but instead of finding myself in the comfort of my demesne, it felt like we were falling.

I gripped Stephen’s falcon form, holding it close as I willed my Temporal Aura to surround both of us. Doubling our subjective time allowed me to comprehend what was happening, though comprehension was not enough to save us from our fate.

‘Not again,’ I lamented, though I found comfort in the feeling of Stephen’s falcon form pressed against my chest. ‘At least this time, I won’t be alone.’

With Temporal Aura active, the ground approached far more slowly than it would have otherwise. Though we’d clearly been pulled into the incursion breach, nothing about the environment screamed ‘demon realm’ to me.

Our surroundings were certainly different from the jungle we’d just left, but there were still plenty of familiar-looking trees and plants in the vicinity.

The air felt markedly different, though. It only took me a moment to realize just what the difference was: this realm had no essence.

As soon as I stabilized our fall using Telekinesis, I felt Stephen’s falcon form pushing against my grip, as if he wanted to be released.

Leaning close, I whispered, “Give me a minute to find somewhere for us to enter the sanctuary. I don’t want to do it from the air, and I can’t track you when you’re in stealth.”

I didn’t mention that I’d tried to move us there already but had failed. I was pretty sure the failure was due to the instability involved with being pulled across realms, though I wouldn’t truly be sure until I tried again.

So far, the demons hadn’t noticed us. That was probably because we both still had two layers of stealth active, though that wouldn’t last for long.

I could feel the cores serving as batteries for the equipment I’d been given by the guild draining at an alarming rate. Similarly, it felt like maintaining my Stealth ability was taking a lot more effort than normal.

I could also feel a steady siphoning of the essence I’d absorbed, and I instantly understood that if I continued using essence-based abilities, it would eventually lead to the loss of levels. Given that, I wanted to find some kind of cover from which to enter my demesne.

Because the demons had been spread around the breach when it closed, they were scattered a far distance from our initial location.

At a glance, the array of demonic races seemed to be spread roughly the same distance they’d been from the incursion breach at the time it had closed. Given that, it only took a couple of minutes to fly far enough away that I didn’t have to worry too much about being seen if I were to drop stealth.

Not wanting to drain any more essence than necessary, I quickly dropped the manually cast ability and relied solely on the enchanted cloak I’d gotten from the Porter Guild to remain unseen.

A minute or so later, I noticed some old ruins marked with what looked like the remnants of smoke stains. There were no other buildings visible in the area, so I figured it would make as good a place as any to dip into my demesne, so I quickly made my way there.

Mentally crossing my fingers that nothing went wrong, I pulled us both into my soulbound sub-realm. When we appeared without any issues, I let out a breath of relief and set the falcon in my arms beside me on the couch.

A moment later, Stephen sat in his Dian form beside me.

“Can you sense any of your Dimensional Anchors?” he asked, looking at me with worry in his eyes.

After taking a moment to consider his question, I shook my head. “Only the one I left behind when we just entered this place. I think crossing through the breach reset everything just like entering a rift used to in my old realm.”

I’d hoped that wouldn’t be the case, but it seemed my luck hadn’t held out this time.

“Do you have any ideas on how to get back?” he asked.

“Well, I doubt they built all of those fortifications in one go,” I said. “I thought that incursion breaches were random, but given how much they built in that location, I’d be willing to bet that’s not actually the case.”

Whether it was a new situation or how it had always been was another question, one whose answer would have strong implications.

“Given that,” I continued, “I think it’s likely they’ll reopen the breach eventually. Maybe we can just cross back through using stealth? They didn’t notice us this time, so it’s likely they won’t notice us next time, either.”

“Did you notice the drain when you used skills after we crossed over?”

Nodding, I said, “I did. Not entering the sanctuary, but when I used Telekinesis and Stealth, I definitely noticed. The drain with Stealth was actually pretty significant, probably because it was manually cast. It feels like it’s draining the essence I’ve already gained toward the next level.”

“I was thinking the same,” Stephen agreed. “We should probably be careful not to use any essence skills if we can help it.”

That was going to limit us quite a bit.

“I’m pretty sure being outside of the sanctuary also drains our equipment a lot faster,” he added.

“I agree. That’s going to be a problem considering it’s not like we can blend in or anything.” I paused. “Well, you might be able to if being in beast form doesn’t take any essence, but I won’t be able to.”

“You might be able to get by with a cloak,” he pointed out.

Thinking about it, I realized he was right. While most demons wouldn’t be able to pass as Dian from a distance, there were several demonic races that would probably be able to blend in a Dian city if they were covered well enough and nobody looked all that close.

And since rogues were a thing, it really wasn’t all that unusual for people to walk around with hoods and partially covered faces.

Really, as long as they didn’t have any clearly demonic features, I suspected that it wouldn’t be all that hard for certain races of demons to infiltrate Dian cities at all. In fact, that was likely a contributing factor to the surprise attack we’d experienced shortly after ascending.

I mean, it wasn’t like cities made use of gates or welcomed many people traveling on foot. Nearly everyone used spatial magic or airships to move between cities. Given that, it was not all that unusual to see people appear out of nowhere.

Travelers were supposed to use the arrival platforms in whatever city they were going to, but even I teleported around the city instead of walking. It wasn’t as if seeing a cloaked individual appear would have drawn any real interest.

Given that, Stephen was probably right. With the proper clothing, he and I could likely move about with relative freedom.

“Do you think we should wait a bit before scoping things out?” I asked. “There didn’t seem to be much on this side of the breach aside from some staging materials. I doubt the demons are going to stick around indefinitely.”

“That’s not a bad idea. We should probably eat something and get some rest. We have no idea how things work in this realm, so starting things fresh would probably be a good thing.”

I couldn’t help but agree. A quick test showed my system still worked, but for all I knew, that could just be because we were inside my demesne.

I didn’t think that was the case since the sub-realm was tied to my soul, but I honestly had no real idea of how any of that worked.
