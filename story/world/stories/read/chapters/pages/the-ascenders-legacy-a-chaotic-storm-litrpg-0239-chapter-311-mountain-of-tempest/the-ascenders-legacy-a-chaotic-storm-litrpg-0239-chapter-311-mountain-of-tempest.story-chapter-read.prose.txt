While the elders briefed Aodhán and the other initiates on the new trial, outside the initiation center, a small crowd was already gathering—outer sect disciples who were either too idle or curious to drag themselves out of their beds at such an early hour of the morning, just to see the result of this month's initiation with their own eyes.

They expected no excitement or joy, despite the rumors of powerful newbies. Anyone who passed would be taken to their designated sect, while the others would be led out to join the outer sect. And that was hardly any cause for celebration.

However, all of that changed the moment they saw all eleven newbies stepping out of the cathedral with anxious expressions. Confusion rippled through their ranks first, then disbelief as they wondered if all those powerful newbies had somehow failed.

Curiosity piqued, they slowly moved toward them, murmuring quietly to each other.

"It's not possible, is it?" they asked themselves. "Remember the newbie who defeated Gorvak? Do you think he failed too?"

Their whispers rose as speculations ran wild. But as they drew closer to the center, the truth soon became clear. The newbies hadn't failed. Their trial had only just become harder.

And far more exciting!

A Mountain of Tempest trial for mere newbies? It had never been done before. It had barely even been imagined. And now, they were about to watch newbies take the test that had stumped thousands of outer sect disciples over the years.

The news spread through the sect like wildfire, and in mere minutes, a dozen disciples had become a hundred, with more still trooping in, flying in from every corner of the sect, including the inner and core sects. They arranged themselves according to their ranks with practiced instinct—core disciples at the forefront and the outer sect at the far back.

Whispers rose from among them in wonder as they speculated about the reason for the sudden change in the entry trials. For centuries, the Mountain of Tempest had been reserved for promotional trials, but now it was being used for an entry trial?

Some wondered whether the High-Elders had simply changed the trials on a whim, while others argued that someone interesting must have shown up to spark such a drastic change.

Among those arguing the latter were core disciples Cyrenna Blackstone and her best friend, Orpheus Al'ruhn—a descendant of High Elder Zarxan Al'ruhn himself.

They sat in comfortable storm constructs at the forefront of the crowd, watching as the eleven newbies arranged themselves at the base of the mountain, expressions tight with terror, except for one who almost looked resigned to his fate. Indifferent.

"Who's that?" Cyrenna asked, pointing discreetly at the boy with a frown. "Have you ever seen a newbie with a bloodline before?"

"Not that I can recall at the moment," Orpheus replied in his usual grandiose manner. "He's certainly not from the shelter or the mundane cities with a bloodline like that, which means he must be a foreigner."

"Or someone with very powerful parents within the sect," Cyrenna suggested, glaring at her friend. "That's some leap you took there."

Orpheus shrugged. "I'm smart, Cyrenna. I've already considered that angle, and if you gave it a decent amount of thought, you'd realize how stupid your suggestion sounds."

Cyrenna grimaced but forced herself to breathe. "Do enlighten me, Orpheus."

"Gladly." Orpheus nodded regally. "My family is one of the most powerful families within the sect, but even we don't have the power to simply hand out soul seeds to our descendants."

Cyrenna frowned, forced to see Orpheus's logic. "I guess that's true. Still, concluding he's a foreigner is a giant leap. He could be from the mundane—"

"No." Orpheus shook his head. "No mundane king is rich enough to give their children a storm-based soul seed. The only way he could have gotten that bloodline is either by inheritance or …"

He trailed off, the rest of his words fading as a new thought suddenly rose in his mind. He frowned immediately, the implications of the thought heavy on his mind.

"What is it?" Cyrenna asked, her voice low with concern.

Orpheus glanced at her, his expression morphing from shock to utter disbelief as the thoughts arranged themselves in his mind. Then he spoke. "You know, now that I think about it, this has happened before, when my great, great, great, great, great—"

"Skip to the point, Orpheus."

Orpheus smirked. "Well, I just remembered that High Elder Al'ruhn also came into the sect with a bloodline. And there's that rumor that he left the sect very recently and came back with someone. A boy."

The implication of his words struck Cyrenna like a bolt of lightning, and her eyes widened. She glanced at the boy, then back at Orpheus. "You think he's the boy High Elder Al'ruhn came back with?"

"I think so," Orpheus replied slowly, his expression still troubled. "And if I'm right, then he must be very special indeed."

Cyrenna glanced at the boy once more, waited a moment to process her thoughts, and then asked, "Do you think you both might be related?"

Orpheus's gaze hardened at that, and he leaned deeper into his chair. "That depends entirely on how well he does, now doesn't it? If he makes it into the core sect, then we'll most certainly become friends."

"And if he doesn't?"

Orpheus shrugged. "Then he's nothing to me."

Cyrenna smiled and settled in to watch the scene unfold. However the boy did, it would no doubt make for an interesting day.

At the base of the trial mountain, Aodhán felt himself shudder involuntarily as his mind struggled to comprehend the sheer size of the mountain.

From their position at the cathedral, it had been intimidating and terrifying, but standing here at the base of it, Aodhán had no words to encapsulate the scope and volume of the emotions he was feeling.

Two words, however, came very close. Small. Inconsequential.

Never in his life had he ever felt so inconsequential in the grand scheme of things. Right now, he wasn't a transmigrant or a man fated to spark a continent-wide war. He was simply a man about to climb the largest mountain he'd ever seen in his life.

Only this mountain was no mere rock. It was an embodiment of spirit supremacy, and as Aodhán stared at it, it seemed to glare down at him with a sense of inevitability.

You cannot defy me. You cannot overcome me. You are nothing.

The words, though not uttered, were loud and clear, and beside him, the other initiates shuddered in nervous terror.

And yet, the terror of the mountain was nothing compared to that of the crowd gathering behind them. They filled the earth and the skies—thousands of disciples who had heard the news and had come to watch them take a single trial.

Crixis peeked at them and promptly faced the mountain, a shuddering breath escaping his lips as he whispered, "Imagine failing the trial and returning to meet this crowd of witnesses."

Serapha let out a choked whimper, her expression paling at the thought. Even the triplets turned to glare at him.

"Keep your imaginations to yourself, big guy," the inheritor spoke, her voice a hiss. "They're not helping anyone."

"Apologies." Crixis nodded nervously, glancing at Aodhán. "I'm just very nervous. I didn't have time to mentally prepare for this."

"None of us did." Aodhán smiled encouragingly. "But anxiety isn't going to get you anywhere. You need to calm down."

Crixis nodded, his gaze lingering on Aodhán for a long moment. "You're not anxious?"

"I'm terrified," Aodhán smiled at him. "But it's not of failure."

Crixis's eyes widened, but before he could speak, Elder ThunderWind, the male judge, spoke, his voice loud enough to cover the entire area.

"A few ground rules before we begin, initiates. First, you are not to help anyone up the stairs for any reason. Doing so will result in your immediate disqualification from the outer sect. If the pressure on your spirit becomes too much for you to handle, for your own safety, turn back immediately. No pushing or shoving will be tolerated among participants. Any sabotaging will result in your immediate disqualification."

"That's right," the second judge, Elder Sylvie, said, seamlessly taking over. "You are allowed to use every skill or power at your disposal to make it as far as you can. And lastly, if you gain a seal mid-climb, you will have to redo the entire trial until you can safely conduct it without any… interruptions."

Elder Sylvie smiled. "That being said, we wish you all the best. There is no time limit to the trial. You can begin any time you like, starting right… now."

A gong chimed loudly from somewhere behind them, signaling the start of the trial, and without hesitation, the triplets stepped onto the first stair to begin their climb. Serapha stepped up next, and a few seconds later, only two people remained at the base of the mountain.

Aodhán and Crixis.

Crixis exhaled deeply, staring at the lightning-weathered steps of the mountain before finally taking the first step. He didn't look back at Aodhán or the crowd behind him.

He was locked in.

Aodhán watched him take the first few steps, then followed, exhaling as he felt the subtle pressure of the mountain suddenly sharpen, pressing down on him like a sudden increase in gravity.

It was negligible, though, and after taking a moment to get used to it, Aodhán easily shrugged it off. He realized then that taking things slow was a potential strategy for winning the trial. Doing so would give one's spirit the chance to slowly adapt to the increasing pressure and perhaps make it further than if they rushed.

Aodhán considered doing exactly that, but the truth was that, unlike the others, he didn't need any strategy to go far in the trial. He could feel it in the way his core pulsed and his spirit hummed lazily.

He was too strong to waste time on the lower steps, and without hesitation, he teleported, leaving everyone behind to appear at the 100th step in a burst of violet lightning.

The pressure of the mountain slammed down on him once more, this time far more intense than it had been at the base of the mountain, but to Aodhán, it was still negligible, and with a sharp inhale, he teleported again, clearing the entire outer sect steps in an instant to appear at the 250th step.

This time, he felt the pressure slam down on him like a punch to the gut, and he grinned. Things were finally picking up, but it wasn't enough yet. With another burst of violet lightning, Aodhán teleported again, clearing the entire inner sect steps to appear at the 500th step.

Roars of shock and disbelief erupted from the crowd behind him, but Aodhán barely glanced at them as the pressure of the mountain descended upon him like an avalanche. His spirit jolted awake immediately—chaos and willpower rushing into his soul to fill him to the brim.

They rushed through his pathways and essence threads, filling his body and spirit with so much strength that the pressure of the mountain was instantly cut in half, becoming so manageable that Aodhán was certain he could teleport again without breaking his neck.

And that was exactly what he did.

He appeared at the 600th step an instant later, eyes widening in shock as the mountain's aura slammed upon him with a force so strong his spirit groaned.

Somewhere between step 500 and 600, the pressure of the mountain had intensified so sharply that any more teleporting would risk his demise. And Aodhán certainly wasn't ready to die yet.

Which meant it was finally time to strategize.

He glanced up at the hundreds of steps remaining, and his expression hardened in determination as he whispered, "Are you ready, Varéc?"

Varéc growled eagerly within his spirit, his excitement undeniable.

Aodhán smiled. "Then let's do this."

He activated his Merge perk immediately and hummed contentedly as another wave of strength rushed into his spirit. Massive black wings burst out of his back, and scales raced across his skin like liquid armor, each one locking into place with a soft click. And from his head, inky black horns emerged, glinting as they curved around his skull like a crown of obsidian.

And once again, the pressure diminished.

Teeth bared in excitement, Aodhán smiled widely and took a step, then another, his chest heaving as he pushed against the rising pressure of the mountain's aura.

With each step he took, the pressure heightened—weighing down on his spirit like an unspoken judgment.

But Aodhán wasn't deterred in the least.

He still had plenty of cards to play, and he wouldn't give up until he had used them all.

And high above, six pairs of runic irises watched, waiting to see just how special he would prove himself.

High above the trial mountain, a few minutes before the trial began, the High Elders floated among the clouds, watching the initiates like inscrutable gods—judging, assessing, and pointing out distinct peculiarities among them.

Each cultivator was distinct despite their shared affinity, and that diversity was the true power of any sect. One might have assumed that the gathering of hundreds of thousands of cultivators in one place would blend them into a single indistinguishable mass, but their differences in understanding, bloodline, and path meant they sharpened one another simply by existing.

And this particular group was not lacking in interesting peculiarities.

Out of the eleven, at least seven of them were already walking a cultivation path marked by their experiences and understanding of the storm. To anyone else, that understanding might not be noticeable from a mere stance, but to Calamities, it was as clear as day.

"Storm Superiority," High Elder Mystral—the second seat—hummed quietly, her crimson locks floating behind her as her gaze landed on Crixis, lips slightly downturned. "A generic outlook on the storm if there ever was one. Marcellan really is determined to pass this path down his lineage."

High Elder Tharn—the fifth seat—snorted in offense. "The path is generic because it has been tested and trusted. Explosive power and loud strength are the most common facets of the storm. That doesn't make it weak. In fact, I'd argue it's a more balanced path compared to Momentum, or"—he glanced at High Elder Kaelthorn—"any path that has to do with convergence."

High Elder Mystral glared at him, but before she could speak, High Elder Kaelthorn did, his tone disinterested as he watched the triplets.

"Do not drag me into your petty squabbles, Tharn. If we're talking about unbalanced paths, then our focus should be on that one." He pointed to the inheritor among the triplets and continued. "Purity. That's an unbalanced path if I've ever seen one."

The High Elders turned their gazes to the inheritor, and Velora—the third seat—frowned. "Such low combat potential, that one. I wonder how she stumbled upon such a path."

"Perhaps she witnessed a tribulation," Mystral offered.

"Or an experiment," the First Seat spoke for the first time, his eyes gleaming with a hint of fascination. "Her father is a known alchemist in the elder sect."

"Interesting," Velora muttered, raising her opinion of the girl immediately. "The storm as a source of purity and cleansing is fascinating indeed. I'm sure she would be very useful in a death rift." Her gaze strayed to the one they'd truly come out to see, and she frowned. "The transmigrant, on the other hand, seems to be juggling three paths at once. It's a miracle he can advance at all."

Kaelthorn nodded in agreement. "Having multiple paths isn't uncommon among transmigrants. Double inheritors have it worse, having to deal with their own paths, that of their patrons, and that of their patrons' patrons."

High Elder Mystral snorted. "They wouldn't have it so hard if they just stuck to the paths of their patrons."

"Indeed," Tharn replied. "However, I doubt Aodhán would have much trouble merging his paths of unpredictability with that of Chaotic Storm and the path of Raging Tempest. Whether he will succeed, though, is a different matter entirely." He glanced at Zarxan, who hadn't uttered a word since, and asked, "What do you think?"

Zarxan shrugged without glancing up. "You forget, Tharn, that I know almost as little of the boy as you do."

"But you knew his patron," the First Seat spoke again, taking an interest in High Elder Tharn's question, and Zarxan finally looked up.

A heartbeat of silence passed before Zarxan nodded. "I did know his patron. The path of Raging Tempest was Az'marthon's path. And I suspect the path of Chaotic Storm to be that of his familiar." He glanced back at Aodhán then, golden irises intent. "My concern, however, isn't whether he'll succeed. It's what a merge of these three paths would produce when he eventually succeeds."

The gong resounded in that moment, signaling the start of the trial.

Zarxan glanced at the other High Elders and smiled. "And I bet it'll be a path we've never seen before—maybe even a perfect path."

The High Elders all glanced at him at that, and after a beat of silence, the First Seat spoke. "Those are bold words, Zarxan."

"Too bold, perhaps," Velora scowled. "We've had transmigrants in the sect for years, and none have ever created a perfect path. I fear you've hyped up this child too much. You even made us break protocol just for him, and we still have no idea what's so special about him aside from the fact that he's a transmigrant. Why are we treating him specially?" She glanced down at the trial, and her scowl deepened when she saw the boy simply standing there, arms folded behind his back, while the other participants climbed the mountain. "What's so special about him?"

But Zarxan only smiled, and as Aodhán finally took the first step, that smile widened into a grin.

"I'm sure he's about to show us himself."
