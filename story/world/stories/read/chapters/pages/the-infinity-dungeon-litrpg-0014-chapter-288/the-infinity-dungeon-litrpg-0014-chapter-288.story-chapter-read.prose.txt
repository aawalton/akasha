Chapter 288

At the center of a gigantic chamber of stone, reinforced concrete and steel deep beneath the earth, there was a small spherical shield that glowed with energy. It was almost impossible to see through it, and even its hexagons looked all blurry and glitchy, emitting enough light to blind a normal person even from several dozen yards away.

A surge of mana, and spacetime melted like an amorphous substance. From the unstable portal, Michael emerged, holding hands with Icarus.

“The energy draw has almost doubled,” he said.

Icarus leaned in, getting closer to the shield but without letting go of Michael’s hand. “Some demonic energy is beginning to leak through. Look! It’s interacting with the portal.”

“It is as Johanne suspects, then. Let’s see,” Michael continued, summoning a screen. “The draw on the shield increased to match a sudden and permanent spike in exotic energy emissions from the shard. It happened both here and at the Vatican at the same time. Timestamp coincides with the moment the kids disappeared in Korea, moments after the Faith signature.”

“The Domain is providing it with more than enough energy,” Icarus said. “For now.”

“For how much longer, I wonder…” Michael said. He also leaned in, finding himself inches away from Icarus’s face. Touching the shield, he found it warm.

Then, his hand phased through the shield and went inside. Icarus gasped, taking a step back and squeezing Michael’s hand hard. They did not let go, instead attempting to pull Michael back with raw strength and failing.

Michael grimaced, gritting his teeth but not pulling his hand out of the shield yet. He resisted several more seconds, then finally with a grunt of pain, he pulled it out. There was a lot of resistance, as if the shield did not want to let him through anymore. When he finally managed to remove the hand from it, Icarus’s pulling was no longer being balanced, and he stumbled back a few steps.

He ended up against Icarus’s chest, but the AI was staring transfixed at Michael’s arm. It was blackened, covered in smoldering scales. His fingers ended in dark, sharp talons and oily smoke wafted up from his cracking skin. More worryingly, the corruption was spreading up, toward his shoulder, and what was happening in the material world was also being reflected in the inner space, where data lines were becoming red–just like they had when the devil attacked him.

With a quick motion, he let go of Icarus’s hand and used the now free hand to make a sword out of pure mana. He slashed at his corrupted arm, above where the corruption had managed to spread, severing the limb completely. Icarus yelped, the sudden lack of physical contact making them shiver and look around, and then their body vanished in a puff of magic, leaving Michael alone.

Meanie, the AI sent to his mind.

He still felt their presence and knew that they were watching. Holding his stump, which was already no longer bleeding, he directed some magic toward it and began to heal it. It was slow and sluggish, and the tingling was almost uncomfortable.

“It’s slowly transforming,” he said, looking at the severed part of the limb on the ground. “Fascinating. Look, it’s trying to reach the blood.”

“I say you should destroy it,” Icarus said in his mind. “You meanie, you let go of my hand just to stare at that abomination?”

“You’re right,” Michael replied. “I think I should.”

He blasted the area with mana, and frowned when the corrupted limb did not immediately disintegrate. Slowly, he upped the dosage until he was forced to draw upon his store of magic coins, all the way to Renegade Energy. It finally began to slowly affect the limb, but even then it took a long time to see any real damage. Only when he finally summoned the spiral did the limb finally vanish.

He turned to face the containment shield again. As he did, he felt a small hand grab his. It was small, but its grip was like iron, afraid to let go again.

“What were you trying to do?” Icarus asked, standing on their tiptoes to look at the shield above Michael's shoulder.

As Michael's other arm regrew, he slowly approached the thing again. “How many shards are in there?”

“Seven.”

He felt Icarus pull harder to prevent him from approaching again. “Don’t worry, I’m not going to do it again.”

The pull slackened a bit, but not completely. “You tried to use the spiral on them, didn’t you?”

“Yes. It did not work.”

“We knew that already.”

“I am more powerful now,” Michael said. “But it made no difference. Let’s go see the pope.”

“We have some materials we can gather,” Icarus said, eager to help. “If you stop by the warehouses, I can begin building some upgrades while you talk with his holiness.”

Space bent and he stepped through. Behind him, he felt through the Domain that his passage left the veil of the world rather weakened, something that should not have happened.

Just like in Korea.

When Michael emerged from the portal, he was in an ornate yet mostly empty room. Its only feature, save for the decorations in the walls and the fresco on the ceiling, was the massive containment shield occupying most of the free space.

The strain on this shield produced so much light, that another shield had been erected around it just to prevent the room from combusting.

“Three hundred and ninety three shards,” Michael muttered.

Icarus was behind him, wearing a loose t-shirt and a strange fishnet top that reached down their arms all the way to their hands. When the door made a small noise and began to open, they pressed themselves against his body, wrapping around Michael’s arm.

“Pope Damasus,” Michael said, nodding at the man.

The pope paused for a moment, and Michael could swear that he was doing his best not to contort his face in disgust at the sight and failing. A slight disturbance made the faint mist of Faith energy in the room ripple.

“Michael Lexington,” the man said, struggling to look him in the eye. His gaze kept returning to Icarus as they clung to him.

The short elf was pouting, looking displeased at being interrupted by his holiness, and the pope’s eyes almost shot out of his skull when Icarus stuck out their tongue and made strange faces at him.

“Don’t mind Icarus,” Michael said.

The pope looked as if struck. “That… person…” he paused, noticing Icarus’s ears and then deciding he wasn’t going to care anymore. “He’s Icarus?”

“They, yes.” Michael corrected him.

“The Icarus? The AI?”

“Yep,” he said.

The pope breathed, trying to collect himself. Icarus simply clung to Michael harder, putting one hand around his back and pushing their face into the back of his arm.

I swear you’re doing it on purpose, Michael thought.

Icarus mentally pouted. You were a meanie earlier and I want to feel you close to me, what’s wrong with that?

“You made me wait. I suppose late is better than never,” the pope said after a long time. He was young for a pope, but the way he stared at the multilayered shield around the artifact in the room made him look beyond ancient.

“I was… otherwise occupied,” Michael replied. “Recent events shifted my priorities.”

The pope did not move. The shield was mere inches away from his face, hexagons glowing as they caught stray particles. This shield was much thicker than the one at Site 00, but it still wasn’t enough to prevent some spillage of energy.

“Will the shield you have put around the mirror be able to contain the Prima Azothica Corruptio?”

“Is it the official name of the energy that’s spilling out from the shield?” Michael asked.

The pope nodded. “As is documented in the books of the Inquisition. Azoth is the universal alchemical solvent, affected by the Prima corruption, the primordial betrayal that spawned all evil.”

Michael inhaled, nodding toward the shield. “Biblical, it fits. Why did the Don use an artifact that produces this… P.A.C. Energy? Did he not know of the consequences of its usage?”

“For the same reason I was elected pope, I suppose.” The man said solemnly. “Arrogance and greed.”

He sighed, looking straight at Michael. He was slightly shorter than him, but taller than Icarus–who he ignored completely.

“I was arrogant in thinking that I could control the Don,” he said. “And just like I arrogantly made a deal with him, planning to double cross him, he too arrogantly made a deal with a devil while planning to double cross it.”

“Why the Don? Why him?”

“He had found the lost texts, and promised us a way back to power long lost. My predecessor was wise, he knew that the Church’s power had been relinquished at the end of the Dark Ages for a reason, and the mirror sealed away. The warnings, in time, faded to legend. To pagan legend.”

“The Don made pope Francis abdicate,” Icarus said. “I found proof.”

Pope Damasus smiled wryly. “I don’t need your proof, machine mind. The Don never hid the fact from me. A papal renunciation, power in exchange for cooperation. It was a deal too good to be true, but also too good to turn down.”

“What about the PAC energy? Did you not know he was dealing with devils?” Michael asked.

“He did not tell us of the sacrifice needed to use a shard to contact another realm,” the pope said. “He made us believe that he had a special bloodline that could use the artifact without paying its price. The truth was different… he had made a deal with a devil, paid God knows what price.”

“The devil fed on the PAC energy, covering the Don’s tracks,” Michael said. He finally understood. “That’s why it was so strong! As for the sacrifice, it was kids, probably. Have there been disappearances?”

“Multiple, yes.”

Michael sighed. “How did he manage to fool you?”

“He forged an official document, showing the methodical destruction of most of his lineage by the Inquisition, with him as the sole descendant of the few survivors. Only later, using Faith, did I discover the forgery. By then it was too late. He was using the shards to provide us with Faith artifacts.”

“You could have stopped him,” Michael pressed.

The pope laughed. “I did not want to stop him. The moment he snapped, I told myself, I was going to smite the devil that resided within his soul with the light of God. That’s how the Inquisition had done it. And that is how I slept at night. I knew even then that I was fooling myself.”

“In the end, you couldn’t even smite him. You made De Ponti do it.”

“That I did.”

A solemn air descended on the room, only ruined by the constant hum of the shield.

“And now here we are,” Michael said. “I studied the shards as much as I could. When the Don finally died, he–or rather the devil inside of him–did something to them. The moment we try to move them, they begin to self-destruct. If we leave them where they are, they slowly gather PAC energy, corrupting everything around them. Even then, it was going to be fine. The energy build-up was slow and gradual. In time, I was going to find a way to deal with them. Now, however? To answer your original question, will the shield be able to contain them? Not for much longer.”

“What changed?” the pope asked.

“Korea. A classroom of twenty three children and a professor suddenly disappeared.”

“A sacrifice,” the pope said gravely. “Someone used a shard.”

“Except, there was no devil there to absorb the PAC energy released by the event. Do you know what happens when PAC energy is allowed to linger?”

The pope shook his head. He was leaning on the shield, using it like a wall to hold himself upright as the weight of his sins threatened to make him topple over.

Michael swallowed words of contempt. Instead, he spoke calmly. “The PAC energy weakens the veil of the world. Long ago, I used to be able to peek beneath the veil, your holiness. A single glimpse is enough to turn people insane. But the PAC energy does more. It originates from a single place, and it seeks to return there.”

“The Netherworld,” the pope said.

“When we teleported to Korea, our sudden use of spacetime magic suddenly ripped the veil apart. Instead of whatever lies beneath reality itself, the PAC energy made use of this rift to connect to the Netherworld. We were almost overrun, and only my Domain prevented the total collapse of the veil there.”

“The Domain?” the pope’s eyes lit up. “Is it the same thing that blankets this room even now?”

Michael nodded. “And you, you have the power to close the rifts with your… spiral. Don’t you?”

“I do,” Michael confirmed.

“Then perhaps not all is lost.”

“Perhaps,” Michael said slowly. “The fact remains. Somebody is making sacrifices to use the shard, leaving a right mess behind them. And the devils are watching, pope Damasus, waiting for the veil to weaken so that they may try to slip through. They want to invade our world, your holiness, and your greed is part of the reason why they might succeed.”

“My greed?”

“Yes. Give me your cross.”
