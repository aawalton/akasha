Chapter 42

“Every civilisation asks the same question at the first classification threshold. What am I becoming? The answer is always the same. You are not becoming. You are being recognised.” - Codex of the Long Watch, Watcher’s Commentary

The magazine level smelled of rust and nitre from the age of gunpowder. The saltpeter saturating the walls.

But his eyes were on the doors. They had changed; the iron was darker than it should have been, carrying a grain that elevated it above the foundry cast manufacturing they had been. He pressed his palm against the nearest one and felt the warmth and the denseness.

He moved his hands across the metal, and his Resonance told him that the Ether signature was strongest where the hinges met the limestone, the contact points where iron had been worked into Ether-soaked stone. Those joints were the most transformed.

“This is what we came for,” he said with a grin and then got hit in the face as the door flew open.

The impact boomed through the corridor like a battering ram. And a beetle, the size of a large dog, came through the gap. Its chitin plates overlapping across its back in layered armour and six legs driving it forward with a speed that the confined space made worse. Its mandibles snapped shut on the air where Arthur’s leg had been a half-second earlier.

Donna danced to the side, and her axe found the largest chitin plate. Full force, perfect angle. The blade bounced, leaving a white scratch on the armour and nothing more.

“Can’t cut it,” she said, and that meant everything about the next sixty seconds had changed.

The beetle charged Arthur. He caught it on the Ironhold, and the creature’s mandibles clamped on the shield’s edge, the force driving him back a full step. The thing was strong. Its legs churned against the stone floor, pushing, the mandibles grinding against transmuted iron.  Arthur’s boots were slowly sliding back; the beetle was winning.

The carapace made it almost impossible to injure the thing from above. Arthur tried to formulate while being driven back. They needed leverage, and they needed to flip the script.

"Steve, aim at its face. I need the shield free. Donna, destabilize it, use leverage from below, we need to flip it."

Steve jabbed the Reacher at the beetle's mandibles. The blade skidded off chitin but the mandibles released the shield to snap at the polearm and that was the opening Donna needed. She hit the beetle from the side, low and hard, both hands on the axe haft driven like a lever under the edge of its carapace. She wasn’t strong enough to flip the beetle, but she didn’t need to be. The thing's back legs left the floor, and that was the opening Arthur needed.

He dropped his shoulder into the Ironhold and drove it across with everything he had. The shield's broad face slammed the beetle from the same side Donna was lifting it from, and it put the thing flat on its back. Legs churned in the air. The soft underside turned up to the ceiling like an offering.

Arthur put his weight on the shield and held it there. The beetle thrashed under him. Every shove tried to buck the shield off. But he was now pushing from above; the leverage favoured him.

"Donna, now."

It was a butchery. Donna struck again and again, and the creature's thrashing weakened with each hit until the fourth strike found something vital and the movement stopped.

“One down,” she said. “How many more?”

“Chitin plating of that density is atypical for this Ether concentration,” Archie said. “The ventral seams are the primary structural limitation.”

* * *

The second beetle was larger than the first. Thicker carapace, heavier mandibles and a body that almost filled the corridor from wall to wall, which meant the one behind it couldn't come abreast. That was the only thing going right.

It came straight at Arthur in a head-down charge.

The flip wouldn't work, it had too much mass. And even if Donna could crouch low enough to lever the carapace, she'd be putting herself in the second beetle's reach to do it.

Arthur kept the shield moving, trading mandible strikes for hammer blows, refusing to let the beetle latch. But he was giving ground. Donna tried to feint wide and drop low, looking for the ventral angle, and couldn't find it, the approach angle was wrong with the second beetle still pacing behind. It was turning into a stalemate. The kind that would end the moment Arthur ran out of corridor behind him.

They needed an opening at the front and the front was all carapace and mandibles, no gaps, no seams. Unless.

“Donna, I’ll give you an opening, aim for the throat,” Arthur shouted while exchanging blows.

“How?”

“By doing something incredibly stupid,” Arthur replied.

He held the shield out to the beetle like an offering.

The mandibles clamped on it immediately, and Arthur let go. The hammer dropped next. And for a second he was unarmed and in arm’s reach of a two-meter, iron jawed beetle.

Before the beetle could drop the shield he grabbed the mandibles where they met the head. Two fistfuls of chitin, the jaw-seams sharp enough to cut. He hauled up, forcing the beetle's head back, the neck joint opening exactly the way he needed it to open and triggered Structural Reinforcement.

The technique locked through him in a wave he knew by now, his body becoming the thing that doesn't move.

The corridor had a new sculpture. A rendition of Hercules and the lion, rendered in chitin and a man refusing to let go.

The beetle bucked and thrashed. Arthur didn't move. Buildings don't flinch.

"Don't think we aren't going to talk about this," Donna said, and went to work with the axe.

Arthur released Structural Reinforcement before the beetle finished falling.

"Steve - fire. Keep the third one off us."

The flame came out tight and controlled, not the ragged cone from the swarm fight. The third beetle flinched and backed off.

Arthur picked up his hammer and shield and waited for his legs to remember they had bones in them. With his increased Constitution and Strength, the recovery period was getting shorter.

The three of them held position and so did the beetle. It wasn't coming forward. It was watching them, mandibles tucked close, chitin angled, waiting.

It had learned.

"Steve, you got another blast in you? Just a flinch."

Arthur moved forward. Donna stepped into position on his right, half a pace back. Steve followed, the Reacher angled low.

"Maybe. Running on fumes. I can't tell you how much is left."

“Don't push it. Just the flinch. And when you do, drop the Reacher next to me."

"Drop what now?"

Arthur didn't answer. His eyes were on the beetle. Four metres. Three.

The Reacher clanged onto the stone at his left.

"Donna, on my mark."

Arthur threw the Ironhold at the beetle's face.

The creature's mandibles jerked up to swat it aside, and that was Steve's window. A tight burst of superheated Ether reached the beetle's face just as the shield glanced off.

The beetle reared up in panic. Chitin plates flashing, forelegs clawing at nothing, its underside towards the party.

Steve's flame guttered out, but it had bought the window Arthur needed. He scooped up the Reacher mid-step, lunged, and drove the blade up into the soft tissue under the rearing creature's thorax. He kept driving, two hands on the haft, prying the wound wider.

Donna came in behind him. Fast and low and straight to the opening Arthur had pried. The Refined axe carved an X into the exposed underbelly.

Everything went silent.

* * *

Arthur went back to the doors.

The hinges were the prize. Thick iron plates bolted into Ether-channelling limestone for five hundred years, soaking in Sanctum-grade density for seven days. The sections furthest from the walls were dark but recognisably iron. The hinge plates themselves were something else, the grain carrying a crystalline structure he’d never seen in any metal.

He used the lump hammer on the bolt heads. Three strikes each, precise. Donna levered with the pry bar. Six plates from three doors, each the size of a hardback book.

Steve picked one, turning it over with bandaged fingers. “This is my focus. I just need to figure out how to make it into one.”

They divided the rest between their packs. Carry what you can. Leave what you can’t. Everything in the dungeon was a trade between weight and survival. Unless you had a bag of holding. There was no bag of holding.

* * *

The stairway to the third level changed. Military stonework gave way to something rougher and older, limestone cut from the hill itself, the tool marks unfamiliar, the dimensions following a logic that predated anything in Steve's books.

“This is pre-fort,” Steve said quietly. “Pre-everything. This was here when Pierre built his chapel eight hundred years ago and nobody knew.”

The stairway opened into the cistern.

The chamber was circular, twice the size the hill’s geometry should have allowed. The walls were rough limestone. The floor was smooth, worn by centuries of collected water. The cistern had been sealed when the fort diverted its water supply elsewhere, sealed with stagnant water still inside.

That water had been sitting in a chamber beneath a major Grid node when the Ether arrived. A week of Sanctum-grade saturation had turned it into something that wasn’t water anymore. It filled half the chamber, dense and faintly violet, the surface rippling with a slow energy. Ether-saturated water. A battery that had been charging since Day Zero in a sealed stone container that channelled the Grid's overflow into a space with nowhere for it to go.

And in that water stood something difficult to classify. Stone and iron and saturated water fused into something that filled half the available space. It was humanoid the way a landslide is a sculpture, the rough suggestion of a shape produced by forces that didn’t care about form. It stood while Ether flowed around its mass, climbing up through its legs in ribbons.

As the party positioned, it slowly stepped out of the pool, and the chamber shook. The Ether ribbons connecting it to the pool dragged behind it.

"It's feeding off the pool," Arthur said.

Steve was already tracking the logic. Power source, drain strategy. Cisterns had drains by definition; that was what made them cisterns. His eyes tracked to the far wall, past the construct's shoulder.

"There. Behind it. Blocked with debris. If we clear it, the water runs off into the limestone channels under the hill."

"The drain that's behind the room-sized monster," Donna said.

"There's a gap along the right wall," Arthur said.

"That's not a gap. That's a dare."

"We'll keep it off you." Arthur raised the Ironhold.

He brought the hammer down on the shield's face. The sound rang through the cistern, bouncing off limestone, filling the chamber with a note the construct couldn't ignore.

The construct swung.

Arthur caught the blow on the shield, and the world narrowed to physics and its effect on biology. The impact was unlike anything he’d absorbed. Heavier than the alpha, heavier than the raptor’s dive, a mass of animated stone delivering force that the Ironhold’s channels struggled to distribute. The shock travelled through his arm, his shoulder, through his locked legs into the floor. His boots cracked the stone beneath them, and his forearm screamed.

And still he held.

The construct drew back for a second swing, and Steve hit it with fire - a sustained blast to the head, burning through Reserve he couldn't afford to spend. The construct turned toward him, and Donna went for the gap.

She had taken three steps along the right wall when the construct's arm came around in a backswing. She dropped flat, the stone limb passing over her by centimetres, close enough to pull her hair. She rolled, came up, and ran.

She didn't make it. The construct's leg moved, and the gap closed to nothing. Donna reversed, throwing herself back toward Arthur, and the construct's foot stamped down where she'd been standing. The stone floor shattered under it.

“Again,” Arthur said. “Steve, harder. Make it commit.”

Steve grimaced and pushed everything he had left. This time, along with fire came something new, a concussive ripple that compressed the air in front of the construct’s face. The creature staggered half a step, and its weight transferred left.

The gap on the right opened.

Donna triggered Reflex Acceleration and went. She sprinted, her body low, every movement stripped to its absolute minimum. The construct half-turned and reached for her, and Arthur threw himself at its leg. The Ironhold connected with the back of its knee, and the construct stumbled. Donna was through, and it was turning towards Arthur again, who was lying flat on his back where his leg-check had left him.

Donna reached the drain. The debris was transmuted stone, mineral deposits cemented over the channel mouth. She hit it with the prybar. It cracked the surface, but the mass held.

“Arthur!”

He looked up at the debris mass from twenty feet away, calculating load paths and weak joints.

“Bottom left! Where the dark stone meets the lighter deposit. That joint is carrying everything above it.”

The construct’s arm came down, and Arthur rolled. The fist hit the floor where his chest had been, and the impact cratered the stone. He scrambled to his feet, got the shield up, and took the next blow standing. His left arm went numb, but the bones held. He didn’t know for how much longer.

From behind the construct came a sharp crack, and the drain opened. Saturated water poured into the channel, flowing down into the limestone, into the hill's channels, into the Grid infrastructure that had been blocked.

The construct shuddered. The energy connection through its legs thinned as the pool drained, and stone cracked along its torso. Its next swing at Arthur arrived slower, still powerful enough to kill, but readable. He stepped aside. The arm hit the wall instead, and fragments of stone sprayed across the chamber.

Donna came from behind. Strike after strike, finding the cracks that widened as the pool emptied. Arthur joined from the front, his hammer doing what it was made for, demolition. The construct shed pieces of itself with each blow, shrinking as its source drained, until Donna’s prybar found the core of it, and the whole structure came apart.

The sound was a building collapsing. Arthur shielded his face with the Ironhold and felt fragments ping off the surface.

A core sat in the rubble. Large. Stone-grey with veins of dark iron. Arthur picked it up, and his Resonance read weight, density, permanence.

The pool was still draining. The water flowed steadily into the limestone, and Arthur could feel the hill responding, the node strengthening above them, the Grid’s backed-up plumbing clearing for the first time in millennia.
