The senior Fierkraag gave them a sidelong glance and grunted. “Like you’re going to keep this private from her? I know what you’ve both done. And I’m disappointed. Miss Suzie, I’m sorry, I need you to tell Miss Brightstone that I truly enjoyed her company tonight, even if it was unexpected. She really does take after her father, and I’m delighted she has developed her own signature.”

Fiona’s heart would swell with pride at that, and Darla would be over herself, but they still were on a clock, as Norbert continued. “However, there are matters of family to discuss, and I don’t wish for her to witness any discourteous exchanges.”

You two need to put aside your differences for one damn night!” she scolded. “Dragons, people, whatever! You can set it aside for one night, because I really, really, really want to see what Miss Brightstone has for dessert!”

“Don’t Missy me, Karlin!” Fiona shied away as embers began emanating from Missy's hands. She had a vague idea that if Missy went into a full meltdown, the entire secret underground lair might melt in the process. “Gimmie your datapads, and your attention for one night, please! I get that you two have unresolved issues, but not tonight! You two are so childish!”

Norbert raised a scaly brow at that. “Oh? You know it might have been prudent to let me know my son was leasing the place, in a place where I can at least not be bothered by anyone.”

Fiona stepped in. “Mister Fierkraag, I think you have unresolved issues, too.”

He blinked. “Come again?”

“Where’s the missus? This huge home, so empty…yet I see artwork and other flair that doesn’t seem to suit your style,” Fiona called out. “I’m not dumb. I think I recognize the name, but I’m hoping I’m wrong.”

Norbert let out a soft sigh. “If you’re wondering…yes. I am the widow of the late Fierkraag, better known in Fiefdala as a shepherd of the lost. She...made many enemies in Vale for her efforts."

“Father, we don't need to–”

“No. I think we do. Son, we haven't talked in almost twenty years. What have you done since then?” The father let out a reluctant sigh. He then pulled his datapad out of his vest pocket and handed it to Missy. Even with the beefed-up frame and size, Fiona saw faint claw marks on the screen. “I will…discuss our arrangements in the morning.”

If there was a tomorrow for Karlin that wasn’t in a Fiefdala prison. As far as Fiona knew, Norbert had, at best, a secluded house that his son had used without his knowledge to put forth his schemes, and either knew about it or what happened in Vale.

Karlin’s hands remained stuffed in his pockets. Then, reluctantly, he handed Missy the datapad. “Miss Suzie, I have one question. Why are you here for this?”

“A lot of reasons. Not all of which I care to share right away,” she said quietly. Damn it. Maybe the reason I’m entertaining this at all is that I didn’t get closure with my dad. Not the right way. “Besides, I can tell some regrets are going on between you two.”

“Without going into specifics…you could say that,” Norbert, glancing at his son. Karlin bristled at it, as if his father's presence offended him. “Well, Karlin? Are you going to be a gracious host to your esteemed guest?”

He let out a grumble and led the way. Missy tailed right behind Fiona, flipping through the datapad as fast as she could. “He put a clawprint passcode on it. Paranoid dragon, indeed,” she muttered under her breath. “Still got a passphrase unlocked at least. I think it’s seven letters–”

“You really think his passcode would be his brother, who he despises with every fiber of his being–”

“Just try it.” Fiona realized they had less than ten minutes. She tapped the relay button tucked behind her hair. “Nick, where are we at?”

“Got access. Guards are still an issue for–” the bird trailed off on his relay. “Uh…one looks like he might have just jetted to the nearest washroom.”

“How can you tell?” They were almost in range of the room, with Karlin begrudgingly holding the door open for his father.

“Oh, Fi, there was no mistaking it. Oh, there goes the other, in a different direction.” She heard the softest mocking hoot from Nick. “Remind me never to piss off Bonnie. I might not survive the offense. I let Doug know to be ready.”

All she needed to do was keep them at the table for ten minutes. That should be easy enough. They all got seated as Darla brought out a few items that Fiona thought might be for making candies. Except, this wily darkling had other plans, and brought out a small ice elemental from a freezer box, currently eating…

Fiona frowned. That was her favorite ice cream that little wispy booger was chowing on, minty ice cream, in a world where the concept of frozen treats had barely caught on yet. Darla waved to them. “I was starting to feel lonely out here. You boys hug it out?”

Norbert let out a gruff laugh. “For now. I must ask, what are you doing for dessert?”

“Oh, this little thing?” Darla pulled out what Fiona swore was a fork-like tool and then dipped it into the bright red fluid that almost looked like hard candy in liquid form. The end spun with a slight sound of arcane energy, and she began spinning it, clipping it with incredible speed with a pair of scissors, both speedy and delicate in precision.

Meanwhile, the tiny elemental began casting out a frigid cloud that froze the pieces in place, locking them in shape. Soon, Darla had a colorful display of candied dessert, sprinkling a little powdered sugar and an amber-colored syrup from another hot pan. The elemental froze the syrup to form what looked like a three-dimensional tree. Then, she took this and added a dollop of a third color, giving the tree candied-green leaves.

Darla then spun the edible arrangement around for the whole table to see, with Norbert giving one approving nod. Even smiling. “Goodness, Miss Brightstone. You could open a restaurant with that kind of effort. You’d have a line out the door every night, and I think your father would be proud.”

“I probably could. It's always been about making people feel good, first and foremost,” she answered, giving a slight bow. “Do you recognize the plant?”

“The figurative peace branch?” he asked, brow raised. It wasn’t lost upon him, and he glanced at his son, looking slightly less surly. “It’s a native plant that grows throughout Fiefdala and further north, extending past the Arkantines. The tradition is that if two rivals split a forked branch, and if it split cleanly down the middle, they would set aside their differences and make an effort at reconciliation. It was a tradition of the precursor to Fiefdala, steeped in superstition. But…Some peaceful efforts did endure through this practice.”

Karlin picked up on it. “You know why we have our differences, father.”

“Almost there, Fi,” Doug whispered in Fiona’s earpiece. “I see Nick’s lighting up the teleport, right on time. If you can’t respond…I trust you, okay?”

I hope this is the right call. Or I’m about to start a four-way brawl that isn’t going to end well for anyone. She focused on Norbert, letting out a reluctant sigh.

“We don’t have to keep our differences, if we don’t want to,” he answered back, gesturing to Darla. “I think even our illustrious chef can see the rift. I think I need to detail why.”

Fiona heard a chime in the background as the teleport activated. Karlin’s brow raised, and he stood up from the table, wings tensed slightly. “Father, can we put this big speech on hold for a second? I’m not expecting any other visitors tonight.” He clicked his relay, but Fiona knew the guards were indisposed for the moment.

She heard the soft click of claws coming down the hallway, along with a grumble. Then a sound of surprise. “Classy. Another La’teur? How’d he manage to hold onto this piece for this long? Oh, I love the seaside, though.”

Karlin’s eyes widened as did Norbert’s. The senior dragon stood up as well, advancing to the door, and Karlin sputtered out a protest.

“Don’t open that door!” Karlin shouted.

But he was too late. Norbert opened the door, peering out–then had to adjust his gaze down. Fiona saw Doug standing there, in motion as if he were about to knock, and remained frozen . Fiona saw Norbert’s arms go slack against his side.

“Douglas?”

“Father?”

Yet, the effort wasn’t needed.

Norbert intercepted Karlin’s swing and held him firmly from swiping his claws at Doug, who hadn’t even flinched. Norbert turned with deliberate calm, eyes narrowed at his son. “Yes. I wanted to talk to you about this, son.” He peered at Doug. “How did you get in? I also see the rumors weren't exaggerated. You did get shrunk."
