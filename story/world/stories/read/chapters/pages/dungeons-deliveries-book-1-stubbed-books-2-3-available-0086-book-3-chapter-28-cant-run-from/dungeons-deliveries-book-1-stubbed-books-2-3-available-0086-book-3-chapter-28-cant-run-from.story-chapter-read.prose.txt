Alex ran with his face locked in a grimace.

There were no finger guns to be fired. No music played in his head. Flashy dodges and jukes around pedestrians were forgotten. There was no purposeful destination to race towards. Alex huffed, arms pumping, heart hammering, flameless feet propelling him forward, trying to outrun building tears. Burning like lactic acid behind his eyes, the overwhelming effort barely held his weeping at bay.

As the sun slinked closer to the horizon, and the swirling sky shifted through its orange to purple, Alex held no space in his heart for the beauty of the post apocalypse world.

He blasted east from Wonderland before taking a sharp right at Yonge street. A strange thought flitted through his head.

Yonge’s the longest street in the world.

Or so it had been at one time. He’d read that online once. That the street went from the bottom of Toronto all the way to the top of Canada. Maybe that was true. Maybe it was a lie too.

Pedestrians were blurred blobs he whizzed by recklessly. He jumped off and on the sidewalk, not using any skills to keep moving. Whatever kept his lungs screaming for air. Weaving vehicles grinding through gridlock traffic almost clipped him a dozen times. But Alex didn’t care.

The truth of it all hurt too much to think about.

And it had to be true. The System notification had confirmed it. His noble quest to fulfill the requirements of the heroic hawk core was already partially fulfilled. Out of the four necessary components, his very existence satisfied one.

He just hadn’t known, and once the information became available, the System stamped its marker and called it a day.

He was already related to Nino and Nino. Alex was family.

They knew. They held onto the information, and likely from the get-go. Was it all a lie? Have they been using me? Watching me?

They’re a Lich, of course they were using me, Alex thought bitterly, then recoiled from how violent the thought felt. He’d thunk it as a slur.

Am I just like her? Evil to my core?

“Fuuuuuuck!” Alex yelled as he stormed through a crosswalk. He was running as fast as he could, a full out sprint, and still his brain wouldn’t shut up.

It was close to a year he’d been delivering. His body had trimmed away every bit of fat. He’d sprinted to every corner of the city and through Dungeons each shift for hours. The very job which had solved all his problems had made it impossible to exhaust himself to the point of true fatigue.

It wasn’t working. He couldn’t run to or from this problem.

Still going to flipping try to, though.

He fired a [Phantom Step], porting twenty feet straight into screaming traffic.

A speeding, armoured truck was a foot from decking him, and time slowed to a crawl as his [Relative Voyeur] activated.

His eyes moved as normal, and he stared for a moment at the massive swirling purple mass of the truck inching closer to him.

Just because I see it coming doesn’t mean I need to make a decision, right?

He recoiled again from his morbid thought. He wasn’t suicidal.

Picking a spot to safety, Alex still let the truck get within a hairsbreadth of running him over before activating another [Phantom Step]. The cold iron face of the truck pressed into his skin as he disappeared into the port.

Through the strange tunnel he went, time still at a crawl, the overwhelming nature of flitting through the unspace forcing itself into his brain.

The other places he could witness made themselves apparent. Familiar and unfamiliar at the same time, Alex felt a kinship with them all. Even the places that felt like an abyss of nothing. And there were plenty of those. The dark ones were far more numerous than the places that reminded himself of himself. Most were different enough to feel unlike him. A guy who looked sort of like him polishing a glass behind a bar and handing it off to a human-sized spider creature. A girl with his eyes drawing on a beach of black beads.

But a few of those others felt just like him.

They ran their hearts out. Or flew through forests or buildings or over desserts. Or punched through walls or rock. One ground glass in his hand. Almost none looked exactly like Alex, but he just knew they ran from the same revelation as him. Or same enough.

And then he was ripped out and shoved back to reality. Into his loud city and louder head.

The thought of his parents bubbled up, and he cast another [Phantom Step] to focus on something else.

Anything but that.

Again and again he [Phantom Stepped] in whatever direction his legs took him. Quickly, his core began to drain of available essence. He pushed himself to his limit in favour of thinking about them. And finally, through activating his skill numerous times, he began huffing.

Right as his core began to hurt, warning him of its eventual cracking, he became aware of where his legs had delivered him.

Breathing heavy, Alex finally slowed from a sprint to a walk and came to a stop. He craned his neck to look upwards.

I ran all the way here?

He stared up from the base of the tallest building he knew of. The CN Tower.

The ancient building was half crumbling and covered in graffiti. Its famous needle top had been shorn like a bamboo stalk at some point. The bulbous portion, where people had once eaten dinner or partook in the infamous skywalk around the outside of the building while strapped tight, was blown out and burned. Alex had read online about the last party that had happened there. That would ever happen there. Some aged, pudgy rapper with a fake Jamaican accent had gone and exploded it and himself up, and all for a music video that had barely gotten any views.

Thoughts of his parents tickled back in and—

“Nope,” Alex huffed, shaking his head. “Definitely nope.”

He stared back up at the derelict building, then around the surrounding square. Pedestrians bustled by. Now that the hunt for the Tax Guild Stone was over, life had resumed as normal.

“Bet the stairs are still there.”

Eager to move, he took off, and surprisingly, when he reached a utility door, it creaked open at his pull.

Alex stared into the dark corridor for a moment, then back into the square, feeling as if he should be getting in trouble from someone. Surely there were guards about. Yet no one even spared him a glance.

Peeking back into the corridor, he spotted a set of stairs. He wasn’t sure how, but something told him that these were the right set. As if someone was tipping him off to go to the top, and the stairs would provide the way.

He pocketed the Tax Guild Stone, not a single surrounding civilian aware of it being right there in front of them, and slipped into the building.

As the door shut behind him, he was happy to learn that the CN Tower was in such disrepair that light filtered through cracks in the concrete, making it possible to see.

“Orange light. Real dark orange. Sunset doesn’t leave me much time.” Alex said to himself and he jogged up to base of the stairs.

He leaned forward and looked up. There were winding sets of stairs going up forever.

How many floors is the CN tower again? I wonder if my paren—

Alex started his ascent before allowing the thought the chance to finish.

One set became two and quickly turned to ten. He pushed harder, taking two steps at a time, refusing to port up and make it easier.

By the thirty-third set, at which point he was doing three stairs at a time in giant lunges, his breath ragged. Sparing a glance upwards into the vertical belly of the tower, there was still plenty of stairs to ascend.

It felt good for his legs to burn. For his lungs to grasp eagerly for air. To not think about them. To lose himself in physical effort.

What monsters he passed were small. Clusters of chittering spiders in corners were easily avoided. Sentient slime mould that stretched along many of walls was too slow to grab him. The entire time, something called him upwards to the top, not flaring any pangs of warning in his belly.

Two minutes and eleven seconds was all it took for Alex to reach the last set. Slowing his aching legs, he had to wipe his forehead of sweat. He couldn’t even remember the last time he’d been tired from running.

Cresting the top set, he came to a simple utility door.

Though his heart hammered in his chest, he didn’t feel any fear about exiting. His stomach didn’t churn or clench, and his legs had led him exactly where he needed to be.

Alex pulled the door and walked out onto the top of the bulbous portion of the CN Tower, to stand at the highest point of the city.

The massive sun was tucking itself in the west, and the first swirls of stars and night sky were arriving. Spring wind cooled him without blasting. Despite his muddled mood, Alex took in the view.

Lake Ontario’s waters glittered under the sky, the clean air providing sight on the cities on the other side. He spotted boats bobbing around, some small and filled with riders brave or stupid enough to take on lake monsters, and some freshwater yachts surely better protected, where the rich drank enhanced champagne and enjoyed their station. He turned his eyes from the water towards the city.

The sprawling metropolis was awaking for the night. Lights of all colors came from the streets and from advertisements floating around or plastered to buildings. The snaking traffic looked practically organized and quiet from so high up. What surprised him most was how many houses, condos, and apartments there were.

Alex imagined families living behind those lit squares, just living as if any of the world was normal. He walked along the top of the CN Tower to stand a safe distance from the edge, feeling his old hurts bubbling up. Part of him was astounded at how much life there was down there.

Alex’s chest hitched as he held back his tears. There was no place left to run to.

He couldn’t remember his parents. There wasn’t even a photo left behind to admire.

All he could remember was his mother’s curly brown hair. He was pretty sure about that.

He remembered how crowded the orphanages were. How he’d been moved from place to place, sleeping when it was safe or when his body demanded it. Food was either plentiful or scarce, all dependent on how bad the hordes were, if people with food magic were available, or if the orphanage workers remembered.

Not all of them were bad.

Some of the nicer orphanage workers had assured him, along with all the other children in those crowded places, that their parents were heroes. That they’d died nobly, fighting the initial hordes of monsters. All of them would grow up strong and full of power.

Most of the workers had barely treated Alex and his ilk better than animals to be watered and fed. Kind faces and words were rare, and now that he had some perspective, Alex realized that those rare few were the real heroes.

He remembered skinny workers sneaking him treats, undoubtedly trying their best. Their faces or names were long gone from his mind. The haphazardly thrown together lessons in math, or that time an older girl taught Alex and other hungry kids their letters. It was shit, but there was kindness hidden in the muck.

As he approached his teens, he’d been moved to a new orphanage, and then it got bad. Stolen blankets in the dead of night was the least of his concerns. Kids learned their bite. So did he.

And then, Alex had taken to the streets at eleven.

At least I think I was 11…

He wasn’t sure. It wasn’t as if he’d even celebrated his birthday.

Not once did I have a birthday in an apartment.

Looking over the city and apartments, Alex felt anger course through him. He was angry his parents were gone and he that didn’t remember them save for the black curls of his mother. That his parents hadn’t been strong enough to survive for him. Then he was angry that he thought of them that way. He was angry at Nino and Nina for keeping their secret. While he’d never opened up to them fully, surely they understood how alone he felt. Everyone had lost someone or everyone since the System came. They knew his origins and hadn’t said a thing.

They used me.

His parents were gone, and apparently, his last relative was psychotic and powerful. If Nina was to believed, and Alex did believe her, despite their previous lies, she had created the cursed bracelet. She was also helping some mysterious Dungeon Boss in the north. Nina’s sister, and his own grandmother, Fatima.

Do I look like her? And is she my mom’s mom, or my dad’s mom? Alex wondered.

“They didn’t even tell me that.” He practically drooled out, finally realizing what he was doing.

He was crying, alone at the top of the CN Tower.

What he felt most of all was alone. The first sob hit his throat, and Alex felt his body began to shake as all his hurt prepared to rush out.

But before it all came, something interrupted him.

“Meow.” The recognizable call startled Alex.

He spun around in the direction of the call and spotted its source.

“Emilio?” Alex gurgled out with tears beginning to stream.

Emilio floated as if lounging on a pillow of air with the last vestiges of the sunset illuminating him from behind, just staring at Alex with his bright green eyes. That wasn’t all Alex saw. His cat had brought along a catch by the hook of a nail.

“Emilio!?” He asked again, bubbling snot. “What are you doing here? Who the hell is that?!”

“Meow.” Emilio answered and dropped the person from his claw.

Panic flared though Alex for a moment as the body dropped.

THUNK

Relief replaced his panic as the man Emilio had brought crumpled onto the roof from the short drop. The man was fat and blonde, and Alex spotted the rise and fall of his belly from breathing. Thankfully, Emilio brought a passed-out person and not a dead body.

“Is that Pete!?” Alex yelped as recognition pinged him. “You brought Pimple Pe—"

Emilio interrupted his question with a tackle that sent Alex sprawling onto his back. He wasn’t afraid that he’d fall off the CN Tower. There was even a gentle cushion of energy as his back met the concrete roof.

Emilio attacked Alex’s with face-rubs, and Alex hugged back his cat, allowing tears to flow and be licked off.

I’m not alone, he thought as he cried into Emilio’s fur.
