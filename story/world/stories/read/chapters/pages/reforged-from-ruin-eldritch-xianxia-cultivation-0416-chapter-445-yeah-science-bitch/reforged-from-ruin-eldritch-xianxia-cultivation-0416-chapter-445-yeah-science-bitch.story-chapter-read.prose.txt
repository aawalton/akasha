While most Raikas are hard at work learning what they can from each other and developing new techniques and applications, a few Raikas got a different mission.

They’re spreading.

Splitting herself into this many selves is interesting. It wouldn’t have been possible at any other point in her life, or even in the original Raika’s life, but with her sense of identity so diluted and so many safeguards removed, it’s become practical, albeit risky. Keeping themselves distinct allows them to avoid the kind of ontological destruction brought about by the Man Who Is Not There, but it also means they can have differences of opinion, different sets of information, even conflict with each other, and she’s Raika, so they can’t exactly just vote and have everyone agree with an outcome. Most of the ones who voted against will try to just do it anyways, or at the very least get pushy about it.

The distance kind of helps with that, actually. While most Raikas are all clustered together back in their primary body / node, all working on their own projects, bumping into each other or debating what to do and what not to do, the ones sent outside are a bit more independent. It gives some room for thought, and helps quite a bit with getting some peace of mind in which to meditate.

She doesn’t think that the original Raika meditated very often, but she did do it sometimes, and repetitive tasks can be a very good outlet for that.

That repetitive tasks being sneakily terraforming and exploring part of the world doesn’t change that.

A mindless spread across the landscape would be stupid. Easy to track back to the source, easy to bomb out of existence, easy to prepare a defense against. Plus, spreading her biomass all over the entirety of the Jiang’hu would be exhausting, and a colossal waste of energy even for her levels of power generation.

Her ability to warp Space subverts the need for it anyway.

From a patch of land that can be as small as a drop of blood, she can project herself outward. A little bit of blood splatter over a few miles of terrain gives her hundreds of directions to move in and anchors to launch further droplets from, and if she feels the need, she can generate the muscles required to pull Space into a different shape. Whether that means throwing blood out farther, making little pockets where she actually can start building things out of clear view, or find ways to hide any trace of her presence even further, there’s never not a use for a bit of spatial warping.

She’s making sure not to use it all the time, though. She knows that some of the other Raikas are using it more and in distinct ways, but that’s not her priority. Some of them are actively trying to draw attention away from where their main body is currently located, others testing to see how visible the spatial warping is to potential enemies. So far, no one’s been outright erased, but the Republic of Morae is huge, even if it’s not much compared to the Empire.

She thinks about that as she sends out another few droplets of herself, wondering.

With her spatial warping, if she were human-scale, she could probably go… a very long way in a day. A very long way. Someone specialized in movement or transportation likely could outpace her by an order of magnitude, though, even if she mastered her Daos enough to be able to use Space itself to “walk”. Even with all that potential speed, based on what she remembers of maps and of her travels, it would still probably take at least a few days to cross from one side of the Republic of Morae’s borders to the other.

Assuming moving in a straight line, of course, without being impeded by sects. So, realistically, maybe a week, even for the fastest movements she can picture outside of pre-prepared teleportation.

And the Republic of Morae is smaller than the Empire. Probably a few times over. Oh, she has the vague feeling that it would be a sizable chunk of said Empire, sure, but half? No. A quarter? Doubtful. An eighth? Eh, still feeling a bit off, but close.

Where that very unscientific impression is coming from, she’s not sure. Could be wrong. Then again, considering that most of the original Raika’s long term memories are still in “kill-you-if-you-think-about-them” storage, she’s learned to trust her subconscious. Bigger than an eighth, smaller than a quarter.

And it would take the fastest possible higher realm being she can think of about a week to move through it.

So multiplying that by, say, eight-ish? Nine, maybe? Two months or longer to travel across the Empire in a straight line, on the assumption that the traveler can, say, literally cross from one horizon to the next by stepping, and can move at speeds a few times faster than sound.

That’s big.

It feels… weirdly big.

How much space is that? How many different places, how many people? Billions, sure, the Empire’s admitted as much, but how many billions? And where? And how can they possibly have much to do with each other, without access to grander powers or technologies?

And how can the Empire rule over all of it at once?

Sure, there’s teleportation, but there’s no way they’ve managed to build so many teleportation arrays across so much land, even in three thousand years, never mind maintaining them, that they can be everywhere at once. There’s no way to have enough soldiers to patrol all of it at once. There’s absolutely no way to distribute booklets and civil servants over all of that, even over two or three thousand years.

Then again, the heights of cultivation are strange. She’s probably exaggerating there being no way at all.

But it feels off.

Everything inside the Wall is Empire.

But who made the Wall? And how? The distance to completely encircle the Empire is larger than the distance needed to cross straight through it. She might not know the math to figure out how much, but she knows that part just fine. She usually does that sort of math by feel anyways. She’s pretty sure the original Raika has/had no idea how it was built or how long it took, and everyone talks about it like it was just there one day, a fact of life. Did it all go up at once? Is that the sort of power someone in the Titan Realm can achieve, wrapping a landscape almost ten times the size of the Republic of Morae, a nation already larger than some of the shifting plates within the earth, in a single action?

Maybe.

But as an exponentially replicating, exponentially growing, exponentially spreading entity herself, Raika doubts it.

It feels off.

It’s too much space. Too much distance. Too broad as an idea, for it to be held together by something so sensible as trains and messengers that cultivate just to travel from one place to another. Hells, how would information get anywhere, transferring from one person to another? It would take years to reach the first ring.

And then there’s the teleportation arrays. Can they have indefinite distance? She’s not sure. Space matters, it has weight to it. It takes her more energy to pull one place to another if they’re further apart, even if it is, technically, still the same Space as before she pulled at all. Even using tricks of perspective, there’s only so much that that’ll work before the cost of maintaining arrays that powerful across that much territory stops making sense.

She splatters in a rain of crimson droplets that trickle over a patch of forest. Choosing the one that made it the furthest, she reabsorbs all the others, their presence both where they landed and also still in her body simultaneously.

One singular crimson raindrop forms enough muscle tissue to pull a palm-sized bit of Space together, hiding itself in a fold. A few seconds later, it’s grown organic tubing, pressurized with Supreme Body Technique: Compression to hold a tremendous amount of force.

And then more blood goes flying, spreading her further.

Each time she diverges too far from a previous path, she forms another Raika, and they part ways, heading in two different directions.

She’s not going anywhere too specific. Other Raikas are pursuing vaguely-sensed directions towards where other parts of her might be, but she’s just spreading out, using it as an opportunity to meditate and think.

The Empire doesn’t make sense. Sure, fair enough. Cultivation can do a lot, especially modified by perspectives and madness, as can other powers she can kind-of recall existing.

…

How solid is dirt?

It’s not that solid. And if you put enough of it together, it’s forced to compress from all the weight. If she dug down, would she eventually find dirt so compressed that it just can’t be dug through anymore? And how far down would she have to go? Not more than a few miles, surely.

She lets another Raika take over her direction and splits herself off (or maybe she generates another Raika to split off while keeping her expansion consistent, it’s hard to tell). While her Dao of Space usually depends on line-of-sight, it’s technically tied to her awareness in general.

A cluster of eardrums trapped inside a body like a spider and a snake and a mole at once hits the ground and starts digging.

Time passes.

More time passes.

More time passes.

Hours at least. A day? It doesn’t matter. She feels other things outside herself, other Raikas experiencing emotions and reactions to things, but she doesn’t pay attention. She trusts them/herself to handle what needs to be done, and to kill/reabsorb her current self if the resources are truly needed.

The dirt does compress. It compresses at its normal pace, the pace consistent with its materials and weight.

Then it doesn’t.

She “hears” the ground a few hundred feet away, pulls herself to that Space using her Flesh, and then digs deeper, repeating the process. Over and over.

She can feel the distance from her current point to her original start, even if she’s also still technically “there”, occupying multiple points in Space at once through that weird trick she has. Something to do with directions or dimensions, she thinks.

She’s far down.

She is very far down.

The dirt should all be stone. The stone should be some other thing. There’s enough weight directly atop the material she’s in to crush flesh into carbon crystals, then crush that into something else. An impossible amount of weight, more than every mountain in the Jiang’hu dropping down on one point all at once.

But the dirt is still dirt.

That doesn’t make sense.

And the sky is full of holes.

How? Why?

The attention of higher beings, maybe? Is something deciding that the world should be like this, and bears enough power to simply make it so?

But if that’s the case, how could she even be able to tell anything was wrong? She’s not particularly sane, but she’s not divorced from reality entirely. Why would it feel wrong? Why would the rules that things operate by just not apply in some places, in some ways?

Does it matter? There’s other shit to do. Enemies to fight, pieces of herself being found, safeguards to remove, an identity to build/rebuild. There’s things to learn and people to help and things that need dying and ideas to try out. Why is she fixated on this?

It doesn’t feel like meditation anymore.

Something’s wrong with the world.

Maybe it can only be noticed from a large enough scale. Maybe it can only be felt by someone whose conscious thoughts are so closely tied to every impulse and instinct, partially untethered from ego.

It’s strange. It’s off. It’s wrong.

It shouldn’t work like this.

She’s kept digging.

She is very far down. She’s moving in a straight line, a few hundred meters every other second, and she hasn’t stopped for an entire day.

It’s still dirt and rock.

Different types, yes. Different pressures, yes. It is changing, bit by bit.

But it’s changing too slowly.

The pressure should be higher, the consequences of said pressure much more intense. There shouldn’t be miles upon miles of overlapping kinds of soil and stone before it experiences more profound changes.

She changes tactics. She hunts for a cave.

With her echolocation, she finds one in minutes. She travels upwards to it, stopping her journey directly downwards to take a diagonal trip up-

It’s deeper.

She feels nausea. An urge to vomit and recoil and scrape whatever’s just touched her off her skin.

The cave she just went up to get to is deeper than the point she was at before.

Of course it’s deeper. It’s a cave. It’s a cave at the center of the world, miles below the surface, of course it’s completely alien to the soil above-

But she was just digging below it.

Something’s wrong. Things don’t make sense.

The world is too big.

She’s not sure what that means.

As the rest of Raika continues ever forward, one particular Raika starts setting up some new areas in which to generate Qi. If she’s going to make more of herself enough to poke at this, she might as well not overtax the main node. She’s pretty sure that whatever she’s figuring out, it’s going to be an expensive line of thought.
