The vast majority of the three dozen skeleton troops rushed past us like we were part of the scenery, their boots thundering on concrete as they rushed toward some threat they clearly considered more important than the five random people standing out in the middle of a hallway.

Still, as empty as those skulls were, a handful managed to notice. A half dozen of the ones at the rear broke formation and turned our way, their rifles coming up after only a half-second’s hesitation.

They were a half second too late.

Richard’s hand came up and I almost felt sorry for the poor fuckers as his ability activated. The skelly grunts just collapsed mid-stride, their bones falling along with their combat armor, scattering across the floor like someone had cut their puppet strings.

“Well,” Richard said casually, as though he’d just crushed a cockroach. “That's bloody new. Usually they're all over us like a rash.”

“Anyone want to explain what that was about?” I asked, still staring at the empty corridor where the better part of a platoon had just ignored us. “Because I'm pretty sure we're supposed to be the priority targets here.”

Yashas holstered his rifle, and I recognized that look on his face. The one that said he knew something we didn't.

“We are not the only Champions in the area,” he said simply.

My stomach dropped. “Does that mean what I think it means?”

“Indeed. I just picked up Eskil and Ohana on the surface, heading into the compound.” He gestured vaguely upward. “They are, it appears, using a much more direct approach.”

Of course they were. Subtle wasn't exactly Eskil's strong suit. The guy probably just kicked down the front gate and started swinging, machine guns be damned.

“Grace's Champion?” Aerion asked, raising an eyebrow. She knew how I felt about the giant Viking asshole, but Ohana was a complete unknown to both of us.

“We met briefly during our introduction parades,” Yashas said, his expression softening with something that looked like fondness, but I could’ve been mistaken. “She lives up to her station. One of the most elegant beings I have ever witnessed. Like a porcelain doll come to life.” He paused. “Her composure is unshakeable. Even in our short time together, she impressed me greatly.”

I raised an eyebrow at that. The General didn't hand out compliments unless he meant them. It made me want to meet this Ohana. Anyone who could make our stoic general wax poetic had to be something special.

I somehow felt I’d get my wish before long.

An explosion cut off my thoughts, the shockwave traveling down through the complex and shaking dust from the ceiling. The lights flickered, and somewhere in the distance, metal groaned, making me wonder what the hell could’ve caused that.

A missile, probably. No sane army would drop one on their own compound, but these were animated skeletons who’d proven they didn’t give a damn about friendly fire.

I waited for the rumbling to stop. “Looks like they're running into about as much resistance as we thought they would.”

“Indeed,” Yashas agreed. “Which means we will likely be on our own for some time. Unless we decide to wait for them to arrive?”

I considered the option. Waiting meant sitting still in a compound that was now fully aware it had intruders. Every second we delayed gave the enemy time to organize, to set up ambushes and trap us in an unending horde of monsters and weaponry.

On the other hand, having a ten-foot Viking and Grace’s Champion as backup sounded pretty damned appealing.

I ultimately shook my head. “It’s too dangerous to wait. We should keep moving while they're distracted. What do you think, General?”

“I concur,” Yashas replied, giving me an astute look, as though he were consulting the opinion of a military strategist and not a Champion who got most of his knowledge from a computer screen. “This is our best opportunity.”

“Right. Formation time, then.” I looked at our resident general, who gestured back to me.

“You’ve proven to possess an uncanny knack for this sort of thing. Please, I would like to hear your opinion.

“Alright then,” I muttered, my brain already working through optimal party positioning. “Arianna and Aerion? Why don’t you two take point? Aer, summon Senara. Cosmo knows we’ll need the extra firepower,” I added under my breath.

Aerion closed her eyes briefly, and the corridor suddenly felt rather cramped as her spirit companion materialized. Senara was the size of a small horse, and had the force to match. The winged spirit dog nuzzled her head against Aerion, nearly pushing over the diminutive girl with the force, though by her giggles, I got the impression she didn’t mind.

“Richard, you're in the middle with me,” I continued, already changing into my Vigor armor. Push comes to shove, I can be your human meat shield. “Yashas will bring up the rear.”

I saw Richard open his mouth to protest—probably about to make some joke about being protected like a precious jewel—so I cut him off.

“I know I'm built like a tank in this Vigor suit, but my homing bullets make me better suited for mid-range combat. This way I can shield both you and Yashas when the bullets start flying.” I adjusted my armor's settings, feeling the familiar surge of enhanced constitution.

“And they will fly,” Arianna stated matter-of-factly, her Brazilian accent somehow thicker than it was before. I’d noticed that earlier, too. She seemed to get that way before a fight. Like she was suppressing her accent normally for our benefit.

“What do you think?” I asked, turning to our resident general, who’d been giving me a wry smile as I spoke.

“I think,” he said, his smile widening, “that had I not known you, I’d have assumed you were a strategist in a past life.”

We fell into formation like we'd been doing this for years, despite this being only our second real outing together, and damn if it didn't feel good.

Arianna led the way, looking like a walking fortress in her heavy plate armor. Aerion moved beside her with that predatory grace that made my heart do stupid things, her Mythril blade ready to screw up some skeleton’s day. And that was without Reave, which she hadn’t activated to conserve Essence.

Senara padded on her other side, and the spirit animal constantly sniffed the air, somehow managing to sense danger a split second before Yashas could call it out.

Having one radar was good. Having two was even better. Especially one with teeth that could rip apart a bear.

In hindsight, the skeleton patrol never stood a chance.

We rounded a corner into a junction, coming up behind a formation of 8 soldiers. We knew there would be eight and that they’d be looking the other way because Yashas’ awareness got more detailed the closer we got, and at this point I was convinced nothing could sneak up on the man as long as he was awake.

Arianna didn't slow down. She just lowered her shoulder and charged, her Vigor-enhanced strength turning her into a battering ram. Even a flesh-and-blood human would’ve crumpled under that onslaught, but the skeletons, lacking tendons and ligaments keeping their parts together, exploded in a shower of bones that clattered against the walls.

Aerion and Senara were right behind her, and I was reminded why watching them work together was as beautiful as it was terrifying. Aerion's blade took two heads in a single fluid strike that looked effortless but I knew required insane precision and timing. Meanwhile, Senara's jaws clamped around a third skeleton's spine, shaking it like a chew toy before tossing the pieces aside, evidently unhappy with the taste.

Richard caught the two that tried to flank around Arianna, and I saw their bones just lose cohesion mid-stride. His ability was getting stronger—he used to struggle with undead, but now he could drop skeletons almost as easily as flesh-and-blood enemies. Still, it cost him more Essence, which was why we had Aerion and Arianna taking out as many as they could. The more we conserved his Essence, the more power we’d have against whatever came later.

As people loved to say, this wasn’t a sprint. It was a marathon.

I handled the last pair with my shards, the homing projectiles curving through the air to punch through their skulls before they could get a shot off. The [Seeking] ability was proving its worth. I barely had to aim anymore, just pointing in the general direction and the souls embedded within did the rest.

My bullets would’ve been even more lethal, but only against fleshy targets. Unfortunately, they tended to pass right through the skeletons without doing any damage whatsoever, and even when they broke a bone, it was just a single bone and not a lethal wound. For some reason putting a bullet through their skull didn’t faze them like decapitating did.

There was probably some magical reason behind that, but I’d probably never know.

The whole engagement was over in five seconds.

“Damn,” I muttered, recalling my shards to my inventory. “Not bad at all.”

“Do not get cocky,” Yashas warned from his position behind me, though even he sounded a bit impressed. “Overconfidence has killed more warriors than enemy blades ever will.”

Which was fair, but still. It was hard not to feel good at having the advantage for once.

We fought our way down another level, then another, and each fight was like watching a perfectly choreographed fight scene in a movie. Arianna would break their formation with her charge, Aerion and Senara would exploit the chaos, Richard and I would clean up the rest while Yashas positioned us to be in an unfair position before the fight even began, calling out enemy presences well before they ever became a threat.

For example, when a squad of skeletons tried to set up a machine gun emplacement at a stairwell, clearly planning to turn it into a kill zone.

Unfortunately for them, Yashas had seen them, and we intercepted them right as they were focused on deploying the base.

They didn’t notice Senara until she was on top of them, and by then, it was too late.

The spirit dog launched herself over Aerion’s head—which was possibly only because of Aerion’s stature—and crashed into the emplacement like a furry missile. The equipment scattered in every direction.

Aerion was there an instant later, her sword work turning the two skeletons into bone fragments, and by the time I got there, there was nothing left to shoot.

“We can only hope things continue so well,” Arianna said, idly kicking the helmet of a downed skeleton.

“I can guarantee it will not, for we aim for the heart of their operation,” Yashas said.

By the time we reached the next landing, we’d somehow managed to hone our tactics even further, improving with each group of skeletons we encountered. Arianna downed two while Aerion took out the third, leaving none for the rest of us.

We didn't even slow down.

We descended two more floors like that, a well-oiled machine of destruction that turned skeleton patrols into scrap in seconds. It helped, of course, that the enemy was scattered and scrambling to respond to whatever chaos Eskil and Ohana were causing topside. Every group of skeletons we encountered was caught off-guard and completely outmatched. We hadn’t even seen a single one of the eldritch brain commanders yet, and I guessed they’d all been sent topside.

Despite all odds, we’d made it through the zombie infested subway. We’d infiltrated deep into their compound, and for the first time since entering this nightmare dungeon, I felt like we might actually pull this off.

Which I knew, of course, was the exact thought I shouldn’t have, because our luck ended the moment we reached the reactor room.

The corridor ended at massive reinforced doors, the kind you'd see on a bank vault or a nuclear bunker. Yashas motioned for us to halt, which we had to do anyway since that door wasn’t going anywhere fast.

“I sense nothing coming from within,” Yashas said, looking more worried than I’d ever seen him.

“That’s… a good thing, right?” I replied. Of course, even if they didn’t have an army in there, who knew what sorts of automated defenses they’d have guarding their most precious asset?

“Yes,” he said. “Yes, it should be. However, it feels as though my senses are slipping off whatever is inside. Like chasing a shadow you can never catch. I’ve never experienced this before.”

Well that was weird. I exchanged glances with Aerion, who looked just as worried.

“You thinking what I’m thinking?”

“Perhaps,” she said, nodding.

“And what might that be?” Arianna asked.

“If I were a wagering man?” Richard said. “I’d bet the dungeon guardian’s in there.”

“It’d make sense why they’d leave it unguarded. Every Dungeon so far has had one. Trials too.”

They would, wouldn’t they? I thought darkly. Everyone said Trials were designed to mimic dungeons to prepare the world for the dungeons. To let them train in a similar environment, just with a few more guardrails like Sanctuary rooms and early bailout points.

I’d believed that too, but now that I knew better, there was a much simpler explanation, wasn’t there?

Why wouldn’t they be similar when they were made by the same beings? That part made sense. What didn’t was the why. Why even make the Trials in the first place? Especially if they were worried about the ‘cost’?

“That would be unfortunate,” Yashas replied, pacing around with a frown on his face. “Though if this is true, I should have sensed it.”

That was a good point.

“It could be something else,” I admitted. “But regardless, the clock’s ticking, and this is why we’re here. Ambush or not, it feels to me like we gotta spring that thing. Maybe we take a page out of Eskil’s playbook.”

“You’d have us storm through the door?” Richard asked in alarm, and I grinned.

“No, I’d have us storm through the door after bombing the place.”
