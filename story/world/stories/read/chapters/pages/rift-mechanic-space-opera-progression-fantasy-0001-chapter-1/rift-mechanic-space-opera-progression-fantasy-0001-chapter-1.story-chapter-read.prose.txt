Chapter 1

“Come on, we’re going to be late,” Marcus cried out, as he began running and straightening out his crisp red school uniform denoting him as a magi, part of the top 15% of the world by birth.

“I’m already ahead of you,” Ava called out, her voice echoing from down the street. Marcus who was still looking at his family’s mansion door,  turned to see Ava already standing, well floating technically at the far end of the street. Seeing her just floating there, Marcus shook his head angrily as he realized part of why he was now late was that he was looking for Ava, who had, apparently, already left.

Realizing his mistake, Marcus put his head down and began running towards the free floating Ava. Technically he could use his powers to try and fly to Ava, but that was something he instantly put a stop to, as he knew it was exactly what she would want. His flight would make it so Ava could fly higher and faster to keep up with him.  As it was, she was bound to the standard one foot of clearance from the ground, a feat she took every advantage of. The fact that she could just hover stationary in the air, without any aid, showed just how much she had practiced this very act. Seeing her floating without having to constantly regain her balance just caused a pang of annoyance to build itself inside Marcus, as he knew he too could be at the same level of competency, had he spent more time working on his own craft.

In a way, it should have been embarrassing to have a servant be more powerful and capable than he was, and for a while he was resentful of Ava as she seemed to excel at everything she did. It wasn’t until a particularly memorable conversation with his father, Pyrus Aurillius, that he began to see things differently.

“You claim that having a so-called attendant that is better than you, is demeaning. And your suggestion to me is to punish her competency,” Marcus’s father began. Marcus, being only six at the time, nodded in agreement. He was getting picked on by the other magi in his class, for the fact that his attendant could already fly, while he himself was having a hard time running. “Since this is your first time, I will correct you without punishment. But should you ever come to me again with complaints about how well your attendant is compared to you. Know that I will punish you, with the only way I know how. See I will not punish my staff for exceeding the expectations that society has thrust upon them, that is no way to lead at all. Rather, I will punish you and force you to work twice as hard as your attendant does to catch up to her, and then surpass her, so you no longer have these feelings of inadequacy.”

That was it, the one-time argument they had about Ava. Well, they had multiple arguments about Ava, mostly it was Marcus saying he didn’t need her to be assigned to the same elite classrooms that he was. It wasn’t until later, when his father would just laugh at his audacious statement that the truth of the matter hit him. A truth that his father then reinforced with the comment, “do you still think that I have any input in where your attendant gets placed? Have you not seen how she works harder than you and any of your peers? Rather than wondering who is lifting her up, try instead to see the people that push her down. Though realize that despite the best attempt of others to push her down, she still rises. My dear Marcus, rather than trying to see if I bring her up to your level. Ask yourself if I might in fact have to pull strings to keep you within her trajectory instead?”

After that, Marcus found the ledgers. What he had originally thought of as ways for his father to bribe the school to keep Ava in his class was wrong. For rather than bringing her up to his grade, he was being brought up to her grade and ability. That was the first day Marcus realized the truth of the matter. His family servant, a girl that had been like a thorn in his side ever since he was young, was exceptional Not only was she exceptional, but she made the mistake of being seen as better than him, even though being seen like this would mean she had to work twice as hard to overcome his latent Magi status.

Just watching her now, it was clear why she excelled as she always took the hard way to train. While others might be content to just stand at a corner and wait for their master to arrive, she hovered. A feat that most of her kind and below could only dream about, even Marcus himself still had a hard time with control. He could finally move and fly about for his classes, but to hover, the ability to just choose a fixed point in space and linger, that was beyond him. Looking at her, it was clear that she was one of the best. Had she been born a Magi, well that was a different question entirely, for had she been born a Magi then she would have undoubtedly been Marcus’s first bridal candidate. As is, she would have to be relegated to the role of first concubine, a status neither Marcus nor Ava would ever accept. Marcus wouldn’t accept the role as it would mean that he would effectively have to cheat on the one person he considered to be a true friend in this life. While Ava would never accept such a role as she would be more likely to punch any bridal candidates, over accepting their authority over her as well. No, as was becoming all too familiar, Marcus realized that his time with Ava was coming to a close. She would be eighteen soon, and would be considered her own adult, at which time she could create her own familial contract with his Aurillius family, or what was most likely be the case, her getting sucked up into one of the more prominent families in the city.

These thoughts and more ran through Marcus’s head as he crossed the street to meet up with Ava who was still casually floating, the way a ghost might at a street corner, if said ghost had decided to haunt a particular light post out of spite. As Marcus approached, he could see the all too familiar clothing of a standard caste family coming toward them. A mother pushing her child in a stroller, both wearing their government appointed bare brown linen. As Marcus expected, the child in the stroller was instantly drawn to the floating Ava.

“Pet!” The child cried out in excitement. Hearing this, Marcus could only quicken his stride as he ran forward, knowing that this would not go well.

“What?” Ava asked, turning to face the mother and her child, a look of confusion on her otherwise angelic features.

“Pet, pet!” the boy continued to cry out excitedly, as he pointed right at Ava. It took Ava a second to look down at her chest, at the area where the boy was pointing, to realize what he saw. There on her chest were the three badges that explained her currently known capabilities.

Looking down, Ava first saw her navy school uniform, then she saw the three unmistakable letters of: PET.

“Oh, P.E.T., yep that does spell pet, you are correct,” Ava began, her voice switching to her fun loving tone that told Marcus he was not going to like anything that happened next. Then as if he predicted the future, he got to experience in real time the embarrassment that he hoped to avoid, “I am a pet, his actually.”

As Ava concluded her statement, she then pointed up at Marcus who was only now arriving, a bit out of breath and equally embarrassed by her statement.

“You are not a pet,” Marcus stammered out, though he could already see the faint laugh lines appearing on Ava’s face, letting him know that she was loving every second of his discomfort. “That is P.E.T. showing that she is a Psychic, who can both interact with and absorb every type of magical waste, and that she has the second form of Telekinesis, the internal form, which would make her place the ‘T’ to the right of her tilted ‘E’, rather than before if she could just manipulate objects externally from her body,”

“Externally?” The boy asked, the word clearly being something he didn’t know or could comprehend. Hearing Marcus trying to talk to the little kid and failing miserably, Ava swooped in closer and pointed at the letters on her chest.

“This ‘T’ here means I can fly, if it was between the ‘P’ and the ‘E’ that would mean I could make you fly,” Ava explained in a way the kid could understand.

“I want to fly,” the boy cried out excitedly, picking up the one part of that statement that truly interested him.

“And you will, just train really hard and practice on your exams and you will do just fine,” Ava declared. Before the boy could take up any more of their time, she gave a quick head tilt to Marcus who just nodded, knowing that she wanted to continue going to school. At the gesture, Marcus began walking forward, knowing that he was the slower of the two and that Ava would easily catch up. “Well, if you will excuse me, the master is moving, which means I need to keep up.”

Ava then spun about and proceeded to glide down the street towards Marcus who was already shaking his head at being labeled as the villain in this scenario. The moment she pulled up next to him, he let out his frustration.

“You know, I don’t always have to be the bad guy, right?” Marcus began, before continuing with his thought, “you can just tell people that you need to get to class.”

“I know, but blaming it on you, the big, scary mage is a lot easier,” Ava declared, reaching out to pull at the red fabric of Marcus’s uniform to emphasize that he was wearing the colors of the elites. The colors that everyone was taught to respect and fear from an early age.

“You know, you might be more believable if you showed any form of respect towards any mage in our class,” Marcus shot back, feeling somewhat relieved that the school was already within view.

“If they make a mage worth respecting, then I will respect them. Until then, realize that this is my world, and you all are the side characters in it,” Ava declared.

“Your world? You’re delusional. You are a second-class citizen who is at most used as a warning for other future students of what they could aspire to be, if they weren’t born with the genetic predisposition to be a mage,” Marcus replied, letting a bit of his frustration show as he loved Ava, he truly did, but he did not want to see her get in trouble for her heretical viewpoints. Especially, when said heretical viewpoints would come directly back on Marcus and his family.

“You know, I might be a supposed second-class citizen, but realize that even all powerful mages need us to clear out your corruption levels. While you might hate us, you still need us, and as long as that happens, we can be equals,” Ava declared. As she spoke, Marcus felt his heart racing, these were some of the same thoughts that he himself had, over many a conversation with his father, but suddenly hearing them spoken out in public caused Marcus to instantly look around for people and recording devices that would have picked up said heretical comments.

“You can’t say that,” Marcus hissed, still looking around for anyone that might be watching the duo make their way up the steps to school. As was customary, Marcus walked, while Ava somehow managed to float up the steps at a perfect 45 degrees, making the process look effortless as she moved.

“I just did,” Ava declared, and all but dared Marcus to continue criticizing her now that they were both in the school. Realizing that they were both in the very building that was all but guaranteed to have multiple cameras recording both video and audio of their conversations, Marcus dropped his protests. Still he had to worry about his friend who was just smiling viciously as they entered the scanning equipment.

Marcus being the mage was first to enter, as Ava made sure to take a floating step to the left, and give a faux-courtly bow as she let Marcus go in to get scanned by the detection systems first. There was a flash of light, as the intrusive scans searched his body first for prohibited electronic devices, and then a second scan was made for alien tech. Well not exactly alien technology, but for the radioactive signatures that are endemic to alien tech. Seeing that none of the sensors went off, Marcus was waved through. After which it was Ava’s turn.

“Silvers, you need to step on the ground for the scans to be conducted properly,” the bored security guard Jagir said, speaking to Ava, using her surname as he did every day. Hearing him, Ava just nodded then landed perfectly on the two foot pads indicated, then held her hands up and waited.

“Clear,” Jagir confirmed. With that intrusion over, Marcus saw the moment a smile filled Ava’s face as she bent her knees, before focusing her mind and taking off. Seeing her fly, Marcus could only shake his head, as he knew she would do this all day if she could.

“Isn’t that exhausting?” Phoebe Kirkland asked, pulling up right next to the floating Ava, walking on her open left side as if they were friends. As if their status would somehow allow them to be friends.

Just seeing Phoebe get close to his attendant caused Marcus’s blood to boil as she was the one person who Ava always treated with respect. To make matters worse she was practically perfect, queen of the Mage Duels, and the one everyone in the school knew was the most likely to succeed. What she would succeed at was still to be determined, but it was clear that she would succeed at nearly anything she put her mind to.

“Nope, being awesome comes naturally to me,” Ava responded.

“Seriously though, how do you fly everywhere? It’s exhausting as is with magic, and your using psychic powers to create the same effect has to be enough to cause a headache, right?” Phoebe asked the very question that Marcus himself had both thought and asked numerous times. Hearing the question, Marcus only waited with anticipation as to what her reply would be today, as it always seemed to change.

“See, that is where we are different. Had I wanted an easy life, while in the womb I would have asked for an easy life where I could call forth the energy of the worlds, so that I might call the heavens to bend towards me,” Ava began, her hands gesticulating wildly as she began to fill this role of how she imagined mages acting pompously. Only for her to switch midstream as she dropped her arms and tried to appear meeker, “alas, I was humbler than that. As I said, just give me a perfect mind, body, and soul and keep the heavens right where they are, so that I might scale them at my own pace and time.”

Hearing his attendant clown around like that, Marcus could only shake his head. It was outbursts like this that caused Marcus to lose face with some of his peers, as they often told him to keep his attendant under leash, lest they do it for him. At first, he tried to do just that, to hold her back, but found the process to be both taxing and nearly impossible. Finally, after a long deliberation and a few encouraging words from his father, he finally absolved himself of any responsibility, finally telling the perpetrators to “go ahead and try.”

That was it, those four words were all that was needed to cleanse Marcus’s hands of the issue, and leave Ava up to her own protections. While Marcus didn’t know what happened, the other mages who tried to put her in her place chose a time when they were separated. While he wasn’t there during the actual event Marcus did see the aftermath, particularly as his father was called in to help mediate between the different families.

Ultimately, it was Ava who came to her own defense with the statement, “we can take this to trial, if you want it forever known that your four magical children ganged up on, and then lost to a single middle caste student, also known as Purgers. I’m sure that will do well for your family reputations.”

There was more, but that was the gist of what she said, or at least all that Marcus could recall of her statements at the time. From that day on the four left her alone. Well, they left her alone physically, though it was clear they did still have their own wants to exact revenge. But those four would never be a match for his Ava.

“Come on, it’s time for advanced training,” Phoebe declared, having walked with Ava and Marcus to the designated launching pad for their first class of the day. As she spoke, she was already calling forth the powers of the wind to carry her. Her eyes glowing a magnificent light blue shade right before that same color wrapped up her body in a pristine shine. A second later, her body lifted into the air, her feet kicking out for balance the only sign that this was a struggle for her. Of all the mages in the class, she was by far the smoothest flier, though she could not hold a candle to Ava who just hovered there, holding out her hand welcomingly for Marcus.

Seeing her, Marcus knew that if needed, she could not only fly herself, but do so while carrying Marcus on her back, a task that has sadly had to be performed more than once. Then as if reading his mind, she held out a comforting hand, the same hand that would be able to lift and carry the two of them to the promised practice island if he but took the hand and thereby the easy path to the island. For a moment that hand seemed too tempting to resist, but taking a deep breath Marcus centered himself and called upon the same elemental forces that Phoebe had summoned a second ago, and used them to coat his own body in the strength of the wind, then with a colossal focus of will he forced the wind to grab and push him upward, towards the training grounds so their day could begin.

“Come on, stop taking so long,” Phoebe declared, a note of exhaustion in her voice. Marcus knew this all too well, it was the sound of someone pushing themselves harder than needed, while they waited for someone to catch up. This was the magical equivalent of trying to hold a full conversation while swimming in the middle of the ocean, possible, but easily exhausting if not handled quickly enough.

In a moment Marcus gained his balance, despite being wobbly and having to kick and swing his appendages to gain his balance he did manage to do this much. Then in only a few seconds, he was flying over the open fields to the free floating practice island, knowing that despite how tough this particular task was, it was only the beginning of just how painful today would ultimately be before him.

“You take too long,” Phoebe cried out, pulling ahead and going towards the island, all while Ava just stayed right next to Marcus, flying sideways to make sure she was there to catch him if he fell, like an over attentive lifeguard.

“You’ve got this,” Ava declared excitedly.

“You-like this, way too much,” Marcus finally panted out.

“What’s not to like, I finally get to fly at real height,” Ava declared. Marcus heard the comment, before he realized what he was doing as he chose that exact moment to look down and see the fact that he was in fact flying well over the city. That’s when he lost focus and felt his concentration break ever so slightly. That’s when he realized the difference between swimming in the ocean and flying. In the ocean, you can at least float if you forget to focus on keeping your head above water, when you are in the sky and lose focus, terrible things happen.

Before Marcus realized it, he was plummeting quickly, staring up at Ava who just seemed to be staring at him, waiting for him to make up his mind as to whether he would fly on his own, or not. Seeing her, Marcus knew two things. First, that Ava was not impressed with his efforts and would let him continue to fall until he at least tried to recover. Secondly, Marcus knew that this was going to be an exceptionally long day.
