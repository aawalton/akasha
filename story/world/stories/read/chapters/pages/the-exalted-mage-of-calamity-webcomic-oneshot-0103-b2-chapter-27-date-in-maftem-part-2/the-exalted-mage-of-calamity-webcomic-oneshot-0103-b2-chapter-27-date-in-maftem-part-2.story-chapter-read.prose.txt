They slipped into the edge of the crowd.

At the center stood a thin man in a dark green coat and a tall black hat. He wore a bright smile, white gloves, and had a small table set up beside him. On the table were three cups, a deck of cards, a red scarf, a silver coin, and an empty wooden bowl for tips.

The magician picked up a single vix coin and held it between two fingers. He showed both sides to the crowd, then tossed it lightly into the air and caught it.

“A coin,” he said. “Small, simple, and capable of ruining friendships if thrown onto a gambling table.”

The magician closed his hand around the coin. “Now, watch carefully. If you blink, it will be gone.”

Veronica paid close attention, not blinking, as the man instructed.

The man tapped his fist once. Twice. A third time, and then he opened his hand.

The coin had vanished.

The children gasped while several adults clapped.

Veronica’s gaze sharpened. There had been no mana. Nothing that she could sense. No spell circles. No artifacts that she could detect.

The magician turned his hand over, showing both sides before wiggling his fingers. “A tragedy,” he said. “That single coin was all I had left. Alas, my fortune is gone.” Then he straightened exaggeratedly. “Unless…”

He walked forward and knelt before a young girl holding her father’s hand. He reached behind her ear, and a moment later, two coins appeared between his fingers.

The girl squealed, clapping both hands over her left ear, as if she had suddenly produced vix out of thin air.

Veronica simply stared at the performance.

“Sleight of hand,” Nasha said beside her.

Veronica looked over at her.

“It’s all a performance. It takes skill to do, but you can make a living from it. Performing magic without using magic,” she said.

The two of them moved closer, watching with the rest of the crowd while they ate their food. The magician continued, making small balls disappear beneath three cups. Summoning a familiar from within a black hat. He even made his clothes change colors several times with a swipe of his hand.

It was all things Veronica had seen before, done with magic. But not the way this man did it. No, he wasn’t using magic. And somehow, that made it all the more entertaining and mysterious.

After the magician finished his performance, the crowd scattered slowly, with several children still begging him to do one more trick. Nasha dropped a few coins into his wooden bowl. Veronica stared at it for a moment, then did the same.

The magician looked up at her and smiled. “Thank you, my lady. Always happy to earn the approval of a skeptic.”

It seemed he had heard some of her comments from earlier. “It was… a good performance,” she said slowly.

He tipped his hat to her. “Magic is all about joy and whimsy, m’lady. Real or fake, it’s all the same.”

Joy and whimsy, huh?

Veronica wasn’t sure if that quite applied to her.

Her magic was anything but.

After the short lunch and the magician’s busking performance, Veronica and Nasha continued their date, exploring what the city had to offer.

They passed through the entertainment district for most of the afternoon, drifting between streets filled with music, food, laughter, and the occasional performance so strange that Veronica had to stop and make sure she was seeing it correctly.

At one point, they even entered the goose play. Veronica simply couldn’t let go of her curiosity after seeing the signboard hours earlier. She had only intended to watch for a few minutes, just to understand why the entire play seemed to revolve around a goose. Instead, it took far longer than she expected. So much so that she ended up watching the entire thing.

It was not a serious performance. Not even slightly. The plot involved a noble family fighting over an inheritance, a mysterious prophet, two hidden lovers, a stolen ring, and a goose that somehow knew all of their secrets. The actors were loud and exaggerated. The dialogue was ridiculous. The goose itself was played by a man in a feathered costume who ran across the stage honking whenever anyone lied.

The audience absolutely loved him. And Veronica herself couldn’t look away. There was one moment when a royal duke let slip a lie, and the feathered goose actor rushed in, honking wildly and frightening the duke.

Veronica covered her mouth. She couldn’t tell if that had been in the script or not.

Nasha leaned closer. “Was that a laugh?”

Veronica glanced over at her, but didn’t answer.

That probably told Nasha everything she needed to know.

After the comedy, everyone attending was even given a small goose plush, no larger than her hand.

Continuing on, they left the theater and walked toward a small park parlor. Several rows of benches and tables were laid out around the area, along with stands selling refreshing chilled drinks. The courtyard was shaded by large trees, and children ran from table to table. People sat together, playing games with cards, dice, and tiled boards Veronica had only heard the names of in passing.

Nasha taught Veronica how to play a game called Old Butler. And surprisingly, Veronica was quite good at it.

After a few practice games, a young boy came over, wanting to play. His parents were at the table beside them, watching him go. Veronica couldn’t find the will to refuse.

“Like the games commoners have to offer?” Nasha asked, nudging her in the side with her elbow.

Veronica ignored the taunt. “It’s fun.”

Midway through, a few other children came by, wanting to play against her. And proudly, out of the roughly ten games she played, Veronica won seven.

They left the parlor a couple of hours later and continued roaming the streets. They passed by a painter creating portraits of people walking by. Unfortunately, the painter was already in the middle of someone else’s portrait, so they couldn’t have their own done. Aside from that, there were more musicians playing near a fountain, and a dance performance by a trio of traveling bards.

As the sun began to sink, the entertainment district changed. Soft orange hues covered the streets as the lanterns and street lamps shifted their colors for the darkening sky. Many performers packed up for the day, and the streets filled with the sounds of taverns and cafes, alongside rumbling carriages and moving carts.

By the time they returned to the Maristar Gold Inn, night had begun to settle. The entrance hall was warm, and the smell of cooked meat and wine drifted in from the common area. Several guests still lingered at the tables, talking softly among themselves.

As they passed through, they spotted Gwen at a far table, finishing her meal. In front of her was a bowl of noodles covered in sauce, but beside it, she had also stacked several books.

She noticed them as they came closer.

More specifically, she noticed the two of them holding hands. “Oh,” she said, with a mischievous smirk. “You two are back.”

“So are you,” Nasha said. “Long day?”

Gwen nodded. “I spent most of it reading at one of the smaller libraries.”

“You know, you could have taken today slowly. You didn’t have to spend it studying. I was going to teach you more anyway,” Veronica said.

“No, no, it’s fine. It’s not like I only spent the whole day reading. I did stop by a massage parlor to get some knots out. I also got my nails done and bought some new shampoo. Apparently, Maftem has over a hundred different brands. In Ronswick, there are only around ten. And five of them are mint-scented.”

She looked at Veronica more closely. “I can see you’ve had quite a fun day, Miss Exalted Mage, strongest in the world.”

The evidence of Veronica’s day was all over her. She had the charm from the game parlor, a small star sticker on her cheek from playing with the children, a small goose plush, and even a rainbow scarf that a busker had speed-knitted in under two minutes.

Veronica glanced down at herself briefly. “Well, it was certainly eventful.” She looked over at Nasha and smiled. “Thanks, Nasha.”

Nasha returned the smile with a slight tilt of her head.

“Ew,” Gwen said plainly. She stood, holding her tray of finished food. “I’m going to hit the hay for tonight. I’ll see you two tomorrow.”

“Alright,” Veronica said. “Take it easy.”

The two of them returned to their room as Gwen finished putting away her dishes.

Since they were in a luxury inn, each room had its own bathroom, which was exceedingly convenient.

Veronica took a bath first, and surprisingly, there were no shenanigans.

Veronica came out, wearing a white nightgown the inn had provided. She spotted Nasha on the bed, reading some novel she had bought from a bookstore during their trip.

“Bath’s free. You can go now, Nasha,” Veronica said.

Nasha peeked over. “Looking good, sweetstuff,” she said with a wink. Then she got up and headed to the bathroom herself.

Veronica, meanwhile, had to double-check that she was fully inside, using her enhanced senses. The moment she heard the water splash, she got to work.

Veronica reached into her bag and pulled out a small cloth pouch, placing it gently on the bed. She untied the string, then emptied its contents onto the bedsheet.

A small metal case fell out first, attached to a fine silver chain. It was oval-shaped, with a hinged front and a tiny clasp on the side. The inside was lined with dark velvet, soft enough to keep the main component from scratching against the metal.

Next, she pulled out the item she had kept hidden away for most of the day.

It rested in her palm, faintly gleaming under the lamplight.

Veronica stared at it for a moment.

Then she carefully opened the casing.

It was supposed to be simple. Insert the component. Secure it inside. Close the latch. Make sure it wouldn’t fall out. That was all.

Naturally, it was more irritating than expected.

The interior had been made to hold something similar in size, but not perfectly. She had to shift it once, then twice, pressing carefully along the edges to make sure the casing held without damaging what was inside. The chain slid against the bedsheet as she worked, glinting softly whenever it caught the light.

Veronica frowned.

“Just fit properly,” she muttered.

It shifted inside the case once more. She adjusted it again. A quiet click came from the case as the clasp finally settled into place.

Veronica exhaled.

The bathroom door opened.

Veronica’s head snapped up.

Nasha stepped out a moment later, carefully drying her hair with a towel. She had changed into a red nightgown, loose around her shoulders and soft against her skin. Her damp hair clung to the sides of her face, darker than usual from the water.

She stopped when she saw Veronica sitting stiffly on the bed, both hands hidden behind her back.

Nasha blinked. “What are you doing?”

“Nothing suspicious.”

She chuckled with a raised brow. “That is the most suspicious answer you could have given.”

Veronica looked away. “It’s not suspicious. It’s just…” She trailed off, unsure how to say it, then groaned. “Ahh, here. I don’t really know how to give gifts, so just take this.”

Veronica pulled her hands from behind her back. The necklace hung between her fingers. “I got this for you,” she said.

Nasha’s eyes lowered to it.

For a moment, she said nothing.

The small case glinted beneath the overhanging lights, the chain swaying lightly between Veronica’s hands. Within the casing sat Selene’s Tear, framed carefully so its beauty could be seen.

Nasha stepped closer, looking down at it. “For me?” she asked.

“Yes.” Veronica looked down at the necklace instead of directly at her. It was a bit too embarrassing. “It seemed awkward for you to keep carrying it around in your hand. And leaving it somewhere else felt irresponsible. So I thought this would be better. We can’t have an ancient priceless artifact like it go missing, after all.”

Nasha simply stared at her.

Veronica cleared her throat. “It’s just a regular chain. I was able to stop by a craftsman and ask for some empty necklace cases, then bought this before you noticed I had gone missing. It’s a bit unremarkable, but I’ll probably find a way to enchant it later so it doesn’t look so dull.”

“You… came up with this yourself?”

“Well… I did ask Gwen for a few ideas in the morning before you came back. A necklace was one of them. But I thought of making it hold Selene’s Tear myself.”

She paused for a moment. “I had… a lot of fun today. I didn’t even know people made plays as ridiculous as the goose one we saw,” she said with a small chuckle. “The board games were fun too. I’ve seen village children play them before during my travels, but I never had the chance to learn them myself. People revered me, but they were also wary of me. No one wanted to make a mistake around an exalted mage. But today was… different. I didn’t have to worry about any of that.”

Veronica stared at Selene’s Tear, its surface glinting within the metal casing. “I wasn’t really sure how to repay you. I’ve never given a gift to someone before. Not one like this. So I’m not sure if it’s up to standard.”

Nasha reached out and took the necklace in her hands. She brought it closer to her face, looking at Selene’s Tear resting inside.

Then she moved to the bedside drawer and set it on top.

Veronica frowned. “Do you… not like it?”

Nasha came back in front of her.

Then she pushed Veronica backward onto the bed.

“E-eh? Nasha?” Veronica said, surprised. “Was it that bad—”

“You know,” Nasha said, cutting her off. “I was holding myself back today.”

“What?”

“I promised that I’d let you experience things you never could in your last life. The joys of being a regular person. I didn’t want to make any moves on you in case that made things feel too superficial.”

“Um, Nasha? What are you saying—”

Nasha leaned forward, placing one knee beside Veronica’s thigh. “Today was supposed to be about you. But now…” She leaned even closer, face to face with Veronica, only inches away. “I don’t think I can hold myself back tonight.”

Veronica completely froze. Her heart beat faster than ever. Her mouth felt dry.

And all she could do was gulp.
