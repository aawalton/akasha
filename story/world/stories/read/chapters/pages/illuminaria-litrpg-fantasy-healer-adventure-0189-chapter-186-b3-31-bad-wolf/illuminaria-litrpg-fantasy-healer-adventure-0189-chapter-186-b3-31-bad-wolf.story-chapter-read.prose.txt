Chapter 186 / B3-31: Bad Wolf

As the wildness and the Erlking stared each other down, Joe desperately tried to regain control over the weird amalgamation he was now part of. When Joe fought Sougath for control of his body, he had relied on his Spirit to fight with. This should have been a good thing since that attribute had become his highest score. It was now double what it had been during his battle with the spectral Night Geist.

Unfortunately, the wildness was not an enemy invader; it was part of Joe. It, too, had access to their massive twenty-point Spirit. To make matters worse, Joe had deliberately handed the reins to his primal side, and that wild spirit did not want to hand them back.

No matter how hard he tried to reassert himself, Joe found he was reduced to a spectator in his own body.

He watched the imperious fey-lord bring his bow to bear and draw another arrow from the jeweled quiver. His highly-enhanced nose sniffed the loathsome cold aroma of silver. At the same time, his predatory gaze cut through the gloom even more clearly than [Night Eyes] had a minute before. They tracked the willowy king’s aim, knowing the exact moment the blackened fingers loosed the string.

Folding their body backward, the shapeshifter slipped under the dreadful silvered shot. As they rolled back upright, all Joe could feel was the overwhelming urge to tear the lord into pieces and take its pack for themselves. They cared nothing for some vaguely recalled plan to delay for time. Joe had a nagging feeling there was something very important he was forgetting, but the fury pouring off the wildness made it almost impossible to remember what that was.

In that second, nothing else mattered but destroying the challenger, and Joe had no way to derail that desire. The wildness’s sole focus was bringing about the death of the Erlking.

As the werebeast leapt over the groveling undead minions to charge the dais, a cadre of feathered mages unloaded spells at the bounding form. Arcs of electricity, balls of acid, and wreaths of flames splashed against their feral form. The attacks were merely pathetic magics, barely singeing their regenerative flesh, but the insult of making an attack was not one the beast would dismiss.

Their daunting aura had turned the rest of the rotting pack into cringing submissives. The wolves, wargs, and weasels had their heads pressed to the ground. Even the ursine mummies were cowed by the primal wolf’s domineering presence. Only the insolent scavengers dared to defy them. Turning on their heel, they charged the squawking arcanists, diving among the flock and ripping them apart.

Joe recalled how all of his spells against Sougath had failed during the fight in Vyhne’s tower. He now realized this was not due to a personal trait of the Demon Wolf. It was the nature of werewolves. All of their magical resistances were through the roof. So were their physical resistances. And possibly the mental ones as well. This explained why the wildness was so dismissive of the wolves and martins. Even if one could manage to muster enough bravado to attack them, their claws and arrows would only be able to bruise them. Only the Erlking’s silver arrows and the argent arming sword worn on his belt posed any real threat to the wolf.

The reach of this new body's limbs had massively increased. His wicked claws savaged the beaked buzzards around them. The extra two feet of arms they now possessed added power and momentum to every slashing strike. Their butchery was more akin to swatting flies than it was to martial combat.

Messages from the Hawk spiralled by, telling them of magical damage fully or partially resisted, but these words in the air were unimportant. Only their rage mattered.

As the beast lost itself in its bloodlust, Joe found some room to assert a bit of control. He didn’t attempt to stop them from dismembering the uppity scavengers, but he found he could redirect the amalgam into choosing targets. Joe nudged them to ignore those that were out of commission instead of wasting time rending the buzzard mages into minute, oozing chunks. If he noticed any of the other minions looking to rise from their terrified state, he encouraged the beast to correct that presumption as well.

Soon, he and the wildness were working in tandem. With Joe using [Witch Sight] and [Brawler] based suggestions, they began to make more strategic attacks, dispatching the dead left and right. The loom was still churning out new minions, forcing their intertwined selves to get moving again. They bound back and forth across the massive chamber, only needing to dodge or defend when one of those abhorrent silver arrows sprang off the fey lord’s bow.

Bit by bit, Joe forced his awareness against the wildness, gaining ground in their collective headspace. Unlike his spiritual battle with Sougath, Joe knew they were not actually enemies. He did not have to fashion imaginary walls and weapons; he just needed to assert which of them was boss. Until now, that had never been in question. That is, until Joe made the foolish choice to hand full control over to a wild, primal spirit. It was not in the wildness’s nature to yield.

Joe was pretty sure he could rip control back but that would cause problems of its own. The primal wolf’s furious focus and the enhanced resistances from the [Skinwalker] werewolf transformation were what were keeping them from being overwhelmed by stuns again. He needed to find a middle ground with his wild side.

Bit by bit, Joe took hold of his Spirit and mind while leaving the [Skinwalker] effect completely alone. The wildness was so focused on its slaughter, it barely noted the scales of control tipping in Joe’s favor. The primal beast cared more about mutilating the undead than it did about Joe’s slow creep back into the driver's seat.

Finally, Joe knew he was once more the master of the mix, though it was from the innermost core of who they were. Physically, they were still a werebeast, dodging and jumping around the room, dominating the undead beastlings close to them, completely integrated with the [Hide of the Hunter]. Mentally, the wildness was raging away on the surface of the amalgam’s thoughts. Yet underneath all this, Joe stood in the center uncontested by the wildness. He knew that if he showed any weakness again, his primal side would almost surely try to take full control back again, but as long as Joe was not stopping it from its rampage, the wildness was fine with this arrangement.

Joe kept his will strong as he sent his awareness into himself, looking for the others. He sensed not two but three spirits on and in the bestial body. Yuk was partially riding on his furry pelt, but there were large traces of the bugs inside his torso as well. Taylyn was there too. Her pocket remained intact. Even better, she was somehow getting fresh air. Joe had no idea how this was happening, but figuring that out could wait until things were not so completely insane.

The last presence was absolutely alien to Joe. It was coming from the sash that was now encased by Joe’s new skin. While the sacred item was not outright rejecting what Joe had become, it was definitely judging him … them. The holy relic did not at all approve of this deep infusion of baleful lycanthropy. It was still enhancing his mental defenses, but it clearly was not happy about it.

Something else to worry about later. Much later.

Finding the [Parasitic Bond] still intact, Joe reached out to his allies.

‘Sorry. We kind of lost it there for a second. How are you guys doing?’

‘IT HAS NOT BEEN A SECOND, YOU LUNATIC!,’ snapped Taylyn’s mental shout. ‘It’s been almost three whole minutes! I have had to add power four times already. It is going to break any second! We are going to die!’

“Oh shit! Sorry. I’ll make an opening in the hide, and you can toss it.”

“Bad idea, Joe,’ Yuk interjected. ‘The king controls this space, remember. We need her bomb to fall right at his feet, or he’ll shunt it away. Maybe right onto us. It’s got to be too close for him to banish, and even better would be if he were distracted. We've got enough bugs to set off a diversion. If we time it right, he should dismiss the wrong explosion.”

‘I think I can help too,’ Joe added.

‘We could have had this planned out already,’ seethed the panicked mouse, ‘but you were off growling and biting things. Now we have no time. Everyone else has evacuated. Just get there, fast, and let me out. And MAYBE we live.’

Joe and the wildness pivoted, galloping for the Erlking, the dais, and the loom. It turned out four limbs were faster than two. They lumbered through the enemy like a charging bear. The medallion jumped them around any dense packs of the dead, while a pair of significantly more feral amber paws and brute force punched holes through the sparser lines.

The next hurdle was, what the hell were they going to do when they reached the quickly approaching dais? Joe knew that the pelt would not change shapes the way it had before [Skinwalker] altered its functions. Taylyn was literally inside him. He toyed with the idea of slicing open his own skin, but knew that wouldn’t work. Assuming the pelt was now truly werewolf hide, that meant it could only be cut by silver.

It would come down to timing. The wildness would have to drop [Skinwalker] and fully hand control back over to Joe. That would leave Joe maybe a second or two before he was overwhelmed by stuns again. In that second, they had to distract the Erlking, drop the explosion or explosions, not die or get stunned, and activate the teleport out.

‘Sure! No problem,’ Joe silently groaned.

A brief image of the fiery wolf running in front of Joe flashed from the wildness. It was the first time his feral side had used imagery instead of just raw emotion to communicate. It was telling Joe to let it stay angry and in the forefront of their mind. The wildness would tank the first stun to get through. That would give them a third second to work with.

Joe summoned a [Lucid Juice] from the belt inside his body and tossed it into his elongated maw. Knowing the glass couldn’t cut his gums, he bit down and strained the potion down his throat, spitting out the remaining shards. One more second.

As Joe came within a teleport range of the dais, he bulleted the plan with his cohorts. Straightening back up, the bestial body powering forward on just his legs. He loosed a fearsome growl at the Lord of Spring, getting exactly what he expected: an arrow shot, which he batted away with [Heavy Hand].

They teleported, appearing behind the unloaded lich. Any of the scarabs on Joe’s back let go and tumbled off in an arc around the back side of the platform.

The savage-looking [Casting Claws] latched onto sections of the bow, one on the bottom tip, the other the bow-string. While the dead king was far stronger than the paws, no one was strong enough to make a confident shot with something jiggling the weapon and string about.

The Erlking stashed his bow into a spatial item and drew the elegant blade from his belt. In one fluid motion, the silver sword speared straight for Joe’s chest. The precision of the attack made it impossible to evade. Joe twisted to shield Taylyn and let [Heavy Hand] intercept the strike. He was shocked when the arming sword effortlessly whisked right through his hand, clipping three fingers off his left paw.

‘Vorpal friggin’ blade!’ Joe growled. If he had not seen his hand being bisected, he would not have known it. The edge was that sharp. He was sure pain would follow, but he blocked that thought out. He had to make everything work, or they all died.

Joe summoned the one non-volcanic piece of rock he had in his [Bullet Bag] to his hand.

As he readied these last, final desperate measures, Joe sensed another presence join the party. Joe could feel the [Mark of Death] come alive, gathering scattered moments of time and space. The prophetic seal wouldn’t do the work for them. Yet in this instance, where the world stood on a razor’s edge, where a great change could be brought about, the Thirteenth Omen was willing to lend its cryptic support, nudging reality in their favor.

‘On three! One!’ Joe announced.

The wildness dismissed [Skinwalker]. Joe felt the hide unlatch from his flesh. He willed it to open from his shoulders down.

‘Two. Yuk. Blow!’

With the hide flapping out behind him like a ragged cloak, Joe dashed around to the front of the dais, pitching the small, round field stone at the wrapped, blackened lich. At the same moment, Yuk's curving line of insects detonated.

The team had called it correctly. The erlking reacted instantaneously, waving a hand and banishing the [Bug Bomb] to another layer of existence. They could see the vermin-infused explosion go off, but as if it was being played on a drive-in movie screen instead of in reality with them.

Among the dozens of resisted mind blasts, Joe barely noted,

You have failed to resist the Mournful Thrall’s [Hope Hammer]. [Lucid Juice] has negated the effect.

Your sub-pattern has failed to resist the Mournful Thrall’s [Hope Hammer]. That aspect of you is stupefied.

There would be no more free blocks, unless the Mark decided to step in.

‘THREE!’ he cried to the collective of minds. ‘Tay! SEND IT!’

Out loud, Joe barked the word “Udaan!” His first magic item, Madina Spooner’s old [Slow Stone], something so weak it likely didn’t even register as a threat to the fey lord, activated. As towering as the Erlking's presence was, physically, he was not much bigger than Wen, and the stone worked against mass.

The {Earth} magic, something the fey were notoriously vulnerable to, locked up the once-beauteous king for maybe a second. But that instant was all they needed.

As Joe swung his half-hand towards his throat, a blazing spark of churning brilliance flashed away from the mouseling clinging to his chest.

Karate-chopping the collar shattered the choker, and with a violent wrench, he and his teammates were yanked through space. Not gently. Vexor must have prioritized activation speed for this spell. Though the transition only lasted a fraction of an instant, in that micro-span of time, Joe felt like he had been stretched like taffy and then shot from a cannon.

Everything brightened as the trio appeared in the air over the dark valley. The instantaneous transfer must have also ranked swiftness over distance; they had not gone far. Before the group could even start falling out of the sky, the world erupted into a blinding, deafening, and battering pandemonium.

Your Perillost ward has been expended

Joe’s ears burst for the second time that day. It felt like the entire sky slapped him across his face, breaking his nose and shoving his eyeballs into their sockets. He wrapped himself as tightly as he could around Taylyn and healed as he willed the hide to envelope them once more.

The trio shot through the air like a bullet, crashing through the branches. They smashed to a stop when their luck of missing the trees finally ended.

You have been reduced to 0 Health. [Punching Bag] has activated. You have 10 seconds before you lose consciousness. Cooldown: 12 Hours

As they slid down the rough trunk, Joe repaired everyone using [Wellspring] to spread the healing around. While branches and stones rained from the sky, Joe curled tighter around Tay and the remaining bits of Yuk, sheltering them.

As he cowered in the lee of the tree, he experienced the strongest sense of deja vu. He had witnessed himself in the midst of this titanic explosion. Granted, at the moment, he couldn’t recall where he had seen it. Exhaustion, tension, and several thorough beatings had taken their toll. Somewhat stupefied, Joe wrapped himself tighter around the small guilder against his chest, hoping that after surviving the blast, they would not be ignominiously squashed by a chunk of falling ziggurat.
