Chapter 276

October 20th, 2023 - Three days after the Huntington disaster

“Let me get this straight,” President Terris said. “The church gave me fronto-temporal dementia, so that I would develop temporal aphasia and be unable to communicate properly, together with holes in my memory which the confabulation symptom of the dementia would then fill with made-up stuff I believed as if it were true, to the point of being able to pass a polygraph test. Am I on the right track here?”

The man accompanying him, Michael, waved his hand and the door leading out of the Saint Hernest medical facility opened on its own. The President paused, staring at it.

“Did you just?” he asked. “It’s not an automatic door.”

Michael shrugged. “Is it really the strangest thing you’ve seen these last few days?”

“I suppose it is not,” the President said, then shook his head and continued forward.

“You got your story mostly right,” Michael said. “A mafia Don from Italy, who had also done a deal with a devil that then took over his body, was actually the true culprit behind the whole thing. He manipulated the church, made the old pope give up his seat, put an arrogant and traditionalist new pope in charge, convinced him that magic was heresy, promised the man holy power in exchange for collaboration, and slowly but surely built himself an unassailable position where he was the sole supplier of artifacts the church needed to keep using their magic while they were more and more indebted to him. Eventually, he had a cardinal influence your mind while doing similar things to people in positions of power all around the world. We are finding evidence that he was behind the last several armed conflicts that have arisen ever since the old pope resigned.”

“And this new pope…”

“Owes Unity Corporation big time. Not only has he lost the only supplier of artifacts he needed to keep using the Faith magic, but he also needs us to deal with the aftermath of what Don Casellaro has put in motion.”

They walked toward the black, unmarked car in silence. The President’s gait was steady and even, and his whole body was balanced and strong. He had been rejuvenated by ten years on top of being healed of anything that was wrong with his body and mind, using a low-potency version of something they called the Methusalah drug. He had also been shown things that Unity Corporation was doing or was involved with. It was a way to prepare him for the incoming meeting, he had no doubts about it.

“This is a lot to take in,” the man said. “I feel like I’ve just woken up from a bad dream I can only half remember.”

He looked out of the window as the car slowly drove out of the parking lot and into the road. He pursed his lips, adjusting his suit around his neck. Where once the fat threatened to spill out of the too-small suit, making him look ridiculously like a man trying to hide the signs of a decaying body ruined by bad habits, now his skin was tight and elastic, and there was a slight gap between it and the fabric.

“I don’t know if you can believe it, but the things I remember from before… They make less sense than what your colleague has shown me or what you are telling me now.”

Michael nodded. The President’s eyes kept scanning the far away horizon as they drove past houses and trees, slowly merging into bigger roads, but he also kept an eye on the young man. His face was relaxed and he too looked out at the passing trees and then at the sky, in a strange way that put him at ease. Eventually, he gathered the words and spoke more.

“I felt like every day, I was walking into a new nightmare. I was surrounded by monsters, old men that looked like demons. I kept getting lost among the corridors of the White House, until eventually some people I was sure I had never seen before would come to pick me up and carry me somewhere, always a different place. They spoke to me like I was a little child, and I remember being so angry, so frustrated. They put me in machines, they gave me medicine. They showed me things on screens and technology I did not understand, graphs I pretended to be able to read. They would hand me little notes telling me what to say and to whom, but then I just kept forgetting…

“When people, journalists, asked me questions,” he trailed off, shaking his head. “Most of the time they made no sense to me. I didn’t even know who they were, even those that I had been told were our journalists. I thought they were trying to confuse me, that they were plants from an enemy I thought I was sure was all around me. They wanted to hurt me, to embarrass me, to put me on the spot. And so I told them off, oh how I told them off. Called that guy a moron, even though I had no idea who he was. That other girl, I think, reminded me of a long time lover. Told her she was a whore.”

Beside him, Michael listened to his rambles for close to a whole hour. He was grateful for it. They had assigned him the right person for the job. A young, empathetic man with enough pull in the company to be able to listen to these kind of talks, who asked the right questions and showed the right emotions on his face.

As he lapsed into silence, President Terris reflected on his own past, what of it he could remember. In his youth, he had been nothing short of a monster. He was sure the young man had been informed of it. Thus, the President made sure to tell the tale of the slow decay of his mind, and then the sudden clarity that made him acutely aware of just how low he had fallen. It was a sad sight, even for him.

They finally reached their destination. Where the city of Huntington, West Virginia, once was, now an opaque dome rose into the sky. It was easily more than twenty miles in radius, an enormous sphere, black as the night, that stretched across the whole horizon. On its surface, orange lines drew gigantic hexagons, each as big as a neighborhood. Toward the sky, the dome pierced the clouds and the lines blurred into each other, becoming a haze of orange smeared across the darkness of the barrier of energy.

The road abruptly ended in blackness, disappearing into the solid wall of utter darkness. There was a checkpoint before that, a gathering of hastily thrown together buildings and shipping containers, as well as some better built structures that were new but couldn’t have possibly been built in less than three days, no matter the money spent to make the impossible possible.

Such thoughts swirled in the President’s mind as they left the SUV behind and approached the main building on foot. He noticed that all the guards were nodding at the young man beside him, letting them through without question and with a sparkle in their eyes, as if they had just met a celebrity.

Perhaps they had. He had a sudden flash as he remembered the end of the nightmare that had been his life until three days ago. The images made no sense, strange demons and priests and magic and clones of a strange boy who looked like a girl. A hole in space, an explosion. Him shooting at the monster in a man’s skin and the terror when he realized that all had been for naught. He remembered the face of a young man as he grinned and summoned a terrifying power in the sky. He thought the world would end.

But then again, even though the nightmare had been a bit strange, it had been no more frightening than what his waking life had become in the later years. Except, in the last three days, he had been able to piece together what had happened. A patient man, impossibly tall and with a southern accent, helped him distinguish reality from fiction.

Slowly but surely, the President came to a second, more important realization. These people… They had given him his mind back. Not only that, but they had also healed his body, giving him back a life he had thought he had lost forever. Perhaps they didn’t even grasp the full weight of what they had done, not really. He was losing himself in a strange, terrifying maze that was also his mind. Somewhere deep down he knew that he had perhaps months left to live, and he had been lashing out against the world to make them pay, to make someone pay.

Now he was back. Better than ever. Just like that.

His mind was sharper than ever. His body was healthier than ever.

He owed them, he knew. He owed them everything.

He glanced at the young man leading him toward the buildings. He was smiling. His face was cheerful and relaxed. His tall body was strong.

No, it can’t be.

In the distance, a man was talking to some of the paramilitary soldiers that had filled the area like worker ants. The soldiers nodded at him, and then ran off with determination and purpose on their faces.

You don’t see that often in the military, the President thought. These people believe in what they are doing.

“President Terris!” the man said with a big, fake smile. Back then, such a smile would have confused him, lulled him into believing the man was a friend. Now, he could see the terrifying truth behind it. “Travis Tyrell, CEO of Unity Corporation, a pleasure to finally meet you.”

“The pleasure is mine,” he replied.

He vaguely remembered the man from somewhere, but the man was giving him face by pretending this was their first time meeting.

They shook hands. Despite his bulging muscles, the military haircut and the shirtless vest that made him look like a goon, the man’s handshake was almost perfect. Firm but not to the point of hurting, arrogant but not overreaching, friendly but not eager. This was a dangerous man, President Terris thought immediately. The right hand of the boss.

“I see you have already met the owner of the company, Michael Lexington,” Mr Tyrell said, still beaming his perfect smile. His white teeth caught the sunlight strangely, almost too bright against the backdrop of the dark dome that took up most of the sky.

The President felt his blood freeze. He had, of course, not been told that Michael was actually Mr Lexington, and seeing the frown on the young man’s face he immediately understood whose ploy it had been. It confirmed his early suspicion about who of the two was the real danger.

Then he froze again. His mind suddenly made the connection. The man who had fought against the devil, who had summoned the terrifying power in the sky, the man who had saved him from the destruction of the city, the man from his nightmare… was the same young man in front of him. He and the owner of Unity Corporation were one and the same.

Now it makes sense, all these theatrics are nothing but a farce.

“Please, come inside,” Mr Tyrell said. “I want to introduce you to someone.”

A woman, wearing a white lab coat and thin rectangular glasses, walked toward them. Her gaze was sharp and uninterested as she studied him, and he felt it like a shard of glass tickling his skin, only a small twitch away from drawing blood.

“She’s Johanne, the new head of the Candle Light division. You see, I used to run the division before I became CEO, and I know it well. I can confidently say that Candle Light is the heart of our company. You might have already heard the name because of our charity efforts, but… the true role of Candle Light is to handle the more, well, esoteric and delicate matters. With us, an incident such as what happened here would never have happened.”
