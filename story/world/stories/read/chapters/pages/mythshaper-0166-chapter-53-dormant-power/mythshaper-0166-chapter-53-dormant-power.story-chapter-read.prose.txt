Chapter 53: Dormant Power

Elsbeth was finished. Over an hour and a half had passed in the competition, and she had barely finished repairing two pieces of noble fabricator. Half of that time had already been wasted on the prestigious-grade hammer she had gotten from Tera. Repairing it would perhaps allow her to advance into the final round, but she was uncertain if she was even capable of fixing it, even if she had the full two hours at hand.

Despite being a journeyman artisan, Elsbeth had little experience in repairing fabricators. It was ironic that she had delved into this path at the tender age of five, when she had been trying to fix an old pendant of hers. Yet now, this repair work was bringing an end to her participation.

Elsbeth was never short of money to spend on her crafting hobby. When it came to artifacing, she always moved on to something new. Even during her apprenticeship at Dragonforge, they had never treated her like a common artisan, but as the heir to one of the most prominent houses of the empire. Who knew that would come back to bite her like this today?

Now, there were similarities between crafting something new and repairing something anew. But to fix a ruined fabricator, she had to understand the original creator’s vision behind it. She would have to work with the information already engraved into the items. Elsbeth was not particularly good at that.

At her wit’s end, she eyed Tera, finding her friend fixated on the red-haired boy in front of her. With her Mind essence, it had not been too difficult for her to notice that Tera and the youth were bickering over a broken artefact.

It was quite an eye-opening experience for her to see Tera dealt a short hand at something.

And from the look of it, the red-haired youth seemed to have already repaired the frame, and was now... painting?

“Is he insane?” she intoned.

Tera shot her a look, her fist clenched tight. “Now he's just showing off,” she said. “You think you are only capable of rune painting? I can paint it too.”

She was too competitive not to see that she had to give it to him in this round, even if he failed to complete whatever he was drawing in time.

Elsbeth knew of rune paintings. She had even had to practise by imitating many rudimentary forms in her earliest studies, though her skills in it were painfully inadequate. One would assume that with her Mind essence and calculative ability, she would be a natural at it, but they had failed to consider the artistic endeavour involved.

“What is he drawing, though?” she asked.

It appeared to be a pair of hands, afloat as mist and clouds swirled around them. With a more scrutinising gaze, she could peer into the intricacies involved in the painting. Each of the fingers of the palms seemed to have been built with dozens of rune lines, as well as small figurines etched into them. His essence threads and engraving pen worked with swiftness, despite the sheer amount of intricate detail he was pouring into the painting.

He was sparing no effort on materials either, already investing a great deal of enchant essence and dawn essence, diluting it with oil colours to give it a more otherworldly feel. Finishing with the palm and the clouds, he quickly drew scarlet and black colours, diluted them in a dawn essence solution, and began etching a figurine of a tree in the middle of the palm.

The oracle tree, Elsbeth identified immediately. The more she gazed, the more transfixed she became, forgetting her own work.

Finally, he added sun rays filtering towards the sky, added some greenery and flowers above the palms, and finished with a small figure on top of the thumb of the left palm, almost indistinguishable in its tiny form. Because of the red hair, Elsbeth thought it depicted the painter himself, but on closer inspection, the figure seemed more feminine.

Finally, his eyes rested on the painting as a whole. He peered at it deeply, then looked at his wristwatch before adding a few more strokes.

He only stopped when there were barely a couple of minutes left, heaving a breath of relief.

More than Tera and herself, many other contestants, as well as the judges, were looking at him, but his gaze was drawn to the other section of the hippodrome before returning to the finished work at hand.

He swiftly attached the painting into the frame, connected it to the formation, and allowed its effect to take hold. An illusion of a pair of colossal praying hands flashed within the hippodrome. Elsbeth immediately found herself standing upon the thumb of the praying hand, her figure insignificant against the grandeur of the structure.

Now, with a flick of her Mind essence, she could have cleared the illusion from her mind, but she did not do that. Instead, she peered more closely at the palatial structure. Each part of it seemed to be made out of grand black columns. Even the figurines etched into the fingers were far more... and then they were all gone.

Satisfied with what he had created, the red-haired youth turned off the illusion and turned towards the judges.

“Here,” he said, holding the item, “I completed my repair.”

“You have more than just repaired it, Journeyman Arilyn,” said Master Luc, stepping forward to take a more intensive look at the image.

Whereas Journeyman Arilyn’s gaze was once again drawn to the other section, perhaps looking towards some friend of his partaking in the Strength or Endurance test.

“Journeyman Arilyn,” Administrator Aurine asked, “are you aware what you have drawn?”

Immediately, the boy seemed to sweat profusely. “It wouldn’t be a problem, would it?”

“Not for you, I hope,” the woman said. “Though I can see a number of the affluent families bidding for that painting of yours. It’s going to be quite a headache for us, I fear.”

Elsbeth had to agree with that. She might not have jaded eyes like her grandfather’s, but she was sure he would like the painting. More than the grand otherworldly structure, its themes revolved around hope and renewal. It would make a fine showpiece for any guest he brought to the house.

As her grandfather had been the only one supportive of her in her foolish attempt to become an artisan, she decided she would acquire it for him. She reckoned it would be quite a hassle, not because of the steep price of an artefact. Gold was never a problem for House Solvyn. Masterpiece or not, she feared the Artisan Guild would be unlikely to part with the piece.

But of course, ignoring the artefact, the creator himself would have the right over the painting and who he sold it to. For this reason alone, Elsbeth wanted to become better acquainted with him.

Yet before she could even introduce herself, he called out to the judges:

“If the judging process is as extensive as the first round, can I be dismissed now?”

Master Luc eyed him and gave him a nod. After delivering a quick bow of respect, Arilyn swiftly dashed off the field, amid the incredulous gazes of all his competitors.

Eren felt as though his entire body was being torn into a bloody sap. In the final round of the endurance test, not only had they changed the block of concrete to a block of alloy, they had even latched thick plates of the same heavy alloy onto all four of his limbs. If that were not enough, those plates were etched with a rune formation that seemed to be sucking his stamina like a leech.

Over the hour and a half, many of the contestants had begun to falter, and now only three of them were still standing. Yet somehow, Eren managed to hold on.

He would have been among the eliminated long ago if not for the pressure allowing him to advance to the seventh tier in the common class. The boost in physique granted him the ability to keep up for some time longer.

That had been over half an hour ago. Once again, Eren was hanging on the brink of elimination. His body could barely hold up for a minute or two. The pain was not the problem. It had already dulled from remaining in the same position for such a long time. His fear was for his spine, which could snap at any moment.

His only consolation was that there was a Fabled-class Lifegiver ready to mend him back into one piece.

As Eren wavered at the edge of giving in, an opulent light flashed all over the colosseum, coalescing into an illusion of a colossal pair of hands. In his exhaustion, Eren could barely make out the details, though he suspected it was related to whatever Arilyn was up to. His best friend might not admit it, but he was the biggest show-off out there.

The illusion dissolved as soon as it appeared, but at least it gave him a moment of respite. Not only that, it seemed one of his competitors was taken aback by it and let go of the weight, eliminating himself from the competition.

The boy cursed loudly in the direction the illusion had come from, while a smile twitched on Eren’s rigid face.

Through blurry eyes, he peered at his only remaining competitor. It was a boy of similar age and stature, now completely drenched in sweat. Despite his gift of endurance, his situation seemed to be no better.

But then the bell of the second hour echoed, and the referee increased the burden on them once again.

Instantly, a terrible weight hammered at his core, seizing all the remaining strength from his body. Eren’s knees wobbled, cracked, almost on the point of snapping like a twig. His eyes darkened completely, and suddenly, the thought of giving up did not feel all that terrible.

Eren grunted, his jaw clenched tight. No, unacceptable!

He bit his lower lip fiercely, the pain bringing only a moment of clarity. And with that clarity, he brandished his last bit of resolve when suddenly a surge of raw power flooded from his abdomen.

Eren let out a joyful squeal as his whole body straightened like an arrow. His spine, which had been on the edge of snapping, hardened like cast iron, and the burden over him seemed to have mysteriously halved.
