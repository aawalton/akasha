A blood-red, ghostly octopus flitted in and around the shredded eels like a loose blender blade, shredding all that it encountered while it trailed a cloud of blood and viscera behind it. The Predation-soaked Octomind was like an avatar of carnage, slicing and shredding through the eel swarm while electricity crackled around its form as it zipped from one clump of eels to another.

Infusing the new Octominds with Aspects was a bit more impressive than he’d expected. Slapping an eel that tried to bite him aside, Henry continued his observation while a green-hued, tiny octopus watched from above his head. Its limbs pulsed with vibrant green lights, waiting to heal him if he’d ever need it, but most eels lacked the power to really injure him. After having cut through a decent amount of them, Henry opted to let his Octominds and clone do his work for him. Not to mention, Maurice needed the practice as well. The crab was shooting up a storm, using his bubble like stationary mines to secure the area around them.

With the boost they’d gotten, this is becoming a valid strategy, he thought. While the Octominds could dip into his Aspects for a little conceptual essence to strengthen their skills, Henry realized that he could take it a step further. By pumping them full of concepts themselves, it transformed the Octominds projections into little avatars of the Aspect, which, while it did altered their behavior, it also provided a pretty significant boost to their capabilities. It was why filling an Octomind with the conceptual essence of Predation had turned the little bugger into a hungry, raging whirlwind of blades and death that could slaughter B-rank eels left and right.

In contrast with Predation’s projection, Lifeweaving’s change was a little more peaceful. It stayed with him, waiting for any scratches or injuries to heal.

Henry was curious to see how the other Aspects would change them. But before he could test that, he wanted to created a little space. He wanted to thin the swarm a little. So Henry sent out a batch of Trickster’s Shadows all around, having them slip through and around the eels. By now, he could handle around seventy of the new clones, a number which he expected to grow once the Aspect moved forward to A-rank. But for now, it would do.

With the help of one Octomind, Henry had all of them encase themselves in spiky, barbed bones, which the eels that had followed them quickly decided it was not worth their effort, and while not all his clones managed to form the shell in time, more than fifty of them had done so. Fifty, hard-shell encase clones surrounded the area, most of which were being ignored by the swarm.

Above him, the red took a moment away from his carnage and just waited. It knew what was about to happen. And so Henry didn’t make it wait for long and with a flex of his will, all bone-grenades activated Spiteful Detonation.

It was like a series of fireworks had gone off all at once, crackling all around them while spheres of blood and ink appeared once after the other. The rumble continued for a couple of seconds, shredding a decent part of the swarm with bone shrapnel and Henry’s interface was flooded with death notifications. Henry didn’t pay it any attention. Instead, he studied the feedback he was getting from the freshly integrated skill, which was now happily part of the Predation Aspect.

Spiteful Detonation was going to do great with these new empowered clones. It had integrated into the Aspect as a D-Rank skill, but Henry could tell it was already approaching the upper limits of that rank. It was already close to an upgrade, or at least, he believed it was. And so were all of his Aspects, actually. Once he was done testing the effects of his Aspects on his new Octominds, he’d start looking into finishing getting started on their upgrades. Maybe he’d get some levels as well. After all, none of the eels were managing to land any significant damage, and between his regenerating skills and the handful of mana stealing and capturing abilities at his disposal, Henry could honestly go on… indefinitely.

But back to the Octominds and their different forms, Henry thought, turning his attention away from the theory and back to practice. For his next attempt, Henry channeled the subtle and elusive essence of Dancing Shadows to one of the Octomind, and he watched as the magical octopus grew darker while rippling purple patterns began dancing on its skin. It zipped away, popping in an out of existence in between what remained of the decimated swarm, appearing just behind the eels’ heads and stabbing the base of their spine with its bone-tipped arms. By the time it was noticed, and whenever an attempt was made at biting, attacking, or blasting it away, the ghostly Octomind would be already two blinks away, far out of reach. It was practically impossible to catch. No Telekinesis was fast enough. No bites or blasts of electricity. Nothing could even land a tail-whip on it—not that low B-ranks could damage it—so it tore through the swarm with complete impunity.

At least, until it ran out of essence, of course.

Finally, Henry tested the effect of the last Aspect—which happened to hold the skill that was responsible for the Octominds themselves, and unsurprisingly, the Octomind projection he empowered grew larger. Realer. It’s body rippled with the deep blue glow of the arcane, and hovering above him, it began whipping its arm, throwing sharp blades of echoing magic that tore through the bodies of the eels. Whenever one of the creature managed to reach it, the Domain would activate and it’d either move the creature into the path of one of its many projectiles, or it would trigger a very localized Dispelling Pulse to kill the eel’s attack before decapitating with a sharpened arm.

The Octomind being infused with Thaumaturgy didn’t mean it couldn’t gets its arms bloodied.

All in all, creating little avatars for his Aspects was a pretty interesting and valuable new ability. One that hadn’t been really advertised, sure, but he could see himself using it quite often and once this swarm was dealt with, Henry had a few plans for what came next. Empowered by the new Octominds, it might be time to locate some more of the orbs, and if that still didn’t work, maybe he could attempt another contact with the turtle. For that, he had to finish bringing up all of his fused Aspects to B-rank, and there was no better time than the present, especially now that Maurice had just called in his break.

***

With Maurice safely tucked away in between a couple of empowered and strengthened arms, Henry turned part of his attention inwardly as he—along with his Octominds and clones—continued to tear into what remained of the swarm. By now, he was sitting on a literal mountain of B-rank cores. A treasure trove of fragments and potential levels. But levels weren’t going to be his first investment.

While he and Maurice dealt with the swarm, Henry slunk a clone to Arisia and asked her about upgrading his maxed out Aspects.

It turned out to be easier than he had expected.

Scanning the different parts of his soul, Henry located his first target pretty quickly, and it took him only a few moments to completely wrap his awareness around the area in his spirit that Predation of the Trickster and its cornerstone occupied. Though putting his finger on that area was only the first step.

As Arisia pointed out, the System’s assisted upgrade had consumed tokens. The energy contained in the cores he’d been collecting for the last hour or so. So what he needed to do was grab hold of that energy, then corral it down to the matured Aspect to kick-start its upgrade.

In theory, it sounded easy. Picking one of the death notifications, he claimed one of the cores and converted it to tokens and as he did so, Henry watched the reaction in his soul.

He had to sense that energy to be able to guide it.

The first time, he didn’t sense it. So he converted another core. The second, he thought he sensed something. But he couldn’t tell. So he kept converting the cores, one after the other, and on the 6th, he was nearly sure he got it, and it surprisingly felt very similar to the light of the current, though what he had in the cores and within his soul felt a bit more concentrated than what was floating around him. Still, Henry quickly noticed a problem.

It’s going past the Aspect, he realized. It was going to some reserve deeper in his soul still, and he had to either access that, or figure out a way of capturing the energy before it got there.

By now, the eels had scattered. The swarm—or whatever had been left of it—had run away, so Henry nestled himself on top of boulder and fiddled with his soul. It took an hour or so to finally coax that odd energy back out of its reserve. It took him an hour more to guide it to the Aspect, and while he sensed his Aspect react to the tokens’ presence, there didn’t seem to be enough of it to do much. So Henry kept drawing on that thread, pulling more and more of the core energy out of storage and toward the Aspect. He had no system to help him, so he had no clue how much he’d pulled, but at a certain point, he must have reached a significant threshold as out of the blue, the Aspect pulsed, and the tokens were sucked in within seconds, mixing and matching with the spiritual matter that made the Aspect, and as he sensed the effect it had on his soul, he knew he had it.

It really was much easier than he’d expected.

The process took only minutes, and Henry knew it had worked even before he’d opened his eyes. He could sense it. The ravenous drive of the Aspect had grown stronger. More demanding. That yawning hunger that just wanted to devour everything and anything it could get its fangs in.

Checking the interface, Henry pumped one arm in victory as he confirmed his success.

Henry whistled, and if he had been in his human form right now, he’d be wincing. The new B-rank ability was legitimately nasty. He liked that the cornerstone seemed to be evolving somewhere between the Hunger and Fury. There was still theft, but now it was much more offensive than just copying skills or stealing some conceptual essence. Eying the rest of the Aspect’s skill, Henry nodded along. Not much was changed as the other skills needed to be upgraded individually, but the level up certainly helped one in particular.

Maw of the Trickster can now hold up to 37 thousand cubic meter, Henry noted. Man. I can’t even picture that at this point…

Henry turned his attention out for a moment to slap a couple of straggling eels while he continued converting some of the cores he’d gotten to tokens. Now, he just had to do that whole process three more times, and he’d have all of his Aspects upgraded. At which point, he could move on to the skills.

This ought to be fun, he mused. Now that he’d taken the time to stabilize his rushed foundations, he could really start moving forward. He just hoped Maurice would get the evolution he wished for.
