Xavier sat, stock still on the stool in the Social Rooms, staring at Palini Damascus.

He almost tried to contact the man using their Communication Stone link, but he stopped himself. He’d heard what Dybal, one of the twins in Theta Squad, had said. Palini hadn’t arrived alone. He was with two others.

A new squad.

Which meant Palini’s memories had been removed.

He would have had to sign a contract to become an agent. Would Palini do that?

Xavier stopped himself from drumming his fingers on the bar. Instead, he wrapped them around the glass in his hand and took another sip. It hissed down his throat. Alcohol never was his first choice, but this was one of few situations where he didn’t mind drinking it.

As his mind worked on the problem, he concluded that there were two reasons Palini might have signed the contracts required to become an agent. First, because he feared for the lives of his family. Second, because they might have scooped his memories out of his mind before making him sign the contract.

Either way, the man had no idea Xavier had come to save him.

He wouldn’t even know who Xavier was.

The only information Xavier had gained about the base’s whereabouts was from the spell he’d manifested and cast on the portal that led here. That spell gave him the coordinates to this base. The problem was, he wasn’t familiar with where those coordinates led. It was perhaps a hundred galaxies away from the Orin sector. Nowhere near the inner sectors, but a place with far more powerful Denizens than he was used to.

I asked for this and more by stepping into the wider universe. This place, whoever runs it, isn’t even one of the universe’s main contenders.

Xavier kept his breathing slow and even as he sipped the rest of his drink. He didn’t go to Palini. Alica, the Mistress of Time he impersonated, wouldn’t have bothered, and her squadmates were already suspicious of her actions on their last mission.

He turned away from the new arrivals and worked on drinking his drink.

Roln, the dead Wanderer who was now nothing more than a mental construct in Xavier’s mind, appeared on the now vacant stool by his side. “Well, this is quite the predicament.” The dead Wanderer had a drink of his own, not that he was even really there. He sipped it thoughtfully all the same.

You’re not going to say something about making foolish decisions? Xavier said, communicating telepathically without glancing at the man.

Roln shrugged. “Honestly, after what you did with the World Destroyer, I just want to see how you’ll turn this around. Something tells me it’ll be interesting.”

Xavier chuckled into his drink. Some of the tension he’d been holding since he stepped through the portal leaked out of him. His shoulders no longer felt like there were rocks in them, and he spent a moment finding that inner calm that always helped him in difficult situations.

Thinking a little more clearly, he sat straighter in his stool and polished off the rest of the drink. With The All-Seeing Eye, he clocked every observation crystal in the room around him, a plan slowly forming in his mind.

There was one silver lining to what had happened to Palini, even if it was a small one—despite Xavier’s worries, the man was alive, and now he knew exactly where he was.

If he was going to get him out of here, now might just be the perfect time.

Xavier retreated with the vast amount of his consciousness into the rooms of his mind, leaving a small part to control his body still sitting at the bar, and sped up time within a mind space, running simulation after simulation to figure out exactly what he needed to do to get out of this mess alive.

The obvious solution would be wrapping the most powerful time dilation field around himself and Palini that he could manage, grabbing the man, and portalling out of here.

There were several problems with that. First, even if he could somehow circumvent the protective runes in the walls that he knew for a fact would stop his portal from functioning and get Palini the hell out of here, Palini didn’t have his memories. When Xavier had made his way from Alica’s mind he hadn’t found her old memories locked away.

They’d been ripped out of her. The wound of their loss had left a raw, sore spot inside her.

The same thing would have happened to Palini.

Have they even stored his memories somewhere, or have they been destroyed?

He didn’t know how to answer that question. Looking around the room, he knew no one here would have that answer either.

The only person he’d encountered since entering the base who might know was Striker.

And what if even he doesn’t know? an annoying thought asked. Well, then I’ll cross that bridge when I come to it.

The simulations he ran weren’t perfect. He could only glean so much about how the observation crystals worked.

If he probed at them any harder with his spiritual sense, he would risk detection. But the observation crystals had been made with the use of runes. That was something he’d come to learn over the years—most magical technology was made with the help of runes.

From the runes he could read inside the crystals, he found they only observed audio and visual, which meant they didn’t track energy fluctuations.

Assuming he was right, they wouldn’t be able to detect if he cast a spell.

Xavier was curious about that. This place appeared to be under heavy surveillance, but lacking the ability to detect spell-casting seemed like a huge oversight.

Until he remembered the System contracts.

The Collector had held an entire galaxy under his sway with the use of System contracts. The surveillance probably wasn’t even needed. The Denizens in this room, and any other Denizens that had suffered the same fate of becoming a memory-wiped agent, wouldn’t be able to even try to escape.

So why use observation crystals at all?

Xavier decided he didn’t need to answer that question. There were likely many reasons for the observation crystals. From the way Striker had acted about being late by a measly two seconds, adding another 100 years on Alica’s servitude sentence because of it, they likely used the observation crystals to catch small infractions.

Any excuse to add to the agents’ sentence.

Very big brother.

After he’d run enough simulations and was at least half-way confident in his plan, he returned his full consciousness to his body sitting at the bar. He raised a finger, getting the barkeep’s attention, and ordered another of Alica’s highly alcoholic drinks.

All the while, inside his body he was manifesting different spell patterns. Spell patterns he’d come up with in the simulations. When he cast these spells, the observation crystals wouldn’t be able to detect them at all.

That wasn’t the case for every other Denizen in the room, however.

If the other agents had a powerful enough spiritual sense—and he’d already pinpointed several A Grades—and were paying attention, there was a good chance they’d detect what he did.

That simply meant he had to move fast.

A smile slipped onto Xavier’s face. The challenges he was about to face, the things he was about to do to get out of here… well, despite what was at stake, he was starting to have a little fun.

“That’s the Xavier I know.” Roln smiled before promptly disappearing from the stool beside him.

The Social Rooms weren’t under a time dilation field. There had been a time dilation field encompassing the Mission Room, one that had been synced up with Alica’s while the mission was running, but that field had been cancelled when Theta Squad had returned. Unlike Xavier and those Denizens trained under his rule, the vast majority of Denizens suffered from a hell of a lot of drawbacks while a time dilation field was active.

Having one constantly running at the base wouldn’t be feasible.

And so, the first spell Xavier cast was Time Alteration. The time distortion wouldn’t be visible on the observation crystals, and even if the A Grades in the room could detect it they would take too long to respond.

Xavier didn’t move from where he was sitting at the bar. He made his body move as slowly as the others in the room—slow enough to appear as though he was frozen in time—then cast the next spell, one he’d manifested for a very specific purpose.

Thin lines of energy flowed through the air, shooting toward the observation crystals in the room. When the lines of energy reached the crystals, they encompassed every inch of them and projected a highly realistic simulation of the bar and those inside it. The energy blocked light and sound from reaching the crystals, providing its own to each of them. It was like he’d hacked into a set of surveillance cameras on pre-integration Earth and started a loop.

Except the simulation wasn’t a loop—it was a highly detailed rendering of the immediate future as it could have been if Xavier weren’t about to intervene.

Anyone watching wouldn’t know a damned thing about what was happening in that room.

When all the observation crystals were effectively disabled, Xavier allowed his grin to broaden into a wide smile.

He liked his plan. He liked it very much.

He was about to show whoever ran this place what happened when they messed with Xavier Collins. He glanced over at Palini, allowing himself to move and erasing the glamour that made him appear as Alica. He rolled his head around his neck, eliciting several satisfying cracks, and stretched out his black dragon wings.

And what happens when you mess with my friends.

He made a beeline for Palini. To do what he needed, he had to lay a hand on the man’s head. The instant he did, Palini would be encompassed by the time dilation field. As an agent of whoever ran this place that would mean Palini might try and fight him.

Assuming Xavier gave him the chance—which he wasn’t about to.

Xavier closed the gap and touched the back of Palini’s head. He broke into the man’s mind in an instant, seizing control. He didn’t like doing that to Palini, but it was a necessary evil.

As swiftly as he could, Xavier erased the contracts etched into his soul. While inside Palini’s mind, he searched the man’s memories and despaired when he found they’d been wiped clean. There was barely a hint of the man he’d been. Enough for him to function as a capable agent. Nothing more.

Whoever had ripped the memories from his mind mustn’t have spent any time examining those memories. If they had, they would have found out a lot of interesting things about the man—like the fact that he had an extra core.

Amateurs.

The instant Xavier had freed Palini from his contracts, Xavier pulled back his hand, leaving the man outside the time dilation field once more.

He looked around the room. Erasing contracts required a certain amount of control over an opponent, but surprisingly he didn’t need full control over them. Not anymore. The only reason he’d entered Palini’s mind was to get an idea of what memories had been lost.

He didn’t need to do that again.

Over the years, he’d managed to cajole Roln into helping him refine the process of erasing contracts. He’d known that one day he would want to erase contracts on a grander scale than he had in the past—and he’d want to use the ability on those more powerful than himself.

Now, he finally had an opportunity to test the methods he’d learned.

Xavier cast several more manifested spell patterns, summoning a host of drones into the room, one for every agent. He spread his time dilation field to the drones as he took control of their minds.

Simultaneously, every drone cast Strike Out, laying their hands on one of the memory-lost agents in the room, bringing them into the time dilation field.

This was the riskiest moment of this gambit, as they all entered the same timestream he was in, but he only needed the barest fraction of a second…

There.

He cancelled the Strike Out spells.

The Denizens were once more frozen in time, only now every single one of their contracts had been erased.

Xavier tilted his chin up.

He’d just given every agent their free will back.

The only problem was, this was about as far as he’d gotten with his plan.
