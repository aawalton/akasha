Months passed as we tested again and again. Chien, frustrated with my refusal came up with a better screen, it even had basic color, though I'll admit it took him awhile to get that right, and the images were still less than stellar. That was important, but other things were too.

For now on that screen the world was mostly white, winter had come. It was a rare thing, a passing thing, rather than the yearly change I was used to, but it had come, and across the land the water froze and rather than rain we had snow.

It was bad, winter was always bad, even though we'd seen it coming a few months ahead there was much to do. I did what I could to see to it that all of the villages of any real size had at least one large heating item, something that could help the very young or weak stay warm during this particularly cold time. The specialized items were better, but we'd even distributed some simple heating crystals, much like those at Icehome had used long ago.

We also had a system for food distribution and checking in on people now. It wasn't a full welfare system, but it was enough to help, to take excess from those few places that had it and to those places that needed it, the former now booming because of the long spread of farming. There were still settlements it hadn't taken over, but those were becoming fewer and fewer.

“I find I hate winter more and more as time goes on,” Chien said. “Though I guess since I signed up with you it's not like I ever had any real issues.”“Did you before?” I asked. “You'd have been... not very old when the first one hit right?”“Indeed, it's all a bit weird from my perspective, and I didn't understand it surely, but I remember it being miserable.”“Ah, that tracks,” I nodded, I'd been pretty young when it had come through too, he had to have been barely out of his crib. Our memories all stretched back that far, but without perspective they didn't exactly form the same, it was difficult for others to describe, and not something I understood as I had had that perspective.

“Want to see the latest we've got?” he asked, pouring himself a cup of tea from my teapot, most people would balk at daring to do that, but he knew I wouldn't mind.

“Sure, throw it up,” I said, pointing to his version of a monitor.

Rather than simple images a map began to appear, not 2D but 3D. The small table morphed, little clear crystals pulling themselves upwards and small lights behind them beginning to shine, giving hues to the different elevations. It we gathered round it, looking at the various different magnifications.

“Look at this,” he said pointing to a bit from one of our furthest out drones.

“Some of those oceans aren't nearly as deep,” I nodded along.“The storms though, that was where our stuff got obliterated.”

“If we find anything of note over there we'll have to hug the northern winds, though from the speeds I think it'll be a real pain, slow going.”“And colder than this nonsense,” Chien added, not that we were cold in the workroom, or that either of us couldn't fix that with a thought.

“Fair point, and if we go by air worse still...”“Assuming we find anything boss, even you admit you don't know.”

“I suspect,” I made clear. “Because that's all I can do, if there is land here, it should be possible there is land elsewhere.”There were other reasons too. Wind and wave mostly, since if those weren't being blocked they should have been much worse unless there was some magical nonsense keeping them so. Of course there might be some magical nonsense keeping them so, but that was a problem only if I ran into it.

“Fine, fine, your other projects though? Have you seen how those have gone?”With a sigh I nodded. “Yes, sadly.”What he referred to were my first, failing, attempts at golems. Failing because no matter what I did I just couldn't get the damn things to walk right. There was more to it, and I suspected that had I known more about things like AI and the like programming them to make it from point A to point B would have been easier. Alas I wasn't particularly experienced in those, so it was going... slowly. Several (and all of them were the size of a small cat) had run at speed into various objects, often damaging themselves beyond rep;air. Others just got trapped in loops, doing the same action again and again, it really was a pain.

“Sadly indeed, and why? I mean, sure, it would be nice to have things deliver messages or whatever, but the people who've taken to training animals, at your advice by the way, are already working on that.”“I like machines, animals are good for some things, but sometimes you just need the strength and certainty of steel. At any rate, nobody has yet managed to even tame a weak magical beast, only those without abilities, abilities I can put into my creations.”

“You make me tired sometimes boss, some of those efforts are going very well you know. And here you are undermining them.”“Different things for different purposes,” I informed him, before he heard a loud Ding noise.

“Which one?” he asked, turning suddenly to look at our workstation.

“One of the further out ones,” I answered, seeing where the alarm had come from. “Far to the East, surprising, but not too much so.” There were really only two directions, and we'd started East first anyway.

“Look at the change, that's no island, nor a reef, sudden drop in the water's depth, massive, just like our shore here. Could it have circled around back to us?”“No, shouldn't be that far based on what we've measured,” I responded.

It was a continental shelf, an actual continental shelf. We hadn't seen land yet, but in our argument we'd at least seen the smoke for the fire that could be out there.
