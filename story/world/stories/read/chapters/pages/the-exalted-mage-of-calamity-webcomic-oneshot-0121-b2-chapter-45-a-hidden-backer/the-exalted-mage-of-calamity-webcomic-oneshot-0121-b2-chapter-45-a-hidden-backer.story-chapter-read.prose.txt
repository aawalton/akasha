The auction hall had gone silent; everyone was watching the exchange between a senior member of the Mage Association and the nervous auctioneer on stage.

The earlier contest between the commerce groups and Orban had been ugly, but seemingly not unexpected. Many people clearly knew about their dispute. Some may have even anticipated it.

This was different.

“What do you mean he’s after you?” Gwen asked.

Veronica kept her eyes on the older man. “He’s probing for information. He doesn’t care about the notes themselves, but what they represent.”

“A breakthrough in a research field,” Nasha said.

Veronica nodded. “One or two manuscripts are enough to tell him the author is knowledgeable and valuable. He wants my identity so he can get access to me. Maybe to take me under his wing, collect more of my research, or even pressure me into revealing what else I know. He’s using the Mage Association as leverage against the auction house.”

“But… doesn’t he work for the Mage Association?” Gwen asked. “He’s wearing their robes and everything. Is he allowed to do this just by himself?”

“Technically, no. He’s affiliated with them, but that doesn’t mean he has the authority to speak for the entire association. But no one is willing to challenge his words. He’s only a seven-star, one level below a tower master.”

“Like Hugh?”

“Roughly. Hugh would be around that level academically as head of studies, but Ronswick’s Mage Tower is only located in a viscounty, so his practical authority is lower. Rarran, as tower master, would be an eight-star regardless of location.”

Nasha turned and pointed toward the servant waiting in the corner. “You. Who is that man?”

The servant followed her gesture. “That is Mr. Varetus. He currently serves as head of studies for the Ivy Mage Tower.”

“The Ivy Tower?” Gwen echoed, frowning.

“You know it?” Veronica asked.

“Remember when we first got here and you told me to learn about the mage towers?”

Veronica nodded.

“The Ivy Mage Tower came up a lot while I was studying at the Starfault Archives. They’re the strongest of the four towers in Maftem, but they have a terrible reputation with the others. They’re obsessed with monopolizing resources. Greedy, rude, arrogant—pretty much everything bad you could hear about them, I heard.”

“I suppose that tracks, considering Harris is standing behind him,” Nasha said.

“What should we do, then?” Gwen asked.

“…We wait,” Veronica said. “Right now, it depends on whether the Grand Auction is willing to betray a client just because someone threatened them.”

“I apologize, but revealing the identity of a client is against the rules of the Grand Auction,” the auctioneer said. “Please understand.”

“I understand your rules,” Varetus replied. “What I’m asking is whether you believe those rules are more important than making an enemy of the Mage Association.”

The auctioneer’s ears twitched again.

“Mr. Varetus,” she said carefully, “the Pelotema Grand Auction has no intention of disrespecting the Mage Association. However, every client who submits an item to us does so with the assurance that their privacy will be protected unless they agree otherwise.”

“And anyone bidding on an item does so under the assurance that they are not being misled,” Varetus said. His voice remained calm, but the pressure behind it sharpened. “The fact that you cannot personally guarantee these manuscripts is a problem. Their origins are already suspicious. If it were a regular item, there would be no problem. Swords change hands all the time. However, since this involves knowledge, there should be a bit more scrutiny, don’t you agree?”

The murmurs below grew louder.

Then a man in the lower section stood, paddle still in hand. Veronica recognized him as the buyer of the first manuscript.

“I would also like clarification,” he said. “I purchased the first set of notes believing the Grand Auction had fully verified its claims. If there is uncertainty surrounding the second manuscript, then there may be uncertainty surrounding the first. I would rather not unknowingly make an enemy of the original author.”

That was enough to embolden the others. Like an ember catching on dry grass, voices began spreading through the hall. Mages whispered more loudly among themselves, and a few even called questions toward the stage.

The fox-kin auctioneer’s smile remained in place, but it had grown strained.

Veronica could practically see the calculation behind her eyes.

If she revealed the consignor, the auction house would betray a client’s trust. If she refused, they risked losing the confidence of every mage in the room.

“I’m not even asking you to reveal who the consignor truly is,” Varetus continued. “A simple name would be enough. Should that name resurface later, it would at least prove that the Grand Auction was working with integrity and not tricked by some ploy.”

Nasha’s eyes narrowed. “He’s cunning.”

Veronica glanced at her. “What do you mean?”

Gwen turned as well. “Yeah. Isn’t giving him a name fine? Even if they say Veronica Everwells, he still won’t know who she is.”

Nasha shook her head and gestured toward the servant again. “You. Who funds the Grand Auction?”

The servant perked up. “Ah, yes. There are twelve sponsors in total.”

“Name them.”

She nodded. “The two Great Noble Families, Lexington and Aranauld. Then Twistwell, Founders Bank, Lilybed, the Starfault Archives, Silver Pen Registrar, Pelotema Civic Vault, the—”

Nasha raised a hand. “That’s enough.”

She turned back toward the opposite balcony. “I know what his plan is.”

“What are you talking about?” Gwen asked.

Nasha wasn’t looking at Varetus anymore. Her gaze had settled on Harris, standing calmly behind him with a faint smirk.

“I see,” Veronica said, following her gaze. “So that’s his plan.”

Gwen sighed. “Ugh, I hate being in the dark. Can one of you explain it without the intelligent-archmage and calculating-rogue talk?”

“Even if they give him only my name, he has a way to find out who I am,” Veronica said.

“How?”

“Harris.”

Gwen looked toward him. “What? But how would he—”

“He works at the Starfault Archives,” Nasha said. “And they fund this auction. As their lead appraiser, getting access to an attendance list probably wouldn’t be difficult. Finding out the identity of an anonymous seller would be much harder because of the auction’s rules. But if he already has Veronica’s name, all he needs to do is match it to someone here.”

“Especially if they can connect me to the Mage Association afterward,” Veronica added. “I don’t particularly care if people know who I am. But Varetus is dangerous right now. If he’s willing to throw around the Mage Association’s name this casually, then he clearly has an agenda. If he pressures me directly, I may not be able to avoid a confrontation.”

“What should we do?” Nasha asked. “If Gwen’s right about the Ivy Tower, this may not end with them simply introducing themselves after the auction.”

Veronica’s eyes narrowed.

On stage, the auctioneer’s strained smile finally disappeared.

“Mr. Varetus,” she said, “even providing a name may violate our client’s privacy. While I respect your position as one of our VIP guests, you are overstepping certain boundaries.”

“Then tell us whether the name belongs to a recognized researcher,” Varetus replied smoothly. “Or whether the consignor is affiliated with a mage tower. Or whether the manuscripts were submitted under their own authorship. You should possess that information, should you not?”

Veronica’s fingers tightened. “He’s narrowing the road,” she murmured.

The man who had purchased the first manuscript tightened his grip on his paddle. Rather than shouting, he used the same voice-amplification magic as Varetus. “I believe a name is reasonable. I have already spent thirty-six thousand vix. If these notes are later contested, I would prefer to know whether I purchased them from a legitimate researcher or a thief.”

“The seller’s privacy matters,” another mage called, “but so does the buyer’s protection.”

“Agreed,” someone else added.

The murmurs swelled again.

The auctioneer’s ears continued twitching as instructions reached her from elsewhere. Her lips remained tightly pressed, careful not to say anything until, finally, she drew a slow, deep, breath. “If…” she began, then paused.

The hall gradually quieted.

“If a name is all that is required to ease our guests’ concerns, then we may be able to provide that much while withholding all further personal information.”

Veronica’s eyes sharpened.

Gwen stiffened beside her. “Wait. They’re actually going to do it? They have to know he can figure out who you are from just a name, right?”

“They know,” Nasha said. “But they probably can’t afford to drag this out much longer. Their choice is between offending someone carrying a viscount’s signet or a seven-star senior with connections to both the Mage Association and the Starfault Archives.”

“They’re going to regret saying my name,” Veronica said quietly.

Gwen glanced at her, swallowed, then turned back toward Varetus.

“Of course,” he replied. “A name will suffice. From there, we can determine whether the consignor is the author or has some connection to them. If they are affiliated with the Mage Association, all the better. At the very least, if any incidents or trickery were to occur, we’ll know the conspirator.”

The auctioneer’s lips thinned. “Very… well. The consignor who registered these manuscripts is—”

Her words were cut off as a sudden wind swept through the entire chamber.

Across the hall, three cloaked figures rose from one of the upper balconies. Their robes hung still despite the wind. Hoods concealed their faces as they floated silently toward the stage.

The auction hall went quiet.

Veronica stared intently, and then her eyes widened.

Badges from the Mage Association were pinned to their chests. The two figures flanking the center wore five stars, marking them as high-ranking seniors—two levels above Harris. But the one in the middle…

“What’s going on?” Gwen asked. “Those are Mage Association badges, right?”

Nasha remained silent, her eyes tracking the three figures.

Veronica’s lips thinned. “Three stars, like Harris, mark a senior who has completed their basic studies and can take on juniors. Five stars are assistant professors. Six are professors. Seven are department heads. Eight are equivalent to tower masters. Nine are spire headmasters.”

Gwen looked from her to the figure in the center. “Then… that mage in the center with eight stars is…”

Veronica stared in silence.

“Esteemed seniors,” Varetus said, dipping his head and pressing one fist into an open palm.

The three ignored the courtesy. They continued forward until they hovered before the stage, directly in front of the auctioneer. She stood frozen, her ears completely stiff.

The central figure spoke first. His voice was smooth and clear, with a deeper undertone. He didn’t sound particularly old—perhaps in his mid-to-late thirties—but he carried the practiced cadence of someone accustomed to addressing crowds.

“You do not need to reveal the consignor’s name.”

The fox-kin blinked. “Wh-what?”

“We of the Mage Association will act as guarantors of the authenticity of these manuscripts. There is no need to probe further into the author’s identity.”

A murmur swept through the hall.

“Veronica?” Nasha asked, looking over. “What’s going on?”

Veronica remained silent, staring closely at the man in the center. His face was hidden beneath his hood, but that voice… why did it sound familiar?
