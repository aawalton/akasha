“You know I’ve always wondered about this Card, but until now I could never afford to play him.” Said Alley with a little nod towards the leftmost Card image floating before him. Denderra, who was sitting with one leg crossed over the other on a bench or chair that simply didn’t exist on his right-hand side. The already pale girl visibly paled further.

“I know you don’t listen to me half of the time, but I’m begging you not to play Lynillian. I’ll tell you what his effects are if you swear you will only bring him out this match if you are about to lose.”

Alley turned to look at his ghostly companion. She wasn’t as shaken as she’d seemed upon realizing Xu-De, Queen of Nothing, had become a Card. Still, he couldn’t deny her nervousness. Whatever this Card was capable of, Denderra did not want to see it utilized.

“Okay, he's probably too expensive to feasibly use anyway, but why?”

Denderra opened her mouth to answer, but before she had a chance to, the old bearded man across the arena called out in what Alley swore was a jovial attempt to distract him.

“Oh, and now you are chatting away with an invisible Deck sprite! Is your strategy just to have so many activatables attached to your Dealer that I die of old age and lose by default?”

Alley placed a hand over his mouth to cover the smile that he was barely holding back. Beside him, the Oblivion maiden’s eyebrows twitched a few times, and her face turned from pale to a little flushed.

“They always think I’m a Deck sprite.” She hissed. “It’s like comparing me to one of those life-sized dolls they show off clothes on in stores nowadays. Yes, we are vaguely the same shape, but I am not an accursed Deck Sprite!”  With the way his eyes worked now, it could be hard to tell when Denderra made herself visible to everyone, but from the lack of reaction across the Arena, he was pretty sure the ghost hadn’t revealed herself to Vern.

“I know, I know, and I promise I’ll crush him for your honor, but you were telling me what Lynilian, the Carrion King, does.”

The white-haired ghost bit her cheek and made an obvious effort to fight off a pout.

“Promise?”

“Promise.”

Denderra took another second to compose herself as Alley made a rude gesture  by way of reply to his opponent.

“Okay, so you know how Unique Cards do unique things?” She asked.

“Sure. I will probably never forget that fat shark thing those pirates were feeding.”

Denderra nodded.

“Well, Lynillian is a very unique Card. The Six knights of The Cursed King accepted their own death and rebirth as Cards so they could keep my brother and me safe for all time. Lynillian did it so there would never come an era in which he wouldn’t be able to take human lives. If you play that Card I promise you he will do his utmost to make sure either you or your opponent dies during the match. He will try to convince you with words and actions, and you will find yourself drawing him more often than you should.”

Alley took a moment to digest everything that had just been said to him. The creature Card displayed a man with hair as white as Denderra’s holding two swords amidst a pile of bones, as crows and other carrion birds descended around him. He would already cost six of Alley’s eight Vials. So Alley hadn’t intended to play him if he could help it anyway, but the warning Denderra was giving him, along with the nine thousand Power the Creature possessed, made him a lot more appealing to Manifest, however.

The Oblivion Maiden went on to explain exactly what the Card did, information that almost knocked Alley over.

​

​

‘Alright, let's move away from Lynillian for a second. No matter what, I’m not going to be playing him on the first turn, so I need a plan for the rest of what I’m holding.’ Alley mused to himself as he inspected the rest of the Cards he was holding. As per usual, he was the weaker rank and thus went first.

Other than the Carrion King, he had ‘Shadowed Eater, Brutish Zombie, Wraith Master, Bone Weaved.’

Alley let out a little sigh and Manifested Brutish Zombie. It was a fairly generic Card, with mediocre effect. Still, its stats for a free creature weren’t bad, and Alley wasn’t willing to give up the momentum advantage that came from playing the first Creature Card.

The huge and muscular Zombie Manifested on the field, its shirtless form sporting purple skin, and its face showing signs of rot.

​

Alley spent a few seconds considering his options. He didn’t want to overinvest in Brutish Zombie, but at the same time, he needed to play Cards to draw Cards. While  that might be true of every Deck and every player. The ninety-card minimum of a Cursed Deck made it a particular concern for Alley. As much as he preferred to play a more conservative style in which he relied on his reflexes to keep himself safe while building a Hand that could suddenly sweep through and defeat his opponent in one to two devastating turns, it simply wasn’t always a viable option.

“Bone Weaved.” He declared to activate the spell in his hand.

The spell was cast and it immediately began transfiguring the Brutish Zombie. Purple Undeath Resonance poured into the monster from Alley’s Cloak, and as it did, the bones of the Manifested Creature began to burst bloodlessly from its skin from all over its body apart from its head. The bone spikes quickly grew and changed, creating a horrific latticework armor with pale spikes pointing in all directions

​

That left Alley with Lynylian, whom he had promised to avoid playing, Shadowed Eater, which would set him back a Vial to Manifest, and was better used later in the game, and Wraith Master, whose cost was two Vials, and was all but worthless without a Crypt full of spells. Now that he knew what Shadowed Eater did, there was a certain degree of symmetry between the Cards. The simple truth however, was that it wasn’t enough to incline Alley to rush either monster out onto the field.

Perhaps, he thought, more importantly, he had played two Cards, and would redraw them at the start of his next phase. Going first meant there was no War phase, so that was a second Creation phase that he didn’t think he’d be overly likely to take advantage of.

The first redraw was Phoenix Urn Phylactery.

‘Not bad.’ Alley thought for a little over a single second until the next card redrew, and he had to repress a sigh. Alley had drawn Veil of Storms, a spell Card that used Sky and Deception Resonance. It wasn’t all that bad of an effect, but Alley didn’t see himself spending a Vial and risking Resonance sickness to play.

So Alley discarded it at the end of his turn. It would mean having to wait till the start of the next turn to draw his next Card. At least it kept his Deck flowing and new Cards entering his Hand.

“Your Turn.” He called with an open-handed gesture over at the large, old man he was facing.

“Oh, you do know that phrase, huh?” The old man called back with a cheeky grin.
