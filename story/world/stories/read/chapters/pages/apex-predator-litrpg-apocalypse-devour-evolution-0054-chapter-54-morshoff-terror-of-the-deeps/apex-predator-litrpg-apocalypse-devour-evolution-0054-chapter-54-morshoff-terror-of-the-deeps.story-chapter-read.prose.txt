The tentacles came for him immediately when his boots hit the swamp floor.

Don landed hard and sprinted, eyes locked on the far side of the bog. The ground squelched underfoot. Moist, uneven, and covered in tangling roots. The reek of stagnant water and rot hit like a physical wall. But Don didn’t slow down. He leaned into the run, arms pumping.

The tentacles slithered around, surging towards him like swarms of snakes.

He felt the first one coming more than he saw it. Survival Instincts doing work.

He slid low. A whip-thick tendril carved through the air where his torso had been a second earlier. It clove a nearby torch pole in half with its jagged barb.

Another one darted from the left. Don saw it too late to dodge, so he blocked. His psychic grip pulled the buckler strapped to his back into his hand, and he took the blow edge-on. The impact vibrated through his whole arm and knocked him sideways into the muck.

He hit the swamp hard. Water and sludge flew up around him. Something grabbed at his ankle immediately. He didn’t bother looking—just rolled, twisted, and carved down with a claw.

A wet snap. The ease at which Don cleaved through the tentacle was a massive difference from before.

The tentacles also moved much slower— or at least it felt like it. More of them were gathering, like Morshoff had let out some underwater clarion call. But they were slower, and Don had a real chance here.

Another tentacle attacked. Don sliced through it. Don shoved off the ground and scrambled upright, breath ragged, blood singing.

More tentacles surged towards him. More than he had even thought possible. These things weren’t messing around.

The tendrils didn’t strike at random. They moved like hunting snakes. Coordinated. Watching. Testing his timing. Trying different angles.

One flicked in low, trying to sweep his legs out again. He jumped it. Another coiled in from above. Don bent backward into a half-sprawl and let it whistle past his face, then came up swinging with a psychic-launched spearhead from his satchels.

The weapon struck flesh and pinned a tentacle into a gnarly root.

Not enough to stop anything. But enough to irritate. And irritation was exactly what Don wanted right now.

“Come on then,” he muttered. “Let's dance, bastard.

He broke into a run again. This time angling across the swamp floor. His boots kicked up foul water with every step.

He whistled. Echo Location painted the whole scene in his head. The water in the middle was deep. Maybe fifty feet across, maybe more. Something big was down there, swaying slow. The gnarled shell floated just at the surface, bulbous and still.

But the stalk. That’s what he needed. The huge rootlike cord that went down, anchoring Morshoff to whatever bottomless pit lay below. That was the leash. That was the weakness. Break the stalk, Morshoff couldn’t run.

Simple plan. Hard execution. He would have to take a plunge. And that wasn’t an option with this many tentacles chasing him. He’d lose this speed and coordination in the water.

Don zig-zagged between rotten structures and patches of dry ground. The tendrils were everywhere now. Coming faster. More aggressive. They struck from three angles at once. One came for his chest. He threw up the shield and caught it again with a hard smack. Another tried to wrap his midsection. He dove sideways and slid through a sludge-filled trench. Came up filthy but grinning.

Predictable and slow.

Don bolted the other way. He grabbed two spears from his sling and sent them spinning like missiles. One struck a tendril mid-curve, knocking it off-course. The other missed entirely. Didn’t matter. It gave him another opening.

He dove forward, tucked his knees, rolled, and came up running again. The swamp water was close now. He could see the edge of the horrific white mask popping up from the water. a low, endless sound like tectonic plates shifting under pressure. Water rippled out from the base.

Morshoff knew he was coming for him.

Good.

Something huge surged from the pool.

A massive tentacle slammed down in front of him. Don didn’t stop. He launched himself into the air and used Telekinesis to guide a floating shield under his feet mid-jump. He landed on it for a split-second, pushed off, and kept going.

The swamp floor was giving way now. No more dry spots. Just mud, water, and a narrow rim of slick terrain around the pool. The deep end loomed ahead. Black water churned. Tendrils broke the surface like serpents and vanished just as quickly.

Don skidded to a stop near the edge. The ground here was half-rotten wood, half-slimy roots. Slick stones and false floors. No cover. No safety. Just open bog and murder noodles.

He crouched low. Echo Location flared out with a sharp whistle. The image he got back was sharp.

The whistle also showed him all the places his foot would hold, and more importantly, where he would sink two feet into the swamp water.

A huge shape in the middle of the pool outlined in Don’s mind through Echo Location. Morshoff was right there. Thirty feet out. Floating. Waiting.

Don braced himself. This would be stupid. This would be risky.

But it’s the only way.

The tendrils surged.

Four of them whipped toward him from different angles. One went high, two middle, one low. Don reacted instantly. He dropped into a slide and let the low strike pass just overhead. The second he cleared it, he twisted his body and kicked off sideways with psychic force.

He flipped once in the air and landed on all fours. Wet. Dirty. Breathing hard.

He still had the shield orbiting above him.

The giant tentacle struck again. From above. The strike came down like a hammer. Don pulled the shield into its path mid-swing. The shield exploded. Don jumped backwards, but the giant tentacle grazed him.

It didn’t have barbs, suction cups or poison. But it was fucking heavy. Fortunately the shield softened and misguided the blow.

[Health at 86%]

Still fine.

Don pulled two more spears from his bundle and launched them at the boss as he ran. Both spears clicked off the wide mass in the water. But Morshoff stirred. It had a thick shell, but it wasn’t immune to damage.

He could distract the boss. That was key.

Because it meant the stalk was vulnerable.

Don grinned.

He launched himself forward. Mud splashed. A tentacle shot up to intercept him. He launched a spear at it with Telekinesis. The tendril reeled from the impact, stumbled mid-arc. Don used that beat to close another five feet.

He ran full speed for the edge of the deep water jumping from hard land island to another. One last root jutted out like a diving board. He planted his foot.

Tentacles followed him like a swarm of snakes. The giant one shot out from the air.

Morshoff was too late. Just a beat off.

Don vaulted off the root with a full-body leap. Mid-air, he ripped a floating shield to his feet and used it as a second step to push off again.

He soared through the air, eyes locked on the shell.

He could see the eerie white mask clearly. An open maw with rows of teeth. It was widened, as if charging for an attack.

But all Don could look at were the eyes. Double pupils, like a calamari. Keen, predatory and really fucking malevolent. They watched Don with absolute focus and pure anger.

Morshoff let out a wail. It was like a whale call. Eerie, long, alien. Like a ghost lost at the bottom of the ocean.

Some kind of psychic attack. That made sense. Don already knew Morshoff had been affecting his mind subtly before. But now his Mental was high enough. He wasn’t disoriented.

He pushed through it.

He came down hard on top of the shell.

His boots landed with a crunch.

Slick, wet, stinking.

But he was in.

Don crouched low and flicked out his claws. The tentacles gathered over his head like an angry swarm. Don plunged one of his claws into the evil eye of Morshoff and the monster wailed again.
