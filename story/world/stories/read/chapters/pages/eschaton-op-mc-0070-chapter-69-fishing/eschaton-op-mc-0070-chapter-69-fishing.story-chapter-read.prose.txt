The water swallows me up, and the sounds of Second Squad going about their tasks becomes muffled. It makes me nervous to leave them behind where I can’t help them quickly, but Sergeant Callisto is right, as she often is. We all have jobs to do, and I need to trust them to do theirs as much as they trust me to do mine.

So I march into the lake to find out what’s down here. Beyond magical crystals, I can’t say I really know what I’m looking for, but Callisto’s advice of “weird and unrecognizable” seems good enough. I rip up a few lily pads the size of a car up by the roots and let them float to the surface to be collected later.

These plants, unlike everything else I’ve collected so far, might be able to be replanted. I’d rather not store something living away. I have the strange feeling that’d be bad for its health. Everything organic I’ve disappeared has been dead or dying, so I can’t say for certain what it would do.

Every step I take sends up clouds of silt and muck. Little fish and crawdads scurry away, and I ignore them. They’re too quick for me to grab. Not without extensive collateral damage, and I’d rather not damage something without knowing if I want to take it with me.

The water itself is exceptionally clear, giving me a great view of what lies underneath the lake. Which so far is a whole lot of nothing. Fallen trees and rocks litter the lakebed, but nothing particularly interesting. I’m very thankful for the cloak Morgan gave me. The walk would have been much, much slower otherwise. Even with the magical effect it provides, I still sink in a few inches. Without, I’m sure I’d be up to my knees. I doubt it’d trap me, but it’d make moving an unbelievable pain in the ass, and a huge mess as well.

A curious catfish, only a little larger than a mundane one, drifts lazily by to investigate my presence, and to gobble up any tasty bits dredged up by my passing. I stop moving, and very so slowly bend towards it. After creeping close enough, I whip out and grab it, just tight enough to hold onto it without hurting it.

“Sorry, pal. But I’m probably doing you a favor. You’d just vanish wherever Fractures go when they close.” I tell it.

I carefully aim towards where Second Squad is, and send them a radio burst. It has to be short and powerful to be heard through the interference caused by magical materials, which includes the lake water.

“Callisto, catch!”

I hurl the squirming fish out of the water and towards the shore where I can barely see Second Squad working to pull samples out from the lake under the careful eye of the Bradley.

Callisto’s head snaps up as soon as she hears my radio transmission, and she stumbles backwards to catch the flying catfish. She barely manages to get a grip on it, and barks at Eric who quickly scoops up some lake water in a cell. She forces the catfish into the canister and clicks the lid tightly shut.

She shakes a fist in my direction and mouths curses at me. Her radio is too weak for it to burn through the interference. The distortion caused by the water means I can only barely read her lips.

“Give…heads-up…bastard!”

I chuckle, and turn back towards the center of the lake. The farther I go, the deeper it gets. Even the clear water begins to turn dark, and the surface becomes a distorted mess of shadows and reflections. I send out subsonic pings, making use of my sonar capabilities.

It seems like the few fish I can see notice the noise, and they all bolt away into the murk. At the center of the lake, nearly a hundred feet deep—that’s pretty deep for a lake, right?—is a long, rough looking object. It could be a boulder of some kind. It’s rather out of place, and I can’t really tell. Sonar doesn’t give me the resolution or other kinds of information to truly identify it.

Just before I get within visual range of the mystery object, something hidden in the mud crunches under my book, and I look down. It’s a snake skeleton, or at least what’s left of one. Most of the skeleton is missing, with only the head and a couple of feet of spine behind. What is there is cracked and broken too, and not just from me. Teeth marks are easily visible on the bones. Big ones.

Uh oh.

I ping with the sonar again, and what I thought was a rock shakes and bolts for me. It’s a massive crocodile—Crocodire?—three times the size of the already giant snakes.

It bolts for me, swimming faster than a speed boat. I try to get my sword in the way, but it’s far more nimble under water than I am, despite its size. It races out of the gloom, jaws wide. It chomps down around me, and rips me out of the mud. It shakes back and forth while rolling, trying to rip me apart.

Great, this again.

At a bad angle and unable to use my sword, I awkwardly slug it in the side of the head. I can hear the crack of bone despite the weak hit, and it squeals. It throws me up and out of the water. It’s much weaker than the Alligraver. Just thinking about that name makes me cringe. It even made it into the final After-Action Report.

I breach the surface with a splash, tumbling wildly. I twist my body, canceling some rotation and adding some more in another direction.

I love the conservation of momentum. And an internal flight control computer.

I slam into the bank, boots first, and slide backwards in the mud. I sling the algae laden muck all around. It coats my armor, though sloughs of my cloak, leaving it pristine.

I come to a stop coincidentally right next to Callisto. I look down at her, and she grumpily wipes some of the mud off her face.

“Sergeant, you need to get away from the water. There’s a Crocodire in there.”

She blinks only once before jumping into action.

“Everyone get back, now! Mean stuff in the water, gogogo!”

Second Squad scrambles backwards, and Eric slips in the mud. His brother goes to pull him out. The Crocodire leaps from the water with a roar, clearly more intent on an easier meal than me.

Too bad, asshole.

I leap forward, spraying mud behind me again. I grab both jaws, and swing it to the side. It goes flying into the forest of tree-sized cattails, shattering their stems with a crunch and knocking them down. I charge after, blade now in hand.

It lunges out of the dense foliage, and I slam my sword into its mouth, carving deep into it before a thick leg hammers me into the mud.

No friggin’ leverage against the ground here!

It still lets loose a hideous scream as blood pours from its mangled jaws, though it fights through the pain. It still tries to bite and scrabble at me, though it makes more of a mess than anything. Blood and mud mixes together and coats my armor.

I throw it off me and onto its back. I struggle out of the mud, though with great difficulty. The cloak’s enchantment only focuses on my boots, which is why it’s so powerful. With focus and specificity comes strength.

Both myself and the Crocodire get onto our feet at the same time, though it’s clearly more tired than I am. Blood still pours from the grievous injury I dealt earlier, though for what is clearly a 2-Star boss, it’s far from mortal. It charges me, and I dodge to the side. I grab it by the shoulder, lift it up and spin it around. I wrap my arms around it and give it a bear hug. I’m sure it looks almost comical, considering the Crocodire is three times longer than I am tall.

Its ribs crack and splinter, and more blood, mixed with chunks of lung and flesh now, bubbles out of its mouth. It gurgles and tries to struggle, but it’s far too late for that now. Even monster durability has its limits, and I’ve pushed it well beyond those.

I grab its head and twist. It’s neck breaks with a sickening snap, and it goes limp. The exit and reward orb appear like they always do.

“Well, holy shit Mach, that was one hell of a show,“ Tom says over the radio.

“Thanks, Tom. Well, do you guys want to go now?”

“*That’s up to you. You’re the boss here. I can tell you we have a tiny bit of room left on the Strykers for a few more samples, but not a whole lot. Did you see anywhere else you’d like to go?”

I prepare the dead monster for storage, chopping it up into smaller pieces while I talk. Eric and Jericho both drag over some of the few remaining crates and load them up with meat, hide, and bone.

“Hm, no, this was the only other interesting thing here. This Fracture had a lot of space but not a lot of interesting places.”

Sergeant Callisto cuts in. “What about the glass roof? The walls on the horizon? Shouldn’t we investigate those?” She asks.

“I doubt we can. Just because we can see them doesn’t mean they’re real, or reachable. It’s a hard thing to grasp, I think. Fractures are so limited but they look like they aren’t.”

“It’s got to be something, right? How could it just not be real?”

“I don’t know,” I tell her honestly. *“I don’t have the answers to your questions, I wish I did. But I can tell you I’m sure that if we just drove for as long as we could, until the gas tanks were empty, and walked until you dropped, we’d never reach the walls.

“The boundaries Fractures have are different every time. Sometimes they’re obvious walls of mist, or thick stone walls that refuse to shatter, or fences that are always in the way. Sometimes they’re just holes into nothingness. But they’re boundaries all the same. Just because we can’t see them doesn’t mean they’re not there.”

“Okay, Mach,” Tom says. “If that’s everything, let’s get out of here. I’m getting a tad low on ammo, anyway.”

“You’d have stayed if I asked? Despite being low on ammo?” I ask.

“I keep telling you you’re the boss, dude. Look, man, when I say I’d follow you into Hell, I mean it.”

I don’t know how to feel about my friend—are we still friends?—basically telling me he’d die if I asked him to. So I won’t.

Thread terminated.

I store the Crocodire’s carcass away, and then use a winch hook off the Bradley to fish the lily pad I pulled up earlier out of the lake. I have to carry that out by hand.

Second Squad just climbs onto the vehicles instead of getting inside the cramped vehicles full of samples and haphazardly tossed equipment. They managed to wash off most of the foul smelling bug guts using the lake water. As I follow behind the vehicles as they roll out back onto Earth, I catch Callisto glancing at the sky. She sighs and turns away.

Some questions will never be answered.
