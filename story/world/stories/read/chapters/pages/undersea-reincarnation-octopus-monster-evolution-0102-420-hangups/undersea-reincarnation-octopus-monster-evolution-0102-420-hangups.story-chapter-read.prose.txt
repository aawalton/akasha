The blood of her people was already billowing by the time her barriers managed to materialize. It spread out like a creeping red fog, mirroring her own doubt and dread, while the target she wished to capture already slipped away. She had been too late to act. Again. This time, it was understandable, as the kraken had attacked faster than she had expected, and while he’d sent his real body down to the team that had come to support their extraction, he’d already had invisible clones lurking around them. She hadn’t noticed those, even though she’d scanned for them. Which was yet another mistake on her part.

She should have used more essence.

By now, there were so many of her mistakes that she couldn’t just barrel through. They were weighing heavier on her by the second. How had it all gone so wrong? No—that wasn’t the right question to ask. The real question was staring her down, and Sel’Veth couldn’t ignore it any longer.

Why had she let the situation deteriorate this badly? When had she dropped the first rock that had started the landslide?

The kraken flitted away from the cage she wanted to trap him in, leaving severed limbs and broken legs in his wake as he dodged the charge of Gar’Dak’s shark. The kraken dodged easily enough, and Sel’Veth’s throat tightened as she cast Circle of Regeneration under her people, lest they bleed out, while the mantis shrimp sprang forward. It disappeared mid-lunge, wrapped by the skill of the anglerfish, but that wasn’t enough to catch the kraken.

It had just teleported away again. Sel’Veth considered putting her artifact back on, but that last stunt had burned at least half of her reserves. She was wary of burning the second half, even if it might give them an opening.

The kraken had already proven he could abuse it.

Her gaze flicked back to her people. In just the blink of an eye, her squad, four warriors and each of them A-rank, were out of the fight. Kael might be able to regenerate her severed arm in half a minute or so and get back into the fray, but Sel’Veth knew this wasn’t the kind of fight that’d last that long.

Rattling detonations echoed as the mantis shrimp flitted around the cave after the kraken, dodging the imploding orbs the crab was leaving in their wake. She knew she had made the wrong call somewhere, but she couldn’t back away now, and as she tracked the flitting specter and dodged away from two whipping limbs, Sel’Veth tried to find the turning point that had started this downward spiral. Was it because she’d let Gar’Dak be the one who watched over the kraken while it evolved? She had been obligated to, to keep an eye on the humans and on the other more dangerous creatures of the Deepways, and she had warned him not to attempt the domination skill, over and over, and she thought she had gotten him to understand the risk, but in retrospect? She couldn’t deny the glint of greed she saw in his eyes. As a beastmaster, of course he’d want the kraken in his menagerie. That was Gar’Dak’s whole identity. You couldn’t float a piece of bloodied meat in front of a shark and be surprised when it went for it.

She wished the Depth Lord had sent her alone. She neither had the strength nor the authority to stop her fellow Guardian. Still. Was that the point that ruined everything? Or did it happen even before that? Was it because she’d sensed the humans tracking the kraken and feared he’d align with them instead of her and her people? His friend, the hermit crab, had been wearing a human disguise. That had been a possibility that was only reinforced when she saw the tunneler and his people.

They had come down here with the shadow of their failure following after them, and every action, every inaction she had made had only made that defeat more assured. Now, it was a question whether they’d be able to capture the kraken and eventually get him to listen or… what? Part ways and hope for the best? Sever its future before it grew strong enough to choke theirs out?

Krakens weren’t known for their forgiveness. She had heard of how humans considered crows and elephants dangerous for how they could nurse grudges, but humans rarely had to face and be tormented by krakens.

Worse and worse decisions kept flashing into her mind. The best would be to just leave, but even that felt like too little and too late. No matter what happened here, the Depth Lord was going to have their hides for this. They had strayed from the original orders. Well. Gar’Dak had, with how hostile he’d been at first, before the attempt at dominating the kraken. And she’d been too much in her head to stop that. Even then, before the attempt to order the kraken, they might have been able to mollify him. Now though?

Sel’Veth hit Gar’Dak with a Hasten skill, which should give him enough speed to keep up with the six clones hounding him and with the other hand, she tried to hit the kraken with a Slow, but he just teleported away and let another of his weaker clones take it.

Unsalvageable, the words echoed in the back of her mind, but she ignored them, focusing on the clones. Three kinds of clones. Maybe four, she noted, still aware of her surroundings lest they sneak up on her. Sel’Veth continued lobbing debuff after debuff in the hopes of hitting the kraken or the crab. She could still tell which one her real target was just from the strength of his aura which… was probably because of their lack of experience.

That thought almost had her drop her arm as her heart hammered in her chest. The kraken had just hit A-rank, and it was holding up the assault of two guardians and three A-rank monsters that would terrorize most.

A shiver ran down her spine. He was a monster, and with every turn, they antagonized him a little more.

The situation couldn’t be salvaged. At least, not by her, and certainly not by Gar’Dak. This was beyond her, and she wasn’t even sure if they were running from the humans or working with them. In either case, she might need to trigger her emergency measure, and she’d worry later about what that’d cost her. Still, as she continued her casting, empowering her ally and his pets, she kept having the same thought: They should have just left the kraken alone. They had only made things worse by following after him.
