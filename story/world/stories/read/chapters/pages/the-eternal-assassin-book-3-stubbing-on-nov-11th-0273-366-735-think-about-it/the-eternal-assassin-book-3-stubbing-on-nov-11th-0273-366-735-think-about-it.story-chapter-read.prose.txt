“Asher!” Brian shouted, smiling as Asher popped into the isolated room located somewhere within the hidden truthseeker academy. Asher had kept his promise to Donvath not to try and figure out where the academy was located, but he knew it was somewhere below the capital. Brian leapt from his chair and tackled him around the waist like always, and Asher smiled as he returned the enthusiastic hug.

“Glad to see you too,” he said, ruffling Brian’s hair affectionately. “One second.”

With that, Asher tossed up a Spatial Lock, focusing on Samantha’s Eternal Mark and using Recall to warp them back to the palace courtyard. Instantly, the bare, boring room that was used as their pick up and drop off point for Brian was replaced with snow-covered trees and brisk, outdoor air.

“Brian!” Samantha scolded, her warm smile quickly being replaced with the frown of a disappointed caretaker. “Where is your coat? You’re going to freeze dressed like that!”

“I don’t have a coat,” Brian pointed out, causing Samantha to blink in surprise. Brian only had the few possessions they themselves had supplied him. It wasn’t as if anyone else in the truthseeker academy was planning on going outside anytime soon. Brian was a unique case, and Samantha sighed when she realized the blame for his lack of appropriate winter clothing actually fell on her.

“In that case, I know the first thing we’re going to do today,” she said, handing Brian her too-large gloves in the meantime. “Time to get you some more clothes.”

“What?! What’s wrong with what I have on?!”

Asher could only laugh as the siblings began bickering while they walked, and he smiled at the sight of them reunited once more. Brian had been in the truthseeker academy for months now, yet the one day a week they got to spend with him never failed to fill his chest with warmth. He loved both Samantha and Brian individually, but the two of them were also a package deal. Getting to spend time with them both like this was always a nice change of pace from all the deadly battles and vigorous training that filled the other six days of his week.

Despite Brian’s knee-jerk reaction against clothes shopping, the twelve-year-old quickly grew excited when Samantha brought him to the largest clothing shop in the capital and let him pick out whatever he wanted. She did exert her veto power a few times, such as when she rejected a winter coat that had what looked like actual antlers sewn onto the back for whatever insane reason, but by the end, everyone was happy.

“Does this mean we’re still rich?” Brian asked as they walked out of the shop. Rubbing his appropriately-sized gloves together in glee, Brian’s eyes twinkled while he waited for an answer.

“We’re doing just fine,” Samantha said, at the same moment Asher answered with ‘Filthy rich.’ As Asher responded to his fiancé’s glare with an apologetic grin, Brian jumped up and down excitedly.

“Awesome! Does that mean I can ask for whatever I want for my birthday?”

“Birthday?” Asher blinked, turning toward Samantha in confusion. He figured she would have mentioned something as important as that. “It’s his birthday?”

“Not for another month it isn’t,” Samantha said, frowning down at her brother. “And no, you cannot ask for whatever you want. The last thing I need is to try and figure out how to explain to the residents of a small town that the new lord in charge of their castle is a thirteen-year-old boy. You have to pick something reasonable.”

“You’re no fun,” Brian pouted, turning to his much easier target. “Asher, wanna go check out the Grand Auction? I haven’t been in a while!”

“Sure, I don’t mind,” Asher shrugged, immediately realizing that was the wrong answer when he spotted the furrow on Samantha’s brow. “…And all of a sudden, I do mind. Sorry, Brian, looks like the Grand Auction is a no-go today. Anything else you wanted to do?”

“Sam!” Brian shouted, whirling on his sister and glaring up at her. “Stop butting into everything!”

“Stop trying to throw around shards that aren’t yours!” she countered, narrowing her eyes at him. “I think these last few months in the truthseeker academy and your vacations with us have changed you, and not for the better. Have you already forgotten what life was like for us just a few months ago?”

“I’m trying to!” Brian shouted, tears welling up in his eyes as he stared defiantly back at his sister. Before she could respond, Brian turned and took off, running surprisingly fast for his age as he vanished amongst the crowd of heavily clothed shoppers wandering about the city.

“Brian!” Samantha shouted. Brilliant emerald wings of pure tritessence burst out of her back without warning, eliciting gasps from the surrounding crowd. But right before she could take off, Asher stopped her with a hand on her shoulder.

“Maybe give him a few minutes to cool off,” he suggested, vividly recalling the fights he’d had with his own parents when he was Brian’s age. While Samantha was technically Brian's sister and not his mother, she’d filled both roles, having raised him since he was only four. “I’ve got my Eternal Mark on him, he’s not going anywhere. If a deranged murderer stabs him for no reason, we’ll run over to Donvath and make him undo it. If anything, the fact that Donvath didn’t just call me and instruct me to stop Brian means nothing bad is going to happen to him.”

That last part wasn't necessarily true, what with how much trouble Donvath had viewing the future when Asher was directly involved, but he still felt reasonably safe letting Brian run a few shops down.

Samantha looked like she wanted to argue, but a single look into his concerned eyes made her pause. Taking a deep breath, she let it out, deflating slightly as she leaned against him.

“…Do you think I’m wrong?”

“I don’t think you’re wrong… but you probably could have been a bit less overbearing. I wasn’t about to start buying him anything he wanted at the Grand Auction.”

“I know,” she sighed. “Even so, I don’t think all the private booths and special privileges normally reserved for the heads of noble families and literal royalty are what he needs right now. I’m just worried about him. I’ve watched him my entire life, but after only a few months of this new living arrangement, I feel like I barely know him anymore. Like I took my eyes off him for just a little while and he’s not the little boy I once took care of.”

“Samantha. He’s turning thirteen,” Asher said, chuckling at the confused look she gave him. “It’s not going to happen overnight, but he’s growing from a child into a man. He’d be changing even if he was still back in Horntho Village living the same as he always had. It’s normal for teenagers to go through phases where they hate their parents and their siblings and think they’re the worst things ever created, and you’re both of those two things to him.”

“He doesn’t seem to be mad at you,” Samantha grunted.

“That’s because I’m basically the fun, rich uncle,” Asher grinned, laughing at her annoyed eye roll. “But that works for us. When Brian’s emotions flare and he gets upset with you, I can be there for him as someone to talk to. And when he realizes he misses you and wants to apologize, I can help walk him through how to do it.”

“I guess that’s fair,” Samantha said, looking out over the crowd as if hunting for her brother. Asher could tell from his Eternal Mark that Brian hadn’t gone far, and had actually ducked into one of the nearby shops to presumably get out of the cold, but he didn’t say anything. With Brian growing up, every week now he expected him to declare he was too old for Asher’s Eternal Mark and demand he remove it from him. Doing so would certainly make their weekly visits a bit trickier, and Asher didn’t want to give Brian any reason to make that decision any sooner than he had to.

“…Realms, I’m going to have to have the talk with him soon, aren’t I?” Samantha muttered, before tossing him a side-eyed look. “Though maybe that would be better delivered by a certain fun, rich uncle.”

Asher’s smile fell from his face in record time, and he coughed as he brushed the snow out of his hair and stepped away. “You know, I should probably go check on him and make sure he’s okay. Meet up in a bit?”

“Don’t think you’re getting out of this that easily,” Samantha said, smiling at him as she stepped after him and gave him a loving kiss. “Come find me whenever he’s ready.”

Samantha’s wings faded just as quickly as they’d appeared, and Asher watched her walk off. He took a moment to admire the confidence with which she held herself even without her bow strung along her shoulder, and he smiled before turning to catch up with Brian.

Despite not wanting to draw attention to his Eternal Mark, Asher didn’t want to insult Brian’s intelligence either. He knew that Brian knew about his mark, which meant pretending to call out his name and look for him would probably just make the boy mad. Instead, Asher stepped into the store, breathing a sigh of relief at the warm air that seemed to be coming from a large glowing ruby jutting out of the ceiling. The capital certainly had its luxuries, and Asher wasn’t going to complain about them. If he was feeling this cold even through Complete Resilience, he couldn’t imagine how much worse everyone else had it.

The shop Brian had ducked into looked like one specializing in walking sticks of all things, and Asher shook his head when he found Brian fidgeting at a bin filled with sticks as tall as he was. Even if he was slowly growing into a man, the actual growing part had yet to come.

“I think you’re a couple years too early for that.”

Brian jumped at the sound of his voice, turning to offer Asher a sheepish grin as he placed the stick he’d been holding back in the bin. “Yeah… you’re probably not wrong. Where’s Sam?”

“I don’t know,” Asher shrugged, noting the raised eyebrow Brian was giving him. “I’m serious. Just because I can tell she's about a quarter mile to the east, doesn’t mean I know what store she’s in. For all I know she’s off enjoying the best warm doughnuts the capital has to offer and eating our shares as well.”

“She wouldn’t do that,” Brian argued. “She’s too thoughtful. Even now, when she has all the shards in the world, I bet she barely spends any on herself.”

“She does struggle with that a little…” Asher admitted, staring at Brian in shock as it all clicked. “Brian… are you trying to get Samantha to spend shards on herself?”

“Yes!” Brian all but shouted, looking at him like he was an idiot. “Sam’s never had the shards to treat herself to anything nice, and now that she does, she’s still too afraid to! She’s amazing, and she deserves to be happy. I thought maybe if I could get her to grow more comfortable spending shards on me first, she’d realize it’s not the end of the world and start buying things that make her happy as well. But no matter how hard I try, I can’t seem to help her.”

Man, children can be weirdly perceptive at times, Asher thought, pinching the brow of his nose as he tried to wrap his head around all this. “…So you’re telling me you don’t want a castle?”

“Of course I want a castle!” Brian scoffed, frowning as he looked up at him. “That would be awesome! Sam doesn’t even have a house anymore, and you can’t burn down a castle!”

Asher’s breath caught when the truth behind why Brian kept floating this idea about purchasing a castle came to light, and his heart went out to the poor boy as memories of Horntho Village burning to the ground flashed through his mind. Stepping forward, he surprised Brian by pulling him into a tight hug, causing his eyes to widen at the sudden motion.

“I can promise you, Brian, Samantha’s plenty happy right now,” he said, letting up after a moment and holding Brian at arm’s length. “You’re safe and getting the training you want, she’s got all her skills and is improving them with every day, and the work we’re doing matters. Not just here, but across the entire continent. Even if there’s something Samantha wants but it’s expensive enough to make her hesitate, you better believe I’d buy it for her anyway. You don’t need to worry about that.”

“Okay… that does make me feel better,” Brian said, before blinking as if he only just heard what Asher had said. “Wait, all of her skills? Are you serious?!”

“Oh yeah, she’s even evolved a bunch of them at this point,” Asher admitted, laughing at the excitement in Brian’s eyes. “I’m sure she’d be more than happy to tell you what she’s capable of if you ask.”

“We have to find her, right now!” Brian said, all but dragging him away from the walking sticks and the annoyed-looking salesman who appeared to be debating whether or not he should interrupt their talk to ask them to leave. “I can’t believe she didn’t tell me!”

“Must have slipped her mind,” Asher said, nodding a silent thank you to the salesman even as he let himself be dragged back out into the cold. While Brian demanded to know what direction she’d gone, Asher could only laugh and help direct him. Part of him kept expecting the world to explode at any moment, and he could only hope that they at least had this one last day of uneventful peace. After everything they’d been through, the two siblings deserved that much.
