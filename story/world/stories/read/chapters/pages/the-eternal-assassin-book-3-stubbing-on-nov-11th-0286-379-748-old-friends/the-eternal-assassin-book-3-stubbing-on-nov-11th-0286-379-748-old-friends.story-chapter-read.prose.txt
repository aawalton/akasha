Having spent a couple of days living there himself after the destruction of Horntho Village, Asher recognized Draken’s manor the moment he popped into it. Rosh was currently huddled in a corner, doing his best to read a book. But based on the shaking in his fingers and how his eyes kept darting over to the door, Asher had a feeling he wasn’t getting a ton of reading done. For the first time, rather than scream at his arrival, Rosh let out a sigh of relief when he popped into existence beside him.

“Oh, Asher, thank the realms you’re here! Is this what you were warning me about the other day?!”

“Yep. I’m glad you decided to heed my advice and are staying at the manor,” Asher said, slipping into the astral just long enough to poke his head out the window. The streets were largely empty, but there were a couple of people running around, desperately trying to find places to hide out. He could still hear those distant screams, but it wasn’t as if people were dying in the street at the very least. Pulling his head back, he turned to Rosh and returned to the material. “What’s happened?”

“I don’t know, the entire city was put into lockdown without warning by the Frent family,” Rosh explained as he tossed his book onto the bed and got to his feet. “Since then, there have been screams coming from the wall. Asher, this is the second ring of the city. If we can hear screams coming from the city wall…”

“The first ring has either already fallen, or is under attack,” Asher finished, his fears confirmed.

“Attack by what? What’s trying to wipe out the city?!”

“A lich. From the Council of Death,” Asher admitted, nodding at the look of utter terror on Rosh’s face. “Don’t worry, that’s what I’m here for. I know you’ve done your best to keep your nose out of my life before now, but I’ve been hunting the Council down for some time now. Almost a dozen liches have fallen at my hand, and this one is about to join them.”

For his part, Rosh actually managed to take in the fact that Asher was hunting liches in relative stride. If anything, the knowledge seemed to calm him down somewhat, and he let out a shaky breath before nodding.

“Well, I can’t say that’s what I was expecting to hear when you finally broke and told me about your adventures. I figured you were working private security for the High Prince, or something along those lines. But lich killer? I suppose I can see it.”

“You have the strangest amount of faith in me,” Asher chuckled, his chest warming as he realized his mere presence here was enough to calm Rosh down from his panic. “Whatever you do, stay inside the manor. If the lich has already managed to breach the first ring, there’s no telling when they’ll be able to do the same with the second. The streets aren’t safe.”

“Trust me, I’m not planning to go anywhere,” Rosh scoffed, as if the idea was utterly insane. “You go do your daring deeds and be all special. I’m going to sit here and try to come up with ideas for my curriculum next semester. Oh, I’m getting back into teaching, by the way! My old academy had a position open, and when I went to apply, they said they’d be happy to take me back!”

“I mean this with complete sincerity, Rosh, I want nothing more than to sit down and hear all about that,” Asher said, smiling proudly at his friend even as he pulled out his daggers and mentally prepared himself. “But I have a lich to kill and a city to save first. Rain check for once I’m done?”

“I suppose I can wait. I’ll see about grabbing some of those cookies that go well with your favorite tea blend. You know, assuming that shop isn’t destroyed before you finish.”

“Sounds good!” Asher said, giving him one last pointed look. “Stay inside!”

With that, Asher slipped into the astral and darted out of Draken’s manor. As much as he wanted to check on Draken and the others, he’d wasted enough time as it was. Recovering more of his energy had been important, but still, with every scream that reached his ears from the first ring, he couldn’t help but wonder if he shouldn’t have just sprinted headfirst toward the next lich as fast as possible.

As he ran, a powerful, chest-rumbling roar from the heavens stopped him dead in his tracks. Asher forgot all about the lich attacking the city, or the people who needed him for a split second as he looked to the sky and spotted something he’d been waiting for ever since appearing in this world filled with magic and wonder.

A dragon.

It was hard to tell just how large the dragon was from down on the ground, but based on the beast’s powerful wings and the force of its roar, he had the feeling it was pretty big. The beast’s scales were jet black with a dark blue hue running down its spine, and before Asher could take in any more of its majestic appearance, it opened its mouth.

Unleashing death and destruction upon its foes.

Asher’s eyes were wide as dinner plates as he stared at the literal wall of flame that descended from the dragon. Based on the angle, the flames had to be aimed at the outskirts of the city, and the dragon kept the fire up for a few seconds while it flew in a large arc up in the sky. From the width of the fiery gout, Asher could only imagine that a good chunk of the city’s exterior had just been utterly incinerated, and he shook his head as he forced himself to keep running. If Winged Death was letting out that kind of literal firepower and the first ring had fallen nonetheless…

Just what kind of force were they fighting here?”

He got his answer soon enough. Running from rooftop to rooftop, he made his way to the top of the wall dividing the first and second ring with the aid of his boots of Levitation. As soon as he landed on top, his breath caught when he took in the state of the first ring.

The outer portion of the city lay completely in ruin.

Everywhere he looked there were people running for their lives, chased by swarms of undead that he’d never seen before. Zombies darted around with far more speed than should be possible, their rotting bodies supported and enhanced with freshly grown plants that seemed almost grafted into their form. There had to be thousands of people running for their lives everywhere Asher could see, because there were tens of thousands of the horrifying plant zombies flooding after them. Despite the wall of dragon fire that was still burning around a good quarter of the city’s exterior, the zombies were pouring in over the city’s outermost walls with seemingly no end to them. Occasionally he would spot zombies far larger than the rest—eight-foot-tall behemoths with bulging muscles that were capable of smashing straight through walls as they chased after fleeing civilians. As he looked upon the remains and chaos of the first layer of Whikoga, Asher somehow knew without a shadow of a doubt he’d found his main target.

Lethinal is here… Somewhere.

Unfortunately, Asher didn’t have the faintest idea where the lich could be hiding.

But he had a pretty good idea of who might be able to help him find him.

Asher raced along the top of the wall dividing the first and second ring, gritting his teeth as he kept himself from leaping down and rushing to the aid of those fleeing from the zombies. Everywhere he looked he witnessed people being torn apart or crushed as the zombies reached them, and as much as he wanted to help, he knew the best way for him to help would be rooting out the source of all this evil and putting them down for good. Lethinal’s death should stop the zombie horde in its tracks, or at least make them far less of a threat. At the moment, the zombies were running around in coordinated groups and pushing toward the center of the city. Without someone directing them, they’d lose their cohesion.

Eventually, he reached the main entrance leading from the city’s first ring into the second, and there he found just the man he was looking for.

“Keep moving, don’t stop!” Bordan shouted at the fleeing civilians while they raced for the safety of the second ring. The powerfully built truthseeker was cutting down zombies left and right as they tried to rush past him and go for the defenseless people of Whikoga, but Bordan wasn’t having any part of that. His greatsword shone in the early light of the rising sun as it swung through the air, every slash calculated and every strike dispatching anywhere from two to three zombies, if not more. One of the large zombies roared as it lunged at him, but Bordan didn’t even flinch. His greatsword changed its trajectory instantly, arcing down and cleaving the undead in half as easily as if Bordan were tapping a child on the head.

At his side, Clare was working just as hard. Four glowing chains of violet were whipping around almost too fast to keep track of, smacking zombies back and grabbing civilians who had stumbled and fallen to the ground in the chaotic rush for safety. She was keeping people from getting trampled by their own kind, all but throwing them over her shoulder to a team of guards waiting farther back.

Bordan and Clare weren’t the only ones fighting in the defense of Whikoga by any means, but they were the ones Asher was hunting for. Ignoring the cluster of nobles who were backing the truthseekers up, he dropped down, landing beside Bordan just in time to elongate Crescent and slice through a giant undead rushing him from the side.

“Hey, Bordan! Long time no see!”

“Reaper!” Bordan said, barely sparing him a second glance before turning his attention back on the attacking horde and cutting his way through another three zombies. “Did the High Prince send you?”

“Something like that,” Asher admitted while using Distortion to stab a zombie from twenty feet away that was about to latch its teeth into someone’s neck. “I need your help. There’s a lich controlling this undead army somewhere, but I have no way of finding it. I was hoping you might be able to help me with that.”

“I am a little busy holding the line here,” Bordan pointed out, as if that weren’t obvious. “Regardless, I can’t help you. I don’t have the skills required for that sort of divining, and liches are naturally resistant to many of the skills my Truth element does grant me. You need to find someone who is specialized in bypassing those sorts of defenses if you want any hope of uncovering where they are hiding!”

“I don’t suppose Clare might be that sort of person?” Asher asked hopefully, glancing to the side just in time to watch two of Clare’s chains wrap around a large zombie and tear it in half. The truthseeker’s apprentice then hurled each half at two different approaching hordes, knocking them down like bowling pins as zombies were sent flying in all directions.

“She is not,” Bordan confirmed, cleaving through another few zombies. “Your best bet would be searching among the nobles. They have a wide variety of skills due to their unique elements. The combat-focused ones are scattered throughout the first ring, fighting back against the horde, but ones with the skill set you’re looking for are probably still hunkered down within the fourth ring!”

As soon as the words left Bordan’s mouth, Asher froze. The truthseeker was forced to step closer and cut through a zombie that tried to take advantage of his pause to sink its teeth into his neck, but Asher almost didn’t notice. In fact, he actually knew of one noble in particular who had the power of bypassing concealing skills thanks to those freaky black voids for eyes of hers. He’d done everything in his power to bring an end to her life at one point, stopped only by Gran and her promise that the noble wouldn’t reveal his secrets to the world. Now, it looked like it was a good thing he hadn’t killed her.

Leighann might very well be the key to figuring out where the leader of the Council of Death was hiding.
