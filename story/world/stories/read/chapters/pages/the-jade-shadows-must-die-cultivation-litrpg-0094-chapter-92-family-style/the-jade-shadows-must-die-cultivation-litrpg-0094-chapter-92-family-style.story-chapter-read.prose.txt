After the lesson, they finished up what little remained of their hour with some actual physical training. Like most elements of his progress, Rix’s combat style wasn’t an immediate priority, but there was no sense wasting the time they had with Master Zhen. In truth, he’d been somewhat neglecting the physical side of things since hitting Mid Rank. In an ideal world, he’d get a more advanced style than his current one before dedicating more time to pursuing martial improvement.

But even with that as an end goal, there was work he could do now. When his style ranked up last, Master Zhen had told him that the jump from Mid Rank to High Rank was all about a connection to the body. It required a deep physical familiarity with every element of the style, and while much of that would come from ceaseless repetition, like everything else, it was also grounded in martial fundamentals.

Rix could do the stationary thousand blows perfectly every time now. He’d been proud the first time he’d pulled that off, despite the Weaponmaster doing his best to pop that bubble.

“Such expressions are for genuine achievement,” he’d said, noticing Rix’s smile, “not for reaching the absolute baseline of competence.”

While that sort of tangible progress felt good, in the intensity of his last couple of months in the Farm, such training had taken a back seat. He’d continued to work on the mobility drills Master Zhen had given him, but it hadn’t been with the same fervor as before.

That would need to change for him to take the next step.

Given they had limited time, for now, Master Zhen just added another two katas to Rix’s suite of things to work on.

“In the Farm, you had to teach your body to strike,” he said. “To progress further, you must teach it to fight.”

While Rix still nursed some of that long-held bias against fighting without his weapon, having been disarmed temporarily in the battle with Han had taught him the value of being at least competent with fists alone. And it was clear by now that Master Zhen genuinely knew what he was talking about. If the man said this was what was needed, then that’s what Rix would do.

The new katas were more holistic. There was less focus on striking and more on the flow of the body through space. When the attacks did come, they were more varied, utilizing kicks for the first time, but also elbows and knees. Most of these movements felt far less intuitive to Rix than throwing a simple punch.

What was most surprising was Luna, who had previously taken no interest in any of Master Zhen’s unarmed lessons, actually stopped working on her sword forms to come and join them.

“These look different to what they taught us back home,” she said, by way of a defense. “Our lessons were good, but they were very focused on our particular style. Lots of fast punches and low kicks, but none of this other stuff.” She tapped her elbow. “Could be useful.”

Rix cocked his head. It felt obvious now, but it had never occurred to him before that there would actually be different ways to learn unarmed combat, just as there were different ways to wield a weapon.

He turned to Master Zhen. “What sort of style are you teaching us? Is this what they teach you in Ironguard?”

The man snorted. “Hardly. The corporations understand the importance of a strong foundation, but much like the girl’s sect, they teach a more streamlined approach.” The derision in his voice said how much he thought of that. “They believe unarmed combat is something to be tolerated in service of your System-led growth, rather than a valuable mode of battle.”

“But you don’t,” Rix offered.

The older man grimaced. “The Zhen family is not the most powerful of the Thousand-Year Families, but we are one of the oldest. My forebears would often speak of a time when the Martial Path was more than just what was written on your System display. It was a journey of martial excellence in every sense. We have always chosen to live by that ideology, however unpopular it has become.” He paused, as if weighing whether to continue. “It is one of their styles that I teach you now. The Bladeless Way.”

The Bladeless Way. Rix was almost embarrassed by the giddy rush of excitement he felt upon hearing that name. It sounded like a real style from the Chronicles. A world away from Foundational Quarterstaff Basics. It may ‘only’ have been an unarmed style, but that didn’t take away from the significance of it.

“We are both honored you would share that with us elder,” said Rix reverently.

Luna nodded. “We really are. Also, my elders would hate that I’m learning something foreign, so that’s a plus.”

“Does Spiritlock know that’s what you’re teaching their inmates?” asked Rix.

“I don’t particularly care what my seniors think,” Master Zhen replied. “There is little else they can do to me. At least this way I can take some small measure of satisfaction that perhaps I am leaving the world with a fraction less mediocrity than when I found it.” One side of his mouth twitched. “Though seeing the two of you stumble through the forms like freshly born calves, I may need to revise that assessment.”

Rix grinned back. The man’s barbs had long since lost their bite for him. “We’ll endeavor to do better, elder.”

The man nodded. “I should be clear that I cannot hope to convey to you the fullness of my family’s knowledge in this place. We have neither the time nor the resources. All I can do is give you some small guidance and hope it stands you in good stead.”

“It already has, elder,” Rix replied.

He had little doubt he’d have fallen long ago without Master Zhen’s guidance. He didn’t know why the man was so different than most of their captors, but he was thankful for it.

***

Their dive that day involved more of the same. Luna continued to burn in her meridians, while Rix practiced the Torrential Cycle. The reality of their accelerated timeline seemed to have settled over Breaker, because the man was snappy and terse, even by his standards. It didn’t make Rix’s job particularly easy.

The breakthrough he’d had in his cell the previous night meant he was consistently able to spin the technique up. With every attempt, he found the visualization came a little more easily and felt a little more real. But maintaining it once qi began to enter was still a challenging proposition, especially with Breaker barking orders to speed up in his ear. He made some small headway, but despite six grueling hours of work, he never reached the pace that would actually create the centrifugal force to grow his capacity. Keeping a mental hold on the qi as it looped felt like trying to guide a laden cart down a hill. At a steady pace, it was manageable, but at some point the momentum just wrenched away all control.

By the end of the session, Breaker’s face was set in a permanent scowl.

“I’ll keep working at it as much as I can tomorrow,” Rix promised him.

The man didn’t reply. He’d found it particularly galling that they’d miss a training session for their ‘childish fake duels’, but there was nothing Rix or Luna could do. The prison didn’t dive on arena day.

Though he didn’t express it, Rix was very much looking forward to what the next day held. It was the first opportunity he was going to have to speak to Kokuryu. He had a great many things on his mind, but chief among them was their escape, and this was finally a chance to start planning it.

***

Kokuryu seemed equally eager to meet. Rix had expected to have to fight before the man would call on him to offer sponsorship, but he was summoned before the first Whisper bout had even begun.

The Wellspring representative was waiting for him below the arena in a room that might as well have been identical to the last one they’d shared. Though his expression was neutral, there was a tightness to his eyes that spoke of a sense of worry. On the table, he’d already laid the two small blocks of wood that formed the privacy array.

The man looked Rix up and down, and then some of the tension bled out of him. “Well, you don’t seem to have been harmed.”

“You should see my insides,” Rix deadpanned, sliding into the spare seat. “I take it you heard what happened.”

“Rumors,” Kokuryu replied. “From Tolson and others like him. First, I should say congratulations on toppling Xu Han. I’ve seen the man fight in the ring. That was no small task. In truth, I was somewhat convinced I would not see you again once you revealed your situation.”

“It was a close thing. Opening my Mountain Gate mid-fight certainly helped.”

Kokuryu’s eyes widened. “That is fortuitous indeed. In a battle between Whispers, that would make a sizable difference. Though I am not surprised you were capable of such a feat given your pedigree.” He drummed his fingers on the table. “But that was not the end of your troubles.”

“It was not,” Rix said grimly. He recounted a brief summary of what had happened since ranking up.

Kokuryu let out a long breath after he’d finished. “I was hoping Han’s death might just fall through the cracks of this place like so many others.”

“You and me both,” Rix replied. “Rumor has it Sho is often away for months on end, so at the very least I was hoping to get a little more time, but we got unlucky there too.”

Kokuryu nodded. “So it seems. At least this secret protector of yours bought you a reprieve. You are sure you don’t have any idea who that might be?”

Rix shook his head smoothly. He was getting closer to trusting Kokuryu, but Master Zhen had stuck his neck out for Rix and Luna at great risk to himself. He deserved to have that secret guarded absolutely. If that particular nugget of information became vital at some point, Rix would reassess, but for now his lips were sealed.

“A pity,” the man said. “It would be good to have more allies in here.”

Rix hesitated. “I assume you understand the implications of the Truthseeker.”

The older man gave a heavy nod. “I do, Zao Rixian.”

“I’m sorry you’re now at risk,” Rix said. “I didn’t realize such a thing was even possible.”

“Neither did I. Nevertheless, I do not blame you. This intertwining of our fates was out of your hands. We must simply focus on what’s in front of us. We have three months to save ourselves.”

Rix appreciated the man’s practical attitude. He had every right to be angry.

“It’s not a long time,” Rix replied, “so we need to make the most of it. Since we spoke last, did you manage to find anything that might help us escape?”

Kokuryu’s expression brightened. “As a matter of fact, I did. You mentioned tunnels, so I went and did a little digging. While I haven’t actually sighted plans yet that confirm it, I have a strong suspicion such pathways do indeed exist.”

“Why?” Rix asked.

“For a prison to have such an obvious security risk, they must be serving some very specific purpose, and through a process of elimination I worked out what it must be.” The Wellspring representative paused dramatically. “The healing array. While all mana arrays produce waste, most of them are small enough that it simply vents harmlessly into the atmosphere. But arrays such as the one powering the arena here are a different proposition. Not only do they need vast quantities of runes to power them, which must be buried deep in the earth but still be accessible for repairs, but the waste they produce is dense enough to manifest physically, which means it must be disposed of. Ironguard keeps their secrets close, and I’ve yet to gain access to the specifics of their installation here, but I’ve seen plans for arrays of similar scale, and all include access tunnels for disposal. If you wish to escape, I believe they are your best shot.”

Rix felt a wave of relief wash over him. While he’d been acting as though escape was a sure thing, the truth was that the whole idea was built atop little more than rumor and hope. To hear there actually was a possible way out made him feel slightly less silly to have put all his eggs in that basket. Perhaps he wouldn’t doom himself and everyone he knew after all.

“That’s excellent news,” he said.

Kokuryu beamed back at him, clearly proud of his efforts. “I am working to try and secure actual plans of Ironguard’s array, though it will take me some time. Wellspring Antiquities has some unique connections, but we are still fundamentally a small fish compared to the large corporations.”

“That’s fine,” Rix replied. “In the meantime, I can ask around and see if I can get a sense of where they might be from in here.”

He reached up to tap his collar. “This is going to be another significant barrier. As long as we’re wearing these, any guard who even glances our way can incapacitate us. And while they haven’t said as much, I suspect the same would happen if we tried to walk outside the prison perimeter with them on.”

Kokuryu’s lips compressed. “Hmm, I will need to investigate. I’m not familiar with the way such artefacts work. If they operate the way you say, I suspect they probably connect to some kind of central hub, but I can’t be sure.”

“I’ve got one,” Rix said. “A spare one I mean. Found it on a body in the realm. So I can see what array symbols they used to make it. With qi being a superior resource and all, I was hoping there was a way to override them, you know?”

“If there is, I don’t know it.” Kokuryu glanced away, seemingly embarrassed. “Our understanding of qi arrays is…rudimentary at best. Like I said to you last time we met, our cultivation is founded on scavenged texts and hoarded secrets. Not unlike your own. To be blunt, our approach is full of holes. It is difficult to build a proper knowledge base when there are forces out there actively trying to destroy it.”

That statement led to so many questions Rix wanted to ask; about the Jade Shadows, his parents, the wider universe. Breaker continued to be tight-lipped about anything other than their immediate studies, which made Kokuryu the only other source of answers in their life.

But once he started, he wouldn’t stop, and none of that was going to help their immediate predicament.

“Do you think you could get me some kind of more advanced text on System arrays then?” Rix asked instead. “What they’ve got in here only covers the basics.”

Kokuryu nodded. “I think I can do that.”

It might not amount to much, but it still felt worth exploring.

Kokuryu’s expression turned conspiratorial. “I do have something else for you right now.”

Rix raised an eyebrow.

“As you’re now a Spark, you’re officially eligible for sponsorship,” the Wellspring man said. “And to seal such a contract, it’s customary to give a gift. It is System-related, of course, but it should still offer a significant boost in power.”

A grin spread across Rix’s face. “What is it?”

The man gave another dramatic pause. “A technique evolution gem.”
