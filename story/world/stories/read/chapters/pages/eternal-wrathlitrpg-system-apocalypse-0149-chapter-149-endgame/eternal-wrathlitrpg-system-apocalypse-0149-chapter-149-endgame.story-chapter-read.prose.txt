“Anderson and Sanders, I will leave this town and its inhabitants in your care. Please evacuate everyone to the next town before the blood moon arrives. This would at least buy us some time,” Andrew said as he called for Sanders.

“Huh? But if the blood moon arrives, what about you?”

“The book said that the Yggdrasil is immune to the blood moon’s blessing. I’m not sure, but I suppose we would be safe once inside. Ah, I also need several beef jerkies. Can you buy them for me, Anderson?”

“Understood.” Anderson nodded and scrolled through his shop.

“What happened there, James? Did you need something to enter the tree? It’s not like you to find a unique place and leave without exploring it. Are you afraid of the blood moon?” Kouki asked.

“No. I don’t think the tree was protected by the system, but it was protected by four tree knights,” James said.

“Oh, you didn’t finish them, James? How rare.” Kouki raised an eyebrow. He didn’t like how James operated, but he knew James was the strongest among the survivors.

“I lost, alright. They could heal themselves, and they could regenerate their spears, effectively giving them unlimited ammunition before using a spear skill to shoot those spears at me.”

“I see. One might be doable, but four might be a bit too much even for you, huh?”

“Don’t worry, there are three of us now! I will cut down those monsters!” Kouki said.

James climbed onto his raptor, Kouki onto his dragon, and Andrew onto his pegasus. Then they raced toward the north. Since James knew the path, they braved the wasteland without stopping, even to rest. So, instead of four days like James did, they arrived in merely two days. However, fatigue had crept in, and they could barely walk. For the first time, James was racing against time. He could feel his body burdened by fatigue.

“Let’s rest before entering, Andrew,” James said.

“…Yes. You’re right. Sorry. I am too hasty,” Andrew replied. His haste was not merely because of the people of the city, but because he knew he would die when the timer ran out.

The book said that Yggdrasil was immune to the moon’s blessing, but it didn’t say anything about the people inside. They didn’t know how long they would stay inside the tree.

Alas, haste makes waste. Andrew realized they were too tired to fight four experienced knights right now. So, he accepted the suggestion and camped near the city’s edge.

“So that’s the tree, huh? It didn’t disappoint. It really is huge. I think it’s even bigger than the Census Bureau itself,” Kouki muttered.

“And taller too,” Andrew commented as he made himself comfortable. But James interrupted. He knew there was nothing left in this wilderness. All the animals had died of starvation. Still, he couldn’t help feeling insecure. He felt too exposed. In this wasteland, the white dragon and horse stood out like sore thumbs.

“Let’s sleep inside the city. The treemen shouldn’t wander far from the tree.”

“Is it safe?”

“There shouldn’t be any animals inside, because the cockroaches consumed them all. But I can make an ice wall to block any gaps, if you like,” James said.

“Well, better than sleeping on the ground.”

“Yeah. I know there aren’t supposed to be monsters around here, but I can’t help feeling uneasy,” James said, voicing his thoughts aloud.

Despite their tired bodies, they forced themselves into the city and entered one of the houses. It was empty, just as James remembered.

“No furniture…” Kouki frowned, expecting a bed inside this abandoned village.

James raised an ice wall at the door and windows, sealing them off from the outside world. Now he felt safe. Even if something were to break the wall, they would know.

“All organics were devoured, huh?” Andrew said. “Well, it’s still better than sleeping outside.”

The three of them lay down. Despite their fatigue, their eyes refused to close, hovering beneath the insistent countdown to their doom. Fewer than forty hours remained. A question haunted them. Should they really be taking a break here? Shouldn’t they at least push a little harder?

But their weariness finally caught up with them, and they fell into a deep slumber. Kouki was the first to wake. Seeing the countdown had dropped from forty to thirty, he turned toward the others.

“James, Andrew, come on! We have a tree to conquer. Stop sleeping.”

“Huh? Shit!” Andrew woke up with a start. He assessed the surroundings and was horrified when he looked at the countdown. Meanwhile, James merely yawned. His laid-back attitude was baffling, but Andrew held his tongue.

The four treemen were still where they were supposed to be, lying still before the great tree. James used 200 coins to buy two flashbangs and two incendiary grenades before the battle. With a nod, they leaped toward the treemen.

“I’ll handle the left one! Hurry up, Ralph!” Kouki commanded his dragon. He leaped with his sword, casting flying slashes before he even reached them. The treemen reacted with their shields, blocking the attacks with ease. The wooden shields chipped here and there, seemingly about to break, but James knew the treemen could simply regrow them.

James also cast slashes toward the treemen on the right, but unlike Kouki, he didn’t rush to the front. His flying slashes did not come from sword skills or unique abilities, but from his sword itself. The results were far weaker than Kouki’s sword skills, but it was enough to aggro two of them.

Meanwhile, Andrew sidestepped. He didn’t leap to the front. He was not a vanguard. His worth was proven soon after James clashed with the first treeman. A golden flash shot out from Andrew’s side, aiming at the second treeman that was about to flank James. The flash was quick and precise, taking advantage of the moment when the treemen’s attention was on James.

Without Andrew, James would have had to use his magic, flight, lightning bolts, or breath attacks to deal with the second treeman. But with Andrew at his side, he cleaned up both of them in no time. James won the skill clash, smashed the treeman’s skull, and cleaved its torso in half. Then he fired his fire breath at the remains for good measure, reducing it to mere charcoal. Meanwhile, the treeman knight Andrew had stabbed began to recover.

“Whoa, shit. It’s still alive,” Andrew muttered. His spear skill had stabbed it dead center, where the heart would usually be. The gaping hole slowly regenerated.

“That’s why I ran away. This one didn’t regenerate; that means it can be killed.” James pointed toward the dead treeman.

“Guys! Hurry up over there! These fuckers just won’t die!” Kouki leaped away as one of the treeman knights threw his spear at him. His dragon bathed it in fire, but just like before, it cast healing magic on itself. The burned branches slowly regained their verdant hue.

Kouki tried to press on, but their teamwork was flawless. The moment he stepped in, the other took the chance to lunge. Even when Ralph distracted them, each treeman was strong enough on its own that Kouki couldn’t finish them with a single weapon skill like James.

“I’ll handle this one. You can help Kouki, Andrew.”

“On it.” Just like before, Andrew stood behind Kouki. Now it was three against one. Unlike before, Andrew didn’t sneak in. He faced the treeman head-on. All he needed was to buy time. He believed James or Kouki could deal with the others one on one.

Andrew began his spearwork. He jabbed his spear at the treeman’s shield, then took distance, keeping it busy. Meanwhile, knowing the treeman would no longer bother him, Kouki went in for a full frontal attack. His expertise lay in chaining sword skills together. First came the lunge, which was successfully blocked. Then came another slash from the side, which cut off the treeman’s hand. Another strike from above was blocked, but since it was not a shield skill, Kouki’s sword didn’t stop. Golden flashes slashed at the wooden shield like a disco light show, each one chipping it until it finally broke. Then, with a final slash, Kouki beheaded it.

“What a show-off,” James muttered. Kouki had obviously finished faster than him. Unlike Kouki and his flashy sword skills, James abandoned his sword the moment his arm reached the treeman. He grabbed its wrist and wrestled it to the ground. His body was coated in flames as he crushed the treeman’s skull into pieces and burned its body to cinders.

He then looked toward the treeman Andrew was fighting and equipped his axe. Despite their perfect teamwork, these monsters were easily lured. It left its back open, and James wouldn’t let it go to waste. The axe glowed blue, then drew an arc straight at it. Unlike a normal weapon skill, the axe didn’t smash through the tree soldier. It merely embedded itself an inch inside.

If this had been a normal attack, the treeman wouldn’t have even registered it as damage, but this was an elemental weapon. The ice enchantment spread from the impact point. By the time it realized it had been hit, it was already too late. It had been fully encased in ice. Andrew delivered the final blow with a jab of his spear skill, and the ice shattered. The treeman’s body scattered into pieces.

After the battle was over, James got a ping from the system about his level-up.

[You have leveled up to level 65. Your Attacker class has leveled up to level 48.]

“Phew. That was tiring. Good work, everyone. Good work to you as well, James. It must be tough facing four of them on your own.”

“They are fairly slow if you really run for your life,” James answered. He then turned toward the tree.

Humongous didn’t even begin to describe it. It was even bigger than Kouki had said. It spanned more than 500 meters in radius—over a kilometer in diameter. The tree stood tall; its lowest branches were higher than the tallest building in town. There was no way James could simply hop into it, even with his jet suit.

“So, any idea how to get in?” Kouki said. “Do we just climb it? Maybe we can use our swords as pegs…”

“No, there is an entrance. Right there. It’s covered by cockroaches, but I saw it before.” James pointed toward the black bark of the tree. At a glance, the black cockroaches looked like tree bark because they didn’t move. James wondered whether the cockroaches formed a symbiosis with the tree or whether they were part of the tree itself.

He stepped closer, then blew flame toward where he had seen the entrance before. The black bark skittered away, burned by the fiery breath. Then, a massive stone gate emerged from behind the fleeing cockroaches.

With a nod, the group went inside.
