After visiting the remains of a Nator village, Ethan and his small team looked at the landscape of Potentia with new eyes. Ethan had debated for some time as to whether to share his findings with Tomo and Selina. On the one hand, it might seem a boon to them to know their people had somehow managed to stay alive far longer than anyone believed. However, that extra time was spent under constant attack, and with their bodies slowly betraying them from corruption.

The bones he’d found in the final chamber had been far closer to demon than Nator, and that had still been several centuries ago.

In the end, despite his misgivings, and the pain it might cause, Ethan decided it wasn’t his secret to keep. Tomo and Selina had reacted very differently from one another. Tomo was proud to hear that his people had continued to fight and struggle for so long, but mourned the many generations who’d experienced such suffering.

Selina had been every inch the scholar. She’d perked up, fascinated by how a culture would grow and adapt with the hardships they faced. She was determined to find more evidence of her people, and watched the landscape eagerly for any sign of the lost civilization.

Sadly, they found little. After two months on Potentia they’d still only found the one city, as well a small collection of stones and artifacts. They had made significant progress in building their army, though. Ethan was slowly beginning to gain a sense for exactly how much of the concentrated corruption they’d amassed in their forces. With Tomo’s help he recognized the accumulation of energy like another source of power, though one safely walled off thanks to the intermediary of Apollo and the Fable Train.

While the new sensation was valuable, and Tomo believed would eventually allow him to measure his accumulation against what was needed to take the Throne, the real indication was something more visceral.

Once again, Ethan could see the light.

The light and the Throne were intimately connected, and for a time, Ethan had seen it even on Viridus. Where the Throne was the goal, the light was the path. Ethan had once believed it to be a guide, pointing in the direction of more power. That was somewhat true, though he’d learned more since its reappearance.

The light was both an external, and internal phenomena. It was his soul’s reaction to the corrupting presence of Potential; that volatile power always craved more power, and would draw its host toward any significant source. That had been the element that drew Ethan toward methods of personal growth. New abilities and sources of strength, which–on Potentia–would have brought him closer to the Throne.

He’d lost the light after cleansing himself, but found it again now–though it appeared to manifest only in its more external variant. Possibly because of the unusual nature of how he was amassing corruption, the light instead drew him to any significant source of that power, and was visually overwhelming at times.

Ethan could always tell when they were growing too close to the ocean, as the entire horizon would light up, similar to when you were in the country back home, with a large city in the distance at night. Ethan had absolutely no interest in getting closer to the sea–he’d learned that lesson quite thoroughly, but the addition of the light had made it much easier to track down Twilight demons.

Their army now had more than fifty of the hulking beasts, which was more than slightly intimidating. Apollo showed no sign of strain from the additional load, and the plan was thus far proceeding as hoped. But Ethan was increasingly aware that, without his dimensional double, they’d suddenly find themselves surrounded by a force of demons powerful enough to destroy them a hundred times over.

The balancing factor was the Fable Train itself, combined with his alarming enormous spirit, which had more than doubled in size since arriving. It was nearly fifty feet tall, even sitting on its throne, and Tomo had informed him that it now rivaled a Master rank spirit in power. Spirit attacks were famously ineffective against demons, sadly, so the bloated behemoth was of little practical value to Ethan himself, but as a battery for the Fable Train, the results were remarkable.

Now nearly as large as its maximum Twilight size–when powered by Ethan alone–it lumbered across Potentia like a land-bound battleship. Ethan had insisted it be almost entirely covered in massive weapons, Prime’s techwork powers adding all sorts of miniguns, heavy artillery, and more. While Revan and Deevee’s powers meant that flamethrowers and dimensional beams rounded out its defenses.

Ethan was admiring his admittedly over-the-top preparations one afternoon, when Strategus appeared beside him, shaking his head slowly. Ethan had been giving free reign to both Zakiel and Strategus now that they had more power than they knew what to do with, and it had led to some amusing situations.

Zak followed Selina around like an excited puppy. Neither one of them seemed capable of remaining silent for too long, and the Runemistress seemed to enjoy an enthusiastic, and somewhat mad version of Ethan nodding along to anything she said. By contrast, Strategus haunted the Fable Train like a gloomy spectre. He stared out at the demon army in disgust, sighed at Apollo and Zakiel, and reminded Ethan at every turn of his promise.

“It all comes down to you,” he’d say, at seemingly no prompting. “You’re the only one you can count on, in the end.”

Ethan’s insistence that he wasn’t so alone as Strategus had been in his own dimension was only ever met with a sad shake of the head. “You must be alone. There is only one Throne.”

His latest appearance seemed to herald more of the same. “This train, interesting though it may be, cannot protect you.”

“It objectively can,” Ethan said. He’d stopped bothering to hide his irritation with Eeyore-Ethan long ago. He’d promised to sacrifice himself to save the worlds, not be chased around by the Ghost of Christmas future. “Do you not see all the guns?” he said, gesturing at the forest of weapon barrels surrounding them atop the traincar.

“Just remember where this all leads,” Strategus insisted.

“Yeah, yeah, thank you. I keep forgetting about my own inevitable death, you miserable a-hole.”

Strategus nodded, as if Ethan hadn’t been sarcastic. Thankfully Apollo lowered himself down to hover close to them. “How’s Captain Cheerful today?” he asked.

“He brings ill-tidings of things to come,” Ethan muttered.

“I’ll bet he does,” Apollo chuckled. “Have you ever noticed how much like Michael he is? Maybe Michael’s future self appeared to bum him out back in childhood.”

“It would explain a lot,” Ethan said. “How goes it, by the way? Do you like the bunker I made you? You could, you know, stay inside if you like.”

Apollo rolled his eyes. “If we’re attacked, or even think we’re going to be attacked, I will go inside and huddle like a good little boy. But if you think I’m spending the rest of this trip hiding in a box, you’re as crazy as Zak. I’m already letting Angelos drive half the time, and I’m still in more danger from boredom than the literal demons surrounding us.”

Ethan sighed. “Fine, I get it.”

“Anyway, I didn’t come down to chat with gloomy over there, I have news.”

“Oh?” Ethan asked. He didn’t mean to sound excited, but week after week of kiting demons to the train wasn’t exactly satisfying.

“As you know, I’ve moved all the Dawn rank members of the crusade to the outer edges of the army. They’re valuable scouts, and we don’t mind losing them at this point.”

“Right,” Ethan said. It felt a little strange to be cavalier with any life, but this was better than letting the larger demons eat the smaller. “So what’s the problem?”

“Well, we’re losing them.” He pointed to the west, where rolling hills and a red-hued lake gave way to a writhing forest. “We sent some in that direction when we spotted a few Gigas moving in the area. Those giant two-headed bastards are strong for any rank, and a Twilight would be a prize. But everything we send that way dies.”

“Then it looks like I get to be the sacrificial Ethan,” he said. “Alright, I’ll check it out. Anything strong enough to kill everything we send will be a strong addition to the army. If I can get it close, we’ll sick all the Twilights on it.”

“Good luck,” Apollo said, rising back into the air. Strategus just turned and disappeared in a flash of blue-white light.

“Oh no, don’t leave,” Ethan muttered with a roll of his eyes.

After quickly informing Selina of his intentions, Ethan began racing off to the west. He ended up traveling through the disgusting forest, having learned that the trees themselves were actually Dusk rank, and their writhing vines wouldn’t reach out to snag any passing Twilights. He’d also learned that anywhere you went in Potentia was best done with some degree of stealth.

While he wasn’t worried about being attacked–besides his own remarkable survivability, anything strong enough to attack him was usually a good addition to their army–hunting wasn’t easy when every creature for miles saw or sensed you. Despite their remarkable proclivity for violence, Ethan had learned that the demons of this world had an equal, or perhaps greater ability to hide.

And so he moved rapidly through the mustard-yellow forest, eyes searching for any sign of the missing scouts. It wasn’t long before he emerged from the other side of the woods, still not having uncovered any sign of the missing demons. The little bastards didn’t even leave footprints in the hard Potentia ground.

Ethan paused at the mouth to a deep crevice carved into the red rock. A deep boom had reverberated through the land, and it took him a long moment to realize it was actually an incredible peel of thunder. He turned his gaze up to the sky, surprised to see clouds so dark as to be almost black approaching from the opposite direction.

“Tomo, when you said there was a storm approaching, I thought you’d been wrong.”

“On Potentia, soon can mean several weeks when speaking of the weather.”

“Do I need to be worried?” Ethan asked.

“You? No, you are Twilight, and could likely stand through the most severe of this world’s hurricanes. The army is not so fortunate, but I have already instructed Apollo on how to handle such an event. Even now, the army is no-doubt digging themselves under ground.”

“Fair enough,” Ethan said, trying to picture more than ten thousand demons hiding in little foxholes. “Well, I’m still going to see if I can find the missing scouts, before the rain washes away any kind of trail.”

He shot into the crevice, which widened to about two dozen paces as it deepened. Vines lined the walls, and it was odd to consider that the perpetually dusty place would likely be a river in an hour or so. As he progressed, he finally noted the signs he’d been waiting for: clawmarks in stone. Demons had gone this way, and hopefully it was the scouts.

Truthfully he might already have his answer, as he was nearing the edge of Apollo’s influence. If the scouts had gone this way, they might have slipped their bonds. But as Ethan traveled deeper, he found another possibility.

Gigas. There were three of the creatures, two Dusk and one Dawn, meaning one was huge, and two were enormous. They were, however, equally dead.

Ethan walked amongst the bulky corpses, wary. Gigas were immense, two headed creatures that might have been mistaken for ogres back on Earth. They were so large and flabby that they’d be difficult for anything to kill, but these ones appeared torn apart. The Dawn was missing its arms, while one of the dusks had its upper body severed from the bottom.

Ethan couldn’t tell much from their positioning, but he was forced to wonder what could do this. As he continued forward, he did so as a Trick of the Light, not wanting to be ambushed by so dangerous a creature. He found more bodies as he continued on, a few more gigas, and some other, smaller demons that might have been the scouts.

The crevice was now nearly eighty feet deep, and Ethan could hear the rain approaching from behind. He kept moving, determined to solve this little mystery, but froze when he found something that absolutely did not belong.

It looked like a man, although he was half-buried beneath another dead demon. Ethan moved carefully forward, mind searching desperately for answers. Could this be a transformed Nator? Or perhaps someone who’d gotten trapped here from Nexum? When he got close enough to recognize the pale, rune-covered flesh, a far more concerning reality presented itself.

Follower.

There was a dead Follower here, and obviously one that was alive very recently. No hope that it might have been left here for generations under some strange circumstances. Ethan dismissed his stealth to roll the strangely pale demon off of the equally pale corpse, looking down at a Twilight general, its cape torn and covered in dark blood.

Ethan was so surprised by the sight, and the implications of what he’d found, that it took him a moment to glance back and put the pieces together.

Pale demon. They were almost all uniformly some shade of black or red. The other corpses had been pale too, but more importantly, how were there even corpses? They’d all been dead long enough to start decomposing into mana. Unless…

The Follower began to sit up, the gaping wounds in its chest and neck no longer bleeding. It looked at Ethan with its tiny, dark eyes, then lunged for him. Ethan leapt back, only for the large hand of a gigas to reach for him. Ethan reacted quickly, flipping away to escape its reach. Then he took in the grisly scene before him, as every dead demon began to slowly shuffle and move.

He’d seen this before, long ago in the mountains of Viridus. There he’d learned what caused such horrors, and that it originated here, in Potentia.

Undead.
