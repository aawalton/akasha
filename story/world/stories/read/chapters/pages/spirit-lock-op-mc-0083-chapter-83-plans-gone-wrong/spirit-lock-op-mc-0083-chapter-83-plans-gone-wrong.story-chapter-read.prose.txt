“Enough!” Halkan demanded. “Are you trying to declare war?”

“I don’t know,” Stella said. “Are you? You ordered a guild that was embroiled in a bitter conflict against Hayden Reskco to enter a rift and steal what he wanted—and you did that while fully aware that a celestial figure’s daughter was in the rift. I have video recordings that prove it.”

Halkan maintained cruel eye contact with her.

Antina was spiraling as she listened. The two were fighting, as she wanted, but Stella had the Quasar Alliance’s full backing—and Stella would obtain pressure on Antina as well. This wasn’t an incident at this point—it was a narrative—and it was a strong one, too.

She set this up… Antina thought. She looked at Karva. “You said your team only attacked because they thought he was dying, right? And you attacked because you felt pressured?”

“Ms. Haeila!” President Mastia snapped. “This is no time for—”

“Tell her, Karva,” Stella said coolly. “Tell them when you determined that you were going to kill Hayden’s team.”

Karva swallowed. “From the start. But it’s only because you allowed Ella on the team! I thought you were sending a message.”

“A message?” Stella turned to her. “Are you implying that you have executors on your rifting team?”

Karva fell silent, jaw loose as she met Stella’s brutal, callous gaze. She couldn’t reply.

“You have killers on your team…” Stella murmured. “Which I don’t think is common. I wonder if Halkan contacted you with that in mind.”

“Stop this!” Halkan roared.

“Karigan,” Stella pressed, turning to the RDA’s truthseeker. “Ask her when she determined that she would blindly wipe out the second team.”

Karva squeezed her eyes shut as Karigan used Veritas on her. Then, she answered:

“Immediately.”

Stella fixed her gaze on Halkan. “As of this moment, we’re treating this incident as a political assassination attempt. Thus, we’re seizing the right to the rift, and will use our own teams to protect and return the Iska sisters. Now move your warships out of our airspace—before we eliminate them.”

The meeting abruptly came to an end, returning Antina to her private suite. She stared at the wall for a few moments and then screamed, picking up a glass of wine and throwing it against the wall.

This. This was the reason that she wanted Stella ripped limb from limb. Stella played Karva and then sacrificed her the next day. That’s what the look on Karva’s face said. She recognized that face—she recognized it on sight. Because once upon a time, Antina, too, held that same expression—

And she would never forget it.

Stella kept them in power for times like these when she needed to control them.

They weren’t friends. They weren’t fellow citizens or allies. They were just criminals who played games, only to be beaten by better players.

Despite that, she still held a grave expression as she walked back to her suite.

“I can’t believe they tried to kill him,” Stella muttered. “Goddamn animals.”

Dasan kept silent for a moment before saying, “You succeeded, though. So let’s just celebrate.”

Stella opened the door and sat down at a coffee table, motioning for the man to join her. A server poked his head in. “Tea?”

Dasan leaned back. “Stella: you won. You implied they should take over Hayden’s protection; they attacked his team—you obtained the right to remove them. You solidified your position with Elden; Antina’s cornered. You did your job. So what’s wrong?”

Things worked out even better than she expected: the Sickles attacked the Iska sisters, allowing Stella to mobilize a strike force of gruesome legal and military mechanisms. As an added bonus, Hayden punished the Sickles without hurting them, which would come in handy in the near future. Still, it bothered her.

“It’s just… It’s hard to explain. Part of me feels this binding horror that I almost lost Hayden. It’s a strange feeling… one that’s reserved for loved ones and children. But the thought of losing him was just…”

Stella remembered Hayden’s confident smile as he said, “To future partners,” emphasising the final word to be as personal as possible. “Shocking,” Stella said. “I guess he convinced me that we were gonna have some grand political alliance, and then he’d then save me from my age dysphoria—and then he almost dies a few days later due to a game gone wrong. But it’s not just that… The fact that he survived…” Her glassy eyes flashed with cold light. “The fact that a celestial guild sent eighty rifters to secure him, only to have all but three die or come back with shattered cores…” She looked up. “That terrifies me.”

“Yeah.”
