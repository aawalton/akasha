“Grace, be careful!” a voice shouted from above, and I didn’t need to look up to know what they meant.

I could feel my fingers slipping from the rock, and they must have noticed it too. Shifting my balance, I fought to hold on, but I was so exhausted. My muscles were trembling from the effort of hauling my body up the sheer rock face, and I wasn’t sure how much longer I could keep going.

It felt as though I’d already been climbing for an eternity, though. I knew it would have been so much easier to just give in and let myself fall, and I longed to do it, but I couldn’t. I refused to let myself give in. I had to keep going until my body failed me.

“Come on, Grace!” I heard someone call over the cacophony of noise echoing around us. “You’re so close. You can do it!”

Gritting my teeth, I sucked in breath and forced myself to push with my legs. My footing was still good. I just needed to stretch a little further, and I’d be able to grab the ledge. It was a little out of my reach, but I should be able to just about do it. Then, I could keep going.

I swung my arm out towards it, but exhaustion made my movements clumsy. My aim was off, and my hands too clammy. My fingers grazed the surface of the rough rock before starting to slide off it, unable to cling on.

A gasp sounded as I started to teeter dangerously, my left hand flailing as it clutched at thin air. Panic exploded within me, and I hung on with my right hand as tightly as I could, telling myself over and over that I couldn’t let go. I had to keep holding on.

Blindly, I reached out again, feeling the stone starting to slip from my grasp. If I didn’t find another handhold soon, it wouldn’t matter how determined I was. My muscles would give way, and I would fall. I couldn’t keep clinging on forever, but luckily, my hand closed around something solid.

A relieved breath stuttered over my lips, and I knew I should have checked to make sure the thing I was holding was sturdy, that it wouldn’t fall too, but I couldn’t. For a few seconds, all I could do was pull myself against the wall and let my head rest against it in a desperate attempt to regain my strength.

“Yes!” someone above me cheered. “Just a little further, Grace! Keep going!”

I didn’t even remember closing my eyes, but I had to fight to open them again. My body begged me for sleep. It pleaded to be allowed to rest for just a little while longer, but I forced myself to look up, searching for my next hold.

Too soon, I found it. More cautiously that time, I reached out, testing the outcropping before putting any of my weight on it. I still needed to find somewhere for my feet, I realised as I craned my neck to stare at the wall around me, but instead, I caught sight of the people on the ground far below me.

A wave of vertigo slammed into me, and I felt my chest tighten as I pulled myself against the wall again. Nausea thrashed within me, forcing me to shut my eyes as I fought to keep it at bay. My head spun. Moisture beaded on my palms, making my already slick hands even more slippery, and fear pulled at me as I realised I might fall.

I just didn’t realise how tall it was when I started climbing. It didn’t look so big from down there, but I was wrong. It was huge, and there were too many people watching me. I could feel their eyes burning into me as they waited for me to finally lose my grip and go plummeting towards the ground.

Around me, a cheer exploded, making me jump. It was accompanied by a chorus of groans, and I wasn’t sure whether someone had just made it to the top or if they’d fallen, but I couldn’t check. I needed to open my eyes and keep climbing. I was running out of time.

My breath was tight and shaky as I exhaled as slowly as I could before lifting my head away from the wall and looking around once more. Carefully, I kept my vision from darting towards the ground, knowing it would only make me dizzy again, and I couldn’t let that happen. Still, I managed to find a space for my foot. It was small, barely big enough for my toes to be wedged onto it, but it was enough.

I shifted my body again, making sure my footing was solid, before stretching out slowly. The tip of my shoe scraped against the rock, nudging it to ensure it wouldn’t fall, before I lowered my foot onto it. Carefully, I pushed down, putting more of my weight onto the stone whilst still clinging on as tightly as I could, but it didn’t give away like so many others had before.

It was okay, I told myself. I had to keep going. My legs shook, begging me to give in and let myself fall backwards, but I managed to drag my way slightly further up the wall, testing each hold before using it. A few crumbled, falling away in my grasp, and I let them, even as a small voice in the back of my head whispered that I was making it worse for everyone who came after me. They’d have fewer options, fewer places to grab, but there wasn’t anything I could do about that.

“You’re almost there, Grace!” a voice shouted from above, sending a wave of shock through me.

They were much closer than I expected, and I couldn’t help but glance up. It was instinctive. I needed to know how much longer I would be climbing, and that was a mistake, but I realised it too late.

I’d barely even glanced at the brightly coloured hold before my hand closed around it. I tugged it only once before deciding it was safe, too eager to reach the person at the top who grinned down at me, but I should have double-checked. I’d been so cautious up until that point, and I’d just let go with my other hand. I was reaching towards the next hold I’d seen when the realisation slammed into me.

A noticeable click came from inside the wall, resonating through my hand. It vibrated lightly, threatening to break free from my grasp, and I fought to hold on, but it didn’t matter. The hold was already slipping back, and I was moving with it.

My pulse leapt into a race as I felt my body start to tip backwards. My eyes widened as fear gripped my heart, and my breath caught in my chest. The undeniable fact that I was about to fall exploded within me, and my eyes darted to the wall, searching desperately for something, anything, that I could grab onto, but it was too late.

A snort came from somewhere nearby, threatening to rip me out of the world. My already spinning head whirled even faster, and my fear sharpened, turning into pure panic. I couldn’t leave. I couldn’t slip away from the world whilst I was falling. The thought of what would happen to me if I did terrified me. I could slam into the ground at the wrong angle, shattering my body and breaking it forever.

“Grace! Give me your hand!”

I looked up, my eyes finding Seth’s panicked gaze. He was leaning over the edge, his arm stretching out towards me, and I didn’t even think. I just let go of the artificial rock that I was still clutching and used the last of my strength to throw myself towards him.

Time seemed to slow down as I hurtled through the air. I watched, unable to do anything, as my hand moved towards Seth’s, drawing closer before shooting past it. I was so close. Only a couple of centimetres away, but I had missed him.

My heart sank. Panic roared in my heart as I started to drop before, somehow, Seth’s hand closed around my forearm. A gasp slipped from my lips as his grip tightened around me, refusing to let go. My momentum sent me slamming against the wall. The air was knocked out of me, but Seth’s hand did not loosen.

One of my hands dangled by my side. Neither of my feet were touching the wall. I was being held up by Seth alone, and I stared up at him in shock as gratitude slammed into me.

“I’ve got you,” he told me, his voice barely audible over the cheers that exploded around the climbing gym.

I was breathless. My lungs spasmed in my chest as I fought to catch my breath, still winded from hitting the wall, as Seth began to drag me upwards. As soon as I got close enough, I reached up with my free hand, feeling for the lip at the top of the wall in an attempt to pull myself up.

Other hands grabbed me, closing around my harness and hauling me to safety. The sudden help seemed to throw Seth off balance, and he toppled backwards. His hand was still closed around my arm, and my muscles were too tired to fight against it. I was dragged down with him.

I fell awkwardly, breaking free from his grasp as we tumbled, and I tried to throw my arms out to catch myself. I didn’t want to fall onto him, but I was too weak from the climb. My hands hit the ground, but my elbows folded immediately, refusing to hold my weight, and I crashed into Seth’s chest.

The air was knocked out of me again, and my forehead slammed against Seth’s shoulder, making the world spin again. My hearing became distant for a moment, taking on a weirdly underwater quality, before returning, and I was vaguely aware of an arm wrapping around me. I wasn’t sure whose it was. My head was still reeling from the multiple impacts, but I was glad that someone was holding me. Without them, I was pretty sure I would have fallen off the edge.

A hand clapped down on my shoulder, and I realised people were still cheering, but it took me a minute to realise they were cheering for me. For me, and for Seth. I’d managed to get to the top of the wall with his help.

“She’s clear!” someone shouted behind. “Josie, go!”

The world was still spinning around me, but I finally managed to suck in a breath, and immediately, the urge to apologise gripped me. I couldn’t stop it from slipping out.

“Sorry!” I cried.

“Are you okay?” Seth asked at the same time.

His breath tickled my ear when he spoke, making me realise just how close we were. Seth had been crouched down before falling backwards, and I was sprawled out with pretty much all of my weight on him. I must have been crushing him.

Awkwardly and with great difficulty, I pushed myself back, a grimace on my face.

“Yeah, sorry,” I said before adding, “Thanks for saving me.”

Seth grinned as he sat up a little straighter, his arm slipping away from my waist, letting me go.

“Ah, I didn’t really save you,” he replied. “You would have made it without me. Well done, by the way. You crushed my time.”

Excitement and shock rose within me, and I stared at Seth.

“What? I did?”

I looked back at the clock behind me, taking in the numbers, but I had no idea what time it was when I’d started my climb. I had meant to check, but I’d gotten distracted. I was too consumed by what I was about to do, and I couldn’t look away from Seth as he neared the top of the wall and was dragged over it.

“Yeah!” Seth told me. “You were a full minute faster than me. Actually… a minute and a half. Damn.”

My gaze remained on the clock for a few more seconds before returning to Seth. I was almost giddy with excitement and happiness. I’d been so worried that I wasn’t even going to make it to the top of the wall, and I had no idea how I’d managed to do it faster than Seth. He’d done so well and barely stopped at all during his climb. I was certain my time would have been double his.

“Damn,” I repeated, unable to say anything else.

Seth’s smile grew wider, and my gaze darted towards it. The urge to lean forward and kiss him gripped me so tightly that it took my breath away. It wouldn’t be hard. Our faces were still pretty close, so I wouldn’t have far to lean.

I almost started to do it before I caught myself, my stomach clenching as I forced myself to remain still. It would be a mistake. There were too many people around us, and they didn’t seem to be paying any attention to us. Their focus had moved on to whoever was climbing the wall below us, but I still didn’t want to do it. Plus, I didn’t know whether Seth felt the same way, whether he wanted me to kiss him. It seemed like his eyes dropped to my lips too, but I wasn’t sure if that was intentional. He might have just been looking at me.

We were still too close together, though. I was kneeling in the space between Seth’s legs, and his bent knees were on either side of me. Even though I knew I was only there because we’d fallen, it was still weirdly intimate, and that made me feel flustered. I’d not been that close with anyone other than…

Guilt pulled at me as I started to shuffle backwards, the burn in my cheeks growing hotter. I hoped Seth would just think I was red because of exertion and not think anything more of it. I didn’t want him to know the true reason was because I’d almost kissed him or because of anything else that I’d been thinking. That would have been mortifying.

My muscles felt unsteady as I carefully put space between us and started to stand, conscious of the drop not far behind me. I still had my harness on, so it wouldn’t have been the end of the world if I’d fallen. It was unlikely that I’d die, but I probably would have lost my team some points, and I didn’t want that.

But I’d pushed myself too far, and I could barely stand. My knees wobbled threateningly, and Seth’s hand closed around my elbow, holding me up. Another cheer sounded from one of the other side of the climbing wall as embarrassment shot through me again, and I looked down, unable to meet his gaze.

“Clear!” one of the team captains shouted in the background.

“Sorry,” I muttered once the noise had settled down, before adding, “thanks.”

“Anytime,” Seth told me. “I meant it, by the way. You did great.”

Somehow, being complimented by him made me even more embarrassed. I didn’t like it when people gave me compliments. It didn’t happen that often, but I never really knew how to respond. I wasn’t sure what a normal or expected answer was, and I rarely felt like I did anything deserving of compliments.

“Thanks. I couldn’t have done it without you,” I said honestly.

Seth’s face scrunched up.

“Don’t be silly. Of course, you could have.”

“I was about to fall when you grabbed me!” I pointed out, gesturing with the arm he’d caught hold of for emphasis. “If you hadn’t, I wouldn’t be up here.”

A grin appeared on Seth’s face, and he opened his mouth to reply before his gaze landed on my arm. The smile slipped from his lips, being replaced with a look of horror.

“Oh no! Are you okay?” he asked, reaching out for my arm.

He cupped it gently, his fingers barely touching my skin, and his tone was so dismayed that concern welled within me. I glanced down at my arm, trying to work out why he looked so devastated and scared, before spotting a red mark that was starting to appear. The distinct outline of fingers circling my forearm where he’d gripped it was growing darker, and Seth’s gaze was fixed upon it.

“Yeah, I’m fine,” I told him.

Seth didn’t seem reassured, though. He just continued to stare at my arm.

“I’m so sorry,” he apologised, finally tearing his eyes away. “Does it hurt?”

He touched the edge of the mark gingerly, and a wince flitted across his face, but I felt no pain.

“No, no, it’s okay,” I said. “It doesn’t hurt. I bet it won’t even bruise.”

Seth’s gaze flicked back and forth between my eyes, seemingly searching for any hint that I might be lying, but I wasn’t. Another shout rang out as he continued to stare at me, followed by a mix of sympathetic and celebratory noises. Someone knocked against my shoulder, sending me stumbling again, as they raced towards one of the other sides.

“Sorry,” the guy said as they glanced back at me before noticing that Seth was still holding my arm. “You okay?”

“Yeah, I’m fine,” I replied, shooting him an embarrassed smile as I pulled my arm away from Seth.

He seemed reluctant to let go, but he did. His expression remained troubled, even as the guy moved away and continued to where they’d been going.

“Kyle fell!” they shouted to our team.

“Yes!” someone screamed out.

“Oh no,” another person cried at the same time.

“Come on! Let’s go, Josie!”

I glanced over my shoulder at the team behind me. As worried as I was about my legs giving way and sending me tumbling over the edge, I wanted to move towards it and cheer on the rest of our team with the others. The atmosphere was electric, and the excitement felt contagious, but Seth was still staring at me with a worried expression.

“Are you sure you’re okay?” he asked quietly as he stepped towards me, seemingly not wanting anyone else to hear our conversation.

The urge to reach up and touch his cheek gripped me. I wasn’t sure why. I’d never done that with anyone in my reality or in the world I was in. I had no idea where the desire had come from, but I had to force it aside. Something told me it would have probably helped him feel better, though.

“I promise,” I said, my tone just as soft as his had been.

Seth’s eyes continued to dart between mine for a moment before he nodded, letting out a breath that caressed my face.

“Okay,” he muttered, seemingly more to himself than to me. “Do you… want to go watch Josie climb?”

A smile appeared on my lips again before I could stop it, and I nodded at Seth. He still seemed troubled by the thought of having potentially hurt me, but he returned my smile and followed me towards the edge of the wall, where my team were cheering Josie on.

Anxiety bubbled in the pit of my stomach, and I could feel Seth hovering close by as I leaned forward as much as I dared and peered down at the girl who was climbing. I was surprised to see how high she already was. Somehow, she was more than halfway up the wall, and I glanced towards the clock. That had to be a new record. If she managed to get the rest of the way up in less than twenty seconds, we’d get an extra ten points.

“Come on, Josie!” I shouted, quickly getting swept up in the mood of the people around me as the crowd at the base of the wall grew.

It felt a little weird to be part of the group who were the centre of attention. Normally, I would have shied away from it. I was more likely to be at the bottom of the wall, looking up at the people who were climbing and wishing I was brave enough to do something like that, but for once, I was. I was one of the people the others were looking at and wishing they were more like, and I didn’t know how to feel about that other than proud, but I was so glad Seth and I had gotten dragged into it.

We hadn’t meant to. We were just going to the gym after dinner. I wanted to work on my strength, and I knew I wouldn’t have a chance to lift weights in the gym for a few more days if we didn’t go. According to my schedule, my fitness classes for the next few days were focused on swimming and cardio, but that wasn’t enough for me. My stamina was okay. It wasn’t great, but it wasn’t too bad. It was my strength that needed more work.

I wasn’t sure if he actually wanted to work out, but Seth had been kind enough to agree to go with me. We’d been passing the door to the climbing gym on the way to the main room, and we’d heard noise. Voices rang out and echoed down the corridor. Seth and I couldn’t help but check it out, and we were quickly roped into the competition by the other trainees.

They were all older. The rest of the people competing against us had been at the Academy for much longer than Seth and me. They were already going out on assignments or working in the GSOD, and that was scary, but the vibe was friendly. Pride was the only prize for the winning team, and it all felt pretty good-natured, but I could feel the undercurrent of competitiveness. It had hooked its claws into me, and I didn’t want my team to lose. I wanted us to emerge victorious, and it seemed like we were going to.

“Yes!” I screamed out as Josie’s hand closed around the top of the wall by my foot.

Immediately, the guy beside me bent down to help pull her up, and I hesitated before realising I should probably do the same. I was in the way. Josie was directly in front of me, and there was no space for me to move aside.

I crouched quickly, the muscles in my legs crying out for help, and grabbed Josie’s harness. Heaving with all of my might, we dragged the girl onto the platform at the top of the wall, but she quickly scrambled away from us. Josie recovered much faster than I had, and within seconds, she was leaning over the wall again.

“Clear! Sam, go!” she bellowed.
