I wake up to the deeply offensive realization that I am, in fact, sleeping on hard wood.

Unacceptable.

My joints ache, my muscles protest, and my brain feels like someone shoved bright lights, adrenaline, and mild regret into a blender and hit purée. For a moment I simply lie there, staring blankly at the hollow ceiling like it personally inconvenienced me.

It probably did.

Slowly, reality filters back in. The faint moss-glow leaking through the doorway, the cool damp air of the Mist-Veiled Grove, and the comforting, smug weight of my tail curled around me like a self-satisfied cat.

…Also, my hair is a disaster.

Tragic.

I sit up, stretching with all the grace of a cat that knows the world owes it admiration. Checking the tutorial countdown reveals I slept for about five hours.

Great.

Eleven hours one night, five the next.

Consistency? Never heard of her.

Annoying.

I stretch again, glaring at nothing in particular, mostly out of principle.

My internal human-world sleep schedule—the one that dutifully dragged me out of bed at 8 AM like a responsible person—is now utterly demolished. Replaced by… whatever unhinged logic stamina operates on.

A system mechanic that determines when I sleep based on mysterious cosmic vibes?

Deeply disrespectful.

Even worse, for the second night in a row, I went to sleep with full stamina.

Full.

Yet—somehow—I was still tired.

Apparently, Inspect believes Stamina only prevents physical fatigue, which implies that mental exhaustion from navigating procedurally generated RPG nonsense, questionable balancing decisions, and terrain placement designed by a sleep-deprived level designer does in fact count as a legitimate way to suffer.

Wonderful.

One ear flicks. The movement contains layers. A concerto of emotion. Somewhere between resigned defeat and existential annoyance.

Then again… perhaps the smug foxgirl capable of melting steel with fire, folding gravity like a fitted bedsheet, and weaponizing starlight like an overachieving mythical cryptid should refrain from criticizing balance.

My ears shoot upright.

My tail joins them in spiritual enlightenment.

Revelation achieved.

Starlight.

I look at my hair, the tragic battlefield upon which physics, humidity, and bad life choices have waged an unrelenting war. It is less "hair" and more "sentient briar patch with attitude."

My eyes drop to my hand.

A plan forms.

I nod at myself. Good. Excellent. Truly, a genius at work.

Starlight gathers in my palm.

Attempt one: geometry personally knocks on my skull and asks me who gave me permission to insult its lineage.

Attempt three: I have… silverware. Not a comb. Not a brush. But a fork. Progress. Technically.

Attempt seven: finally, something functional. A comb. Barely. Spiritually resentful in shape, but usable.

A brush would’ve been ideal, but testing skills costs stamina, and there is an entire forest of hostile flora and fauna waiting to be politely and elegantly introduced to the business end of my sword, so I accept the lesser tool with the grace of a queen forced to drink store-brand tea.

And so, I begin the delicate archaeological process of excavating my hair from chaos.

140 stamina… for a comb.

Worth it. Absolutely. Probably.

…Yes. Worth it.

The new, inconveniently expressive additions on top of my head make the process… complicated, but after approximately thirty minutes of combing, battling, threatening, and possibly negotiating, I manage to detangle my hair—

—Mostly.

Predictably, the comb’s performance reflects its origin. Rushed design, experimental geometry, and the haunting energy of “good enough.” Its work leaves much to be desired, but compared to the tragic disaster it was moments ago, it’s an undeniable improvement.

I will tolerate it.

For now.

Mentally, I add improve starlight manipulation to my already bloated task list. It goes near the top, obviously.

There are universal truths in this world, and one of them is that I must remain impeccably groomed at all times.

Next task: my tail.

I prepare myself for round two, only to discover… it’s already nearly perfect.

No knots. No tangles. No chaos.

Just soft, immaculate fluff.

Apparently Foxkin tails achieve—and maintain—peak fluff with the same effort most people use to breathe.

Incredible.

I must learn how to harness this power.

For science.

Onto the task list it goes, right between become unstoppable and acquire more loot.

With the casual elegance of someone discarding a deeply disappointing prototype, I toss the misshapen comb into my spatial ring and stand. My ears—traitorous, expressive liabilities—perk up the moment I move.

They immediately flatten again the second a foul odor drifts in from the fogged woods ahead.

Rot. Decay. The smell equivalent of a low-budget horror movie.

My tail freezes, likely conserving emotional energy for whatever nonsense is about to arrive.

I follow the stench, noting that the mist has thinned. Visibility is now around fifteen to twenty meters instead of the barely ten I suffered with last night.

Annoying. Predictable. Apparently even fantasy fog respects atmospheric science. Disappointing.

“Four out of ten,” I mutter.

Absolutely unacceptable.

At the bare minimum the mist should be forming ominous tendrils and attempting to mislead me into a tragic yet aesthetically pleasing demise. I came to this world for dramatic fights and interesting scenery, not bargain-bin horror ambience.

And clearly, I am not the only one disappointed.

Two figures shamble through the treeline toward me.

I stare at them with the emotional energy of someone forced to attend a mandatory training seminar.

They continue walking.

Rude.

Illusions are wildly overpowered against anything operating at the intelligence level of a disgruntled root vegetable.
