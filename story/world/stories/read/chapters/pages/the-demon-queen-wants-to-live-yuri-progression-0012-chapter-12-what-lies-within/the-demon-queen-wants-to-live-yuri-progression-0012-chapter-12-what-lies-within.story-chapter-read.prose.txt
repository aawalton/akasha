I woke before the Hero. It was early enough that light had only just begun to press against the shutters.

The room was colored grey by a dimness that only existed between night and morning. Ash was against the far wall, lying on the floor. She was curled on her side, one of her arms tucked beneath the pillow she had claimed. I watched her. Her breathing came out slow and steady.

I sat back and raised my arms above me. There were two Sparks, one on each of my forearms. The Requiem sat on my right, a point of black so deep it seemed to drink the light. The Cradle sat on my left, almost glowing against my skin in this dimness. These were my new powers, and they were small. Almost insignificant to look upon. A child's marks had looked no different.

I could feel these new marks. Beneath the ink of each Spark, something had taken root inside me. There were new channels now, or perhaps it had changed the channels already there. They were as thin as spider silk, and they threaded outward from each mark toward my core. I would make these powers more than they were. The man had spoken of stages, had he not? We had seven days. There was no point in wasting time.

My first test was with the Requiem, as it was the obvious choice. The room had a tiny hearth. I reached over to pick up a piece of firewood that lay beside it. I held it in my right hand, and pushed mana toward the black dot. Nothing happened.

And so I tried again, this time with more mana. My power swirled against the mark and slid right past it. The third time, I tried to be slower, feeding the mana in a thin stream. And now there was a response, the faintest response. I felt an itch from somewhere in my right arm. The wood changed. It felt different beneath my fingers. It was…dryer?  I held it longer and a crack formed along the grain. It was progress. Small, miserable progress but still progress.

I closed my eyes and concentrated. By the time I stopped, Ash was gone. I had not heard her leave. Even diminished, my senses should have caught the sound of footsteps. I glanced through the window and found her immediately.

Ash was across the village, hauling stones and setting them aside. She was wearing the garish pink clothing Martha had given us, which made her easy to find. Her sleeves were rolled past her elbows, and she was sweating through the fabric despite the morning chill. She had been at it for some time, judging by the pile in front of her.

There were more important things. I turned away and returned my attention to the mark.

I moved outside to a spot by the far wall that almost felt familiar now. I sat cross-legged in the grass. I tested a branch first, one that was already dried and stripped of leaves. I wrapped my right hand around it and pushed mana into the Requiem.

I reached for the power and it came more easily than it had before. Perhaps these new pathways needed more usage to widen? It certainly felt like something about it was changing. The bark beneath my fingers aged, peeling back in thin strips to reveal wood that had gone grey. I held it for ten breaths, and the branch crumbled where my fingers pressed.

I did not expect this new power to match even the tiniest of what I had once had, but I had expected it to be useful at the very least. This Requiem was painfully slow.

I picked up another branch. This time, I tried to control the rate of decay. If I could control that, perhaps it had some use after all. I tried to make the rate slower. The branch aged in my hand but did not crumble. Its color shifted from brown to grey, the wood drying until it was light as kindling.

My next test was with a strip of cloth I tore from the hem of the ruined dress Martha had given me. Briefly I hesitated, but then I recalled that she had already replaced it with another. Dirt aside, the thing had stretched too far, especially around the chest. The strip of cloth unwound and then frayed within seconds. A moment later and it fell apart. The power had worked faster on material like this.

The reaction was different from stone. I pressed my palm against the low wall itself and pushed mana into it. I held the flow for thirty seconds before something happened. A crack appeared, emanating outward from my palm in three directions. The crack spread, and something pressed back against my palm. An echo of something so faint I did not catch it. An impression, so faint it impressed little upon me. If this was some key to advancing my power, I did not understand it.

I catalogued the differences and tried to find some pattern between them. Hard materials resisted and softer materials yielded. The rate of decay could be controlled through the volume of mana supplied. The range was limited to direct, sustained contact through the hands.

These were the parameters of my new cage. A cage so small a child would have found it confining. Each time I pushed mana through the Requiem, the Spark seemed to strain. It didn't feel like it was growing, exactly. I did not know what the damned thing was doing. Surely, if it needed power, then I had that to spare.

It wasn't all dissatisfaction though. At least I still had magic. I found myself idly decaying branches, even though I knew the result. Yes, perhaps there was a joy to power for power's sake alone. Each time I did, the Requiem hummed. That feeling of straining came again. It didn't matter for now. Even diminished, I could still unmake things. Destruction I understood well.

I paused to flex my hand. The mana expenditure was slight, and my reserves were quite large, and most of them were still the thicker mana. It was odd -I could pick which one to use -which one to push into the Spark. The older mana was far, far more efficient, and yet I had no way to replenish it.

I looked up. Ash was somewhere else now, not at the wall that had apparently needed repairing. She was by Tom's cart, carrying a crate that Tom had not managed to move. The Hero moved it now as though it were empty. She set the crate down before she ran off again. The Hero was doubtless off to help someone else.

She had been doing this for some time. Perhaps she had not stopped moving since dawn. Something about it pricked at me.

I stared at the white Spark on my left arm, and the Cradle stared back.

I was not neglecting this power, nor was I avoiding it. This was merely  prioritization. Of the two powers, the Requiem was far more useful. In a battle, the ability to decay, however slowly, was a weapon. The Cradle however was…what, exactly? Simply base healing? A power only useful after the fighting was already decided.

This was not the whole truth of this power, and I had known that since the Inking. I steadied myself and pushed mana into the Cradle.

The warmth spread through the pathway and into my palm like sunlight through a window. The power came easily, and when it came it was almost gentle. I reached for a dead branch with my left, the same one I had rotted just moments ago. There was nothing for five seconds. Nothing for seven. I nearly pulled away. Then, I saw a bud.

It was small enough that I almost missed it, had I not been watching so closely. It was tiny and green, pressing out from a joint in the branch where a leaf might once have grown.

I pulled my hand away and the green stayed. It stayed, growing on a branch that had been dead in my grip. I tried again with another branch, until another bud bloomed to life on wood that had been dead.

I tried the cracked stone of the wall as I pressed my left palm to the same spot I had decayed with my right. And I waited for ten heartbeats. I waited longer. And then slowly, so slowly I might have imagined it, the hairline cracks began to fill. It would take minutes to seal even this small wound.

This power was something far more than healing. I had known it, of course. That was why I recoiled so. Still, the power was weak -pathetically weak. Far slower than the Requiem by a wide margin. The Cradle did not sit well in me. Not because it was unpleasant…perhaps that was precisely the problem.

Ash was with the children now.

They were loud, and impossible not to notice. The Hero ran with three of them near the center of the village, weaving back and forth between huts. She was laughing, and the sound of it was warm. I had not heard such a sound from her before.

I caught her favoring her left shoulder, just slightly. It was in the slight hitch in her gait whenever she turned. And there was the redness in her hands, one I could see even from here. They were raw.

I watched for longer than I meant to. Something about the Hero's movements had been strange. Ash had been everywhere. The well. Tom's cart. The section of the wall being repaired. A dozen more places I hadn't seen but had no doubt she'd visited. She had been everywhere, except one place. One hut, and the now quiet mother inside it.

Perhaps I had been watching her too closely after all.

An old man had been struggling with a fence post near me.

He had been at it the entire time I had been training, perhaps thirty paces from my spot by the wall. The post had rotted through at its base, and the grey haired man had been trying to shore it up with mud and twine. Neither had worked so far. It seemed he had no mark for this.

I had been ignoring him in favor of testing my Cradle on a larger piece of dead wood. There was a point to it. How much did the scale of what I attempted affect the outcome? As it happened, the power worked slower and consumed more mana the greater the scale.

The pathway beneath the mark ached but I could still push mana through it. The fence post was right there.

I rose and walked over. The man looked up when my shadow fell on him. His was the startled expression I had seen a hundred thousand times. My gaze had made many at least uncomfortable, even my most loyal of subjects. I wrapped my left hand around the rotted base of the post and pushed mana through the Cradle.

Under my fingers, the grey wood began to firm and color returned to it in slow degrees. It took time. More time than I had expected. And the rot did not vanish entirely, but it receded. The act took thirty seconds. Perhaps less. I let go and walked back to my spot before sitting down and at no point did I look back.

The Cradle pulsed unsteadily in my arm. Something about the Spark was changing, or perhaps it was straining? I had hoped that these tests might be enough to push it further, to one of the stages the Inker had only mentioned in passing. Oh well. This test of scale was still useful.

"Thank you," the man called out.

It had been nothing but a test. He should have known.

I hissed in pain. I looked down to see a cut along my right hand, one I hadn't noticed before. It was along the edge of my palm, and it was now bleeding. It was a small wound, one I might not have even noticed before. It was insignificant. I would simply-

My left hand moved. I channeled mana, and the Cradle answered. Warmth poured from the white Spark and into the broken skin, and I watched the edges of the wound draw toward each other. There was more pain, but the power worked. The sealed skin was slightly raised and pink. And it itched in a way that made me want to tear it open again. But the wound was gone. My instinct had been to mend.

It had been an insignificant wound. I had taken a thousand worse ones. Every time, I had simply waited for someone else to heal me. My powers had let me destroy the world, but they had never so much as let me heal a chipped fingernail. That had been a small part of my chain, one I had never thought to care about. I turned the observation over and set it aside. I did not know what to make of it. I moved on.

It was late afternoon by the time I had exhausted a fourth of my mana. I had ended up using more of my original power than I would have liked.

The marks themselves now ached with a soreness that sat deep beneath the skin. I placed my back against the wall and watched the village. It was a waste. I still had both power and time to spare. The fastest way to grow these new powers was surely to keep using them, and yet I watched anyway.

I saw the Hero. Ash was still going, still moving. She was helping a woman hang clothing now. Her movements were slower than they had been at dawn, but she had not stopped. She had not rested. Perhaps she had not even eaten.

My gaze left her and found that quiet hut again. The only hut in Hamel that did not glow with light from within. Marin, that had been the boy's name. When had I committed it to memory?

The boy's mother sat outside on a low stool. The boy himself was in her arms, wrapped in a blanket. The air was not cold. His head rested against her shoulder and his eyes were open. I knew they saw nothing.

The Hero had been everywhere in Hamel twice over. Almost everywhere. She had never gone near that hut. There was a pattern to it, and it felt familiar. Every task she had thrown herself into had orbited around this hut without ever touching it.

Ash returned to our shared room at dusk. She sat down against the far wall, in the same spot she always chose. Her knees drew up as she rested her arms across them. She was shaking. It was not cold.

"Are you injured?"

Ash looked at me. Her blue eyes were flat. "I'm fine," she said. "I'm just tired."

A lie, and an obvious one. She had not lied to me out of fear. That I was far more used to. I did not push. I did not know how.

The problem was obvious. The question I had asked was wrong. I had known it was wrong even as it had left my mouth. Ash was not injured, any fool could see that. What she was, was something else. A word only a Hero would know. One a Demon Queen never would, or could.

Ash closed her eyes. Within minutes, her breathing slowed and then steadied. Within minutes more, she was asleep. Or pretending to be. I went out.

Restlessness was the reason for it. Our shared room was simply too small, and I simply needed air. The village was asleep at this hour, and the only light came from the moon and the few lanterns still burning. I moved past many of the huts, moved past the hut where the woman and the boy had been.

And as I walked, I passed the fence from earlier. It was still holding. The post stood straight as any arrow, the wood firm where I had pressed the rot from it. It looked sturdy, as sturdy as anything in Hamel did. I looked at it for one second too long and kept walking.

It was some time before I returned. The Hero had not moved. She really was asleep then.

I lay on the bed and raised both arms above me, staring at them in the lantern's dying light. The Requiem, on my right. It was no more than a small black point, though it looked larger than it had before. It had felt like coming home. Destruction was mine, had always been mine.

The Cradle, on my left. A white point, almost as big as its cousin. It had felt like something I had not earned. It was a beginning, in the hands of someone who had only ever brought endings.

I closed my right hand over the Cradle. The warmth pulsed, faint and steady. I held it until I fell asleep.
