A few hours had passed since his victory against Gorvak. But unlike with the High-Elders, Aodhán didn't need to wonder whether Rhetta and her friends had been impressed with his battle—their current shenanigans at the sect's pub were proof enough.

They had arrived two hours earlier, and in all that time, Aodhán had gotten to experience a very different side of the sect. Here in the pub, the people were friendlier and less stuck up, speaking equally to each other regardless of status, power, or identity.

And despite the passing of time, the place was still buzzing with the excitement of his—or rather, his and Crixis's—victories. Toasts slammed onto wooden tables amid riotous laughter, chairs scraped across the polished floors, and every few moments someone would loudly recount how they still couldn't believe the two of them had won, describing the duels from wildly different perspectives.

"It's absolutely bonkers!" a man declared for the umpteenth time, waving a jar of Thundervein in the air. "Both of you were marvelous in that ring, but you—" He pointed at Aodhán. "—You were amazing. That skill of yours was downright grotesque."

"Indeed," a woman agreed loudly from the other side of the pub. "I damn near fell on my ass just watching it."

Laughter rippled through the room, and another woman called out, "Rianat is absolutely right—only in my case, I did fall on my ass."

"She also lost hundreds of sect points betting on that idiot Gorvak," her partner added, face flushed with excitement. He raised his jug toward Aodhán and bellowed, "Another round to celebrate the winners—I won the freaking jackpot!"

More laughter exploded as the woman lunged at him in mock fury. When she failed to grab him, she groaned. "Ugh, it was stupid betting on Gorvak. The man's been face-farming for months—should've known a real talent would knock him flat eventually."

"But he didn't just lose!" Yerxa jeered, drawing another eruption of laughter. "He got drained like a spirit-deaf novice!"

"Damn right he did," the woman groaned, draining an entire jar of lightning beer in one gulp. She grimaced at the taste and shook her head. "Still, I couldn't be happier you newbies won." Her gaze slid to Crixis beside Aodhán, and she smiled. "Both of you. Marvelous duels."

"Thank you, ma'am." Aodhán replied politely—just as he had to nearly everyone else over the past two hours. "We're very grateful."

And he was indeed. Not only had no one asked him to reveal his face yet, none of them seemed to harbor any resentment toward him for his victory either. For that alone, Aodhán was grateful, though the flowing non-alcoholic Thundervein he was drinking was a welcome bonus.

Crixis was just as excited as he was, though a bit more uncomfortable in the rowdy space. His discomfort, however, vanished the moment Yerxa climbed atop their table to loudly declare for the umpteenth time that she had always known they would both win and had been rooting for them both from the very beginning.

Aodhán shook his head in amusement, knowing for a fact that she hadn't even known Crixis existed until his duel against Gump. Crixis didn't seem to mind though. He raised a half-finished jar of unfermented Thundervein in cheers—the same thing Aodhán was drinking—and smiled widely.

"To putting bullies in their places."

Cheers rang out across the pub, glass jars clinking together, and moments later the barmaids arrived, arms laden with another round of drinks for everyone. Whoops of excitement and laughter erupted as wine flowed, and before long Aodhán was handing his own jar over for a refill.

"Oh, this is amazinggg," Yerxa squealed as she stumbled down from the table, her face flushed with excitement. "We totally should have bet on Aodhán and Crixis. Oh, Zarion, why did you end my betting addiction? We could have been swimming in sect points by now."

"Or drowning in debt," Zarion burped, grabbing a jar of wine from a passing barmaid. He offered Aodhán another drink, but Aodhán shook his head.

"No thanks! Someone's got to be responsible for you lot when all of this is over."

"Oh, don't be a sourpuss, Aodhán," Rhetta scolded. "You won your first ever match in the sect. Do you know how rare that is? Most of us lost our first three duels at least."

"Not us, that's for sure," Zarion muttered, returning the drink to the tray. "I only won my first duel after dozens of losses."

Yerxa giggled. "And by dozens, he means eighty-seven."

"Hey!" Zarion protested. "I don't go about revealing your losses. You take that back and tell them you were lying this instant!"

"Make me!" Yerxa stuck her tongue out at him. "I am a gentle woman. I never lie!"

Rhetta snorted. "The only thing you do is lie, Yerxa. You're practically the brand ambassador of lying!"

"Hey! I am not."

"You so are," Zarion slurred. "You're lying even now!"

"Hey!" Yerxa shrieked playfully, and an argumentative banter ensued amongst the three friends. Aodhán and Crixis watched them in amusement, only jumping in occasionally to keep the fun going.

Despite himself, Aodhán was having a lot of fun. He had feared that Rhetta and her friends would be horrified by his duel, but they didn't seem to care. At all. And it wasn't just them.

Aodhán could imagine the reaction this same skill would have had in Lutia. If he had done something like this back home, he would have been front-page news once again. But here, no one seemed to care.

Instead, they had met him with an overwhelming amount of excitement. Apparently, the sect didn't care in what way or form a person's power manifested. All they cared about was strength.

And Aodhán had proven that he had that in spades.

The thought brought him a measure of comfort. If the crowd accepted him so easily, perhaps the High Elders had reacted the same way. Hopefully, he would be able to get in contact with Zarxan very soon. He had a ton of questions to ask and not enough time.

The rest of the afternoon passed quickly, the excitement of the pub only increasing with each hour, and it wasn't until the sun dipped past the horizon that they eventually stepped out, laughing and chatting excitedly amongst themselves.

"Ugh!" Yerxa stumbled, draping her arms across Aodhán and Crixis's shoulders. "What a great day. Your initiation is tomorrow, isn't it?"

"It is," Crixis smiled at her. "Tomorrow we'll become full-fledged disciples like you."

"Oh." Zarion burped. "Don't make me cry. That's so sweet. We can't wait to have you both."

"Yes, yes." Rhetta shook her head slowly, as if moving it too quickly might make her brain fall off. "Too sweet. I bet your trials will be just as sweet. You won't even need to break a sweat."

"Or think, for that matter," Yerxa scowled. "Rhetta only did spirit tests. Not a single combat trial."

Aodhán chuckled. "You really do have to let that go at some point, Yerxa."

"Tell her!" Rhetta agreed. "I mean, it was a year ago. Right, Zarion?"

Zarion burped again. "No, I think it was two years."

Yerxa giggled and shook her head. "These two are so drunk. The incident we're talking about was like five years ago."

Aodhán laughed aloud at that, unable to keep it in any longer. "You're all so drunk. Do you do this every time?"

"After every successful duel, yes," Rhetta nodded. "I must say, we overindulged today, though. You don't realize how much it means for your reputation that you win your first duel."

"And the second," Yerxa pointed out, resting her head against Crixis's shoulder. "Oh, I can't wait for you both to become disciples so we can go on missions together. Delve rifts. Hunt spirit beasts."

Aodhán smiled sadly at that. Rhetta and her friends hadn't officially invited him and Crixis into their group, but it was obvious they wanted them. He didn't have the heart to tell them it was never going to happen.

He was a transmigrant. Even if he somehow failed his trial, he doubted he would be placed in the outer sect. And from the blazing intensity of Crixis's core, he was obviously too strong for the outer sect as well.

It was a shame, because Aodhán really liked this group. They reminded him of his family back in Lutia. Crixis of Daruk, Yerxa of Yurin, Zarion of Aldric, and Rhetta of Andrew.

Aodhán would have been more than content to stay here if the sect would allow him, but they wouldn't. And beyond that, he had a goal to accomplish. If he was to reach the Mythic class as soon as possible, then he had to push himself. He needed resources. He needed rivals who could match him, challenge him, and force him to grow.

And the outer sect couldn't offer him any of that.

They reached a parting junction a few minutes later, and Crixis said, "This is where we part ways. Are you sure you can make it to your quarters on your own?"

"Oh yes. Don't worry about us," Rhetta replied confidently, stumbling even as she pointed vaguely toward the sky. "We live right there. Exactly there. Can you see it?"

"Totally," Aodhán snorted. "But let's walk you back regardless. Just in case."

Crixis nodded in agreement, steadying Yerxa's arm to prevent her from stumbling. She wrapped an arm around his neck and burped loudly. Crixus shook his head with a wry smile. "No, you're definitely not drunk at all."

Fortunately, the residential quarters were only a short distance from the intersection, and a few minutes later, they reached it—a veritable barracks of storm-themed skyscrapers, each one housing hundreds of disciples.

Aodhán and Crixis weren't allowed past the entrance, so after handing Rhetta, Yerxa, and Zarion over to the disciples on security duty, they could finally rest at ease, assured that none of their friends would spend the night in a gutter somewhere. They said their goodbyes amid drunken laughter and more teasing banter, and then they were finally alone.

A contented silence settled between them for a moment, until Crixis spoke. "I must confess, I'm not a people person, but those three are far too much fun."

Aodhán chuckled, nodding in agreement.

Crixus's next words, however, caused his smile to falter.

"It's a shame we won't know them long." His expression sobered, and he shook his head. "I think I might actually miss them."

Aodhán let the words settle before nodding slowly. "Me too. Those three have been the nicest people I've met in a while. No ulterior motives, just pure fun."

Crixis nodded as if he understood Aodhán perfectly. Aodhán thought he truly might.

"Unfortunately for us, I doubt such openness awaits us in the higher sects." He looked up at Aodhán, eyes sharp despite the dim light. "Especially for you."

Aodhán turned to face him fully, his tone even. "What do you mean by that?"

Crixis frowned, as if suddenly unsure. "You… you're different. You hide your face behind that cowl, but occasionally, when you talk or laugh, I catch a glimpse of it. And I'm nearly certain you've got a bloodline. Either that, or you're the color of midnight itself."

Aodhán smirked, amused by Crixis's words. To be honest, he was surprised his cowl had fooled anyone at all. He'd half-expected someone to demand he remove it.

He said nothing, though, and after a moment, Crixis took his silence as permission to continue. "I must also say that the whole mystery act isn't really working. You've admitted to not being from Gordon. Your accent is foreign—Lutian, if I had to guess. Which means you've crossed the continental barrier, something you could only have done with the aid of a calamity. A High Elder, perhaps."

Aodhán frowned, surprised by how easily Crixis had connected the dots. "That's a lot of assumptions."

"True," Crixis chuckled. "But calamities are selfish by nature. They won't leave their meditation chambers unless they have something significant to gain. Crossing the continental barrier for a single storm cultivator? That's not something I see any of them doing—unless they're a High Elder. High-Elders care about the sect's future. They invest in potential."

Aodhán glanced at him sidelong, his lips pulling into a faint smile. "You're still making a lot of assumptions."

"I am," Crixis admitted with a slight grin. "But if I'm right, then you must be someone very special for a High Elder to cross the continental barrier just for you."

"Perhaps they found me amusing."

Crixis snorted. "Calamities are never amused. Not even High Elders. They're too far removed from us mortals to relate to such basic emotions."

Aodhán doubted that was entirely true, but he didn't push the point. Instead, he asked, "Let's say you're right. What does it change?"

"Everything," Crixis replied, his expression turning serious. "Your trial will almost certainly be different from ours. You'll be held to a higher standard—tested more rigorously. I doubt your initiation will be as simple as Rhetta and Yerxa make it out to be."

Aodhán frowned at that. It wasn't new information, but having his worry confirmed sent a spike of tension through him—not fear of failure, but dread of the consequences of passing. Because he would pass.

He was a transmigrant. He couldn't fail the test of his identity and spirit even if he wanted to. He would pass, and then he would be proclaimed the fourth transmigrant in the Storm Sect, sparking what he imagined would be a massive continental war.

No, he wasn't scared of failing. He was scared of passing.

The rest of their journey passed in silence until they reached the visitor's quarters. Then Crixis spoke again, his tone soft. "It seems my words have troubled you. That wasn't my intention."

"It's fine," Aodhán waved away his concern. "I needed to hear it. And I'm glad it came from you."

Crixis nodded. "I just wanted you to be better prepared for tomorrow." He smiled then, as if an amusing thought had just struck him. "You know, when I told you this morning never to turn down a duel, I didn't realize you'd take it so seriously."

Aodhán chuckled. "Well, in my defense, trouble came looking for me—not the other way around."

Crixis shook his head, grinning. "At least you got something out of it. I wish I'd thought of that."

Aodhán smiled. "I'd argue you got something too. Fame and recognition."

Crixis snorted. "That's nothing compared to a sword, and you know it."

They reached their individual rooms a moment later, and Crixis raised a finger to his forehead in farewell. "Thanks for today, Aodhán. I wish you the best tomorrow."

"You too," Aodhán mimicked the gesture. A few seconds later, he stepped into his room and locked the door behind him.

Smiling faintly, he pulled Gorvak's sword from his spatial storage and turned—

A choked scream ripped from his throat as he came face to face with High Elder Al'ruhn himself, runic irises blazing like twin suns in the darkness.

The High Elder smirked. "So jumpy for one so strong. Is this the effect of what they call it? Trauma? It's like a disease these days. Every young cultivator seems to be catching it."

Aodhán's heart hammered in his chest, but he forced himself to bow. "Good evening, High Elder Al'ruhn. I saw you and the other High Elders at my duel."

Zarxan's smirk didn't fade. "And what of it?"

Aodhán would have loved to ask if the other Elders had been impressed, but he pushed past that question to ask what truly mattered. "Can you tell me anything about my initiation trial tomorrow? Will it be different from the others'?"

"Possibly." Zarxan's tone was maddeningly casual. "Are you scared of failing?"

Aodhán swallowed. "I'm scared of passing."

"And yet, only by passing will you reach the Mythic class as quickly as possible—assuming you still intend to return to Lutia."

"I do," Aodhán replied without hesitation, memories flashing through his mind like lightning. "My family is waiting for me."

Zarxan studied him for a long moment, something unreadable flickering behind those blazing eyes. When he finally spoke, his tone had shifted—quieter, almost introspective. "Family. It's rare to see a transmigrant with one at all. You've been truly blessed, Aodhán."

Aodhán nodded soberly. Most transmigrants in Lutia didn't even get the chance to experience friendship before they were killed. He'd had more than friends. He'd had love, brothers, a family, and countless acquaintances who'd shaped him. He had been lucky.

He exhaled slowly. "I'll remember to count my blessings."

"See that you do." Zarxan's smile returned, though it carried a weight now. "And as for passing—I fear you don't really have a choice. The other High Elders were impressed with your display today. More than impressed, truthfully. They're eager to initiate you into the sect." His expression sobered completely. "You'll be proclaimed tomorrow, Aodhán. And I know you didn't choose this path, but I hope—for your sake—that you're ready."

Aodhán opened his mouth to respond, but the words died in his throat.

He wasn't ready for this. But he hadn't been ready for Abyssos either, had he? And he'd survived. More than survived—he'd thrived.

He would thrive here too. He just had to make sure he didn't die first.

And to do that, he needed power. Real power. The kind that could weather the storm he was about to unleash.

He needed to become truly formidable.

Lest the war he ignites swallow him whole and drown him in its depths.
