With Maiya still in his arms, a crushing field of gravity spread out in a hundred-yard radius around Vir, but without first understanding how fast his foe was, he didn’t dare concentrate the field, lest it escape.

The creature stumbled, its impossible weight suddenly multiplied a hundredfold. The ground beneath it cracked and collapsed, forming a crater as the soft Ash failed to support the enormous force.

But the creature was strong. It roared a roar that made the air vibrate and stood back up despite the gravity, muscles bulging as it fought against the incredible force.

Vir didn't give it time to adapt. “Mai?”

“On it!” came his wife’s immediate response.

Ordering his Artifact Chakrams to let loose, he activated Dance of the Shadow Demon and sank into the ground, reappearing behind the creature in no time at all.

His chakrams were already closing in, their incomprehensibly sharp edges promising to tear the creature asunder.

Maiya didn’t wait. Still half in shadow, she fired the S Lightning spell Devil’s Playground.

Ordinarily a spell that could turn half a town into an apocalyptic field of endless Lightning strikes, what came out was a single, focused torrent of Lightning so strong that the thunder that followed it forced Vir to retreat back into the shadows, lest their bodies be cut in two from the blast.

When he resurfaced some distance away, the crater he’d caused had grown tenfold on account of Maiya’s handiwork.

Their weapons bit deep, carving through chitin and flesh that normal metal could never dream of penetrating. Black blood sprayed across the ash, hissing where it landed.

The creature blurred as it spun, its massive claw swiping at Vir's head. But he’d already seen the attack coming, in more ways than one. Iksana’s Clarity showed him where the claw connected and would crush his skull.

He twisted away from its path, the strike whistling harmlessly past his face.

With his Third Eye Chakra opened fully, Vir was granted total spatial awareness, which enhanced Clarity and vice versa. He knew the creature's next move before it did, he could sense Maiya positioning herself fifty yards away for a focused S-rank spell, and could track the wolves as they circled around to flank their prey. Information flooded his mind in a torrent, every secret of the battlefield laid bare before him.

It was, as he had learned, too much information. For what good were a hundred paths to victory when he could only choose one? The more options, the longer that process took.

The creature struck again, and again, each attack faster and more vicious than the last. But Vir moved through them like water, his body responding to threats before they were allowed to materialize. Clarity showed him the possibilities, and his enhanced reflexes let him navigate the safest paths through the storm that would have ended most demons.

Maiya finally joined the assault. Fire erupted in a jet from the Artifact that held her orbs in another space. It was followed by ice, then lightning, mixing into an elemental fury that lanced out at the creature.

Each spell was capable of leveling a city on its own. Each spell would normally take tens of minutes to charge, but Maiya had done all that beforehand, and instead of summoning some grand cosmic working, the attack was focused on a single point, magnifying its devastation tenfold.

A pillar of flame engulfed the creature's left side, but before it could recover, the ice spell combined with it to form a steam combustion explosion, which her Lightning attack seized, arced between the cloud that formed to create a chain reaction that made the creature scream in agony.

Nothing in their universe would have survived that, Vir was sure. Nothing that could be harmed by prana, anyway. The greatest Wyrm wouldn’t have stood a chance.

But here, where all the rules were bent or broken, it wasn't enough. The thing was too large and durable, filled as it was with an unknowable amount of Ash.

No, to end this, they’d need more. They’d need the power of the Crown.

His highest Chakra opened, and reality itself warped as his godly avatar manifested behind him, a towering figure of pure prana that dwarfed even the massive creature before them.

Vir had, over the years, learned to manipulate its size, though it was good for little other than shock and awe. Humanoid in shape but wrong in every other way, it boasted far too many arms and heads, each holding a different weapon in its arms. The avatar wasn’t real, of course. It couldn’t harm his enemies any more than a fly, but that didn’t matter.

Power flooded through Vir’s every pore, and every ability he possessed was suddenly multiplied tenfold.

Balancer of Scales intensified until the creature's own body began to collapse under its weight. Haste accelerated his perception until the world seemed frozen. Even Clarity expanded, showing him not seconds but minutes into the future—a branching tree of far too many possibilities that he struggled to navigate, even with his enhanced mind.

And then there was Aspect of the Demon God.

Horns erupted from his skull and flames of black prana wreathed his body, thicker than ever before, and in that moment, Vir became something more than mortal.

He moved. The creature tried to track him but failed. Vir was everywhere at once, his Artifact chakrams carving through limbs and organs with surgical precision. Each strike was guided by Clarity, aimed at weak points that shouldn't have been visible, hitting nerves and tendons and vital organs hidden beneath layers of armor.

The wolves joined in, their forms blurring as they bounded and struck, each bite widening the holes Maiya and Vir had created.

They were careful, only attacking when they were sure not to get in the way, working in perfect synchrony with Vir's assault.

Maiya's magic never stopped. Wind blades sliced through the air, each one sharp enough to cut into the beast’s armor, and spikes of Earth erupted from below, impaling the creature and restricting its movement.

Water formed into high-pressure jets that punched through chitin like crossbow bolts through steel. If her earlier strike could have leveled a town, the torrent of elemental power on display could have destroyed Sonam.

The creature fought back desperately, but it was outmatched. Between Vir's overwhelming power, Maiya's relentless assault, and the wolves' coordinated attacks, it never stood a chance.

Vir's katar found its heart, and he drove the blade in to the hilt, channeling every drop of Ash prana in his body through the weapon.

The creature detonated from within, its body unable to contain the surge of power. Chunks of flesh and chitin rained down across the wasteland, disappearing into the ash that covered the ground as they fell.

Vir stood in the center of the carnage with his eyes closed, focusing on shutting his Chakras. The avatar behind him dissolved without a trace and his horns receded as his skin returned to normal.

“That,” Maiya said, walking over, “was excessive.”

“It was necessary,” Vir replied. “Not a single soul in our universe would stand a chance against the beasts of this realm.”

Not even Cirayus, he mused grimly.

While the sheer amount of prana coursing through this realm amplified every ability they possessed, while they were stronger here than they'd ever been, capable of feats that would have been impossible in their own universe, they were new to the realm. The creatures that roamed here had been around for dozens—or even hundreds—of years, expanding their prana during all that time.

“This is the reality they wish upon the world,” Vir said quietly, looking around at the grey wasteland. “One where most Ash beasts can't even survive.”

“Only the monstrous ones,” Maiya agreed.

In the distance, they could see movement. Gigantic Wyrms slithered across the horizon, each one miles in length and thick enough to be seen even from this distance.

Other shapes moved in the Ash—monstrous beings too large and terrible to fully comprehend. And far away, almost at the edge of visibility, they saw the distinctive shell of a mahakurma, one of the island-sized turtles that had existed peacefully in their realm.

Here, it had been corrupted. As they watched, the creature's mouth opened, so slowly that Vir wondered if he’d imagined it, before a beam of red death finally lanced out, cutting across hundreds of miles of landscape and razing everything in its path.

Thankfully, its gaze was directed elsewhere, or Vir wasn’t even certain they’d be alive to have witnessed it.

He’d wondered why they’d encountered so few creatures thus far. But there were plenty of terrors out there that thrived here. Beings that had grown fat on the prana, that had adapted to this nightmare realm and made it their home.

They’d simply eaten the ones who hadn’t.

Vir and Maiya continued their journey with the wolves in silence, each lost in their own thoughts, until they finally came upon the ruins of Daha as the light began to fade—though whether there were day/night cycles in this realm or if it was simply the Ash growing thicker, Vir couldn't say.

Hiranya's capital, for all its flaws, had once been the jewel of the country.

Now, it was nothing but a half-buried husk.

Vir wondered just how many corpses the ash had buried. All those who had been unable to flee the march of the Boundary.

He could almost picture them dying where they stood, frozen in expressions of terror and agony, unable to flee.

“An entire civilization,” Vir muttered. “Gone in days. Maybe hours.”

But as they walked through the city, incongruities began to appear.

For one, there wasn’t a single massive wall as Vir had remembered, but several. Tens of rings of smaller walls, with the outermost in the most disrepair. When Vir looked back, he spotted several that had been mostly buried. Ones he’d missed entirely.

“What in the realms?” Maiya asked, catching onto the same thing. “Why’d they build so many walls?”

Or rather, Vir thought, how did they have the time?

Was this some desperate preparation against the future encroachment of the Boundary or something else?

The walls grew taller and in better repair as they pressed into the city, though that could simply have been because they weren’t as buried.

In his universe and here, some unknowable mechanism kept the Ash from burying everything. With so much soot falling endlessly, there shouldn’t be a single building still visible—the ground would climb to the clouds.

Yet it didn’t, only burying some things and not others. It made no sense, but Vir had given up trying to understand the broken realm ages ago.

Regardless, these walls were not some flimsy internal affair, designed to segregate classes. These were defensive emplacements, built to keep out Ash Beasts.

What became increasingly obvious as they walked on, passing through streets that had once bustled with life, was that there had been no haphazard evacuation, as the Hiranyan refugees had said.

Buildings were completely empty. Nothing worth taking was left behind, and even in the interiors of buildings where the Ash couldn’t reach, there were no bodies or signs of upheaval.

No, this city reminded Vir far more of Matali’s Raaka than anything. A city that had endured a slow decline, slowly atrophying until only a husk was left.

“But that makes no sense,” Maiya replied when Vir shared his thoughts. “That doesn’t match what the refugees said. They said—”

“That they’d left loved ones behind,” Vir completed, his voice hoarse as a terrible inkling dawned upon him. “That they’d barely escaped. I remember. But what if they’re wrong?”

“Wrong?” Maiya asked, turning to meet his gaze. “How could everyone… oh, no. You better not be saying what I think you’re saying.”

“They rewrote reality, Mai,” Vir muttered. “They turned back time and erased everyone’s memories. Why should that have been the first time?”

“No.” Maiya shook her head. “That’s…”

“Horrific? Inhuman?” Vir’s voice was as cold as ice. “Need I remind you our enemy is not human?”

“But that would mean everything they know is a lie.”

Vir shook his head. “Who knows for how long they’ve been living that lie? For all we know, our enemy could have kept the same people alive for centuries. Millennia. Continuously resetting their memories and their reality. Nothing more than puppets in a sick play.”

Maiya’s face turned pale. “Vir?” she asked in a tiny voice. “D-d’you think… if they did that here…”

Vir shook his head, knowing where she was going with this. “It’s impossible to say, Mai, but I’d like to think not. I’d like to think they don’t have the sort of power in our universe to do something like this to us.”

“But we can’t know that, huh?” Maiya replied, clenching her jaw.

“No, we can’t,” Vir admitted. “The only thing we can do is ensure it never happens again.”

He turned to regard Daha's castle, or what remained of it. The proud fortress had partially collapsed, its towers broken from centuries of neglect. But the main structure still stood, at least.

Vir and Maiya approached with the wolves padding silently beside them. The massive doors hung open, revealing only darkness within.

“Ready?” Vir asked.

“No,” Maiya said. “But when has that ever stopped us?”

And what choice do we have? her expression said.

They stepped through the threshold and into the empty ruins of Daha's castle, leaving the half-buried streets behind them.

Deep beneath their feet, assuming it hadn’t escaped in all this time, they would find a captive Prana Swarm.

And then they would teach it how to end everything.
