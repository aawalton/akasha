Gary stared at Asim’s message. He remembered seeing that first red flare, then a second as he chased the wizard, then a third as he fought him. Had they come from the same direction? He didn’t think so.

Cog: Holy shit, Gary! Jump up! Jump up!

Gary jumped. The second he passed the grass wall, his heart dropped. Red flares lit up the sky in four different directions. In the near distance, a snake much larger than the one he’d fought creeped on top of the walls, chewing on what seemed like four beef boys. They easily fit in its mouth.

As Gary looked the other way, he crossed gazes with a frog hanging from the top edge of a grass wall. It was looking directly at him. They weren’t close enough to inspect the frog, but he recognized it.

General Whipfrog…

Gary’s heart sank. The frog grinned at him. “Found you,” it mouthed, then leaped in his direction. A crunch from the other side made him turn to discover that the snake had spat out its meal and was slithering rapidly. Its gaze was locked on him. More large monsters jumped over the walls, heading his way. They came from every direction. Screams filled his ears from all around.

Gary finally landed, and the grass wall blocked all horrors.

“Run. Now,” he said, taking off first as he blasted orders into the chat.

Gary: This is a trap. Everyone run for your lives. Goldie will direct you.

Goldie: Everyone head towards the direction of the sun. I’ll tell you if we’re close enough. Run as fast as you can. Don’t jump over the walls. Don’t stop for nothing.

Asim: Already running.

Osteo: Yes, boss!

Evil Shrek: Why am I getting out of my swamp?

Donkey: Donkey!

Gary had no time to check if everyone was following. Those large monsters had to be Core Formations, and not low level, either. He couldn’t face any of them. His group was surrounded, but if they stayed under the walls, they might manage to—

A chill ran through Gary, and he dodged without thinking. A lance landed where he used to stand. No, not a lance—a tongue. A huge, black tentacle coming from the top of the wall right next to him. Gary raised his gaze to find a chameleon sticking horizontally to the wall, tongue flicking in and out. It was, obviously, a tentacle.

Lieutenant Lickmaster, Level 33 (Field Boss)

This boy has skills. He’d won the title of most desired chameleon three years in a row before the puppeteer turned them all into tentacle zombies. Now fully integrated with its tentacles, this swell guy wants to dominate much more than just your genitals. Run, bitch.

The chameleon wore a lax leather shirt.

“Run!” Gary yelled. The tongue shot at him faster than Feng Shen’s thrust. Even thunderstepping away, he took a graze to the thigh, tearing off a chunk of flesh like he was made of butter. The chameleon’s eyes span in different directions, one blue and the other green. The flaps on its ridge turned into tentacles and started whipping Gary.

“Fan the Spear!” he yelled, but the attacks were too strong. One struck him in the stomach, sending him flying into a grass wall. He drilled deep into it. Gasping from the pain, he roared and forced himself out the other way, bleeding from a hundred small cuts. His regeneration was already patching him up. He saw the wall shake as the chameleon climbed over it.

Run, was the only thought in Gary’s head. He’d lost track of the others. For the chameleon to be following so quickly, he could only hope it was pursuing him first.

We’ll hold it back. They’re after us. Everyone, run! Goldie told the chat, having arrived at the same conclusion as Gary. This was a trap. The entire labyrinth was a trap, and he’d walked right into it.

Fighting was senseless. Sheathing his spear, he bolted through the paths, picking turns at random and not daring to jump over the walls. The monsters were here for him, that much was clear. At least some monsters. His only hope was to lose them in the maze.

Mission Announcement: Your sector boss has been assassinated. The mission has failed. Retreat if you can. It’s every boy for themselves.

Assassinated? Gary thought, but that was the last thing he cared about. He slid through a corner, then another. The little starlight energy he’d gathered was focused on his legs, accelerating him slightly. He didn’t dare use Thunder Step in fear that they’d discover him. It wasn’t meant for long sprints, anyway.

“Found you, little one…” an old, creepy voice wafted over. Gary jumped, barely dodging a tongue which stabbed into the dirt before him. Something stared at him from above. It had always been there. If it hadn’t spoken, Gary would have taken the tongue in the back. It was toying with him.

Tentacle General Whipfrog, Level 40 (Sector Boss)

Woah! You’re dead!

This is one of the few tentacle bosses who maintained their rank after their breakthrough, by the way. A rank it gained partly because of you. It’s almost poetic, but I can’t see the beauty because I’m pissed I’ll lose a talented climber like yourself. I’m pretty you’d have won me my bet with Wayrod, who’s the most annoying liaison on the tower.

Oh, well. It was fun while it lasted. Enjoy the afterlife, you crazy bastard.

Gary threw all caution to the wind. He thunderstepped five times in a row, barely dodging the cannonball-like tongue attacks. There was just no way to fight a Core Formation sector boss. He would be helpless even against a regular level 40 monster.

Though the tongue was blinding fast, he suspected the frog was slowing it down on purpose. That suspicion was cemented by the relaxed, sadistic laughter coming from its throat. Red flares burned the sky all around, and everyone from his army was retreating. Even if not, no one could beat a level 40 sector boss.

Gary had never been in deeper shit.

A tentacle from the frog’s back grazed him slightly. The impact broke the skin on his back and several ribs and sent him flying through a grass wall. He gasped. He shakily got up and thunderstepped, sensing the pain skewering him with every step. Even his regeneration couldn’t patch this up quickly.

“You took something, little one, and Mother wants it back…” the frog called from somewhere behind him. Gary had no destination or hope. He just ran.

Turning a corner, he found himself sprinting into a Muscle Tree space. He stopped just outside the tree’s range. He tried to circle it, hoping the tree would somehow delay General Whipfrog, but froze halfway through. A figure stood on the opening of the next path, blocking his way. It wasn’t a monster. It was something worse.

“Well met, Gary Steel,” Jin the assassin said. Faint whirring sounds came from under his leather armor, and his hands held onto two glowing neon knifes. Their blades were spotless, but spurts of fresh blood covered his armor. It didn’t look to be his.

“The brothers…” Gary said, feeling the earth spinning below him.

“Just me for now, but I have a few bosses, as well. You did pretty well for a Birth of Aura. Now, surrender. You’re surrounded.”

Large tentacle bosses stood on the grass walls all around him. All were field bosses. The frog appeared on the opening behind him, wearing a twisted smile. “Mother wants you alive, but she didn’t say unhurt…” it called in its old, creepy voice.

“What is this?” Gary asked weakly.

“You made too many enemies, Gary Steel. We want you dead, and the puppeteer wants you alive—for now. I don’t know why you thought you could survive this war,” the cyborg said.

Gary slowly connected the dots. “You abandoned an entire mission just to bring a dozen bosses to catch me?” he asked.

Jin only smiled.

“You understand so little, little one. What is one battle? What is one invasion? With what you have, Mother will win all wars. We will climb to floor 24, and then… We’ll see…” General Whipfrog said.

Gary was lost for words. He knew the puppeteer would come after him, but to risk the entire war just to surround him with so many bosses just to make sure…

How fucked am I?

“Gary! You still have that life-saving treasure!” Goldie whispered in his mind.

“I know,” he whispered back, gritting his teeth.

“The puppeteer thought a single boss would be enough. She underestimates geniuses, like all monsters. Thankfully, we were there to advise,” Jin said, stepping forward. “I got an interesting report, too. Do you have a Ball Reader?”

“What’s it to you?”

“We’re looking for that item, but it’s surprisingly hard to buy. Let me see it,” he said, stretching out an arm.

Gary pretended to think about it. He’d use his life-saving treasure to escape, but the more he delayed, the better the chance of his friends getting out as well. He had no more use for the Ball Reader, anyway.

“Well?” Jin asked.

“Here,” Gary said, drawing the eggshell-like device from his space ring and tossing it to Jin. The assassin admired it against the sky.

“Great,” he said. The ball reader disappeared. “Because you gave it so nicely, I won’t hurt you more than necessary. It’s time. Please lie flat on the ground.”

“Okay,” Gary said, slowly sheathing his spear. He’d earned as much time as he could. With a sudden move, an item from his space ring appeared in his hand. It was the Flying Fools Card he’d bought with his merit points.

As soon as he poured aura into it, the card expanded into a cloud of aura, which turned into a giant eagle with its claws around his shoulders. The rest of the aura turned into a shield around him.

“So long!” Gary yelled, but Jin only smiled. He tossed something at the eagle. There came a whooshing sound, and the eagle cawed as it was sucked into a ball the size of Gary’s fist. It clanked to the ground. The shield around Gary remained, but without the eagle, he couldn’t escape anyway.

“No,” he muttered.

“Oh, Gary, did you think it would be so easy?” Jin said, shaking his head. “My brothers and I aren’t the idiots you’re used to facing. We know that geniuses are like growth bosses. They must be faced with overwhelming force or not at all, so we prepared all sorts of items. Unless you carry a life-saving treasure above the Nascent Soul realm, you aren’t escaping today. Surrender.”

Gary chuckled in resignation. He didn’t want to surrender. He wanted to escape, but it was impossible. He had no more life-saving artifacts. No allies. His only solace was that, since all the bosses focused on him, his friends had probably escaped.

But he couldn’t. It was completely hopeless. Or was it? He glanced at the tree beside him, then the ground beneath his feet, a crazy plan forming in his mind.

“Fuck you,” he said, diving head-first for the Muscle Tree. A flying dagger crashed into his remaining shield from the Flying Fools Card, exhausting it mid-way. The instant he entered the tree’s radius, the branch slapped him in the face, just as he expected. The world span wildly. The shield protected him from the damage, but the branch’s momentum remained, sending Gary flying. He’d calculated the angle he jumped at so the strike would send him over the grass wall. Mid-spinning, he saw Jin looking annoyed. The bosses looked excited.

“Rough him up,” the frog said, and they all chased him like a flood. He saw them shrinking in his sight as he flew farther away. The Muscle Tree’s slap had sent him flying over the walls, which gave him a small headstart, but there was no way he could escape the labyrinth. He wasn’t nearly fast enough. Once he landed, they’d quickly catch up.

But escaping the labyrinth wasn’t the point.

Gary was falling. The monsters were faster than him now, but he wasn’t looking at them. His eyes were unblinkingly staring at the ground he passed over.

Suddenly, he saw what he was looking for. “There!”

Turning upside down, he thunderstepped, accelerating his fall towards the ground just below. He thunderstepped more.

“Don’t let him break his neck!” Jin yelled, dashing through the grass paths so fast he was a blue streak. The frog appeared in the sky. A tongue shot for Gary. He had no more lightning to dodge, but he gathered all the starlight of his body into his legs and kicked again. He moved himself just enough to dodge. He tumbled head-first into the Grass Eater’s waiting mouth like a comet.

The jaws closed over him, trapping a cyborg arm between them. Jin roared in pain. Gary realized he’d fallen so fast that the teeth had missed him, and breathed a sigh of relief. He hadn’t been sure about that. As he turned to look, his back hit a wall, and he tumbled down a deep, dark hole, barely holding on to consciousness. Thorny teeth shot out behind him, hundreds of them, smashing into the opposite wall of the hole, every tooth trapping him deeper inside. Bones shattered across his body.

By the time he hit the floor, he couldn’t move. His consciousness hung on by a thin line. A tooth slammed into the wall above his head—and then, as suddenly as they’d begun, they stopped.

It took Gary some time to realize he was still alive. He still couldn’t look up, however, as he couldn’t move. He only saw black dirt.
