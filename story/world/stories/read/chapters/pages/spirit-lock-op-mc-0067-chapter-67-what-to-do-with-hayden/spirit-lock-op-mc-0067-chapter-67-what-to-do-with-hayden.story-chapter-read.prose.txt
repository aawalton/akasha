President Marico Mastia of the RDA Council sat with the celestial leaders, face stern. Stella found the woman’s loose, sagging skin perfectly suited to the scowl on her face.

“Grand Rifts are the jurisdiction of the Rift Defense Alliance,” President Mastia said. “You have no right to prevent us from entering and extracting illegal occupants.”

“We are currently in a state of emergency,” Stella said. “Until celestial representatives have been safely evacuated, and outside militaries are out of our expanse, we will treat the rift as a celestial issue.”

“If you felt it was an issue, you wouldn’t have let one ship into the rift, let alone three,” Antina Haelia, the leader of the Sorint Territory, said.

“One was a miscommunication,” Stella countered. “We told our military not to attack Hayden Reskco, which entered our military airspace without communication, and was thus designated as hostile by default. By the time the the issue was resolved, they were already too ahead to stop without force. The second ship was let through because the Morine Empire hijacked our communication and told our military the second ship was with Hayden.”

“I’d watch your accusations,” Halkan warned. “If you speak without evidence, I’ll declare a conflict. And if you speak with evidence, I’ll declare war.”

“Your war proclaimations don’t work on me,” Stella said.

“We’ll see about that.”

“Let’s not get off track,” Antina said during the standoff, abusing the situation for her gain. “Two ships made it in due to a miscommunication. What about the third? Twenty three unmarked, unidentified individuals wearing masks entered through your airspace and body warped the rift without your intervention.”

“Oh, yes, let’s talk about that,” Stella said, snapping her eyes at President Mastia. “The ship in question was an RDA vessel with unrestricted flight actions. It’s meant for officials. Care to explain why your ship is up there?”

President Mastia’s expression paled. “Wait… what?”

“Oh, so you don’t know? One of your most important shuttles is missing and you don’t know about it?” Stella narrowed her eyes. “The fact that you’re interrogating a sovereign celestial in the middle of your investigation is remarkable.”

The gravity in the room increased, but Halkan snorted, dispelling it.

“What are we doing right now?” Halkan asked. “There’s an unmarked ship of masked individuals that followed Hayden Reskco into the rift—and area famous for unsolved murders.”

“We’re assembling a team to follow them right now,” Stella answered.

“I refuse,” Halkan said. “Hayden declared his intention of joining the Morine Domain yesterday, and now masked assailants are following him into the rift? A masked group that magically made it through your military barricade? If you send more people into the rift, I will take it as a celestial issue.”

“Hayden Reskco is our citizen, and this is our expanse,” Stella said coldly. “You can’t lay claim to him as though he’s your citizen.”

“Tell that to my army.”

“You want to declare war?”

“Yeah, that’s what I’m famous for, isn’t it? War? Overwhelming might for strategic matters?”

“Fine, so be it. Declare it. Just makes sure to give a real justification. No one will support you with this justification.”

“Except me,” Antina said with a thin smile. “Of course, I’ll be more eloquent when I decry your treachery. Unless, of course, Eldest here has another opinion."

Eldest Elden Akto, an older gentleman with a dignified expression, remained stoic. He was known for his passivity, building through security alliances, so Stella expected him to stay silent, but to her surprise, he said, “If you two join forces over a matter involving a private citizen, the Quasar Alliance will be forced to take Presider Ingsley’s side.”

Antina’s smile disappeared. “Would you really declare war?”

“Of course. Not for Mr. Reskco, but rather security reasons. Unless you’ve forgotten, the Quasar Alliance operates through security partnerships. If you two clasp hands to impose your will onto the universe, we will naturally get involved with the full force of our military.”

Halkan’s expression hardened as he stared at Stella, who returned his expression. The Quasar Alliance technically had the largest military in the universe, but it wasn't nearly as lethal as Halkan’s, as the Quasar Alliance was a democracy. It was slow to act. That said, if it put his full weight behind Stella, it could easily match a Morine-Sorint alliance.”

“That said, I would like to know if there’s a method to resolve this.” He turned to Halkan.

Halkan smiled thinly. “Of course there is. One that meets the RDA’s standards.” He glanced at President Mastia. “We send in non-governmental guards—powerful ones. Say, the Crimson Sickles. They have a good track record, they’re part of the Golden Belt, and they have the strength to match anyone.”

“They also followed Hayden into the rift illegally,” Stella said.

“A miscommunication issue,” Halkan said. “I have evidence of it.”

President Mastia looked between them. “If it prevents war, we can make an emergency exception."

Elden turned to Stella, eyes telling her to accept the deal. “That’s fine. If… you will stand behind war if the Morine Empire hacks our communications again.”

Elden took a deep breath as he stared at Stella’s cold expression. He looked at Halkan. “I will.”

Halkan’s face darkened. “What if she—”

“Do you have a problem with a government communicating with their own guild?” Elden asked.

He gritted his teeth. “No.”

“Good,” Stella said. She turned to President Mastia. “I’m calling a vote on emergency powers in the council. Until that vote, no one is entering the rift. That said, assemble a team. We will work with the RDA closely to resolve this issue—should you not overstep your bounds. Do you understand?”

President Mastia wilted under the pressure and nodded.

“Good.” Stella scanned the other leaders’ faces. “Remove your representatives and militaries from Heline—immediately. If you don’t”—she turned to Halken—”I’ll be given Presider authority. Then, I suppose we’d be the same. Wouldn’t we?”

Halkan narrowed his eyes. She held his gaze. Then, she cut the connection without warning, and the large council chambers she was in dissolved, returning her to a large, bright white room.

Dasan Morkal, her assistant and voice—the man who relayed her words—entered the room.

“Did you secure a link with the Sickles?” she asked.

“Yes. I’ve sent you the number.”

“Good. Tell the council that if I don’t have emergency protections within the hour, it’ll be a bloodbath.”

“Yes, ma’am.” Dasan bowed his head and left the room. Stella then called her executor, the individual who made sure that things got done.

A cruel, lifeless man answered the call. “Yes ma’am?”

“How strong’s our blackmail over the RDA’s ambassador?” Stella asked.

“Criminal. He’ll take the fall without a word.”

“And the Sickles?”

“Extensive.”

“Good. I want you to exercise so much pressure on the RDA that they never have the nerve to summon me again.”

“Yes, ma’am.”

The call ended, and Stella called Karva Arino via the secure link that Dasan set up. Ana Paiea, the guild’s second in command, answered.

“Where’s Karva?” Stella asked curtly.

“Karva’s stress and temper are preventing her from obtaining anything but packaged information,” Ana said with a poker face. “I will represent the Sickles with full compliance.”

“Okay, Ana,” Stella said. “Then allow me to introduce myself. My name is Stella Ingsley, the Presider of the Golden Belt. For the last fifty years, I’ve ruled an empire that spans half a billion galaxies—and has the largest economy in the universe. Let me ask you something: do you know how one person has managed to control an area so large for so long?”

Ana gulped and shook her head.

“By keeping criminals in power. Not just in government, but in our guilds and businesses. And then I make a habit of recording and storing every single link connection they make.” She tapped her temple to remind them of the chip in their brain. “And you wouldn’t believe what people are willing to say over a link after you let them get away with crimes for half a century.”

Ana folded her arms nervously, and then unfolded them—unsure what pose to make.

“Yeah, it seems you get it. If you cross me, I’ll ensure that every friend, relative, and acquaintance you have ends up in prison. The only people I’ll spare are your three nieces, but I’m sure they’ll have a rough go when they’re in foster care. I hope they get good parents. I hear that system’s… murky.”

Ana’s eyes widened. “Please don’t.”

“Oh, that’s up to you,” Stella said. “Whether you and your coworkers survive this or not is up to you. So when my executor comes, make sure you listen to their every direction.”

Stella cut the call. She didn’t have time to make further threats—she had to get ready to fulfill them. It had been a long time since she had had to assert her authority, and many in her administration had since convinced themselves they were living in a democracy. In six hours, they would remember that they weren't.

Carian didn’t get Stella’s warning. Fifteen minutes before the call, her ship launched into the rift, gaining a grand glimpse of the fifty-mile microcosm before crashing into the forest. Before they fell, she also caught a glimpse of Hayden’s team, which had moved west.

“Should we look for their packs?” Caro Motan, a fellow A-rank in Carian’s team, asked.

Carian turned to Pan Maki, a young, quirky woman with a powerful tracker spirit. Pan took her fingers off the ground and shook her head. “There’s no dig site within a quarter mile. If they did a dig, we’ll either find it on the way, or we won’t find it at all.”

“That answers that,” Carian muttered. “Not that it matters. The last thing we want is to make that kid our enemy…”

Carian couldn’t get their last meeting out of her mind. The two had bantered—teamed up to hold blackmail over students. Despite that, he had ripped through her neck with razor wire without the slightest hesitation. She rubbed her neck, thinking about it.

“Then what’s the plan?” Caro asked.

Carian pondered his question as she studied her team of twenty. The members were part of the hidden team—not the main elites—and they weren’t enough. Inside sources said that there were six people with Hayden, and she imagined they were all A-rank elites. By contrast, their team had three A-ranks, including her, and three Bs. Their only saving grace was that they had fourteen Cs, including four sets of wings, and a wide array of supporting roles. If there were a fight, it would be a bloodbath.

Thankfully, they weren't fighting Hayden. She had to make that clear.

“Before we discuss strategy, we need to make something clear,” Carian said. “We aren’t supposed to be here. Karva aborted the mission, but politics forced us to come and search for the treasure. Since that’s our goal, that’s what we’re going to do. That said, unless you want to be locked up in prison for the rest of your life, you’re not going to touch Hayden Reskco. We need him to leave uninjured, untraumatized, and willing to work with us after this is over. Do you understand?”

The team nodded.

“Good. Don’t ever forget your place. You’re hired muscle, mindless meatheads who were willing to trade your lives and worth for money. I don’t want to hear complaints about the hardships Hayden puts you through. Just do your mission and use your paycheck to drink about it later. Am I understood?”

She waited for them to nod—and then turned to Pan. “Lead the way.”

The tracker nodded and put her hands on the ground, pulsing it with cora, her eyes closed. When she opened them, she pointed west. “That way.”

Carian summoned Koben, her shadow cat, and nodded to the team. “Move out.”
