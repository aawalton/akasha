It had been a couple of days since Archie and Henry had been reunited.

Since then, things had… slowed. In a good way. The kind of way that made it feel like he could actually completely relax and smell the roses around him, rather than constantly forcing himself to sprint headlong off a cliff with some sort of goal in mind.

Camilla, Henry’s two-month-old daughter, was a quiet thing. Wide eyes, messy tufts of light brown hair that permanently stuck up like she’d just been struck by lightning, and for some odd reason, had a strong distaste for the Headdress of the Savage Ursine he had on his head.

Not to mention her reaction towards Volos and Arsenic was something that had surprised both Henry and himself… well, more Henry than him. He still didn’t quite understand where he’d been keeping them all this time, and that they were spirit beasts that he’d hatched.

He and the maid he had were very much hesitant to have both Volos and Arsenic around Camilla, which was… fair. Centipedes didn’t really have a good reputation back on Earth, and with the additive aspects of them being able to use magic and spit poison didn’t really help them in any way.

But upon them making a deal with both Henry and himself on no magic or poison to be used around her, and constant supervision, they were granted to opportunity to play around with Camilla - and to Henry’s surprise, she was over the moon judging by her excited gurgles and babbling, especially so when they repeatedly merged into and out of their merged form.

It had been nice. Really, actually nice.

They sat on the porch in the mornings on chairs that Henry himself made, sipping whatever passed for coffee on this planet that tasted like normal medium roast coffee with a peanut buttery aftertaste. And talked. And talked until the mornings became nights.

About what had happened to them from the beginning of it all.

Archie talked about how he’d chosen the Solo Tutorial because he was too far away from the family and how he didn’t want to deal with the drama of everyone else around him, because he never really got to know his neighbors, nor really cared about them.

How he ended up becoming a magical hippie for a while, a shaman, and eventually becoming a blacksmith, which then led him to show off the Mana Bike and fried Mana Revolver he’d made while in the Group Tutorial.

Then he’d talked about how in the Group Tutorial, there was some psychopath going around and brainwashing people and turning them into thralls and demons, and eventually turned himself into one, and how after destroying his cult and beating him up, he escaped from the Tutorial.

Which then led him to explain his decision to fully empower the Final Tutorial Boss and barely end up surviving it, even when it was technically unstable to itself with all that power in it.

Then, he spoke about what had happened after blinking suddenly and finding himself in front of the System Aspect and being told that their universe exploded and that Mom and Dad were being healed, and that he would need to wait for two thousand years until they would become free.

About how, after being randomly teleported in a crumbling temple where he’d end up killing a slaver and freeing someone from their captivity, they had to escape the country they were in because that slaver he’d killed was a big shot noble, and he’d end up being quadra cursed, which practically ruined his time while on Fractal.

He ended his story by explaining how he’d built a teleporter to escape the planet and reach his patron god – who then helped him reach this planet and be able to meet up with his brother. Funnily enough, that’s also when Archie learned that his brother had a patron god too.

And then Henry had told his story.

How he’d landed on an underground planet with air so thin it felt like trying to breathe through a straw, and if the straw was the size of a needle

How he’d barely managed to keep Camilla safe in those first few days – how her tiny, wriggling form was the only thing that had kept him moving when he’d felt his lungs ready to collapse from the pressure and the lack of oxygen in the first few days before he’d forced himself to adapt to it.

And how, by some stroke of wild instinct or paranoia or some combination of the two, he’d bought a whole cart’s worth of essential survival gear for babies and other various pieces of equipment in the System Bazaar prior to him and Camilla landing on the subterranean planet. Including an infant-grade Air Purifier Suit with adjustable seals and a low-energy barrier enchantment that hugged her body like a second skin, protecting her from any harmful chemicals and allowing her to eat and breathe normally as long as he made sure that the nostril plugs were properly placed.

“I don’t even remember clicking ‘buy,’” he admitted once as they leaned against the outer fence. “It was just… I remembered Maria acting all weird seconds before we all teleported and her telling me to protect Camie… It was all so fresh, and I just panicked and purchased practically everything and anything I could that would be able to help her, and I ended up spending like ten million on everything from food supplies to a spacesuit for her. I barely bought anything for myself and… I just... everything about landing on that planet and surviving there and putting her in danger.”

“But you’re here,” Archie replied, smirking. “Camie is here and safe, you did everything you could at that time to protect her, with practically no one in your corner at the time to help ease up your worries. But I’m here now, so you can lean on me now.”

“Yeah.” Henry smiled faintly, eyes distant, face now shaven and hair tamed. “… I’m glad that you came when you did.”

Then came the stories of that underground world, a subterranean planet ruled by blue-skinned, long-haired people who looked like some sort of bizarre crossbreed between dwarves and elves.

Just like in various fantasy books and other forms of media, they didn’t take well to humans – especially not when Henry emerged from the Omniversal Solutions Tavern. People in E- and D-Grade were even less welcoming once they realized he was a member, despite being only E-Grade himself, well below the usual minimum requirement of C-Grade.

But he managed to survive. He adapted, absorbed as much as he could from his surroundings, and forced himself to grow and learn as much as possible.

He memorized the layout of the subterranean tunnels, figured out how to trade and barter using a broken mix of USL and their native tongue because most dwelfs only used their native language and never bothered to learn USL.

He was doing well for himself after the first month, managing to make enough to hire a maid to watch over Camilla full time, and already generating enough goodwill with the guild to be able to look through planets that the guild was on so he could find a much safer place to raise Camilla.

Until war broke out.

A war that had nothing to do with him, and everything to do with the dwelfs and the undead.

Archie remained silent as he spoke about slaughtering everyone and everything in his way to reach his daughter. He spoke about how he felt the wards around his apartment were shattered, and how Camilla was in danger, and how he quit his quest midway through in order to rush to her.

How he believed that in order to reach his daughter through the sea of dwelfs, he had to cut through them, and he did. And when he finally managed to reach his daughter, he found her beneath her cradle mattress, all red-faced and wailing in a room filled with burnt and mangled corpses. And how all around him were mana vestiges of something called Darkflame, something he’d claimed that only he and his patron god, Ragnar, should have had.

After finding his daughter and calming himself down, he managed to reach the Omniversal Solutions Tavern and escaped by choosing one of the more cheaper, yet still one of the safer planets he’d been looking at, and after paying the fare he practically threw both himself and Camilla onto the teleporter pad and brought them to this planet, Thabos.

It was only a few weeks after he’d finished building his house and after he’d evolved into D-Grade did he get a confirmation from his patron god, Ragnar, that he was the one who intervened and saved Camilla from being kidnapped.

And for that, Ragnar was placed on the list he kept of people that he held with high regard, despite never having met the god face-to-face.

As the sun dipped low on the fourth evening since Archie arrived, casting long shadows across the grass and painting the horizon in hues of orange and gold, the two brothers sat on the porch in silence. Camilla was fast asleep inside, curled up in a self-warming blanket cradle Henry had purchased for her recently, with a soft orange carrot plush being strangled in her tiny arms.

Archie leaned back, arms crossed behind his head, eyes half-closed as he listened to the distant chirping of the birds and chittering of Volos and Arsenic play-fighting each other off in the distance, far away from the traps Henry had set up.

Henry exhaled beside him, putting his mug of beer back into the cupholder within the armrests. “Do you think I’m a bad person for doing what I did? For killing all those people to get to Camie?”

“Not even for a second,” Archie answered, not a second later, before taking a sip of his beer.

“… How are you so sure?” Henry asked him, keeping his eyes on the grass beneath his feet. “How ar–”

“Was Camilla in danger?” Archie asked him, plainly. “Were the protections you placed in your house destroyed and made her vulnerable? Were you in a place where most people thought of you and Camilla to be eyesores?”

“… Yes,” Henry nodded, eyes trailing towards his brother, who sat beside him and was looking at Volos and Arsenic as they somehow managed to simultaneously drop-kick the other.

“Were people actively preventing you from reaching your daughter?” Archie asked, taking another sip of his beer, his gaze flicking back towards his brother, who nodded in response. “Then, taking out something from Grandpa’s books; do you remember what he told us back when we went camping eight years ago?”

After not getting a response from Henry, Archie continued. “He told us that back when he was in the army, he went on a tour, and during that tour, dad was around two to three years old. And as a result of that tour, he ended up far behind enemy lines, and he would need to travel thirty kilometers on foot to reach his base’s encampment.”

“It took him a total of three weeks in order to cross that distance, and throughout his journey to reach his base, he ended up being sent back home after having both his legs broken, eight ribs fractured, severe lacerations all over his arms, and six bullets still in his torso,” Archie continued while finishing up the last bits of beer remaining in his mug. “But in return, he left behind an unidentified enemy encampment, the one that had spotted him, littered with corpses.”

“He said, throughout that whole time he was being hunted by them, the only thing that kept him going was Dad and Grandma, and that he needed to survive in order to provide for them. But it was also then that a thought came to him,” Archie said, putting his mug away into his Spatial Storage. “The enemy soldiers chasing him down were risking the livelihood of Dad and Grandma. And from there, he just flipped and went all Rambo on their asses and barely made it back to base.”

“So, all in all, you did the right thing,” Archie said, getting up from his seat and patting Henry on the back as he stared back at Camilla in her crib. “Never let anyone tell you otherwise.”

“… Ye–” Henry said before both he and Archie received a System Notification.
