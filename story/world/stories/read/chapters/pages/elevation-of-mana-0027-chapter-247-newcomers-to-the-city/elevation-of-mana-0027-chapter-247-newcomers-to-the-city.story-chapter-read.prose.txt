The elves for their part were pretty accommodating. They did indeed have a few rules, but most were easily explained, and clear to teach. Some of those were about sanitation, some were about general behavior. Most were normal and boring to my eyes, the kind of basic legal code that anyone would come up with.

Nor were we the only people who'd been invited here. There was another group, who apparently had been here for about a week or so, that settled in along with the elves. From what I could tell they knew some of the members of my group, at least in passing, or maybe they knew people who knew, it was hard to tell.

It wasn't that they didn't speak to people, it was that they didn't speak to me. Which made sense, I was still basically a baby, why would anyone speak to me other than my family members, or in this case, those from my group. However that didn't mean they didn't help though, my favorite stone worker was looking better almost by the day, and walking slightly straighter each time the sun rose.

For now though I was stuck with the women, who were daily venturing out into the nearby woodland to gather up berries and roots. The elves were weird about it, they wanted guards to go with us, though nobody was quite sure why. So I decided to ask when one of them came by to check on things.

“Why always come here?” I asked the man. “Not hunting.”

“It's important to have protectors,” he said simply, smiling at me.

“From what?”“Big animals?” It was clear he was struggling with the word, and many of the others looked at him strangely. “Ah... not sure what word, dangerous animals and scary things?”“No big animals would mess with a group our size,” my mother observed, confused.

“Odd big animals?” he tried again.

“Monsters?” Old Pa asked, her eyes looking confused. “Big, scary, very dangerous?”“Yes, those.”

“None around here,” she said, shaking her head. “Very rare around here.”They went back and forth for awhile, and it seemed wherever the elves hailed from had an infestation of monsters. That made their protectiveness seem sensible, and might even explain why they had magic like they did. If there were monsters everywhere maybe the place was saturated in magic, or maybe they simply all died if they didn't have magic. Or was that the same thing? I didn't know.

Eventually though we headed back, after the meeting of the hunters. They came in two waves though, one for us and one for another group.

However I saw another oddball in that group, a youngish man who didn't bother meeting the women for 'fun times' after his hunt, instead he came over to where the few women who were older from his group were, and went to talk to one.

“Good hunt son?” she asked him. He simply nodded in response, not even looking at her, instead playing with one of the dogs nearby.

They talked, and Old Pa talked, and soon I had an idea of what was going on here.

“No women like him?” Old Pa asked as the man went to go use the bathroom in the bushes nearby.

“Oh they like him fine,” his mother answered. “He doesn't like them, doesn't talk much either, barely talks to me. Makes me sad, but he's a good hunter, and a good member of our group. The dogs, he understands the dogs like nobody, knows everything about them, how they move, how they think. Never pays much attention to anything but them or the hunt.”

For a moment I was almost taken aback, because I got it. The man was probably severely autistic, and yet, his people didn't care, they loved him, at least the old women here spoke as if they did. Here it wasn't a detriment, there were no schools, no large forced socialization. No, here he was just an odd man, one obsessed with his dogs, who loved his dogs, and who cared for little else. Perhaps there were women he liked, or would be one day, but for now? He didn't seem interested.

The men from their group came back, and sat with us for a second.

“Good hunt today,” one said, looking nowhere in particular. “Loas did very well with the tracking.” He didn't look at the man in question, didn't try to force him into the conversation, just said that he was happy and went on with it. From the look of him, older, more well built, he may well be the leader of that hunting group.

I'd never dealt with men quite as far down the spectrum as Loas before, but in my work as an officer I'd had plenty of experience with others less severe. There were several shops in my old town that were run by people who were obsessed with their particular thing, and experts in it, but who were really bad with people. We'd had to deal with them from time to time, whenever there were incidents, but they seldom caused problems.

As they passed, and before the women returned I saw him looking at the additional blimps that had joined our hosts a few days ago.

“Extra shamans, more powerful,” he said, the only words I'd heard him say.“Bad?” his mother asked, and he made a complicated expression.

“Don't know.”

He was of course right, and I knew it, for I'd seen a few extra elves here and there, and could tell by their auras that they were more powerful. On a lark I looked at Loas, and saw he too had an aura, but a small one, near his skin. That sort of tracked, whatever he did it probably helped him in some way that others didn't really get, as he'd not been introduced as a Shaman.

Even as the women started to come back I saw a trio of elves strolling across the field. One was the leader of this area, I'd seen her before. With her though were two others with little streaks of white in their hair, both with massive auras, and the one... it looked almost like mine, wrong color, very wrong color, but the same general shape.

The new woman, laughed when she saw me, and poked the man, saying something in their language, it was musical, almost happy. As they got closer and closer the local leader also said something in their language which I still didn't understand, then turned to us.

“These are my parents. When I told my father of your son he wanted to meet.”My mother looked slightly taken aback. “Why?” she questioned, but politely.

“His fire is like mine,” the man said, his accent thick.
