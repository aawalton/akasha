In order to reforge my body, I would need a basin to do it in. It had to be some place sturdy, and I couldn’t just hide out in the woods and dig a hole in the ground. It would be far too dirty, and I didn’t want to contaminate my advancement with other debris from outside. I couldn’t do what Sage had done, either, because I was in no way small enough to fit that small of a basin.

But, not wanting to drag it out any longer, I set out into the camp in the evenings and snuck into Sir Ricdine’s tent. When I entered through my secret flap in the back, I made sure to obscure my footsteps as much as I could. It was just a precaution, but I didn’t want anyone finding boot-prints in the mud and getting suspicious. I stepped lightly, keeping to the patches of grass where my steps wouldn’t leave an impression in the dirt. If I had to make a footprint, I swept my hand over it as soon as I passed, until I could no longer tell it was a footprint.

Once I was inside, I used whatever scrap wood I could find nearby to build a basin of my own. It was more like a mould for concrete, but I’d made sure the wood was clean, and I’d scraped off as many splinters as I could.

By the time I finished the basin, it was too late. I waited until the next day. After training, I summoned my squad and requested their assistance.

“Do you plan on controlling the slurry?” Sage asked.

“The maegen should be sucked in by the blessing,” I replied. “As long as I coat myself in the slurry, it should be fine. It should pass through all my muscle, reforging as it goes. And then when it’s all used up, it will be locked in, correct?”

“Correct,” Sage said. “It will be painful.”

“That’s fine.” I kept my expression flat, though it was more like the anticipation of a needle. You knew you could handle it, but something in the back of your mind told you it was going to be unpleasant. Only, this would be far more unpleasant than a needle.

Before I could psych myself out of the reforging, I said, “I need you guys, as usual, to help keep me conscious.”

“What about your resonance nodes?” Anchor asked.

“What about them?”

“When you melt your flesh into paste and let the maegen rebuild it as something stronger, they could fall out of your body,” Anchor said. “Physically, fall out.”

“I’ve planned for that,” I said, pointing to six stones I’d laid out in the bottom of the basin. “They’re aligned beneath the nodes, giving them support during the reforging. Yeah, they’ll be uncomfortable to lay on when it’s just starting.”

“But you’ll reforge them into your body, Brother,” Brawl said.

“That’s why I need you to knock them away in the final phases.” I pointed to the six stones, arranged below where I’d lay. I’d set them out perfectly to match where I knew the nodes in my body were, and where I could envision them with my internal sense.

“I will be able to time it properly,” Sage said. “When your body and flesh begins solidifying again, I will knock them away, so they don’t get sealed in, but not so soon that your resonance nodes have nothing to support them.”

“There’s no time to keep mulling it over,” I said. “Our next Labyrinth run is tomorrow, and I’d like to test it out before then.”

We moved all the ingredients I’d gathered for the reforging into the basin, then dumped them in. First, the bone chunks and golem crystals, then shambler pearls. Next, the paper-thin flesh from the landbain wings. I poured out the minddrake bile, scraping it into the basin, then poured in the three potions I’d gotten from the druids. I stirred it together, slowly diluting the bile without taking away all its potency.

Lastly came the water. I’d prepared two barrels full of water from the stream, and we poured them in overtop the materials, like we were making a massive bone broth. The liquid rose high enough to immerse a human body, but some began leaking through the cracks in the basin.

Using the back end of my spear, I stirred it, letting the potions do their job and dissolve the materials. As they dissolved, the liquid thickened and glommed onto the edges of the basin. It was a dark gray slurry, and it bubbled and popped, pressure wafting off it. The last thing I wanted to do was touch it.

But there was no choice.

“It’s now or never,” I said. I took off my clothes, then carefully immersed myself in the liquid. As soon as my feet entered the basin, the liquid glommed around my ankles. My skin seared and popped, like I’d held a carbonated drink in my mouth for far too long (except the sensation was everywhere), and my bones vibrated.

I aligned myself with the rocks, placing my resonance nodes overtop them, then immersed myself deep enough that only parts of my face were out of the liquid. Enough to breathe, enough to keep seeing.

It didn’t matter. The searing, stinging sensation erupted all across my body, fizzing on the surface of my skin, eroding it. My muscles stopped working, and my flesh slowly melted away. It was how I imagined an acid bath would feel, and it was close enough to the same effect in the end.

I clenched my jaw, forcing myself not to make a noise. If I screamed, it was all over. Someone would’ve been drawn to Sir Ricdine’s tent, and then it was all over. I would be found out.

So I weathered the discomfort. My cheeks melted, and liquid poured down my throat, dissolving its flesh, too.

I had no idea if it was working, not at first. A touch of fear jolted through my body, wondering if I’d pushed it too far, if I’d just melted myself in a massive acid bath for no good reason.

But then something clicked. A wave of resonance rolled through the liquid, and it condensed into a thicker sludge, clinging onto the surface of my body, just the same way as it had for Sage. It was working.

Pulses of energy shocked through my muscles. Instead of just melting them, it rebuilt them. The arcane material I’d melted down into the slurry now flowed into me, material to enhance the strength of my muscle fibres. Anything I’d done before was compounded. The nodes’ work wasn’t replaced, it was simply built on top of.

That was the maegen. It guided the material inside me. Some of it resisted, trying to linger on the surface of my skin, like it had so many times before.

But now, this time, my body couldn’t reject it. The slurry, dissolving my body, forced it in. There was nothing it could do to stop the energy from rushing toward the center of my body.

Where the blessing of Aelfwine’s Heart lingered.

I tried to smile in satisfaction, but although my muscles were rebuilding themselves, I still couldn’t move.

And, although I knew it was working, it didn’t make the pain anymore bearable. All my nerves frayed. This had to be what it felt like to burn alive. There wasn’t much I could do about it except try to stay conscious.

Sure, I didn’t need to stay conscious, but I needed to keep myself steady. If I went limp, or if I seized and started flailing, it could knock my resonance nodes out of place—which wasn’t something non-Dupes had to worry about. But my resonance nodes were the key to me interacting with the System. If they got damaged, then I wouldn’t be able to do anything I’d planned.

I’d be without any sort of magic. No System, nothing.

That impulse kept my mind going for a short while, but even then, the burning was too much. My eyes drifted shut, locking the reforging acid behind my eyelids as it tried to reshape and rebuild my eyes.

Then something jostled my shoulder. I blinked. What was that? Was I falling? Or was I starting to seize—

Anchor had nudged me. Morph supported my head, and Sage leaned over the side of the basin, her fingers coated in the slurry, green maegen glimmering on her palms, ready to heal me if needed. Brawl, on the other hand, stood on the other side, holding my spear, ready to knock away the supports for my resonance nodes upon Sage’s command.

It was an eternity while it lasted, and in retrospect, a blink. The process repeated a few times. I drifted off. They jostled me. I forced myself to hold steady, to press my teeth together, to stop myself from crying out as I melted away.

Finally, the last dregs of the slurry absorbed into my skin, and brown debris and detritus leaked from my pores as a black liquid. It stank almost as badly as the drainage ditch behind the tent.

Sage said something, which I didn’t catch, and Brawl knocked away the resonance node supports. My spine stopped arching, and I fell flat onto my back on the floor of the basin.

Pressure gathered on the surface of my skin, pushing inward. There was no more maegen on the outside to pull in, The last dregs were inside my body, now, rushing toward my core, consuming themselves in my muscles before they could be rejected. It created a void of energy around me. Finally, with a faint boom, the pressure burst apart, creating a wave of invisible force that blasted off me.

First, I moved my fingers. They obeyed instantly, without any delay, and snapped faster than usual.

I leapt to my feet, but I stood up with far more force than I had intended, and I flung myself upward, accidentally jumping a few feet off the ground. When I landed, I stumbled, and I tried to steady myself on one of the upright barrels. My grip shattered the wood, and my arm plunged in. The ragged wood shards should’ve scraped up my forearm, shredding the skin, but instead, they broke on the surface of my arm.

It took a few more seconds before I found my footing, but eventually, I stabilized myself, holding myself steady.

I whispered, “Woah…”

Sure, I had already been far stronger than a regular human. Far stronger than the peak for a mortal man, but those changes had been incremental, earned over a year. Not like this.

First, I looked down. Aside from a coating of reforging expulsions, which manifested as a slimy brown coating of stuff my body didn’t want anymore—impurities and other nasty imperfections—I didn’t look all that different. But I felt slightly more dense, and all my features were slightly…sharper.

Anchor tossed me my clothes and said, “The bathhouse is closed, but you can still clean yourself off in the stream. You stink.”

I took the clothes, then nodded, laughing at myself. I’d actually done it.

Now, it was time to see what I could actually do with it.
