What a terrible smell... Harry thought as he observed the other participants in the room. There are dozens of warriors but not a single cleaner? But the loop on this floor was only 5 hours long, so what was the point? Even so, Harry thought basic sanitation and cleanliness were of utmost importance.

The Locksmith tapped his foot and squirmed in his chair; the wood twisting to support his weight. He felt the sharp splinters in his hands, almost as if even the chairs were fighting for their lives. He closed his eyes and covered his face with his hands. At the same moment, several participants of various sizes looked at him with malicious smiles.

"Hey!" Madeline nudged his shoulder from the side. "Stop doing that; you're attracting everyone's attention."

"What do you mean?" Harry said without removing his hands from his face. "I'm simply thinking. That's all."

"You're striking that loser pose," Madeline said, now more quietly. "Come on, Harry, I know you. No need to sulk."

And then they quickly lost interest as soon as a grumpy, expressive competitor dragged himself across the waiting room floor, lamenting his fate. He was so curious he could hardly be called a man. At least that's what the other competitors thought of him.

The 5th floor, unlike many others, was ruled by nobles. Taking advantage of the coliseum's obvious purpose, the prisoners departed every 5 hours, venting their frustrations, anger, and any negative emotions about the outside world. In a way, that was now their world. A culture of war had formed there, and those who couldn't follow it would likely be cut down. That is, those who chose to stay in the coliseum.

What Harry found disturbing, however, was how the nobles still had control even inside the Tower. Guards from various families protected entire groups of nobles, who, unlike before, now seemed to relish the violent and brutal battles that subjected the prisoners to. I imagine the chaos that would engulf this place if there weren't Emblem users like Matthews...

Before hearing the explanations of one of the prisoners — in charge of relaying the game instructions to the newcomers — Harry found it curious that people chose to participate in the arena. I mean, wouldn't that mean certain death? Even the hardest stone is lost against a constant current.

In earlier times, unfortunately, the rules were more unforgiving. Whether you had the Emblem or not, to be excluded from the arena, one had to participate in at least three games to have a certain number of loops where their presence wouldn't be ordered. A number that Harry's mind didn't bother to recall.

Paradoxically, even within the tower, times were — thankfully — changing. That way, Brun won't have to fight, at least... He thought optimistically. The issue with that, however, was that Brun had a really useful Emblem. Many lives were already lost through the Sand Chasers Initiative in order to find the hatch. After dozens and dozens of loops of folks and guards venturing into the dunes to find the exit, they couldn't afford to hand out more bodies to the desert. That's where Brun would fit in; with her Emblem, she could find an answer before being chewed by the living boat out there.

"I'm not sulking!" he said, frowning. "I'm just... contemplating my indignation."

"Right..." Madeline replied. "So could you do this without showing any physical gestures? We're already in enough trouble. Besides, you, all nervous, are making me anxious too."

"But how could I not be?" She turned to the red-haired girl sitting next to her. "I mean, I thought my literal brother could give us a hand here. It's fine that he wants to screw me over, but you two? This is getting out of control..."

"What's up with you two, anyway?" Madeline inquired while casually playing with her hair. "Why didn't you ever tell me your brother is the fucking general?"

"You know what?" He said. "It never occurred to me to tell you about my brother who had no relationship with the Tower whatsoever. It's funny how it all blows up in your face either way... He's always been the hotheaded one. He was a prick child, let me tell you that; always making it hard for mom. That's why I... Anyway, let's just say he turned out fine. Actually, a lot more fine than I hoped."

"He has two Emblems, right?" Madeline asked, a quizzical look on her face. "So that's why he doesn't have any skin? He used a Quadrangular form for his second one... What could it be, the Bone Emblem? That's why his bones are so dense... But why—"

"Can we stop talking about my brother's bones, please?" Harry asked, shaking his head.

"Oh, what's that?" She asked with a grin, leaning over him. "Are you perhaps... jealous? Is that what this is? Jeez, Harry, you know, this spiteful thing you have going on only works for Daniel, and we all know why."

"...What the hell are you blabbering about?" Harry said, not a hint of playfulness on his voice.

Seeing this, Madeline realized the situation was deeper than she imagined. Shutting her mouth and taking a few seconds to think it over, she then grabbed his shaking hand and gave him a sympathetic smile.

"We're gonna make it, alright?" She said, her voice mellifluous. "Don't sweat it. We don't have to put ourselves at risk. If the rules are to be followed, we just need to survive. Do you hear that? Survive. That's what we are in the end, isn't it? Survivors, Harry."

As if he had synchronized his own heartbeat with Madeline's, Harry lost himself in the vastness of her eyes. For a moment, he felt he could share the guilt he carried with the woman beside him. It was crazy to think he had lived until now without trusting anyone, and it was even crazier to think that someone so trustworthy had always been within his reach.

"The world is your bathroom!" The same strange man from before, who had been dragging himself around the place, shouted as he sat down next to Harry.

Only then realizing he was squeezing Madeline's hand, Harry blinked and let go. He instinctively turned to the stranger beside him, who seemed pleased with something.

"Oh, don't worry about me!" he said, his toothless smile gleaming. "I just went to relieve myself quickly! Every time night turns back to day I wake up with a full bladder... A curse and a blessing, don't ya think?"

The man then guffawed. Before Harry could ask anything, the man turned to him again and said in a more serious tone.

"I heard you're the boss's nephew..." he said. "Is that true ye? Just so you know, we don't tolerate nepotism!"

"What?" Harry asked, indignant. "I'm not his son. We're... None of your business! Where did you hear that, anyway...?"

"You're teaming up, right?" He shouted, for no apparent reason. "First time fighting? Did you come from downstairs or—"

"We're new," Madeline answered for Harry. "On the floor, I mean. We came straight from the fourth floor, obviously."

"Travelers!?" The man flashed a wide grin, another opportunity to see which teeth were missing. "This season is going to be insane! We need some fresh blood, you know? After seeing the same fire or boat floating for the eighth time, you lose the fun in fighting..."

"Speaking of which, where's your partner?" Harry asked, curious.

"Ah, I don't fight well with people on my side," he replied, pointing to all the other competitors who, consequently, glared at the group. "They say I'm unpredictable, you know? People are afraid... I don't know why."

No partner? Harry added inwardly. Either he's totally crazy, or he's absurdly strong. If he's known in the bands, it's certainly the latter. Although, in this case, the two aren't mutually exclusive...

From what the young thief understood of the rules, some modalities were defined once every few loops. This "season," as they liked to call it, would be the time for tag team fights. A less brutal tournament, according to the experts. The experts, of course, were nobles who had never been punched in their lives.

In any case, if someone was really keen, they could fight alone, even while participating in a tag team fight. If someone wanted to fight on their team but didn't have a partner, one of the participants from the previous season — the survivors, of course — would be called upon. In the case of the schizophrenic sitting near Harry, he chose to go it alone. Listening to his reasons, Harry was as curious as he was frightened to see what would lead someone to make such decisions.

At the moment, the pairs had been defined as: Harry and Madeline; Goldman and Vela; and Daniel and Manes. The last participant, for some reason, refused to stay with Brun downstairs and specifically asked to participate in the games, against the advice of everyone in the group. He ended up on Daniel's team, an unfortunate situation for both of them. What Harry found strange at the moment was that Manes didn't seem as upset about it.

They didn't know which opponents they would fight next, given the tournament rules, so they could only wait to hold out as long as possible.

Apparently, the battles were contained within a bubble, like a kind of dome for the audience to see.

"What's your name, fella?" Harry said, trying to sound friendly.

"Carga." The man replied, proudly.

Both Madeline and Harry felt their blood run cold. They tried not to show it, but the impact of hearing the weird man's name had certainly affected them.

Carga. Harry remembered. The bomber. Seriously? He then thought about the purpose of the Logos Tower; a prison. After so long without encountering the heroes, legends, and demons that the world tried to forget, he thought they had either died or that it was all nonsense.

Looking around and seeing the scarred faces of the competitors, he realized where they were all hiding. If they gathered them all here, they could possibly take over an entire army. He hadn't seen the heroes yet, but these were certainly the demons he so desperately wanted to meet.

He swallowed hard and turned to the man.

"...Carga?" Harry asked, his voice trembling. "The Carga?"

"There can only be one, obviously!" He chuckled. "Why? Am I famous in the outside world? I must be, shouldn't I? Tell me, tell me!"

That's it. That's what I'm here for. Harry had a strange thought. Of course, he had a goal that would compel him to climb the Tower, but for some reason, seeing all these prisoners and the legends surrounding him, a flame ignited in his heart. Coursed through his veins like a new form of energy, and Harry inadvertently showed a determined smile.

He then turned to Madeline, who seemed confused by what Harry was feeling. Freedom? It's all about freedom, isn't it? That's what we seek. That's what we fight for.

"We'll do our best, won't we?" she asked.

"...What do you mean... What are you talking about? Y-Yes, of course—"

"I believe these men may possibly be the freest creatures in the universe. Perhaps we can learn something from them, don't you think?"

And so, Harry regained his passion for fighting. He would survive all this and confront his brother. He would climb to the top and steal time. How difficult could it be?
