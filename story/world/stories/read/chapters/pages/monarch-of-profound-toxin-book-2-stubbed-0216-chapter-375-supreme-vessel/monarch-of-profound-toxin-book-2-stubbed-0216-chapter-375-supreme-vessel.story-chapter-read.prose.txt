Chapter 375

The moment the potion touched his lips, jolts of something grand fluttered across his skin, but it was when it made its way through his esophagus and into his stomach, that the real impact made itself known.

Eik could barely catch his breath in the first few moments as physical power unlike anything he had ever felt before jetted through his body like streams of scorching flames. In a moment he went from feeling normal to a bundle of abruptly tensed muscle.

The air vibrated with sudden heat as the sheer pressure of it seemed to cook the molecules around him. Instinctively, Chop even found himself taking a step back.

This was so far above the experience of the grade 6 potion appropriate for S-rankers that Eik could hardly believe that it was even the same type of potion. X-rank truly was something else.

As he gasped for a proper breath, his eyes caught the glow that etched itself into the skin of the underside of his forearm.

“Wh-What the hell?”

[Natural skill evolution triggered — Profound Toxin]

[Profound Toxin evolutionary trait Perfect Consumption elevated]

“What is it?” Chop asked and approached, hoping desperately that nobody had noticed the fact that he had retreated at the sudden spike in Eik’s power. He grunted with displeasure as his gaze flowed over the writing on Eik’s arm. “You have got to be kidding me with this crap…”

[Perfect Consumption elevated to Supreme Vessel]

Eik didn’t have time to come up with a witty comeback because as the last letter of the message sizzled into his skin, a second wave of nearly unimaginable power hammered into him seemingly out of nowhere. The involuntary reaction of his muscles damn near made him piss his pants.

Heating up even further, the vibrations in the air grew more intense as a deep, grinding rumble began to permeate the atmosphere.

“I feel like I’m about to explode, man…” Eik muttered. Somehow, it was as if the smallest movement of a single finger would cause untold destruction. “I need to get this out of me. All of this energy needs to be released.”

Chop spat on the ground and kicked a pebble into a copse of trees off to the side. The trunks that met it shattered along with the projectile like dry twigs. “Then do it already.”

“Yeah… Actually, I’m going to go a little bit further away,” he announced, infusing aura into his voice in order to let the audience gathered behind the barrier up on the artificial ridge hear his words as well. “This is a little more powerful that I had anticipated, so I changed my mind. You guys can stay there no problem, but I’ll move a little bit further away,” he said as he crouched to leap away. “All right, stay put right theeeeeeeeee—…”

The ground was practically liquefied in a radius of a thirty meters by the sheer recoiling force of his feet kicking off the ground, and his voice faded rapidly as he rocketed up into the stratosphere. For a moment, he thought his body might catch on fire before he broke through the atmospheric layer above the clouds, but with a crack that popped his ears painfully, everything was suddenly quiet.

“Fuuuckin’ hell,” he muttered under his breath as he looked down at the clouds far below his fluttering feet. If this was the result of a miscalculated jump, what the hell would a deliberate exercise of power look like? He had only meant to clear a mountain or two, not release himself from planetary orbit. By Odin’s sweaty balls…

Flipping himself upside down in the air, a single brick of crystalline explosive detonated at the bottom of his feet sent him into a straight dive for the ground. As he broke through the clouds again, he headed for a mountain top a few kilometers away from crowd gathered on the ridge. If he aimed his testing attacks in a different direction from there, it should be safe enough.

“Why did my dad leave?” Goo asked from his royal seat upon Uncle Heath’s shoulders.

“I’m not really sure, sweetheart,” his mother told him. “I think he’s just taking a few precautions.”

“I don’t know what that means.”

“It just means he’s making sure we’re all safe,” Michael said with a smile, ruffling the boy’s hair.

“There he is!” Bin shouted suddenly, pointing up into the sky. “That’s my dad right there!”

Like a meteor in the distance, Eik popped through the bottom of the cloud and barreled earthward, a tail of blue trailing his descent. With a distant boom, he impacted the top of one of the larger mountains in the area, whose peak easily towered above the surroundings.

The uppermost ten meters of the peak crumbled like sand, but otherwise it actually held up remarkably well against the force of Eik’s contact.

Andihar watched with a hand on his brow and snorted a laugh. “He must have been careful with that landing.”

When Eik had consumed that potion, they had all been able to feel the change in the air, unleashed in two enormous bursts of power that bathed them in pressure. In the moments before Eik had managed to get it under control, it had been difficult to even breathe.

Andihar and Gul and the other S-rankers present could see Eik’s figure quite clearly as he turned laterally away from them. The A-rankers would probably only be able to barely glean his movements, while the rest of the onlookers would have to wait until the show began.

And they didn’t have to wait long.

None were blind to the glittering glow of the sudden manifestation of a ten meter long war hammer. The first nine meters or so from the handle up consisted of unadorned shaft for leverage, while the final meter made up a devastating hammer head of solid, hyper-reinforced crystal. Rather than a flat surface, the face of the hammer was made into the shape of the face of a massive demon.

Fangs bared in a gleeful grin, four wicked horns sprouted from its forehead and curved back behind the head. Instead of tapered ends, each of the four horns ended in a weighty chunk of crystal that acted as a counterweight for even more unhinged obliteration.

“How long have I been an apprentice again?” Heath asked Michael.

Michael pursed his lips. “I don’t know.”

“A really freaking long time, dude. And could I make something like that if I had a thousand tries? Not a snowball’s chance in hell.”

“Well, I mean you can complain as much as you want about it, but it’s really a simple matter of fact. He’s just straight up better than us,” Michael said with a sigh. “Do you think it makes me feel particularly useful when I’m frantically attending to a handful of patients at the clinic, only for our good friend Eik over there to smell a flower in his back garden a couple of miles away, sneeze, and accidentally cure every person in the entirety of Forest from any ailment they might be suffering from?”

Heath’s eyebrows flew up to his hairline. “Damn. That actually happened?”

“Of course not!” Michael groaned, throwing his hands up. “I’m exaggerating! I’m saying that dude is better than all of us at everything and there’s nothing we can do about it.”

“I’m better at remembering birthdays and appointments than him,” Robert noted.

“And being on time,” Ihasu chimed in. "Not to mention math, geography, and anything that might require one to sit down and focus for a little bit."

“And not having people want to strangle you,” Rasmus added with a laugh. “My son can be quite the loudmouth, I must admit.”

Heath sighed. “Okay, yes, we’re better at some of the normal life stuff, but I would still really like to be able to make a big-ass hammer out of thin air like that.”

Rasmus tilted his head from side to side. “I mean, yeah, I can’t really disagree with that. It is a very cool looking hammer.”

“Something’s happening,” Gul hissed without a glance at them.

As they all looked toward the mountain top, Eik raised the war hammer high into the air and pivoted it behind his back, where he paused for a few seconds. His ear was suddenly itchy. Was someone talking crap about him?

Then, as if the world went through a single frame cut, the hammer impacted the crumbled peak at his feet, obliterating the entire structure of the mountain at once like a watermelon smashed by a baseball bat.

Rocky debris that weighed hundreds of tons sailed through the air like tennis balls, any sign of Eik disappearing among the dust.

A shock wave rocked through the super reinforced crystal barrier, rattling it like a glass window pane in a hurricane. But with the downward direction of the force of the hammer blow, more powerful than the shock wave was the subterranean pulse of destruction that roiled through the earth, upturning everything in its wake.

Any other mountains in the way were split apart by the shattering wave, left as nothing but towering, jagged edges jutting out of the earth.

“Are we really… Are we going to be okay here?” Sireal, the chief cartographer of Eik’s Great Group of Guys asked with a trembling voice, crouching behind the barrier as he felt like he would be thrown off his feet at any moment. “Isn’t the Boss doing a little bit too much, uuh… destroying?”

“Nah, I’m sure we’ll be totally fine,” Heath said with impressive confidence. “The guy is an X-ranker, after all.”

“Yes, but didn’t he also almost kill everybody during that thing where he tossed around Death Stars willy nilly?”

“Ah, no, th— That’s, you know… That was early days. At this point, Eik has it all totally under control.”

Sireal couldn’t help but breathe a light sigh of relief at that. “Oh, thank goodness. That is so nice to he—”

“Public service announcement, everybody,” Eik suddenly spoke as he appeared above them in an instant, his world-permeating voice warping oddly according to the abrupt travel of a few kilometers. “We’re getting the hell out of Dodge while we still have all of our limbs attached!”

While he spoke, the ground beneath their feet seemed to begin to soften. A deep rumble from below signaled something catastrophic coming.

“What in the world is going on?” Andihar asked, Sireal feeling pure panic stealing away his ability to breathe.

“I don’t mean to cause alarm,” Eik said, his eyes moving from face to face as he counted people to make sure nobody was missing. “but there is a slight, little chance that I accidentally hollowed out whatever part of the underground is allowing all of this stuff we’re standing on to remain what we could refer to as… aboveground.”

“What?”

“All right, great! Let’s get going!” he shouted.

Sireal felt something happen to his body at those words. It was a sensation similar to the feeling of being lightheaded, only instead of just the skull, this made its way around the entire body. And just before his vision went white, the deep rumble turned into a roaring cacophony.

The landscape collapsed under their feet.
