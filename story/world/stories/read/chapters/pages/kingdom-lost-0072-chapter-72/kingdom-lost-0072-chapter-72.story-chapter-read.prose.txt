Valrik stood at the edge of the training field and watched as the last group of soldiers prepared for their march toward Hoshin. He had spent the better part of the morning working alongside Thorne, checking every piece of gear with practiced hands, reviewing formations down to the smallest detail, and making certain each man understood the need for complete discretion once they reached the village. The operation required them to blend in among the locals, gather needed resources without drawing unwanted attention, and return with full loads that would buy time for the settlement. When the column finally moved out under Thorne's steady command, Valrik allowed himself a small nod of satisfaction. The men looked sharp, disciplined, and ready for the task ahead.

He turned his attention to the next assignment Riley had given him. She wanted eyes on the raiders’ settlement, and the order had come more direct than usual, as if there were a hint of revenge behind it. A grim eagerness rose in Valrik's chest. It had not been long since those same raiders swept through their camp and slaughtered soldiers who carried little more than tools and harvesting implements. Most of them had been saved in the end, but the memory still burned hot in his mind. In Valrik's eyes a debt remained unpaid, written in the blood that had stained the ground that terrible night. He could still hear the screams and the clash of steel in his dreams.

Valrik handpicked two soldiers for the surveillance mission. Both men were steady and quiet, the kind who had proven themselves reliable in drills and small skirmishes alike. He gave them quick, precise instructions and led them to a quiet corner of the armory. There they removed their heavy armor, setting aside plates and mail that would only hinder stealth and make noise with every movement. Their swords, normally worn at the hip for easy access during patrols, were now slung across their backs. In this configuration the blades could still be drawn quickly if needed, yet they would not snag on low branches or brush and would not announce their presence with every step through the dense woodland.

The three men mounted fresh horses and rode out from the settlement in silence, the only sound the soft thud of hooves on the forest path. They covered three quarters of the distance before Valrik signaled a halt in a secluded grove. They tied the horses securely in a thick stand of trees well off the main paths. One soldier remained behind to guard the mounts and protect them from any wild monsters that might wander too close in the growing shadows. The animals would serve as vital backup if a swift retreat became necessary, but bringing them any nearer would destroy any chance Valrik and the other soldier had of moving undetected through the forest.

Back at the tower, Nikola sat before the glowing HUD map with focused attention. With the shroud now removed from the area, the interface revealed movement clearly in the general vicinity of the raiders' base. He could track figures outside the walls without difficulty, watching the red markers drift like embers. Once anyone stepped inside the compound, however, the map went dark on those details. He would know nothing of their exact numbers or what activities took place within the wooden walls. Riley had ordered him to monitor the site closely for any unusual activity. For the past day or so the map had shown little movement, only the occasional red marker drifting near the perimeter.

The soldier who had first discovered the base had reported hearing plenty of noise coming from inside. Nikola suspected the raiders were simply enjoying the fruits of their recent successes. Raider activity across the realm had increased sharply, which meant more loot flowing in and more reasons to celebrate with drink and loud revelry. The lack of visible movement outside made sense if the men were inside recovering from their exploits.

Valrik and the remaining soldier moved forward on foot through the dense forest. They kept low, using every fold in the land and every cluster of brush for cover, their steps careful and measured. When they drew close enough to make out the settlement through the trees, they dropped into a thick patch of undergrowth and studied the perimeter. Wooden walls around the camp, reinforced at each corner with small turrets designed for a single archer. The location sat far enough off the traveled roads that the raiders appeared to run only a minimal watch. It seemed they posted just two men to cover the turrets, moving between every other position rather than manning all four at once.

From inside the walls came the unmistakable sound of voices and laughter. It carried on the breeze like the general merriment of men who had eaten well and filled their purses. The noise drifted through the trees, mixing with the rustle of leaves and the distant call of birds.

The two men worked their way slowly around the base, moving quickly yet quietly whenever possible. They used trees and dense brush to stay hidden, circling until they gained a clear line of sight on the far side of the camp. There they noted only one major gate, wide enough for riders to pour out in force when the time came. On the opposite wall sat a smaller door, likely used for foot traffic or quick escapes when larger gates were not needed. Valrik and his companion settled into a good vantage point facing the main gate. They remained perfectly still as one of the archers in a nearby turret scanned the surrounding woods with lazy, half-hearted sweeps of his gaze.

They waited there for some time in complete silence, the minutes stretching into what felt like an eternity. Valrik's thoughts drifted as the forest sounds filled the quiet. He wondered how long he would have to wait before he could finally settle the score that gnawed at him day and night. His mind returned again to the night of the raid. The raiders had struck not only the gathering parties but the settlement itself with brutal efficiency. As Riley's military liaison and subcommander, he had felt the weight of that failure more than most. It had been his duty to recommend proper guard details, especially when Riley pushed so hard for maximum production from the gatherers. Safety could not be overlooked, yet he had not pressed the point strongly enough at the time.

Riley carried the final responsibility as leader, of course. At that time she had managed everything alone, long before Nikola joined their efforts. She barely slept, juggling every decision and every urgent need at once. Mistakes were bound to happen under such exhausting strain. Still, Valrik could not shake the sense that he shared at least part of the blame. Raiders were little more than thieves with weapons, not true disciplined soldiers. Even experienced ones relied heavily on the element of surprise. If a proper guard had been posted and the gatherers armed for close combat instead of carrying only tools, the outcome that night would have been very different indeed. The thought sat heavy in his chest, fueling the quiet anger that kept him focused.

His thoughts continued along that dark line until the cold press of a blade suddenly settled against his neck. Valrik's face tensed instantly. Every muscle in his body locked in place as the sharp steel kissed his skin.

"Well, what's this?" a rough voice growled close to his ear. "Are you two lost then?"

Valrik shifted his eyes to the left with the smallest possible movement, careful not to press his throat any harder against the sharp edge. A raider with a badly disfigured face stared down at him, lips curled in ugly amusement. Nearby, two large raiders had already hauled the other soldier roughly to his feet, gripping each arm tightly. The man's face showed clear tension, though he made no sound or struggle.

At that same moment, a sharp pulse of warning reached Valrik through the system link. It carried an urgent order to return immediately to the rally point where the horses waited. Nikola, or perhaps Riley herself, must have spotted the raiders closing in on the map and tried to recall them in time. The signal had come, but Valrik had been too deep in his bitter memories to notice it. Now the warning felt distant and utterly useless against the immediate threat.

Back at the settlement, Riley stood in her private room within the tower. She reviewed the latest status reports on resource production and training queues, her mind already turning to the next set of priorities. A heavy pounding suddenly echoed from the outer door below. She started down the stairs at once, moving quickly but keeping her balance on the familiar wooden steps.

Before she reached the bottom landing, the door swung open with force. Nikola burst inside, breathing hard, his face flushed from running across the settlement.

Riley stopped on the stairs and looked down at him with concern. "What is it, Nikola?"

He drew in a ragged breath and met her eyes directly. "It's Valrik, the raiders have him!"
