After the incident, the apprentice was rushed to the palace infirmary, his pained moans echoing off the cold stone walls. The other two apprentices stood nearby, pale-faced, their expressions twisted with fear and unease.

Luke pinched the bridge of his nose, pretending to be irritated.

"How many times have I told you to be careful? Do you not understand how many people my thunder bombs killed back in Clayton City?" he said coolly.

"I deeply apologize for my apprentice's incompetence, Viscount Drakon," Blake said, bowing low with a voice full of sincerity.

The two apprentices quickly followed suit, their bodies stiff with shame as they mirrored their mentor's gesture.

Luke felt a twinge of guilt. After all, he had intentionally sabotaged the batch. But there had been no other choice. He wasn't foolish enough to believe that the Royal Capital's alchemists wouldn't try to improve the gunpowder recipe on their own.

Even if he handed them an inferior version, it was only a matter of time before their curiosity and skill led them to something better.

By allowing the apprentice to get injured, however, he'd managed to instill fear. If he could keep orchestrating these “accidents,” he could stall their progress by several years.

"It’s fine," he said at last, letting out a theatrical sigh. "Though, all of our purified saltpeter is ruined thanks to this little mishap."

"We’ll acquire more, Viscount. Please don’t be discouraged," Blake replied quickly, eager to please.

Luke nodded. "Prepare the necessary ingredients. I’ll return to the workshop tomorrow after my studies. By then, the clay shells should be ready."

"As you wish, Viscount. Please, allow me to escort you to the gate."

Luke accepted the gesture without protest.

As they made their way through the winding corridors of the west wing, the air grew cooler. The echo of their footsteps bounced between the high stone walls. Up ahead, a group of regally dressed men approached from the opposite end. Leading them was a man with thick brows and a plain, unremarkable face. He moved with quiet authority.

Unlike the two men behind him, who wore embroidered tunics and ornamental sashes, the leader was dressed simply in green robes. But his followers' deferential body language made it clear who held the real power.

"That’s the Right Minister…" Blake whispered, lowering his head. "Make sure you don’t offend him."

Luke’s mind sharpened, and a surge of tension shot through him.

This man held more influence than anyone in the kingdom, save for King Julius himself. One misstep could cost Luke his life.

Without hesitation, Luke stepped aside and bowed deeply, cupping his fist in the traditional show of respect. He kept his eyes fixed on the ornate rug beneath his feet.

He expected the group to pass without pause—but then, a pair of plain leather boots stopped directly in front of him. Luke’s muscles tensed. He didn’t dare lift his head.

"You must be Luke Drakon."

The voice was softer than he’d anticipated, almost gentle. But Luke remained cautious.

"It is an honor to have my lowly name known by a man of your stature, Right Minister," he said, keeping his tone even and deferential.

“Lift your head. My eyesight isn’t as good as it once was,” the soft voice spoke again.

With no choice, Luke obeyed.

The first thing he noticed were the man's cold, green eyes—piercing and sharp, framed by thick, dark brows. They held an intensity befitting of a man of his stature.

‘There’s no way this man has bad eyesight.’ That was Luke’s first thought.

For a brief moment, the Right Minister’s expression shifted—an unmistakable flicker of surprise—but it was quickly masked behind a neutral facade. Luke noticed it, though he made sure his own expression remained composed.

“They say you can see the heart of a man through his eyes,” the Minister said, his gaze unyielding. “Yet when I look at you now... I see nothing.”

Luke remained silent. To look away would be disrespectful—possibly fatal—but to answer without caution could be just as dangerous.

“Tell me,” the man continued, “what do you see when you look into mine?”

Before Luke could respond, one of the elaborately dressed men behind the Minister stepped forward.

“Minister, the court is waiting.”

Luke nearly sighed in relief but held his composure as the Minister finally broke eye contact.

“I find it quite curious that a commoner could make such an Alchemical discovery,” the Minister said, brushing invisible dust from his plain robe. “Even before our Royal Alchemists.”

There was no hostility in his tone—only something colder.

“Nonetheless,” he added, “it was good to finally meet you, Luke. I hope you continue to bring glory to the Marxx Kingdom.”

Luke lowered his head in a respectful bow, murmuring thanks.

Only when the sound of their retreating footsteps vanished into silence did he exhale, his shoulders finally relaxing.

‘Holy shit…’ Luke swore internally, placing a hand over his heart. His chest still felt tight.

‘I wasn’t even that nervous in front of the King.’

“Let’s continue,” Blake said, snapping him from his thoughts.

They resumed their walk through the palace halls, but Luke’s mind was no longer on their path.

If he’d answered the Minister honestly, it would have stirred suspicion—maybe worse.

Because when Luke had stared into those cold green eyes, he hadn’t seen wisdom or authority. He’d seen a tiger—patient, poised in the shadows, waiting to pounce.

A chill worked its way down his spine.

He’d long suspected there was a traitor within the highest ranks of the Marxx Kingdom. Someone orchestrating events from behind the curtain. After this encounter, the Right Minister had leapt to the top of his suspect list.

But suspicion wasn’t evidence. And evidence was the only weapon that could cut through politics. Even then, such a thing would be useless if he were dead.

‘I need to be patient,’ Luke reminded himself, steeling his resolve.

Only now did he begin to grasp the true nature of the capital—the heart of the kingdom. Beneath the grandeur of Ralis City beat something dark and unsightly. A tangled web of lies, ambitions, and carefully hidden truths.

The nobles, the King, his council—they all had their own agendas. And to stand in the way of those interests was to invite death.

He’d only been in the capital a short time, and already his life had twice brushed the edge of a blade.

He was walking a tightrope, high above the fires of ambition and betrayal. One wrong step, and he’d plunge into the flames—consumed before he ever had the chance to climb.

Before he realized it, Blake had already led him to the palace gates. The sun had dipped below the horizon, and lanterns had been lit along the street, casting long shadows across the cobbled roads.

“This is where we part ways, Viscount,” Blake said, bowing deeply. “We’ll ensure the ingredients are ready for your return tomorrow.”

“Very well,” Luke replied with a nod. “I’ll see you in the afternoon—assuming your apprentices don’t destroy the ingredients again.”

With that, he stepped beyond the gates and onto the dimly lit road, his eyes subtly scanning his surroundings.

Part of him had considered staying overnight within the palace walls. It would have been the safer option. After all, if assassins had dared to strike in broad daylight, there was little doubt they would try again under the cover of night.

But everything changed the moment he met the Right Minister.

Something deep in his gut warned him: distance was safer than any stone wall or armed guard. That man was dangerous.

Luke reached for the sword he’d borrowed from the Royal Academy’s armory—only to find empty space at his hip.

‘Damn it,’ he cursed silently. He’d left the weapon in the workshop.

Left with no other choice, he slipped into the shadows along the edge of the street and closed his eyes, focusing. A pulse of energy surged through him as his Eagle-Eye skill activated.

In an instant, his perspective shifted. The world stretched out beneath him like a map—an overhead view of the surrounding streets lit by flickering lanternlight. Shapes moved through the darkness: carts being pulled home, vendors packing up, shadows lingering where the light didn't reach.

Back at the palace gate, movement caught his attention.

One of the guards—someone he’d passed only moments ago—stepped away from his post and slinked toward a shadowy alley. A second figure emerged, cloaked in black from head to toe.

Luke’s eyes narrowed.

He couldn’t hear their words—Eagle-Eye didn’t grant sound—but he didn’t need to.

The palace guard pointed down the main street… the very one Luke had walked.

Luke clenched his jaw, fury welling in his chest.

“This bastard…” he hissed under his breath.

But there was no time for rage. The black-robed figure darted down the alley, vanishing into the maze of backstreets. Luke quickly zoomed his vision out, scanning for signs of more threats—any hint of movement along the path back to the Academy.

‘If I want to make it back alive… I’ll need to move quietly. No mistakes.’

Suddenly, a lone figure appeared ahead—walking straight toward him. Her graceful stride was smooth, almost predatory. The sway of her hips, the fluidity of her movements—it was as if the night itself bent around her.

“Young Master, you’re late,” came a voice—cool, teasing.

The sound slid into his ears like silk dipped in frost, sending a shiver down his spine.

Luke immediately canceled the skill and looked up.

Victoria stood before him, a bow slung across her shoulder, twin daggers glinting at her waist. Even in the dark, she radiated an elegant danger.

‘Did she sense I was in trouble?’

“Come here. There’s no time,” Luke said, motioning her over.

Thankfully, she obeyed without question. But as she caught sight of his expression, the playful glint in her eyes vanished, replaced by a focused seriousness.

“What is it, Young Master?” she asked, her voice low.

“You came at the right time,” Luke said, scanning the shadows behind her. “There are assassins looking for me. They’ll probably make their move before I reach the Academy.”

Victoria blinked at him, unmoving. Then, slowly, her lips curved into a radiant smile.

For a heartbeat, Luke forgot the danger. Her beauty was disarming—even now—but her next words pulled him straight back to reality.

“What great timing,” she said, her tone light but her eyes cold. “My daggers have been craving blood.”

“R-Right…” Luke coughed, steadying himself. “I’ll walk down the main road to draw them out. You take care of them before they take care of me.”

He’d never seen Victoria in a real fight, but something about her exuded lethality. She didn’t need the System’s enhancements to be terrifying. In fact, Luke had a hunch she could go toe-to-toe with someone like Sebastian… maybe even Kayson.

“Yes, Young Master,” she replied, the smile still dancing on her lips.

“Ah—wait. Can I borrow your bow?”

“Of course.”

She unstrapped it smoothly and handed it over. Luke caught it, plucked an arrow from the quiver, and nocked it without hesitation. His eyes returned to the palace gate where the treacherous guard still stood, oblivious.

It wasn’t the most strategic move—killing a palace guard in the capital could spark problems. But Luke didn’t care. Not tonight.

He pulled back the bowstring, exhaled slowly, and let the arrow fly.

It sliced through the night air with a soft whistle before embedding itself directly into the guard’s left eye. The man crumpled without a sound.

Luke exhaled with satisfaction and returned the bow to Victoria. “Much better.”

She said nothing, but the glint in her eyes said everything.

“Go—now!” Luke barked, breaking into a run as shouts erupted behind him.

Victoria sprinted past him in a blur, vanishing into the shadows with eerie silence. One moment she was there, the next… gone.

Luke’s eyes widened. ‘I can’t even sense her anymore. Please let this work…’

He didn’t have long to dwell on it.

A sudden prickle ran down his spine. The hairs on the back of his neck stood up. Instinct screamed.

Without hesitation, Luke dove to the left, tucking into a tight army roll and springing back onto his feet in one fluid motion.

Behind him, an arrow clattered harmlessly against the stone street.

His gaze shot upward.

A black-robed figure stood on a rooftop nearly two hundred feet away, already drawing another arrow.

But before the man could fire, another arrow—silent, swift, and deadly—pierced his throat. The assassin collapsed, tumbling from the rooftop like a sack of flour and crashing onto the street below.

‘Holy shit!’ Luke thought, adrenaline spiking.

But he had no time to celebrate.

A blur of motion struck him from the side—a heavy, sweeping blow slamming into his chest.

The impact lifted him off his feet and hurled him to the ground. His lungs emptied in a rush, leaving him gasping.

Pain blossomed across his torso as he stared up at the sky, stunned.

Then—footsteps.

A massive figure loomed over him, blocking the lamplight. Muscles bulged beneath tight black robe. The man raised a long spear above his head, its sharpened point aimed straight for Luke’s heart.
