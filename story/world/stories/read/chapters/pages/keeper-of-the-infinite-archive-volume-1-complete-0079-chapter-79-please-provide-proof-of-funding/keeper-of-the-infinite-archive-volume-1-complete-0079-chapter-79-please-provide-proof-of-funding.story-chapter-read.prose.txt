The next morning, before the Archive’s first limited public service period, the Patron Services desk displayed a new, quietly ticking countdown.

TEMPORARY ROTA AUTHORIZATION: DAY 1 of 21

General admission would open in two Archive hours. Bart stood behind the desk, drinking a cup of perfectly brewed coffee that Albert had provided as soon as he had entered the room. His first thoughts were back on funding side so he decided to ask the obvious,

“System,” Bart said. “How do we formally request contributions from currently connected institutions?”

The interface flickered, opening a directory of approved institutional protocols.

CONTRIBUTION SOLICITATION GUIDELINES

WARNING: Contributions may not purchase:

Bart  pointed at the glowing blue list to Albert and said “Why did it specify naming rights so specifically?”

“Historical experience, sir,” Albert answered smoothly from near the intake tray,.

Bart considered their options. The Silver Spire seemed wealthy and may have been heavily invested in the Archive previously given scholarly connections and already connected. “System, draft a contribution request to the Silver Spire.”

REQUEST DENIED

ACCREDITING INSTITUTION MAY NOT SERVE AS FINANCIAL GUARANTOR FOR AN ACTIVE APPLICANT

It made perfect administrative sense. The Archive could not accept money from the people inspecting it without compromising the integrity of Senior Registrar Halver’s audit. That closed the most obvious shortcut.

Bart reopened the list of active connections. Greenhollow could probably contribute food, timber, or labour, but its people were rebuilding their own lives. Asking them to finance the institution that was supposed to serve them felt fundamentally backwards.

The merchants and bakers might provide credit or supplies, but they were individual businesses, not an established funding body. Combining their contributions into a reliable payroll agreement would require contracts, guarantees, and considerably more than three days.

Morzath might possess sufficient resources. Unfortunately, Bart had no idea whether those resources included usable currency, rather than preserved corpses, prohibited artefacts, or favours he would regret accepting.

“System, which currently connected source can provide a verified 30 day operating guarantee?”

CROWN OF TREMENDIA: RECOGNIZED GOVERNMENTAL GUARANTOR - ELIGIBLE

“We have exactly one recognized governmental connection,” Bart concluded.

He spent the next fifteen minutes carefully drafting a formal, neutral request to the Crown of Tremendia, citing the Archive’s restored limited public service and provisional external recognition, and outlining the parameters of a recognized institutional endowment.

Gregor leaned over to read the dense and qualified administrative language.

“You could have just written, ‘The library needs money,’” Gregor said.

“That is not an appropriate institutional request,” Bart replied, tapping the interface to send the message.

“It would have been shorter, for sure” Gregor pointed out.

Bart expected a response within a few days, likely from a Tremendian treasury official demanding another mountain of paperwork.

Surprisingly, the response arrived in 45 minutes. General public admission was not due to open for another hour.

The floorboards in the main hall vibrated slightly. A moment later, Mabel’s resonant voice echoed from the entrance of portal.

“Keeper. The Crown of Tremendia is requesting institutional admission. One monarch, 12 attendants, 6 chests, and one unregistered ceremonial object.”

Bart hurried around the desk.

“System, evaluate the proposed entry.”

ENTRY CLASSIFICATION: INSTITUTIONAL BUSINESS

GENERAL PUBLIC ADMISSION: CURRENTLY CLOSED

PERSONS REQUESTING ENTRY: THIRTEEN

PROJECTED NON-STAFF ALLOCATION: 16.2 SPE

CEREMONIAL INSTALLATION AUTHORIZATION: NOT PRESENT

ACCESS GUARDIAN CONCURRENCE: REQUIRED

Mabel studied the royal party waiting on the other side of portal. “I concur with temporary entry. I do not concur with installation.”

“Entry authorized,” Bart said and the party appeared onto the Archive.

King Aurelius strode into the Infinite Archive in full, gleaming golden armour. Behind him came 6 exhausted attendants,5 treasury officials maneuvering chests on small iron trolleys, the 7th being Thaddeus the Royal Historian / Liaison carrying the paperwork. The Attendants struggled beneath the weight of a massive bust of Aurelius.

Aurelius stopped in the centre of the hall, struck a heroic pose, and projected his voice toward the vaulted ceiling.

“The Crown of Tremendia will rescue the Infinite Archive from insolvency!”

“You may contribute to the Archive,” Bart corrected immediately, stepping forward.

“That is what I said,” Aurelius replied, beaming.

“You said rescue.”

Aurelius waved his hand dismissively. “History will decide the preferred verb. Behold the royal endowment!”

The treasury officials set the six chests before the desk. They landed with a solid thud that spoke of weight in serious funding. The attendants lowered the giant golden bust next to them.

“System,” Bart said cautiously. “Evaluate the proposed endowment terms.”

The interface flashed over the Patron Services desk, glowing a harsh, cautionary amber.

FUNDING SOURCE: PERSONAL ROYAL CONTRIBUTION

GOVERNMENTAL ENDOWMENT STATUS: NOT ESTABLISHED

ATTACHED CONDITIONS DETECTED

REQUESTED TITLE: FOUNDING PATRON

REQUESTED PRIORITY ACCESS: PRESENT

REQUESTED CEREMONIAL INSTALLATION: OVERSIZED

NEUTRALITY COMPLIANCE: FAILED

Aurelius narrowed his eyes at the glowing text. “I object to the word ‘oversized.’ It is a perfectly proportional rendering of his royal majesty.”

“Your Majesty,” Bart said, maintaining his most diplomatic librarian tone. “You did not found the Archive. You cannot purchase a faster research service than any other patron and you absolutely cannot place a giant golden version of yourself next to Patron Services.”

“Every important institution has statues,” Aurelius insisted.

Gregor pointed across the hall toward the skeletal custodian. “We already have a dead person standing near the entrance.”

Albert paused his polishing. “I am not decorative, sir.”

Aurelius frowned, his pride clashing with his genuine desire to be associated with the legendary library. He wasn't acting with malice; he simply thought like that, to him, money naturally produced prestige, influence, and statues…legacy.

“Tremendia is prepared to fund 30 Archive days of operation,” Aurelius negotiated, stepping closer to the desk. “What does the Crown receive in return?”

“The gratitude of the Archive,” Bart said.

Aurelius waited. “And?”

“And Tremendian scholars will receive the exact same neutral access as everyone else,” Bart explained. “The Crown receives no authority over holdings, staffing, or evidence. Your requests receive no priority over other patrons. The agreement may be renewed, but it does not renew automatically. All contributions are entered into a public institutional record.”

Aurelius looked disappointed. “Can I have a plaque?”

“You may receive a factually accurate, normally proportioned plaque,” Bart allowed.

Aurelius brightened instantly. “Excellent. It shall read: Founder of the Restored Archive.”

“No,can do” Bart said.

“Supreme Benefactor?”

“No.”

“Principal Patron of All Recorded Knowledge?”

“No.”

“Humanity’s Foremost Supporter of Literacy?”

“No.” Bart tapped the interface. “You may have: FIRST POST-RUIN GOVERNMENTAL CONTRIBUTOR.”

Aurelius considered the title. He crossed his armoured arms, the metal clinking sharply. “Can the word ‘first’ be larger than the other words?”

“No,” Bart said.

“Slightly larger?”

“No can do again,”

Aurelius sighed heavily. He turned and snapped his fingers at Thaddeus.

The Historian, who looked completely unsurprised by the failure of the negotiations, stepped forward. He unrolled a much simpler, thoroughly compliant governmental endowment instrument. He had correctly anticipated that the king’s initial, highly conditional offer would fail the Archive’s strict neutrality standards.

Bart placed the parchment on the intake tray.

The warning that followed flashed in a steady, pulsing amber.

FUNDING CONCENTRATION: 100% - SINGLE JURISDICTION

SUSTAINABILITY RISK: HIGH

It was a dangerous balance. Tremendia had solved the immediate cash problem, but Aurelius had not permanently funded the Archive, nor had he resolved the Archive’s historic staff obligations. If Tremendia declined to renew the agreement or faced a crisis of its own, the Archive’s payroll would fail again. Bart would eventually have to rebuild a broad, diversified partner network.

But for the next thirty days, the Archive's current payroll and one desperately needed engineering position was funded.

“System,” Bart said. “Use the Tremendian contribution as the 30 day compensation reserve. Generate voluntary interim packages using surviving Archive role grades and the current Tremendian public service benchmark. Preserve employee choice of settlement.”

The interface processed the instruction.

The Archive had never paid every species in identical coins. It had recorded the value of their work, maintained an individual compensation profile, and used the old partner network to settle that value in whatever form sustained the employee. The conversion network was gone, but the principle remained intact. Tremendian gold became the funding reserve, not compulsory wages.

The Archive ring fenced her share of the contribution until she selected a useful form of compensation or a functioning local settlement channel became available.

She studied the final two lines for a long time, then lowered her head in a single, deliberate nod.

Bart’s own Keeper stipend also flickered to life under the officeholder schedule.

Gregor clapped him on the shoulder, raising a small cloud of dust. “Congratulations, boss. You’re officially being paid to be broke.”

With the staff packages accepted, the primary interface updated with a satisfying, chiming sound. The amber warning over the payroll schedule vanished.
