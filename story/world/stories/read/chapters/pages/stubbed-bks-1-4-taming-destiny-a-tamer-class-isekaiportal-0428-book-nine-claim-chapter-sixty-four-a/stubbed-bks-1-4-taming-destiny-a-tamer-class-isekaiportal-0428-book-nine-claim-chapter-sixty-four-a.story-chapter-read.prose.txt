"What if we–" River is cut off when a rumbling roar echoes through the cavern, and a tremor shakes the building slightly around us. I'm not alone in glancing nervously up at the ceiling, but everything holds – for now.

It's the third day since the manastorm hit, and conditions have only worsened since it arrived. When the storm front first swept over us, the sky went black, and not only because it was nighttime. I was peeping out of a hole in the wall, but I closed it up as I saw the iridescent lightning bolts beginning to fall around us. Hauntingly beautiful, but awfully dangerous.

At first we felt very safe inside our cavern, confident that the lightning bolts wouldn’t make their way through the thick rock above us. But the bolts that fell on the first day were far weaker than those falling now. Today is the first day that's heralded strikes strong enough to reverberate through the stone of our shelter. At least they're not frequent, and the enchanters have assured me that there’s little chance of them making it through the rock itself. Nor are they likely to angle themselves correctly to hit the more vulnerable newly-grown wall blocking off the entrance to the cavern.

The main function of the walls is to keep out the ambient and mutating Energy that floats around for a while after a strike, like a kind of miasma. It reminds me distinctly of what I learned about nuclear warfare and the radiation that results and can cause more damage than the strike itself. Fortunately, Energy is absorbed quickly by the natural materials around it, so it shouldn’t linger too long once the storm is finished.

I am, however, far more worried about Artio, Artemis, and Orion, because their structure is significantly weaker than this sanctuary, which was first dug into the hillside and then heavily enchanted by multiple enchanters. In comparison, my feet-thick earthen walls imbued with my mana are relatively weak. But it seems that mana lightning bolts aim for the biggest structures and beings just like the mundane kind, so hopefully they won’t be targeted. In the end, all I can do is keep checking in with Artemis to ensure they're okay, if sleepy.

Though I often compare the deris to wolves, it's clear they're not exactly the same thing – it seems deris are capable of a kind of hibernation in times of famine where they remain dormant but still have some awareness of their environment. If a threat occurs, they can react quickly, but in the meantime they use far fewer resources to maintain their health.

It’s just as well. I left them with two carcasses – which I fought against feeling guilty about, knowing they could have fed people in the settlement – along with a large bowl of water I formed out of earth and drew up liquid from the ground to fill. Even so, I fear they won't be in excellent condition when they emerge. But if they're alive, that's better than I currently fear.

Returning to the present, I clear my throat.

"You were saying, River?"

She looks briefly startled, then tilts her chin up.

"Yes, Markus, apologies. I was saying that perhaps we could work on removing one of the conditions required for the treatment to work. If it no longer has to be wet, the treatment might be more widely applicable. Or if it didn't have to be acidic, we'd be able to do more with the dish afterwards."

“That would be helpful,” Kera Isolde agrees. “What we are currently required to do when preparing food is far from ideal.”

She's the head cook, and therefore an important part of this meeting, which is currently between the alchemists, cooks, and healers. As ever, our topic of conversation is how to deal with the food shortages staring us in the face. We've already started strict rationing, but that only buys us an extra few days.

Even if we found a workable treatment today, it wouldn’t solve everything – we have minimal stockpiles of local produce, since thus far none of it has truly been edible, and we didn't have time before the storm hit to gather much more. However, with the Plant Mages involved, they should be able to rapidly grow some plants once we find something that works. If we can find a decent treatment, it would probably be enough to keep us going until we’re able to put better supply chains in place.

I hope. Otherwise, we're going to start losing the most vulnerable members of our community: the very young, the very old, and the sick. An image of a pregnant woman I saw visiting the healers for a check up flashes through my mind and I swallow.

Actually, I need to discuss the health of the population with Thessalanor soon – given the situation, I can’t imagine that the healers are less overburdened than before. Indeed, it’s more likely to be the opposite due to the combination of close-quarters and poor nutrition. There aren’t many truly elderly because of the nature of the expeditions, but some of the slaves are older than ideal, brought for their skills rather than their fitness levels.

Then there are the children – those born since we set off on the expedition and those yet to be born. Their mothers are already being given extra rations so they can nourish their babies in turn, but if our food runs out, that’s it. Already some of the young children are getting worryingly thin – and that’s even though they’re given more relative to their size than most adults.

It’s distressing every time I walk down a street and see my people with such hollow, hungry eyes. But I’m doing everything I can to deal with the myriad of problems that currently beset us. From figuring out ways to stop people from panicking and threatening to turn violent to dealing with the samuran hatchlings who are exhausting their carers now they aren’t using up all their Energy in trips to the forest.

While I've been lost in my thoughts, River and the other Alchemists have been in deep discussion about how to transform the current Energy-absorption treatment so that it still works without having to mimic the conditions of a human stomach.

"It sounds like you need to go away and experiment," I interject as the discussion starts to get heated. "Have you managed to find a way to test the Energy levels in a substance without someone needing to consume it?” Having to test on people and then mop up our healers' time healing those who've been poisoned by the energy is far from ideal.

Sera Kerst tentatively nods.

"There's a potion I know of which is generally used to colour fabric. It works by adding different quantities of powdered beast Cores once it's fully brewed, as it changes colour according to the quantity placed within. It's highly prized for the gem-like colours it produces, unlike anything else in the market. However, I theorise that by putting a certain quantity of Energy-infused plant matter into the potion – and possibly leaving it for a period of time for the potion to break down the plant's structures – we may be able to tell from the shade of the resulting colour approximately how much Energy is held within."

“A sort of litmus test,” I muse, mostly to myself.

"A what, Lord Markus?"

"Don't worry,” I quickly dismiss. “It's the name for a type of test where I'm from that detects how acidic or alkaline something is, based on colour. This sounds very similar, except testing mana rather than acidity." Sera Kerst looks very curious.

"Would you tell me more of this litmus test, my lord?" she asks.

I hesitate, then glance around at the others assembled.

"Let’s speak afterwards, Sera Kerst. I wouldn't like to hold everyone here for a discussion more relevant to alchemy alone."

"Of course, my lord," the alchemist agrees, looking satisfied with that.

I turn my attention next to Kera Isolde.

"The alchemists have their tasks, which I'm sure they'll work on with as much focus as they can spare. But in the meantime, is there any way to make the effects of the current treatment more palatable?” I ask. “Perhaps other substances could be added to reduce the acidity." I remember from chemistry lessons that it's possible to neutralise an acid, which is why I'm considering that as a potential avenue.

Her dissatisfaction with the current treatment is understandable. In order to mimic the conditions of the human stomach, the food ends up extremely acidic – and the treatment doesn't even work with all plants, since some react badly to acid or become inedible after soaking in it. There have been only two different plants that have neither become completely inedible nor reacted especially badly, but they can barely be considered edible – most people don't want to burn the skin off their tongue while eating.

Kera Isolde looks unhappy.

"We've already been trying to make it more palatable, and so far haven't had much success. This is complicated by the lack of resources we have available to us.”

I nod reluctantly, accepting her point.

“We need to figure out a way to remove the acid after the treatment,” I sigh. “But how?”

There’s silence for a moment, and then River leans forward, a hint of excitement playing through her spikes. I raise my eyebrows expectantly at her – she’s clearly got an idea.

"Do you remember how we first started making healing potions together, Markus?"

“I do,” I reply slowly, thinking back to that time. It’s almost two years ago now, but I can still remember it clearly enough thanks to the points I’ve added to Intelligence.

“Do you recall how we struggled with the healing potion – and its acidity?’

I immediately know what she’s referring to – and have an idea of what she might be getting at.

At the time, we were trying to recreate what River remembered of the herbalist's recipes without the magic the herbalist had used. The acid in that potion was necessary to break down the structures of one ingredient, but in too great a concentration it rendered the potion almost as damaging as it was healing. At the time, I was able to affect the acid concentration because it was an animal product, which fell under my Flesh Shaping. In this case – which, I suppose, could be considered a kind of potion, even if it's designed for mass nutrition rather than healing – the Plant Mages might be able to intervene the way I did.

“Good idea, River,” I praise and blue pleasure mixed with pink dapple through her spikes.

I look back at Kera Isolde.

"I'll speak to the head of the mages and ask for the most flexible Water and Plant mages to be sent to you, to test what can be done in the kitchens – consider how you should direct them to help you. Even if it requires a mage to intervene on every batch for now, that's fine. The important thing is that we find some way of nourishing our people.”

After all, we can make the process more efficient later, but there's no point worrying about effective deployment of mages if we don't survive the next few weeks.

"Yes, my lord," Kera Isolde replies, sitting back and looking slightly overwhelmed. She's not a Classer and has earned her position through sheer skill and an uncanny ability to be everywhere at once and organise a kitchen of more than fifty people. I can understand why she might feel daunted at the prospect of managing mages, who are generally seen as superior to the average non-Classer.

"Thessalanor," I turn to the healer. "How are things going on the sickness front?"

"Not good, my lord," Thessalanor informs me grimly.

"All the same symptoms?" I ask, concerned.

Thessalanor hesitates, then nods slowly.

"Approximately, my lord. Headaches seem to be practically omnipresent, as does vomiting and diarrhoea, though the frequency varies from person to person – some suffer only once, while others can barely keep any food in their bodies without expelling it. Fever is another symptom that varies, with some reaching almost dangerous levels while others don't report it at all. Joint pain is mostly present, though a few have reported very little of it. Occasionally I've seen rarer symptoms such as a rash or protruding veins, particularly in the neck, chest, or on the backs of the hands, but those seem to fade very quickly."

I tap the table thoughtfully. Vomiting, diarrhoea, headaches…. I glance at Kera Isolde.

"Kera, I mean no disrespect, but do you ensure that all food is heated sufficiently and doesn't spend too much time sitting out?"

The head cook shifts in her seat.

"As much as is possible, my lord, but with the treatment we're having to use to remove the Energy from the plants, we're forced to leave concoctions out for marks at lukewarm temperatures. And as you know, my lord, we've been trying to supplement our failing meat supplies with what is safe to eat from the plants."

I nod slowly, then glance back at Thessalanor.

"Have you noticed the sickness increasing since we started doing this?"

Thessalanor hesitates.

"I have, my lord," he agrees, "however, I cannot say for certain that the cause is the kitchens. As you'll recall, we had cases before the Energy treatment was implemented and we dismissed the kitchens in that case as a vector of contamination."

"That's true," I admit. "Though you indicated that you didn’t detect anything in the well. Have you asked a Water Mage to check it out?”

He looks down at the table.

“I didn’t consider it, my lord,” he admits.

I sigh.

“I should have thought of it too,” I reassure him, seeing the blame written on his face. “I’ll organise it. And if I get a chance, I’ll go and check it myself too.”

"Do you think there could be multiple causes?” Sera Kerst asks, leaning forwards. “Perhaps what was ailing people before the food treatment is something different from what they're suffering from now?"

Thessalanor nods at her, but reluctantly.

"You don't think that's the case," I conclude.

"I have no data to prove it is all from the same source," he says quietly but confidently, "but I have instincts developed over years as a healer, and they are telling me it is all connected."

I nod slowly.

"That doesn't necessarily rule out something they're eating, though – it may not be a result of the Energy treatment itself, but for all we know there's bacteria in the meat that's been affecting some people more than others."

"Bacteria?" Kera Isolde asks, confused.

"Tiny living things in food," I translate. "You can't see them with the naked eye, but where I come from, we discovered that food spoils because tiny creatures eat it and produce toxins as waste products. By heating food thoroughly, you kill a good portion of them. By keeping it cold, you slow the rate at which they multiply."

"They breed in our food?" Kera Isolde asks, sounding horrified.

"They do," I agree, "but it's not generally something to worry too much about. Bacteria are everywhere – on everything. Not all of them are harmful – some living in our bodies actually help us process food. But if the wrong kind get into food, for example because we haven’t washed our hands properly before preparing a meal, it can be a problem – especially if the food is left out at room temperature. They can multiply very quickly – even within candlemarks."

She looks very worried after that explanation.

"I'll tell you more later if you'd like," I offer, "including how to ensure they don't get out of hand." She shudders, then inclines her head reluctantly.

"I would appreciate that, my lord."

"Right. To summarise: the alchemists will continue in two directions simultaneously – first, testing this colour-changing potion to see if it can reliably measure the Energy levels in a substance, and second, attempting to change the conditions under which the Energy-absorption treatment works. The cooks, meanwhile, will continue testing what can be done with the current treatment, including working with the mages to potentially remove the acid or otherwise make it more palatable for the average person to eat. And Thessalanor, you and I need to try to find where these infections are coming from, because we cannot afford this sickness getting further out of hand." I look around the table. "Any questions?"

No one has anything to add – until the storm decides to weigh in. We all flinch at the loud rumble from above.

"Meeting adjourned,” I tell them levelly, the moment it fades. “Let's get to work."
