I was huffing and puffing, but I did manage to drag myself to the metalsmith’s workshop at something close to sunrise-ish time.

“Come with me,” Barren grunted when he saw me behind the counter, then walked into the back of the store.

“You are now a novice apprentice,” he began, with no enthusiasm whatsoever. “This is the lowest level. In this level, ye shall craft only knives. And yer pay shall be three silver a day.”

I already knew that, but the way he said it out loud just made it sound way more depressing.

“You are to make five bronze knives each day, at fair quality.” He continued, like a man reciting his will. “If ye exceed yer quota, and quality, ye may rise to beginner apprentice. Should ye fall short—ye shall be given a warning. Three warnings, and ye shall be fired.”

“Wait—three warnings per day? Or total?” I asked. But he wasn’t finished and completely ignored me.

“Now,” he said, and turned to walk deeper into the store, “I shall show ye how to make a knife.”

He led me into the stockroom, which was packed with shelves of metal bars—different lengths, thicknesses, and colors. He pulled one off a low shelf, a short yellowish one—bronze, apparently.

“How do we get these?” I asked. “The bars. Where do they come from?”

There were no wagons or shipments coming in and out of town, no blacksmith’s guild or iron mine. I was really curious.

He ignored me, of course. Few-words NPC, this guy.

He led me into the forge room, and instantly a wave of heat hit me in the face. There was a large glowing furnace on one side, a variety of tools lined up on the wall, a huge anvil in the center.

He took a pair of large tongs and placed the metal slab into the furnace. After waiting a few minutes, he pulled it out—white-hot—and laid it on the anvil, then began hammering.But I can’t take my eyes off the furnace. There’s no box of coal or any other fuel nearby. I walk closer despite the heat, trying to peek inside. I don’t see any coal burning—just fire.

It’s... a magical furnace. I’m sure of it. Just like I’m sure the metal bars in the stock room replenish themselves.

I suddenly got it. This was how the workshop system here worked—semi-magical. No delivery carts. No mining operation. No fuel. Just bars that magically replenish in the stockroom and a fire that never dies. But the actual forging? The hammering, shaping, sharpening—that was manual. Like real medieval labor.

So the idea was probably that each workshop would be self-sufficient, without the need for external resources like raw materials and fuel. It was so interesting.Unfortunately, I was so deep in my excited blacksmith-economy analysis that I missed the actual knife making demonstration.

I turned back to Barren and—shit! He had already half-formed the knife. I’d missed it.

“Could you... uh... just show me the last part again?” I asked sheepishly.

No answer. Of course.

He kept working, heating the knife again when it got cold, hammering it some more. Back and forth, like twenty times. The process took over an hour, and I was sweating like crazy, and I hadn’t even touched a tool yet.

It was mesmerizing, actually. The glowing orange metal, the spark bursts, the heavy rhythmic clang of the hammer. Somehow… peaceful.

After maybe an hour, he cooled the blade down and took it to a grindstone wheel. Sharpened it. Smoothed it. Shaped it. Then added a pre-carved wooden handle, adjusted it with a file, attached it with a metal rivet, and finally used a leather strop to polish the edge until it looked terrifyingly sharp.

The finished knife was beautiful. I stared at it. I could imagine making something like that. Feeling proud. Holding it and saying, “I made this.”

And then—just like that—he left. Walked away. Back behind the counter.

No encouragement. No advice. No, “Good luck, lad.” Just gone.

Worst teacher ever.

Still, my turn! Time to make some damn blades.

I remembered what he said - if I exceeded the quota and kept the quality, I could level up to a beginner apprentice. That would mean making better gear. Earning more money. Eventually, maybe I’d craft real swords—or even armor. Maybe I’d rent my own forge someday. And maybe I’d make enchanted weapons… become a legendary smith.

Two hours later, my right hand was jelly, I was pretty sure my eyebrows had burned off, I had burns in seven different places, and the worst part?THE DAMN SLAB WOULDN’T SHAPE RIGHT.

It was glowing red again. Back into the furnace it went—for the 500th time. what I had produced on the anvil looked like a twisted chunk of sad bronze. It resembled a knife the way a potato resembles a spaceship.

I couldn’t do it.

How the hell did Ramon manage this? He wasn’t even muscular. Was he secretly a gym bro? How can he wield this stupid, heavy hammer all day?

I dragged myself to Barren at the front counter, barely able to hold the thing in my hand.

He glanced at my work and said flatly, “This blade be of inferior quality. It shall not be accepted.”

I wanted to cry. My whole day… wasted.

“I can’t go anymore,” I muttered. “I’ll try again tomorrow.”

“The work is from sunrise to sunset,” he said, like a Skyrim guard. “If ye abandon the day’s work, it counts as a warning. Three warnings and ye be fired.”

“Well,” I snapped, “if this is how you treat people after they literally melt their arm off for you, then no thanks!”

I ripped off the gloves and the leather apron dramatically—well, semi-dramatically. I could barely move. My whole body was jelly.

Barren himself didn’t say a word. That right! Don’t try to talk me out of it!Stupid blacksmithing job. So much for the manly trade of fire and metal.I needed work. We needed money for the tribe. I couldn’t even pay for a room at the inn. I'd have to sleep on Juliette’s couch again. Maybe… maybe she’d let me share her bed?

Probably not.

So, I wandered the town. Muttering to myself. Feeling dumb.There has to be something I can do ... I could be a bard? A jester? Beta tester for medieval VR?Then I saw through a shop window: a beautiful girl.

Not like Hildi. But genuinely cute. Soft features. Elegant posture. Long braid. Definitely someone I wouldn’t mind being told off by.

Yes. This is it. I want to work here—at the... I looked up at the sign: Ysabel’s Clothier Workshop.Clothes—I can definitely handle that.I walked in, trying to puff out my chest. “I’m an adventurer,” I announced. “I’m looking for an honest day’s work for an honest day’s pay.”

The woman looked up, nodded calmly, and said, “We are in need of a tailor apprentice. Ye shall begin as novice. That is the lowest level. Ye shall craft only undergarments. For this, ye shall be paid three silver a day.”

She was so much nicer than Barren!“You must make ten garments each day, at fair quality,” she continued. “Exceed this, and ye may rise to beginner. Fall short, and ye shall be given a warning. At three warnings... ye shall be fired.”

So... also not that different.She walked me through the process. I paid full attention this time. No getting distracted by something like enchanted panties.

She pulled a piece of hemp cloth from a stockroom—which, I suspected magically restocked itself. She showed me how to follow a parchment cutting pattern—very clearly a woman’s underpants.

She cut the cloth with iron shears, stitched with linen thread, used bone needles. Even added embroidery and lace.

And you’ll be damned to know this: I actually had a bit of experience sewing. Back on Earth, I once tried to cosplay Link from Zelda, and my mom taught me how to hand-stitch for the tunic.

So I sat down and got to work.

In the first hour, I made two pairs. One was “mediocre” quality and got rejected. The other was “decent” and passed.

Not bad.

In the next hour, I made three more. Two fair quality. One even got an approving nod. I even added little embroidered hearts to one.

By the end of the day, I’d made over a dozen—most at fair quality, three even ranked adequate. I was exhausted, but for the first time in a while, proud.

I made women’s underwear.

And I rocked it.

“You did a fine job today,” Ysabel said, as the sun began to set. “You have earned promotion to beginner apprentice. Ye shall now receive four silver a day.”

OH. MY. GOD.

Promotion on day one! Level two in something! One could say… that maybe, just maybe… making women’s underpants is less manly than, say, forging a sword.

But screw it. I was proud. If I’m going to make underwear, I’ll make the finest underpants in the entire planet of… uh… whatever this planet’s name is.

And most importantly, with the extra money, we’ll be able to help the tribe. Get the weapons. The food. The gear. Prepare for what looks like a war.
