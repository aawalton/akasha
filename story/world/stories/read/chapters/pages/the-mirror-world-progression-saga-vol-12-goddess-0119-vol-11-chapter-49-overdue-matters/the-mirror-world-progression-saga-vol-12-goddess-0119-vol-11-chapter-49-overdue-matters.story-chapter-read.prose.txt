Internally, Samantha was cackling at the development. Nice one, Azure! It’s so awesome that my wife was a dragon! You’re so fucking cool, oh my gods!

Meanwhile, Ashawein was stunned, gobsmacked, and amazed. Her mouth dropped open as she stared at the dragon and was speechless.

The dragon held her gaze with her smoldering purple eyes. “When this started, I expected to be rid of you after a challenge or two, but you just… kept… succeeding, no matter how ridiculous the task. Perhaps I should’ve asked you to steal the moon for me, too? Well, it’s too late for hindsights.” She blew steam out of her nostrils. “Always expect betrayal from my kind, fool. Do not expect an apology. Nonetheless, you’ve earned my respect. Here.”

With a brandished claw point, the dragon picked up the wineskin by its strap and held it out to Ashawein. “You’ve completed all of the trials and profited me greatly. In seeking this particular treasure, though, I aimed simply to end our farce with an insurmountable task. The truth is, I cannot use this.” The dragon shrugged. “Nor do I particularly want it. I return it to you as a gift. Take it and drink, as you are owed. None have ever been proven more worthy of it.”

Still getting her bearings, Ashawein swallowed a lump in her throat and blinked repeatedly at the dragon. “You’re not even a sapphire dragon?”

The dragon queen huffed and rolled her eyes. “Light blue for a time, gradually darkening, until the oldest of the old turns a color effectively black. Other beings grunt their childish and simplistic names for us; they are meaningless to me. Now, take the wineskin before I change my damned mind!” Her claw shook, making the wineskin bounce around.

Ashawein quickly took the wineskin, and then slung it over her shoulder. She asked, “And what of the boon?”

“Your previous intention is obviously impossible. Speak your new request.”

Ashawein took a long moment to pretend to think, tapping her lip with a finger. Finally, she smiled, and declared, “I have it! Ahem!” Clearing her throat dramatically, she finally touched a hand to her heart and declared, “I hereby ask for your hand in marriage, Dragon Queen!”

The dragon stared at her for a long, long moment. “You cannot be serious. You are talking to a Dragon. Queen. Do you even hear yourself? What makes you think my kind marry at all?”

“I just sort of assumed! Especially because of the ‘queen’ part. Would I be, rather, a consort, then? Well, whatever it is you do, the point is that I want to do it. I simply fancied that a wedding might be fun. Dressing up, inviting people, entertaining well-wishers, merriment, making speeches…”

The dragon seemed thoughtful. “Speeches, you say? Dressing up? Would we both wear-” She suddenly shook her head. “Never mind! The point is, this is a terrible boon to ask. That’s all deeply-” The dragon did something of a rumble — a dragon ‘harrumph.’ “It’s deeply personal, to be decided long in the future between intimate partners, if ever.”

“Oh. I see. So you’re saying I’m going too far, too quickly, for you?”

“Yes, exactly.”

“Very well. Can I use my boon… to court you?”

The dragon facepalmed. “You don’t need it for- bah! Just ask for the alliance, you silly erudite. Ask for my aid, and the aid of all those lands we traveled together, that we tied ourselves to in influence, and built already the foundations of an alliance via.”

“Wait a second — did you intend that the whole time?”

“Perhaps. For my own ends — whether I expected your success or not. The question is, do you honestly think you don’t require my boon to obtain my proper help?” There was certainly a warning in that question.

“Point taken, Othenia. Very well. The boon I ask of you is to lend your aid in the coming struggles and the war that will arrive here, if these lands do not rise to meet it. I ask for an alliance on behalf of Yarune, with their king and people, and all those who would oppose the Pale Prince and the menace that is the growing nation of Gerenna.”

The dragon nodded sagely. “Granted, Ashawein of the Load Bearing Clouds. And I suppose I’ll let you continue to humor yourself in the company of Othenia, being as taken with the form as you are.” She shapeshifted down, becoming the tall draconic beauty again. With that, she began walking away — shashaying, more like. “You will come with me, now.”

Ashawein followed. “Your house, your rules, after all. Where are we going?”

“An appropriately accommodating bedchamber. I’d say it's overdue.”

“Oh.” Ashawein, slow to understand the implications, did so a moment later and subsequently missed a step. “O-oh!”
