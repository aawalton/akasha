Risha stepped into the council of Grandmas, the world taking shape into a large familiar tent, two chain swords hung from their displays, their spirits just beginning to gain sentience.

She looked around the tent for a moment before stepping out. A path of broken blades had been completely taken over by vines and purple flowers, leading to a portal surrounded by the stone statues of the races she’d accepted as her children.

Risha walked up to the portal, and gently touched its edges, her gaze distant.

Then she stepped through. The world twisted once again as she stepped onto the snow of a tall mountain. A cave was ahead, faint light coming from it.

Risha walked to the cave, her boots leaving prints in the snow that were quickly covered by the wind. The entrance to the cave brought warmth, revealing several pools steaming into the air.

A woman sat in the pools, jewels covering her hair and hanging from her ears. Empress Shyanne opened her eyes, meeting Risha’s. The faintest smile covered her face before disappearing.

“The others are running late. Join me, Risha.” The Empress’s words allowed no argument.

Risha took her furs off, and stepped into the hot waters, enjoying the comfort of Iris’s home. She let out a sigh of relief as the hot waters relaxed her body.

“How is Soul doing in your home?”

Risha stretched her arms and leaned back, letting her hair fall into the water, “Risha hasn’t spoken to the saint since he came. She’s been busy.”

“Of course, dear. Please do take care of him. Fending off Raphael has given him a rather large ego. A dangerous thing for a human.”

Risha thought back to her escape through the mountains. It felt like so long ago. “He was impressive.”

“I’m glad to hear you say so. And the other humans?”

Risha paused, and opened her eyes from their half-lidded state. She sat up slowly, meeting the Human Mother’s eyes. “One was killed by Fae.”

Empress Shyanne’s look sharpened, “Who?”

Risha shook her head, “I don’t know their name. They died protecting an Elf Tender.”

“Lalaila sent someone then? Are they still alive?”

Risha nodded, “I got there in time.”

“Good luck, young one. As your people grow, the battles become daggers in the dark, and poison in your drink. I will attempt to speak with the Fae Mother, but she can be… troublesome.”

Risha thought back to the small creature with wings she’d met during the last council meeting. She did seem… odd.

Another woman slipped into the waters next to Risha, her dark skin reflecting the low light in a way that caught Risha’s gaze. She hadn’t heard the woman arrive.

Grand Mother Maya, the Dark Elf, smiled at Risha as she spread her arms out and sighed loudly. “It’s been too long, Iris. You need to invite me over more.”

“I’ve been busy.”

Risha looked up, finding the blue skinned woman slowly disrobing as she joined them in the hot water.

“There’s more news?” Maya asked.

“They test the skies, and the waters. Nothing big yet, but with every movement they gain more knowledge and we learn nothing.”

“You haven’t gone looking?”

Iris visibly relaxed as she sunk into the waters. “I don’t dare fly past Hwahwei’s borders.”

The words have a noticeable affect on the gathered Mothers, a tenseness that the hot waters does little to rid them of.

“Is it just one force? Or many?” Empress Shyanne’s voice brought the attention of the others to her.

“Only one tests us. Hwahwei has seen more, but they keep to themselves.”

“That’s good. I will send one of my ships with the Water Saint. She can support you there.”

Iris nodded her head in thanks, then looked at Risha, “And what has our newest Mother been up to?”

Risha smiled wide, “I’ve taken an apprentice. Well, three. And I’m travelling to Freerala.”

Maya perked up, “Oh? You’re finally getting to see it? I’ll have to make a visit. What’s bringing you to the Free City?”

Risha opened, then closed her mouth. “I like exploring.”

Maya raised an eyebrow, and Iris hid a smile. Shyanne gave a small snort.

“Is that so, little one?” Maya hummed, “I’d be careful. The Dwarven King of the city answers to no one, except maybe the Dwarf Mother, but she stays deep in the labyrinth hunting treasures of the first age. And… my children tell me that something strange is going on in that city.”

“What do you mean?”

Maya shrugged, “Nothing specific. I would speak with the Delver’s Guild, they might know more.”

“Delver’s Guild?” Risha tilted her head.

Maya nodded, “They’re the first to know when something comes from the deep.”

Risha smiled in excitement. The idea of exploring the ancient cities of the labyrinth appealed to her sense of adventure, although, she didn’t think she could stay away from the forests of the surface for too long. Still, the stories Siyah had told her made her want to discover like the Delvers.

“And my children, how do they treat you?” Iris’s voice brought Risha from the thoughts.

“You should be proud of Elderoa. She challenges Risha at every turn.”

Iris laughed, then closed her eyes as she sunk into the water, “That is good to hear. Hers will be the first clutch to join the world in-” Iris paused, her eyes opening with a sad look, “a long time.” “The others don’t have to hide.” Shyanne spoke up, meeting the Dragon’s look with a challenge.

“It is their wish, not mine. The oldest are tired, and the youngest follow their elders.” Iris sighed, her gaze looking into the distance, “And we are so few now.”

The conversation continued as the Mothers spoke about plans for the future, and the threats across the oceans.

Risha found herself sinking further and further into the water, just enjoying the hotsprings as her mind wandered.

Risha left the mountains, and stepped into the void of stars that was the Spirit Realm.

Nyx jumped from her, walking beside her as a small black cat. Igni followed in the form of a wolf covered in flames, and the three of them made their way to a star that Risha was deeply familiar with now.

Risha entered the star named “Elly”, and stepped onto a hill of flowers, a small path leading up to a wooden cabin on the top of the hill.

Risha walked up to the cabin, knocked softly, then entered to find a familiar Ancient Shaman laying on the bed, staring at the ceiling.

He turned to her with a half-smile, “Hey lil sis. Finally have time for more lessons?”

“Being an Empress is hard sometimes.”

His smile spread, and he rolled off the bed, a simple staff appearing in his hand. He looked over Igni and Nyx, his gaze resting on Nyx for a moment before returning to Risha.

“I think it’s time to bond a star.” He stepped past Risha, heading towards the field of flowers.

“Bond a Star?” Risha asked, looking at Nyx who shrugged her cat-shoulders.

The Ancient Shaman smiled, then held out his hand. Risha took it without hesitation.

The world shifted as they left “Elly” the star.

Once again, they appeared in the void of stars, some shining brighter than others. Some hiding themselves.

A path of greenery was under them, leading the footsteps of the Ancient Shaman as he walked through the void. “You have your home in the council, but that is a place only you and the other Mothers can stay.”

Risha hurried to catch up with the walking goblin. “You entered the Council.”

“Yes, well, I’m an exception.” He smiled with pride, “You invited me in, and any of the mothers could’ve banished me with a thought. Even then, I could only stay for so long.” The Goblin stopped, looking at Risha, “I’d like to give you a place that is uniquely yours, where any you choose in the Spirit Realm are welcome. Binding a star comes with many benefits.”

“I thought you said the stars are paths, doorways, not the realms themselves.”

The older Goblin nodded, “Yes, but most realms only have one entrance, and when you control the entrance, you control the space itself.”

“Could I change where they lead?”

The Shaman laughed, “Oh, most definitely. And I’ll give you a hint. They’re not limited to the spiritual realm.”

Risha stopped, and the Ancient Shaman paused, leaning on his staff as he watched her brain process that information. “So I could use them to cross my Empire.”

“And so much more-”

“That’s not safe.” Nyx spoke, a soft growl entering her voice. “Bringing the physical into the Spirit planes.”

The Ancient Shaman focused on the black cat, his eyes watching her with a rare seriousness, “Nothing is safe, Spirit Of Void Night. That is why I will teach her the careful paths, not the reckless ones. And you can hide her from the peering eyes of the Eldritch, can you not, Anyxala?”

“Don’t speak that name.” Nyx growled, her form shifting to become larger, a panther in height. “Nothing is certain.”

The Ancient Shaman smiled, “You fear you’ve grown too weak, don’t you?”

The Spirit didn’t respond.

“Trust in my little sister more, her spirit is more than enough to fuel your power in this small task.”

Nyx’s eyes narrowed. “You are reckless.”

“Far from it.” The goblin continued walking down his path of greenery, and Risha followed him. “Forces are at work in the world, as you well know. Any advantage I can give little Risha is necessary. “

They stopped in front of a small star, hidden within the light of another, larger star.

The Ancient Shaman smiled at Risha, “This star has no name from me. What would you like to call it, Risha?”

Risha looked at the star for a long moment, then nodded, “Gateway.”

The older Goblin shrugged “That works. Now it’s time to bind it. You should know how by now.”

Risha looked at the goblin, then reached to her waist where Crimson, her chainsword rested. She wrapped the chain around her hand, then pulled the sword.

Her spirit flowed through the sword, and runes formed in the air, a contract with the star, one that fed it with her spirit, and brought it under her control. The contract was a long one, filled with the runes that the Ancient Shaman had been teaching her.

The star awoke for the briefest moment, its gaze going over her before agreeing to the contract, then the runes faded.

Risha sensed a connection with the star, telling her all sorts of things about the gateway. It currently led to a small forest filled with nature and small animal spirits.

The Ancient Shaman clapped his hands together. “Perfect! Now we can start on the second part of your education. Growing your own spirit realm. It should work wonders for your new apprentices as well.”

Risha blinked, then looked at the grinning goblin. “How’d you know?”

He puffed out his chest, “Cause I’m the coolest goblin, always looking out for my lil sis!”

Risha smiled, “Second coolest goblin.”

His smile turned into a glare, “You take that back.”

“Never!”

Nyx sighed as the two goblins bickered, then turned her gaze to the depths of the void, where ancient gods and monstrosities wandered the deep, searching for ways to become more than spiritual.
