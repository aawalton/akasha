Chapter 24

Workshop

I’m standing in the doorway to a huge single-room building, easily six Remmis deep, ten long, and a little more than three high. The wall my hand is against is about 0.4 Remmis thick, and is buried a Remmi and a half into the ground. I actually had to go down a set of stairs from ground level to reach this thick, heavy door with wood on one side and steel on the other.

A long, two-Remmi-wide ramp is against one of the long walls, stretching up to a loading area at the far end with a two-by-two-Remmi door, and another man door, in the far wall. The balcony-like arrangement gives a place for loading product and unloading supplies, though another exterior ramp closes the last couple vertical feet before reaching the brick-tiled driveway and parking lot, perfect for supporting heavy loads.

Three huge ceiling fans running off of electrical stones are embedded in the roof, represented on the outside by brass grills, and spin to extract smoky or chemically-toxic air from inside the building, while vents along the floor, connected to pipes that poke up above-ground outside, provide the fresh oxygen to replace it with.

In one corner on my level, stretching across the thirty-foot wall on this side, is a tiled, reinforced area for a forge, a kiln, a series of alchemical vats, and a station that is specialized for … selective refinement of very high-temperature materials. The whole idea being that this will be the hottest, most toxic section of the lab most requiring structural exhale.

Aside from a chain and crank at the top of the ramp, cabinetry and pegboard along the wall opposite me, and guide rails and pulleys bolted into the ceiling, however, the place is otherwise completely empty. Only the stuff built into the building and foundation is here. It’ll be on me to get the rest of the tools that I can get in Furinshao, and to use them to build the ones I can’t get.

I already ordered the big ones as soon as I had an idea from Daelon when the place would be done. First thing to arrive should be the biggest, toughest lathe I could get my hands on. It’s not going to be enough, obviously, but it’ll be able to machine the parts I need to upgrade it into an actual metal lathe with an electric motor. I thought about buying a press too, but those are actually kind of lacking. It’ll be easier just to buy massive slabs of iron and assemble it myself. Cheaper, too. Iron’s pretty common, and if you don’t want it as steel, you can pretty much get someone to sell you whatever mass of it you want.

Stockpiles of specific assorted acids, solvents, and other compounds are also on their way, though I understand they’ll be coming separately on a special wagon designed for handling them. Ordering such things has really been an experience in just how sophisticated this world is for not having the combustion engine.

Oh, that’s another thing I’m going to have to build. Not a combustion engine specifically, but I am going to need so many motors of different sizes to drive everything. There’s probably magic for that kind of effect used at the equivalent of industrial scale, but I know motors, and I understand torque. I speak in rotations per minute and punctuate with gears. I’ll never begrudge the locals their way of doing things if it works for them, but I’ll put my faith in oil and graphite.

I finally have my own workshop, and even though it’s still empty, my mind is flooded with so many ideas, and my trigger finger’s itching.

… Have I given up on telling myself I’m not a crafter? Nah, I’m not. I’m an engineer. There’s just no one else qualified to do the work I need done.

My attention turns from my imagination to the far end of the building when the other man door opens and Ayre walks out onto the upper deck. His head is turning every which way as he makes his wide-eyed way over to the balustrade and leans against the railing.

“Remmi?” he calls into the space as if in doubt.

He must have been coming from the range if that was the first door he’d come across. If he’d been coming from the house, he’d have already found me here. Though I suppose, at the moment, there’s not really a lot of reason he would have been coming from that direction.

I step further through the door frame and wave my arm widely to get his attention. “I’m down here!”

His gaze snaps to my movement, and he turns and makes his way down the ramp at a brisk walk. It’s impossible not to notice how he does it with legs together, his knees and hips shifting as his forearms are raised as if to keep his nails away from anything too manly for them to bear. However, it seems entirely unconscious, and he’s not actually looking scornfully upon the environment. It’s just poise. More than even I have. I’m reminded, not for the first time, how much more of a proper lady he is than I am.

Once he’s a lot closer, I turn back toward the open space, though I keep my eyes on him. “Well?” I ask. “What do you think?

He turns to take it in again, as well, once he reaches me. “It’s big,” he summarizes.

I nod, though I do so with my bottom lip thrust out in disagreement. “It’s big, and it’s not,” I reply. “It’ll get a lot smaller as I start filling it up, and as hard as it is to imagine, this is only about twice the size the cabin was. Even the ground floor of the new house is going to be bigger than this.”

“That’s really big, Remmi,” he frowns back at my lack of scale, but sighs. “But I guess you’re right about it filling up. You’ve got as much work space in here as the wheelwright and blacksmith combined, and I doubt that’s all you’re going to be doing. Actually, with that frame of reference, are you sure it’s enough?”

I grin at his sudden shift in perspective as I prop my hands on my hips. “Eh, for them, a lot of that is redundancy. If they shared the same space, you’d probably find that half of each of their spaces was for the same stuff, so in a single workshop, that’s half the total space you could put to something else.”

“Which, in turn,” he concludes, “would only need a fraction of the space it would require in its own workshop because the common operations were all in the shared area.”

My smile cranks up to gigawatt, and I give him a thumbs up. “Now you’re getting it!”

He turns his attention upward, though, sheltering his eyes with a delicate hand as he squints against the light. “Noble lighting sure is bright, though.”

He’s referring to the bright, white running lights set into the ceiling and walls. It looks like industrial LED lighting, and, in a sense, that’s not far off. Instead of electric lights, the rectangular panels actually conceal sheets of light stones stimulated by mana in a marked parallel to electric lights. One pulse at the panel equivalent to a switch, and they come to life, flooding the room with enough illumination that, were it the middle of the night outside, the inside would not be discernible from midday.

On the other hand, the whole reason he called it noble lighting is because only the very rich have it outside of necessary use. I can afford it, but I still fall into the necessary use clause. Only nobles or merchants so wealthy they might as well be nobles can afford that quantity of specialized crystals for mere domestic utility. Modern Earthlings underestimate just how much light candles and sconces can give off, and so, out of sheer convenience and cost effectiveness, most people in this world just use those.

I follow his gaze up to them. “Yeah, but they’re terribly inefficient. They could achieve the same lumens with only a handful of electric stones instead of thousands of light stones if they used filament bulbs or even arc lamps. Arc lamps are a pain in the buns, but you can’t argue with the output for something so simple. I’ll probably work out some bulbs or fluorescent tubes to replace them with and, I dunno, donate all the spared light stones to the bath house or something. I’ve definitely got to do that before the house is finished, or at least before we get too far into decorating it. But I still need light in the meantime, so there they are.”

I look over to see his big, green eyes staring at me like he’s trying to figure out if I’ve suddenly shifted into a non-Euclidean shape. “... What?”

With one hand, he motions back up to the lights. “Remmi, you’re deriding the pinnacle of home magical engineering like it personally offended you.”

I scoff and wave him off with a hand. “That’s going to be a common thing in this particular room, Ayre. You’ll be better off if you just decide to get used to it now. We’re not playing for good enough anymore.”

That just makes his frown deepen. “And that doesn’t cross you as too arrogant?”

But I shake my head. “Ayre, I have the utmost respect for Tolestean craftsmen. My world uses mass production that prints out a thousand identical pieces per minute. The base quality is very high, but it’s because of material science and mechanical precision, not because somebody put their heart into it. Our products are good, but they’re all the same.”

I motion with my hand as I shift the comparison to the empire. “Craftsmen here, they can’t match our speed, and, frankly, they can’t match our materials, our quality, or our prices either. That’s not arrogance. It’s just a mathematical fact. But they have something we’ve forgotten how to achieve, and that’s beauty. Imperial work is beautiful. It has soul. I absolutely adore it. But I won’t, I can’t, put my friends’ lives in its hands if I can get something better at the job, even if it’s uglier.”

I step forward, holding my arms wide toward the interior of the workshop. “Here, Ayre, this is my factory for the ugliness that will deliver us victory!”

“... A rousing speech,” he snarks in sarcasm. “Truly, you missed your calling in politics. Or mercantile.”

It makes me smirk as I turn back to him with my arms crossed. “Does that mean I can count on your brand loyalty?”

His frown gets a little more speculative as he chews on his lip and shifts his weight to one leg with the hip popped out, his own elbows cradled in his hands. “If it really is better, I suppose too much is on the line for us not to use it, but does it have to be ugly?”

I swoop over and reach up to pat his head, a gesture that earns me a glare, but I do it anyway. “Don’t worry! For you, Ayre, I’ll make sure the ugliness is purely metaphorical! I’ll put you in silks that can repel swords and stylized plates that can tank dragonfire! And a bow that lets you wield the lightning bolts of the heavens themselves!"

He steps away from my hand. “I don’t think Yorin would like your talk of putting the power of the heavens in mortal hands.”

My grin leaps back. “Aww, but that’s just what cultivation does, isn’t it?”

“... Point.” He turns back to the room. “So what are you making first?”

“Tools, mostly.”

“Right,” he recalls one of the earliest conversations we had. “The tools to make the tools to make the tools.”

“Right!” I hold up an index finger dramatically. “But then I’m making a bigger gun!”
