“Speaking of which, I wonder if my research on the Dreamscape can help me figure out how to assist KuroUsagi and his friends on Earth,” Victor mused quietly to himself.

He walked through the marble corridors of the Headmaster’s Building and ascended the stairs to the top floor. When Victor reached the end of the corridor, he stopped before a white oak door, the door to Lillie’s room, and knocked lightly. “Lillie, it’s me.”

A brief pause followed, then the door opened a crack to reveal a young girl’s face. Her mismatched eyes — one as blue as the summer sky, the other a deep green — stared at him. “Mentor? You’re back already?” she asked, tilting her head and widening the door.

“I am,” Victor replied, stepping inside. “I need your help with something urgent. Let’s talk in the classroom.”

Lillie nodded and followed Victor to the private classroom. Once the door closed behind them, Victor wasted no time and explained everything in detail: the deadly curse that had struck him, its terrifying nature, and the faint lead he had discovered about how to undo it. He hid nothing from her.

“Mentor has been cursed…? Oh no…” Lillie’s eyes widened in alarm as she clutched her cheeks, her voice trembling. “Are you okay? Are you hurt anywhere? Should I sing you a pain-go-away song?”

Victor couldn’t help but chuckle softly at her innocent concern. He reached out and patted her head with a gentle expression. “Don’t worry, I’m not going to die anytime soon,” he said reassuringly. “But I’ll need your help, Lillie. You’re the only one who might be able to do it.”

Her gaze lifted to him, filled with confusion and determination. “Lillie? How can I help?”

“The curse doesn’t belong to this world,” Victor explained. “It was born from a being that exists beyond the material plane — something even Magi can’t normally reach. The only force that might stand against it is dreamforce. And that means you, Lillie.”

Lillie blinked in surprise. “Dreamforce… like when Lillie made the portal to the Dreamscape before?”

“That’s right,” Victor said with a faint nod. “You once bent reality itself just by imagining it. You manifested a force Magi can’t even comprehend. If you can harness that power deliberately, it might counter the curse’s influence.”

Of course, it wouldn’t be nearly as simple as he made it sound. In truth, countless experiments would be required to find a stable way to use dreamforce against the curse, or if it even worked in the first place. But there was no harm in offering her some encouragement.

Lillie hesitated, then straightened with a firm expression. “Then I will help! If it’s to save Mentor Victor, I’ll do anything.”

Victor smiled faintly, touched by her sincerity. “Good. We’ll start by seeing if dreamforce reacts to the curse’s presence. But we’ll proceed carefully. Even for you, this could be dangerous.”

The girl nodded determinedly. “Lillie isn’t afraid.”

“Then let’s begin.”

Actually, one of Victor’s sigils contained a trace of dreamforce, but to use it now, he would need to unseal his power. And if that went wrong… it could lead to disastrous consequences — the curse might react violently, growing stronger and devouring him outright.

In any case, there was no point in alarming Lillie with this information. This experiment would rely primarily on her power, not his.

“All right.” Victor raised his hand, and a faint projection of the curse appeared above his palm. The black curse mark writhed eerily as though alive, its tendrils writhing like serpents in slow motion. “We’ll begin here. Let’s see how your dreamforce reacts to this.”

Lillie took a deep breath and nodded. “Okay. Lillie’s ready.”

Closing her eyes, she placed both hands over his, a gentle light blooming from her palms. Wisps of silvery radiance spilled into the air, rippling softly like moonlit water. The dreamforce brushed against the cursed projection, and Victor felt the curse weaken. The tendrils shuddered and recoiled, hissing like smoke meeting sunlight. His chest lightened, the ache in his core momentarily fading.

“It’s working,” he murmured, astonished. “The dreamforce suppresses it…”

But before he could celebrate, the reaction began to weaken. The silvery luminescence scattered as his body instinctively repelled the foreign energy. His internal mana paths constricted, rejecting the intrusion outright, allowing the curse to reassert itself and return to its prior state.

Victor clenched his teeth. “So that’s how it is… Dreamforce can suppress it, but because it’s external, my body won’t allow it in.”

He looked at his hand where the curse mark was still pulsing faintly. “That means… the only true way to undo the curse is to wield dreamforce myself.”

Of course, he couldn’t rely on his sigil. Even if he could harness Dreamforce, countless trials and errors would be required to master it. Perhaps the best way to achieve that was by entering the Dreamscape and experimenting there firsthand, but that place was far too dangerous. The mere thought of returning made him shudder.

Lillie slowly opened her eyes and asked, “Mentor, did it work?”

“It did… a little,” Victor replied. “At least, I now know what needs to be done, and it’s all thanks to you.” He paused, then added with a faint smile, “Mind if we experiment a bit more?”

“Of course!” Lillie replied brightly.

Countless trials and errors were conducted for a few days straight as Victor sought to grasp the true nature of dreamforce. Although he had gained some insight from his time trapped within the Dreamscape, he hadn’t had the luxury to study it deeply back then. And the few instances he had used dreamforce spells were merely through copying the spell model that he had generated through the many computers on Earth beforehand. In essence, his use of dreamforce was like a child attempting to wield a sword. Clumsy and terribly inefficient.

Still, he had made considerable progress. Despite the need to enter the Dreamscape for further experimentation remained, at least he had pinpointed what needed to be done. Since he couldn’t yet wield dreamforce with precision — and mastering it would take far too long — he realized that he needed to find something within the Dreamscape capable of producing and storing Dreamscape energy in large amounts.

As long as he had something like that, he was confident he could undo the curse. The only question that remained was what this “thing” could possibly be.

“Hmm… I think the piano in Lillie’s castle back in the Dreamscape might work… But that Dreamscape realm was already destroyed,” he mused.

Just as Victor and Lillie were preparing to continue the experiment, the door burst open, and a familiar small figure flew inside. “Master!”

Victor turned toward the voice and found Lizbeth hovering in the air. Strangely, her expression was unusually tense. “Liz, what’s up?”

“I didn’t want to disturb you two earlier since you looked so focused on trying to undo the curse,” Lizbeth said, looking a bit guilty. “But something important just came up, and I thought you’d want to know right away.”

Victor arched an eyebrow. “Important, huh? What is it?”

Lizbeth hesitated for half a second before she dropped the news. “Someone has arrived at the academy’s vicinity, claiming to be the envoy from the Arcane Radiance College.”

The words struck like a bell toll. Victor’s expression darkened instantly. “So, they’re here…”

To be able to find the academy’s general location, even though it was protected by a magic concealment barrier, meant the other party was not to be underestimated. He knew this moment would come sooner or later, as the Headmaster’s Journal had warned him about it in advance. Still, he had hoped for a few more days to prepare.

“Mentor?” Lillie looked up at him, her brows knitting with concern. “Is this bad?”

Victor gave her a reassuring smile. “Not bad, exactly. Just… inevitable.” He walked toward the door, his Shadowlink Mark glowing as the Cosmic Rubik suppressed all traces of the curse’s presence within him. He couldn’t look weak in front of the envoy. “The envoy from the Arcane Radiance College is one of the kingdom’s top representatives among the Magus circles. He’s not someone I can afford to underestimate.”

Lizbeth nodded gravely. “He’s currently waiting in the guest building, being watched by the players. Also, his mana fluctuation is quite overwhelming. He must be at least a Nexus Temporal Magus.”

“Mhm.” Victor nodded. “That place wouldn’t send anyone weak for something like this.”

Turning back to Lillie, he placed a gentle hand on her head. “We’ll have to continue this later, Lillie. You did well today.”

“Okay,” she said quietly, though worry flickered in her eyes.

Victor turned toward Lizbeth. “Let’s go. It’s time to meet our guest.”

Lizbeth gave a brisk nod and perched on his shoulder. The calm before the storm had ended, and now, the game of words and power was about to begin.

The Sanctum of Interdimensional Magi.

The name alone had sounded absurd when he’d first read the report. Pretentious. Overreaching. Delusional, even.

Now, standing in the heart of its guest building, the envoy wasn’t so sure.

He had sensed them the moment he crossed the threshold of the academy’s magic barriers — not one, not two, but over a dozen distinct mana signatures at the Elemental Adept level, dispersed strategically across the campus. One of them was even on the verge of breaking through to the Nexus Temporal rank.

For a Magus academy that had supposedly been founded less than a year ago, he almost dismissed it as an illusion, but his senses had never deceived him before. Either someone was weaving a highly sophisticated deception, or this academy was genuinely fostering power at a rate that rivaled the top Magus factions in the kingdom.

Well, according to the report, the Sanctum of Interdimensional Magi had absorbed the Asteriscus Magus family and crushed the coalition led by the Merlin family, capturing numerous Magi in the process. Those captured Magi would explain about half of the signatures he detected. Even so, with a headmaster capable of defeating and killing Alphonse Merlin, the Sanctum was no minor organization. In this region of the kingdom, it had become a power to be reckoned with.

If he could make this Magus force submit and offer tribute to the Arcane Radiance College, then his mission would be considered a success. But he knew this task was anything but simple. Several factions back home opposed any form of alliance or peace with this newly founded academy, for reasons he could only guess at. He had a nagging suspicion that being sent here as an envoy was more misfortune than otherwise. If not for Headmaster Magnar’s personal request, he would have declined the assignment altogether.

The envoy’s frown deepened as he watched through the arched window.

Across the courtyard, groups of acolytes were hard at work — some laying bricks one by one to construct houses, others channeling magic to lift heavy materials into place. On the far side of the academy grounds, dozens more were tending to rows of crops and even common magical plants, while in another section, several acolytes were burning coal, shaping bricks, and working the forges like seasoned laborers.

No respectable Magus academy would ever have its acolytes performing such menial tasks. Those duties were for mortals, not aspiring Magi.

And yet, that wasn’t the strangest part.

All around the campus, other acolytes were engaged in baffling behavior he couldn’t begin to explain. One stood in the middle of a path, staring at empty air before suddenly breaking into an odd dance as if performing for unseen spectators. Another was talking animatedly to no one, offering loud commentary on the most mundane things.

Seriously, what kind of place was this?
