Zephyr, flying to the closest portal…

“What was that?” asked Vyrn, in a tight whisper. He flew closer to me then he should, instead of staying in formation.

I sighed, but didn’t dare glance back at the one we’d left behind. Two, actually, if my guess was correct.

Nyx chuckled. “That was something we shouldn’t touch. Not if we want to leave anytime soon.”

“That poor kit has to hide out ‘till night,” argued Vyrn. “We have time to help them get somewhere safe.”

“I doubt it,” I said, keeping my voice low, while trying to make sure nothing else in the swamp would shoot out at us. The Dread had ruled this area for a long while, but was normally much farther away from the settlement. The beast moving in closer only reassured me that leaving was the right choice. I passed on the territory claim.

“That wasn’t a kit,” added Nyx. “I don’t know what that was, but the limited info I got wouldn’t break. When I tried to force it, the skies themselves whispered it was a bad idea.”

Vyrn slowed down for a split second, then pushed himself for a moment to catch back up to us.

“I don’t understand,” he muttered, completely oblivious. “They healed me.”

“With magic not from our world, and because the little creature liked you,” I explained, trying not to burst his worldview too much. “Your treats must have been really good.”

“But if the little guy belonged with him, then…” His sentence cut off, as he pressed his lips together.

“That’s why we are leaving,” I said, not wanting to get into it any farther. If that had been a devourer, so far beneath my own level, then I didn’t ever want to fight one near my level.

“He isn’t in my backpack anymore, is he,” mumbled Vyrn. His voice dipped low, and for a second I felt bad for him.

Whoever that was, they were fighting over their level by a lot. Not that I could tell for sure. Something had blocked any use of insight on them. Nyx couldn’t get anything either, and that said a lot.

Still, they’d saved the little guy, joined the fight, and healed one of our own, even when our Clan was sworn against them.

It didn’t make sense.

None of it did. Unless Bekoyrs was right, and the Clan history wasn’t the truth. He had been there, after all.

We had to hurry.

“Use everything you have, we must sprint,” I ordered, as my wings buzzed faster. Wind rushed over me, and I left the two of them behind just for a moment, before they caught up to me.

Lenna, in the cavern under the waterfall…

They came from the direction of the portal we’d used. The stars dotted out, one by one, so slowly you might convince yourself it wasn’t actually happening if you didn’t know better.

I stood on a rock, staring up.

Whispers echoed all around me, warning me not to move. Not to draw attention to myself.

The air changed, and everything went silent.

There were no birds, no bugs. Nothing.

Darkness swept over the land, and I didn’t move. Instead, I pulled on my class. Bright silver light shimmered over my skin, and all around me.

Those within the cavern whimpered as pressure rose all around them.

This was why I’d had to go with Alex. This little chance I had to turn the tide, for those who didn’t follow the path of the doomed Clan.

Something inched closer to me. Its body was not visible. Then, it paused.

“Pathfinder…” The whispering caused goosebumps all over my body, and for a moment I thought I would fall.

The silver light around me brightened.

Small specs flew through the space in front of the cavern.

Fireflies.

My legs trembled, but I stood as straight as I could.

Something reached out from the darkness. A long claw, pitch black. It almost glimmered in the faint light.

The fireflies swarmed toward the spot, and the silence shattered as whispers rose up all around me.

The claw inched closer, but I didn’t move. I didn’t wobble. Instead, I straightened my spine.

This was my path. Even if it ended here.

Light pulsed from the fireflies, pushing the claw back.

Notifications pinged inside my head, and the presence retreated.

I wobbled, but someone from behind steadied me. A small hand touched my calf. I straightened, and took a shuddering breath.

Morning.

I only needed to stand until dawn.

#

Alex, in the swamp…

Slipping into the canopy wasn’t hard, and I wanted to put some distance between the battle site and the two of us. Still, each leap aimed directly for a certain mountain in the distance. Before long, it loomed closer than ever before.

It was not connected to this valley, but potentially the next one.

After several minutes of pushing hard, I slowed down. I pulled the core out of my inventory, and tossed it into my mouth.

At first it stayed crunchy, then it softened like cooked greens, and that flavor took over my mouth. I swallowed as quickly as I could in disgust.

[You have devoured a level 282, Dread, Primeval and gained insight into knowledge.]

Insight into what?

First another rush of energy hit me, like when I inhaled from the bite. My entire body shook.

Knowledge slammed into my brain so fast that I had to reach out to hold myself steady. Images and pathways through the swamps, my map filled out as images formed of safe areas, plus some not so safe areas.

I closed my eyes to help, but that didn’t do anything as the onslaught continued.

Strange nudged my leg, making me snap out of it.

A headache formed behind my eyes, but at least I could tell what was around us again.

“I’m okay. That was a bit much.” As I glanced around, more information leaped out at me. This area had been part of the Dread’s territory, but it ended soon. After that stood wilderness, where Rank 3 and higher creatures hunted.

And where, of course, did the mountain sit? Right in the dang center.

[The Dread’s territory is unclaimed. Do you wish to claim the territory?]

[Error.]

[Error Resolved. This territory will be added to your holdings during the contested state.]

That caused my brain to stutter. I’d only see an error a few times from the system, and one had involved massive amounts of mana and an unknown mutation.

What contested state?

Yet, that went right out of my mind at the next notification.

[Your bounty has been calculated.]

[You have collected a bounty: Gained a Shield Token. A Shield Token lets you create a shield around a battlefield and stop anyone from entering or leaving the area.]

That’s what the Dread had done. It had stopped me from flying away once I’d snagged Strange. Instead, it’d forced me to fight. Now, I could create one of those shields.

At least, I assumed it was only one.

The token appeared in my inventory.

Taking a second, I allocated a bunch of my free stat points to increase my strength and toughness.

Shaking my head, I motioned for Strange to stay close. We needed to get to the mountain as quickly as we could.

He melted into my shadow, then I leaped up into the air, pulling on my stealth skills. This time I flew much higher, high enough that the frost line tickled the top of my head. The energy inside bounded around, and I funneled that into going faster.

Red dots appeared far below me on my radar. Each gave the impression of being way above my level. Either way, I didn’t have time to stop. Memories from Apochas itched at the back of my mind.

Everything was focused on that mountain.

Cold air increased as I traveled. Warnings from the Dread reassured me that this area only contained those level 250 or higher. The creature had hunted in these waters extensively.

That caused me to fly around a cluster of much taller trees, giving them a wide berth.

Something lingered within the branches, and it flickered on my radar. Something I wanted nothing to do with.

I swung even wider around the cluster as something stared at me from within the shadows. Light reflected off several eyes, but they didn’t focus on me. Instead, they searched the canopy within the swamp.

The cold from above increased, forcing me to fly a little lower.

A ripple rushed through the swamp, and everything went quiet.

Then sound burst from every inch of the canopy.

The noise almost caused me to drop out of the air as my head rang. Still, I focused on the dang mountain, which stood closer than ever.

This was the fastest way to get there, but the closer I got, the more I realized it was tall. Taller than anticipated. The peak stood higher than the portal that brought us to this planet had been\.

The canopy underneath me suddenly cut off as the swamp ended. Rocks and boulders took over, and the red dots on my radar ended.

My shoulders relaxed, and I lowered a bit in the air. The burst of energy from the core started to fade, and I beelined for a big boulder. I landed carefully, and my form shifted. I lost my wings, which ended the drain, thankfully.

Then, the climb started.

By the time I passed the frost line, I was over this.

I started leaping, aiming for massive boulders to land on. The jumping meant faster progress, and my armor blocked out the cold air without my wings out.

A ripple crossed over the land, and I glanced over my shoulder, but saw nothing in the far distance.

Nothing.

No stars. No shapes, I could barely make out. Nothing.

I hesitated just for a moment. Then the darkness moved. Not by much, but it moved, like a living, breathing object.

Cold fear crept up my spine, and I leaped even faster.

Far below the frost-line, the swamp stirred. Creatures of all shapes and sizes climbed out of the waters and onto the rocks.

My radar didn’t show them yet, but none of them slowed down. They were just a mass racing up the slope as fast as they could.

Not good.

Memories of approaching the mountain from the air echoed through my head, and I aimed for more of the right side. The secret cave hadn’t been head-on, but off to one side.

I just needed to find the cave and hole-up.

The air burned for a moment as mana flooded across the landscape. It came from the direction of the wave of darkness.

I didn’t dare stop, even as the cold drastically increased. Without my armor, I wouldn’t be able to breathe.

A familiar area loomed ahead, and I slowed down, trying to picture it from above. For a second, my body moved without my input and I jumped once more. This time, I landed behind a boulder next to a strange crack in the wall.

This was it.

But it wasn’t. We weren’t high enough on the mountain to make it to the entrance I’d seen in my dream-memory.

I approached the crack just as a red dot appeared on the edges of my radar. Only once did I glance back, before turning sideways and sliding inside the very small space.

My lungs stopped moving, but I kept going, reminding myself that I didn’t need to breathe much while swimming, and that I’d be fine.

Totally fine.

Not freaking out completely.

Strange appeared near my feet and started down the crack without a care in the world. The calm radiating off of him helped.

Then the crack widened into a narrow tunnel, and my lungs unfroze.

Air.

Several shuddering breaths later, I continued down the narrow tunnel, which aimed upward. My calves ached a little at the incline, but I didn’t stop. Somehow, the air also grew a little warmer.

Realization dawned on me. This was the direction the hatchlings would take when they were ready to leave the nest.

Moss grew in several locations, and small bunches of tiny flies hung about those areas.

Eventually, I reached an opening, but cobwebs covered it. With a shiver, I knocked them down and stepped inside.

My heart lurched, dragging me closer to the back corner where the ceiling was lower.

Rocks were bunched up along with the moss. With my dark vision, three small eggs came into view, but I had no idea how to tell if they were okay.

Two smaller objects started to glow near the eggs. A small pulse of mana washed over me from them.

[You have entered a legacy territory. This territory has been added to your holdings.]

Red dots appeared on my map that now stretched outside of the cavern.

Strange did a loop around the area, searching for any other entrances, then he stood next to the entrance that Apochas had used in my memories. The shimmering seal remained in place, but didn’t glow as much as in my memory.

Then, Strange stepped back, growling in a low voice.

A crack appeared in the center of the seal.
