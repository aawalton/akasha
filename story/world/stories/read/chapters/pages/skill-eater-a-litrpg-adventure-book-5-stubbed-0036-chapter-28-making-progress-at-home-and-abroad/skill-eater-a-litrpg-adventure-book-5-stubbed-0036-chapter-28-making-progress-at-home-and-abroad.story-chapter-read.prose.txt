The next two days passed in a flurry of training, detective work, and arena battles.

Sakura’s revelation regarding the second Ancient was deeply unsettling, as the monster could erase its presence to a disturbing degree, even to her mana-sensitive eyes. She’d managed to pick up traces of its energetic signature outside of the tunnels she’d found, but nothing that helped her track the creature’s movements or deduce a pattern behind its behavior, let alone reveal the Ancient’s identity.

The network of passageways it used to dump the bodies was well-hidden. Since the creature hadn’t realized the hunters were aware of its existence, she planned to check the tunnels regularly in the hopes of uncovering evidence that would narrow the scope of her investigation. The passage Edge found while hunting with Rue might contain additional clues, and he intended to explore them after he evolved and equipped Monster Slayer.

On a less ominous note, the other Ancient—the monster that left motes of corruption scattered across the mixing bowl—seemed to have departed World’s End. The remnants of filth it left in its wake were old when the scion first spotted them and had gradually faded away. Trapper and Sakura had better luck analyzing its movements before the trail went cold.

The Ancient had crossed the free city dozens of times. It hadn’t set foot in the Association Plaza, the arena complex, or Lowtown but had spent some time on every terrace, including the platinum ring. That meant whoever it was possessing had to be publicly recognized as stage four, which dramatically reduced their list of suspects.

The monster had entered the Magisters’ citadel on more than one occasion, although that didn’t mean much in and of itself. Oddly enough, they’d sensed remnants of its corruption around sewer grates and drainage systems that were too small for a person to pass through, but the women weren’t sure what to make of that observation.

Unfortunately, the crew was still in a position where they didn’t know who they could trust, which meant revealing the Ancients’ existence and asking for help was off the table for the time being.

As those thoughts passed through Edge’s head, he walked to the arena with his friends at his side. The whole crew had turned out for the final qualification match, which would determine whether he and Mel could enter the Duos’ Clash. Yesterday’s low-rung brawl had been divided into three groups. Edge and Mel hadn’t fought together and won their respective bouts, taking them close to the cutoff. If they placed in the top four during tonight’s match, they would manage to eke out an invite just under the wire.

Tonight’s group of aspiring gladiators was considerably smaller, which meant everyone was fighting in the same brawl. Edge stretched out in his ready room while waiting for the gate to rise. He was brimming over with adrenaline—more determined than ever to enter the Clash after the top prize had been revealed: an Epic skill gem for both winners. Even though he couldn’t use them, the gems would provide a timely boost to the team’s power before his final meeting with King.

Not long after, the gate creaked open; he moved to his starting position, and Krix’s exuberant voice started warming up the crowd.

When the brawl began, Edge and Mel worked their way around opposite sides of the arena floor, systematically dismembering the competition and securing their invitation before coming together in the center of the ring. By this point, they had developed reputations as promising up-and-comers, and they were ready to go head-to-head for the first time in their gladiatorial careers.

Edge was keeping some skills in reserve for the exhibition, but that didn’t dampen his enthusiasm. He was excited to get a chance to go all out against Melenia, in a situation where there was absolutely no possibility of maiming or killing her… or the other way around, for that matter, as there was a fair chance that she would land a lethal blow before he broke through her guard.

In short, there was nothing holding him back as he met his lover in mortal combat in front of a roaring crowd. Some couples might have hesitated, but their relationship had always been physical in more ways than one. Some of his fondest memories involved Mel beating the shit out of him while teaching him to box, and even in the bedroom, she liked it rough.

Any injuries would be erased by Rejuvenation’s aura mode, and he could tell she would be disappointed if he didn’t give this duel his all. By now, the crowd knew they were an item, and they went apeshit when they realized what was happening.

Melenia and Edge approached the center of the ring, waving to their spectators to play up the big moment. Accompanied by a soundtrack of screaming fans, they bowed to each other and flourished their mastercrafted weapons.

She gestured for him to make the first move, and he was happy to oblige. He came charging straight in, firing a volley of Vacuum Blasts along the way. Mel blocked the explosive wedges with Extinction’s blade—boots sliding back beneath the impacts without breaking her stance.

She returned the favor with a Force Cleave he barely dodged, then Swapped behind him and let loose with a wicked slash that would have ended the fight if it wasn’t for All-Seeing Gaze. He managed to lean out of the way with millimeters to spare… only to have the steel toe of her boot catch him in the solar plexus.

Edge staggered back, fighting for breath as Mel executed a seamless combination of strikes that drove the crowd into a frenzy. Every time he brought Opulence whipping around to counter, she Swapped out of the way. She caught him off-guard by blocking the next Quadra Slash instead, catching the shaft with the beard of her axe and yanking the glaive out of his hands. Her left fist came crashing into his chin hard enough to rattle his brain… or it would have, if he hadn’t Hardened his head inside and out half a heartbeat before the blow landed.

He felt her knuckles fracture under the impact, but Mel only grinned. She shoved him back hard enough to send him staggering, then followed up with a blitzkrieg of Force Cleaves. He deflected some with Opulence and dodged the rest, cackling like a maniac as she methodically erased his options.

When she cut off his retreat with Wall of Force, he knew she was about to teleport behind him to deliver the final blow, having spotted the stone with Gaze several seconds prior.

The instant he sensed her reactor ignite, Edge let loose with a Disruption surge, causing Mel’s eyes to widen as her magic failed to coalesce. During that moment of disorientation, he worked his glaive his glaive inside her guard, blade pressed against her throat.

She growled in defeat, let Extinction fall from her hands, and said, “I yield.”

Then, in front of thousands of eyes, she pushed his weapon down and pulled him in for a passionate kiss that made the audience explode into cheers, concluding a tale of battlefield romance that would spread like wildfire throughout the free city.

“Thanks to the handful of life cores the manufactory has produced thus far,” Dialla read Doc’s report aloud, “our casualty rate has fallen by 75% over the last six weeks. Their bearers can heal anything short of missing limbs and destroyed organs, dramatically reducing our rate of debilitating injuries as well as fatalities.

“When their wielders reach stage two, their ultimate abilities should be strong enough to erase almost any wound. If we can source the requisite resources to produce a few dozen more, we can include a life core with every patrol in the Ivory Plains and hunting trip into the bordering biomes, taking our losses close to zero outside of emergency situations.”

The mayor shared a smile with Emily and Tessa, who were seated around the table in her office. She was about to change topics when Earl darted through the door without bothering to knock, followed by Able, Ander, and Melchior. Thendel and Gregory were hot on their heels, which meant that whatever was going on, it was serious enough to send some of the most important people in the settlement running like their asses were ablaze.

Dialla rose to her feet as Tessa reached for her blade, assuming the settlement was under attack and entering crisis mode. “What’s happening, and what do we need to do to keep our people safe?” She let out a shuddering sigh of relief when she heard Earl’s reply.

“Relax, boss. It’s good news for a change. There is a heavily armed caravan approaching the north gate. They are laden with trade goods carried in the most badass wagons I’ve ever seen—magitech devices that let them hover instead of roll. They’re making no attempt to conceal their approach and don’t seem to have hostile intentions.”

“By the looks of them,” Able added, “I’d say our expedition managed to reach World’s End, make some friends, and put those funds you gave Edge to good use. They look dangerous, but they’re professional hunters—not jailbirds, Wardens, or mercenaries. I think we’re about to receive a shipment of high-grade materials and get an update from our friends in the free city.”

Dialla turned to her protégé. “Emily, prepare suitable quarters and start putting together a celebratory feast. I want music, fireworks, and alcohol, followed by a barbecue and an assortment of mana-seed-infused desserts. Tell Jenny we’re going to need some barrels of her stage-two brew and a crate of stage three, in case these heavy hitters wind up being heavy drinkers too. What would be the best blend of caution, trust, and hospitality?”

Earl wisely kept his mouth shut, recognizing the mayor’s question as rhetorical. Able had opened his mouth to speak, but she was heading for the door before the words left his lips.

“I want the two of you to come with me to the north gate. Send one of the keepers ahead and tell them to open it wide. Ander, have one of your people move into the tower beside the turret, but don’t activate it or prime the generator yet. Those precautions should be more than sufficient on the off chance that this situation isn’t what it seems.”

Dialla’s precautions were sensible but ultimately unnecessary. The caravan came to a stop a quarter mile away from the gate. Two hunters continued down the road with smiles on their faces and empty hands in plain sight. That didn’t mean much in a world where spatial storage was a thing, and people wielded magic that could level a building, but it was a clear sign that their intentions were good.

“Hello!” The woman stopped at the end of the road and raised a hand in greeting. “My name is Sheela, and this is Apou. We’re the leaders of an expedition chartered by one of your own, a lovely lady who goes by Trapper. We’ve also hunted with Edge on several occasions.”

“That is excellent news,” Dialla replied while taking two steps forward. “I’m glad to hear they reached World’s End. Has their mission gone well?”

“I’ll tell you the details once my people are safe behind your walls, but they’re making names for themselves and send their greetings. A lot of what’s in the wagons is a care package from them, although I brought some goods to barter too.”

“Allow me to introduce Earl and Able, the leader of our peacekeepers and his second in command. They will take your crew to a secure location where you can park your wagons and stable your beasts. There are a lot of people who want to meet you, but I’ll introduce them once you’re settled. We’re preparing accommodations and a celebratory feast, as long as you feel up to some good food, cold drinks, and a lot of curious faces.”

“That sounds great.” Sheela grinned. “I’ll grab my team and see you in a few.”

“It was nice to meet you too, Apou. Welcome to Puppet Town.”

When Dialla returned to her office, she pumped her fist and high-fived Emily. The mayor was intensely relieved that Trapper’s crew had made it to the free city and eager to find out what kind of opportunities and resources they’d managed to secure. She was looking forward to the party and getting to know Sheela better, but she intended to accomplish a great deal over the next few hours.

“It’s time to teach you a few tricks regarding the fine art of negotiation, Emily. Before the night is through, I intend to acquire every resource on those wagons, then purchase the wagons themselves and secure passage aboard their ship. They couldn’t have brought so much unless they found a way to use the river.”

Emily nodded along with her mentor’s words, committing the lesson to memory.

“As important as this shipment was, it’s only the beginning. We’ve established a trade route with World’s End and have a limited window to maximize our profits before other parties send caravans of their own. We need to select envoys to send with Sheela’s return voyage, decide what goods we want to ship with them, and devise an investment strategy for the next six months. Change is in the air, and our odds of living through these tumultuous times just dramatically improved.

Lilly sat between Able and Ander, taking shots of Jenny’s special reserve with Widow and Apou as Dialla watched their growing inebriation with amusement and a gleam in her eye that looked downright predatory.

Whatever the mayor was planning, Lillian was glad she wasn’t on the receiving end of the woman’s schemes. The veteran crew was good company. Dialla had asked some of the settlement’s senior hunters to sit at her table to swap stories and welcome the team to town. Ann was perched a few tables over, along with Rita, Sam, Violet, and Riller.

Everywhere she looked, people were having a good time. The food was great, the drinks were better, and lively music filled the air. The dance floor was packed, and people were gathered around the floating wagons displaying the goods Trapper’s crew had collected. The crafters were salivating over the high-grade resources, although most of the residents were more interested in the wagons themselves.

Two were theirs to keep, and there were enough components to build a dozen more. The crafters were confident they could reverse engineer the design, and every hunter was eager to get their hands on the wondrous devices. The floating transports would dramatically increase the efficiency of material-gathering operations conducted in hostile environments and over rough terrain.

A pile of skill gems that couldn’t be purchased in Puppet Town’s Mortium exchange was the icing on the cake. People were passing around the list Edge had compiled so they could submit requests to be included in the next shipment.

On top of everything else, Lilly was certain that Dialla would purchase everything Widow was selling before the night was done—the various goods the woman had brought with her and collected along the way. She listened with one ear as the mayor negotiated with the battle-scarred warrior, who wasn’t nearly as drunk as she seemed at first glance. They were both shrewd and were clearly enjoying the back and forth as they sought to secure the best possible deal.

While Lilly was happy to see everyone having a good time and thrilled to know the rest of Trapper’s crew was alive and well, she was deeply conflicted. Widow had revealed that the situation in the free city was tumultuous and in danger of spiraling out of control. Monster sightings were on the rise, and Lilly was worried about the negotiations with the Coalition, particularly since the faction entered hostilities with the captains of three of the Warden’s twelve divisions.

Ann must have noticed the look on her face. When Lilly went to grab another helping of honey-roasted meat, her sister pulled her aside for a private conversation.

“I know you’re worried about them. I’m worried too. When Widow sails back to World’s End, I think you should go with the delegation Dialla is assembling. You’re on the cusp of evolving to stage three, and the crew could use some backup. It would also be an amazing opportunity to prioritize your own advancement instead of focusing on helping the rest of us catch up.”

“I promised I would keep you safe…”

“I know, and I love you for it, but I need to learn to stand on my own two feet. With all the new cores and hunts in the bordering biomes, Puppet Town is stronger than ever. We can spare you for a few months. You’ll regret it for the rest of your life if our friends die and you weren’t there to help them. My gut is telling me their mission is even more dangerous than Sheela let on.”

Lillian wanted to argue, but she knew Ann was right. The upgraded manufactory and magicyte-rich biomes bordering the Ivory Plains were exactly what the settlement needed to dramatically increase its percentage of cored residents, cycle them up, and arm them with the quality gear they needed to advance at a rapid rate. Several of their top hunters and peacekeepers had already hit stage three, and her absence wouldn’t make much difference in the grand scheme of things.

She mulled the matter over for the rest of the night. By the time she crawled out of bed the next morning, she had decided to join Widow’s crew on their return voyage. Lilly bid good morning to Ann, who was trying on the gear Edge had shipped her. His garax-leather armor was too big and needed to be resized, but her sister was having a great time swinging the naginata around, which was a solid fit for her fighting style.

“Good.” Ann read her expression like the words on a page. “You decided to go.”

Lilly grinned. Her sister had always been quick on the uptake. “I’ll bring you back something nice.”

Her next stop was Dialla’s office. The mayor was always up bright and early, although Emily was conspicuously absent, which wasn’t a surprise given the state of inebriation she’d reached the night before. When Lillian arrived, Dialla and Tessa were in the middle of an argument.

“Emily, Ander, and Bee are heading to World’s End, representing the town’s leadership, the crafters, and the merchant associations, respectively. I’m sending Able and a few keepers too, but I need a professional bodyguard to keep our noncombatants safe during the trip and when they reach the free city.”

Lilly waited for them to finish before knocking, although she knew the women had heard her climbing the stairs. The mayor’s bodyguard was in a position similar to her own. Tessa was reluctant to leave Dialla’s side after nearly losing her in the war with the Claws and again when the kaiju attacked. But now that the Dome was up and running, the mayor was cored, and the settlement was growing stronger by the day, Dialla felt that Earl and the keepers were sufficient to keep her safe.

The door cracked open, and the mayor waved Lilly inside, inviting her to join the conversation. The woman already knew why she was there and seemed to take her presence on the voyage as a given. After Dialla convinced Tessa to join the other envoys, she sent a runner who brought Widow, Apou, and a bleary-eyed Emily into her office.

They eventually reached an agreement about the next shipment of supplies, including a down payment. The caravan would rest for a few days and resupply, then head back with a load of goods that were in high demand in World’s End, which would partially pay for the next caravan. They shook hands to seal the deal, and everyone left to begin their preparations for the return voyage.

I need to stop by the refinery to refill my implants, talk to Ander about gear upgrades, and visit Melchior’s store for some potions and medicinals. After that, I’ll talk to the crew and see if anyone has any messages they want me to convey.

While Lillian was worried about leaving Ann behind, she couldn’t deny that she was excited by the prospect of exploring World’s End and meeting up with Trapper and Edge. She didn’t notice the strange looks her maniacal grin drew as she crossed the settlement.

I bet his core has gone through some delicious changes since the last time I cut him open. This trip should be rewarding in more ways than one.
