Once Samantha managed to get over her mixture of anger and embarrassment from dealing with Opal, the two of them gathered the team and finished going over the plan. Seeing as they had little more to go on beyond Zygrul’s note that their next target was holed up somewhere within the vast zone of monsters, they had their work cut out for them.

“I know I’m the bloodhound, but we are going to be relying on you and your Forest element to try and track Jormon down,” Rulfar told Samantha as he tugged the glass helmet over his head. Somehow, the bottom portion of the glass was almost flexible in a way, allowing it to create a perfect seal against one’s neck. It was more than a little uncomfortable, but Asher supposed a little discomfort was better than dying to some sort of magical plague. “Do you think you can do it?”

“I think so,” Samantha said, poking at the helmet surrounding her own head. Each one only stuck out a few inches from their faces, but it was still weird trying to get used to the bulky addition. Their voices were muffled as well, meaning they couldn’t hear one another very well if they got more than a few dozen feet away. “Natural Guide points me in the direction of specific features I want to find, but the second-tier effect is even more useful. I’m not limited just to natural features anymore. So long as something has been in the forest for long enough that it’s become part of the forest, I can find it. If I think about a log cabin, my skill will point me toward it.”

“Let’s hope the undead abomination is living in a log cabin,” Moxy snorted, tapping at her helmet curiously. “…This is a bit annoying. Are we sure we have to wear these things?”

“Only if you don’t want to die from bleeding out your eyeballs,” Asher drawled. “I don’t even want to begin to think about what sorts of magical plagues Jormon must have created over the last thousand years. There’s still a chance these helmets aren’t enough, for the record. Magic sort of throws everything I know about PPE out the window. For all we know his disease is capable of bypassing glass, or even skin. If that’s the case, we could all very well be walking into our own deaths. Which is why we do this smart.”

Moxy blinked as Asher tossed her his ring of False Death. Holding it up to the light, she frowned. “Not sure what you’re playing at here, Runt, but it’s bad enough I’m wearing this stupid helmet. You know I don’t like artifacts.”

“Maybe. But last I checked, you liked living,” he said, pointing at her hand. “Put the damn ring on. We need to prepare for the worst-case scenario, which means each of us gets infected regardless of what we did to try and prevent it. If I get sick and die, no big deal, I’ll come back. Not to mention I might be able to purge the disease before that happens. If Samantha gets sick, I can use Permanence’s second-tier effect to keep her stable until we figure out a cure. That leaves you three. As an undead, we shouldn’t need to worry about Kroult. And Rulfar has his Hibernate skill, which slows down his body and puts him in a healing state. I’m willing to bet that will keep him alive for quite some time, even if he gets sick. That leaves you.”

“And this ring will keep me alive?” Moxy asked, not looking too pleased as she inspected it.

“It does the opposite really,” Asher chuckled. “False Death will functionally put you in a state of fake death. Your entire body will shut down, which means the sickness ravaging it should be stopped as well. This way, each of us has a method of survival should we get sick.”

“I don’t like it… but fine,” Moxy grunted, slipping the ring onto her finger.

“Good call, Asher,” Rulfar said, nodding his approval as he hefted his axe. “And the moment any of us feel even the slightest bit sick, make sure you speak up. I don’t care if it’s a runny nose or you just feel a little hot, we’re playing a dangerous game here, there’s no room for error. Kroult, are you ready with that spell to bolster our immune systems?”

“Whenever you are,” the lich confirmed. “Though I feel as though we are getting ahead of ourselves. Does Asher not need to send another pegasus courier with a marked package first?”

“Not this time. We’re lucky enough that the edge of the forest is only about a day of flying away from where we took care of The Lost Lady,” Asher explained as he took off his own helmet. “Of course, the forest itself is rather large, so it might take us some time to explore once we get there. We just wanted to do one last check and make sure the helmets fit before Samantha and I set out. Once we arrive, we’ll Recall back and grab the three of you.”

“Works for me. The less I have to wear this thing, the better,” Moxy said, yanking the helmet off her head and tossing it onto a nearby beanbag. “I’m gonna go spar with Christian. He said he’s got a fancy new restaurant he wants to take me to tonight, and I want to work up an appetite for it.”

“I doubt I’ll be able to find anything about Jormon when Zygrul couldn’t, but I’ll at least do a bit more research about the forest we’re heading into,” Rulfar decided. “It’s a famous monster-hunting zone. I imagine I should be able to learn a good bit. Kroult, care to join me?”

“Sure. I will do what I can to help,” Kroult shrugged.

“Enjoy the calm before the storm, everyone,” Asher grinned as he took Samantha’s hand. “This time tomorrow, we’re back on the battlefield.”

With that, the world twisted around them, and the two of them were back at the mouth of the cave system. Asher had shifted his mark back to the entrance after showing Grant back to the village, and he hummed as he glanced over toward the farmer’s home. The village didn’t look like it had burned to the ground in the past few days, which he could only hope was a good sign.

“Do you think they took him back, just like that?” Samantha asked, looking at the quaint village with a painful look on her face. “I couldn’t imagine waking up from a three-year-long nightmare only to then be ostracized by my own people as well.”

“Ulma hadn’t given up on him. Even if the other villagers are scared of his new body, I’m sure his family welcomed him back with open arms,” Asher said, hoping that was true. “Hell, if my dad vanished and then came back as some sort of magical super man when I was a kid, I’d probably be overjoyed.”

With that, Asher hopped onto Samantha’s back, holding on tight as she manifested her wings and rocketed up into the sky. They had a long day of flying ahead of them, but Asher always enjoyed getting the chance to just talk. Their lives were so busy these days with sparring and taking down city-destroying monsters, that they really had to work at making sure the two of them got the occasional date night and time to themselves.

“I’m sorry again about Opal,” Samantha muttered, her voice difficult to make out above the wind whipping past them as they shot across the sky. “I was just trying to save us shards!”

“I know, you’re fine,” Asher grinned, squeezing her and leaning forward to lay a kiss on her cheek. “Thanks for looking out for me. And don’t worry, you’re still more business savvy than I am.”

“I don’t know about that,” Samantha snorted. “I still remember a certain someone offering a merchant more shards than she asked for, in exchange for learning more about me. That was pretty savvy.”

“I was interested in the mysterious and beautiful hunter who rescued me from getting eaten alive in the giant forest!” Asher teased. “What was I supposed to do?”

“I still remember waking up in that stone tower, spotting your worried face looking down at me,” Samantha said quietly, before giggling to herself. “And then learning that you’d poured a potion of Restoration down my throat. I nearly had a heart attack when I found out.”

“To be fair, I didn’t even know how expensive those were at the time,” Asher admitted. “Not like it would have changed what I did.”

“I know it wouldn’t have,” Samantha said, tossing him a warm smile over her shoulder. “And here we are, only a few months later. I didn’t even know greater powers existed back then, and now I’m engaged to one. My parents would probably have a fit if they knew.”

“You don’t think they’d approve of me?” Asher asked, surprised by the news. “I guess I never thought about it.”

“My mom probably would, but my dad was a bit more sensitive,” Samantha said with a sad smile. “Though I think you would have been able to win him over soon enough. What about your parents?”

“They would have been happy for me,” Asher said, grimacing as he thought back to his family for the first time in what felt like forever. “I know I don’t really talk about them much, but that’s because there’s not much to talk about. Our relationship was… fine. We weren’t close. Things weren’t really good or bad. I realized pretty early on that they liked the idea of having a child more than actually having one. I was well taken care of, ate three square meals a day, and always had clothes on my back. But after I turned eighteen and moved out… we didn’t really keep in touch. They’d pick up the phone if I called, and our conversations were pleasant enough. But that was pretty much the extent of our relationship the last few years. It’s one of the reasons I never really missed them when I was whisked away to this world. Honestly, I missed what few friends I’d made over the years more than I did them. It sounds harsh, but it’s the truth.”

“I get it,” Samantha said, struggling to reach behind her and pat him gently on the side as they continued flying. “Not everyone is close to their parents, there’s no shame in that. It’s awful to even admit this out loud, but sometimes… sometimes I wish that I hadn’t been so close to mine. It would have made the pain of losing them easier to bear.”

“We’re not Donvath. Neither of us can change the past,” Asher said. “I’m just happy I found you and Brian so quickly. The two of you… Rosh… Draken… even Donvath and our current team. This world has so many amazing people in it, and I’m glad to be friends with so many of them.”

“Friends, huh? That’s what this is?” Samantha teased. “For some reason I’d gotten the feeling there was a little more to our relationship than that.”

“That doesn’t surprise me. I mean, I’m best friends with Brian, so that’s probably why you got confused.”

“Ah, my mistake. Here, let me jog your memory a tad.”

Asher shouted as Samantha’s wings vanished and she spun around under him, grinning as the two of them began plummeting through the sky. They were high up enough that they had a very long fall to the ground.

“Friends, was it?” she teased, her long hair whipping around them as they shot toward the ground. “You sure that’s your final answer?”

Laughing at the absurd situation, Asher gazed into her dark brown eyes that reminded him of the forest she loved so much before giving her a loving kiss. Breaking away a few seconds later, he looked nervously past her toward the rapidly approaching ground. “Alright, you win the title of best friend! I’ll apologize to Brian later!”

“I’ll take it,” she giggled, giving him one more quick kiss before her emerald wings flared into existence and she managed to turn their freefall into a burst of speed as her wings caught and they rocketed forward. Despite the fact he knew he could have Recalled away at any point, Asher’s heart hammered away within his chest as he let out a shaky sigh of relief.

“You’re lucky I have Eternal Regeneration!” he called above the powerful wind buffeting them in the face as he spotted the corner of the smile on her face. “One of these days you’re going to be the death of me!”
