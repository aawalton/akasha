As much as Vir wished to rush out and help, that was the one thing he could not do. The more eyes that saw him, the more likely someone close to the Fateweavers would see him.

Yet, while Vir’s group waited, they were not idle. Ashani and Maiya moved to aid the prisoners, while Vir sent the wolves—a small army unto themselves—to aid the attackers, and with their arrival, the remaining guards had no hope of containing the situation.

Andros, thankfully, seemed to have gone far enough away not to be alerted, though Vir doubted that state of affairs would last long.

Moving quickly, Vir sank into his shadow, yet instead of aiding in the defense, he rose up behind Tia. The wolves had the situation in hand, and what he was about to broker could mean making a far greater difference than any physical power of his might.

As ever, the Matali princess displayed incredible battle awareness, as she whirled the second he emerged, leveling her spear at his chest.

“Name yourself!” she cried.

At least she didn’t immediately try to kill me, Vir thought wryly. Would his Tia have afforded him even that much?

“My name is of no worth, for you do not know me,” Vir said, raising his hands in a placating gesture. “But know that I bear no love for Andros, and wish to see his head on a spike. I can help you save these people. As a show of good faith, my group will help here. Once the dust settles, it is my hope that we may talk. I believe I have a way to bring down your father.”

Tia’s eyes widened in surprise, and there was none of the hatred his Tia had shown. To think he wasn’t even in disguise…

Looking at that face, however, stirred up a whole medley of memories. Some, like their friendship and the stories they’d shared at the campfire, made him nostalgic. While others…

Despite all that had happened since then, Vir had never fully gotten over her betrayal, and he nearly cringed, half-expecting her to drop her mask and treat him with the same derision as before.

And though his conscious mind knew this Tia had never known him—was an entirely different individual with a different past—the complete lack of disgust still shocked him more than it should have.

“At this point, I’ll take any help I can get,” she said. “If you can contribute, do it. If you can’t, then stay out of our way.”

“Works for me.”

Vir sank back into his shadow and rejoined Maiya and Ashani, who were ushering prisoners and generally trying to contain the chaos.

“Ashani, do what you can without using your powers,” Vir said. “Same for you, Mai. I’ll act independently and coordinate with the wolves. Let’s make sure these people get out unharmed.”

“Understood,” Maiya said. “It seems there’s a warehouse not far from here. We’re heading there.”

“Good,” Vir replied. “Tia’s rebels will handle the rest.”

The three split up, and while Maiya and Ashani concentrated on herding the panicking demons, Vir picked off Balarian guards in secret. He was naturally suited to the task, and the task briefly reminded him of his own rebellion, all those years ago. Of the hopes and the blindingly bright dreams they’d all had.

It was a time and a place he yearned to return to, and for these briefest moments, he felt as though he had.

For although there were still a handful of Balarian guards left, Vir had managed to eliminate a half dozen the rebels hadn’t noticed.

Prana Vision let him spot even the ones hiding in alleys and behind rooftops, waiting to ambush the group. He also took out the handful who’d fled to report to Andros and the others, yet he did not kill a single target he touched.

No, they’d be far more valuable to Tia as hostages. Not that she would need them, should his plan succeed. Still, there was no harm in taking precautions.

They couldn’t be sure, but if Andros in this universe was anything like the man Vir had known—and Vir had no reason to think otherwise—then he was likely covered in Imperium Artifacts. Artifacts that made him nearly invulnerable to most normal attacks, as Vir had learned firsthand.

Only attacks laden with Warrior Chakra could take him down. Which meant Tia would need a demon unless he wanted to repeat what he’d done with Ira.

Somehow, Vir doubted the eyes of those who threaded Fate would be as easily deceived as a mob of the masses.

Vir could only pray that the Tia of this universe didn’t despise demons. If she refused demonic help…

He pushed the thought away. He hadn’t seen a hint of derision in her eyes. There was no reason to assume she’d reject the plan.

Vir followed at the rear of the group until everyone had been ushered into the safe house. He had to admit, the entrance was clever. Instead of a single doorway that could easily be traced by authorities, Tia’s rebels sent the prisoners through several different doors scattered across the block, all of which—Vir assumed—led into the same compound. It was impossible to hide the general area, of course, but at least it would give prying eyes pause.

Not that there were any of those today. Vir had already dealt with every hidden watcher in the vicinity.

He followed the last prisoner to discover that the safe house was, in fact, a cramped, smelly warren of tunnels and humid rooms dimly lit by wall sconces. Through Prana Vision, Vir linked back with Ashani and Maiya before tracking Tia down in a meeting room where she was talking through plans with her advisors.

All of them froze when the group stepped through the doorway.

“Just who are you?” Tia asked, sounding a bit dazed.

“Just a friendly force that happened to be in the area,” Vir said. “Just doing my part.”

“Well… thank you,” Tia said, and the sincerity in her voice caught him off guard. She’d never once treated him like this after… after she learned the truth of what he was. “Honestly, I was expecting to be swarmed by guards the moment we appeared, yet somehow, those reinforcements never showed. I don’t know what you did, but whatever it was… thank you.”

“It was the least we could do,” Vir said. “Mind you, we’ve probably only bought some time, even with the scouts I took out. You’ll find them deposited with your guards nearby, by the way. I suggest locking them in your dungeon as soon as you’re able.”

“That’s… I don’t know what to say,” Tia said. “You’ve done us an incredible deed today.”

Vir waved away her praise. “Don’t mention it. Despite our efforts, with something this big, someone’s going to notice eventually.”

“Of course,” Tia said. “But we should be all right for now. This tunnel network is vast and leads outside the city. We have caravans standing by to move the people to other towns. By the time Andros figures out what happened and tracks this place down, everyone should be long gone.”

“Then I take it we’ve earned your trust?” Maiya asked.

Tia paused, then nodded. “You have. So, let’s talk about this plan you mentioned. You said you have a way to take down the Imperator?”

Vir nearly shook his head. Not because he didn’t have a plan, but because of how trivially easy tasks became, if only one had a bit of power.

To the Vir of Brij, such a thing would’ve been unthinkable—the realm of gods.

Instead, he simply nodded. “We do. But due to… various reasons, we can’t act directly. We’ll come up with a plan you’ll execute.”

“As much as the realms would benefit with Andros out of the picture, I’m afraid it’s not enough,” Tia said. “We need to take Saunak out too if we wish to hope for a better world. That mad demon has almost as much power as the Imperator. Leaving him loose might be even worse than what we have now, and believe me, I do not say this lightly.”

Vir found himself agreeing. A spurned, shackled Saunak was one thing, but Vir could scarcely imagine a world where Saunak had the sort of power Andros wielded. To say nothing of the Vimana.

It was something he’d wondered about before. Now he had his answer, and the truth was as horrifying as it was unsurprising.

Saunak was, if nothing else, predictable in his own, twisted way. A complication, but easily dealt with once Andros was out of the picture. The demon possessed no power of his own, after all, though Vir didn’t doubt he’d clad himself with all manner of Imperium Artifacts.

“Very well,” Vir said at length. “Do you have any demonic warriors in your employ who wield Warrior Chakra?”

Vir almost dreaded the answer.

“We do,” Tia said. “Several, actually.”

Vir blinked. “You’re… surprisingly open-minded about demons.”

Tia pressed her lips together. “So you’ve heard of me, then.”

Vir shrugged. “I won’t deny it.”

“And I won’t deny the person I used to be,” Tia said quietly. “I was a bigot. A blind and angry fool who blamed demons for the death of my parents. But Andros forced me to realize that humans can be far more demonic than any demon. The real monsters aren’t defined by their species. They’re defined by their choices and the power they choose to abuse. Andros is a blight unlike any either realm has ever seen.”

Vir genuinely couldn’t tell if this was Tia or some body double who’d taken her place. Doubly so considering this Tia was scarred by the same past and carried the same prejudices as the one he’d known… and yet she’d worked through them. The shame on her face was real.

Was there hope, then, for his Tia?

It was a fragile hope, but he held onto it with everything he had.

He hadn’t heard a whisper about her in years. No one had. It was as though she’d vanished along with Vason and Haymi.

Still, he didn’t think for a second she was dead. She wasn’t the type to die so easily.

“Now then,” Vir said, setting those thoughts aside. “I suggest we move fast. Strike while the iron is hot.”

“This still needs planning,” Tia protested. “We can’t just charge in. Even if we do know where Andros went off to. Which we don’t.”

Vir raised a hand. “I’m afraid I’ll have to ask you to trust us. We have your back. And if you trust me, then you have to believe me when I say this is possible. It can be done.”

Tia looked like she was about to argue… but then she met Vir’s expression and saw the determination plastered across his face. The pure, unbridled certainty of someone who knew it as a done thing. As though it had already happened.

“…Fine,” she said. “Let’s hear your plan.”

Vir nodded. And for the first time in a very long time, he felt something other than crushing despair. For the first time in a very long time, the faintest flame of hope burned within his chest once again.
