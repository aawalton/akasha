I now had 114 points in Public Speaking. It was my highest skill by two points, just edging out Networking, but I wasn’t in the right place.

My range had expanded massively but it couldn’t cover the whole town. I needed to reach as many people as I could, and the only place I could do that from was the bar.

“Thanks, Normuran!” I called, as I turned. “I’ve got to go!”

“But… the fight’s back that way!” He called.

“I’ve got an idea! I’ll be back, I promise!” I shouted. Even so, Neogon had a point earlier, so I made sure to put a helmet on and tucked my braids in so no one saw a redhaired girl leaving the wall.

“You’d better be!” he called out after me. “A lot of people have put their faith in you girl!”

“I won’t let them down!” I called, with the same false confidence I’d worn every time I’d told anyone in town that our food shortages were only a temporary problem.

I ran. When I realized that running was no longer the best way to get to town, I leaped. I bounded up on top of Burnom’s new concrete house, wondering if the building really would stay as warm in the winter as he claimed.

If this worked, we might just make it far enough into winter to find out.

I tore across the rooftops, a little surprised by how unstable thatch felt under my light feet. Worried that I might break a roof, I leaped back to the ground as soon as I could and dashed towards the bar.

Rather than go inside it, I leapt to the top. The bar was mentally and physically the heart of Pemolar’s Hill. From its roof, I could see the northern front, a bit of the western trenches, and the eastern wall. Lightning continued to crash down, but it looked like someone had picked up Ekran’s bolt ward. For every bolt that slammed against the wall, another was harmlessly caught by someone holding up the ward, bouncing around the tower, before being blasted out into the enemy beyond it. Vovel, maybe?

I couldn’t see beyond the wall from here, but I could now see arrows flying up and over it. Eschal archers were finally counter attacking, and we didn’t have wind barriers to protect us.

Flames were exploding out from the trenches to the north, and bolts filled the sky to the west. It seemed that Freyna had engaged rift monsters, as a small hoard of them was assaulting the western trench. How they’d gotten over there, I had no idea. They were dying in droves, but they were still enough of a distraction that the soldiers on that side couldn’t help the more heavily pressured north and east.

The wind blew hot air and the scent of blood from the west. It was enough to make me gag, but somehow I held on. I didn’t think I’d get many chances at this.

I engaged my Public Speaking skill and sent my voice out. It was enough. Barely.

It reached all the way to each of the fronts, but stopped short of the edges. Those closest to me on the wall I could reach, but there were some near the river or the lake that wouldn’t be able to hear me. The entirety of the western front could hear me and so I ran a bit further back toward the wall to encompass as many people as possible, all the while marveling at how useful the skill was. I could instinctively tell how far my words could travel. Crossing one hundred had truly turned the skill into something amazing.

Not amazing enough, but it would have to do.

“Everyone,” I called. “Don’t lose hope. I have… a plan.”

What did I need? A plane? A fifty caliber sniper? A fucking nuke? None of those would help everyone. There were probably a million better ideas, but the only one that kept coming to mind was what Neogon had said. “If only I had a fortress.” With a fortress, Neogon could turn this around. His flags, and my voice.

Eschal soldiers were coming up the hill to the north. They were dying, but not fast enough. Not hard enough. Neogon had lied to improve morale, but the North was by far the least defended. The woodline was too close, and the height advantage was the least prominent as the hill swiftly lead into other tall hills in that direction.

The woodworkers were up there. Akkiwa and her grandfather, Negirot. Reid’s dad, Korlotom, was there too. His wound had been healed enough for him to fight.

I couldn’t let them down. Who knew how many had already died, because I’d been too slow to think of this. Too stupid to tie the implications together when they’d been standing right in front of me.

I knew now. I just had to believe it worked. Had to believe that everyone… the people of this town I loved so much, would trust me.

“If you can hear me, I need you…  I need you to think of what you need the most right now. A bow, a knife, armor? These fucking Eschal bastards to be gone? I need you to change that.”

No one was close enough for me to hear a response but I was certain they thought I was crazy. They were fighting for their lives and I was telling them to change what they needed? I had to be clearer. Precise instructions.

“You’ve seen what I can do. What I can build. It’s based on what you need most, and right now, I need everyone to need the exact same thing. If you have it in you, please. Believe with me, that the thing you need most, is to protect our town. Protect our town and everyone in it!”

I felt it almost immediately. One of the need bubbles in my mind instantly morphed.

Normuran. I was almost certain. I’d probably never know what that odd need of his had been but for now, he needed what I did.

Millions of iron ingots. The eyes of an infamous witch. Tons of ether dust. The collected sweat of an entire generation of humankind. A literal mountain of clay. An ocean’s worth of water. Diamonds in carat sizes that didn’t exist. The love of a swordsman prince. More wood that there were trees in the entire country. More straw than the entire world could make in a year. Never, in a hundred lifetimes, could I hope to gather a fraction of these requirements.

“Believe that what you need most in the world right now, is for this town to be safe! We need a wall so strong, a tower, so high, a fortress so impenetrable, no one would even think of attacking it!” I screamed.

I didn’t know if they could hear me, but I kept going.

Two need bubbles morphed. Protect the town. Protect Pemolar. The requirements became slightly less. A smaller infinity.

“Please! I know you’re fighting. Fight hard, but the moment you have a breath, I need you to change the thing you need most! Think of us! Of our town, and all the years we’ve spent here! Think of the bar, and the beach! Think of the songs and the stories and the lazy nights catching the breeze in the months of Burn and Blaze!” I screamed, hoping against hope that I wasn’t just shouting into the abyss.

Seven bubbles. Nine. The requirements were lowering.

“Think of Sowing and Harvest. Your fields and your families. Your crops and your craft. Think of everything you love about this town and then join me, and need the same thing I need! Protect it! Fight for it, and I’ll build you something so grand they’ll talk about it for a thousand years! It will…”

I paused. Hesitated. My whisper echoed across three separate battlefields.

“...It will be a thing of legends.”

Nineteen need bubbles. Twenty five. It was working.

I wasn’t addressing everyone here though. Not by a long shot.

“For those of you not born here! Think of your own towns! You’e been fighting for months, always on the backfoot! Always giving up ground. Well I want — No. I demand! — that you hold this ground! Need to protect this place, and it will protect you in turn! Here is where you stop losing. Here is where you stop being pushed back! This is the last line! Fight for it! Protect it! Need it!”

How many people thought I was a raving lunatic? Plenty I was sure, but plenty more had seen me work. My crafts were all over the town now. Tools to help with every day tasks, and mementos to help people remember what made life worth living. Swords, amulets, bows, body armor. Shoes, and dresses, lightning wards, and trowels. I’d even made a damn sieve that let our bartender make stat improving alcohol!

Surely all of that had given me enough credibility that the soldiers who barely knew me might just believe.

“I crafted the Quake Blade that you’re fighting against right now,” I said, bitterly. “It was stolen from one of your captains. If you protect this place with me, we can beat them. We can take back Berkenem’s Sword.”

Sixty! Ninety!

The requirements were plummeting, but no matter what, I would still need supplies. Even if the need became nearly free, I would still need some stock. For that, I had to go back to my house. Stoutgruff’s barn where the village had gathered up supplies so I could build them whatever they needed.

“I made the Pepper Paste you used across the Eastern Woods! I made the axe that built the wall you stand on now! I made the stone that’s blocking lightning from—!” I cut off abruptly as lightning crashed down onto the northern trench, sending dirt and bodies flying. The traitor had apparently grown frustrated with the bolt ward and decided to attack somewhere else.

The morphing need bubbles slowed down, belief wavering, or possibly people who had believed had died, their needs lost from the collective pool along with their lives.

"May the Gods curse that fucking lightning mage!" I shouted, without Public Speaking active. I regretted ever trying to help him. Rage boiled in my veins, but I calmed it. That wouldn’t inspire people, and right now I needed loyalty.

I… I needed love. I needed bonds to craft something worthy.

“I know you probably don’t know me, but I’ve been trying to help you. I joined when the draft came, and only chance brought me back to this town. Back to this place, where I could be here and help you now. Please… trust me, and this ridiculous request. Make protecting this place the thing you need the most. Need it for real. From the gut. From the heart. From the edge of your spear! Don’t need to protect yourself. Make your deepest desire be to protect everyone!”

I asked, and the town gave.

The requirements plummeted. In my mind, the need bubble grew. They didn’t have size, but if they did, this one would be massive. The combination of a hundred people. A hundred twenty five!

It slowed, but I thought it would be enough. The exotic requirements evaporated, leaving only solid, mundane materials. Iron. Wood. Straw. Stone. Heat. Clay. Huge amounts, but lowering with every soldier and civilian that joined me.

Even from my vantage point, I could tell that the Northern lines were fighting desperately. Some enemy soldiers were already leaping into the trenches. They were dying, but as they came in greater and greater numbers, our forces would be overwhelmed.

We were exhausted, hungry, and tired, but Pemolar’s Hill itself had a need, and thanks to everyone, I was going to be able to build it.

“Thank you!” I breathed, tears coming to my eyes. People I knew and loved might already be dead, but at least I might have a chance to save everyone who hadn’t yet been hurt. “Thank you, my friends! Thank you, soldiers of the Rift! This place won’t let you down! I won’t let you down!”

I leaped down off the roof, sprinting desperately for my home.

Just one more step.
