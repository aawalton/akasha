It feels like I should’ve seen at least some of this coming.

Alex was shocked on one level.

On another level, the revelation that Bella was an Awakened, and an incredibly talented one, felt quite natural.

It felt like she’d excelled at everything she’d tried, ever since they were kids. He recalled his parents had placed him, and Bella, in a series of extracurricular activities—and it was really the existence of Bella that’d opened his eyes to the differences between people.

Alex wasn’t surprised when she played D1 soccer for UCLA, wasn’t surprised when she was recruited for a PhD at MIT, assisting a Nobel prize winner in a life science lab. He’d believed, since as far as he could recall, Bella was a universal genius. And it felt like everything he’d seen since reinforced the idea.

It irked him a bit when folks assumed he was jealous of her, or when a well-meaning teacher would say ‘Alex, you have the gifts. You could be like your sister if you applied yourself’—like he couldn’t simply be, well, Alexander Arden, and be happy as he was. It might’ve felt like the natural thing to assume sibling rivalry given the differences in talent.

But the existence of Bella actually had the exact opposite effect on how Alex perceived the world.

He’d simply accepted reality. She wasn’t his rival at all. It felt at times like they existed in separate realities. If anything Alex had always been quite proud of her achievements—and whenever she’d asked his help prepping for various soccer program tryouts, getting a fresh pair of eyes on her training, he was always happy to support her. In the same way she’d always been happy to support him whenever he needed it.

Which was all to say Alex was shocked, if kind of also unsurprised, that his sister was, well, still Isabella Arden.

What did surprise him was the ring on Stella’s index finger.

He was sitting across from her, and his uncle Matthew, at the sushi table, in silence; the fish was untouched.

Alex’s F-rank Class Card sat on the table as well, which he’d conjured when he’d said he was an Outworlder, to show he wasn’t joking. Clearly he hadn’t been an Awakened before, or had the potential.

To which Stella said, “Oh. Um. Okay.”

Awkward silence.

Alex felt a little nauseous; his heart sank. “Yeah,” he said self-consciously.

He didn’t really expect them to leap out of their chairs and embrace him—maybe a part of him had wished something like it, but it was obviously unrealistic. But this silence stretched for a few seconds, and he wasn’t really sure what was going on.

Alex’s parents had died in a car crash when he was 7, killed by a drunk driver.

Certain impressions—their smiles, the way they spoke to him, certain images and memories were still vivid in his mind, but it was just true they hadn’t been a part of his life for twelve years.

As vivid as his most vivid memories of his parents was Alex’s recollection of the first time he’d been dropped off at Stella’s and Matthew’s rented apartment on the Lower East Side, a very dry December morning so many years ago, with Bella, his eyes all red and puffy from crying all night; he’d recalled the streets were piled high with dirty snow. Bella had cried as well. She’d been incredibly rebellious those first few weeks.

At the time Stella was in her early twenties, as was Matthew, and in retrospect they were utterly overwhelmed, and weren’t at all prepared to be parents; Stella was a young professional at the start of her career, working 70-80 hours a week when she'd received the call, and Matthew was in the midst of finals in med school at NYU.

Alex recalled walking into their townhouse that first time, seeing the little welcome party Stella and Matt had prepped, and, after an initial awkward silence, Stella saying something like, “You guys must be really tired. I—we—were just hoping, um—”

And Bella storming right past her upstairs, tearing down the hand-cut ‘BELLA’ sign, with its painstakingly hand-drawn decorations, and slamming the door, leaving Stella almost in tears.

Alex knew the two were under incredible stresses those first few years, and also later, with the health scares they had a few years in. But what was peculiar was he never felt it in his memories.

All he recalled was Stella cheering embarrasingly loudly front row at Bella’s games, and his uncle Matt taking him to Mets games on weekends with his $15 student discount tickets, and patiently helping him with his English homework for hours, his eyes bloodshot with sleep deprivation, before studying until well past midnight for his own surgery exams.

Things weren’t perfect. In those early years the two made plenty of mistakes, and despite their best efforts they were still up to their eyes in work, even as they sacrificed their own social lives and sleep and whatever else. Bella’d still had to grow up quite a bit, quickly, and with the sheer business of his aunt and uncle’s lives, she’d also had to help raise Alex, as well as herself.

But in Alex’s mind, it’d all turned out okay in the end.

Stella seemed startled at Alex’s face.

“Oh—that wasn’t what I—Alex. Listen to me,” she said. “Alex, baby, you’ll be fine.” She seemed to say it almost reflexively.

“Okay,” said Alex, a bit thickly; he cleared his throat.

Another few seconds, and—“I’m sure you have a few questions,” said Matt gently, meeting his eyes. “We might have a few questions too, if that’s okay.”

“Of course,” said Alex, thinking, he’s never looked at me like that.

Of course, he’d be a little guarded. A tiny part of him had anticipated being slapped, if their interpretation of it was his transmigration had killed this other Alex. Being a little careful was natural.

He kind of wanted to say, I’m still me—or at least, he wanted to convince them of it somehow, but the words were useless. And was he even sure he was the same as this alternate Alex?

Was this what I’d imagined? This weird silence, where we’re all kind of just looking at each other?

Alex said, “You’re an Awakened? When did that...?”

“When I was 25, a few years after you and Bella first—well, yes.” Stella’s eyes flickered to his face. She saw him focus on Matt, who was frowning.

“You’re wearing contacts in this world?” Matt isn’t wearing his glasses. For some reason, this felt totally absurd to Alex; he almost laughed.

The guy who’d helped raise him since he was seven paused, hesitating. “Alex, what do you know about how Awakening works?”

At which point Alex noticed Matt was also wearing a steel, gem-embedded band on his index finger.

This time, Alex did laugh.

1% of this world was Awakened.

You’re expecting me to accept Bella, and Stella, and Matt—they’re ALL Awakened?

This is some kind of joke, obviously.

It felt insane to him that Stella and Matt were less incredulous than he was, given he’d set up this meeting to tell them he was an Outworlder.

“Clearly,” he said, giggling a bit, unsure why he was even giggling. “I’m clueless how Awakening works. Please tell me?”

Matt pursed his lips at Alex’s reaction, seeming a bit concerned, but he continued.

“You’ve probably heard about Awakened families, right?” said Matt, still gently, in a tone Alex appreciated. “Or Awakened siblings.”

“Of course.” I’ve seen several. Clarissa and her siblings, the Devereaux’s.

“Have you wondered why that is? And this is just to get to a place of understanding.”

“I guess, genetics?” said Alex, with a kind of half-shrug. It was true he’d never considered it deeply, but after a little consideration, it did feel kind of strange.

“Partially. Awakening potential’s a spectrum, with a tiny fraction of people at the highest end.”

At which point Alex learned that Awakening was a weighted lottery, in which the various Awakened Associations would give their limited Common rarity Awakening Stone stores to the winners. Winners which, right now, were selected from the top 1% of Awakening potentials. Outworlders, with a soul twice the size and sky-high Awakening potentials, tended to get selected quite quickly.

It was theorized that between 1 and 10% of mankind, by Awakening potential, were capable of being Awakened by a Common rarity Awakening Stone; the reason Awakening rates were rising worldwide wasn’t because mankind was randomly increasing in talent, but because more higher-tier rifts provided more Awakening Stones.

By the end of the next decade, Awakening rates could be up to 20%, and there was even speculative evidence that alien societies featured 100% Awakening rates.

“Stella’s Awakening potential was high enough to be selected. Mine wasn’t.”

“But you’re an Awakened,” said Alex, realizing. “You needed a higher-grade stone.”

His uncle gave a weak smile. “Well, it wasn’t my choice.”

“I wasn’t going to let you go the rest of your life like that,” said Stella, glaring at him. She glanced at Alex—“In your world, did he also get hit by that idiot?”

“He did,” said Alex, and he was starting to see how this all came together.

The first two-ish years with Stella and Matt, he could recognize now were quite stressful, if rewarding, for the two of them; but as Alex and Bella grew up a little more, and Stella and Matt were a little deeper into their careers, Stella got cancer—breast cancer, caught early-stage and curable, but Matt had had to sacrifice aspects of his own career during that time to help care for her. It would’ve been—I guess, just around the time she Awakened in this world, maybe a little before? She’d been fine in the end, after a few months of chemo.

A few years after, when Alex was starting high school, Matt had been hit by a car while cycling, which fucked up his back. And Alex’s uncle, for all his qualities, wasn’t an especially built man to begin with; Alex recalled it’d taken a while just to walk again, and he was chronically in pain after, at times crippling pain.

Life really hadn’t been kind to his godparents. But Stella was resilient and bounced back fiercely, and Matt never said a word about it; what Alex admired most about him was his utter refusal to allow pain to break his spirit. It was like he woke up every day resolving to be happy, and to make his loved ones happy, and in Alex’s memories he was.

In this world, Alex learned, Stella acted. Treasures like potions didn’t work on unAwakened, due to the natures of their souls, so her options were, getting a specialized healer that could operate on tissues directly, without touching the soul, or, more simply—Awaken his uncle.

An Uncommon Grade Awakening Stone was only found in two-star and above D-rank rifts, and it cost well Stella over 10 million; his uncle had pleaded for the specialized healer, if she insisted—still expensive, but less so. But Stella wanted to give him more than just a removal of pain; she’d seemed a little guilty as she said it, and Alex got the sense she was remembering what her husband had done for her when she’d needed him.

Such a stone wasn’t even on the market, and she’d purchased the Stone off of one of her clients—a client who liked her, and sold it to her at a special rate; 10 million was the discounted price.

It was revealed that she was a staffmaker, and a rather prestigious one, which was Alex reading between the lines; his uncle volunteered that she had a staff up for auction at Exeter’s this weekend.

Matt, meanwhile, was a Jeweler specializing in pendants.

Alex asked questions, and listened, mostly, and occasionally his godparents would ask about his life in his world. And it seemed like despite the radical divergence of, well, his aunt and uncle Awakening, and also Bella’s Awakening, his life was remarkably similar. The events all kind of lined up—for instance, it wasn’t Bella trying out for colleges or prestigious programs, but Bella trying out for Guilds, and a past version of Alex had helped her build prep.

It wasn’t exactly the same, and there were memories, including apparently moving to a new, bigger townhouse when he was 14, which were entirely absent in Alex’s mind.

Stella did seem to relax a little, as did Matt. The two seemed more conflicted than anything.

They started to touch their food, at least; Alex started, as did Stella and Matt.

It felt kind of like he was a kid they were adopting, from a relative they hadn’t heard from in a while, but who’d recently died; there was clear care.

Maybe it’s a little more care than just that, he allowed.

But he clearly wasn’t quite ‘Alex’ in their minds.

Hours went by. After they finished sushi they walked along the waterfront; the sun was still bright, and there were children playing in a spray park nearby.

Alex was feeling quite raw, and also a little drained. He felt awful, and relieved, at once—relieved that at least it was over with.

“Alex,” said Stella, and he glanced over. “Are—are you okay?”

“Just fine,” he said, smiling a bit.

There was a silence, and Alex stared out over the sparkling bay.

“I remember the day you and Bella first arrived,” she said, and when he looked back, her eyes were a little wet. “It was the happiest day of my life. And—I remember your eyes. You were curious, and bright, and so, so scared, like you were barely seven, and all alone in this world, and all I could think was, ‘So help me God, he’ll never feel like this again, a day in his life. I don’t care what it takes, I swear I’ll move heaven and earth if I have to.’”

She was choking up a little. “Alex, you have the same eyes.”
