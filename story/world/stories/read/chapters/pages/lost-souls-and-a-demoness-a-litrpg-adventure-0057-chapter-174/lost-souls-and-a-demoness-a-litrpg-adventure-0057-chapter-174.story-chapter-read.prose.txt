The first step, finding the dimensional rifts, was trivial. The Labyrinth's global quest included map markers that indicated the zone and quadrant where the demon incursion was occurring. As Seris wasn't a Wayfarer he hadn't received the quest himself, but that was easy enough to fix. All Jade had to do was supply the pilot with directions and give him permission to view her interface, and they were on their way. Tara's intuition that her people would be keeping their city ship close to the action proved to be correct, and a little more than an hour after beginning their inbound flight, they reached the outer edge of the Ironpeak Mountain quadrant.

"Stealth systems powered up." Seris announced, slowing their approach to a crawl as the first planar tear came into view. The entire party crowded around the cockpit door, trying to get a good look at what they were walking, or rather flying, into.

"Woah."

Luis was the first to speak. He let out a low whistle.

"That's way more than I thought there'd be…"

Jade focused on the nearest of the rifts. At this distance, high above the mountain valley that held the distortions, scale was difficult to grasp. Still, the fact that she could clearly make out the rifts spoke to their immense size. They looked like jagged, vertical rips in the air, with edges of rippling prismatic energy, like an enormous blade had sliced through the very fabric of the world. They stood out amidst the shadowed, moonlit valley, the glowing borders illuminating the rocky scrubland for a quarter mile in every direction. At least two-dozen of them extended into the distance, fading into an indistinct glow on the horizon.

"No kidding…" Jade breathed. From this distance, the vision was almost beautiful. The rifts danced and twisted, like bonfires of glowing sunlight reaching towards the night sky. "I don't think we need to worry about the ship fitting through them."

"She'll fit." Seris grunted. "Getting through unnoticed won't be easy, though. There's a lot of activity down there, and our concealment isn't perfect."

That wasn't news, they'd known ahead of time that it was very unlikely they'd simply be able to fly up to one of the rifts and pass through undetected. The magical stealth effect was impressive, but making a ship as large as this completely invisible was beyond the ability of tek'kalla magic, at least if they wanted it to be able to move. In flight, the craft left a telltale shimmer in the sky, reminding Jade of invisibility effects in some videogames. The small disturbance was easy to miss, but to pass through one of these tears they'd have to be low to the ground and moving slowly. Not ideal when an army of alert demons were nearby.

"There are thousands of them." Naomi said, looking down at the shifting mass of creatures pouring through the rifts.

"And they're just the ones we can see." Tara added. "We flew over more than one battle with Labyrinth defenders on the way here. There must be skirmishes like that all throughout the quadrant."

Faint flashes of light briefly illuminated small patches of the surrounding mountains, evidence of the ongoing conflict between invaders and defenders. They had no way of knowing how many people from the Labyrinth had answered the call to take up arms in its defense but judging by the frequency of the visible bursts of magic, the number had to be large. Further afield, barely visible through a split between two mountain peaks, a larger shadow moved. None of them knew much about the titanic golems the Labyrinth had summoned to its own defense, but Seris had been wise enough to give them a wide berth on their way in.

"We stick to the plan." Jade said, returning her attention to the cluster of planar tears arrayed beneath them. One of them, near the outer edge of the valley, looked like it had a little less activity nearby than the others. "That one."

She pointed, and her companions took a few moments to study it for themselves. When they agreed, Jade began making her way to the rear hatch.

"I'll find somewhere to set down." Seris said, bringing the ship around.

"No need."

Jade shifted into her demon form, mindful of her large wings in the cramped space. She kept them furled behind her, careful not to knock any of her friends over as she turned to face them.

"It's too dangerous to get any closer than you have to. I'll fly myself down."

"Oh. Right."

Jade grasped the hatch's handle. This was it. Their last chance to back out safely. They could fly away, leaving this mess for someone else to fix. Was she really about to do this? Wasn't there anyone else better suited for the job?

Maybe there was. But she was here, and they weren't. And with the rapidly increasing number of demons in the valley below, waiting for them to show up wasn't an option.

A pair of arms wrapped around Jade's waist as Naomi grabbed her, pulling her into a tight embrace.

"Be safe, Jade."

She returned the embrace, squeezing back. The air was thick with the anxiety and hope of her companions. In the past she'd had to shut her mind to the unpleasant distractions of her succubus senses. Now, finally, she could sense the positive emotions without the demonic aspect of her spirit twisting them. Jade focused on the hope, breathing it in.

"I will be."

The others added their own words of encouragement, and Jade was surprised to find so few traces of hesitation in her friends. They were anxious, even afraid, but not one of them doubted that this was what they had to do. Even Seris, whose only close connection in the group was Tara, was filled with a sense of stoic determination to see this through.

"Promise me one thing." Jade said, looking at each of her friends in turn. "If they spot you, you're out of here."

"Jade-"

"I can get out of this on my own." Jade said, cutting Luis off. "You can't. If it gets too dangerous, we can try again or find another way. None of this will work if we make it through, only to have an army of them chasing us."

The goddess had shown them a vision of the demon lord's palace. He was sequestered in isolation, alone and uninterrupted as he focused on pouring his own strength into the breaches. For their plan to work, that was how they had to face him.

And I'm not about to let any of you die.

They were all coming back from this. She didn't care about fame or prestige, nor for any accolades they might earn for what they were about to attempt. All that mattered was that they would get to experience the joy of living in the worlds they'd protected.

"Promise me."

They did, and Jade tasted the conviction behind their words. One final check confirmed her radio earpiece was in place, her weapons were sheathed, and the dimensional bag was firmly fastened to her belt. She double-checked that as well, verifying the divine relic and her other equipment was ready and waiting. Everything was in place.

"Have a plan for the distraction?" Tara asked, stepping back to give Jade room as she opened the hatch and spread her wings. Wind whistled through the aperture, whipping Jade's hair across her face.

"Luis!" She called over the noise, pulling her hair into a tail to keep it out of the way. "You stocked up on explosives when we were back on Earth, right?"

"Yeah! They're all in the bag!" He called back with a grin. "Everything the quartermaster had left!"

Jade matched his expression.

"Oh, I have a plan."

Jade landed softly on the ground, taking care to make as little sound as possible. She was on the outskirts of one of the smaller demon encampments, close enough to hear the clamor of activity but far enough that she wouldn't be seen through the sparse trees that dotted the valley floor. Not that there was anything for them to see, Jade was shrouded in the folds of her invisibility spell. She wasn't about to take the chance that some overachieving demon grunt would spot her descent from the open sky and start asking awkward questions. Just in case, Jade waited half a minute to see if anyone was coming to investigate her arrival. When no one did, she signaled her companions with three clicks of her radio. She was safely on the ground, and it was time to begin.

Jade dismissed her invisibility, revealing her demon form and halting the drain on her mana. She wore her custom made enchanted "armor" along with a glove over her right hand to hide her Wayfarer mark. Who knew if her current outfit at all matched what passed for fashion among succubi, but Jade had a feeling that her revealing attire would fit right in. Besides, her assumed role was of one of the many demon scouts that had been infiltrating the Labyrinth in recent years, it made sense that she would be wearing Labyrinth gear upon her return.

You look just like one of them. She reminded herself as she started towards the camp. Confidence. Act like you belong.

How had Malice acted? Haughty? Arrogant? She could do that. Adopting a straight-backed, imperious stance, Jade boldly strode into the glimmering light cast by the boundaries of the nearby dimensional rift. The first thing she noticed was that the camp was surprisingly organized. Demons, both varieties she had seen before and those she had not, worked in teams to erect tents and stack boxes of supplies. Officers marched down open lanes left between the structures, shouting instructions and casting menacing glares at any who didn't immediately respond. Even in this camp, which was relatively small compared others she'd seen from above, Jade saw hundreds of demons at work. And as the rift itself came into view, she could see more streaming through the breach to join their brethren.

Something bumped against her shoulder, breaking her chain of thought. She turned to see one of the horned, red-skinned bruzak's cringing away from her, his hands raised in supplication.

"Forgive me, my lady!" he said, bowing his head. "I didn't see you!"

For a brief moment, Jade's brain locked up. Being surrounded by so many demons dredged up memories of the second floor's corespace and her confrontation with Malice. A battle that she'd barely walked away from. Her heart thundered in her chest.

Get a grip! She thought, mentally kicking herself. Play the part!

"Watch where you're going." She growled, pushing past the contrite demon. "Touch me again and you'll lose a hand."

The bruzak scurried off, a distinct sense of relief clearly detectable in his mind. So, succubi were relatively high in the demon hierarchy, then? It hadn't just been Malice. The knot in Jade's stomach eased slightly. That would make things a little easier. She continued moving, walking with purpose. She had to finish this before anybody asked her a question she couldn't answer. Jade took in her surroundings, looking for inspiration. The camp was larger and busier than it had looked from above. Most of the demons were humanoid, although she spotted a few with more monstrous forms dotted among their number. The dimensional tear directly abutted one edge of the camp, extending more than a hundred feet into the sky. Unfortunately, that meant her friends would have to pass directly over the mass of demons at a relatively low altitude to pass through the rift. Whatever distraction she implemented had to capture the attention of everyone nearby and keep them occupied long enough for her friends to slip past undetected.

Her original plan had been to surreptitiously plant a series of explosives throughout the camp and detonate them all at once, sowing panic and confusion. Now that she was on the ground, she was having second thoughts. A big blast might confuse them for a moment, but then they'd just be on alert, looking for whatever had caused it. She needed to give them something to focus on, not something to react to.

Jade's gaze landed on a series of tall, spindly rock formations rising from the top of a steep hill on the far side of the camp. They might've been the remains of some ancient structure, or just random boulders that had happened to pile atop each other after sliding down the mountainside. Either way, the sight gave Jade an idea. If she could give them a nudge in just the right place, it would send an avalanche of tumbling rocks hurtling down the slope, right towards the rift. It might not actually reach the camp, but that was fine. As long as it looked like it might, there'd be a mad scramble to get out of the way.

Jade changed course, cutting through the middle of the camp and making her way towards her new target. Going around would take too long, and she didn't want to arouse suspicion by skulking in the shadows. Most of the demons hurriedly got out of her way, and she made it a point to shoulder her way through the few who didn't. The gesture would've been more effective accompanied by a spike of her aura, but she held back. She couldn't risk anyone noticing the traces of celestial power her spirit was infused with.

Doing her best to avoid high-traffic areas without making it clear she was doing so, Jade walked quickly and with purpose through the crowd of assembling demons. More than a few of them stared at her as she passed, and she caught hints of both fear and admiration directed her way through the general haze of anticipation and bloodlust that hung over the camp like a thick fog. Halfway through, she could do this. Jade remained on high alert even as she tried her best to appear relaxed and in control. She clutched the whip she'd taken from Malice, partly to add to her image and partly to keep her hands from clenching into fists from the strain of being so thoroughly surrounded. She shoved her way between another pair of bruzaks, and then passed another, taller demon.

Thud.

The larger figure didn't budge, stopping her in her tracks. Slowly, the red-skinned, broad-shouldered demon turned to face Jade, glaring down at her with eyes of molten metal.

"What are you doing here?" He growled, shoving her back a step. Jade opened her mouth to reply but nothing came out. A jolt of fear coursed through her like paralytic poison. Had he seen through her? Was her cover blown already?

Fortunately, the offended demon didn't wait for her to answer his question. He took a menacing step forward. "Another succubus acting all high and mighty just because she was sent here ahead of the rest of us." He continued in a voice that sounded like two stones being ground together into dust. "And after you all screwed up the plan so completely. I'd say I was surprised, but nobody expected better from a bunch of lust lickers."

Oh, good. Jade relaxed, or rather, relaxed as much as one could with a giant, angry demon bearing down on them. Just demon racism.

The relief must've shown on her face because the demon's eyes blazed red, anger billowing from his mind like crackling flames.

"Think I'm funny, do you?" He snarled, reaching a clawed hand over his shoulder to draw a ridiculously large axe. She took a step back, preparing to flee… and then stopped, quickly pulling up his information. The system labelled him as a rage demon. It didn't give a level, but judging by his aura strength, he wasn't any stronger than her. Time for a new plan.

"No." Jade spat on the ground and then made a show of turning away. "I just think you're weak and pathetic."

It wasn't particularly inventive as far as insults went, but the demon was clearly spoiling for a fight.

He didn't need further persuasion.
