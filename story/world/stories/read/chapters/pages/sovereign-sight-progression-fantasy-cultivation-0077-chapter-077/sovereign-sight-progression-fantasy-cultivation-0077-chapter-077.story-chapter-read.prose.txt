Negotiating is a delicate art.

The merchant was going to kill him.

Harker could tell the moment he put away the black coin. It wasn’t any one thing, but he’d been around people that hated him enough times to see the signs.

The mask was working though. The voice too, even if it was starting to hurt his throat. Both features together had the merchant on the backfoot from the start, and the revealing knowledge of the man’s secret cabinet had him sweating.

Now he thinks I’m a dangerous killer. A Shroud member. Beneath his backward helmet, Harker frowned. Not enough to stem his greed, of course. It seems I’ll have to put in more effort.

Boggs led him through a narrow arched doorway barely wide enough for the brawny merchant. Harker kept his Talent extended to the full five feet, but Boggs didn’t make a move. He slipped into the next room and the man was waiting at the other end of a short hallway.

“The range, as requested.”

Through the hall was a narrow room. At most it was twenty feet wide, but it was long—at least one hundred feet according to the distance markers scribed onto the heavy wooden targets. The floor was gray stone that had perhaps once been nice. Now it was pocked and pitted, the old slabs tilted at odd angles by who knew what sort of Talents. The ceiling was high, though, fifteen feet at least, and criss-crossed with exposed timbers that looked to have been chewed on by animals.

A stickjak leaned against a wall, deactivated and gray. Its limbs were patched across the forearms and shins, and its torso looked to have been severed at least once, but it seemed sturdy enough. To the side of the stickjak, weapons lined the wall. They were split into buckets and barrels by size, type, and expense.

"Those closest are going to be the cheapest weapons I got," Boggs explained. "Simple things forged of plain steel. I got daggers, throwing knives, sling stones. A few bucklers too."

Harker nodded along as the man listed out his wares. Next up came heavier items: maces, reinforced clubs, even a flail. Beyond that were swords and spears, though the swords were the most expensive, even for common steel.

"Are these all the bows you have?" Harker gestured to the wall where a number of them were hung on mounted racks.

Boggs frowned, but it lasted only a second. "Yes. I had a recurve and some hand crossbows, but those go fast in these parts."

Harker hummed, careful to seem unimpressed. "No saltsteel?”

“Too rich for my shop, I fear."

"Pity." Sovereign Sight pulsed, keeping track of the merchant at all times. The man hadn't made a move yet. Perhaps Harker needed to give him an opportunity. "Do you have anything else like this?"

From his waist Harker unsheathed the blue dagger. Boggs' interest was immediately piqued. He reached out. "May I?"

"Of course."

Harker handed over the blue dagger and the merchant turned it over in his hands. "Normally I'd charge a fee for this, but being able to see that dazzling coin was payment enough. First Depth—Assess The Earth."

The merchant's smile was easy as his Talent gushed from between his fingers. The Water coated Harker's dagger, limning it in blue-green light and Boggs' smile dropped. "Oh, how interesting. It seems the weapon once held in an infusion. Most likely to make it move more swiftly through the air. Strike or perhaps Swift. First Depth at best."

Boggs lifted the weapon and swung it briefly. It trailed blue-green water like a ribbon of light. "Hmm. Possibly for wear, too, but the metal is made of something that makes such things pointless.”

“What metal is that?"

"Calabor. A rare enough metal. Similar quality to obstinite without being as hard to shape. It’s durable though, hence the lack of need for a wear infusion. The edge will hold for quite a long time, but its real advantage is how well it takes infusions. Some metals can only accept impressions or partial extractions, but calabor’s mid-tier. It’s known to take a fully extracted Talent up to Third Depth."

The light faded from around the blue dagger, and much to Harker's surprise, Boggs handed it back. "If you can find a quality thaumist and afford a proper tidal gem, it is quite possible they could re-infuse it with little issue."

"Could I change the infusion?"

"It’d cost a lot more. The labyrinth has already been laid. See there, along the grip?"

Harker had already noticed those, of course. And he gave an appreciative nod as he sheathed the dagger at his hip.

He considered the weapons on the wall. Harker had gathered quite a bit of money from all of the foes he'd faced and defeated. Bandits and mercenaries carried more coin than one might expect.

Mercs and monsters are still on the road ahead. No reason to be frugal. Soon, he had stacked before him a new short bow to replace the one he'd lost, as well as a quiver full of arrows. It had hurt when he realized that he’d lost the Aberrant tips somewhere in the river. These arrows, however, were different from most. The tips were exceedingly narrow, and a few of them had specialized indents. Needleheads and notched types. Meant for punching through armor and detaching inside people, respectively.

Useful, if grim. Harker took them all.

Oh? Now that’s interesting. Amid the barrel of arrows was the scent of Water. Harker followed it, a faint, ghostly impression that snaked between the feathered shafts. When he found it, the scent was emanating from two arrows, both of them infused.

“How’d those get there?” Boggs muttered. “They’re set with an incendiary impression.”

Less than a full extract, an impression was enough for a weak or specialized effect. These, Boggs explained, would ignite into a small but potent flame upon impact. Harker could think of a number of uses for them off the top of his head and took those as well.

He restocked his throwing knives and steel darts, of course. Not all of them had made it out of the river and they were eternally useful. But arrows and thrown weapons weren’t enough anymore. Harker needed something for when things got up close and personal.

He considered a sword, but everything on offer was too big. Even the smallest of them would would stick out, and it didn't help that he was not as familiar with the weapon. His mother had given him some training with one, but it was perfunctory at best.

“Is that…a stick?” Behind one of the barrels and tucked against the wall were a pair of dust-coated rods. Cobwebs clung to them and the barrel both, and Harker grimaced at the spiders he sensed scurrying away from his reaching hand. Still, he pulled them free.

Grimy under his palm and almost gray from the accumulated gunk, they had clearly been forgotten for a long while. He swung them and the wood flexed, tossing off the lighter clods of detritus before holding them up and sighting down their two foot lengths. The wood hadn’t warped or split, which was encouraging, and they were quite stout.

“Ah, those sticks. I think they call it a shortstaff. Mad of waxwood, if I’m not mistaken. Strong but very flexible.”

Harker had noticed that. When he swung them they bent, whipping slightly in the direction of the swing before springing back. He’d never heard of the material, but as he adjusted his grip on the ends, it felt exceedingly comfortable.

“I confiscated those from a would-be burglar. They weren’t much use to him, not after I was done. Why not take a mace if you want brute force? Sticks aren’t a proper weapon.”

Harker moved them around, taking a few practice swings. He fell into the first step, the movement ingrained into him since he was five, and the dual shortstaff just…fit. The wood was flexible but the spring was satisfying and speedy. He took the second step, letting his body flow into the next shape, and brought the sticks up and around. They whistled through the air.

“A Combat Form…” Boggs laughed; a thin sound. “A sword would do you better for something like that. Sir.”

Combat Form. He wanted to laugh. “I like these.”

“Very, ah, very good, sir. Since you’re set on them, I would note that you can feed waxwood Water. Just a little will harden them, removing their flexibility for a greater impact.”

Harker nodded, twirling the sticks. “Turn on the jak. I’d like to try them out.”

"As you wish.”

Boggs activated the stickjak. A gush of Water gurgled out of its reservoir, jerking the construct to its feet and forward into the center of the narrow room. It settled into a neutral stance, mitten-like hands lifted and body sat into a slight crouch. Harker lifted his sticks, and the jak turned its knob of a head toward him.

Harker advanced.

The construct was slow, but so was Harker—at first. One after the other, the dual shortstaffs rapped against its body, blow after blow taken across shoulder, forearm, thigh, and shin. They were light strikes, barely more than taps, as he felt his way through the familiar weapons. Of all the items on Boggs’ shelf they reminded him most of his mother’s lessons, when he’d pretend a simple stick was a sword.

No. It’s not a Combat Form. No, I don’t care what the other boys say they’re learning—they’re most likely lying. A Form…it’s far more than this. What I'm teaching you is about survival, not art. You understand?

Good.

Now, hold it tight, but keep your wrist loose until you strike. You can do this with a stick, a sword, or even a mace—but I’d keep it simple. Light. The point is to be mobile. Hit them hard and get out before they can strike back.

Now watch me. First step is…

The movements came back to him piecemeal, like a half remembered song. They’d spent countless hours on those lessons, learning how to dodge, taunt, and use the terrain against his attackers. Mostly though, he’d learned how to run away.

None of that now. Harker slipped into the first step, flowing into the second and third with only a few hitched strides. Every fight he engaged in took pieces of these steps, but all too often he fought with daggers or thrown items. He’d never gotten very good at it—hadn’t even thought of the full sequence since she’d fallen too sick to spar with him. With the shortstaves in his hands, it almost felt like she was there.

He stepped faster, and the jak followed.

Fourth step. Harker cut around the construct and its body twisted to keep up, but before it could complete the turn, he brought both staves down onto its shoulders. Its arms dropped, as if injured, and Harker followed up with a backhanded crash against its lumpy head.

They bounced off with a loud crack.

Hmm.

The waxwood was certainly flexible, but the impact was missing something, just as Boggs had said. Harker funneled Water through his palm Locks and it gushed between his fingers. Much of it was lost, evaporated into the air, but a small piece of it soaked into the waxwood. The staves stiffened, moments before Harker struck with all the force of his arms. The stickjak reeled backward, its feet scrabbling for purchase. Harker was atop it, hammering home several blows to its head and hands before hooking its leg and dropping the construct to the stone.

“Enough. Estet!”

At Boggs’ word, the construct dimmed. It did not stand back up.

Harker panted, a little out of breath as he admired his new weapons. While he wasn’t strong enough to make blows like that truly matter, the length of the staves and the ability to be flexible or rigid gave him some interesting options. I might be able to make you proud, ma.

It would be far more effective if he could refine his body further, but Harker would get there soon enough. He smiled, thrilled at the prospect.

“I assume that means you like them, sir.”

“I do. They are exactly what I was looking for.” He spun them. “Do you have a sheath?”

Boggs did, taken from the same burglar apparently. It was a baldric, made of leather with steel fastenings and a strap to hold it to his back. Harker set them aside, wrapped carefully. Only then did he pick up the shortbow. “Do you have practice arrows?”

“By the first target.”

Harker secured a handful of arrows before returning to the chipped line someone had painted on the stone. The shortbow was made of horn and wood in layers, which made it surprisingly hard to bend. He was forced to loop his leg around it and use his bodyweight to bend the bow and string it, and by the end he was breathing a touch heavier. He still wasn’t fully recovered from the poison. The simple steps against the stickjak had tired him more than he thought.

The draw felt heavy too, but fifty pounds was standard for hunting around the Vale. Harker nocked an arrow and pulled back, grimacing behind his mask and trying not to tremble. His attention drifted momentarily to Boggs, who still watched avidly from the weapons rack.

Perhaps a bit too close, all things considered.

Harker turned his back on him. If the merchant didn’t make a move now, he never would. Harker was tired of waiting.

With Sovereign Sight held close, no more than a few inches out, Harker fired. He hit the target at the twenty-foot mark with the sound of hollow wood. The posts were marked with stripes of white paint marked with bright red dots no bigger than a fist. Without his Talent, the arrow had stabbed into the red easily.

Harker hefted the bow, inspecting it. “Good stock.”

Boggs only grunted.

Smiling, Harker let his Talent stretch outward. The line of it pressed to twenty feet, fully engulfing the target post. He drew and fired. This time the arrow struck dead center on the lowest red dot. He fired again, two more times, and each arrow found a dot. It was as easy as reaching out and tapping where he wanted it to go.

Oh, I like this. Taking a few steps to the side and closer to Boggs, Harker considered the next target. Let’s try forty feet.

Still keeping his twenty-foot line extended, he pivoted it until it was aimed toward the new target. That was tricky, forcing Harker to squeeze his sphere in just the right way—not hard, exactly, but slower than he liked.

“There it is,” he whispered.

He fired.

It flew true, hitting a red dot dead center. Talent alone was powerful, but he had used a bow since he’d been able to draw a ten pound weight. Skill is everything, but Talent is a force multiplier.

Switching targets to the fifty-foot mark, Harker lined himself up. He could feel the air, taste the stone on the ground, and feel the sound of soft cloth rubbing against itself as he adjusted his position. He fired, but this time his aim was off. The arrow stuck to the post, but only a hair above the uneven stone flooring.

He adjusted again, squeezing his Talent until he felt a whisper of certainty. This time, the arrow hit higher, just barely grazing the fist-sized red dot.

Some more practice is required for these distances. Harker moved slightly, closer to Boggs than ever, and considered the target at the far end of the room. One hundred feet. Easy.

Sovereign Sight pulsed around him as he figured out his positioning. Details filtered through to him, not just from the end of the twenty-foot line, but from its entire length. The sensation there was less acute, but it steadied him, almost like leaning against a half wall. The ebb and flow of minor air currents, the adjustments of his own body, all of it was details that sank into his mind like drips from a leaking pump. That whisper of certainty faded and grew by turns, until Harker distinctly felt a groove in the air itself, as if that was where the arrow wanted to go.

He fired.

Less than a second later, the solid tok of its striking rang out.

Harker frowned. He'd hit on the first try…but he’d missed the red dot. He could do better.

He expanded his Talent, letting it bulge around himself. He needed to feel his movements, not just the target. Harker let the sound of his breath move through him, followed by the steady bump of his pulse, and the strain of fabric across his shoulders and chest. He sought the groove…

…and tasted the muffled sound of steel against wood.

He could feel it, cold and sharp against his tongue. The light glinting off a custom wooden handle. Most of all, was the sweat and sour fear.

The steel moved.

“AH!”

Harker retracted his Talent, fingers stinging slightly from the bowstring, and blinked his unseeing eyes beneath his mask. The merchant stood against the wall, his hand pinned by an arrow into the wooden panels, a knife at his feet.

"I didn't mean—!"

Harker stepped closer and the man sputtered to silence. He kicked away the knife before reaching out. Boggs balled up a fist, and Harker laughed. “Try it.”

The merchant’s knuckles went white…before his fingers loosened.

“Good boy.” Harker ripped out the arrow, drawing a cry form the man. "Clean yourself up."

Boggs nodded, his jowls shaking vigorously with his agreement. Cradling his hand against his body, he pulled bandages from a hefty pouch at his side and wrapped himself up. His pulse was racing, but so was Harker's.

The young man watched, suppressing his nerves as best he could, and loomed over the bleeding merchant. "I believe now is a good time to talk about price."

Boggs blinked at him. "What…did you have in mind?"

"Free sounds right."

The merchant's throat bobbed. "Free.”

“Sounds fair to me.” A knife expertly tumbled across Harker’s knuckles. “Unless you’d like to negotiate?"

"Very well. Free it is." The words squeezed from Boggs’ lips, as if he had to force them out. He looked ill, and Harker was certain that it had nothing to do with his hand.

“I also need information. True information,” Harker said. “Everything you have on the Bestials that are wandering the riverlands. And the schedule for the Waveskim leaving Antrim. Can you get it?”

"I can."

Boggs packaged up all of Harker's chosen weaponry under his all-seeing gaze. Soon enough they were up front, and Harker raided the man’s stash there too. An expensive number of mixtures, potions, and concoctions disappeared into Harker’s bag, bulging its lower pockets.

“Is that all?” Boggs couldn’t look Harker in the face. He simply pushed the pack toward him.

"Yes, I—wait. What is this?" Below the counter, folded atop itself was a set of leather and metal plates. "You didn’t tell me you had armor."

Harker pulled it free, his Talent flowing across it in a wave. His mouth dropped behind his helmet. It wasn't just armor—it was Talent armor.

"You wouldn't want that,” Boggs said hastily. "It's not properly infused. Just a minor impression of Stoneheart. It makes the leather and mail a bit tougher, but it's a thin set, meant for—”

“For thieves." Harker said, grinning. "It's perfect."
