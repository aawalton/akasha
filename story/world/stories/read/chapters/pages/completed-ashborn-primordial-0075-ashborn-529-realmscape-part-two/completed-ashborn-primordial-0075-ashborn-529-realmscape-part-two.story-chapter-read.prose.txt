Janak had lied.

Yes, the process may have been startlingly similar to Vir's countless practice runs. Yes, his predecessor had designed the Chakra mindscape to simulate the actual machine to the best of his ability, and barring minor issues thus far, he had.

The pain, however, was another matter entirely. It was not pain of the soul. Although terrible, Vir had spent years living with a shattered soul, after all. He was nothing if not proficient at managing that pain.

No, this pain was purely of the body. Which should have made it simpler to deal with, as Vir was no stranger to physical pain either, but generally, people only need endure the pain of dying once.

Vir's body, however, was being ripped apart at the most basic level only to be healed anew by Yuma's Embrace. Feeding off the infinite prana, the Ultimate Art of Tara's clan knew no limits, and though the art was normally heralded as a miracle capable of curing even the worst ailments and the most gruesome injuries, all it did was prolong Vir's suffering.

Vir doubted there was any mind in any realm who could emerge from such an experience unscathed.

To say nothing of the monumental juggling act he was supposed to perform while under this duress.

So far, he had. He'd activated Jungal Beckons, shunting the excess prana buildup to begin the creation of an illusory world

A unified realm without the scarring of Ash Tears or Gates, just as he had a thousand times in his Crown Chakra mindscape. The task was only possible thanks to the machine. It enhanced his mind a million times beyond what he was capable of, and an instant later, the land of the new realm was in place.

But even Vir had his limits. True, hard limits that could not be surpassed no matter the effort. Demons could not fly simply by wishing it, and so it was for Vir. His body would succumb long before Janak had a chance to sacrifice his soul. There would be nothing of Vir left to finish the process, and that was hardly the end of it.

Without Vir serving as a blind spot to the enemy, crippling the Fateweavers' eyes and ears, he couldn't begin to guess how reality would unravel.

For while his mindscape might not have had the detail to show him, now, inside the machine, Vir beheld the entirety of the souls of those he copied, with no detail hidden.

Which meant he also saw where the Fateweavers had exercised their power, corrupting souls.

There were so many of them. So many more than he'd expected.

Agents of the enemy, uncountable insects scattered throughout the realms in numbers that made his stomach churn, even if in most cases their influence amounted to little more than foggy eyes and near-deaf ears. Exceedingly few bore the capacity to alter reality directly. They were simply watchers, seeded throughout civilization, not one of them aware of the fact that their souls had been tampered with.

Vir had thought long and hard about excising the Fateweavers' influence from such people. Had spent countless sessions in the mindscape thinking the problem over, looking for a way to free them without permanently maiming their souls. He’d crafted dozens of theories and even more solutions.But now that he saw them, he didn't dare try.

For one, his Crown Chakra Mindscape was incapable of showing the Fateweavers’ influence without the actual machine, and as Vir now saw, it was impossible. Their influence was far too deeply woven into who those people had become. To cut out that part of their soul would be to fundamentally alter the person, and not in any good way. Vir found the very thought revolting, but it was only when he laid his cosmic eye upon Maiya's soul that he nearly lost control of the whole process.

Their infection was so entangled in her that he couldn't even begin unraveling the rotted veins that pierced deep into her core.

It had been there ever since she'd become the Blessed Chosen, growing and festering all these years.He clenched his teeth against a fresh wave of pain and primal rage he had no outlet for. There was nothing he could do. To remove it would be akin to ripping out her heart, and nothing short of death would release her from that imprisonment. She would persist as their foremost agent in the new realm.A realm in which there would be no Akh Nara left to shield her.

Vir couldn't say whether it was a mercy or the worst cruelty imaginable that she’d keep her mind through it all.

She’d live each day as she always had, slogging away at whatever cause she dedicated her mind to. All of it pre-scripted. A puppet dancing to invisible threads.

The pain broke his thoughts, forcing him to move on.

It was the smallest slip. A fraction of a second where his concentration frayed under the accumulated weight of that agony, and he fell behind the process. Not by much. Likely not even a Fateweaver intervention, but an honest mistake. He had simply fumbled.

And everything unraveled.

The prana that had been rushing through his body piled up, and Vir's body began to burn from the inside out, coming apart at the most basic level.

Vir flared Aspect of the Demon God and Yuma's Embrace, pulling absurd amounts of prana to heal the damage, and understood with Clarity that this was it. He hadn't even gotten close to where he needed to be. Hadn’t even reached the most dangerous step.

Concentrate, a foreign voice said directly to his mind. A calm and ancient voice, and even had he not recognized it, there was only one soul who could maintain such composure in such a trying time. I will aid where I can, Janak spoke into his mind, and the prana abated. Not all of it, and not for long, but it was enough.

Vir didn't waste his breath thanking the deity for this miracle, instead seizing the chance to regain control of the process as Yuma's Embrace swiftly rebuilt his body.

Soul after soul, the people of the realms flowed like a raging river as Vir pushed both his mind and the machine to the limit of its capability. Perhaps it was because of his experience reforging his soul, perhaps it was his experience in that broken Alt Ashani universe that multiplied his prana capacity, but the task came easier to him than he felt it should have.Until, at least, his mind came to rest upon her.The tale has been illicitly lifted; should you spot it on Amazon, report the violation.A soul fighting monsters far too powerful for any human. A soul lost in the Deep Ash, where it had no right to be.Three souls, in fact. Three he recognized well.How? Vir wondered as he copied them, bringing them from the Ash to near Matali, where they belonged.But he had no time to dwell on the matter, for Janak's voice spoke again, and although still calm, there was an unmistakable urgency to his words now.

Time is running out. I cannot hold the prana at bay much longer. Prioritize those you care for. Copy them now.

Vir didn't answer that either. He had already copied Maiya, Cirayus, the wolves, and everyone else who mattered to him, having transferred them early in the process.

But that wasn't what Janak was asking. Janak was asking him to stop before he finished. To accept the erasure of untold thousands.

He refused.

There were souls in the realms he knew were rotten. People who had done things that should never be forgiven. People who were agents of the enemy.And now, Vir could make them cease to be. Not through any great feat, but by simple inaction. In doing nothing, his burden would ease, they would die, and Janak would praise him for it. To do otherwise would jeopardize the entire task. Yet Vir copied them anyway. It was a decision he had made right at the outset.For while he was no stranger to killing, that was as an individual.

Vir not the arbiter of what was right. He refused to embark upon that path, for he knew where it led. When one wielded as much power as he did, one had to adhere to a higher standard.Because otherwise, people died. Countries burned, and realms suffered.No, everyone would be transferred, with no exceptions.

Your time is up.

Vir copied the last soul.

I’m done! He shouted into the void, but received no response.

He became aware of why through his Crown Chakra mindscape, searching for Janak’s existence within the machine-enhanced awareness that showed him everyone and everything in all the realms.

He found him, and what Vir saw was Janak's existence burning away. The one soul he couldn't transfer because there would be nothing left. Vir could relate to the agony Janak must be enduring, but not even Vir had been subjected to such a complete, brutal liquefaction of the soul, feeding the machine like coals to a fire.

Fresh Inscriptions blazed, so complex that they wreathed the entire machine in pranic light. And something else. Something Vir only saw because of his Third Eye Chakra.

Vir eyes went wide. This was no mere Imperium inscription. No matter the complexity, Imperium inscriptions channeled prana and prana alone.

But this? This was a Chakra inscription, and as far as Vir knew, nothing like it existed in the world. A creation invented by Janak's successor after the Fall, and likely the only one of its kind in existence.

As for its purpose, Vir couldn’t begin to guess. A moment later, he no longer had to.

Something opened in Vir's perception. A sensation similar to when he opened his Third Eye for the first time, yet so much greater.

For the first time in Vir’s life, he saw.

Not reality as he knew it, nor even prana or Chakra.

He saw something deeper.

He saw the universe in an entirely new and alien way.

The way they did.

The tapestry of reality was laid bare before his eyes, and what a glorious sight it was.

Every thread of every life stretched out across time in both directions.

He saw the explosion of light that burned like the sun when they began and the darkness they left behind where they ended. Saw how some threads intertwined, while others intersecting only briefly.Vir and Maiya. Their encounter with Riyan. Vir's first meeting with Janak under Daha, and so on.

Through the threads, he saw the rebellion he had led against the Chitran, witnessed Ash Tears ravage the land in the present, and glimpsed the future.

Fated moments. The concept of destiny. All of it laid bare before him.

And so, for the first time in his life, Vir truly understood the monumental task that was influencing reality.

A single tug on a single thread spawned or unraveled a million others, as the upcoming war between human and demonkind promised to. In a handful of the universes that lived on, anyway.

There were so few of those. Such precious few options that led to something other than eternal darkness.

In the tapestry, he beheld life and death. Saw a thread run its course and terminate abruptly. Not in years or centuries, but mere months.

His blood ran cold.

Because he knew that thread. Knew it better than he knew his own.

No! No no no no!

Maiya.

Vir forced down the panic. Forced himself to remember that these were not guarantees. Merely potential pathways, and any of them alterable.

His relief was cut short when he realized he would not be in those futures to influence them. To protect her and change her fate.

Vir desperately located his own thread and finally understood why the enemy had never been able to touch it. Rather than a single line, it appeared and disappeared, seemingly at random. Altering events the Fateweavers could not see or predict.

And then, between those threads, he finally saw Janak's plan.For there was very much a plan. One laid out across five thousand years. He saw how it was supposed to work and why it hadn't failed yet and what it was that Janak had truly hoped for by keeping Vir in the dark, not risking divulging that plan to one who ought to have been shielded from the enemy's sight.

And then... then he looked back. Through time and space, and what he saw froze him in abject terror.For he saw the Fateweavers and knew in that moment that everyone, from Janak to Yuma, had been wrong about who, or what, they were.They weren't a force of nature, as Janak had said. They hadn't even existed when the infinity of universes was born, spawning so many alternate realities that the tapestry became nothing but noise, making Vir's head spin. A true infinity.And then, it had all come crashing down.

There became so many, whatever fabric supported reality began to buckle in the same way it did when Ash Tears formed.It began with a single universe, which grew so weakened, so riddled with holes in its fabric, it became corrupted. Not only did life die out in that universe, it weakened the fabric of the ones near it. The corruption spread like a plague, infecting them, and before long, that glorious infinity had been reduced to a paltry handful. Less than a dozen.All of creation had almost ceased to be.Yet in the crucible of those last survivors, a mutation came to be by no intelligent design or conscious will. A pure manifestation of random chance and nothing more.The mutation countered the corruption, killing off the other blighted universes until only one remained. A healthy universe from which realities once again exploded. And in each of them, the mutation manifested.Culling universes when they grew too long. Laying them to rest when their fabric weakened, threatening to fall to corruption.Keeping the number of universes in balance such that the disaster that happened before could never happen again.It was ironic. Janak and Yuma had referred to it as a force of nature.

They had no idea how correct they had been.The Fateweavers were not a conscious enemy. They were not even alive.They, or rather it, was nothing more than reality's cosmic analog to the body's immune system. Cleaning dead matter, that new life might prosper. Spawned from chance to protect the infinity of universes as a whole. An immune system so advanced, so evolved through branching universe after branching universe, it even mimicked intelligence.

And they had laid the seeds of their gambit in his universe many millennia ago. So deep in the past that no living being had any hope of noticing it, worked into the very ore of the metals used to construct the machine itself.

Vir almost laughed at its simplicity. It had been there the whole time, hiding in plain sight. Invisible to Janak, to Ashani, and to every incarnation of the Akh Nara ever since.

There would be no grand battle for the tapestry. No final stand against their enemy.

No, the process would fail simply because the weakness in the foundation of this machine would cause it to buckle under the pranic load. Nothing less, nothing more.

And it could not be countered for the simple reason that the process had already begun.

The tapestry showed him countless futures, and in every one, the world that came after was doomed to follow the threads the enemy had laid. Some survived longer than others, but all short-lived.

Janak’s plan could have worked. The new realm would exist exactly as it had been planned, and the souls within it would live in clean air under a real sun, the Ash nothing but a distant memory.

But not anymore.

There was one last thing the tapestry showed Vir in that moment of insight.

His own thread.

It ended here. Right now. This moment, in this machine, was where it stopped. The Fateweavers' final play, worked into causality so long ago that even Janak had never seen it coming.

Vir could not save the world...

Or at least, that was how it was supposed to be. How the tapestry had been laid out.

Until she interfered.

“Oh, Vaak,” a melodious and enchanting voice sounded in his head. A voice he knew well. “It would seem our final farewell is upon us at last…”
