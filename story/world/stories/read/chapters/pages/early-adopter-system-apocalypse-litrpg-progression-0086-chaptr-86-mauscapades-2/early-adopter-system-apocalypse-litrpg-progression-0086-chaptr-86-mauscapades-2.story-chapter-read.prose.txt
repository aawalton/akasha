Entering the cell was revelatory for Maus.

It was somehow both better and worse than the decrepit prison cells in the previous hallway. Those had been all stone and straw mattresses, dim lighting and quiet world–weariness, permeated with the accumulated odours of captivity.

This cell was different.

Much cleaner. Much brighter. But in many ways much colder as well.

Maus couldn't read energy the same way Boss could, and yet the oppressive sterility of the prisoner’s cage was loud enough that he didn’t need to.

The cell was pristine, the colours a uniform greige, the enchanted walls were bright.

There was a single narrow bed, a single desk, a washing and waste area, and a single occupant.

The vulpian in the cell was barefoot, with sandy–brown fur, wearing a long, thick white shift, and cuffs on their forearms that reminded Maus of Boss’s enchanted, training bracers.

However, the biggest contrast between this vulpian and the other prisoners Maus had seen was their attitude. There was no sightless gaze, no defeated slouch, no vitriol, or malaise. There was only a profound sense of resignation that came off her in almost palpable waves.

The prisoner felt diminished to Maus’s spiritual sense.

Well, no matter. It would appear this was the prisoner Maus would be helping to escape.

Looking around the inside of the cell, he couldn’t see any obvious opening in the door. If Maus waited inside until after the guard and attendant left, he would end up just as trapped as the prisoner, with no quiet way out until another guard entered.

So, his best chance of getting the Administrator out would be to subdue everyone else and do it quickly and quietly enough that nobody could call for reinforcements.

No one else in the prison was Tier 1 except the Administrator, but he wasn’t too worried about her. Unfortunately, Maus had only just crossed the threshold himself. Well, Boss was always saying the best way to understand your power was to use it.

What better training than live targets?

He didn’t need to kill them, he just needed to subdue them. The question then became, how to go about it?

Knocking everyone out would be the easy part. Maus was super good at doing that. The problem was what came after.

Boss always said to go in with a plan. Which Maus found kinda strange because she also said no plan survived first contact. Maus didn’t know exactly what the plan was contacting, but figured a dead plan must be better than no plan, because the Boss always planned anyway.

Maus’s plan was simple. Knock everyone out, then escape, then run into a crowded place to hide. Maus didn’t know how far, or at what point the System would decide he’d completed Phase Two, but he figured once they got back to the settlement they could wait it out.

So, with the plan firm in his head, he executed it.

With no need for subtlety and no fear of friendly fire, he was free to express his power.

For the first time since he’d entered the portal Maus could use the attacking aspect of his Echo. Maus sent out a resonating pulse strong enough to knock out anyone below Tier 1.

A wave of energy passed through the prison. Everyone caught by the pulse collapsed. Some went down like a dropped rock while others took longer.

The prisoner clutched her head, eyes squeezed closed as she gritted her teeth in pain.

Maus didn’t pay her much mind as he began the next step of his very good, very brilliant plan.

Looting.

There was a quote Boss had used to describe what he was doing: To the victor go the spoils.

Maus wasn’t interested in spoiling anything, nor did he want to collect anything spoiled. That’s why he liked the Boss’s version better: Never let a victory go to waste.

It would be wasteful to leave perfectly useful things behind.

Maus was not wasteful.

Nodding to himself, he Blinked from cell to cell. Finding nothing of worth, he went from guard to guard, taking armour, weapons, jewellery, documents, keys, and anything else that wasn’t physically part of their person, including their clothes.

Boss had once explained that clothing was cultural, and that in most societies not covering yourself was considered improper.

Maus figured the guards would find it difficult to chase anyone if they felt improper.

By the time Maus got back to the prisoner, the Tier 1 had finally regained her senses.

“Hi, hi, hello,” Maus said. The vest projecting his thoughts.

The prisoner looked down at the tiny, murine creature, blinked once, looked around herself, and then looked down again.

Maus Blinked onto the small table to be at a more comfortable viewing distance.

“You are real?” she asked.

“Yep. Are you ready to go?”

“I – what?”

“Are you ready to go?” Maus asked again, Blinking onto the vulpian’s shoulder and causing her head to jerk backwards, startled.

“Go where?”

“Away from here.”

For the first time, the prisoner really took in that the door was open. Not only that, but anyone who would’ve impeded their escape was unconscious.

She could just leave.

Except–

“No. I’m not sure who you are, but I shall not be going away with you until I know your objective. I’ll not be substituting one prison for another.”

Maus wondered briefly if this was what the Mother was talking about when she said some people would cling on to a life they weren’t truly living. Though he supposed he couldn’t fault her caution.

Unfortunately, his plan hadn’t accounted for the prisoner just refusing to go with him. Truly, first contact mortality rates were formidable.

“My objective is escape,” Maus said.

“Truly?”

“Yep. I just gotta get you out of this prison,” he continued.

“You’d be willing to sign a mana document to that effect?” she pressed. Maus paused.

“If I can write it, yeah. Else, I’m not allowed to sign things by myself,” Maus said gravely.

For a moment, the Administrator’s expression softened with something like amusement.

“I see,” she said. Then she looked around at the cell, the naked, passed out guards, and back to the mouse. “I’ll tell you what, if you can get these cuffs off, and you have a way to get us past the soul–ward shield, I’ll let you draft our agreement."

“Deal, probably,” Maus said. “What’s a soul–ward shield?”

“It’s a type of forcefield that prevents living creatures from passing through a certain area. Anything with a soul will be stopped. Different shields have different effects. The one around the prison is set to kill. It even accounts for incorporeality and teleportation. How did you get in without encountering it?”

“Oh! The glowing death field.” Maus bobbed his head. “I walked through. Don’t worry. I’m pretty sure I can get us out of here. I might need your help though,” Maus said. He’d done it with Boss’s help, and then by himself, and now he was pretty sure he could do it with another person without Boss’s assistance.

“I beg your pardon?” asked the Administrator.

“No need to beg. Are you ready to go?”

“I– who are you?” she finally asked.

“Oh, I’m Maus.”

“Yes, I can see you’re a mouse.”

“I am a mouse,” Maus confirmed.

“Yes, I mean obviously. That’s not–” The vulpian shook her head. “Well met… Sir Mouse. I am Gelken. I regret to inform you I’ll be of minimal assistance.” She held up her arms, the metal cuffs glinting on their surface.

Maus tilted his head. “How do you get them off?”

Maus knew how the Boss had gotten hers off. He doubted the same would work for the vulpian. That super cool energy the Boss used just wasn’t there with him.

“The guards,” the Vulpian said. “They have a key.”

“Oh!”

Maus thought about all the things he’d stolen from the guards, then a pile of keys dropped at their feet.

The Vulpian blinked.

Then she shook her head and picked out something that looked less like a key to Maus and more like a small rectangular cuboid, about half the size of a paying card and only a couple centimetres thick. Protruding from the top was a smaller cube the same thickness as the base.

The Vulpian slotted the device into a small, square indent in each cuff. The enchantments on both the cuffs and the ‘key’ glowed faintly before the heavy, metal suppressors popped open with a gentle clink.

Once that was done, Maus scooped everything – suppressors and ‘key’ included – into storage, before pulling out a mana document, and drafting a fairly simple agreement that amounted to ‘I haven't lied to you, yet, so you’ll cooperate, and we’ll make a break for it.’

Then it was time to leave.

Still on Gelken’s shoulder, the two headed towards the exit at a loping run. There was no real point in Blinking ahead when he’d just have to wait for the vulpian at the glowing death field.

Just as they were approaching the prison doors, Gelken stopped.

“What’s wrong?” Maus asked.
