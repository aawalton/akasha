“What do you mean? I’ve got nothing valuable on me.” The man shook his head immediately. The atmosphere grew tense—especially for Nephrin, caught in the middle. What was this man thinking, suddenly acting like a street thug shaking down villagers? But she believed Jinyo wasn’t being irrational. How could someone in the top ten of the teaching license exam be stupid?

“Mr. Southerncross, could you explain in more detail? You’re giving people the wrong impression.”

“I know all of you have most likely collected purple berry-like fruits in your bags—about a handful each. I know you see them as something valuable. But they’re actually dangerous.” Jinyo had been using Bloodline Inference to scan for Cursed Fruit the entire time. No one within his radius escaped detection. Everyone’s bags contained no fewer than twenty Cursed Fruits.

Cursed Fruit wasn’t abundant, but it wasn’t hard to find in the Cursed Forest either.

In everyone’s eyes, the Cursed Fruit was special produce of great value. If sold outside, it would fetch a fortune. Even in this era where cultivating special produce outside of dungeons was possible, certain products still couldn’t be bred. Fruits with special powers that accelerated cultivation were in extremely high market demand.

Most people here were rookie Hunters who hadn’t even passed their licensing exam yet. Taking special fruit back to sell in the outside world would provide them with income.

“What are you talking about? Why should we give anything to you?” The man frowned. He was displeased by Jinyo’s attitude. What right did he have to take money from them? This was no different from robbery. In that instant, some of them gripped their weapons tighter. They had seen Jinyo’s prowess. If Jinyo chose to use force, they’d have no choice but to fight to the death.

“That’s right—we picked this fruit ourselves. It belongs to us.”

“If you’re going to take our money, we might as well fight to the death right here and now.”

“I’m dirt poor. I don’t even have decent weapons like you people. This fruit could save my life!” Voices of protest thundered. Jinyo was pushed to the opposite side of the majority. Nephrin was caught in an uncomfortable position. She needed to maintain her image and reputation in society if she wanted a career in the education industry. If her reputation was damaged, her future would suffer. But Jinyo was also her benefactor. Compared to him, these people had just stood on the sidelines watching and only showed up after everything was over.

“Everyone, please calm down. Mr. Southerncross must have a very important reason. Please settle down and hear him out—consider it a favor to me.” Nephrin stood up to smooth things over. She turned and blinked at Jinyo, signaling him—she’d calmed the crowd, now hurry up and talk!

“I’m… Jinyo Southerncross. I’m a botanist. I have a PhD in Plant Science. I know this is hard to accept, but the purple berry-like fruits in this forest—while not poisonous—contain something terrifying.” Jinyo chose to reveal his background. When everyone heard this, they went blank for a moment. This man had that high an education? Most of them had only finished high school before entering the workforce. They couldn’t afford to put themselves through college. Why else would they be trying to become Hunters? Standing next to Jinyo, they were sparrows before a phoenix.

“Dangerous?!” Nephrin grew uneasy the moment she heard. She had picked and eaten the purple berry-like fruits found in the Cursed Forest to stave off hunger. They’d helped her recover Planar nearly twice as fast. Running didn’t tire her as quickly, and they even boosted her spell attack power. She’d stuffed her bag full—though some had been damaged during fights—she’d still eaten quite a few herself.

“Long-term danger. They help wounds heal faster, recover power more quickly, increase physical strength, and make skills more effective. But the price you pay isn’t worth it at all. It’s not boosting your power—it’s borrowing power from your future.” Jinyo wished he had a whiteboard to write his explanation. He scratched his head until his scalp was raw. Multiple pairs of eyes locked onto him. It made Jinyo nervous—a habit from having to present research to his university professors.

“You’ve probably noticed that the monsters here—both the pumpkin-heads and the giant deer—have incredible regeneration. You can’t kill them unless you decapitate them. That’s because they’ve consumed enormous quantities of this fruit and gained a skill called [Boost]. Boost is a curse that converts lifespan into power. If you eat it, your body ages faster. The more you eat, the faster you age.”

“Age faster! Does that mean I’m going to die?!”

“Oh, no.” Everyone panicked. The more they ate, the faster they recovered power and stamina. They hadn’t even counted how many they’d consumed. What good was strength gained at the cost of a shortened lifespan?

“Doctor, you have a cure, right?”

“If you know what it is, you must know how to treat it, right? Please, Doctor—save our lives!” Everyone’s attitude flipped completely. Jinyo was swarmed, and it made him uncomfortable.

“Please, everyone, give me your Cursed Fruit. If possible, I’d also like everyone to help gather as much as you can for me. I have a skill that examines the properties of produce. I want to find a fruit that can counteract the defect. Once we get back, I’ll find a way to help you. And please try not to eat any more. No matter how much you want to, you have to endure it.” Jinyo tried to calm everyone down. But even so, it was a hard thing to accept—that something so valuable was actually an evil thing slowly killing them.

“Why should we believe some random stranger? This is ridiculous.”

“Exactly. It’s just empty talk, isn’t it? Maybe it’s actually good medicine. That rich boy just wants free fruit.” Not everyone believed Jinyo’s words. In fact, some deliberately spoke loudly enough for others to hear. At first, learning the Cursed Fruit was severely dangerous, they’d panicked and listened to everything Jinyo said. But Jinyo had only spoken with empty words, hadn’t he?

Even if Jinyo really was a PhD in botany—who knew? Jinyo could be lying!

And yes—Jinyo was indeed lying. About claiming to already know about the Cursed Fruit.

Jinyo had read hundreds of books and still didn’t recognize the Cursed Fruit. Especially the Boost curse and the Boost status—without the system’s power, he wouldn’t have believed they existed at all. Jinyo still had no evidence to prove the purple berries were dangerous fruit that burned through lifespan. It was too difficult to convince people to believe.

Some people handed over their Cursed Fruit to Jinyo. But most still couldn’t bring themselves to part with something so valuable to a stranger. In the end, what they clung to was poison in the guise of greed. They couldn’t let go of the money. They chose to prioritize it over the risk to their lives.

“I hope no one is stubborn about this.” Jinyo desperately wished everyone would resist the temptation. The Cursed Fruit had the property [Addictive] 1-star. Jinyo didn’t know how severe that was. He prayed it was on the level of cannabis—not cocaine. Otherwise, the lives of those who’d eaten the Cursed Fruit would be extremely difficult. Because the more they consumed, the worse their bodies would become.

“Mr. Southerncross—no, Doctor. I thought you were fully focused on teaching. So you came to take the teaching exam as a secondary goal, didn’t you?” Nephrin smiled sweetly at the young man who had saved her. She reached out and grabbed Jinyo’s hand before he could shake her off. Jinyo grew flustered being so close to a beautiful woman—especially this woman, whom the system had flagged as a suitable candidate for a wife.

“I had problems in academic circles, so I came to work as an assistant teacher. Now I’m planning to open a sword school, so I needed the professional license.”

“The academic world and the education world have completely different exam content. For someone who studied a different field to score so highly on the teaching exam—that’s truly admirable. At the very least, I couldn’t manage being excellent in botany, education, and swordsmanship all at once. Just education and magic are enough to keep me at full capacity.”

Nephrin liked capable people. Especially smart, capable people. Compared to Theo, who was like an unhinged lunatic from the countryside without a functioning brain, Nephrin found Jinyo—someone she could actually converse with in the same language—far more appealing.

Morning arrived along with fog thicker than usual. But strangely, despite the dense fog, the area surrounding Nephrin and Jinyo remained clearly visible. They could even see someone sitting under a tree roughly two hundred meters away.

Jinyo was certain he had already dispelled the Otherworldly Domain. All that remained was the fire still burning through the trees. Nephrin narrowed her eyes because she’d noticed the same anomaly.

“Ms. Nephrin, did you use any magic yesterday? I mean during the daytime when the fog was thick.”

“No. I managed to avoid monsters the whole time, so I barely had to fight during the day.”

“Would you like to try firing a fireball during the day?”

“I was actually just thinking the same thing.” Nephrin gathered Planar and compressed it in the air. She converted its properties into fire. The young woman didn’t fire the fireball as described. Instead, she walked into the dense fog—into the area that should have swallowed her from view. But Jinyo and the others could still see her.

“105 meters… 110 meters… 115 meters… 120 meters… At what distance could you no longer see me?”

Nephrin walked back to ask.

“110 meters.”

“That means fire can repel the fog. A flame this size repels the fog about ten meters. Which means this fog isn’t natural—it’s some kind of magic that can be countered with fire.” Nephrin had made a breakthrough. Living in the Cursed Forest was incredibly difficult. During the day, visibility was severely restricted. Shouting carried only within a hundred meters. Without walking up close, you couldn’t know what was waiting a hundred meters ahead.

But once they solved the fog problem, the skill that gave humans superiority over other creatures would be fully restored.

The skill of communication!

Humans relied on complex vocal language communication far beyond what mere beasts could produce. The reason so many people had died here was that no one could help each other or cooperate because they couldn’t hear one another.

“Would it be possible to set fires at various points to draw people together into one location?” Jinyo offered his idea. He wanted to find Naho and Rina. Luciria also wanted to find Sven and James. Without Nephrin’s fire signal during the night, there would have been no way to gather this many people in one spot.

“My magic power is limited. My Planar control range is only fifty to a hundred meters. Beyond that, I can’t sustain the flames.”

“I see. Then we’ll need another method. I do have one in mind.”

“???” Nephrin wondered what Jinyo was going to do. Was he going to have her burn the whole forest and let the fire spread? That was far too reckless.

“I’ll make torches.”

“Torches? Those things only last a few minutes before going out, don’t they? Ideally they’d need to burn for several hours.”

“Don’t underestimate natural torches. When I had to explore deep forest zones, one torch lasted more than two hours. The longest I ever managed burned for a full five hours.”

“Five hours! A natural torch?!” Nephrin couldn’t believe it was possible. They’d been pulled into this Hollow Gate completely unprepared. They had no materials to craft any magical items. And Jinyo was going to build a torch lasting more than two hours out of nothing?

Jinyo entrusted his son, Luciria, and the others to Nephrin’s care and set off on his own. He needed to find materials for the torches.

The Cursed Forest was a tropical forest. There was a high likelihood of finding what he needed. Jinyo activated Bloodline Inference and expanded its radius beyond 500 square meters.

“System, search for genetic traits.” Jinyo input the details through the system. He’d been working with it long enough to know how to use it properly. For instance, if he specified a search for banana trees, the system would ignore all other trees except banana trees. The moment he walked near one, the system would alert him to the discovery.

Jinyo just kept running until Bloodline Inference found the tree he was looking for. It seemed luck was on his side. Jinyo discovered trees standing twenty to fifty meters tall, lined up in rows. It was the species Jinyo had been searching for—nicknamed the “Gas Station of the Forest.”

Resin trees. The most flammable timber species in the Southeast Asian region. Their resin caught fire easily, but best of all was the heartwood inside—once dried, it became a supreme firestarter that burned for hours.

The problem was the creatures loitering around the Resin trees.

They were all Eltharoks. These beasts that normally didn’t gather in herds—what were they doing hanging around there?

Jinyo watched them for a while. He observed their behavior. One Eltharok took a strange-looking purple stick that Jinyo didn’t recognize, dipped it into the resin pooled in a hollow of the Resin tree, and lit it on fire. Then it inhaled the smoke into its lungs.

These lunatics… they were smoking.
