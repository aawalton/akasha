Ana listened rivetingly as Cass described the defense of Liora. The Dark Guilds, the monsters. The battle at the river and the chase, too. He also spoke of his family, of Gary, Bella, and Adya. Of his friends, Pellin and Orla as well, even if the older woman didn’t take a shine to his merchant friend.

“Too much like those here.”

When he got to the point of being chased across the city, monsters in his wake, she held her breath, letting it out quickly when she learned of Adya leap-spearing the Driftclaw over his head. He finished up by mentioning he’d blacked out, only to wake up and find himself approaching Liberty.

But that isn’t what she focused on, “That’s a good woman you have there.”

When he looked confused, she clarified, “Adya.”

“I don’t know if I’d say I have her. More along the lines of we work well together and we’re friends.”

She rolled her eyes, “Confidence is key, young man. With it, you can do anything. Now, let me give you a little of our history.”

Cass ate as Ana told him about the city’s dealings with monsters. According to her, Liberty had never been attacked by Dark Guilds. Not in all the years since its founding. But they did get something else.

She called them Monster Trains.

Every few months, a huge collection of monsters would gather up and invade the outer-reaches of the city. There was no rhyme or reason as to why it occurred. It was, for all intents and purposes, out of nowhere.

Even though it was considered random, the same situation occurred every time. Farms needed resowing. Funerals had to be carried out. And plans for what to do next time came from the Guild.

But, like always, Liberty carried on.

After Cass inquired, she explained that there was a warning system with strategically placed bells along the furthest edge of the city, but sometimes it came too late. Then, tragedy and hand-wringing occurred.

Cass didn’t like the sound of it, but until he felt the breath of the city on the back of his neck, it was all theoretical. He needed to get out there.

Ana left not long after. She excused herself by saying the kitchen needed a little cleaning. But she didn’t exit the room without speaking one final time, “Why did the Quest have to start with pushups?”

“Maybe that’s the easy starter?”

She snorted, closing the door behind her.

Then, Cass was back in the second ring. Here, everything was named and numbered. Moving in a circle, everything in the south and moving counter-clockwise made logical sense. Private Bivouacs #1, then two, three, and so on. Bowyer#1. Armorer#1.

But just because things were logical didn’t mean they were fun to look at. Sure, he could guess, just based on the number, what direction something was, but it created a stale environment. Cities were meant to be wonders that grew organically, not whatever this was.

That’s the Lioran in me. I’m used to haphazard design and randomized shops. There’s nothing wrong with a little more organization.

He placed another Field Standard down, this one for Generals' Quarters#2, only the second he’d seen.

Then again, this is plain ugly. If I were building my own city, I’d probably set up sectors and efficient spacing, but then let the citizens decide things from there. Every part of the city could use a nearby carpenter and a restaurant, rather than bunching them up in specific areas.

Liora isn’t perfect, but Liberty is also far from it. Very far.

And maybe this is hubris, but I’d make sure everyone has autonomy to make real decisions. Liberty is too structured, Liora, too chaotic in design. A blend of both could make a place that felt like a living thing.

He spent a moment thinking of what his hypothetical city would be called, a simple break for his mind, then moved on to the first and final ring. Each was far enough apart to have its own Anchor, so that’s just what he did. By the time he was finished, what was left of his food had grown cold at least an hour ago. He finished it off and decided on something important.

“I love steak.”

His happy feeling from having a full stomach faded away as he looked at the tower still awaiting him. He’d done the boring part of labeling every location; now it was time to break down the day’s Quests. Taking a notepad and pen from his bag, he placed them down on the Annex, then opened the first one up.

“Delivery of dried flowers to the Bowman Residence in the third ring. I don’t see that being repeated often, but if I’m doing this, I’m doing it right.” Saying so, he made a note in the pad.

And so it went. It wasn’t exciting work, nor was it fun. But it was the kind of necessary, logical step that Cass had come to appreciate over time. Each logged Quest was put aside, its contents entered into the notepad, and then the next arrived. He hadn’t done any drafting yet. This was purely about understanding his Quest-load.

Rather than specify the delivered materials as the grouping category, he leaned into the departure location. Working alphabetically after assigning several pages to each ring, Cass left several blank lines after each named location so he could fill them out more as he went.

The whole point and purpose of this was deciding which Field Standards to Anchor, and which not to. He likely wouldn’t know much by today, but as he completed each day’s work and got a better idea of what was required, everything would become easier. He hoped.

Once everything was logged, he found only one location used repeatedly for the day.

“Liberty Loaders C in the fourth ring, why are you so busy?”

According to his notes, the location was used 20 times in a single day for Quests that often said close to the same thing. Even more curious, each assigned Questor was a different person, but always with those same three letters in front of their name.

Then Cass slapped himself upside the head, “P-V-T means Private. I need to remember that.”

Moving through the stack still wasn’t fast, but it was faster. Now that every highlighted location had its own marked Field Standard, looking something up was much faster. And more than that, he’d taken the time to go through each location and learn about where it was in the city and what it looked like.

Obviously, it wouldn’t transfer as perfectly as if he were there in-person, but that’s what Profile was for. It had been ticking away all along, taking up a small part of his vision. Tapping on the ability, he searched Liberty Loaders C from the now hundreds of entries.

Liberty Loaders C:

Location: A large depot located in the northern quadrant of Liberty’s fourth ring.

Known Quest assignments: Outgoing deliveries of flour, rice, beans, iron ore, silver ore, treated timber, and wooden planks.

Visit the location to add additional information…

“If only,” Cass said in a glum voice.

He worked and worked until he felt like every Quest was what the Senior QuestWright had asked for. They weren’t perfect, but as he’d realized in Liora, the more he practiced something, the better he would be.

It was also the single largest amount of Quests he’d drafted in a single day that didn’t have the added pressure of potential deaths in the mix. Cass knew he should feel a small sense of pride in the fact, but just by where he was, he didn’t.

That didn’t matter, though. Because it was all a part of his grand plan. Creating a few templates that he thought might help with the Loader locations, Cass’s internal thoughts continued at speed.

Every Quest gives me a certain amount of System Reputation. And, more than that, a couple of Minor nodes I unlocked in Logistics protect me from a few failed Quests each day. If I keep that in mind, I can build close to 150 System Reputation before the next series of Quests needs to be made.

That’s a lot of reputation every day. Not as much as I get from killing monsters, but at least I don’t have to worry about dying.

And these Quests have a purpose. If I can increase my Reputation enough, maybe the System will increase the number of Quests I can do in a day. Then, I can head out into the city and try to help with a bit of goodwill.

That’s what I need to focus on. That’s my mission here.

It’ll all be worth it if I can help people and find a way to matter again.

Setting the template for dry foodstuffs, he started another focused on ore, with a plan to swap a few items as needed. The hours moved by at a crawl as Cass finished up the templates, then completed all of the Quests for Liberty Loaders C.

After that, he moved on to the next Quest. And the next. And the next after that.

By the time every Quest was almost completed, he could see letters and icons anytime he closed his eyes. They were always there…waiting. Watching.

He shook that thought away and refocused.

The early evening hours pushed themselves far away before he was done. At what he figured was past midnight, Cass stood up, a tower of completed Quests in his hands. There was a small satisfaction to completing it, but the time necessary to get everything finished had been obscene.

Carefully opening the door, Cass stepped out of the room and into the intake room. Head collapsed on his arms across a desk labeled Deliveries, Quirrel’s quiet snores filled the room. He looked peaceful, which made Cass feel a little bad about what he would do in a moment.

Moving as far from the man as he could, Cass dropped the stack of Quests down on the table enough to jitter it. Quirrel didn’t budge.

Frowning, Cass tried again, lifting and dropping his many hours of work on the table in the hope that it would wake the poor Clerk up. No dice.

Sighing, Cass tapped his shoulder, “Quirrel.”

“Huh, what? Oh, Cass! You scared the Bejesus out of me. What time is it?”

Cass ran a hand through his hair, “I don’t know, sorry. I only just now finished up.”

Quirrel flicked an arm out of his robed sleeve and looked at his watch, “My goodness, it’s 1 a.m. Please tell me this isn’t your standard and you were much faster in your home guild.”

“Much,” Cass confirmed with a nod, “as I said, I’m trying to keep this to a one-time deal.”

“Well, let’s hope so.” He tilted his head, “Please stay here a moment, I need to go over these.”

Cass patiently stood there as Quirrel went through every Re-Quest and Quest individually. Four times, he put aside a folder, then pulled the vellums out of each and swapped them around. Doing a second run through the stack, he nodded.

It took twenty minutes.

“Okay, we’re all set. Please make sure all of your Quests and Re-Quests match up in the future. We wouldn’t want to give the wrong Quest to the wrong Questor!”

“Sorry, I have no idea how I did that.”

“Not a problem, not a problem.” He yawned to the air, “I’m gonna head off to bed. You all set?”

“Yep,” Cass nodded, “Any tips for how the Quests read compared to what you’ve seen in the past?”

“Mmm, you’re a little nicer in your drafts than I’m used to, but your vocabulary needs a lot of work. Lots of repeated words.”

Cass laughed, “I think I’ve heard something like that before. Alright, Quirrel. Thank you. I’ll be out of here in a moment, just have to grab my things.”

“Alrighty. Good night, Cass.”

“G’night Quirrel.”

Cass headed back to his bright, lonely room. His Bella ping hadn’t moved, but he could tell for a surety that she was closer now than she’d been when he first arrived. That made him think of something while he stood at the edge of his desk.

Tapping into Profile, Cass searched out Adya’s name.

Adya Korring: Profile Register Administrator.

Note: Administrator’s information cannot be displayed.

Cass sat down with a whoosh of air, his legs giving out on him as a swell of relief crashed upon his shoulders. “That’s confirmation. She’s definitely alive.”

He hadn’t expected so much tension in his body to release all at once. It was like a mountain had fallen off of him and he could finally breathe again. Then, a thought arrived.

“Maybe she’s talked to some of the others. If she has, then Profile should be updated.”

First, Cass went to his mother, father, and Janine. None of theirs had changed, though. He moved on to Gary.

A huge number of additions made their way to his eyes, including one special note at the very top.

Location: Currently traveling long-distance on horseback to an unknown location.

Wait…what? Where could Gary be heading right now, and he doesn’t know how to ride a horse.

A gear rotated in his mind with a click.

“There's…there’s no way Gary and the rest are coming here, right?” He pulled up Mr. Moore and Kara’s information next, but there was nothing strange in there about their locations. Orla and Pellin, however, both had the same notes.

“They’re all coming? But how do they know to find me?” It took him only a moment to figure it out. “I told Adya that I could sense Bella’s location. They’re using her like some kind of bloodhound. Holy shit. But it’s incredibly dangerous out there. Noah said there were monsters everywhere.”

Hanging his head at the thought of his friends dying in their attempt to reach him, he only had to hope that, if he was right, and they really were heading his way, they were doing so in a safe manner.

Adya and crew on the road…

Adya sprinted as fast as she could. The Tier-gate Quest was almost complete, but that wasn’t an immediate thought in her mind as the wind rushed past her ears. Trailing her only a few hundred feet away was a huge group of toad-like monsters. She hadn’t come upon them unaware; there had been a plan.

She would attack them quickly, then run away. Based on the clear blood leaking from the lead monster, she’d at least done a little damage. But she hadn’t thought about how fast the fat monsters could move.

Screaming at the top of her lungs, she waved her arms in the air as Graceful Steps took her to her friends, “Let’s go, fast fast fast. Monsters incoming!”

Orla looked at her with dead eyes, “More! Why do you keep doing this! First, you used up most of our bandages on your arm after taking a nasty bite from some small-”

“Shut up and move!” Gary bellowed. They’d taken a break for the horses and a moment to rest. Now, adrenaline took the wheel as everyone leaped up, packs already slinging onto their backs again.

Gary winced as Champion started at a trot, “God, this hurts so much.”

Adya, already astride Bella, looked his way, “You have to move with the horse, not against it.”

“That makes no sense. Besides, you spend most of your time running, not riding.”

“It’s good exercise.”

Pellin’s voice called from the rear, “Umm, not to be the bearer of bad news, but they’re coming up fast behind us.”

“What is!” Orla yelled back.

“Some kind of thing.”

All four of them heard the creature trailing them as it called out, “Wraugh!”.

“Such a helpful description, Pell!”

“Keep going!” Adya screamed, “They won’t be able to match us for long.” Leaning forward, she let Bella pick up the pace. The draft-horse’s hooves ate up the ground as they continued down a dirty, decrepit road.

They didn’t slow until the monsters were far, far behind them. Then, Adya hopped off of Bella, just as Gary jumped off of Champion. Mounting the draft-horse, he looked to the back of the group.

“Pellin, you’re next on Bella, no complaints.”

“She keeps trying to bite me.”

“Then stop pissing her off, she’s fine with me,” Orla commented. “Besides, it takes some of the burden off the other horses. Bella’s just different, accept it.”

“She hates me.”

“So do I, but you’re still going to do it. We’re eating a lot of distance this way.”

A weak “Fine” came from the System Engineer, then Gary looked down at Adya.

“Ready?”

She nodded, “Let’s go.”

Adya activated Graceful Steps again, her feet eating up the ground as she moved as efficiently as possible. She pulled up the Quest for the dozenth time since getting it, sliding it to the side so it wouldn’t impact her vision.

[SCOUT TIER UPGRADE QUEST PROGRESS]

Required objectives:

Enter an active threat zone alone and gain vital intelligence: 1/1

Observe over one-thousand individuals: 1000/1000

Travel over 1000 miles on foot and by traversal abilities: 913/1000

Track a high-threat entity for over thirty minutes without engagement: 1/1

Slay ten monsters: 10/10

Optional objectives:

Fight while outnumbered and withdraw safely: 3/3

Correctly identify disguised threats: 2/2

Protect bonded partner from immediate danger: 1/1

Reach Level 50 in your Observation skill: 34/50

Fight while injured: 2/2

System note: All experience is held in abeyance until the Tier Upgrade Quest is complete.

She knew she wouldn’t get Observation all the way up in time, but that was a small price to pay for reaching the second tier. As Dev had taught her, she continued to sprint while working on her breathing.

In and out, that was all that mattered.
