“Ah, so you know me,” Prator said with a chuckle. “But I haven’t the pleasure. What is your name?”

“I need to report to a foreman,” Elion said. “I found something.”

“I rescind all your prior orders,” Prator said. He held up Elion’s Starholder pendant. “I hold your Bondhold. Tell me, truthfully, what your name is.”

“Elion.” Elion’s heart thundered in his chest.

“And Elion, where is the girl? Venya told me she’s looking for two of you?”

“I don’t know,” Elion said, locking his eyes onto Prator’s, trying not to show his surprise that the man didn’t know where Keyla was. “How did you get my Bondhold?” Elion asked.

“When I heard what happened here, I sent someone to purchase it,” Prator said with a shrug. “Am I right in assuming that you are the Aurelian Venya is searching for?”

“I might be,” Elion said. “I can’t speak for Venya.”

“She told me to be on the lookout for an Aurelian Knight. She also said you might be traveling with a girl. She didn’t tell me you were just a boy.”

“Are you going to turn me over to her?” Elion asked. He didn’t want Prator thinking too hard about Keyla. If he could keep the man’s focus away from her, maybe she could escape whatever Venya intended.

“Venya told me that you were both connected to the army that’s out there,” Prator continued. “She said she also had an interest in you, and that we might work together to see justice done. But I think this was a partial truth, at best. So who are you really, and what does Venya want with you?”

“I’m Elion. I honestly can’t say I know why Venya wants me.”

“Don’t play dumb, boy,” Prator ordered. “I want to know what is going on around here. I don’t know if I can trust Venya. As a fellow Knight of Dawn, I feel I owe it to you to hear your side of the story. What are you doing here?”

“I am—I’m looking for someone,” Elion said, his mind racing. He tried to figure out how to withhold information from Prator, while simultaneously wondering if he may have inadvertently found an ally. Maybe he should just tell him the truth.

“Who?” Prator demanded. “Who are you looking for?”

“My…” Elion’s mind raced through the possibilities. He could honestly claim to be looking for many different people. Keyla’s mother, Keyla, Liora, or Zev. Each would reveal something different about him.

“…my mentor,” Elion said. “I’m looking for my mentor.”

“Your mentor?” Prator stroked his beard. He did not pursue that line of questioning further. “Some of my people reported a skyskimmer going down and exploding out on the plains a few days ago. Venya showed up shortly thereafter, and, though she did not say as much, I believe the explosion was her skyskimmer. Did you have anything to do with that?”

“Yes,” Elion said.

“What happened?” Prator asked.

“She was chasing me,” Elion said. “And I destroyed its Argent Drive. The explosion helped me get away.”

“Get away where?”

“Down into the tunnels.”

“And what did you do there?”

“Wandered,” Elion said with a shrug. “Until we were ambushed and enslaved.”

Prator grimaced at that. “I prefer the term ‘bonded,’” he said. “Where were you before you escaped from Venya. Her prisoner?”

“No," Elion said. "I was not her prisoner."

"Where were you then?" Prator asked. "What were you doing?"

"I was hiding," Elion said.

"Tell me," Prator said. "How long have you been a Knight for? Who trained you? Where did you get your education?"

"Nobody trained me," Elion said. "I have not been a Knight for long."

"How long?" Prator asked.

"Only a matter of weeks," Elion said.

"Where did you swear your oaths?"

"In Aterfel."

"Is that where you were then, in Aterfel, hiding from Venya, swearing your oaths? Why did you swear your oaths there? Why did you not have a mentor? Who are you really?"

“I told you my name. Elion.”

“Elion who? Where do you come from Elion? Answer me truthfully.”

“My name is Elion Walker and I come from Earth.”

“I've never heard of Earth. I'm getting tired of you playing games with me. So tell me, what does Venya want with you? What are you doing here in New Kairn Tol?”

Elion shifted uncomfortably. He looked at the man lounging on the large piece of machinery. He had to tell him the truth.

“My name is Elion Starholder,” Elion said. “My mother was Aria.”

“You are the missing heir to the throne?” Prator asked, eyes widening in surprise. “You? Where have you been all this time? Now I understand why Venya's so interested in finding you.”

Elion tried to meet the man’s gaze. Prator smiled, but the smile was not kind. It was a smile of

a predator investigating its prey.

“If you are the heir to the throne,” Prator said, “Venya has not offered me a sufficient price for your capture. And I believe that we Aurelians should stick together. So I think I'll keep you for myself.”

“If you’re going to turn me over to Venya, just do it,” Elion said. “I’m tired of this.”

"Ah," Prator said, "Now we are getting somewhere. I don’t just want to hand you over. I want to work with you. Look, you’re going to tell me what I want to know, one way or another.” Prator tapped the Starholder pendant. “So stop playing games with me. We can work together on this. You don’t have to fight me. I could be an ally. Trust me, you don’t want Venya to get her hands on you.”

Elion considered Prator's offer. Everything he had seen and heard about this man made him suspicious. But the man was an Aurelian. He had sworn oaths. Perhaps he could teach him something. If anything, stalling would be better than simply letting the man turn him over to Venya and Dorian.

Elion sighed. "I was born on a planet called Earth,” he said. “My mother, Aria, and her brother, Zev, fled there to get away from my uncle Dorian." Elion paused, wondering if Prator knew about Liora. Elion decided to leave her out of the story.

“Earth,” Prator mused. “Is it one of the outer colonies?”

“No,” Elion said. “I don’t think so. It’s very far.”

“It is where your mother fled to after The Breaking of The Sky?”

“Yes,” Elion said. “I lived my whole life there. I knew nothing of Kylios until Dorian showed up. He killed my family and tried to kidnap me. In the process I fell through his portal and ended up in Aterfel.”

“Kidnap you?” Dorian asked. “Why kill your mother but not you? You are now the only one standing between him and the Throne.”

“I can’t tell you what he’s thinking,” Elion said. “All I know is that I ended up in Aterfel. While I was there, I found the Aurelian Altar. I thought that having Aurelian powers might help me evade Dorian for longer. So I swore the oaths, became a Knight, and I tried to help the people of Aterfel.”

“So,” Prator mused. “Zev and Aria, also dead at Dorian’s hand. You are all that remains.”

Elion quirked an eyebrow at Prator’s assumption that Zev had died, but did not correct the man.

“Did you have something to do with that explosion?” Dorian asked.

A vision sprang into Elion’s mind, Domas, crumbling to pieces as he drove the bomb into the distortion field of the crystal shard.

“You know about the crystal shard?” Elion asked. “The infected?”

“I have heard of trouble in that direction. We always watch out for danger on the horizon.”

“The people there were preparing to fight back. They built the bomb. I helped them fight past the infected and deliver it to the crystal shard.”

“You lost men,” Prator said. "You have the sad gleam of innocence in your eye. You think you can make a difference in the world, without paying the cost."

“People died,” Elion said, fighting a lump in his throat. “I only knew them for a short time.” Elion marveled at how short it had really been.

“You really are Aria’s son,” Prator said. “The lost heir to the throne.”

Elion nodded. “I am Aria’s son.”

“In that case, we need to figure something out. Venya is searching for you, and it would be in both of our interest to keep you away from her. A lot of the workers here at this dig witnessed your little fight, but I think we can compel them to keep quiet about it.”

Prator’s eyes twinkled as he twirled Elion’s bondhold. “I think we can work together. I’ll help you, you help me. What do you say?”

Elion scratched his head, running dirty hands through his hair. He looked around at the pit, then back to Prator, who still held Elion's bond hold dangling from its chain. Prator gave the star a flick, and watched it shimmer as it twisted.

"What choice do I have in the matter?" Elion asked. "You can compel me. So why bother asking?"

"I've never been the biggest fan of compelling people,” Prator said. “They always find a way to weasel out of orders eventually. I’d rather have an honest effort to work together.”

"What if I decline?" Elion asked. "If I do not agree to work with you?"

“If we are unable to come to an agreement, I will simply turn you over to Venya. I will be deeply offended that she did not reveal your true identity to me, and will demand a much higher reward though, now that I know who you are."

“So you will use the Bondhold,” Elion said.

“Not if I don’t have to.”

“Will you free me from it? Give my pendant back to me, as a show of goodwill?”

Prator laughed. “Elion,” he said. “I hold all the cards. You are the one who should be offering gestures of good will. I will retain this Bondhold. Call it insurance. I promise not to use it if I don’t have to.”

He looked at the star-holder pendant in his hand and nodded approvingly. "This actually makes sense. I was curious as to where you would have received a pendant like this."

“It was my mother’s,” Elion said.

“Well, what will it be?” Prator asked. “Me or Venya?”

Elion looked at the walls of the pit. He really didn't have a choice here, did he? Bond hold or not, Prator had him trapped.

"Fine," Elion said. "Suppose I work with you. What do we do now?"

"First, we go examine whatever it is that you found in that tunnel. And if you are willing, perhaps I can provide a bit of instruction to you about your Aurelian power.” Prator dismounted from the machine and walked across the pit toward Elion. "I trust that I will be safe in your presence?" Praetor asked. "You won't try fighting with me, or forcibly taking your Bondhold from me?"

Elion shook his head. "I imagine that you could easily deal with me if I tried," Elion said.

Prator laughed. "Ah, a good assessment. Well, you're not stupid, so that's good at least. Come now, show me what you found."

Elion led Prator back to the tunnel that he had dug.

Prator acquired a lamp and carried it with him. Crouching, he scrambled back to shine the light on the portion of exposed statue that Elion had dug up.

"You found it," Prator said. His mouth worked, wordlessly, disbelief coloring his face. “I mean, I knew it would be here somewhere, but you dug straight to it.”

Elion had nothing to say about this. He had done all his digging in an emotionally distressed, bond-compelled state. He hadn’t understood his own actions.

"Perhaps it's your sense, your new Aurelian knighthood," Prator said, rubbing his beard. "Maybe it called out to you. If I had been here, it would have called out to me. I could have directed the digging better. I never considered that option though.”

Elion remembered how back on earth he’d felt the call of Aurelia. “Do all Altars call to Knights?” he asked.

“No,” Prator said. “It’s something generally only mentioned in legends. Aurelia herself, coming to a weary Knight to guide him on the Path of Dawn…” Prator trailed off, lost in his own thoughts.

Elion considered making a move, trying to snatch his Bondhold from the man. He felt exhausted just thinking about it.

“Elion this is why you can trust me,” Prator said. “I may appear to be working for Venya and Dorian, but I am not. I walk a knife edge; very few Aurelians are allowed to live on Kylios these days. If Venya discovered my true purpose in digging this hole, I would be in terrible danger.”

“She doesn’t know about this dig?” Elion asked.

“She knows I’m digging. She thinks I’m looking for Thrandicite veins. It’s the mineral that they extract from the acid we ship them. There must be veins around here, because it has leached into the acid pits. In the solid form, it’s worth significantly more.”

“If she knew about this Altar,” Elion said. “What would she do?”

“Destroy it,” Prator said. “It is part of their war on our kind.”

Elion remembered the destruction of the Altar at Aterfel. “Why? Didn’t Dorian used to be an Aurelian?”

“Yes,” Prator said. “But he turned against us, and now he does whatever he can to destroy our Ascendency. I believe he will succeed. Aurelia has forsaken us.”

“Why search for the Altar then?” Elion asked.

“Because I have a lot of Ascendency Stones I’d like to use,” Prator said. “Dorian wants to keep us down, by preventing us from leveling. If I can level in secret—” he cut off, then looked Elion in the eye.

“Never mention this to anyone,” he said. “That’s an order. I shouldn’t be telling you my plan.”

“If we’re going to work together—”

“Need-to-know basis,” Prator said. He turned back to the statue. “This is the reason you can trust me. You know my secret now.” Placing a hand tenderly on the exposed arm, he took a deep breath.

“I appreciate you finding this for me,” Prator said. “Obviously I would have found it within a few weeks, at the current dig rate. But I appreciate your work nonetheless.”

“Why not use the Altar at Aterfel?” Elion asked. “Why do all of this?” he gestured.

“I need one in secret,” Prator said. “Aterfel only retains their altar because of its distance and obscurity. It served a purpose, attracting Aurelian’s into the open. Now that you have used that Altar and escaped, I doubt that it will stand for long.”

“It has already been destroyed,” Elion said.

Prator smiled sadly. “We will dig this one out. We will keep it a secret. Perhaps we can even hide you down here.”

Elion considered his lack of alternatives. “Okay. If we’re working together, then will you also mentor me? Help me to learn how to use my abilities?”

“I will,” Prator said.

A voice echoed down into the pit. “Prator, sir, are you there?”

“What is it?” Prator called back, brushing dirt from his clothes as he emerged from the tunnel Elion had dug.

Elion remained in the shadows.

“You’re going to want to come see this. The army is on the move.”
