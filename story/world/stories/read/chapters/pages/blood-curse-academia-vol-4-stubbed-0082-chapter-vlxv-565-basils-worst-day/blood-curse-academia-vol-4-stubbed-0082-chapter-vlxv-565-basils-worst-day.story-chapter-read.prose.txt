Chapter V.LXV (5.65) - Basil’s Worst Day

Kizu stepped into a forest with Mort once again perched on his shoulder and now with Ione at his side. As far as he could tell, the forest was unlike any other he’d seen before. It wasn’t the lush jungle of the Hon Basin or the dense woods that surrounded Shinzou Academy. The trees that grew here looked almost plain in comparison to what he knew. The underbrush was also scarce enough that he could stroll through the forest without any bushwhacking.

“Do you know where we are?” Ione asked.

“It should be Basil’s dream. For Mort, I went to the Hon Basin. For you, it was Shinzou Academy.”

“Basil grew up in Tross,” Ione suggested.

Kizu nodded. But he knew enough about Tross’s vegetation from brewing tomes to know this was not that. And he knew that before Tross, Basil lived on an entirely different continent.

Ilosin-Don.

His suspicions were confirmed when they stepped out of the forest and found themselves at the gates of a town. A town built for people half his height. Every structure appeared child-sized from the town walls to the buildings beyond them.

Despite her earlier comment, Ione clearly expected the same as Kizu, as she showed no sign of surprise at the sight of a wall she could easily touch the top of. Neither did she react to the guards stationed outside.

A gnome in wooden armor at the town gate eyed them with suspicion but allowed them entrance. In real life, Kizu suspected humans like him and Ione would be questioned by local security. This dream seemed to allow a bit of leeway for the unrealistic to smooth things out.

As they wandered through the dirt streets, they quickly realized an issue with seeking out Basil. They had no idea what form the shapeshifter would take in this dream. Recently, he’d settled into a male body that he wore most days, but that wouldn’t likely be the case here. After finding no sign of any human-sized individuals, they settled down in the town square to discuss their next move.

The majority of the buildings were made of wood, with stone only being applied to the foundations. The style, while obviously smaller than what Kizu was used to in Hon, also was unique in its design. The frames crisscrossed with timber support and the roofs were covered in layers of moss and grass. Trees, while sparser than in the actual forest, were scattered throughout the streets. With the proper illusions implemented, Kizu could see this entire town disappearing into lumps of moss in the forest.

Gnomes sold street food at nearby stalls. They appeared to mostly be mushroom-based, from what Kizu saw. He wondered if this town specialized in harvesting shrooms. That would be ironic, considering how they got here.

“He could be in the forest,” Ione suggested. “Originally, we appeared there, not here. Plus, who knows? We might encounter some strange Ilosin-Don monsters out there. Imagine getting to study foreign creatures nobody else has access to! New summons might help us.”

“Would that even work? Could you learn about new monsters through a dreamscape like this? Nothing here is real.” “I dunno. Would be interesting to try though.”

Rather than return to the forest, Kizu watched as a troupe of gnome performers stepped up onto a stage at the head of the town square. They bowed and introduced themselves. From the introductions, Kizu gathered these were simply gnome volunteers from across town. Still, gnomes happily meandered over and assembled to watch their performance.

It took only a few minutes into their performance before Kizu spotted an oddity. A boy off to the side was shaking and jittering. His skin seemed to be sloughing off a bit at the side. With everyone else focused on the performers, nobody paid his quiet breakdown any mind.

Rather than bring attention to himself by standing, Kizu silently asked Mort to go check on the boy. As the monkey crossed the town square, Kizu closed his eyes and connected to his familiar bond, taking in information through Mort’s senses.

“Everyone dies. They all die. I kill them. They’re gone. I did this. Everything is my fault. Tomorrow, so much death. This is the end. I can’t be here. I should die instead. But I can’t die. How could I do this? Why me? I don’t want this. Is it fate? No. I’m just weak. I should die. Kill myself. But I can’t. Why can’t I? I just want to die. Please, let me die. Save them all. End this.”

The quiet ravings of the boy went utterly unnoticed by the gnomes around him.

Even a decade younger, Kizu recognized the voice. Basil. His friend and the Ooze Harbinger. From the sounds of it, this was the final day before the Calamity invaded the continent. What might be a happy memory for some, was torture for him as he was forced to spectate the final moments of so many who’d perish in a brutal massacre hours from now.

The crowd erupted into laughter at an ironic joke on the stage, giving Kizu the chance to move without creating too much of a disturbance.

He took a seat next to the young Basil, who rocked back and forth, clutching his knees with his eyes transfixed on the stage.

“Basil,” Kizu said softy.

“I’m a blight. Evil. I only ever destroy. I don’t deserve happiness.”

“Basil,” Kizu repeated, this time firmer. It snapped his friend out of his trance and he looked at Kizu with one eye far too big for a gnome’s face. As he emotionally spiraled, he was losing control of his transformation.

“Who are you? You don’t belong here.”

“I’m your friend.”

Basil flinched at the word, like it was an insult.

“I don’t have friends. I have my fathers. And I have those I killed. There’s nothing else. No mother. No friends.”

“That’s not true,” Kizu said. “You have me.”

“I don’t know you.”

“You will though. This is a bad day, I know. But it will pass. You’ll have the chance to make things better in time.”

Basil shuddered and turned his face back to the performers up front. They were acting out a scene where they ran away from an illusionary bear. They ran in circles, their hands waving in the air as they comically cried out. The audience laughed once again. This performance was clearly a comedy.

“They won’t have time, though,” Basil said softly. “What good is my time, if they have none for themselves? I’m a thief. A parasite that feeds on their lives.”

“That’s not true. You never wanted these people to die. It’s not your fault that they did.”

“It is my fault. If I never existed—”

“No!” Kizu said firmly. “Don’t you dare take on the guilt of those who deserve it. Your kin killed these people, not you. You were a tool manipulated and tossed aside.”

“What does it matter? Dead is dead. They would be alive if I didn’t exist.”

“No. If you didn’t exist, the Oozes would have had another child with a human to create a Harbinger. It’s happened to every other continent, Ilosin-Don is no different.”

“You’re saying my existence doesn’t matter,” Basil said miserably. “So, I should just disappear.”

“You’re not listening to me.” Kizu took a deep breath and thought over how, exactly, to best break his friend’s misery.

For a few minutes they simply sat there, watching the gnome performance. They’d met a druid who’d helped them communicate with the angry bear. As it turned out, it simply wanted to be their friend and didn’t know how to express it. It was a very childish story. Which made sense, as half the audience looked like gnome children.

Mort, having decided to give Kizu and Basil space, darted amongst the stalls and listened to snippets of conversations. Not revolutionary information. Just the daily bits of their lives. All were blissfully ignorant of the tragedy that would soon befall them.

“They’ll die, no matter what,” Kizu said. “What comes next is up to you. Instead of taking on the burden of their deaths, take on the burden of their legacy. Make their lives mean something.”

“Burden of their legacy,” Basil repeated wistfully. “That’s so much responsibility.”

Kizu looked over to Ione, who stood off to the side, watching the performance’s finale. Her eyes were fixed on the illusionary bear as she silently mouthed something to herself, trying to comprehend something about it. She was simultaneously more alert and more relaxed than he’d seen her in ages. She looked… unchained. Happy.

Responsibility suffocated one friend while it brought life and meaning to the other.

“Yes,” Kizu said simply. “Will you accept their legacy? Or wallow?”

“I….” Basil’s form grew as he paused. He stood up, taller now. “I accept it. I’ll carry their legacy. More than that. I’ll make certain my fathers’s sins are never repeated.”

With that pledge, the gnome village faded.

Basil fully shed his childhood body and to the mass of his usual teenager form. He raised his hands to clutch at his head.

“Wait. Kizu? What….” He shook himself, then noticed the others standing in the white abyss that surrounded him. “Ione? Mort? Is this a dream?”

“It is. But we’re real and here to retrieve you,” Kizu said.

“Retrieve… me.” Basil’s eyes went wider than humanly possible, reverting to their earlier oversized state. “We fell into a trap! We’re in the World Dungeon right now!”

“Exactly.”

“How do we get out?”

As he asked the question, a door took shape out of the nothingness in front of them.

“This will lead us out of here?” Basil guessed.

“I wonder what will happen if there’s another person trapped by the shroom toxins,” Ione mused, stepping forward. “Will we end up in their dreamscape next?”

“Anyone else caught by this will likely have rotted away decades ago,” Kizu pointed out. “The World Dungeon is massive. The chances of us randomly stumbling across someone else are miniscule.”

“If you say so,” Ione said.

Together, the four of them stepped through the threshold and exited the dreamscape.
