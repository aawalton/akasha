“You sure you don’t want to use potions?” Álvaro asks.

“Don’t want to waste them on a sparring match,” I say, adjusting the goggles over my eyes. It’s nearly midnight, so the three of us have to wear goggles artificed with night vision spells to make out our surroundings. Cyros and I already had a pair of our own, due to all the Blackcloak work, but we grabbed a pair for Álvaro, too, before we left the city.

“This sparring match is already a waste,” Cyros grumbles, picking at his lunch. After several hours of pestering, he finally agreed that we could take the time to practice with our weapons when we paused for a lunch break. “You’ve got five minutes.”

“Five? Boooo.” Álvaro sticks his tongue out at Cyros. “You’re no fun.”

“You guys are going to make this trip take twice as long,” he shoots back.

I slip the felis claws over my knuckles as the two banter. Pressing a trickle of my mana into the claws, I feel the metal thrum to life beneath my fingers, latent magic shimmering in the blades.

There are three spells, though they all unfortunately consume mana to maintain, and I have extremely low mana reserves. I’d started out with a pathetic 10 points, but I’ve managed to slowly train it up to slightly-less-pathetic 30. In comparison, Álvaro has 75, and Cyros has 150. I find this annoying but unsurprising; they both have more magical ability than me.

The claws’ first spell is Cut. This makes the blades sharp enough to cut through rocks, which I desperately want to try out.

The second spell is Slash, which creates shimmering, immaterial claws that extend beyond the weapons another couple feet. It’s not anywhere as sharp as the Cut spell, but it gives me some range.

Finally—and most interestingly—is Imbue. This one allows the user to imbue the claws with one of their magic affinities; in my case, poison. I’m really curious to see what that looks like.

(And yes, I did double check I’ve got plenty of antidotes at the ready, just in case I poison Álvaro. Cyros would kill me if I sabotaged this mission by crippling our guide.)

Álvaro spins the spear around in his hands, smiling to himself as he twirls it like a bo staff. “Okay, maybe I should have tried to use a spear before now. This is kind of fun.”

Cyros watches him skeptically. “I thought you said you were proficient with a spear?”

“No, I have a Proficiency,” Álvaro clarifies. “Which means I can use it at a basic level, and level-up my skills with it the more I practice. Right now it’s only at level 1. But my whip Proficiency is level 15!”

“It’s a System thing,” I tell Cyros. It still feels weird to talk about this stuff out loud. Even with Iski and Gugora, they only knew I was from a different world. “Seems to help us learn skills faster than normal.”

Cyros lets out a disgusted sigh. “No wonder you were catching onto things so fast. I thought you were a natural—but you were cheating this whole time!”

Álvaro laughs. “It’s not cheating! It’s just a short cut. That only a few of us have access to.”

“That sounds like cheating.”

“Hey,” I call to Álvaro. Man, those two like to banter. “You ready or what?”

“Four minutes left,” Cyros helpfully adds.

“No fair!” Álvaro cries. But he turns to me with a grin. “First to concede loses?"

“Works for me,” I say, holding up the claws like boxer gloves. They feel a little clunky in my hands, but that should iron itself out as the skill levels up.

“Ready,” Álvaro says. “...Go!”

We both dash toward each other. The spear comes whipping around before I’ve closed the distance, and I have to skid and duck around the weapon. I pivot, lunging for him again, but he’s already whipped the spear around, forcing me to back off once more.

The range of that weapon is a problem. I can’t win the fight if I can’t get close enough to touch him.

Annoyingly, Álvaro grins as he fights, taking way too much pleasure in fending me off. The next time the spear comes stabbing toward me, I activate Slash. Swiping down at the spear even as I side-step the attack, the weapon is deflected into the ground. A flicker of surprise flashes over Álvaro’s face, and now I’m the one grinning. Keeping the spearhead down and out of the way with one hand, I stab forward with the other, finally making it inside his guard.

Faster than I can track, Álvaro spins the spear back toward himself, pulling the tip out from under my hand while the blunt end cracks down on the top of my head. I stumble back, half dazed.

“Hey, don’t give her a concussion,” Cyros remarks rather passively.

“Don’t worry,” Álvaro tells him as I awkwardly rub my head, trying not to stab myself. “System stuff. She’ll heal up.”

“Convenient,” Cyros grumbles.

“Until you’re forced to hunt monsters down each week,” Álvaro says.

“Or prepare meals every day.” I wince, glancing at my hand, but there’s no blood. This is a bad match up for my weapons, but I’m not going to let that stop me. The only way to get better is to keep using them, and it won’t be the first time I’ve brute-forced skills.

Álvaro raises an eyebrow at me, and I nod.

“Keep going,” I say. Time to step this up a notch.

Álvaro comes at me as I activate a second Slash, this time coupling it with Imbue. The previously invisible claw extensions turn green as my magic flows into them.

I Slash toward his spear, and this time it’s Álvaro who skips warily back.

“That’s not going to break my weapon, is it?” he asks, poking the spear in my direction but hastily pulling it back any time I take a swipe at it.

I have no idea, actually. Probably not, since it’s a poison affinity. But acids also fall under the affinity’s umbrella, so who knows? “Come at me and find out.”

“I’d rather not,” he admits.

“Sal,” Cyros warns. “We need to return these all in one piece.”

Yeah, I know, that’s why I haven’t used Cut yet. Ignoring the objections, I keep on the offensive, chasing Álvaro down as he ducks and weaves, retreating from my attacks. And with each swipe, his moves become more predictable, and I get closer and closer to getting through his guard.

Álvaro is no longer smiling, his face instead pinched in a focused frown. He’s so zoned in on me that he’s no longer taking the time to scan his surroundings—so he probably doesn’t see the rock that’s only a few paces behind him. I lunge forward, hoping he’ll skip back and trip over the rock.

[Mana extinguished,] Echo cuts in. [Slash and Imbue spells expired.]

The green claws flicker out mid-stab.

Álvaro grins. “About time!” He snaps his spear around, cracking the shaft against my claws.

Shoot. Was he watching my Mana levels? I’m not used to fighting someone else who can see the same sort of info about me that I can see about them.

“It’s okay to give up,” Álvaro taunts, now driving me back once more. “I’ve still got most of my Mana left, and your weapons aren’t much without their spells.”

I grit my teeth, still blocking and attempting counter attacks, which are easily deflected with Álvaro’s spear.

He’s right. I need magic to use these weapons to their full potential.

Something Piria once said comes back to me. “You’re connected to Shirasil, so you’re connected to his magic. You can use his mana as your own, if you open yourself to the connection you now share.”

Is that true? Do I have an unfathomable well of mana I could tap into, if I just tried? It would open up so many possibilities. It could change my very relationship with spells and potions and magic. No more meticulous planning ahead and rationing of mana.

Hesitantly, I reach out.

And I can feel it, just like Piria said. Like I’m standing at the base of a mountain, its incredible presence looming over me. It’s there. All I have to do is embrace it.

A flutter of fear passes through me, and I retreat from the power. It’s too much. What would happen to me if I connected to it? If I let the tether between Shirasil and I grow in strength. I don’t want to be beholden to anyone. I want to be strong on my own. I need to.

One of Álvaro’s strikes catches me on my forearm, and pain lances through my bones.

[10 points of Bludgeoning damage sustained]

I let out a cry, stumbling and then falling back. I grind my teeth against the throbbing in my arm, but Álvaro isn’t letting up. He towers over me, pointing the spear toward my chest.

No. No, I won’t lose because I’m too stubborn to use everything at my disposal. And Shirasil’s mana pool isn’t the only thing I can use to fight. “If you’ve got a card like that up your sleeve, you should play it more,” Álvaro had said.

Alright, then. If that’s what you want.

[Noxious Gauntlet activated.]

I grab the spear with the massive, ghostly green claws. Álvaro jumps back, alarmed, but he isn’t able to pull the spear from the Gauntlet’s grasp. I yank back, and the weapon is wrenched from his grasp. I toss it to the side, pushing myself back to my feet. A look of uncertainty passes over Álvaro’s face.

“Time,” Cyros calls. He holds his bowl upside down. “Lunch break is over.”

“Awww, come on,” Álvaro objects. “It was just about to get exciting!”

“You were just about to lose,” Cyros says flatly, and his assessment of the match fills me with pride. Then he turns his gaze to me. “If you’re going to use it, commit to it. Don’t pull it out as a last resort after you’re already beat up. That’s just setting yourself up for failure.”

I huff, ready to argue. But as I look down at the Gauntlet, curling it into a fist, I know he’s right. The match would have been over before it even began if I’d led with it. The Gauntlet even has mana-stealing potential; combined with the felis claws, I wouldn’t have to worry about running out of mana.

“Yeah,” I grunt. It’s harder to force the words out than I expected. “You’re right.”

[Noxious Gauntlet deactivated.]

The ghostly claws whisps away like mist in the wind.

“Boooo,” Álvaro says, giving Cyros an emphatic thumbs-down. “Party pooper! Come on, Sal, one more go!”

I awkwardly pick up his spear with my left hand and toss it back in his direction. I meant to toss it back to him so he could catch it, but lacking most of my fingers on that hand sort of complicates that. I’m pointedly not using my right arm—the blow he dealt it hurts a lot more than I’d like to admit.

“We can go again at dinner,” I say, and Cyros nods in approval. In truth, I need the couple of hours for my arm to heal.

Álvaro lets out an exaggerated sigh. “Fine. But you have to use the gauntlet that time!”

I pull the felis claws off, flexing my fingers. “I think I will.”

The travel starts to become routine. We wake at dusk for breakfast, pause at midnight for lunch, and find shelter near dawn, whereupon Álvaro and I spar to level up our weapon Proficiencies while dinner is cooking. The heat of the daytime is brutal, but we have cover artificed with cooling spells to sleep and take watch under, and honestly the temperature doesn’t seem to bother me as much as it bothers the boys. I suspect my class’s Heat and Cold resistance is coming into play here.

During the night, however, there’s not much to do but chat and think while we walk.

On the third day, after lunch and my and Álvaro’s routine sparring match (Cyros has even joined in once, now—I guess the boredom is getting to him,) I remove the refiner from my Inventory to fiddle with while we walk. I haven’t had a chance to play around with it in about a week, but now that we’re on the road, we’ve got plenty of free time on our hands.

Last time I discovered it seemed to respond to my mana. And by respond, I mean feel a little different. But hey, that’s more than anything else had done. So I decide to try it again while we walk.

I’ve still got a couple points of mana left after our lunchtime sparring match, so I touch a finger to the hairline crack in the refiner and press the last three points of mana into the device. Once again, I feel a tingling beneath my finger. The impression of something shifting, like a cat blearily waking up and stretching out. The more I focus on the sensation, the more it feels like there’s something more than magic in there. Shirasil had described the remnant as a dangerous weapon, but this feels like… I don’t know. Not a mind, exactly. But it sure feels more complicated than an inanimate object.

Mentally, I try to focus on the odd sensation—force it to resolve into something sensible. It stirs in response, and I think I can feel it trying to reach back out toward me. Or, more accurately, the mana I’d given it.

“Sorry,” I mutter. “All out. I’ll give you some more later.”

“Watcha playing with?” Álvaro asks, glancing back toward me. He and Cyros had been chatting, walking a couple paces ahead, and I thought I’d been out of earshot.

I stick the refiner in my pocket. “Nothing.”

Álvaro laughs. “Yeah, real believable.”

I glance toward Cyros, and he’s watching me with curiosity as well.

“Aw, come on. Tell us!” Álvaro says.

I don’t reply.

“I won’t drop it, just so you know,” the boy says brightly. “I can be really annoyingly stubborn.”

Cryos grimaces. “Great. Now there are two of you.”

“You do seem to like to collect stubborn people,” Álvaro teases him.

A smile twitches at Cyros’s mouth. Then he gestures to me. “Come on, already, just tell us.”

Probably can’t weasel my way out of this one. And it’s not like I really need to hide it—though I can imagine what Cyros’s reaction will be when he learns I stole something from a Blackcloak job.

Reluctantly, I take the refiner out of my pocket and hold it up for them both to see.

The boys drop back, walking on either side of me as they look at the marble.

“What is it?” Cyros asks.

“A refiner,” Álvaro says.

I look at him in surprise, but his gaze is unfocused—probably Checking it with Echo.

“What’s a refiner?” Cyros asks.

Álvaro’s gaze refocuses, but he gives a helpless shrug, looking to me for an answer.

“I’m not completely sure,” I admit. “A container, I think. Inside it is something called a remnant.”

“What?” Álvaro straightens up. “Are you sure?”

“Yeah,” I say, giving him a wary look. “Why, do you know what a remnant is?”

“Do you?” he asks.

“It’s a weapon I think.” I rotate the marble around between my fingers. “Dangerous to the gods, even.”

Álvaro snorts. “Yeah, no kidding.”

Now Cyros and I are both looking at Álvaro.

He holds up his hands. “Look, I don’t know a whole lot myself. I don’t know what these things are or where they came from. But you’re right that they can be dangerous. Though, if you can get them on your side, that isn’t necessarily a bad thing.”

I don’t particularly care where it came from, anyway—I just want to know what it can do.

“You’re talking about it like it’s some kind of creature,” Cyros observes.

Álvaro waffles a hand. “Kind of? I mean, they’ve got minds, from what I gather. Weird minds. But I don’t know if they are even alive, really.”

“Álvaro, spill,” I demand. “Stop talking around it. What do you know?”

“My sibling has one,” Álvaro admits. “And another girl we met. But both of those remnants were attached to objects. Not stuck inside one, like this, but… how do I explain it. They were part of the object. A sentient sword and shield, basically.”

I grin. “Cool!” A sentient weapon sounds sweet.

“What?” Cyros asks, his voice going up an octave. “You’ve encountered two legendary artifacts?”

Álvaro shrugs. “I guess? They seemed to have some historical importance to the kingdoms that were fighting over them. The weapons didn’t particularly care about sides, though. They were pretty single-minded. Like I said, not exactly minds how we think of them.”

“Explain,” I demand, eating up every crumb of information he offers. This is starting to sound awesome! Now I have to get this refiner cracked open.

“Look, I didn’t wield either of the weapons myself.” Then he pouts. “Though I should have. Anyway, Nye was the one who wielded the shield, so everything I know about it is just from what they’ve told me. It seemed like the shield was really proud—to a fault. It kept getting them in trouble, and Nye had to stop it from trying to eat everything. But it was really strong, and it came with its own spells, which is pretty neat.”

“Sorry,” Cyros says. “Did you say eat everything?”

“Yeah but it’s okay.” Álvaro waves him off. “Nye convinced it not to.”

“What about the sword?” I ask, leaning in eagerly. Cyros doesn’t appear nearly as excited anymore.

“That one was kind of a problem,” he admits. “It was always angry. Which, what with being a sword, made it kind of violent. It was always trying to instigate fights—way worse than the shield.”

“What do you mean?” Cyros asks, starting to look worried. “How did they start fights? Could they move on their own?”

“Well…” Álvaro smiles guiltily. “No. But they could maybe sort of influence the minds of the people who wielded them.”

Cyros’s eyebrows shoot up. “And you wanted to wield one?”

“Hey!” he objects. “They’re really cool.”

I mean, I kind of agree. “How was your brother able to control it?” I ask.

“Sibling,” he corrects me. “They just were nice to it, I think? I mean, I wasn’t there from the start, but I heard it was a lot more trouble when Nye was treating it like a tool. But at some point they came to some kind of understanding, and after that they seemed to work together pretty well.”

“Neat,” I say, rolling the marble between my fingers. “If I broke this remnant out, you think it might be grateful? Would it want to work with me?”

“Couuuuld be,” Álvaro says, hesitating.

Cyros gives him a flat look. “What aren’t you telling us?”

He grimaces. “Well, those weapons weren’t the only remnants we ran into. Sort of. These things seem to be attracted to each other for some reason. And not in the chummy way. In the ‘I’d like to eat any other remnant I find’ kind of way. And the other remnants we found were a lot smaller—a lot less intelligent. Those ones were wild, infused in rocks and ash and the bones of dead animals. And they absolutely weren’t interested in working with anyone. They just had one thought: eat other remnants, and anything that got between them and their next meal.”

“Great,” Cyros says. “So releasing this remnant is just as likely to eat us as it is to maybe work with us.”

“Pretty much!” Álvaro says brightly.

“Sounds worth the risk,” I say.

Álvaro shrugs in helpless agreement, while Cyros gives me an aghast look.

“You two are going to get yourselves killed,” he says.

“Hey, we all die some day.” Álvaro grins. “Some of us, more than once. Ayyy!” He holds his hand up to me for a high-five.

I can’t just leave him hanging. And I also can’t help but be infected by his boundless enthusiasm. Attempting to hold back a smile, I clap his hand.

Cyros opens and closes his mouth. We appear to have rendered him speechless.

“So what are you going to do with it?” Álvaro asks me. “If you don’t want it, I’ll take it.”

I close it in my fist. “No, thanks.”

“Ahh, come on,” he begs. “You already get to be a champion and have that cool claw thing. At least let me have the remnant! I’d take care of it. Pleeeease?”

I snort. “Sorry, but still no. Shirasil told me to take good care of it.”

Álvaro sighs dramatically. “Oh, sure, now you’re on his side.”

I narrow my eyes, stuffing the remnant back into my pocket.

“Ow!” Álvaro cries. “That’s the eye daggers you’re shooting at me. The pain!”

“You two,” Cyros says with a sigh. “You two are…” He doesn’t seem capable of coming up with words sufficient to express his feelings on the matter, so in the end he just shakes his head.

Álvaro seems to quickly get over my rejection, as he’s back to bantering with Cyros only a minute later. It seems like he just enjoys hearing his own voice, no matter who is willing to listen.

I drop out of the conversation, still smiling faintly to myself, as I mull over everything Álvaro revealed about the remnants. Can the one in my pocket be reasoned with?

I can’t wait to find out.
