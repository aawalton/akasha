Opening his eyes, Xander found the world had been reduced to a child’s plaything.

He blinked. Or rather, he performed the motion that would have been considered blinking, a reflex left over from a body he no longer possessed. After all, he had no eyelids, or much of anything that could be called flesh either.

Such a realization should have unsettled him deeply, but oddly enough, it didn’t. Nothing could disturb him now. Not when every fiber of his being thrummed with a power so overwhelming that the very idea of him being disturbed was simply laughable.

He let his gaze drop, and the world rearranged itself with an eagerness to please, as though it had finally understood it existed only to be seen through his eyes.

At his feet lay the remains of the building that had once dominated the landscape, the same building that had seemed so impossibly large when he first set foot inside its shadow. It had collapsed into a shapeless heap of half-melted stone, a wound in the earth that steamed and bubbled like a pot left too long over a fire.

The rest of the complex sprawled out around the wreckage, though the word “complex” now struck him as almost comical, a term far too grand for something so diminutive, so barren, so utterly pathetic. And over there hung the false sun of this floor, small and pale and harmless, like a lamp held aloft by some servant, and he could reach out and pluck it from the sky if the mood ever struck him.

He felt no particular urge to, however. Why would he? What could a god possibly want with such a petty light?

A laugh rolled up out of him, and the ground under his feet had no choice but to shudder. Yes, he was a god. Of course he was. What else could he be? What other word in any tongue spoken by any mortal lip could hope to capture the sheer magnitude of what he had become?

The sound he made was strange to his own ears. The laugh was deeper now, with a crackling, popping resonance to it. Like a fire that had learned to talk. Like a hearth that had been given voice. And it pleased him, all of it pleased him, every last detail of this new arrangement pleased him beyond measure. From the way flame licked and coiled along the length of his arms, to the sheer crushing weight of his own existence bearing down upon the world beneath.

This is what I was always meant to be. The thought settled into him with the comfortable finality of a truth he had long suspected and now, at last, seen confirmed beyond doubt.

Strange, wasn’t it? Just moments ago, he had thought he was about to die, killed by that cursed suit of armour and its minions. But then, he had spoken those words, and in doing so, had burned away everything that had made him small.

And what was left was this. Something greater. Something eternal.

He glanced down at the ruin again, and let himself wonder where his enemies might be now. Melted away along with the building, most likely. Good riddance to them. That was exactly what they deserved for failing to know their place, for having the audacity to offend a being so far beyond them in every conceivable way. Still, it was a shame. He would have liked them to hold out a while longer, long enough for him to truly enjoy breaking them.

“I see that you’re rather fond of your current self,” came a voice.

What?

Xander was startled. Because not only did the voice sound familiar, but it also... it wasn’t so much heard as it was simply there, echoing somewhere inside his head.

He tried to respond, but what emerged instead was nothing more than a low, formless rumble, a collection of sounds utterly unintelligible even to himself.

“Don’t try to talk,” the voice continued, and there was an unmistakable note of amusement threaded through it. “That body of yours isn’t suited to such a task. Instead, formulate the words in your mind as you would a thought, then project them to me.”

What? What the hell is this? Still, he gave it a try anyway, because there seemed to be no better option available to him.

You... you are the Apostle...

“I am,” the voice said, with a chuckle. “I’m glad you remember me.”

Bastard... Where are you?

“Not in the dungeon anymore, I’m afraid,” the Apostle said, his tone as casual as if he were discussing something of no consequence. “Which means, unfortunately, I can’t lend you any direct assistance from where I am. The best I can offer is a bit of guidance.” He chuckled. “Good thing I’ve established this little mental link beforehand, isn’t it?”

Xander didn’t like this man at all. The bastard always carried himself as though people around him were little more than slow-witted children he was doing the great favour of explaining things to. That condescension bled into every word he spoke, and while it was fine when it was aimed at lesser men, Xander found he had far less patience for it when he himself was the target. Show some respect, you smug bastard. He swallowed the urge to say it to the man’s face.

You knew this would happen once I spoke the Forbidden Words, didn’t you?

“Of course. That Reliquary was always meant to produce this particular outcome. Well, there was a small chance it might not have functioned as intended. Failure is always a possibility, even in the cleverest designs. So you can imagine how pleased I am that everything worked out exactly as planned.”

Xander frowned, or rather, did whatever his new face did in place of frowning.

What happened to me?

“The full explanation is long and complex, so I’m going to give you a short, simplified version, one that even someone like you can follow,” the Apostle said, the hint of a smile curling around the edges of his words. “There was a fragment of a god’s power sealed within the flaming sword you had been wielding. When you spoke the Forbidden Words, the seal broke, destroying the weapon and setting the power loose. That power was then drawn to you and transformed you into, well, this.”

The power of a god. Xander liked the sound of that.

So I am a god now.

The Apostle barked a laugh, and it was not a short one. Instead, it was a rolling chorus of laughter that seemed to stretch on and on without any sign of stopping.

What is so damned funny?

“You are... a very interesting man,” the Apostle said, still struggling to hold back another wave of laughter threatening to spill out of him. “You have no idea, none at all, how much labor one must undertake, how many ordeals they must claw their way through, in order to claim divinity. No, my friend, you are not a god. What you have now is but a tiny, tiny fraction of what a god commands. Nevertheless, even with that small sliver coursing through you, you now stand leagues above the vast majority of mortal men. There is little any of them could do to stop you, should you decide to move against them.”

Fine. That would do. Xander could live with that. Those lesser men, all of them, would soon learn to know their proper places. Bow before me, vermin.

“Anyway, enough small talk for now. We have work to do. Your mission remains unchanged. So, make your way to the Core Room.”

What? Xander bristled at the audacity of such a request. Who do you think you are, giving me orders like some errand boy? And why should I lift a finger for you?

“Because what good is having such power if you don’t use it? Don’t you want to see what your newfound strength can do? This dungeon happens to be an excellent testing ground, does it not? And, isn’t there someone down there you’ve been dying to settle a score with?”

Who?

“The dark spirit, of course. Who else? He won’t be running from you this time. Force your way to the Core Room, and he will have no choice but to fight you.”

That damn coward. This time he would break him properly.

You’re certain he’s here?

“Very certain. I fought him myself, moments ago.”

A realization dawned upon Xander, and he guffawed.

You went ahead by yourself, and you fought the dark spirit. But now, for some reason, you’re no longer in the dungeon. Why? He beat you, didn’t he? He beat you, and you ran.

“Well, yes. He was more resourceful than I’d anticipated. What really surprised me, though, was that blade of light he carried. Where in the world did he get his hands on something like that? Did the Queen of Brefjord pass it along to him somehow, and if so, when did that happen? I may need to look into the matter further.” The Apostle cleared his throat. “I digress. In any case, yes, I was defeated by him. Which means, if you manage to succeed where I failed, you will have proven yourself the better man.”

I’m already better than you.

“Make good on your claim, then.”

Xander grunted. How am I supposed to reach the lower floor from here?

“Look to the far end of the desert. Tell me what you see.”

He turned his head, slow and deliberate, fire sheeting off his shoulders like a cape of living flame, and gazed past the perimeter wall of this whole miserable complex.

There’s something over there. A chasm, maybe.

“Just as I thought. After that boulder struck me, I’ve come to suspect that there might be another way to go down. A chasm, is it? Go on then, Xander. Let’s find out whether that new body of yours can squeeze through it.”

Xander snorted, but he moved his leg regardless.

He took a step, and the ground groaned beneath him in protest. Buildings that stood in his path he simply swatted aside; the wall that dared to block his way he tore through as if it were merely a sheet of parchment.

Below him, the desert burned. Above him, the sky trembled. Everything in sight, every last thing of this small, pathetic place, bowed before his magnificence.

“Good. I’ll walk you through your abilities as we go, so you can put them to use effectively.”

Xander marched on, each footfall a cataclysm, each heartbeat an inferno. He could feel it now. In the fire that had become his blood. In the stone that had become his body. There was more power to be claimed, more glory within reach. All he had to do was keep walking. The dungeon would yield to him. The world would yield to him.

Dark spirit, he thought. I’m coming for you. And when I find you, I’ll show you how helpless a mortal is before a god.

Yes, he was the fire that consumed, the flame that purified.

He was the god of ash and ember.

In all his life, he had never felt more alive.
