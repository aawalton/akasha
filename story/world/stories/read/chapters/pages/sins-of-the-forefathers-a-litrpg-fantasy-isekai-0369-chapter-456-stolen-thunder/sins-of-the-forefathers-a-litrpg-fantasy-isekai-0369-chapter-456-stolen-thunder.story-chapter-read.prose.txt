“I’ve been assisting the Unchained for some time now,” Preceptor Josha told me, his dark hands curled around an oddly expensive porcelain tea mug, steam rising from the surface. The sight of it was just another incongruous oddity I’d seen down here, among the Unchained. When I had asked about it, the Preceptor had smiled and said the Savoy had left many things down here in their hidden fortress. “Nearly five months now, I’d say.”

“How did that happen?” I prompted. The five of us, including Rafael, to my surprise, had followed the Preceptor over to a small side table where a tea set had been prepared, as if the preacher had been expecting company. Which, to be fair, he probably had. There, he had served everyone some extremely fancy tasting tea. To my surprise, even Rafael had taken a cup of it, one slim, leather-clad hand emerging from his voluminous robes to grasp the stem with both surprising delicacy and dexterity.

He was even sipping at it, and to all apparent pretexts, seemed to be enjoying the taste. I don’t know why that surprised me so much. Maybe I had expected a walking corpse not to be able to taste anything, what with having dead taste buds and all. But I shouldn’t have been surprised. All things were possible with sufficient skill in Magic.

Including, apparently, giving a sense of taste to a zombie.

The contrast of everything was enough to crack a smile on my lips, even as I sipped my own tea. After all, even my own clone could taste things.

A far-off look entered the Preceptor's eyes then. “I must admit, the impetus arose from a profound sense of shame,” He said quietly. At my raised eyebrow, a humorless smile crossed Josha’s lips. “I was ashamed at my own inaction during the Construct war. For nearly the entirety of that conflict, I huddled shamefully within the comforting walls of my church, content to allow others the chance to provide succor to the downtrodden. I told myself that surely, so many moral, upright people of the Uprising, so powerful and righteous, were more than enough to win the autonomy of the Sculpted. I had even had the great fortune to meet prominent leaders amongst the rebellion. Such as yourself, Sir Hart.”

I hid a grimace and repressed the urge to fidget at his praise. These days, I wasn’t uncomfortable being referred to as a ‘prominent leader’. Especially not after I had literally founded a large, upcoming Martial Order. It was an observable fact that I had a place of power in not only Herztalian but Veredenese culture as well.

I was a bit uncomfortable with being called moral, upright, or righteous. These days, it seemed like I just kept running headfirst into my own moral failings. Even worse, I had a tendency to acknowledge them, and then simply continue with my actions anyway if they benefited my position.

I was anything but righteous, I felt.

“With the close of the war, I wrote to the Primector about my feelings of shame and inadequacy. To my surprise…I received an answer,” Josha smiled wryly and shook his head. “The Primector, in his wisdom, informed that if I felt my actions were unbefitting of a Preceptor, then there was another cause that I could give to my aid. Far to the north, he had learned of a group of freemen, breaking the chains of those that the Principality had sought to subjugate. Thus…I left my parish within Silvercrest to a younger man, eager to prove himself a capable Preceptor, and ventured north. It did not take long for the Unchained to find me. A free human within the Principality is a rare enough sight. A man of the cloth in an entirely different matter indeed. The rest, as they say, is history.”

I nodded slowly. I…could understand where he was coming from, wanting to provide a helping hand to the downtrodden. I could even empathize, considering that was one of the founding reasons I had created the Polaris Reach.

Hmm.

There was an idea. I had always thought it might be a good idea to have an official chaplain within the ranks of the Order. Someone to provide some religious guidance to those within my ranks who sought it. Sure, we had a few Preceptors who had worked with us in the past, but not anyone who had chosen to join our ranks entirely.

I wonder if Bleddyn would be opposed to a little bit of poaching between friends.

Another time.

To my surprise, Sylvia set down her cup with an audible clink onto its plate. She reached across the table, and to his own wonder, took Josha’s hand in her own. She met the Preceptor’s eyes with a serious, compassionate gaze. “I can say, with certainty, that none of the Sculpted begrudge you for not doing more during the Construct War. This guilt you feel is not something we, as a people, feel as well. We understand that it was a very fraught, very complicated time in the history of our country. We carry no enmity for those who did not explicitly dedicate themselves to the Uprising. Not when we won in the end. It is enough that you did not support our enemies. Even then…we were willing to forgive those who fought for them.”

I noted there that she didn’t say that the Sculpted had forgiven the people who had driven the Construct War. After all, as part of the post-war negotiations, and to help ensure a lasting peace, more than a few of the nobility on the side of the Loyalists had retained their positions. Not without concessions, and not without heavy fines levied against them once Wenzel had taken office. But they were still prominent members of the nobility, and they were treated very coldly by the rising Sculpted caucus within Herztalian society.

At least, they had been before the onslaught of the Skyfall.

I think we were all very lucky that in the end, the Sculpted were a people that were far more logically inclined than the rest of us flesh creatures.

If Josha’s smile was a tad watery, as he clasped Sylvia’s hand in return, nobody commented on it. “Thank you, Lady Sylvia. I must say, though…” Josha continued slowly, letting his eyes drift over toward Rafael. “You certainly keep…interesting company, Sir Hart. I’ve never served tea to one of the undead before.”

My own cup froze before it could reach my lips. I held it there as I stared at the Preceptor in genuine astonishment.

How could he tell?

Genuinely, Rafael seemed to be a master of suppressing his presence and state of undeath. Before we had reached the outer reaches of the settlement, the Lich had made a few adjustments, abruptly reeling in the sense of Death Aether that he emanated simply by nature of his level of strength. He had fitted on some gloves over his dessicated, leather hands, drawn his cloak closer to his body to obscure any inch of his skin, and deepened the illusion over his hood. His literally flaming eyes didn’t peer out of it anymore, leaving him looking…honestly, like a fairly suspicious-looking person. Oddly thin, and without any of the normal unconscious movements among those of the living, I would have thought he was strange at any time.

But I’m not sure I would have clocked him as an undead, though. It really wasn’t out of the ordinary for people on Vereden to clad themselves in full-body hooded robes, if only to protect from the elements. More than that, his staff, so similar to my own, marked him as a Magi, and people expected eccentricities from those who wielded Mana.

The most Rafael had gotten from either the guards or the people among us as we had journeyed to the church had been a few side-eyed, wary glances.

To my further bewilderment, Rafael didn’t even seem all that bothered by his cover being blown. He even took another sip of tea before he spoke. “We’re old acquaintances, the student of an old comrade of mine.” He said calmly, setting down his cup. “We only happened to encounter each other when he barged in quite uninvited, where I was…resting. I was unaware of the somewhat dire state the surface was in, and offered my, shall we say, services for the duration of the emergency.”

My mouth fell open a tad when Josha actually smiled at Rafael in return, visibly approving. “Thank you for your assistance. It’s always a pleasure to see those who are vitally challenged choosing to aid those of us who remain.”

Alright, that was enough. As Rafael nodded regally, as if we were supposed to bow and scrape for his offer to help, I held up a finger in weak protest, drawing the attention of them both. “Isn’t this…” I waved weakly between the two of them. “A problem? I was expecting more cries of ‘abomination’ if he were discovered. Especially when it was a priest who found him out. Not…polite approval. Aren’t you guys supposed to be all, ‘burn the undead’ or some such?”

“What’s the matter, Nate?” Renauld butted in, smirking at me over his own cup. “Not used to a little cross-existential friendship?”

I scowled at him as Josha looked at me in polite confusion. “I’m not sure what you’re talking about? The Church of the Gyre has no explicit problems with the undead, as long as they’re non-violent.”

“Not quite true,” Rafael said mildly, spooning more sugar into his teacup and stirring lazily. “I’ve met some Preceptors who have viewed me with distinct pity. Something to do with how I no longer have a Status, no matter the strength I actually possess.”

Josha winced. “I do apologize for that, Mr…Rafael, was it? It’s simply that, as one of the non-living, you are denied the grace of the Holy Gyre and its System. It’s not proscription, or even something as simple as disapproval. Undeath is viewed more as a…disability, perhaps. Something which should be viewed with accommodation in most circumstances, and at the least, sympathy. Do you require…accommodation?”

Even though his eyes were hidden, I could tell that Rafael had rolled them. “No, thank you,” He said, audibly bored. “I chose this existence, and do not require assistance in passing on. Enough of me. We’re here for the boy. Now, tell me. You were inspecting his body when we entered. Presumably, this one-”

He waved dismissively to totally unfazed Renauld, who just raised his cup in a toast.

“-has told of the plans to fix his issue. Do you believe you can assist?”

“Ah?” Josha blinked before smiling. “Yes, Healer Renauld was telling me about the injuries Sir Hart had sustained. I believe it’s well within my capabilities to help in this matter. Are you going to help with the operation as well, Mr. Rafael? I would welcome another set of hands, if you have surgical experience.”

Rafael outright scoffed, an odd sound for someone who literally didn’t have lungs.

I should know. I’d seen into that chest.

“Do I have experience, he asks?” The Lich said, sounding a tad affronted for the first time in the conversation. “Child, I was knee deep in entrails before your father was naught but a sparkle in your grandfather’s eye. I invented many of the modern surgical procedures. Am I going to help indeed.” Abruptly, the Lich set his cup down and stood up from the table. “No, I am going to lead the procedure. I wouldn’t trust such a delicate, structural procedure to some whelp trained by that ham-handed harridan and an admittedly polite provincial Preceptor.”

Both of them took the implied insult in stride, to my relief. Renauld even seemed to sigh in faux-regret. “If only she had been here to hear you say that,” He griped with a smirk, before standing himself. “Fine with me if you want to take the lead. Maybe I’ll even learn something.”

Rafael sniffed. “Perhaps,” He said in a doubtful tone. Renauld just grinned.

I stood up from the table at the same time Josha did. “Waitwaitwait,” I said hurriedly. “We’re doing this now? Don’t we need to set up a clean room or something?”

“We can secure this building for the procedure just fine,” Rafael said boredly, already striding for the altar my body rested upon. “Assuming, that is, if the Gnoll isn’t completely incompetent and knows the proper Spells for sanitation, it can be prepared in a matter of minutes.”

“Already got ‘em primed,” Renauld said easily, coming to stand at the Lich’s shoulder like a patient assistant.

I blinked rapidly, looking for assurance from an amused Sylvia. I just got a pat on my hand in response. “I…alright. Then, I guess I’ll just get ready to help with the fusion of the bones to my skeleton and the soul grafting-”

“No need,” Rafael said dismissively. “I am perfectly capable of both of those, even if the woman wasn’t, when you fumbled your way through that hack job on your arm. Coincidentally, would you like me to revise that bond as well? What am I saying, of course you would. Who wouldn’t want a deeper sense of touch? I’ll handle that while you’re unconscious as well.”

For some reason, I was starting to feel distinctly hunted. I hunched my shoulders a bit protectively. “But…I…melding?”

The Lich seemed to understand my stuttered protests just fine. “Do you really think that your odd little Profession can measure up to the accumulated experience in the fields of osteocraft, surgery, and soul surgery to a Lich who was a surgeon in life?”

I…didn’t have an answer to that.

“No? Good. Good night, Nathaniel. When next you wake, I’ll have dealt with your little issue, and we can get on with saving the world.”

Before I could even react, Rafael pointed a figure my way without even looking. From that desiccated, leather-bound digit leapt a small bolt of what I could only call anti-light.

It impacted my body, and I immediately felt the Aetheric bonds holding my clone together start to unravel.

The last thing I saw before I unraveled was a smile of reassurance from Sylvia…

And then I was back in my own body as the Outer Lattice, stunned by the audacity of that motherfucker.

In the time it took me to recover my irritation, Middle and Core absorbed my memories of all that had occurred since the last I had merged back.

They certainly had their own opinions.
