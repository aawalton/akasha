Kyle departed Duroc’s village with little fanfare the next morning.  Still lacking a proper spatial storage, he lashed the large metal scrolls to the C.H.A.D.D. pack with a tightly-woven wrap of vines, and then covered the whole contraption with thick bark.  All of that was infused with the concept of Immutability, which Kyle hoped would keep them safe and secure from the threats on the road.  Because there would be threats.

Earlier that morning, orc scouts sent to check the roads reported back with hodag activity along the ancestral path to Kyle’s first destination.  A small nest, with only a lone C Grade, but finding it so early was troubling.  The largest issue was the relative proximity of the nest, and that it hadn’t been there for long.  Fortunately, Kyle was more than up for resolving that issue.

When the hodag nest was within range of C.H.A.D.D.’s scanners, he started taking to the sky, preparing to deal with it the same way he had the first nest.  He paused, however, when he felt a nudge from Phaegos.

You realize that I will not gain proficiency with conceptual Death if I am not afforded opportunities to practice, correct?

As he pulled another Model: Cannon out of the World Tree Seed, Kyle replied.  I don’t really understand why you are so insistent with using it at all.  It can’t be giving you that much of a snack.

It certainly isn’t to sate my appetite, Phaegos agreed.  However, the work done now is to set up for the evolution of the Parasitic Resonance skill.  Good control and integration of the concept of Death will be integral for a good outcome.

Kyle didn’t necessarily like the implication of Phaegos’ words.  While it was true that the impending upgrade to Parasitic Resonance was one that didn’t have as much clarity, Kyle had been angling for something that would either align well with the connection between himself and the World Tree Seed, or otherwise just a standard upgrade to range and effectiveness.  Even though it wasn’t terribly flashy, the effects were still incredibly potent, even at C Grade.

Sensing Kyle’s moment of hesitation, Phaegos pressed.  If I can grasp the concept better, it only benefits you and the drone.  It affords you options, while reducing the risk of accidents.  And when you invariably encounter a practitioner who uses the concept in the future, you will have the tools to adequately defend yourself.  Besides, would it not be wiser to save your explosive?  I know they are difficult to make, and your journey has only just begun.

Strangely, the last point Phaegos made was the on  that most immediately grabbed Kyle’s attention.  Each Model: Cannon projectile took nearly a week to craft, not counting the excessive amount of mana required to do so.  If he used another right now, that would leave him with only two more shots.  If there were other larger hodag nests, or variant hodags roaming around, keeping the long-range explosive available as an option would be important.

As he paused to consider that point, the other arguments Phaegos made also started to gain some traction, even if he didn’t particularly love the idea of experimenting with Death.

Fine.  But we’re not going to cause unnecessary suffering.  If you’re going to control it, you need to put in the effort to keep the concept in check until a kill is assured.

He immediately felt Phaego’s agreement.  Withdrawing Model: Cannon back into the seed, Kyle activated Parasitic Resonance and Genesis Engine before descending like a rocket toward the hodag nest.  The largest of the creatures reacted, launching a spray of needle-like fur to meet Kyle’s charge as the smaller D Grade hodags roared in anger.

It was the wrong move.

A hastily-constructed barrier from Aura Force deflected the incoming projectiles as Kyle closed the remaining distance.  A single tendril of Parasitic Resonance connected to the D Grade creatures, while the rest bound themselves to the C Grade.  Kyle felt Phaegos struggle to control the flood of deadly energy, and added his own Willpower to the effort.  He dodged their attacks, suppressing the power to contain it to each tendril.  All the while, he gained ground within each of the hodags, finally allowing the deathly concept to flood out when he had full control of their mana networks.

The results were devastating.  As before, the D Grades immediately fell, unable to stand against the energy invading their bodies.  The C Grade faltered for a moment, but its eyes grew unfocused as it collapsed to the ground.  It held up much better than the others, but Parasitic Resonance had already infected all of its vital organs.  The shock of being attacked all at once overwhelmed the hodag’s natural resilience, and the creature passed a moment later.

Before Kyle could admire his handiwork, however, he felt a wave of ice-cold pain run through his mana network.  Staggering, Kyle caught his balance as he took a deep breath.  It wasn’t the same as the pain from when the death affinity energy invaded him during his job for the Pillar. First, and most importantly, it didn’t invade the World Tree Seed.  The damage was much more contained, though the lingering effects still impacted him far more than they should have.

I apologize for the backlash, Phaegos said, and Kyle felt genuine contrition matched with frustration from the entity.

It isn’t your fault, Kyle said, feeling Adaptive Regeneration at work helping to mend his internal injuries.  He channeled the concept of Adaptation through the skill, accelerating the pace of his recovery even further.

It is a difficult concept to control.  Without your aid, I wouldn’t have been able to prevent the energy from flowing into the creatures immediately.

Kyle looked at the dead hodags, then let out a deep sigh.  We still have a lot to learn, no doubt.  I can see why the Pillar was interested in absorbing some of the ambient energy for their project.  And also why you seemed so interested in it.

It’s more complicated than that, Phaegos said.  It is a concept that shares a deep connection to the forebear’s power, and one that I believe is beneficial to you as well.

In case you haven’t noticed, my path takes a very different approach, Kyle thought.  Healing, growth, plants, not exactly aligned with the concept of Death.

What is Death, if not the end of life? Phaegos countered.  Life and Death are far more connected.  Life can come from death, just as death can fuel life.  Consumption, growth, and change.

The idea was an interesting one, and Kyle would have some time to reflect on it.  They still had a long road ahead of them before reaching their destination, after all.

After sending word to Skippy that the hodag nest had been cleared out, they continued to follow the map outlined by Duroc’s people.  As they traveled, the terrain grew progressively rockier, with sharp stone outcroppings jutting out like the fangs of a colossal beast.  The creatures wandering across the landscape grew similarly in size, with large, plated mammals resembling yaks being the predominant species.  And, of course, hodags.

[DR. MAYHEW, ANOTHER NEST IS ON THE ROAD.]

“How many are there?” Kyle asked, scarcely believing that so many of the beasts would have picked this specific path to form a nest.

[SIX D GRADES AND A C GRADE,] the drone confirmed.

Not as well-established as the first nest we found on Guancious, Kyle mused, feeling Phaegos stir.  It had been a couple of days since they departed Duroc’s village, and Kyle was entirely recovered from the experimentation with conceptual Death.  This time, he was going to try to reinforce his body with the concept of Immutability as Parasitic Resonance activated, hopefully reducing the backlash even more.

Fortunately, this part of the path didn’t carry any of the deadly environments that made the deliveries dangerous, so he could afford to be a little more cavalier with his training.  It wasn’t a luxury that could be afforded when he had to worry about both C Grade beasts and deadly environments, after all.

Kyle approached this hodag nest with the same tactic, rushing in with explosive speed to take the creatures unaware.  This C Grade reacted even more poorly than the last, charging energy for its destructive roar to try and blow Kyle out of the air.  Before it could, Parasitic Resonance connected and Kyle disrupted the skill, while not allowing any of the deadly energy to leak through the skill.

Gritting his teeth, Kyle got into the dangerous dance of avoiding major attacks while his skill infected his opponents.  The split focus between maintaining control of Parasitic Resonance while channeling Immutability led him to some incredibly close calls.  The only reason he was able to narrowly avoid one tail strike was due to Reflexive Evasion and Synaptic Barbs giving him enough warning, or else he’d have been sent flying.

Still, in mere moments Parasitic Resonance gained enough influence to allow Phaegos to silence the hodags permanently.  The backlash still felt very similar to how it had before, the deep ache seeming to come as much from his own mana network as it did from his body.  He sighed and allowed Adaptive Regeneration to get to work patching him up as he sent yet another message about the nest.

Finally, as they crested a particularly large stone crag, Kyle spotted their destination.  The village looked to have been constructed around the crater of a massive meteor impact, with a large lake present in the middle.  A group of D Grade orc guards stood outside, each dressed in thick plate mail with short swords and kite shields. Kyle waved to them in greeting, preparing the speech he’d been practicing.

“I’m here on behalf of War Chieftain Duroc, to deliver a formal invitation for the upcoming Tournament of Ascension.  May I be granted audience with your War Chieftain, as well as lodging for the night?”

The orcs whispered something to one another, then the most powerful of the group turned to him and gave a gentle nod.  “We recognize you, Courier, though truthfully we did not expect your arrival, what with the presence of powerful monsters in the area.”

“I am thankful to have made it through,” Kyle said, suppressing a smile.  “Now, will you assist me in making my delivery?”“Certainly,” the orc said with a shallow bow.  “Follow me, but be warned that the War Chieftain is quite busy.  While it sounds like you avoided the worst of the monsters along your path, a dangerous variant has been spotted not too far away.  The War Chieftain is the only one with a strong chance of success, and he is busy making preparations.”

At the words, Kyle felt his ears perk up.  “A variant, you say?”
