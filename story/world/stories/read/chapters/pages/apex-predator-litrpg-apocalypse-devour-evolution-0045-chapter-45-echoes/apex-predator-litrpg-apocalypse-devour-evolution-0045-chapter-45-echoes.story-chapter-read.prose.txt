This part of the dungeon required a completely different approach than Don had needed before. He had been going all gas no breaks since the beginning. Run, fight, repeat. Full-hog, no breaks.

He quickly realized that was a great way to get killed here.

The ice cavern operated with a singular logic. Get more than two bats on you and you’re in trouble.

While Don was basically immune to the sonic attacks, the bats were smart. If he got overconfident and didn’t expect another attack, immediately after one bat missed its attack, another one made an attempt a few seconds after. That gave the initial bat enough time to escape. And Don had no way of knowing how many bats were clustered in that location. One time he had four giant bats attacking him consecutively. They even got some sonic blasts in, because Don had to release the vacuum from time to time to breathe.

So the bottom line was, Don had to be really careful and slow.

Once he understood that, the dungeon was kind of nice. It was still dangerous because he didn’t know how much danger he was in before the bats were literally on him.

This was the first time in a while Don could take his time, think and plan. He moved at a meticulous pace. Walk a few paces. Stop by where there was a bigger pile of guano. Wait for an ambush. Count how many bats there were. Back off from their range. Re-engage, pick them off one by one.

And yup, the vampire bats were definitely venomous. One of them managed to sink its claws into Don’s neck. Not only did it suck in some blood, Don also got a status effect for moderate poisoning. A health potion sorted that out, but after that Don needed to upgrade his Poison Resistance evolution.

[80 Evolution Points spent]

[Evolution Upgraded!]

[Poison Resistance (F-grade)] ->[Poison Resistance (E-grade)]

[Poison Resistance (E-grade)][Poison Resistance: Your immune system has adapted to be able to deal better with toxins, venoms and pathogens.] [+3 to Constitution]

The Evolution points were racking up. Don made a point of always finishing off the last bat from the cluster alive, netting more points.

After he had cleared around ten clusters, and had managed to travel inside the icy cavern for a whole hundred yards, the finished off the pivotal bat and Devoured it.

[82 Evolution Points acquired!]

[Health at 100%]

[Energy at 100%]

[Debuffs: N/A]

[Evolution trait Vampire Screecher: Evolved!]

Oh hell yeah. What is it going to be? I really hope it's flight, but that’s probably not in the cards. Those would be some ugly ass wings, but flight is flight.

Unfortunately it wasn’t flight. However, it was something arguably better.

[New Evolution Acquired: Echo Location]

[Echolocation (F-grade)]

Your auditory system has evolved to emit and interpret high-frequency sound pulses, allowing you to map your environment in darkness or poor visibility. You can detect movement, terrain, and living beings with remarkable accuracy—so long as they make a sound, you’ll hear it.

+3 Mental

+1 Agility

Now, this was a game changer. One might argue its not all that useful when Don already had Night Vision, but only thing better than having a way to know where the enemy was coming from, was two ways to know where the enemy was coming from. There were absolutely uses for this. What if there was smoke or something else obscuring vision. What if Don was blinded? What if the enemy was hiding behind an obstacle? What if the enemy used a camouflage technique or something similar?

No, this was great. Don closed his eyes and listened.

At first, it was just silence. But then Don focused—not with his ears, but with something behind them. A strange pressure in his jaw, a tightening near his temples. He exhaled, not just breath, but something higher-pitched, something mechanical and tight. The sound bounced. And then the cave lit up inside his mind. Not with sight, but with shapes. The floor, jagged and uneven. The stalactites above, long and still.

Don kept his eyes closed and breathed a whistle again.

And there—movement. A flutter, light as a whisper, somewhere high and to the left. Don couldn’t see it, but he knew it was there. Like muscle memory for sound. His mind painted outlines in real time, flowing and reactive. It didn’t replace his vision. It layered with it. Reinforced it. This wasn’t just backup. This was battlefield awareness turned up to eleven.

Don didn’t have to rely on his Survival Instincts and Night Vision alone anymore. Now he knew when the bats left their perch in the ceiling.

That gave him few seconds of forewarning. And fights at these speeds? A few seconds made a big goddamn difference.

When the first bat surged at Don, he jumped at the cavern wall, and pushed off it. When the bat landed, its talons gouging the stone floor, Don was already drop kicking on the bat’s head. He accelerated his fall with Telekinesis. The bat’s head exploded like a melon.

[You have slain [Vampire Screecher – lvl 13] – You have gained experience for defeating an enemy. 30 System Points earned]

Another bat descended, but Don already knew it was coming. He jumped at the wall again and intercepted the bat’s descent. He bear hugged the wings together, and the creature screamed in anger as the plummeted on the ground like a rock. Don fell on top of the creature and wrestled it against the wall, where he cut the beast down.

[You have slain [Vampire Screecher – lvl 14] – You have gained experience for defeating an enemy. 34 System Points earned]

The third one was already descending. Don knew it was coming, but he had no time. So he dodged sideways, and when the creature landed, he immediately punched. Risky move, and the bat paid in kind, swiping Don with its talons.

[Health at 89%]

The creature used the time Don was recoiling to escape. Don used telekinesis and dragged it down. The monster’s wings fluttered desperately, but it was too light. Don couldn’t have done this to something like a Grimoid hobgoblin. But a flying creature with hollow bones? He yanked the bat down just enough for him to reach a taloned ankle and smash the creature on the ground.

Don climbed on top of it. It tried to scream out a sonic blast, but Don used the vacuum around his head. He sliced its ugly head off.

[You have slain [Vampire Screecher – lvl 13] – You have gained experience for defeating an enemy. 30 System Points earned]

Then he immediately removed the vacuum and listened.

It was silent now. His Echo Location didn’t ping off any more movement in the ceiling. This cluster was done.

The only problem with using his new ability to make certain whether he was safe or not, meant that he couldn’t Devour things alive.

Yes, he could move faster now. He didn’t need to do back and forth to verify how many enemies he was facing before killing them.

Now he could with certainty know how many enemies he was dealing with, which meant he could deal with this dungeon faster.

Should I sacrifice a few evolution points for speed?

Don figured yes. At this point it was worth it. The back and forth was boring and slow. This new ability gave him double or triple speed. There had to be at least half a hundred more bats inside. If he killed them all, he might level, and even get another evolution.

He was ultimately on a timer, and he had no idea when the sand would drain from the hourglass. Jacques could already be ready to kill Blazeclaw. The sooner he cleared the dungeon, the better.

Because after I’m done here, I think I’m ready to take on the boss.

Don couldn’t wait. He would finally get access to a System point dispenser. He had no idea what he could buy with his points, but if it was more equipment or some ways to increase his stats, he couldn’t wait.

After all, during his time in this new reality, he had already accrued a whopping 6380 System Points.

Don had no comparison point, so he wasn’t absolutely sure if it was a lot of points, but he figured it was on the high side.

Finding the dungeon had been massive for his growth. It had everything. Experience, loot, varied monsters for Evolutions. A treasure trove of progression. And Don wanted to loot it all.

And so he continued.

After clearing a few more clusters, he arrived at a wall. In that wall was clearly a gate. Definitely not something made for bats. Don whistled, and indeed “saw” that in the ceiling there was a vent in the wall. A tunnel the bats could use.

His whistle was answered, and it chilled Don down to the bones.

It was a pitch perfect imitation of his whistle.

He whistled again.

That something answered, echoing it perfectly. It was coming from the other side, beyond the doorway. Don could feel a distinct, deep cold emanating from the other side. There was a boss there. And it knew Don had come for it.
