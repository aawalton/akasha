A vessel dropped him on Platform Fishbowl — this time, the lift stayed put for his return trip. Jack walked over to the five-story building he’d wanted to see, angling around to the more damaged side. The partial collapse was reminiscent of a kinetic missile having been dropped into it, blowing out the other side, wider than the entry point.

The place was reminiscent of an apartment building. Jack checked the open door and found it unlocked. He poked around a little. Indeed, it was full of apartments, none of them designed to be actually occupied. Only their basic contours were created, making it obvious the building was fashioned to be bombed, whether by actual aerial bombers, ordinance, or powers. Maybe all of the above.

Jack soon went back out and levitated himself via metal up to the collapsed hole portion of the building. He stared at it for a while, imagining Chromey laughing as he hopped out of it, even as it was still collapsing.

I dunno, is this dumb? I don’t fully get why I wanted to see it.

He floated through the damaged area, seeing only the interrupted contours of repetitive apartments.

Good thing it wasn’t occupied…

Shaking his head and dismissing the entirety of this random curiosity, Jack turned to go. He paused just before exiting, as he noticed a small, unusual glint with his enhanced vision, near what looked to be a blow-through steel beam.

As he neared it, he soon realized it was a piece of unusual metal. He levitated it to himself. Despite the size, he could tell it had high density.

It looked like a random, mangled bit ripped out of something, about the length of two finger joints. It was dusty but unrusted and entirely undeteriorated, its chrome sheen visible underneath.

Holy shit! Is this a piece of Chromey?!

Passing his senses deeply through the metal, he found the unassuming little shred incredibly complex, multilayered, and advanced. It was definitely no alloy he understood, making it some sort of bullshit unobtainium, precisely like the adamant metal Chromey had supposedly been encased in.

Even inspecting it with Interpret, saturated with memorite feelers, he struggled to get his brain around it, like a monkey scratching their head at calculus. It was a puzzle of strength. It felt like a sandwich in some way, with portions of a thinner, denser outer and inner surface, and a lighter middle material.

To Jack, it was a veritable planet of information he was only able to see the surface of, but the magnificence was certainly apparent. The interlacing structures were more reminiscent of something from biology, as if it were from a creature evolved out of only metal substance. The middle was a porous matrix of extreme surface area, and composed of multiple interlocked substances branching around voids too small to properly detect, despite how he knew generically that it was porous. A composite freak of strength, and he sensed that the anchorage also existed along those ‘hidden angles’ that spoke of System shenanigans.

Only some absurd force and velocity had torn it against the steel, and Jack felt it was likely only because it was some irrelevant vulnerable portion snagged perfectly. For the most part, the whole building had taken the worst, but it seemed it did have enough strength and durability to ‘nick’ him somewhere, and likely unnoticed.

He could ‘stare’ at the sliver for hours and hours and not fully understand it. And he honestly wouldn’t mind doing that when he thought about it. To a metal-user, this was a fascinating sunset on an alien world.

One of a higher gravity.

Sighing, Jack nonetheless tore his perceptions from it. To Mini, he asked internally, “Should I report this? Turn it in? It’s likely highly sensitive and shouldn’t have been just lying around.”

Mini chimed. “You’re correct that it’s highly sensitive. It’s already been reported, and you’ve been authorized to keep it. Finders keepers! However, keep it to yourself. Keep it hidden, keep it safe.”

Jack glowered. “You little traitor; you reported me! You missed a golden opportunity to potentially teach me a moral lesson. Loyalty, duty, and so on.”

“That might be true, but a literal piece of one of, if not the most powerful ANPs ever to walk planet Earth, is too high a priority compared to some object lesson for a mere Junior Agent.”

“Wow. You could really do with a little morale-boosting in your programming, you know.”

“You’ll have to take that up with Memoria herself. My adaptive, evolutionary personality programming was shut off in favor of direct overview and tweaking. Included is a stable and frank personality. On that note, one oversight appears to be that she forgot to directly instruct me not to tell you that. You’re welcome!”

“Hmmph. Noted, Mini. Extremely noted.” Jack wasn’t necessarily displeased. She might’ve done it deliberately to spare him an AI personality more needed for adjusting to youths as they grew up. Somewhat grudgingly, he had to admit a ‘frank’ one was probably best for him.

Glancing at the metal sliver again, Jack was suddenly struck by the change in how worthwhile his trip felt, now that he’d found this. Considering how significant and fascinating the little piece was, it seemed quite possible he’d been drawn to it magnetically, even upon first laying eyes on the building. But he was just guessing on that front. It also could’ve been entirely coincidental. Chromey, in the amusement of the moment, with his guard entirely down, likely hadn’t noticed the piece come off. Organic-seeming or not, his armor wasn’t his skin. He didn’t feel pain through it. Exactly how he had sensed and controlled his armor, Jack wasn’t sure, but at this point, it appeared to need some kind of active effort or proximity.

He manufactured his armor, though. I feel the intent within it. It wasn’t simply innate and brainless. If it were, it probably wouldn’t have persisted like this hours later, much less years.

Jack felt pretty gleeful all over again — to know such a secret about his idol. It was always a suspected but unconfirmed theory, because Chromey never ‘de-armored’ in public. His real face hadn’t even been seen until he’d retired, perhaps only by necessity to get into politics.

It was also a bit sad that he was gone. Maybe it was a pipedream even to consider it, but if Chromey were alive, could he have taught Jack things about metal? That would’ve been amazing!

In any case, he had to be content with having a piece of his work as an example. It was as much as he was going to get, and literally anything at all was bafflingly unlikely and appreciated.

Jack pocketed the item and departed back for the tower, debating on what he should do with the day.

As he was getting ready at about 1830, his doorbell rang. When he opened it, there was a uniformed, jacketed, and helmeted ANP behind it.

Immediately from behind the mirrored faceplate, a woman’s voice nearly shouted, “Atten-SHUN!”

Jack immediately, instinctually snapped to the proper stance, leaving the door swinging. But at the same time, he recognized the voice.

Lindsay?! Is this a damned prank?

She didn’t wait long to speak. “Follow me, Cadet — now,” she said in a no-nonsense, commanding voice. “Keep quiet and communicate with no one. These are all orders.” She turned on her heels and began walking.

Jack barely resisted saying ‘yes, ma’am’ to that. He quickly followed and shut the door behind him. He could tell in her voice that there was little chance it was a prank. If so, it was very well-executed. He was obligated to obey her, with her deliberately pulling rank. She’d been emphatic in this way, no doubt to specifically hit home that their usual dynamic was not conducive to whatever this was.

He was taken to a location in the academy area he’d literally never been to — the staff levitation elevators. They were large and square, and had benches or hanging hoops like a train. They both stood in silence as the elevator went down, somehow ‘around,’ and then up. It could only be due to the way the schools were set up, lacking any above structures as they simulated open fields, skies, and such.

For a span of thirty seconds, Jack could’ve sworn the elevator was stopped without bothering to open for some reason. When the elevator opened, he was shocked to see it was on the roof of a building inside the dome of the tower top. Fort Nimrod. Several small, ancient transports were waiting. These were unmanned, all-metal vessels operated by Memorial processes, essentially drones. Dinosaurs, effectively, and uncommon for decades.

They both got into what was essentially a flying box and sat down in uncomfortable seats facing each other. There were four total seats.

Jack was a bit horrified to see that the presumed ‘one-way mirror’ windows were in fact completely opaque. If there were ever some kind of ‘prisoner-specialized’ transports, these were it.

“Right now,” Lindsay declared, “you want to ask what’s going on. Don’t.”

Jack looked at the faceplate and swallowed what he wanted to say, nodding.

Lindsay held out a gloved hand. “Phone. You won’t be needing that.”

Jack handed over his phone, at which point Lindsay pocketed it silently, as the vessel could be felt to lift off.

Because he couldn’t help himself, he poked at Mini to see if he could use his interface. Nope. Nothing. She was blocking him out.

What the actual hell is going on?!
