Chapter 394

“Dad?”

Both Ihasu and Olivia had been looking up to try to get a sense of what Bin was seeing, but at that, their heads snapped to her.

They froze.

For a good handful of seconds they just stood there, waiting for it to sink in. Then Ihasu spoke, slowly but with a firmness that felt almost odd in the moment. “Bin… What did you just say?”

Bin didn’t immediately respond, or even make any indication that she had heard her mother speak to her. Then, with a slight start, she slowly looked at Ihasu.

“Wh… What?”

“I asked you what you just said. About your father. Did you say Dad?” Ihasu flew forward and gripped her daughter’s arm, perhaps more forcefully than she would have if she had been of a mind to think about it. “What do you mean? What’s going on?”

“Dad, I… He’s here. Somewhere close.”

Olivia seemed to snap out of it. “He left the delayed Toxic Initiation for you, after all. It makes sense that a remnant of his presence was embedded as well.”

“No! It really is him! I can feel it! I’m not lying!” Bin was furious. Did they really think she would go so far as to make up a lie about something like this?

“We don’t think you’re lying, Honey,” Ihasu said softly, following Olivia’s reasoning. “Of course we don’t, but maybe it’s best to be prepared to feel him go again. This feeling might not last very long, but you can enjoy it as much as you want while it’s still here.”

“And after that, you will always carry around a very real part of your father. Beloved Daughter of Toxin.” Olivia smiled warmly.

“It’s not just a feeling!” she shouted. “I can feel the Toxic Initiation as well, but this is different! They’re separate!”

“Bin…” Ihasu tried, but a scream from outside stole their attention. They ran to the window.

“It’s your neighbor,” Olivia said and went for the door.

“What’s going on?” Ihasu shouted when they got outside. “Hey, Melanie!”

“It’s… The clouds are…” the woman stammered and pointed a trembling finger at the sky.

Following her gesture, Ihasu and Olivia inhaled sharply. The clouds were gathering and churning as if charging up for a tornado, backlit by a worrying, greenish blue, shimmering light. Melanie’s knees buckled and she fell onto her backside. “Oh… no, no, no,” she breathed, lips trembling. “It was supposed to be over already!”

Ihasu could barely rip her eyes away from the ever-growing phenomenon, but her fingers dug into Olivia’s bare arm. “Oli, take care of Bin, will you? I have to go and find Goo.” Her voice was calm, but that was probably more due to the paralyzing dread she was feeling than anything else.

“No, Ihasu, we should all go together. It’s safer that way.” Her calm looked more genuine, forged through countless life and death struggles.

“We don’t need to go anywhere,” Bin said, her eyes never leaving the cloud cover. “I know those clouds.”

Mis came sauntering out into the garden and slung herself in the grass by Bin’s feet, rolling onto her back and watching the intimidating clouds with a sense of supreme relaxation. Even more so than Bin, she seemed utterly unbothered by this phenomenon that exuded such a crushing aura.

“Mom!” a voice yelled from down the street. A little boy was running toward them.

“Goo! Where have you been? Get over here right now!”

Dashing forward at a speed that could overwhelm almost any B-ranker, he skidded to a lawn-ruining stop. “It’s my dad!” he told them with five times more excitement and animation than he had shown in total over the past thirteen months.

“You—” Ihasu stopped herself and looked up again. This time she really took it in. Really saw it for what it was. For the familiarity. For the warmth. It was her turn to have her knees buckle as she clasped a hand over her mouth for the second time that day. “But, that’s…”

With both children, intrinsically connected to their father’s power, claiming the same thing independently of each other, and a cat who should rightly have been hiding under a bed somewhere, even Olivia was now reconsidering the implications of this development.

As they watched, the cloud cover only expanded, spreading more and more rapidly with every second that passed. It wouldn’t take long for it to claim the entirety of the visible sky, from horizon to horizon.

In fact, if it continued like this, it would barely take any time at all for all of Earth to be enveloped.

It was difficult to describe exactly what it was about it, but any sense of fear seemed to evaporate. Even Melanie had sat down in the grass to simply watch it happen. The terrifyingly heavy aura somehow reminded the witnessed in Forest of a father’s loving hug.

Then, as if scattered by a shock wave that never reached them on the ground, the clouds split outward in a circle, jetting past the horizon’s line like a curtain of the universe. Blue didn’t do justice to what the sky had become. It was glittering azure, fluid, and bright as the stars. And it almost seemed to move with a will.

Ihasu couldn’t believe her eye. Her lips bobbed open and closed repeatedly while her brain struggled to convert chaotic thought into coherent words. “It’s Tox— It’s like on Toxin… Why?”

Eik had managed to bring most of his close friends and family to Toxin at least once, so they could all recognize the sight now that they were actually in a state of mind to look properly.

“Look!” Bin shouted and pointed to the sky. Something was causing the inverted ocean to roil violently. Ominously. It was like it was about to crash down onto their heads. Shrieks of terror sounded throughout the neighborhood.

A moment later, it erupted as if an underwater nuclear detonation had been triggered just a few dozen meters above the inverted surface. From the millions and millions tons of glittering liquid launched into the air, the head of a leviathan beast emerged.

“Spaghetti Jones!” Bin cried gleefully, hopping in place and waving her little hands above her head. “We’re down here, Spaghetti Jones! Down here!”

The mountainous serpent responded instantly, its enormous, slitted pupils boring into their little group. From deep within its throat, a rumble sounded like two mountains grinding against each other.

They blinked, and Spaghetti Jones was in front of them, staring.

“What’s that in your mouth?” Goo asked, patting the snout of the beast next to his sister.

Unceremoniously, a large chunk of solid crystal dropped from its maw. It measured something in the range of three meters, oval and opaque. Having delivered his package, Spaghetti Jones retreated slightly, leaving them to investigate.

Olivia was the first to put a hand on the heavy object. She smelled the skin that had come into contact with it. “Hm.” Cryptic.

“What?” Ihasu asked, putting her own hand on it. “What is it?”

“Doesn’t smell of anything.”

“All right,” the mother deadpanned. She put her eyes all the way up to the surface of the crystal. Leaning back on the balls of her feet, she barely managed to keep her voice steady. She seemed to be consciously keeping hope at bay. “There’s something in there. I can see a shape inside the crystal.”

“Bin! Goo!” It was Rasmus’s voice, but for a moment he seemed larger than he should have been as he turned the corner onto their street. “Olivia! Ihasu! Are you okay?” Once he was fully visible, they saw that he was actually riding piggy back on Andihar Dayarunar. Gul Ougi was right by their side.

Bin waved. “Grandpa! Gramps! Uncle Andi! We’re fine!”

The two S-rankers were doing Rasmus a favor by not running at a speed that would have torn the ligaments from his bones, so when they finally were in line of sight, Gul rocketed the rest of the way at full throttle. Caring arms went around his granddaughter and great grandchildren. “What happened?”

“Spaghetti Jones came down and gave us this!”

Now calm, the old man smiled. “I saw him from the fracture hall, yes. So that’s what he did, huh? What is it?”

“We don’t know,” Ihasu said, jumping in. “But there’s something inside. I can see the silhouette.” She leaned in a little closer as Andihar and Rasmus arrived. “Bin and Goo say they can feel Eik’s presence.”

Rasmus gasped, his eyes growing wide as plates, but Gul and Andihar had no outward reaction.

“Where’s Chop?”

“Here.” At once, all heads snapped to the annex where Chop lived. He was sitting on the roof, munching away on something wrapped in a paper bag.

“Since when were you there?”

The geezer pretended to look at a wristwatch—a gesture he had learned from Eik. “Well, when did you start making waffles?”

“That long?” Olivia asked, dumbfounded. “You didn’t think you should have come when we were freaking out?”

He shrugged. “Why would I? There was never anything to freak out about in the first place, after all.”

Olivia grumbled. “Then at least help us crack this damn crystal, would you?”

“Sure,” he said, and hammered the pommel of his blade into the azure chunk in the next moment, essentially teleporting from the roof. “You guys would never have managed anyway.” He smirked.

A single crack snaked down the length of the crystal, reaching all the way in to whatever was inside. Chop watched it separate with a face that seemed to already know what was inside.

It was so freaking bright here.

In fact, it was so bright that he couldn’t see a thing.

Actually, wait. No, yeah, he just had his eyes closed. That explained it.

But in that case, it was still so bright that it shone through his eyelids. Whoever had designed this bedroom should be ashamed of themselves. Such poor attention to lighting.

Eik cracked his eyes open just a bit, but quickly closed them again. Yeah, that was way too brutal.

“Dad!” Something barreled into his chest, followed by another, and another, and then another, on so on and so forth, until they were piled on top of him.

“Hey, Bin,” he said. His throat felt as if it hadn’t uttered a word before. Tight and smooth. “How sweet of you to come wake up your dad in bed.”

That’s when he felt the grass under his back, and something hard that felt like jagged rocks. “What’s going on?” he asked groggily and rolled over onto his side. People yelped as they rolled off.

Suddenly, Ihasu was in his arms, tears streaming down her cheeks. “I didn’t know you were going to come back,” she whispered into his ear. He squeezed her, unsure what exactly was going on.

When he noticed the rest of them standing around him, he began to understand. He looked from face to face. His dad was crying, and so was Oli. Gul was pretending not to, while Andihar wiped at his eyes. Chop just smirked. There was a kid on each side, hugging him tightly.

“How long was I gone?” he finally asked, dreading the answer.

It was Andihar who spoke. “Thirteen months.”

It struck Eik like a hammer. “Thirteen months? But that’s…” he breathed. “I’m sorry, I didn’t know.”

“What the hell are you apologizing for?” Gul grunted.

His father nodded and came in to join the hug, followed by Olivia. “Yeah, the fact that you’re back is more than enough.”

“But how did you come back?” Olivia asked.

Eik looked up into the endless ocean of Toxin above their heads. It now well and truly enveloped the planet, and as his senses expanded, he found himself connected to every flower and every creek. Every bird in the sky and every rock in the ground. Connected to Earth.

He scratched the back of his head. “I suppose I don’t really know. Divine will, maybe?” he said with a grin.

“I didn’t think you believed in that kind of stuff,” Ihasu said.

Eik laughed. “Gotta have a bit of self-confidence sometimes, don’t you think?” The looks he got were puzzled.

He stood up and noticed for the first time that he was naked. Armor of the Scourge instantly appeared across his body. “Whoops. Anyway, shall we get going? I’d love it if you would all tell me about everything I’ve missed while I was gone.” He took his children’s hands in his and started walking.

“Sure, but… Where are we going?” Ihasu asked as they all followed.

“To get a bunch of flowers.”

She blinked. “What for?”

He stopped and pressed his forehead against hers, smiling with love.

“Why, a wedding, of course.”
