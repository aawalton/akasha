The sun perched on the edge of the horizon, casting its final golden rays before retreating for the night. The rattling of armor and the thudding of heavy footsteps echoed along the stone walls, prompting a pair of guards to groan in relief.

“It’s about damn time,” muttered a tall soldier, his posture relaxing as he leaned against his spear.

“Quit yer bitchin’, Murphy. You know how short-handed we are,” grumbled a squat man as he nudged past. “Besides, we all know you’re headed to the Silk Pavilion after this. The girls’ll still be there no matter what time you finish.”

His jab earned a round of chuckles from the nearby guards, though Murphy remained unfazed.

“Fairy Qin plays an hour after dusk,” Murphy said flatly. “If I miss her show again, I’ll kick your ass.”

“Yeah, yeah.” The squat man waved him off with a scoff. “You should probably bathe first—you reek like a latrine that hasn’t been scrubbed in weeks.”

Laughter rang out again, louder this time.

But it died just as quickly.

The squat man, still grinning, noticed Murphy had stopped moving. A frown creased his brow. He opened his mouth to hurl another insult—only for Murphy to suddenly grip his spear and let out a curse.

“Soldiers—there’s thousands of them!” Murphy barked, pointing through a gap in the battlements.

“What banner do they fly? Are they enemies?!”

“Shit! Someone go notify the commander!”

The relaxed, jovial air vanished in an instant, replaced by shouts and scrambling feet. The unexpected sight of a massive force approaching the capital threw the garrison into chaos.

This was the capital—one of the most secure places in the kingdom. Or so they believed.

Still, these men were trained for moments like this. As the initial panic faded, the officers barked orders. Archers rushed to reinforce the walls. The large gates groaned as they swung shut. Everyone prepared for the worst.

As the mysterious army drew closer, their numbers became clear—though the fading light made it difficult to be certain.

A massive figure climbed the stone steps to the ramparts.

He was built like a war elephant—shoulders nearly twice the width of an average man, with a longsword at his side that most would wield with two hands. He needed only one. His long hair was tied in a topknot, and a scar ran down the left side of his otherwise clean-shaven face.

Soldiers stepped aside instinctively as he passed, eyes filled with recognition and respect.

“Sir Boyd, the enemy numbers nearly ten thousand. What are your orders?”

Riann Boyd said nothing at first. He stood silently atop the rampart, his eyes narrowing as he studied the silhouettes advancing through the dusk.

The silence stretched. The soldier beside him shifted nervously, sweat beading on his temple.

Finally, Boyd spoke. His deep, resonant voice rolled like distant thunder, cutting clean through the tension.

“We cannot be certain they are enemies,” he said. “There are no siege weapons in sight… and the king ordered our soldiers to return home.”

“But Sir… wouldn’t they have sent a messenger first? It doesn’t make sen—”

Riann raised a large hand, silencing the man with a single motion.“Open the gates,” he ordered simply.

The soldier flinched, hesitation flickering across his face. But when the Master of Arms turned to meet his gaze, the young man jumped into action.

As the soldiers moved around him, Riann remained still, his eyes fixed on the plains beyond the walls. The approaching army had halted. From its ranks, two figures on horseback began to ride toward the eastern gates.

Without another word, Riann turned on his heel and descended the stone steps. The groan of wood and the clatter of chains echoed around him as the gates began to open.

By the time he reached the courtyard, two guards had wordlessly fallen in beside him, flanking him with spears gripped tightly. Tension rolled off them in waves as they awaited the riders.

A few hundred feet from the gate, the horsemen stopped and dismounted.

One was a hulking man, the hilt of a greatsword rising visibly over his shoulder.

The other stood in stark contrast—leaner, more compact, at least a head shorter than his companion. Yet he moved with practiced ease, the fluid dismount of a man who had lived much of his life in the saddle.

The larger man stepped forward first, cupping his fist in greeting.

“I, Richard Boyd, greet the Master of Arms,” he said, bowing his head.

“Acting Vice-General Tristan Gale greets the Master,” the smaller man followed, his tone calm and precise.

Riann remained silent for a beat, his sharp eyes evaluating the two before him. A small frown tugged at the corners of his mouth, his eyes lingering upon Tristan for a few beats.

“Where is the second prince?”

At the question, both men lowered their heads. A tense silence followed, thick enough to cut.

“His Highness and General Drakon… were killed during the retreat.”

Riann’s towering frame stiffened. His muscles coiled with tension as if bracing against an unseen blow. A tremor passed through him before he exhaled a slow, weary sigh.

“Richard… I’m afraid you’ll bear the brunt of this,” he said quietly, his voice laced with sorrow.

“I understand, Uncle. I will accept whatever punishment the king deems necessary.”

At the mention of the king, Riann's body tensed again—barely perceptible in the dim light. After a moment, he nodded.

“Bring the men inside. I’ll take you to the Right Minister at once.”

Both Tristan and Richard cupped their fists in respectful acknowledgment and turned to retrieve their mounts. A heavy silence accompanied them, one that weighed more on Richard’s broad shoulders than on Tristan’s.

“I still don’t know if I like this plan…” Richard muttered.

Tristan placed a hand on his companion’s shoulder—a comical image, given the vast difference in their size. And yet, the gesture carried a quiet strength, a silent reassurance that seemed to settle the larger man.

Together, they rode toward the waiting soldiers.

Tristan raised his voice. “Men! Let’s go back home!”

A thunderous cheer erupted in response, echoing through the night. Despite their exhaustion, the soldiers’ spirits surged. Everything they had endured—every battle, every loss—had brought them to this moment.

Home.

And for now, that was enough.

They were finally home.

Despite the excitement humming beneath their battered armor, the soldiers marched in orderly silence toward the city gates—a testament to their discipline and training.

Rather than riding at the front, Tristan guided his mare along the outer edge of the formation, casually drifting alongside the ranks. His sharp eyes scanned the column until they landed on a soldier wearing a plain bronze helm. The armor hung awkwardly on his frame—too loose at the shoulders, sleeves slightly too long.

To the untrained eye, he blended in well enough. But under scrutiny, the oddities became harder to ignore.

Tristan looked away, pretending not to notice.

“We’re going to see the Right Minister as soon as we enter,” he said calmly, absently rubbing the neck of his mount.

“Riann greeted us at the gate. First thing he asked was the prince’s whereabouts. If things go as you say…” His voice trailed off, unfinished, as if speaking the full thought might make it real.

“What was his reaction when you mentioned the king?” asked the soldier in ill-fitting armor.

No one turned to look at him. No one even acknowledged he had spoken.

Tristan sighed, his wiry frame slouching slightly in the saddle. “It was as you said…”

“Gather everything you can—but quietly. If it gets too risky, get out. I won’t be able to do much from the outside,” the soldier replied.

Tristan finally looked at the man, his expression tight with concern.“Be careful, Luke. If they find out—”

Luke waved him off with a grin, making a shooing motion. “You’re in more danger than me, old man. My friend and I will be enjoying ourselves at the Jade Silk Pavilion.”

He threw an arm around the shoulders of another soldier beside him, who stiffened at the sudden contact.

Luke’s nose wrinkled. “Though… maybe you should bathe first. I doubt the girls will get near you in that state.”

“It’s these robes and armor you made me wear!” came a low hiss—the unmistakable voice of the second prince, sounding thoroughly displeased.

Luke only chuckled in response. “Go on, Tristan. Don’t forget—you’re my dear friend. I care more about you than this damned kingdom.”

If such words had come from another man, they might’ve stirred unrest or sparked whispers of treason. But in this case, no one flinched. No one spoke. No one even seemed to hear. It was as if the speaker didn’t exist.

Tristan let out a quiet laugh. “Alright, I’m going.”

He clicked his tongue and spurred his mare forward, breaking into a canter as he returned to the head of the column.

Luke watched him go, a flicker of concern shadowing his hazel eyes.

“Be safe, my friend…” he murmured.

Beside him, the disguised prince exhaled sharply. “I know you told me to trust you, but I really hope you know what you’re doing…”

“Me too…” Luke muttered under his breath.

“What was that?” the prince asked, incredulous.

Luke raised his hands in surrender, laughing lightly.“I’m kidding, really. All you need to do is lay low for a while. Then you’ll head back to the palace. Just bear with it a little longer, alright?”

He placed a reassuring hand on the prince’s shoulder, his smile warm and familiar.

The second prince sighed again, shaking his head.“I’m worried about my father. If what you say is true… once the traitors believe I’m dead, they might kill him and seize the throne. My younger siblings—they’re too young to be eligible for succession. But that might not stop the traitors from murdering them too.”

Luke’s grip tightened involuntarily, and a heaviness settled in his chest.“No… I don’t think that would go over well with the people,” he said after a pause. “Even if something happened to your father, they’d likely install a proxy king—use the excuse of waiting for the rightful heir to come of age.”

He spoke with confidence, but inwardly, doubt gnawed at him.

Please let that be the case... he thought grimly.If he was wrong, if things spiraled out of control, then his actions today might have sentenced the entire Marxx bloodline to death.

And no matter how much he’d adapted to the cutthroat nature of this world, the thought of condemning innocent children to such a fate...That was not something he could ignore.

At his core, Luke was still a man of principle. And he intended to remain that way.

This place will not corrupt me, he vowed silently, repeating the words like a mantra.

He felt the tension ease slightly from the prince’s shoulders, a small but telling sign that his words had offered some comfort. The two continued forward, hidden among the ten thousand soldiers as they approached the capital’s towering gates.

Luke’s heartbeat quickened.

His eyes flicked toward the guards standing at attention. As his turn came, he dipped his head, doing his best to appear unremarkable.

If they recognize us… everything I’ve planned will fall apart.

But no one spared him a second glance.

The soldiers pressed forward in orderly columns, and Luke moved with them, slipping through the gates and into the city unchallenged.

The streets were packed. Citizens had gathered to welcome the returning army, the air thick with anticipation and unease.

News had spread fast.

Yet many who waited were about to be left heartbroken.

Of the more than thirty thousand men sent to Yunxi and Xiandong… only a third had returned.

A deep solemnity hung in the air. The wails of mothers, wives, and children echoed off the buildings—raw cries of sorrow and grief. For some, joyful reunions brought relief and tears of gratitude. But for far too many, the silence of absence was louder than any sound.

Luke felt their sorrow like a punch to the gut, the cries reverberating in his bones.

Would someone cry like that for me, if I had died?

The thought struck him out of nowhere, and he faltered. He didn’t belong to this world—not truly. But there were people here who mattered to him now.

Sebastian. Kayson. Brian. Tristan… Victoria.

Luke clenched his jaw and forced the thought away.I’m not dying. Not yet.

His heart beat steady and strong, like the pounding rhythm of a war drum.

I will survive.

And I will fix this godforsaken place… even if I have to burn it to the ground and rebuild it from ash.

Starting with the Marxx Kingdom.
