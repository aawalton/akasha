Envy was a rarely felt emotion for me, and I felt it in droves whenever I spent time around Joey. Sometimes I wished I could be like him—I did that more often than not, actually. He just seemed like he could find fulfillment in everything, regardless of how mundane or extraordinary. If he did anything, he did it with all of himself and held nothing back. Intense? Maybe, but that sort of intensity fit him well. The maddening part is that he did all that without a drop of venom. It was like all the anger he was supposed to have was given to me instead.

Hell, a couple of hours before, I told him his girlfriend was dead and gone. What did he feel about that? He was distraught, frightened, confused, and hopeful in a way. What he wasn't was angry, and I wished I could be like that and just let the world roll off their shoulders, and to be kind without trying, and to want to help. I wanted to be that person. I just couldn't reconcile that fictional image with the path before me.

It's why, in that moment, I left Joey with the convertible, as he gave whimsical lies to the gaggle of children, I vowed to never run an Instance with him. If he wanted to run one, I wouldn't stop him. I'd give him some extra Presence to ensure Ananke didn't take him, but Joey and I in the same Instance wouldn't happen. We'd just be liabilities to each other. Instances thrived off of hesitation and cheating. All it would take for Joey to lose would be to dangle a suffering person in front of him.

He'd try to help, and I'd do what we'd need to win. A recipe for disaster, so I wasn't going to engage with it as an option. If Maerin or the others wanted to go with him, all the better, but it'd be without me.

'Carlson wants to talk to us. Seems nice. Be wary.' Maerin whispered in my head.

Speak of the devil, and there she is. I chuckled at the joke and got the equivalent of an unimpressed mental stare from our shared link.

'Why wary?' I asked. I started stepping through the crowd. My natural height and build forced me to squeeze awkwardly through the crowd.

'I think he's suspicious. Clearly grateful we found his friend, but suspicious,' she said.

I didn't give a response back, just continued pushing my way through the crowd until I reached the edge of the largest crowd. They circled Maerin and three others: the paladin, Minevera, and another figure who looked like they surfed daily. Barrow, if Minevera's description was accurate. I caught Maerin's eye, and with great relief, she waved me in, freeing me from the crowd.

The armored man, Carlson, gave me an appraising look. We were about eye level with me, eaking out an extra half inch if you included my hair—I saw it as only fair on account of his plated boots.

"My name's Carlson," the man held out his hand

I took it. "Dorian," I said in kind.

"I can't thank you enough for finding Minerva. Our bodies are next to each other, if one of us were to fall I fear we all would."

I nodded slowly. They still thought the Dream was a curse of some kind. Actually, that may not be inaccurate. The pressure of the past couple of days and the constant action had dulled my curiosity. I knew some things about it, but basic things like 'Where is the Dream located' had barely gotten a passing thought. Calling it a curse may be closer to the truth than what I assumed.

"Yeah, that'd be bad," I agreed.

The crowd still made my skin crawl. I didn't like this many people or being surrounded on all sides. They were staring at us. I felt their eyes. I'd have done a lot of things to stop them from staring at that moment.

Maerin proved that maybe there was a use for her being inside my skull by extricating us from the situation, "Forgive us, but we've been in the Dream for a while now. This is all a bit overwhelming. Is there a place we can go and talk without so many eyes?" She looked between me and the crowd with a worried expression. Somehow, she seemed more concerned for the crowd than me. She didn't need to be. I had already agreed to help Joey help these people. How I would do that was still up in the air because I honestly had no clue.

"Of course. Barrow could you take them to the side hall? I'm going to give Minerva here a tour."

Both of us let Barrow guide us to an empty house. The interior was completely empty, with only a few mismatching pieces of furniture. Seeing our confusion, Barrow spoke up. "Happens a lot in the Dream. There's a lot of buildings, but they rarely have anything inside of them. Sometimes they're completely solid. Only echoesecho's hold mundane detail."

"Echo's?" I asked.

"Places the Dream grabs from people's minds and thoughts. The curse is … strange, but it plays by rules and has goals. It's looking for something and as long as we help it it'll let us go." Barrow said.

The gangly man spoke smoothly and confidently, as if he didn't have a care in the world and everything would be alright. I smelled bullshit. I'd heard that type of voice before. Jeren used it a lot. The voice of someone trying to soothe an animal. I resented being treated like an incompetent instantly.

"Right," I said.

Maerin picked up on something else, "How do you know so much about the Dream?" Her tone was guarded and probing, and I knew she already had a follow-up to press him.

The man put a finger to his lips, "Can't talk about state secrets. Shouldn't have brought it up at all." He then laughed. "Now, if you'll excuse me I'm going to check in on Minerva." The man left.

Maerin didn't get the opportunity to use whatever line she prepared and huffed in frustration.

"I think he just snubbed us," Maerin said.

"What does that even mean here?" I asked.

"Well, I was rooting around in Minerva's head."

"As one is naturally to do." She rolled her eyes, and I chuckled.

Internally, I was more than a little worried. The mind was supposed to be the one safe space. Instance Holders and Ananke shouldn't be able to touch them, and thanks to Rowan, I had just learned that the line between Instance Holder and Visitor was thinner than I had thought. Which begged the question: how the hell was Maerin doing it so easily? Either she was the only person in the history of the Dream to figure out a way to reliable get into people's heads, or my thoughts weren't as safe as I thought. I wasn't sure which result worried me more.

"Well, I was rooting around in her head, and I found a lot of information about their society and politics. By leaving, he's one, being a bad host, and two, giving us time to coordinate. It's a subtle way of saying, 'I know you're up to something, go ahead and get your story straight.'

"Maerin," I sighed. "I'm pretty sure I could kill most of them with a thought. We don't exactly need to worry about being snubbed."

She paused, not seeming to have considered that possibility.

"The only person that we know of that can directly harm either of us outside of an Instance are each other." I continued. "Speaking of, I want my half of the bridge."

She glared at me, but gave up when I matched her gaze. "I'll make the bridge, you can just take control over it." She said. I almost asked if she could do that, but stopped myself. If she said she could, she could, that's literally how it worked in the Dream, and as long as I didn't question it.

It took around eight minutes for her to make her mind construct, and another ten for me to take stable control of it, but once I did, I could feel her thoughts. I couldn't see all of it at once. It was a confusing maelstrom, and most of what I felt were vague impulses rather than coherent thoughts. I had to focus on a particular strand and—

'Hello!' Maerin jump scared me in her mind.

I fell back in my chair, sprawled out onto the floor, and cursed.

"Why was that so loud!" I shouted in exasperation.

"I just cleared my mind. You wouldn't not how loud things were, so this was my only chance to do this."

I couldn't help it. I laughed. She laughed too.

"At least we know the feedback gate you wanted works." I offered. It was one of the many safety nets she had insisted on. I was content with letting her handle not getting us killed.

"Full mental communication will be useful. Will it work in an Instance you think?"

I nodded, "I think as long as it's not something you actively maintain it'll work? If Belief doesn't work on Boons, Restrictions and the like it makes me think it won't work inside an Instance either."

The door to the side hall creaked open. I was still on the floor. Minerva, Carlson, and Barrow entered and froze. Carlson let a confused expression touch his face before looking to his two companions. "Is everything alright Dorian?"

"Yep, just leaned too far back," I shrugged.

"Well, I think our cover is blown?"

"Why?"

"People from — would never be caught dead lying on the floor. Cultural thing."

Perfect. I shook my head. Pretending was a waste of time anyways. Other people needed to pretend, people without power or bargaining chips. We had both, so our little disguises meant nothing. If I wanted Joey's hope of helping these people to become reality and have them help us as well...well I'd have to be proactive, not reactive.

'Don't fight me on this. Trust.'

I focused on the room. I felt for the space. Eight corners. Six walls. Empty space. No. No. I need to be careful. Not empty space. It's full of air. There's a door. It's a metal double door. I didn't remember the color, so I just thought of it as blue. I thought of an interior. A meeting room. Long oaken table, ovular, with chairs on either side, a projector on the table, and a screen behind me—a fake office plant in one corner and a water cooler in the other. The chairs would be black mesh office chairs. I imagined glass and a city beyond—

Pain. Head-splitting pain. It almost felt like how it did when I died and came back in my world. I felt shattered, like I existed in multiple places at once, and the vertigo almost made me throw up, but I held on. I opened my eyes, and the scene was set. I took a seat at the head of the table. I glanced at the windows and almost burst out laughing when I saw a brick wall on the other side.

Maerin was quick on the uptake. I felt her Belief like a physical object brush against the table. The edge I sat at flattened and elongated, leaving room for two seats. She wheeled a chair beside me and took a seat, and motioned for the others to do the same. Minerva was wide-eyed. Carlson was all smiles, but I saw his hand on the hilt of his sword. His other hand pushed the knives in Barrow's hands down.

"Sorry for the deception," Maerin shook her head as if she was truly sad about it. I was in her head, though. I knew she didn't give a shit, especially after getting snubbed. Wow, she was holding onto that way more than I would've thought. "But it pays to be careful," she said with a smile.
