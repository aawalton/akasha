The impact came with a sickening boom unlike anything Ace had ever heard—part crunch, part scream, part deafening squelch.

The Behemoth's massive bulk crashed into the ground and pulverized the boulder Rachel had chosen as cover. The rock simply ceased to exist, reduced to nothing more than powder that billowed outward in a thick cloud.

In the terrible stillness that followed, the cloud of dust thinned. A lone hand protruded from underneath the monster, its fingers splayed in a final, silent plea for help. The pale skin had split open. Bone fragments poked through the shattered forearm, visible even from a distance.

Rachel was dead.

The thought struck Ace like a physical blow. His chest tightened, his lungs refusing to draw in air as he stared at her shattered hand. A teammate, dead. Another summoned hero, gone.

Another soldier lost under his watch.

That was how it felt, anyway, even though this wasn't Afghanistan. The surge of guilt slayed him, and it took concentrated effort to stay focused on the fight. He’d only ever lost one soldier in the Corps—and, years later, that man’s final moments still sometimes haunted Ace at night.

Now, he’d lost two.

For a moment, the Behemoth lay motionless. Its ragged breaths vibrated through the soil. A pool of dark red blood snaked out from underneath its body—slowly at first, nothing more than a ribbon or two, but it thickened with each passing second until rivers of Rachel’s blood snaked down the hill. The streams dipped and dove across the grass, filling every depression in the soil, soaking into the thirsty dirt.

The silence that followed held a physical weight, pressing against his eardrums, against his sanity itself. No screams. No final words. The analytical mind that had neurotically tracked every variable, the woman who had meticulously calculated survival probabilities and optimized every encounter—had simply been erased.

The Behemoth adjusted slowly, as though it were still dazed, and it pushed off the ground with both hands. As it lifted itself off the patchy grass, a nauseating squelch echoed through the hills. Chunks of flesh and bone stuck to the barbs on its back—parts of Rachel that no longer resembled anything human. The wounds Olivia had managed to inflict were already sealing themselves as fresh armored plates grew over the damaged tissue, green light pulsing beneath each new growth like a heartbeat.

Ace wanted to run over there. He wanted to check, to know for sure whether or not she was beyond saving. She was his teammate, after all, someone he had sworn to protect.

But this was war.

As much as he hated himself for it, Ace turned his back on the fray and ran to Tara. He reached her just as she scrambled to her feet, her attention shifting between Rachel’s flattened corpse and Olivia’s unconscious body.

Their healer pushed off a tree, ready to race into the Highlands to help, but Ace grabbed her waist and pulled her back down to the ground. A jagged rock soared above them, shooting straight through the air where her head had been a second before.

Together, Ace and Tara rolled into a hollow formed by exposed tree roots, momentarily hidden from the monster's sight.

"Let me go!" Tara struggled against his grip. “Ace, maybe I can save her! Maybe I can save them both.”

"You’ll go down if you run out there.” Ace pinned her shoulders to the tree trunk in an effort to hold her still. "Think, Tara. Tactical assessment. Clear head. You’re a paramedic, for fuck’s sake. Focus!"

The words penetrated her panic. She stilled, her healer's instincts evidently warring with her sense of self-preservation. “Fine. But Ace, my Soul Meter is draining fast. I can’t sustain this. We have to end this fight already.”

“I know,” he said.

They shared a knowing glance, and Tara’s eyes widened in recognition.

They both knew what they had to do.

With a deep breath to steady herself, Tara squeezed her eyes shut and tilted her head away from him. Her fingers hooked on her collar, and she pulled it away from her neck, giving him full access to the blood just beneath her skin.

"Make it count," she whispered, her voice steady despite the tremble of fear in her hand. He could smell her terror, her adrenaline, even the coppery blood in her veins. His enhanced senses could detect them all like individual notes in a macabre symphony.

Carefully, he cradled Tara's head with one hand, supporting her neck as she exposed her throat. Her veins glowed softly red beneath her pale skin, a delicate thread that seemed to call to something ancient inside him.

Something ravenous.

She scrunched her face in preparation for pain. His fangs lengthened without a conscious command, his primitive vampire instincts bypassing rational thought. The soldier in him began calculating angles, pressure, depth—the perfect strike to extract maximum benefit with minimal damage.

The monster in him, however, ached.

His body shook as he fought the sudden urge to rip out her throat. This wasn't some enemy to be drained on a battlefield. This wasn’t a throwaway monster. This was Tara—the one person who'd offered him genuine understanding in this nightmare.

The soldier in him wanted to maintain control. The vampire in him wanted to surrender to instinct. His thoughts pooled together, loose and loud while his thumb rested against her throat. She swallowed hard, her breath quickening, and that only made his deteriorating self-control slip further.

He didn’t know which side of him would win.

If this killed her—if he lost control—he would never forgive himself.

The Behemoth roared, close enough now that chunks of bark and fetid breath washed over them. Marcus shouted incoherently as he raced toward Olivia, who was still sprawled across the grass. Each of the Blood Dancer’s ragged breaths was potentially her last. Victor lunged out of the way as the Behemoth swung at him, and the man cast a sidelong glance at Ace—no doubt trying to figure out how to use him as bait, too.

There was no time for hesitation.

His lips brushed against her neck, a gentleness at odds with the violence around them. He caught the subtle shift in her scent—fear intermingling with something more complex and primal. Her fingers dug into his shoulder, neither pushing him away nor pulling him closer, but simply anchoring herself against what was to come.

The first puncture was surgical, and he managed to restrain his inner vampiric hunger. Her blood bloomed against his tongue like liquid starlight. The sweet, heady taste carried information his conscious mind couldn't process, but which his transformed body instinctively understood—blood type, hormone levels, genetic markers, power signatures. Fragments of Tara in chemical form.

And it tasted like heaven.

His grip on her tightened possessively. She gasped, her body tensing against his. Not entirely from pain, either, as her palm flattened against his chest. The sound rippled through him, awakening instincts he had been fighting since his transformation. His arm—still around her waist—pulled her closer, pinning her to his body as he drank deeper. Each sip filled him with a strange intimacy he didn’t understand.

It was a connection neither of them had intentionally granted.

Images flickered behind his eyelids—Tara hunched over a keyboard, directing a digital night elf through a dragon fight with practiced efficiency; Tara with gloved hands pressed against a stranger’s artery, her face calm despite the blood soaking through her uniform; Tara alone in a pitch-black apartment, staring at city lights through rain-streaked windows, an emptiness inside her that mirrored his own.

Memories.

Her memories, carried in her blood, transferring between them like data packets in a corrupted download.

His Soul Meter surged. Power filled the hollow spaces inside him, rushing through his veins like liquid lightning. The magical energy coiled around his spine, branched through his limbs, and pulsed beneath his skin with an almost painful intensity. His muscles twitched with renewed strength as the cold emptiness that had plagued him vanished in a wave of heat.

With the power came clarity, scrubbing away the fog of exhaustion that had clouded his mind. His senses sharpened to knife-edge perfection—the scents in the air separated into distinct notes, sounds clarified into perfect pitch, and his vision gained a predatory focus that made the world seem to slow around him.

He could think again. Each thought crystallized with brilliant precision where before they'd been sluggish and muddled. Focus returned like an old friend, bringing with it a heady confidence that bordered on euphoria. Hell, even his breath came easier, though he didn't technically need to breathe anymore.

The sensation reminded him of his first kill as a vampire—that same intoxicating rush of strength and potential that made him feel invincible. The power whispered dark promises in the back of his mind, urging him to use it, to revel in it, to become the apex predator he was meant to be.

He could hear the Behemoth's movements with stunning accuracy. He could detect the specific timbre of Marcus's breathing amongst the others in the field. He could feel the microscopic tremors in Tara's body as her strength began to wane.

Too much.

He was taking too much.

With effort that felt like ripping muscle from his own bones, Ace carefully pulled away. Two perfect puncture wounds marked Tara's neck, and beads of blood seeped slowly from the wound as he struggled against the urge to go in for more. Instinctively—before he could even think about it, much less stop himself—he ran his tongue over the wound, and he instantly hated himself for it.

The Behemoth's roar brought him back to reality. He forced himself to pull away from her body, from her blood. His hands still held her shoulders as she slumped against him. Her eyes were unfocused, her skin paler than usual.

"You okay?" he asked.

She nodded weakly.

A lie.

"Soul Meter?" she asked.

"Back to full!” the System replied for him. The girl’s voice echoed through Ace’s skull in the seconds before she popped into existence beside them. “More than enough!”

Doing his best to ignore the floating menace beside them, Ace gently lowered Tara to the ground. The paramedic’s blonde hair splayed out around her head like a halo as her eyes flitted shut.

“Stay here,” he ordered. “I'll handle this."

Though she muttered something in reply, he couldn’t make sense of it beyond a quiet hum of agreement.

With renewed strength surging through his veins—his new borrowed power carrying echoes of Tara's essence—Ace shifted his full attention toward the raging Thornhide Behemoth. The creature's bark-plated body shuddered as it swiped at Victor, who pivoted just beyond its reach. The man's shadow-forged whip cracked through the air like a thunderbolt, but the monster pivoted out of the way. Those pulsing green nodes flared beneath its wooden skin as it dodged the attack that would have severed its neck.

And, to Ace’s frustration, its healing had already repaired both of its eyes.

He shifted his attention to the System, and he didn’t hold back as he glared at her. “We can kill it by spearing it through the eye, huh?”

“Oopsie,” she said with a wicked little giggle. “Guess I was wrong! Sorry, Sergeant Grumpy Pants!”

Ugh.

Carefully, with enough stealth that the beast had no idea he even existed, Ace circled through the treeline until he was a safe distance from Tara. Ideas snapped through his thoughts like the rapidly-flicking pages of a book as he debated his next move.

Duskblades: available.

Shadow Mark: functional.

Lifeforce Siphon: functional.

Abyssal Armory: accessible.

He knelt beside an ancient oak, his mind racing through combat scenarios with the cold efficiency he'd developed during his years as a soldier. The monster's movements followed a pattern, now that he could get a clear view of it without being actively hunted—left swipe, counterbalance with the right leg, pivot, overhead smash. A rhythm born of instinct rather than thought.

His enhanced senses caught every subtle tell. The way its weight shifted before each attack. The way those green nodes pulsed brighter just before it struck.

His first thought was to use a Duskblade to strike the base of its neck where the bark armor seemed thinnest, but he dismissed the idea almost immediately. With the way Victor darted across the hillside, drawing the monster’s attention, Ace would never be able to stay in its blindspot for long enough to make a critical hit.

He needed something better.

As Ace debated his options, Victor darted past, and something clicked. Now that the man had no one left to use as bait, Victor was drawing the Behemoth's attention with each strike of his whip. It remained focused on him, even as he kept trying to lead it toward Marcus and Olivia.

Huh.

For once, Victor's ego might serve a purpose.

The Behemoth's weak points were in its joints and those glowing nodes, which were protected by the thickest plates of its bark armor. However, each time the creature attacked, the plates separated slightly as its body stretched, exposing the nodes for a fraction of a second.

If Victor could force it to overextend...

…then Ace had a real chance of finishing this.

Ace would have to time this perfectly, and he couldn’t risk missing even one blow. Shadow Mark would allow his Duskblades to find their targets with unerring accuracy, even if the beast tried to escape at the last second.

The challenge would be getting close enough to apply the Mark without being detected—but that was where Shadow Realm Immersion came into play.

Victor was still bolting around the monster, seemingly oblivious to Rachel's broken body nearby. That cold indifference hardened something in Ace's chest. If his plan worked, Victor would be in the direct path of the Behemoth's death throes. Maybe he'd get caught in the collapse. Maybe he wouldn't.

Ace didn't care much either way.

As his plan settled into place, he glared out at the Thornhide Behemoth—and at Victor, who landed on the ground beside Rachel’s lifeless body. The merc stared up at the beast as if he weren’t standing by a corpse.

In fact, the man acted as though she had never existed at all.

And, for Ace, that was the final straw.
