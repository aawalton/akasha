Turning around, Luke saw Ella sitting there, her eyes open, and a somewhat confused, thoughtful expression on her face.

"You do?" Luke asked. "You see the weave?"

"Sort of," she began as she got to her feet. Then she tilted her head and made a face. "Well, maybe not. Not the way you describe it. Except kind of."

He waited for her to continue as Riak let out a sigh of relief, mana flooding back into him now that Luke released him.

"I'm fast," Ella said.

Luke nodded. "How fast?"

"You read comic books?"

"It's been a while now, but yeah. Sure."

She took a step forward, then bent over and slapped her thighs with both palms. "I'm faster."

"Right."

"When I go very fast, I see something."

"Except it's not a weave, is it?" Luke asked.

Guide had alluded to that, hadn't he? He'd said something about other individuals on Earth being able to do what Luke did when he almost let the void in. At that time, Guide hadn't mentioned anything about other Lifeweavers, just other people. It made sense that they wouldn't see the exact same thing as him. The weave would just be the way his mind interpreted the system.

"No, it's not cloth or string at all." Ella waved her hand in the air, as if trying to find the right word for her experience. "Maybe like stars, except they are lines. I'm so fast, they turn into a blur. Sometimes it feels like I can reach out and touch the stars."

Luke couldn't help but stare at her as he asked the next question. "And what is beyond the stars?"

"Nothing, I think," she said with a shudder. "The end. I don't want to go there."

"The void," Luke said.

Ella took a step back. "I don't want to go there."

"I'm not going to force you," Luke said. "But I think you might be able to close portals too."

"Really?" Tilde said, and Riak echoed her sentiment.

Luke nodded and answered without looking back at them. "Yeah. It's not a Lifeweaver thing, I'm thinking. It could be anyone." With a nod of encouragement, he continued his questions directed at Ella. "Do you have a skill for teleportation? Anything to affect the world around you?"

She looked over Luke's shoulder at Tilde as she answered. "Well, yes. But-"

"Then you should practice closing portals."

"How?"

Luke laughed. "I have no idea, but we should find out."

What followed was a frustrating hour of attempted guided meditation that led nowhere. After that failed, Ella raced around the large room. At first, she ran at incredible speed, but that was just the start. Increasing the speed little by little, she was soon nothing more than a blur passing by. Once she got going for real, the Rusher moved too fast for the naked eye to even perceive her. It was impressive as hell, and Luke soon turned off Weaver's Perception since following her through his senses made him queasy. Talking when she was going like that was impossible, so they resorted to messages.

Ella: "I can see it now."

Luke: "What happens if you touch it?"

Ella: "It's not an object I can pick up."

Ella: "The stars are everywhere and everything, but at the same time, they are nothing."

Luke: "They are part of the metaphysical realm, not the physical. You'll have to shift your understanding to reach them."

Ella: "I don't understand."

It wasn't a matter of a language barrier, at least not in regard to the English language, but explaining the concepts he had arrived at while experimenting and almost dying more than a couple of times proved difficult. Perhaps someone more well-versed in physics would be able to put better words to the concepts Luke was trying to explain, but repeating the same thing over and over again with different synonyms wasn't it. After another hour, they were both frustrated, while the others were bored out of their minds.

Tilde was to the one to call it. "Ella, vi har en tidsbegrensning. Kanskje det er på tide å gi opp?"

The speedster stopped, coming into focus. "It is rude to speak so they don't understand, Tilde."

"Jeg bryr meg ikke. Vi må tilbake til Oslo. Jeg har pasienter."

"There are more things for us to try," Luke said, not quite ready to give up.

"Tomorrow?" Ella asked, giving him a pleading look. They were launching their attacks the day after tomorrow. Since neither Tilde nor Riak had managed, that didn't leave much room to work with, but Luke needed some time to pull together everything he needed for what he wanted to try. Even so, he was about to argue that what they were practicing was of more importance than a few patients. A shock of sensation stopped him, and he went from tired and exasperated to full of energy in a second. "Tomorrow morning," he said, reaching out to give her a quick heal. "I'll find you." He headed toward the plaque to leave the room, waving goodbye. "See ya!"

An echo had appeared, one he'd been expecting for a good long while now. Hannah.

In a matter of ten seconds, he left the training hall, then the guild headquarters. The moment he breathed the fresh air of a city in ruins, Luke concentrated, grabbed the echo thread, and weavestepped to Hannah, then away from her with a yelp.

What he found wasn't his long-lost friend and potential girlfriend, but a sphere of undulated shadows with what looked like flames rising from it, clawing at the air. It was silent but radiated a sense of danger he didn't recognize.

"Hannah?" he asked. They were in a pine forest, in front of a small wooden cabin that looked like it had seen better days, with red paint chipping, the roof partially caved in, and the glass in the windows broken in several places. When he spoke, the shadows reached for him. Rather than step away, he put his hand out and let them grab him. She was in there, that much was certain, but maybe she needed him to pull her out.

There was great force in that shadow, but rather than let it slither up his arm, Luke held fast and squeezed. "Hannah!"

More tendrils of shadow reached out to him, and together they pulled him in, squeezing and pulling, but they were tired, he felt, and lacked the strength he'd seen in Hannah before. It must've been quite the ordeal she'd been through in that dungeon. Now she was out, but it had changed her somehow. Even from outside, and with her engulfed in shadows, Weaver's Perception picked up that much, at least.

Using a thread of mana, he reached into the shadows. That didn't work. Surrounded on all sides, the thread didn't last long enough to reach Hannah. On the second attempt, he made it farther. Focusing with all he was worth, Luke willed the third thread to keep its shape and rebuff the shadows attempting to destroy it. His mind felt like it was going to burst from the pressure, but he refused to let go. She was in there, and he wouldn't stop until he got her out.

Inch by inch, he reached, pushing through the sometimes solid-feeling shadow, until the tip of his thread reached Hannah's shoulder. As soon as his thread of mana made it into her mana channels, the shadows seemed to implode, and the sphere contracted, pulling into itself and into her, disappearing to wherever her shadows went.

All that was left was a skinny, pale, and very naked Hannah on the ground. Luke was there a second later, covering her with a robe he purchased from the Integrated Shop.

"Hannah?" he asked, his face close to hers.

She stirred, let out a soft groan, and opened her eyes. Looking into them was like staring into the void itself. They were the darkest black without a hint of white or her usual eye color. It was startling enough that he almost pulled back, but he stopped himself. "Hannah?"

"L-Luke?" she whispered, blinking. As she came to, the blackness drained from her eyes and into her face, turning into moving, writhing tattoos. The tattoos expanded across her body until he was pretty sure they covered every inch of her.

"I'm here," Luke said. "You're safe."

There was nothing wrong with her health-wise, but something was going on. The tattoos began to swirl in a circle, all of them pulled into a vortex with the epicenter right in the middle of her chest, above the meta-heart.

"It hurts," she whimpered, her eyes returning to their old selves. Tears ran down her cheeks, and Luke got closer, wrapping his arms around her shoulders as he watched the tattoo growing smaller and smaller until nothing more than a dot remained. Then that, too, disappeared, leaving unblemished skin. The shadows were still there. They had just settled inside Hannah, but in the metaphysical realm, away from prying eyes. Some, however, oozed out on top of her head, hiding among the strands. That's when Luke noticed her hair. It was longer, like a lot longer, by a foot at least, and she had bangs now. They were uneven at best.

"Did you cut your own hair in there?" Luke asked as Hannah settled down, the rigidity of pain leaving her body.

After a moment of long, deep breaths, she opened her eyes again. "What?"

"Nothing," Luke said. "Nothing at all. I'm just glad you're back."

"The void," she whispered, reaching up with a hand toward the tops of the trees.

Luke leaned in. "What about it?"

Pain seemed to course through her again, and she grunted, her face twisting. "The void, Luke."

"What?" he asked, coming even closer.

Her eyes opened wide. "It's coming! The void is coming!"
