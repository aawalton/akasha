The cursor blinked at the end of the final paragraph. John Acrinoe sat back in his chair, letting out a long, uneven breath as he flexed his fingers.

Done.

He hadn’t planned to write a novel. He’d been… urged, for lack of a better word. At first just little compulsions to sit at the keyboard instead of grading papers. Then whole sentences flowering in his head like someone whispering just behind his ear. Words he never would have thought of normally with phrases and memories that weren’t his own. His dreams of stars, steel, gunfire, impossible choices had forced him to write.

And a small voice, always a voice, deep and calm but faintly amused: Tell them about me. And now it was finished.

The story of Max; an uplifted, augmented dog who fought and clawed against incredible odds to help his family and find his way. A creature of honor and curiosity who had touched something he shouldn’t have, an alien artifact beyond anyone’s comprehension, and paid the price.

It had been a story worth telling, despite its strange predictions and crazy technology. John smiled faintly as he saved the document and closed the laptop. He wasn’t the most tech savvy guy in the world. He’d leave that to his friends at the university.

“You’re welcome,” he muttered, almost to himself. He could still feel that presence in the back of his mind, faint but satisfied. Then the doorbell rang.

He glanced at the clock and shook his head. It was already dark outside. He rose to answer it and found his friend from the university, Sebastian, on the stoop with his dark curls windblown and his long coat trailing behind him. He was carrying a bottle of wine and grinning ear to ear. John opened the door.

“Well?” Sebastian said, without preamble. “Is it done?”

John stepped aside and gestured him in. “It’s done.”

Sebastian laughed and clapped him on the shoulder. “Congratulations, you madman. I told you you’d finish it before the next term.”

John shrugged, smiling despite himself. “You did. Come on. There’s someone else you should meet.”

They walked through the quiet little house, past the office with its stacks of books and scraps of manuscript, to the laundry room where the dog bed was laid out. Queenie, his sleek black shepherd, lay curled up there, looking exhausted but proud. And nestled against her belly were five squirming, soft little puppies.

Sebastian crouched down immediately, his face lighting up. “Oh my god. Oh my god, John. Why didn’t you lead with this?”

John chuckled, leaning against the doorframe. “Figured I’d save the best for last.”

The pups squeaked and wriggled against one another, impossibly small and fragile. Queenie thumped her tail against the bed, giving John a tired look before setting her head back down.

One of the puppies—smallest of the litter—was trying to climb over its siblings, its ears sticking out at odd angles, one of them flopping forward awkwardly.

Sebastian pointed at it. “That one. Look at him. The one with the bent ear. He’s perfect.”

The voice in John’s head stirred again. It was no longer calm or amused in the slightest. There was the faintest sense of resistance, like a thread pulling loose. John felt a little dizzy.

No… not yet. Not yet…

The presence, the one that had whispered and prodded him for months, was slipping away. John could feel it. He pressed his hand to his temple instinctively as the tugging grew sharper, more insistent. And just before it was gone, one final impulse pulsed through him like a heartbeat.

Tell him my name.

Then there was silence. His double inner monologue was suspiciously quiet after so very long. John blinked and shook his head, thinking he might be a little cracked from working too hard on the book. The room felt emptier somehow, though nothing had changed. He drew in a quiet breath, held it, and crouched down next to his friend.

Sebastian looked up expectantly. “What do you think?” he asked. “He looks like a… what? I need a good name for him.”

John studied the little puppy as it began to crack open its eyes for the very first time, staring back with a strange, bright intelligence. His bent ear twitched…almost in something akin to recognition.

John smiled faintly. “He looks like a Max to me,” he said.

Sebastian grinned. “Yeah? You know… yeah. He really does. Look at him go. Maximum Effort Mitchell. It’s got a nice ring to hit. It’s nice to finally meet you, Max.”

John reached out, and the tiny puppy’s paw clutched at his finger, warm and alive and full of possibility. Somewhere, deep down, John thought he heard the faint echo of a laugh. And then it was gone.

Sebastian poured the wine, and they shared a drink while the pups wrestled and tumbled about, each taking their turn to nurse. The little one with the bent ear—Max—seemed determined to keep up with his larger siblings despite his size.

Too soon, Sebastian gathered his things, promising to pick up everything needed for a new puppy. "I can come back tomorrow if you want," he said, already reaching for his coat.

John shook his head, smiling as he watched Max burrow deeper into his mother's warmth. "No rush. He's still too small, and honestly? A little extra time with Queenie will do him good. His brothers and sisters had a head start on him."

Sebastian paused at the door. "You're right. Let him get his strength up." He grinned. "Besides, I need time to puppy-proof my apartment. See you in a couple days?"

"See you then," John said, closing the door behind his friend.

With Sebastian gone, the apartment felt suddenly too quiet. John's eyes drifted to the open laptop on his desk, the cursor still blinking at the end of his manuscript. The ending felt too abrupt, somehow, unfinished.

He picked up Max—careful not to disturb the sleeping mother—and settled at the keyboard. As he began to type a quick epilogue, he felt something brush against his mind again, that familiar presence returning like a whisper.

John smiled down at the tiny puppy in his palm and typed the last words that came to him:

The story isn't done. It’s happening differently already. I'll try to remember and pull it back into alignment. I'll fix this… and make sure we win at the end this time. I’ve just got to live through the next hundred years, holding on to my memory through my fuzzy and dull dog years, two rejuvenations, and an uplift procedure. No problem…. I hope.
