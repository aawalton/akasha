The next morning Ferris received breakfast and a sack lunch from Mother Lily, then headed to the library bright and early. As Hemon had explained yesterday, Ferris would not be assigned a job until after he had bonded. Instead, Elder Harper would assist him in choosing the item and core type to bond while exploring potential research projects.

The library was a grand building, towering above him. Ferris had never seen such a large building and had to take a couple minutes to appreciate it before he could enter.

The entry hall was a large room with, oddly enough, no books. Rules were clearly written for newcomers, which Ferris spent a few minutes reading. People would queue up to talk to an attendant, who would usually escort them to a private room. Books would be brought to the room based on request. Only the library staff would actually enter the back areas that stored knowledge.

Paper books would only be used for mundane or reference information; Ferris assumed written words were too vulnerable to theft. Instead, cultivation secrets would be stored on magical jade slips… somehow. He knew Adepts could copy information to their soulspace, so there was often no need for a physical copy unless you were sharing with someone else. The rules stated it was possible to purchase a copy, but the clan regulated what information was allowed to leave the library.

Ferris did not see much security, but he knew this would be one of the most heavily guarded buildings in the clan. He’d heard about formation experts that could create magical defenses, but it was described as an expensive and complex process. Of course, most of what he knew came from stories, so it was hard to tell which parts were real. There were no obvious glowing magical effects, so whatever protections they had were hidden.

When Ferris made it to the front of a line, he showed them his identity token and told them he was expected by Elder Harper. The attendant led him to a large room on the second floor. The building had large, grand staircases, so it took him a short while to hop up the steps. The experience made him feel a little thankful to Mother Lily for his room on the ground floor, despite not wanting special consideration for his leg. Reaching the room at last, Elder Harper was already there, surrounded by books.

“Ah, Ferris Bluereef, is it morning already?” She looked up from the tome she had been reading.

Ferris was surprised by the number of books. There must be at least thirty of them organized neatly on the large table in the center of the room. There were more in this single room than he’d ever seen in his entire life.

Like everyone else back home, Ferris learned to read and write with chalkboards. The lessons had been very thorough; literacy was known to help unlock the soul, after all. This included reading from a few precious textbooks under supervision. Careful and vigilant supervision. Still, he thought, I’ve never seen this many actual books, much less all together.

“Elder Harper, have you been here all night?” Ferris asked.

“Of course! There are so many possibilities. Starting a new research project requires work! You best be ready.” She stood and just handed him two books. “Now, I want you to study these two while I will search for more options.” She pointed to a smaller table against the wall. “I expect you to evaluate every potential bond, then write a report detailing your thoughts and conclusions for each. Include any potential ways you would want to develop the abilities, inspiration for research can be found everywhere.”

Ferris was a bit taken aback, and not just by being casually handed two books. The elder was still as intimidating and serious as she had been yesterday, but there was a passion today. She was in her element.

He carefully took the two books over to the side table, apparently his workspace now. Ferris was even more surprised to see a stack of paper and ink provided next to a more familiar chalkboard. Back home paper would be too expensive to use casually. Is it cheaper here, or am I just not used to being an Adept? Ferris wondered, as he grabbed a piece of chalk to take notes.

While the source materials for both paper and velum could be gathered easily from rifts, they needed a lot of time, effort, and processing. In comparison, chalk was also commonly found in rifts and almost immediately usable on a painted board.

This was the economics that determined the price of most things in a bastion: how often the base materials were found in rifts, and how much work was required to make the final product usable. He wouldn’t touch the paper until he was ready to make a final draft. At least his schooling had included writing with ink… a few times about four years ago.

Ferris sighed, he would just need to write very slowly and carefully when making his final draft.

Glancing at the two titles, they were general primers on cores with specific aspect types. As far as Ferris knew, all monsters had a core. Bonded weapons were made by taking one of these cores and forging it inside something like a sword, spear, or really anything. Brix’s strange metal alchemy pot came to mind.

While the bond was not the only way to become strong—cultivation had many paths—it provided a powerful boost. Every cultivator Ferris had ever heard about bonded a core soon after unlocking their soul.

Any core could be bonded, but embedding it into a well-crafted weapon or other item added a focus to the potential abilities. For example, forging a hammer around a core could give a strong crushing attack ability.

Beyond the shape of a weapon or tool, bonded items also have an aspect from the core used to craft it. This aspect type was inherited from the monster the core was harvested from. A spear made with a fire aspect core would be different from the water core spears his clan specialized in.

The two books gave general information on two types of aspects: fire and plant. They listed potential tier one abilities that any item with that core type could gain, and then described some common item combinations.

Ferris had another shock. The amount of detail within these books was valuable. It didn’t give any specifics on how to gain abilities or the resource requirements, but it did provide a list of known possibilities. A clan that specialized in fire or plant cores would pay handsomely for just this information.

He glanced over at Elder Harper, but the woman was focused on her own work. He remembered the list of rules in the entryway, stating that sensitive cultivation secrets were never presented on paper. Now that he looked for it, she had many green stones on the table next to her pile of books. He assumed they were ‘jade slips’ which could magically store information. Occasionally she would touch one, briefly but deliberately placing a finger on it. Was that all it took for her to read it?

If so, why were these primers written in a physical book? Was it for his sake, or was he just unfamiliar with what the knowledge was worth? Was Hollowfield so rich it simply placed less value on something like this? Or were the rules less enforced in the presence of an Elder, who could better prevent anyone from stealing it?

Briefly, he thought of various ways he could copy the books. There were many possibilities, or he could just memorize as much as possible to write out later. Only… that would violate his oath. I shouldn’t need a magic oath to keep me honest, he thought.

Pushing such thoughts out of his mind, Ferris sat down and dug into the first book. It was the primer on fire cores.

Fire was a very common choice for cores due to its destructive potential. Ferris had often seen delvers from his clan shoot water around. The fire equivalent would be a burning projectile.

The universal drawback to fire cores was their self-destructive nature. Shooting a bolt of water from your hand is fine, but fire would burn you. The recommended solution was an ability called Fire Resistance. Although the ability specifically made it harder for the cultivator’s body to burn, it also provided a general tolerance for heat. According to the book, all fire cores had the potential to find the ability.

Potential was the key word. Abilities provided by bonded cores were somewhat random. They could be predicted and influenced, but nothing was guaranteed. A delver with a fire core that failed to find the Fire Resistance ability would be stuck—forced to keep searching for it long after they should’ve advanced.

This was where Ferris knew a martial tradition or support of a large clan was important. Abilities may be random to find, but two Adepts could teach each other abilities. There was apparently still a process involved. His uncle had never explained, but he knew time, magical resources, and experimentation was necessary to learn how to share each ability. Still, it was much better than relying on random chance.

Seeing the warning about compatibility for Fire Resistance made Ferris sigh. In his best-case scenario, Hollowfield would have a healing ability that would work with a water core spear. He doubted he would find anything like that in the book on fire aspected abilities.

Reading the entry for Fire Resistance, there was a note that it was available for purchase from the library, with no other prerequisites. The book only listed abilities that Hollowfield had discovered how to teach, but many had a list of other abilities or requirements that were needed first.

Ferris wasn’t surprised, but decided to start a list of topics to evaluate each potential core bond on his chalkboard. ‘Achievable requirements’ was the first item, then he added ‘usable underwater’ and ‘affordable’ beneath it.

Reading on, Ferris soon found why Elder Harper handed him the fire core book. His heart beat faster as he read about the ability: Revivifying Flame. The flames would rebuild the body, creating new flesh from old and healing any injury as long as the cultivator still lived. The ability had been reverse engineered from phoenixes, and required a fire aspected core inside a weapon or item that could ‘stir the wind.’ While it was possible to use in the first tier, the ability had to be progressed from a weaker ability called Burning Blood: a self-buff strengthening ability. Burning Blood itself required Fire Resistance to use, for obvious reasons. Elder Harper had written a note with the resource requirements and tucked it into this section.

Unsurprisingly, Revivifying Flame was not suitable for use mid-battle. Ferris had asked around at home as much as he could, and consistently heard that tier one healing abilities were slow, resource intensive, and almost always required focused concentration. It was probably possible to heal while fighting at a higher tier, but at that level attacks hurt more than just the physical body.

Ferris had found most of what he needed from the book, but still meticulously went over every remaining page. His oath prevented him from spreading or selling this information, but it was still extremely valuable to him. Knowing an ability was possible could lead him to deriving a similar ability, and it could help him fight with—or against—others with a fire core.

Tucked in the back, there was a smaller pamphlet with a few brief notes about different weapons or tools with known fire synergies. Spears were listed, but not much more than a comment that the longer reach helped keep the wielder away from the heat. There wasn’t any mention of martial traditions using a fire core. Ferris wasn’t sure if that meant Hollowfield didn’t have any, or they simply weren’t included.

When finished, he composed a draft of the report Elder Harper required. The fire core probably appealed to many people, but it failed Ferris’ criteria badly.

His clan fought underwater, where most known fire abilities fared poorly. He remembered the huge clawling that his clan members had fought. The monster probably had a fire core and its abilities worked fine, but the Hollowfield clan’s known abilities did not include anything like the heat blast the clawling had used. Furthermore, Hollowfield had no known fire ability for water breathing. More than just spears, Ferris’ clan used water magic to survive and explore the depths.

The resources required for Fire Resistance and Burning Blood were reasonable, but evolving the ability to Revivifying Flame required a special magical ash. There was no way he could afford it on his own, and it would be an expensive request to ask of the Bluereef clan.

Lastly was the restriction on weapon form. An example of being able to ‘stir the wind’ was a fan. Ferris wondered if this came from having been originally learned from a bird with feathers. He imagined himself waving something like that around underwater, and grimaced.

While this was a problem, it could also be an opportunity. Elder Harper had instructed him to give suggestions for research topics, and this was his best idea. If they could find a way to improve Revivifying Flame to remove this restriction, it would be more appealing. However, he didn’t want to gamble on not learning it at all.

Happy with his draft, Ferris slowly transcribed it to paper. The day was half over, so he decided to take a break for lunch. Elder Harper had never stopped working. He wasn’t sure how many books she’d gone through that morning, but it was more than a few. Attendants often brought more, and then took away others.

Watching them come and go, Ferris noticed that most of the books appeared to be references or journals. Every once in a while, an attendant would bring another green stone: likely a jade slip storing cultivation techniques. Elder Harper would just touch the stone briefly, somehow reading the knowledge in an instant. Then it would either be taken away or added to her growing pile.

After eating his lunch—far away from the books—he cracked open the plant core primer. He had more hope for this aspect type than fire. Plants could be insidious in water after all, and what is a thorn but a small spear?

Reading the introduction, Ferris became wary. Plant cores were even more popular than fire… for their agriculture abilities. A cultivator bonded to a plant core could raise an entire field of mundane crops in a week. This was vital to the survival of any city within a bastion. The Wild was a constant presence, pressing in on all sides. There was simply no room for many large fields of crops within the barrier. Instead, they focused on using magic to improve the quantity and speed of harvest from a small area.

Ferris’ home bastion didn’t even have farms. Instead, they relied on either rifts or regular trade with Scalefort. A great diversity of fruits, vegetables, and grains were cheaply available in abundance here, and soul storage made transportation easy.

Back home, Bluereef’s rifts had a lot of fish, plus several local naturally growing plants like the Charla pepper of his family name. However, the clan didn’t have any Adepts specialized to magically grow large fields of crops locally. The plant magic of large groups like Hollowfield ensured that Scalefort had plenty of produce to trade, and the rifts could supply Bluereef with enough food in an emergency.

Ferris knew that plant magic could be terrifying in battle. He’d heard horror stories of fighting plant core monsters from his clan’s delvers. But Hollowfield’s research and knowledge was primarily focused on farming.

Many more of the abilities listed were non-combat, compared to the fire core primer. One in particular caught his eye. They have an entire ability specialized in the creation of paper? He couldn’t quite believe it. Was that why they had so many books? Was paper… cheaper here?

Ferris sighed, feeling out of his depth. The paper creation ability was just one of many, all listing different ways of shaping plant matter to produce economic goods. Apparently, his understanding of the cost of many things was skewed compared to the power of a large clan like Hollowfield.

While the abilities were fascinating, they felt like a waste. Back home, cultivators delved, while mundane people did everything else.

Even if an Adept could make paper faster and easier with magic, they were more useful to the clan delving. Without the food brought back each day, everyone in the bastion would starve. Without resources gathered from the rifts, no one would have houses… or anything else. The chalk in his hand came from a rift, as well as the wood the chair he was sitting in was made from.

Was that different here? Back home people often talked about how it was harder, ‘surviving on the frontier.’ Yet, no one really explained what that meant. Privately, he thought most of them were just complaining.

Even if Hollowfield had enough delvers to feed and provide for everyone, who would want to languish away at this kind of work?

The stories all agree that battle helped a cultivator better than anything else. He guessed it was possible not everyone had to delve to grow stronger. At least, this book appeared to imply this was the case. Later in the book, there were at least some combat abilities described. However, they appeared designed to provide emergency defense rather than a delver’s arsenal.

It all felt… wrong. Ferris didn’t know why anyone would choose this life. Well, unless they have no other options, Ferris thought, looking down at where his leg should be.

He shook his head. Now wasn’t the time to sink back into those thoughts.

Ferris sighed. He wouldn’t find the answer to that question in the book. It was better to focus on what it could teach him.

Continuing to read, he soon found the healing ability, simply titled Regrowth. The only restriction listed was a plant aspected core, which he was happy to see.

Unfortunately, the rest of the description made him frown. The ability could regrow limbs at the speed of a plant, which was slow. He had always expected a tier one healing ability to take weeks or even months, but Regrowth was measured in years.

This could be sped up with expensive resources, but Ferris doubted he would have access to them.

Still, years was better than nothing. He’d already committed a decade of his life to Hollowfield. He would rather spend that time delving, but if waiting was the cost he would pay it. He could have his leg back. The mere thought made him giddy.

Reaching the small added booklet at the end, which detailed weapon combinations, was a disappointment. The most detailed sections were about things like a watering can, shovel, hoe, or scythe. All were heavily focused on farming.

More interesting was a brief section on a shield. Hollowfield had only one recorded delver that bonded to this combination—part of another research project—but at least they had been focused on delving. They had concentrated on defense combined with a retaliatory thorn ability. Their goal was to improve Regrowth to let them heal small wounds between fights, but never succeeded. Sadly, the delver had died before breaking out of the first tier.

There was no information on a plant core spear.

Like the fire aspected primer, this book only covered tier one. There weren’t any listings of a water breathing ability. It was at least more likely to imagine plant magic allowing him to breathe underwater than fire magic. Ferris had been told that tier two offered more freedom for all core aspects, so he might be able to wait until he advanced to delve underwater, but it wasn’t ideal.

Ferris finished the primer and moved onto his report. The plant core was better than the fire core but had significant flaws. Ferris didn’t like the idea of farming. I’d do it if there are no other options, he thought, but I’d always be looking for a way out.

He wanted to go home one day. Bluereef had no farms… although maybe he could start one? The idea still felt wrong. Like he’d be laughed out of the bastion.

The minimum resources required were not a problem, but the time to regrow his leg concerned him. At least he would have plenty of time to search for combat abilities. What would he even do sitting around for years?

Furthermore, Ferris knew in his heart that if he chose a plant core, he would embed it in a spear. None of the farming tools in the book spoke to him. Going against one’s heart would never work in cultivation. Yet if he picked a spear, his progress would be harder.

He had mostly come to terms with not having a martial tradition, but this would be worse. Hollowfield didn’t even have reliable offensive abilities to help him develop his own synergies around. He would have to rely on the random abilities provided by his bond, which could be anything. Ferris suspected the shield delver from the book might not have died if they had more support.

For research topic suggestions, Ferris was pretty confident a bonded spear focused on delving would qualify. The shield delver had contributed several abilities to the clan that were still available for purchase.

Finished with the second report, the day had finished. Ferris delivered both reports to Elder Harper and excused himself. Overall, he had mixed feelings. It was better than he had feared, as the clan had solid knowledge of at least two tier one healing abilities, but they both had clear downsides.

Back at Lily House, Mother Lily served another bird dish for dinner. He wasn’t sure if the lady was fond of it, or it was simply a coincidence. It appeared to be a different variety, a bit less juicy but more tender. This time it was pan-fried and served over a bed of pasta. The sauce was heavy on the citric and pepper, but again lacked any heat. He did enjoy the mix of vegetables on the side. At least those were more familiar.

After the meal, Ferris headed somewhere he had been looking forward to all day: the bath house. Bluereef was a clan of water delvers, and the large communal bath was a social cornerstone back home. It did not matter that water magic could clean someone instantly, baths were important!

With a professional eye, Ferris evaluated the building’s exterior. It certainly received points for style, with extensively carved stone everywhere. Begrudgingly, the service was nice as well.

Oddly, there were separate sections for male and female bathers. Ferris had heard about split baths, but hadn’t realized Hollowfield would have them. It all felt a bit prude and irrelevant, making the entire experience less social.

For all the stylish decorations, it was missing that feeling of home where everyone knew each other. Ferris hadn’t expected to know anyone himself, but it felt like everyone else were strangers to each other as well. A lack of water Adepts also made for a less exciting experience. In Bluereef, there was always someone showing off some elaborate water shaping or prank.

Still, soaking in the water felt nice.

He could close his eyes and pretend he was back home, surrounded by family.
