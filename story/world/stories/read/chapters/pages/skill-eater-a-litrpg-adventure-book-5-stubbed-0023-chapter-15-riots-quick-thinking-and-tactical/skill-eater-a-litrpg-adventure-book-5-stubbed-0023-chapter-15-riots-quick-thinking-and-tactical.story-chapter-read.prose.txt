Edge arrived at World’s End an hour after sunset.

There wasn’t a line today, and he wasn’t carrying anything besides the gear strapped to his armor and the pack on his back. The Magister on duty didn’t bother to look inside. After she waved him through the checkpoint, he stepped into the stone tunnel leading to the mixing bowl.

Battling the nanex had helped him sublimate the frustration he’d suppressed during his meeting with King. He was ready to confer with his team and determine their next steps.

While the head of the Coalition’s Problem-Resolution Squadron seemed friendly and empathetic, King was the smoothest manipulator Edge had ever seen. Even with Mental Fortress shielding his psyche, he had no idea which of the man’s words rang true, and which were lies tailored to influence his emotions, coax him into giving something away, or pressure him into behaving exactly how King wanted.

Regardless of the details, he was dead certain that the Coalition wouldn’t let a scion slip through their fingers. Unless he was willing to join them, only presenting a significant threat or offering something they wanted even more would stop the faction from murdering him on the spot or coercing his cooperation by holding his friends hostage and threatening his home.

The problem was, he had no idea how he could bribe or dissuade the planet’s second most powerful faction. Further muddying the waters, their offer seemed good at face value. If he could find a way to ensure the Coalition adhered to the terms of their agreement, joining them might be Puppet Town’s best bet after all.

However, a great deal could change in two weeks. At the very least, Edge would feel a lot better after evolving to stage three, obtaining a new Epic power, and filling his empty slots with some awesome new skills.

While these thoughts drifted through his mind, he walked past a group of masked people. Something about the magic emanating from their cores caught his attention, and it seemed the feeling was mutual. A man wearing a wolf mask and a bright-purple cloak stopped and turned to face Edge. He was about to speak when the clamor of angry voices echoing down the street drew everyone’s attention.

When Edge turned his head to track the disturbance, he caught the stench of smoke riding the evening air. He could sense concentrated mana and aether wafting up from ahead—a clear sign that people had ignited their cores and activated magitech devices.

He wasn’t sure what was going on, but he needed to find out, since this development might affect the crew’s plans. By the time he looked back, the masked people were gone, so after telling Rue not to leave his backpack no matter what, he sought out the source of the problem.

When he reached the end of the block and rounded the corner, he discovered the answer. An armed group had surrounded one of the Magisters’ checkpoints, screaming at the officials inside to come out and listen to their demands. Half were carrying torches, which explained the smoke, and while a dozen or so were stage one, most of the crowd was uncored. Judging by their disheveled appearances, the residents of Lowtown had emerged to voice their grievances and take matters into their own hands.

A small squad from the city watch was standing in front of the building, trying to talk the dissidents down but unable to get them to listen. The warriors were nervous. They could subdue a group of this size if they were willing to kill, but they were reluctant to make the first move. It was a wise decision. There were hundreds of people watching, and whatever happened next would reverberate across the free city. As matters stood, the situation was only one wrong move short of a full-blown riot.

Given the anger and fear that had been simmering since long before Trapper’s crew arrived, Edge didn’t think the violence would stop here. If this protest became a uprising, chaos would engulf the settlement. Blood would surge through the gutters before the First Among Equals regained control, leaving the residents of Lowtown even worse off than before and bringing the gears of industry and commerce to a screeching halt.

This would probably be taking place in front of her stronghold if they had a way up there, which explains why it’s hanging from the rooftop to begin with.

“We have heard your demands,” the official in charge of the station appeared in a second-story window, raising her voice to be heard above the crowd. “I promise to deliver them to the First myself. I understand why you’re upset, and I swear I will do everything in my power to ensure the conditions in Lowtown improve.”

It was what the crowd wanted to hear, which is why they were still listening, but words weren’t sufficient to diffuse the tension in the air. Edge wasn’t sure if there was anything he could do, but he was going to help if he could. The crew couldn’t support Puppet Town if the situation spiraled out of control, not to mention all the people who were going to be hurt or killed and the irreparable damage a riot would inflict on the city’s infrastructure.

He spotted a contingent from the watch in the distance, along with several high-ranking magisters, but it was going to be a few minutes until they arrived. The official was doing an admirable job of buying time, and as matters stood, the conflict might be resolved without bloodshed after all.

That was when a rock flew from a nearby rooftop, striking a woman in the head. It was followed by a second stone that broke a man’s nose. Everyone started screaming. Even though the rocks hadn’t come from the building they were surrounding, the confusion provided the spark that set this powder keg alight.

In that moment, the crowd became a mob. Right now, it was only the group in front of the station, but Edge knew that as soon as the fighting began, the violence would spread like wildfire, leaving hundreds dead before the city watch dispersed the crowd.

The first blow would be struck within seconds, taking them past the point of no return. As the protesters raised their weapons and charged, Edge opened his mouth and ignited his core.

After its rank-two upgrade, Intimidating Roar only affected the targets he chose. He willed the sonic spell to impede everyone who was out for blood and leave the guardsmen and bystanders alone.

In the blink of an eye, a ferocious wave of sound engulfed the dissidents, quenching their bloodlust in a tsunami of terror. They froze in place as primeval fear invaded their brains, standing stock still as their minds were suffused overwhelming dread—some so stiff they were unable to breathe.

Edge only wanted to stop the riot. He had no desire to hurt anyone. He ran toward the afflicted residents and unleashed a Disruption surge, canceling Roar’s magic and letting them move once more.

The watchmen had their hands on their weapons but hadn’t attacked, and the onlookers were still trying to figure out what was going on. He had delayed the onset of bloodshed, but the situation was balanced on the blade of a knife. One wrong move, and the calm he had created would give way to a maelstrom of mayhem.

Before anyone had time to react, he made a decision. He reached into his storage rune and started pulling out mid-grade materials, starting with the nanex bones. Edge added all the rocks and timber he’d collected earlier in the day, followed by the gear he had picked up in the beast’s lair. He kept adding to the pile, tossing in some random resources he’d held onto because the Association didn’t think they would sell.

No one knew what was going on but watching a mound of valuable goods appear out of thin air had drawn their attention for a critical moment. He raised his voice and addressed the crowd while they were distracted.

“I’m sorry for frightening you,” he said while handing out medicinal pills to the pair who had been wounded by rocks. “I didn’t want anyone do something you couldn’t take back or engage in a confrontation you would regret. I know times are tough. Everyone in Lowtown is in desperate need of Credits and supplies, but violence isn’t the answer.

“I won’t stand by and let you suffer on my watch. I’m donating these resources to you here and now, and the watchmen behind me will help distribute them equitably. I’m also going to transfer a thousand creds to anyone who needs them. I know this is only a drop in the bucket, but it should tide you over for at least a few days. During that time, I will confer with my fellow hunters. We will find a way to provide more assistance—make your lives more comfortable while we solve the problem over the long-term.”

A few people ran as reinforcements from the city watch arrived, who were waiting at the end of the block instead of charging in. However, most of the group stayed, and people who had been watching from a distance approached Edge with guarded hope in their eyes. They were afraid, but the promise of Credits and resources quelled their anger and coaxed their rational minds back into action.

Just to be safe, he activated Roar’s passive component, extending an intimidating aura that only affected people with hostile intentions. Several individuals went stiff when his magic touched them, melting into the crowd before vanishing without a trace. He stood beside the pile of resources and had everyone line up, transferring a thousand Credits into their accounts while the guardsmen divided the goods between the low-stage hunters who needed to craft better gear.

While the watch gently dispersed the onlookers who weren’t involved in the incident, the magister walked out of the building and came to a stop beside him.

“Thank you. Your quick thinking and generosity prevented a worst-case scenario. Those people are in desperate need of a reason to hold onto hope. When word your actions spreads, it should buy us a few more days to get this dilemma under control. We’ve been handing out supplies while working on a solution, but we ran out earlier in the afternoon. I didn’t expect things to escalate this quickly.”

“Don’t mention it. I suggest you keep an eye out for hidden provocateurs the next time protestors gather. My gut is telling me this wasn’t an accident—this riot was orchestrated. It’s going to happen again if steps aren’t taken. The rocks started flying at the exact moment the crowd was on the verge of becoming a mob. I know the free city’s resources have been stretched thin since the disaster, but it’s going to get a hell of a lot worse if someone is inciting the residents of Lowtown into violence.”

“That is a chilling prospect,” the official replied. “I hadn’t considered the possibility that someone was deliberately making things worse. I can’t see how anyone would profit from the ensuing chaos. Everyone living behind the walls will suffer if the watch has to fight dissidents instead of guarding the settlement. Hunting and commerce would be severely impeded as well.”

“The details are above my paygrade,” Edge said, “but you should let the factions know what happened today. That way, they can warn their members to be on guard against this kind of manipulation going forward.”

With that, he ducked down an ally and shifted into shadow form, having drawn far more attention to himself than he was comfortable with, although he knew he’d done the right thing. He hadn’t wanted to reveal his storage rune in public, but it wasn’t really a secret, and relinquishing a modest fraction of his wealth was better than watching people die.

That wasn’t the only reason for his actions. While he felt sympathy for the city’s uncored and low-stage residents, since he would have been in a similar position if he hadn’t found the reliquary containing his Unique core, it was vital for Puppet Town’s future that the World’s End didn’t descend into anarchy.

From that perspective, Edge’s handout was just another way of investing in his people’s future. If it kept some of Lowtown’s residents from losing hope, so much the better.
