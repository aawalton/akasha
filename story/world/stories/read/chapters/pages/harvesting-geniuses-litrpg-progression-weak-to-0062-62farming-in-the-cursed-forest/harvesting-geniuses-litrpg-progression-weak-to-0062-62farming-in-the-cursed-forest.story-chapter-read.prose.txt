Humans are fundamentally different from other living things. Animals don't rationalize in their own favor. They exist only within the reality unfolding before them. Humans, on the other hand, spend their lives lying.

Not just lying to others—but to themselves. If they believe that doing something is the right choice, they'll blindly commit to it without accepting reality. For instance: if I work hard enough, I'll definitely get rich.

Hard work leads to wealth—that much is true. But do you truly expect that working a part-time job twelve hours a day will make you rich?

You feel that earning an extra four hours of overtime pay has improved your finances. If you work even more, you'll earn even more and edge closer to prosperity. But at the end of the day, you're still a part-timer. You're just fooling yourself that the extra money makes life comfortable, that you don't need to bother finding a better job. Believing that continuing like this will somehow make everything better.

Because you don't know what kind of life you should be living, what decisions would truly benefit you—that's why you lie to yourself. Tell yourself this path is the right one.

That was the state of the survivors right now.

Jinyo found himself under pressure so crushing he could barely breathe. He sat in a room with Nephrin, Rina, Sven, Esther, and a few others he didn't recognize. Every pair of eyes was fixed on him.

"You're serious, Rina?"

"You can do it."

"I can't!"

"You can. Don't underestimate yourself. You're the only person who can help everyone. They've placed all their hope in you. If you fail, the worst that happens to me is being paraded around the village as a con artist."

"Ugh. It's too late to back out. I don't have any choice, do I?"

"No. Everyone's been working themselves to the bone for days precisely because their hope rests on you."

"But they don't even know me. Why would they trust me?"

"Just my name, Nephrin's, and Sven's is enough. As long as the three of us believe in you, everyone else will too."

A distinguished young lord. The top-ranked Teaching License examinee. A high-ranking government official. Three people of significant social standing had vouched for Jinyo as the last hope—a man who'd been unconscious for nearly three full days and knew nothing about what had transpired. Now he had to shoulder the lives of hundreds, possibly thousands.

"Mr. Southerncross. I know this is difficult, but I want you to try. Whatever happens, we'll follow your lead." Sven spoke as the representative of the civilians. He'd heard plenty from Luceria about Jinyo's sacrifices. This man had performed miracles multiple times. Even if he hadn't done everything himself, the community's very existence had grown from Jinyo's guidance.

"You're putting me in an impossible position. But I'll give it everything I've got. First—tell me what we have. How many people, how bad is it?"

"Currently: 610 Hunters, 210 civilians, 30 children. Total 850. Forty-eight wounded who haven't recovered. Each night, monsters assault the village—but because the village defenses are solid and we have numbers, we've managed. Everyone's weakening by the day, though. In a few more days, people will start succumbing to hunger." Butler James delivered the report.

"They're attacking at night too?"

"Yes, sir. Between daytime resource foraging and nightly monster raids, everyone is nearing their breaking point."

"Sounds like I don't have much time."

"No. And there's nothing more dangerous than the fury of a desperate crowd. Right now everyone still has hope. The only reason they keep working is because they believe we can get them home alive. At the very least, I'd like you to show them something to believe in. It doesn't have to be tangible results—even a beautiful lie would do."

"Mr. Sven. I don't do lies. Especially not the kind that give false hope."

"Then you'll have to deliver tangible results. But you need to hurry—people can only stay blind for so long." The war room was tense. Jinyo looked to Rina, hoping she'd say something more, but nothing came.

Another thing Jinyo now understood: no outside help was coming. Normally, when a Hollow Gate appeared, external assistance arrived within twelve hours at most. If they wanted to survive, they had to kill every boss themselves.

'Rina hasn't told anyone about the Annow, has she? No—she couldn't.' There was a very high probability that the Hollow Gate's final boss was an Annow. They now had two Ether weapons. Rina, a Special-tier Hunter, was on his side. Once they restored their fighting force, victory was still possible.

But that nagging unease—what was it? Was it because he still hadn't found Rice? His adorably earnest daughter—where had she gone? Jinyo didn't know. He had one more Heaven's Secret Spy Ticket, but he couldn't bring himself to use it again.

Jinyo didn't know how many humans had been pulled into the Hollow Gate. The current population under his charge was 850, with a strong possibility it could triple or quadruple. Even if Jinyo found edible plants in the Cursed Forest, it wouldn't be nearly enough.

He had to produce food himself. Here. Right now.

A crop that was easy to grow, easy to eat, and nutritious enough to sustain three to four thousand people: Bomb Potatoes. A single one contained twice the nutrients of a normal potato. He budgeted at least seven to ten days of provisions—the estimated time to defeat the remaining bosses.

"We need about fifteen rai of potato farmland. This is a rainforest—the soil is moist and should be rich enough for growing. The problem is, to grow them we need that." Jinyo grabbed pen and paper and began sketching. He called Butler James over and handed him the supply list.

"Could you gather these for me?"

"Monster bone powder, cores, blood, and clean soil. The bone powder is easy enough, but cores and blood will be rough."

"The original recipe calls for ten times this amount. I've already adjusted the formula to use less."

"In that case, I have no choice."

"And—I'd like you and Naho to prepare the land. I need about fifteen to seventeen rai of open space. Clear the trees and level the ground. Let everyone see the progress. That should calm their nerves a bit."

"I see—visible open space to give people a tangible glimpse of hope. That should boost morale somewhat."

"Not just somewhat. I'll show them they weren't wrong to trust Rina, Miss Nephrin, and Mr. Sven." Jinyo grabbed a wooden sword and walked out of the hut. He went to Esther, who sat scowling beneath a tree. His freedom had been restored only after Jinyo vouched for his innocence—but even then, Nephrin, Gretel, and Luceria still avoided him.

"Tch. Because you took so long waking up, I was treated like a prisoner. On top of getting pummeled by your wife. What the hell is up with that sword? Tell me. Now, demon." Esther vented. He didn't dare mouth off at Rina, who had trounced him, and had no desire to provoke Naho either. Jinyo looked at Esther's cheeks, swollen like steamed buns, and laughed.

"Sorry about my sister. She can't control herself when she's hungry."

"Fine, whatever. Pay me back with two pots of rice. Right now."

"Can't do that. But if you help me with something, I'll upgrade your ration from potatoes to rice."

"Why?! Why do I keep having to help you?!" Esther fumed until steam practically poured from his ears. Then he glimpsed a shadowy figure—a dark-haired woman in the distance. Every fighter's instinct in his body screamed at him to shut up and obey.

"What's the plan? Can I help?" Rina stepped in from behind.

"I need anyone who can move to lure monsters here. As many as possible. Pumpkin heads, blood-mad deer—anything. Not just at night. I want them during the day too."

"???" Esther and Rina exchanged glances. Which farming manual said you needed to drag monsters onto your fields? But what could they do? They weren't exactly agricultural experts. Just follow Jinyo's orders and that would be enough.

"RAAAGH!" Naho wielded a bronze axe, felling trees one after another. She channeled her elephant-tier strength—combined with hunger-fueled rage—and snapped massive trunks that no ordinary woman should have been able to topple.

"Hang in there, Naho!"

"I HATE hanging in there!!" Naho was nearly in tears. The words she despised most right now were "be patient" and "hang in there."

Felled trees became fences. Boulders were hauled to make traps. The civilians who couldn't fight were conscripted for heavy labor—but it wasn't entirely grim.

"What are you doing, Doctor Jinyo?" Nephrin found Jinyo setting up a stove as though preparing to cook. She was puzzled—he couldn't plant anything yet.

"No one's eaten in a long time. I want to get at least something into their stomachs. It'll give them the energy to keep working."

"But you're out of supplies."

"A small amount won't hurt. What matters in a crisis like this is morale. It's precisely when we have nothing that we prove we're on their side." Jinyo opened the Gardener's Bag. He pulled out twenty kilograms of Sage Rice and poured it into a pot, followed by thirty kilograms of Happy Carrots chopped into small pieces—unpeeled, because the skin was nutritious too. The Sage Rice mixed with water became simple congee. A straightforward recipe, yet one ingredient didn't belong.

"Doctor Jinyo, what are these?" Nephrin pointed to the wooden table beside him. Piled there were freshly picked green plants—not even the same species.

"When I was little, my grandmother told me that during the Civilization Restoration era, food was terribly scarce. To stave off hunger, she'd dig up whatever grass grew nearby and boil it into congee. She ate it without even washing the dirt off."

"Huh?! That's completely unsanitary! Or are you saying you're also—" Nephrin's pointing finger trembled. Jinyo laughed—he could tell exactly what she was thinking.

"Soil back then wasn't contaminated with chemicals. Mixed in with a little earth, it was technically edible—if not exactly tasty. Grandma said there was grit crunching in her mouth the whole time, but she never forgot the taste of that day's congee. I'm not going to make anyone eat dirt porridge. I'm just collecting edible weeds to mix into the congee. Grass-family plants grow everywhere." Jinyo proceeded to toss the weeds into the pot. Nephrin, who had never known true hunger, felt her stomach turn. Grass congee. She was actually expected to eat grass congee. Grass—the stuff animals ate. Feed—the stuff you gave to livestock.

"Want some? Straight from nature."

"Th-thank you, but—" GURGLE. Nephrin could lie to anyone, but not her stomach. The thunderous growl put an end to all pretense. She stared at the grass congee with longing in her eyes, unable to refuse. Her body had reached its limit.

Jinyo began distributing the grass congee. People lined up without complaint. Nobody cared whether it was roots or grass—as long as it was edible and wouldn't kill them.

"There's no grassy taste at all. How?" Nephrin had eaten Sage Rice before. She never imagined its rich flavor could mask the weeds' bitter green smell almost completely.

"It's still delicious!! And filling, too!"

"Best meal I've ever had in my entire life!" The chorus of praise never stopped. Jinyo blew out a tired breath by the fire. The felled trees had been cut into blocks for fuel. Thanks to everyone's labor, ten rai of forest had been reduced to stumps and bare earth.

In the immediate vicinity there was no open field. Even Bomb Potatoes, which grew underground, couldn't develop without level terrain where light could reach.

"The land's been cleared, Yo. Should we start tilling?"

"No. Not yet."

"What do you mean? We're running out of time. If we don't till, when are we supposed to plant?"

"Naho, just clearing the trees used up everyone's last reserves. If you make them till on top of that, they'll drop dead. Look at their faces—they're one breath away from keeling over. If they hadn't had that grass congee, they'd already be in the afterlife." Jinyo pointed behind him. The men who'd helped Naho fell trees were either panting with tongues lolling or had faces tinged green. Nobody was in any shape for more labor.

"Sometimes I forget I'm tougher than a bison."

"Self-awareness is a good quality."

"But I don't need to hear it from other people!" Naho punched her brother's midsection lightly—but to her surprise, her fist struck something hard. Not armor. Not a metal plate.

"Hm. That's strange."

"Strange how?"

"It didn't even hurt you. My punches are pretty strong, you know." Curious, Naho lifted her brother's shirt in public. What she found were abdominal muscles that hadn't been there before.

"Gasp! A bookworm nerd like you with abs?! Impossible. Even if you stole my whey protein, this shouldn't be possible!"

"I don't know why either, but please don't do that again. It's embarrassing." Jinyo swatted his sister's hand away and straightened his clothes. Exposing bare skin was something he disliked. Naho pouted and ran off to chop more firewood.

The sound of many running feet thundered from one direction. The villagers went tense. Movement like this only happened at night—when monsters raided the camp.

A few brave souls peered through the narrow gaps in the perimeter fencing. What they saw was a swarm of monsters chasing a young man in black. The creatures paid no attention to the village and simply barreled past.

"Why are they chasing him?"

"That direction—isn't that where they're planting? This is bad! We need to warn them to evacuate!"

"Everyone, stay calm! Remain where it's safe. There's nothing to worry about. Let others do their job—don't get in their way!" Those who seemed to know what was happening shepherded everyone back to shelter.

"Damn it, why am I stuck doing this? But whatever—once this is done, I'm collecting every bit of compensation I'm owed." Esther sprinted along the cleared path. Trees on either side formed natural barriers shielding him from the pursuing monsters. Branches snapped and trees toppled as Esther leaped into the open clearing where Jinyo stood holding his wooden sword, centered and ready.

"Brought you sixty. Can you handle it alone?"

"Who do you think you're talking to? Just sixty? If you want rice, go bring me a hundred."

"A hundred? If you can't even handle sixty without begging for backup, I'll laugh like a duck!" Esther detonated a burst of power and pulled away from the pursuing swarm. Jinyo popped a Cursed Fruit into his mouth. Energy surged through his bloodstream. Eldaroks and cursed villagers alike were drawn in by Jinyo's scattering peach blossoms—the fragrance tugging all their attention toward him.

"Establish Domain. Realm of Paradise." The Realm of Paradise engulfed the surrounding square kilometer. Jinyo swung his sword in flowing arcs, feeding in Sword Spirit. The maddened Eldaroks charged at full speed. Just as Jinyo launched himself forward with Floral Step, they chased recklessly—and stumbled on the tree stumps.

The stumps left behind from the felling were like oversized obstacles blocking every running path. Jinyo dashed back and forth, doing little more than swing his blade in broad arcs, scattering peach blossom petals everywhere.

"What's he doing out there, James?" Sven watched from a distance, per Jinyo's explicit request that no one interfere. Even if Jinyo's skills had improved, facing dozens of Eldaroks simultaneously was reckless.

"He keeps running around but won't actually kill any of them. I know his sword style focuses on drawing aggro, but what does running around accomplish?"

"I couldn't say, sir. But I'm certain his actions aren't pointless. I believe that with all my heart." Butler James spoke his admiration from the soul. For James—who did not praise easily—to speak so highly of Jinyo, it seemed that during Jinyo's unconscious days the young man had performed enough to earn the butler's deep respect.

Good—because the more James admired Jinyo, the easier it would be to convince the old man to send Luceria to study under him.

Jinyo cut a pumpkin head from its shoulders. Once every cursed villager was dispatched, he turned to the Eldaroks. In both strength and speed, Jinyo was leagues beyond his former self. He leaped, rolled, and executed basic sword forms—twelve cuts to sever four Eldarok necks. Rinse and repeat until every last one was dead.

It took Jinyo over ninety minutes to behead them all. As the sun began to set, Rina went out and dragged back a haul equal to Esther's. That night, no monsters assaulted the village—they'd all been lured away for Jinyo's purposes. As the moon replaced the sun, conditions grew even more favorable. Jinyo went sleepless, luring Eldaroks to stampede across the cleared ground again and again. What surfaced above the soil were tree stumps, now yanked free from the earth.

"Look at that! The topsoil is completely churned up—from the monsters' stampede?" Sven's blood surged when he saw the results. The deer-type monsters had a habit of stomping the ground when they charged. Their powerful legs ripped even deeply embedded stumps from the earth. No human effort required. "Even if we'd brought oxen in to plow, it wouldn't have been this fast."

"So that's why he didn't order anyone to till the soil. There were plenty of volunteers for the tilling—but one wrong step and you'd end up under a stampeding herd. Even I wouldn't have risked that. Remarkable young man." Butler James noted how Jinyo's movements grew sharper by the hour—training his skills even as he prepared the farmland, without spending anyone else's energy. Two birds with one stone. What no one realized was that Jinyo's true purpose in having Rina and Esther lure monsters in wasn't to find someone to plow. There was a reason far more important than that.
