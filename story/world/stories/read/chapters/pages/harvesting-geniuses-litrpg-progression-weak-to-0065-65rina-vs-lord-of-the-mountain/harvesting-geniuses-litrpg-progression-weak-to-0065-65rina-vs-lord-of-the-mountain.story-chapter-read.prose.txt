Rina patrolled around the village, checking whether any of the torches had gone out one by one. She never minded working harder if it meant everyone would be safer.

The young woman, her red eyes glowing in the darkness, carried a pink ether sword to use as a guiding light. Then she spotted a red iron rod several meters away, along with the person holding it. Rina could not force a smile when she saw the young man’s utterly unfriendly face.

“Even if I look free, I’m actually very busy right now. I don’t want to waste my mental health fighting with you, Esther.”

“I only wanted a chance to talk with you in private.”

“Sorry, but I’m a woman with a child already, and I have no intention of chatting with some crooked man either. Get lost.”

“Who would care about a blood-crazed woman like you? If it weren’t for that demon, no one would want you.” Esther flinched. Every hair on his body stood on end. Just imagining a man-woman relationship with the woman in front of him made him want to throw up.

“Oh wow, bold words. I hate to tell you this, but actually I’m pretty popular in my unit. There’s a line of people buying me coffee from the cafeteria all the way to the gym.”

“A flower in the desert, that’s all. When there’s nothing pleasing to the eye, even a cactus starts to look beautiful.”

“...” Rina realized it took real effort not to cut down another human being with her sword. Her job was supposed to be protecting ordinary civilians, yet here she was wanting to kill someone.

“We can talk while walking.”

“Fine by me.”

Rina resumed her inspection around the village, except now Esther tagged along beside her. It was boring work, but having someone walking with her eased the monotony a little, no matter how irritating that someone was.

“I’m curious about the enemy calling itself Heaven. It seems to call that demon a Heavenly Demon too. Do you know anything about it?”

“No.”

“Don’t lie to me. You definitely know something.”

“Where’s your proof? Proof that I know anything? I don’t know what makes you assume I do, but it’s ridiculous. I know nothing.”

“I don’t have proof, but I trust my instincts. You don’t look panicked like everyone else. You’re less worried than the others, and you’re that demon’s life partner too.”

“...” Rina stopped walking. She turned back to look at Esther, who instinctively stepped back once. Was she really about to say it? Was this woman really going to say it?

“What did you just say? Say it again.”

“I said I trust my instincts, and you seem to know something. That’s why you’re not as shaken as everyone else.”

“Ugh, boring.” Rina let out a breath, her eyes filled with contempt. Esther had never been looked down on by a woman this hard before.

“What? What did I say wrong? Want me to repeat it again? That should’ve been enough. Or do I need to say once more that it’s because you’re that guy’s life partner?” Esther grew more irritated as he spoke, and when he finished that last line, Rina’s ears twitched slightly. Nothing that happened escaped his notice.

“Hah... so you made me say it again on purpose just because you wanted to hear me say you’re that guy’s life partner? You like him that much?”

“If you want to know about those things, I’ll tell you.” Rina put on a serious face and lifted her chin as if deciding something incredibly important.

“So now you just change the subject?”

“I don’t know what that thing calling itself a representative of Heaven really is. But in my organization, there’s something called the Unknown, and Jinyo himself said the Unknown and the enemy from Heaven are the same thing.”

“Unknown... heh. What a lazy name.”

“Because we don’t know what it is, where it came from, or its origins, we call it the Unknown. Only a secret government agency knows what it really is. It’s the one responsible for Hollow Gates appearing all over the world, and it likes targeting children or adults with special qualities. Once it marks someone, it won’t stop until that person is dead. That’s what my unit knows.”

“Feels like I’m hearing a world secret I really shouldn’t know. That’s bad news for me, isn’t it?” Esther raked a hand through his hair, messing it up, like his heart now twisting in turmoil. Learning a government secret was never good. He never expected the enemy from Heaven to be tied to the government too.

“Until now, we always thought the Unknown was no different from wildfires, floods, storms, or earthquakes. We were never able to make contact with it. We didn’t even know it was a living thing with thoughts.”

“Wait a second—you’ve never contacted them? But that guy and I have talked to it already. It speaks human language too.” Esther said.

“I’d like to talk to them at least once myself. I’ve killed so many I’ve lost count, but we’ve never once opened up and had a conversation. I want to know what kind of thoughts run through the minds of the things that ruined my life.”

“Then your wish is about to be granted, female human.”

The voice of an uninvited guest echoed in their minds. Rina and Esther whipped around and saw the inhuman being standing out beneath the moonlight atop a high rock. It had a large, striking form, yet somehow neither of them had sensed its presence at all. They had no idea how long it had been eavesdropping or when it had arrived.

“Let me tell you something, since I am feeling generous. It is not that we have never contacted humans. Rather, the humans we contacted never told anyone else.”

“You bastard, eavesdropping on people’s conversation is rude as hell,” Esther snapped, glaring at the Lord of the Mountain. Its form differed from every Eltharok he had seen, but there was no doubt it was special—just like the kind he and Jinyo had once defeated together.

“The real thieves are the ones who barge into someone else’s home and harvest crops others carefully planted and tended. You fools don’t even realize whose land you are trampling.”

The Lord of the Mountain pointed at the ground. Esther immediately drew his sword, ready to fight at any moment, while Rina remained calm. She stared at the Lord of the Mountain’s face with eyes that seemed ready to devour it whole.

“So you are one of the Unknown? No... a representative from Heaven, right?” Rina lifted her head and met its gaze. Not the slightest ripple of anxiety showed in her eyes.

“Sadly, I am not the exalted one. We are merely loyal servants, permitted to exist through His power.”

“Oh. What a shame. So I wasted my breath on nothing but a servant.”

“You insolent woman! Fine. I shall personally teach you the end that awaits those who challenge Heaven! I will prove that the brazen words of a lowly wench are nothing but lies!”

The Lord of the Mountain pointed and cursed furiously, its aura of wrath swelling forth to menace the seemingly delicate young woman. A small leather ball materialized in the air and was caught in its five-fingered hand.

“That’s dangerous! Don’t let that ball touch you, no matter what!” Esther shouted. That small spinning ball, driven by some bizarre technique, had nearly killed Jinyo. Esther hadn’t been hit directly by it, but he had seen its destructive power with his own eyes.

The Lord of the Mountain gripped the ball in its palm, feeding power into it until it spun with terrifying force. At this distance, the odds of missing were zero. The chance of Rina dodging a baseball traveling at 300 kilometers per hour was basically nonexistent. If she could do that, she wouldn’t even be human anymore.

“You don’t know this, do you? It’s called baseball!”

The Lord of the Mountain hurled the leather ball. Rina stood perfectly still, showing no sign of dodging or deflecting it with her sword. Two choices lay before her: take the ball head-on, or gamble on slashing it off course. Or perhaps she would choose neither.

Rina stepped aside and dodged the 300-kilometer-per-hour ball. Gawakarn surged outward in a pink aura. She slashed, sending a wave of sword energy toward the Lord of the Mountain. Her sword intent was so intense it became a tangible wave, crashing into the enemy and opening wounds.

She dodged it! Not only that, she counterattacked immediately.

It was not enough to simply react to danger—she also had to be fast and decisive enough to answer it in the same instant.

“Sword spirit...?” Esther swallowed hard. When one trained sword intent to its utmost limit, the next stage was to awaken a spirit within the sword itself. That skill was called Sword Spirit. A swordsman who mastered Sword Spirit could attack at mid-range, and that was exactly what Rina had just shown him.

“You’re not bad, human woman. But while you’re here, you don’t actually think you can kill me and still make it in time to save the Heavenly Demon, do you?”

“What... or was your real target actually—” Esther watched Rina’s expression. On her beautiful, cool face, a faint crack finally appeared. There were only two things that could shake this woman mentally: her son and Jinyo.

This bastard dragged her away on purpose while sending his underlings to destroy the ritual, didn’t he? Because she’s the strongest human here, he couldn’t allow her to stay there, Esther realized, seeing through the Lord of the Mountain’s entire plan.

He turned to look toward the direction where Jinyo was conducting the rite. He heard faint screams drifting in on the wind. They were quiet, but they carried unmistakable chaos.

“Esther, protect Jinyo for me.”

“And what about you!? You want me to leave you to fight something like this alone? Even when that guy and I worked together, we nearly coughed blood fighting one of these!”

“Don’t compare me to you two. This thing is just an appetizer. It doesn’t even qualify as a steak. And besides, your divine armament doesn’t work well on them anyway. Go. If even a single hair falls from Jinyo or Gretel, I’ll kill you.”

“Tch. Fine. Hope you don’t die—though getting crippled would serve you right, you rotten woman.” Esther spat out the curse before running straight toward the fields.

The Lord of the Mountain hurled a ball at Esther’s stepping leg, but instead of smashing into his ankle and breaking it, the ball missed and flew wide.

“Hm. This feeling... it’s just like what the previous Lord of the Mountain felt. But you haven’t even launched a technique, have you? Why are you already able to steal away my senses?” The atmosphere radiating from Rina was strangely similar, yet utterly different from what it had felt from Jinyo.

The pink aura pouring out of Rina was so intense it almost transformed the cursed forest into a field of flowers. Her pink ether sword trembled and sang with delight. The Lord of the Mountain began to see visions of butterflies of every kind dancing around the young woman, though this cursed forest had never known such strange wild butterflies before.

“So you inherited the memories and experience of the one who was taken down before you, huh? Then I’m sorry to disappoint you, because the experience you inherited is worthless.”

“!!!”

“First, I’m a woman, while Jinyo is a man. The terror of the Hundred-Flower Poet Song Jinyo showed you only had half its real power.”

“Second, Jinyo is an inexperienced weakling. He may have gotten lucky enough to kill one Unknown, but that was just a small fry. Don’t compare him to me. I’ve killed hundreds of them.”

“And most importantly, third... I was chosen by the sword.”

Rina poured immense power into her blade. The ether ore began to crack, shift, and change shape.

“Release divine armament—Yuan Yang.”
