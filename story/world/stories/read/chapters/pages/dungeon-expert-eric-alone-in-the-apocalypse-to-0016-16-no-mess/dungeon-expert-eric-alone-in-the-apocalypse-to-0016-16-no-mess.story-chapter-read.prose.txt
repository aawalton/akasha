The houses were tiny, not even knee height, the kind of ridiculously adorable decorative houses that only influencers and bored housewives with too much time on their hands bothered to set up. As he watched, though, the door opened, revealing a dark space within. A boot appeared, filling almost the entire door, and then a pointy red hat squeezed through, followed by the body of a knee-height man with more beard than face wearing a simple blue robe. Another and another burst out of the tiny house, disregarding the fact that the first knee-height man couldn’t have fit inside, let alone the five, ten, twenty that poured out of the house. They had shiny skin and they clicked as they moved around, like porcelain bumping into porcelain. The other houses burst to life as well, spitting out knee-height men faster than Eric could count them.

Not knee-height men. Gnomes, he realized abruptly. No mess. If I said it aloud, it would’ve been obvious. Gnomes!

On his shoulder, Cy dug his claws in as his whole body went rigid. He thumped Eric’s shoulder with his rear leg. “What are you waiting for? Shatter them! Shatter them before they multiply any further!”

“The gnomes or the houses?”

The thumping grew more insistent. “All of them!”

Eric took a step back. The gnomes milled about, not actively attacking yet, which gave him a chance to rifle through his inventory and pull out his baseball bat with +1 damage. Blades wouldn’t do him much good right now, but a good blunt instrument? Perfect.

He stepped forward, pulling the bat back. The gnomes are a low-threat enemy. Isn’t this the perfect chance to try some patter? It wasn’t like he’d have many subscribers yet one way or another, and he definitely needed the practice. Like Joe said, it was the ideal opportunity to try things and make mistakes.

“These little guys won’t activate until they sense your mana, but when they do, whoa nelly,” Eric said. He gave a little sarcastic head shake to the imaginary camera and swept his bat forward in a low swing, more golf than baseball. Gnomes went flying, and porcelain smashed in the most satisfying way as they struck trees or hard earth. Even so, a small part of him criticized the rest of him: “whoa nelly?” What year is it, 1832? When was the last time a human being said “whoa nelly” in anger? Am I okay?

“That’s right. But once they sense it, they won’t stop until they eradicate it. It’s what makes them so dangerous,” Cy chimed in.

Eric glanced at him. Is he doing patter, or does he think we’re just having a casual chat? Either way, it worked; Cy clearly knew more about these monsters as he did—as expected, given that Cy was a veteran of the Game—and could fill in the gaps in Eric’s knowledge.

“So I… maybe shouldn’t have also sent a mote of mana over them?” Eric asked.

The gnomes pivoted in the spot. Their kindly crinkled eyes glowed a malevolent red, and they rushed him as one, porcelain knees clanking as they closed in. They were faster than Eric expected, and one grabbed his leg before he could get the bat ready for another swing. The second it latched on, its body grew hot, and light shone through cracks in its rapidly expanding pottery body. Eric’s stomach sucked to his spine. He bashed it off his leg and jumped back instinctively a split second before it exploded, spraying his legs with razor-sharp shrapnel and nearly sweeping him off his feet. Another two gnomes shattered from the force of the explosion, but not a single one flinched. Instead, they kept charging, eyes gleaming with mindless violence.

Cy shrugged. “If you didn’t, they’d catch your scent when you got closer to their houses anyways. Your mana cloaking isn’t perfect. You don’t even have snakeskin pants or gloves.”

“I’m gonna be so dripped out one day,” Eric muttered under his breath, imagining himself in head-to-toe black snakeskin. He wasn’t sure he liked the look, luxurious as it was, but it was better than not having armor.

And then the next wave of gnomes reached him, and he was too busy fighting to think coherently. Eric smashed left and right, kicking the ones that got past his bat. The gnomes were easy to break, but there were so many of them, with a veritable field of red hats between him and their houses. As fast as Eric smashed, as quickly as he kicked, he was still slowly forced backward. He gritted his teeth. “I’m getting nowhere!”

“Just use your aura as a repulsive field,” Cy commented.

“Use my what as a what?” Eric asked.

“Your aura… dear gods, you don’t even know about your aura?”

“I know I’ve got more than you.”

Cy leaned back on Eric’s shoulder to get a better look at him, and though Eric had no time to look at the little rabbit, he knew Cy was squinting at him. “Categorically untrue.”

“It’s slang. Old men like you wouldn’t understand.”

“Like how you don’t understand magic.” Cy sniffed, lifting a paw to examine his nails.

Eric gritted his teeth and punted another gnome halfway across the field. “Well, would you care to illuminate me?”

“I don’t know. What could an old man like me know, anyways?”

A gnome leaped at his leg, and he barely sidestepped in time, only for his hip to clink into something on the other side of him. He turned to find a tower of maniacal gnomes reaching out for his entire leg, ankle to hip, already glowing and ready to pop. Unleashing a very manly squeak, he smashed the tower backward, and the three glowing gnomes exploded in a field of their fellows. They took out a good half-dozen gnomes apiece, but it still wasn’t enough to make a dent in the sea of pointy hats.

“Old man? I only see a jaunty, handsome young rabbit,” Eric quickly countered.

“Who is also a master of magic?” Cy prompted.

Eric backed up. His heel hit a tree root, and he stumbled. For a split second, he lost his balance. In that instant, gnomes latched onto both his legs and began glowing, vicious grins splitting their faces as their grabby little hands clawed their way up his jacket. Eric snatched one off his leg, but the other detonated while he was dealing with the first. The blow sent him staggering sideways, his feet clicking off porcelain bodies. He looked down, expecting to see a bloody void below his knee, but instead, aside from a burn on the coat and a fresh red sear mark on his flesh, he was unharmed. A faint golden glow cloaked his leg, and when the gnomes reached it, they bounced away rather than being able to grab ahold.

“Well… I guess young and handsome will have to be enough,” Cy said.

“That’s your aura?” Eric asked, slightly amazed. Of all the things Cy had done, it felt the most “organic magic” of all of them. Swarm was just another spell; an impressive one, but with a spellbook, he was sure he could learn it just like any other. That… golden aura shield thing, now that was something special.

“Mhm. I extended it over you. You’re welcome.” Cy flopped down against Eric’s shoulder, and the glow receded. “And now I’m going to take a nap. Don’t die. The stress would be terrible for my fur.”

Eric took a deep breath, quickly transferring Cy to his chest pocket. The aura thing was impressive, but he still didn’t know where or what his aura was, or how to activate it, let alone how to craft a solid shield from it. Sending a mana mote was one thing; it was basically just a simpler version of firebolt or any other spell that sent mana out of his body. But the aura thing… he didn’t even know where to start with that.

Nothing for it. He glanced left and right, then sprinted for the houses. He kicked his way through the gnomes, knocking the ones that clung to him off by striking them against the other gnomes. Blasts chased his steps, blowing the gnomes behind him away and sending the tail of his jacket forward between his calves. He swept his bat, smashing the gnomes out of his way. The houses loomed. Hats squeezed out of the doors as a dozen more gnomes tried to spawn all at once, but he wasn’t having it. He slammed the bat down on the house’s roof. Cracks shot through the entire house, and light gleamed through the cracks, blazing brighter than any individual gnome had.

Oh shit! Eric spun on his heel and sprinted back through the blasted path of dead gnomes and pottery shards behind him. The house rumbled and shook as the light grew brighter and brighter, until it beamed so bright he saw his shadow on the trees across from him. He dove, ducking for cover, as a huge explosion rattled the ground and slammed into his back, propelling him ass over head toward the trees. He ducked his head and raised his arms just in time to smash into the trees shoulders-first. Gnomes rained down all around him, shattering against the trees and the floor.

Eric climbed slowly to his feet, struggling against the world wobbling all around him. Gnomes stumbled to their porcelain feet, while others trembled, light shining through the fresh cracks in their skin. The house he’d smashed was a blackened hole in the moss, but the other houses trembled as gnomes poked their pointy hats out of doors, windows, even the chimney: every orifice on the house brimmed with gnomes.

“I refuse,” Eric growled. He staggered toward the houses as fast as he could, smashing them one after another. Gnomes poured out of the further houses, but he ignored them, instead beelining for one house after another. This time, rather than running after the houses started glowing, he just charged for the next one, setting off one after another in a chain of delayed explosions that rumbled, glowing brighter and brighter behind him. The gnomes turned and chased after him, but his legs were several times as long as theirs, and he left them in the dust. He reached a natural lip in the ground and dropped below the earth, dropping to the ground and curling up on his rabbit-free side to fit entirely below the natural lip. The first explosion hit a second later, throwing heat and force just over his head. Dirt sprayed, raining down on him, and so did shards of gnome.

“One,” he counted.

Another blast. The earth shook. Dirt piled on top of him, so much it began to bury him, and the lip wore down.

“Two.”

Heat and fire burst over the edge, singeing his arm and shoulder. He hunkered down, digging his other shoulder into the earth to get a little deeper below his makeshift bunker.

“Three.”

The force of the explosion hammered his shoulder and knocked him flat on his face. He arced his back a little to save Cy and laid there, waiting.

Nothing poured over the ledge but friendly sunshine. No more cracks rang out. The explosions were over.

“Four,” he said triumphantly, and climbed to his feet. From a higher vantage point, he confirmed that there were no more gnomes alive and standing and that all five houses were blackened craters in the moss, then dusted himself off. Dirt sunk into every crack of his snakeskin jacket. It was down his neck, up his sleeves, in his hair. He got the worst of it out, then gave up. He’d be here all day chasing down dust at this rate. Hope this safe zone has a laundry machine, he thought, then: hope this jacket is machine-safe.

Once more alone and safe, he turned to where he hoped the camera was and gave it his winningest smile and a big thumbs up. “And that’s how you take care of gnomes!”

Not at all convinced that he’d done a great job with commentary or patter, Eric sighed under his breath. Oh well. It is what it is. He went over his gear, checking the burn on his leg and the various scuffs on the snakeskin. The jacket had held up pretty well, with only a few surface scuffs. His leg, on the other hand, was bright red and swollen. Dirt had gotten into the burn, and it had begun to seep, clear fluid running down his calf.

That’s not good. He was no doctor, but he was pretty sure dirt in wound plus seeping was a bad sign. Or did burn wounds just seep? Either way, he couldn’t just leave it be and keep trucking. He pulled out his water bottle and poured some on his leg. The wound stung, but it washed the dirt out. The wound still seeped, but there was no dirt in it anymore. Capping his water bottle and sending it away, he paused for a moment. He sat down on a rock and elevated his leg, then focused on Regeneration. The wound slowly healed, growing less and less severe. As it healed, his mana decreased. He paused the healing when he could sense he only had enough mana left for one last Firebolt. The burn was much paler, and the surface of the wound had closed over. It no longer seeped, and although it was sensitive to the touch, it didn’t sting or burst with sharp pain when his clothes brushed it anymore.

He stood, testing it out. He could handle the pain, whether it was walking or running, and while he deeply did not want to roll or crouch, he could if he needed to. It would have to be good enough.

I could turn back, but… whether this is a real run or a scouting run, I want to see it through. If this was the video game it sometimes felt like, then he had two-thirds of his health left. It would be ridiculous to turn back with so much left in the tank, especially when the previous dungeon he’d double-run had reacted to his initial run and cranked up the difficulty on round two. Not much, but enough that he wanted more data from this dungeon before he gave up and went home. If he went back now, after all, he would’ve cranked up the difficulty for very little positive return. If he pressed a little further and got a true feel for the layout of the full dungeon and boss fight, then he would’ve gained enough knowledge for it to be worth the difficulty spike.

Eric considered his equipment. The bat was still in good shape, and for a dungeon with enemies he needed to smash, it was far superior to a bladed weapon. He put all his bladed weapons away except for the poisoned dagger, which was light enough to leave out as a contingency, and pulled out the small, compact wooden bat he’d picked up at his first gas station as a backup for his main bat. As little as I want to rely on melee, it really is my only option right now, with my limited spells and mana. He needed to level up, and fast, to achieve his ideal ranged mage class where he didn’t need to engage with melee any more. But for now, he needed a variety of melee weapons to handle the scenarios the dungeons threw at him.

He turned the bat over in his hand. He was fortunate to have options; or rather, he was fortunate that he was the kind of weapon-hoarding scum that never threw any item away in a game or, it turned out, real life. If he only had bladed weapons, he would’ve struggled more with the gnomes. No matter what kind of weapon I get my hands on, I should take it. It could be valuable in some dungeon. Though… the lava dungeon came to mind, and he grimaced. Yeah, I have no idea what I’m supposed to use to get through that. My wits, I guess?

“But first, eyes on the prize,” Eric commented aloud to himself. “Let’s clear this dungeon before we worry about the next one.”

Brandishing his bat, he strode onward.
