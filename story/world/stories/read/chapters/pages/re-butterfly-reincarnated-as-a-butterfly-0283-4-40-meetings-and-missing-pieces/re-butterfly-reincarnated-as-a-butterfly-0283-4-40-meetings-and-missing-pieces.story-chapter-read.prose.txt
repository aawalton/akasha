Adon could only shake his head.

I don’t know, he sent to Rosslyn only. If the Empire loses, we just have no way of guessing what the Monster Kingdom might do next. We do know that they’re aggressive, though. They feel confident trying to fight the world. Since it’s not just the Empire they attack, they must view everyone on the surface as an enemy.

The Queen was a little nervous at those words.

All right, so the idea does carry some risks to it, we knew that, she thought after a moment. Does your personal experience give any, um, insight into what their leader might be thinking?

I don’t even know if it’s the same entity now that it was back then, Adon sent.

“We are either doing nothing or leaping into the great unknown with this,” Rosslyn muttered.

A few eyes glanced her way and then studiously stared straight forward again as the Queen frowned at them.

You’re not wrong, the butterfly admitted.

Well, we must have a bias towards action, she thought. When half the country is suffering under the Empire’s heel, we cannot let ourselves be paralyzed by fear of the unknown. Her thoughts were framed in decisive terms, but to Adon’s practiced ear—and his telepathic grasp of her emotions—it was clear she was trying to convince herself as much as him.

The butterfly would have to do his best to try and reassure her—or confirm that the worst fears of those gathered here were correct, that the Monster Kingdom was actually somehow worse than the Demon Empire.

As the discussion of logistics and timelines moved on around them, he delved back into his memories, simultaneously holding in his mind the substance of the Dungeon Core’s message to the Monster King and sifting through his recollections of his own incarnation as a monster. Hopefully some answer would be there. The Goddess had left him with his past life incarnation memories for a reason. Maybe it was just so that he would be able to confirm right now that the Monster Kingdom existed as a separate power with a single mind behind it. Maybe it was to tell the humans gathered here that the monsters and demons were, in fact, hostile to one another.

But he hoped there would be something more.

The message from the Dungeon Core was not itself very enlightening, but the fact that it referenced Adon being a reincarnator was interesting. Perhaps all the Monster Kingdom’s important entities were reincarnators. That might mean they could be negotiated with. Shouldn’t beings who had lived multiple lifetimes and recalled at least one prior life be broad-minded and wise? At least compared with the Empire’s single-minded determination to dominate and conquer other nations.

Then again, I’m not really wise or something, Adon thought. For me, multiple lifetimes of memories just remembering lots of times I made the same mistake over and over. More embarrassing than informative. Two lifetimes would have been enough to enlighten me about the common thread.

He kept going, the work tedious, moving through his monster memories in slow motion, almost frame by frame if it were a movie, looking for something that would tell him something about the Monster King.

But the boss creature or chief Dungeon Core or whatever it was hadn’t even communicated to Adon and his cohort in actual words. It made sense, since Adon’s incarnation hadn’t been the most intelligent sort of creature.

What kind of terrain does the Empire have in its interior? Adon silently asked Rosslyn.

All sorts of biomes, she thought in response. Swamp, desert, plains, good farmland, everything in between. The only thing I believe the human-dominated regions have that they do not, as far as I know, is frozen areas. To our far north is a land where it is functionally always winter, but the Demon Empire’s lands are, as far as I know, unseasonably warm at all times of year.

Adon frowned at that. If this world was like every other world he had ever inhabited, having one cold pole meant that you had another cold polar region on the opposite side of the planet. This just told him how little the Queen and her predecessors knew about the geography of their own planet, in part thanks to the fact that they could not safely travel south without risking capture by the Empire.

Why? Rosslyn added.

Just trying to confirm things as best as I can about where the memory I have took place, Adon sent. I know it has to have been in the Empire, but I don’t really have location information. I did see it was in an arid, mountainous region.

Maybe the Imperial Heartland, Rosslyn suggested. Their traditions say that they come out of deserts and swamps. They claim to have grown up in those sorts of spaces as a way of hardening themselves.

Hm, Adon thought to himself very quietly. Is that a sort of evolutionary biology reason for the structure of their society? No, I’m overthinking this. Don’t get distracted.

He returned to considering his past life as a monster, but there were frustratingly few clues beyond what he had already uncovered.

The locations of both the Monster King his previous incarnation had interacted with and the present Monster King, assuming that he was being given orders by a similar or the same entity in that time period, were in the Empire. They might be the same location, but he could not confirm that.

That Monster King, just like the present day monsters working under the management of Dungeon Cores, was clearly aware of the world above wherever its dungeons developed. The entity that had controlled Adon’s prior self had sent him an image of the place he and the other monsters were meant to destroy, and the Dungeon Core’s dying message had mentioned the city of Wayn by name. So, when there was a dungeon break, there was every reason to believe it was not a random event but rather aimed at a specific target.

And the commands from the Monster King from that time period seemed to be irresistible, while the present day Monster King clearly, from the Dungeon Core’s message to him, commanded a sort of fanatical loyalty.

Strong Telepathy, in both cases? Adon wondered. He was aware that he could push on people’s minds if he wanted to, use Telepathy for more than just communication. He had tried altering the perception of other creatures before, and it took more power and effort, but it was definitely possible.

If that was the case, Adon himself might be able to act as a sort of counter to the Monster King. Maybe. He had spent most of his life strengthening his own Telepathy. If he could break the telepathic link between the King and its subjects, or even threaten to do so, that would make it very hard for the Monster Kingdom to wage war effectively.

Without a leader, the monsters Adon had experienced were almost all dumb beasts. There had been a couple of intelligent monsters in the dungeon he and Rosslyn had cleared and literally an army of brutish creatures. The golden ants in particular had been nothing but stupid, to the point that the only intelligent creature from their level had been influencing them telepathically.

The meeting ended without Adon being able to present any further information or refute any of either his own or the gathered generals’ speculations about the Monster Kingdom.

Over the next two weeks, plans unfolded to ready the combined military force to go forward with a bold attack on the capital.

The preparations unfolded at a relatively relaxed pace, not because anyone truly dreaded the task—certainly not now that they knew the Empire’s internal defenses of its conquered areas was weakening—but because the continued reports of scouts and spies streaming in told the leadership that the Demon Army was indeed reducing the concentration of its strength in the occupied territory day by day. Delay was actually beneficial to their chances of success, contrary to all the normal expectations they might have had, given that they needed to feed and house all of these people.

Adon had a feeling of dissatisfaction about everything. Not a bad feeling, or a sense of foreboding. It just seemed like things were incomplete. He wanted to know whether Claustria was going to get attacked by the Monster Kingdom after they retook their country from the Empire. He wanted to know if the Empire was going to defeat the monsters or if it would lose. Or maybe there would be another stalemate, as there seemed to have been for centuries, based on the fact that both the Empire and the Monster Kingdom still existed and skirmished.

But it felt like he was missing information to know whether the attack on Wayn was even something to be celebrated or something to dread.

The sun rose on the sixteenth day, and the butterfly—along with the entire population of the fort town—awakened to the sound of warning bells ringing.

“Demons! Demons are here!”

Adon heard the shouts repeated through the streets as he flew out the window of the room where he was staying. He quickly rose through the air until he was far above the fortifications and could easily see what was going on below.

Approaching in full view, walking openly on the path that led directly to the walls of the town, the butterfly could see there was indeed a party of demons. Their long horns made it obvious not only that they were not human but that, from what Adon recalled, the leaders must be from among the demons’ elite castes.

They wouldn’t send these people in such small numbers to go and deal with this fort, he thought even before he had scanned their minds and tried to perceive their intent. They’re important types. They must be here to negotiate.

When he did reach down telepathically to skim the surface thoughts in each individual’s brain, Adon confirmed what he had already suspected. The demons had come on a diplomatic mission.

Just seconds after he had read their thoughts, as they were almost within arrow range of the fortification’s walls, the demons raised a flag of truce.

“We come in peace!” yelled the leader from the front. “We wish to negotiate on behalf of His Imperial Majesty, the benevolent Demon Emperor!”

There was an uproar from within the walls, but Adon did not stay in the air to listen to it. He shot back to Rosslyn to inform her that the demons were, in fact, genuinely here to negotiate. That didn’t mean their intentions were good, only that there was no trap set up for the humans if they opened the gates.

As he found her in her room, she was donning her battle armor.

“Adon, good to see you,” she said. “Could you help me with this strap? I cannot reach. Normally, I have someone to assist me, but I have yet to properly set up my household again since we were forced to flee the city.”

He quickly transformed and moved to assist her, but he didn’t let that delay him informing her of what was going on.

“The demons are actually here to negotiate,” he said. “I read their minds briefly. The envoys who came have no backup hiding in the forest, no troops ready to ambush us or even rescue the messengers. And they sent elites who are from important bloodlines. You told me how important that is. That doesn’t mean they’ll be honest or trustworthy, but at least, I’m not sure that you need the armor for this meeting.”

“I would wear it for a diplomatic meeting with those animals anyway,” Rosslyn replied, continuing to fix the armor now that Adon had helped her with the part she could not reach. “They only respect strength and martial prowess, and they have little faith in female leaders as a rule. From the first encounter, I must signal that I am another Maud, not some pushover girl they can step on.”

“I hope the meeting will give us some useful information,” Adon said.

“I am certain it will, since you will be with me,” Rosslyn said, smiling. “If they lie to us, you will know. If they have useful information in their minds, you will simply take it. This meeting is going to be the worst decision the Demon Empire has ever made.”
