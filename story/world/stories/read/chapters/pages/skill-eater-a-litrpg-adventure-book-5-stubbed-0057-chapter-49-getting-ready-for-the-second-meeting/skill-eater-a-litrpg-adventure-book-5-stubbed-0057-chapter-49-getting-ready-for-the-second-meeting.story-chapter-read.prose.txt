The crew had been discussing their options all week, preparing for Edge and Sakura’s second meeting with King. They debated the merits of dozens of ploys while looking for a way to decline his bargain, but in the end, they hadn’t come up with anything that seemed likely to succeed.

Edge didn’t trust the leader of the Coalition’s Problem-Resolution Squadron, and he intended to pay Corpse back regardless of any deals they made. However, Edge believed the scion when he said the Wardens would take what they wanted without offering any concessions in return, which meant approaching them wasn’t an option.

Even if that wasn’t the case, King knew Puppet Town’s location. While the crew might be able to lay low and weather the faction’s wrath, he had no way to protect his home from an army that was multiple orders of magnitude stronger than the settlement’s collective might. I don’t think we could stop Corpse by herself if she were willing to go all out.

He hated the fact that the scion’s threat had worked out exactly the way she wanted, but he wasn’t willing to risk more lives by letting anger or pride guide his hand. Besides, while the situation was deeply frustrating, it wasn’t all bad news. His hometown would effectively be a hostage going forward, but it should receive some considerable benefits from the arrangement, including calling in the big guns if another kaiju appeared. Dialla would have to work hard to hide the existence of the core manufactory to preserve any semblance of autonomy, but he trusted the mayor to get the job done.

All other considerations aside, Edge had a hunch that if he refused King’s offer a second time, he wouldn’t be walking away. The crew couldn’t run without abandoning Puppet Town, and if they didn’t come up with a solution within the next twenty-four hours, he was prepared to take the deal.

When that topic reached a dead end, the conversation turned to negotiation tactics instead.

Before he signed on the dotted line, Edge planned to acquire as many concessions as possible, even if Sakura had to reveal her status as a scion to seal the deal. They wanted to secure the best possible terms for Puppet Town—all the resources and support the settlement needed to thrive over the long haul, along with a promise not to draw them into the war that was brewing between Ord’s strongest factions.

While he hated being played like this, Edge couldn’t deny that he was excited too. He wondered what kind of missions the Coalition would assign him. He was certain there would be considerable risks, but there should be profound opportunities for advancement too. He hoped to negotiate a deal that let him retain some autonomy, working as an independent contractor instead of King’s subordinate, although he wasn’t sure if he could make that happen with the leverage he had right now.

Edge had a hard time reading King. He had no idea if the man would keep his word regardless of the bargain they struck. But without any better options on the table, he didn’t have much choice other than to take a leap of faith and hope allying with the Coalition wouldn’t be as bad as he feared.

“At least you managed to hit stage three,” Trapper said as the conversation wound down. “That should give you wiggle room with the terms, especially if you decide to disclose Sakura’s core. Being strong-armed is incredibly aggravating, but we’ve done everything in our power to make the best of a bad situation.”

“On the bright side,” Sasha added. “The Coalition is the real deal. They’re strong enough to help us oppose the Ancients and thwart the monsters’ invasion. Sakura took a long look at Shield and King. While she can’t guarantee they aren’t compromised, she didn’t sense any traces of corruption in their base. If neither of you spot any sign of the filth during this visit, the odds are high that the Problem-Resolution Squadron hasn’t been infiltrated, and we can consult with them about the monster in World’s End.”

“I’m willing to work with some unsavory characters if it prevents a ravenous tide of alien predators from devouring all life on Ord.” Jumo sighed.

“Me too.” Mel nodded. “And my gut is telling me that what the Ancients are planning is even worse.”

The crew hunted for a few hours on their way back to town, letting Edge pick up another cycle and steal his daily skills.

You have accumulated sufficient experience to cycle up your core. Your new cycle is: 5.

You have 7 attribute points awaiting allocation.

This time around, he decided to invest in Reflex so he could make the most of Kinetic Evasion. He put five points into the attribute and one into Amplification and Disruption. He sat in the grass while the potentia sank into his nervous system, enhancing his responsiveness on a cellular level.

When the changes were complete, he stepped into his inner world to oversee Enhance Sense’s upgrade.

The skill Enhance Physical Senses has advanced to Rank 2.

Its mana efficiency has been enhanced.

Please choose between one of the following options:

Warning: If no option is selected, your skill will remain unchanged and gain a slight bonus to mana efficiency.

As Trapper had requested, Edge returned to his body to discuss his options before making his decision.

“Number two isn’t ideal for me. I’ve had so much practice using Gaze’s combined mode that I’m getting pretty good at dealing with sensory overload. I plan to put more points into Perception soon, which should help too.”

“That leaves you with a choice between restricting your senses while making one sharper or enhancing your ability to detect certain stimuli. The first will be useful in a wide range of situations, but the second is great for hunting. It will help you track prey, locate resources, and detect threats, though it may require some setup. I doubt you can flag a stimulus unless you’ve encountered it before.

“If we were still working out of Puppet Town, making a living by hunting beasts and gathering valuable materials, I would recommend going with that option. However, we’ve expanded our playbook in recent days—become explorers, warriors, monster slayers, and diplomats on top of anything else—so I think making your senses stronger on demand is the smartest play.”

Edge enjoyed having an opportunity to brainstorm with his team before committing to an upgrade and hoped they could do it more often. Everyone offered their insights, ultimately agreeing with Trapper’s assessment. He felt the same way. If he had been offered an upgrade that could flag magical signatures, he would have used it to track the Ancients’ filth, but since that wasn’t the case, improving the physical sense of his choice was clearly the better bet.

He popped into his central chamber and voiced his decision to the System. When the avatar stopped shining, the images formed within its swirling cloud had assumed a new pattern. Sometimes, it displayed representations of all five senses at once. Other times, the haze formed a single organ that was bigger and more detailed. He congratulated Enhance on its accomplishment and spent a few minutes chatting with every power in his collection before returning to his body, where his friends were waiting.

When they made it to World’s End, the team spent the final hours at their disposal preparing for the fated meeting. Edge was drilled by Sasha and Trapper, honing his negotiation skills. Sakura consulted with the free city’s information brokers in a final bid to acquire knowledge that would strengthen their bargaining position. That left Jumo and Mel to concoct a contingency plan in case negotiations broke down despite their intention to take the deal.

Later that evening, Edge went shopping for weapons for Tri-State Sentinel, picking out equipment ideally suited to each clone’s characteristics. The crew pooled their resources to purchase some spellshots and high-grade consumables, which meant the scions had some surprises in store if they were forced to fight their way out of the fortress.

By the time everyone returned to the Lost Traveler, midnight had come and gone. The hunters were too keyed up to sleep. They talked for hours around the table, considering one idea or another as the final seconds ticked past.

They left the free city accompanied in the murky light of predawn—uncertain what the future would hold but determined to face it head-on.
