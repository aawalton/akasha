“Issa, that’s his name,” Merlin said as he leaned over Kyu-Ri, resting his arm across the headrest of her swiveling gaming chair. She didn’t do much gaming with it, and was more of a computer freak obsessed with hacking into things that should be left alone, but the terminology of the name of her expensive seat contrasting with her obsession was not something Merlin was particularly concerned with. It was the boy he had seen just yesterday at Club Quilox.

Kyu-Ri, however, didn’t share the same enthusiasm as Merlin did on the matter.

“Remind me of your promise, will you?” she said as she chewed a piece of bubble gum noisily. When Merlin didn’t respond, she raised a finger to the roof, as though pointing at something, and added, “Ah! A full day to me humble self.” She turned around to face him, her lip shifting to one side slowly to reveal a sly smirk. “Ain’t it?”

Merlin clicked his tongue and took a step back.

It was not that he had forgotten his promise, this was just far more important than an adult lady sleeping all through the day or wandering about the city of Lagos with no particular purpose in sight. But he was well aware of how sacred a promise was, and since he couldn’t wait, he decided to sweeten the offer.

Showing two fingers, Merlin said, “Two days. Give me today, and I’ll give you two free days.”

Kyu-Ri frowned. “You’ll give me? What’d you think you are? Me boss?”

Technically, he was. She had been sent to Nigeria with him to ensure he kept a low profile and was not found out. Even though the events of the past night had gone against that rule, the fact that he had escaped before the arrival of the law enforcement made sure that his identity remained unknown. But, of course, Merlin realized that mentioning such a thing to Kyu-Ri would just make her angry. He had done enough teasing already. The easiest way to get her onboard was to make things fun. And he knew just how to do that.

“You know, we’ve basically become vigilantes,” Merlin started as he strode to a couch in the sky apartment that had become their home ever since their arrival in Nigeria. Kyu-Ri, where she was seated before her computer setup, turned around on her chair to follow Merlin’s movements. “What kind of vigilantes lets a young boy with a bright future ahead of him be entangled in a drug scandal, huh? Now that we’ve exposed the dealings occurring in Quilox, it’s only a matter of time before the boy is traced and caught. And that’s including if the police are involved in the whole fiasco. Think, K, a bust was made at Quilox, it can’t be easily covered unless someone takes the fall.”

“And the boy could be a target…” she mumbled.

“Exactly,” Merlin replied. “I ask you again. What kind of vigilantes lets a young boy with a bright future ahead of him be entangled in a drug scandal? I know I don’t want to. And besides, if we can get to the boy before the police, he might just lead us to the rest of the drug networks. You never know. And, personally, I think this could be fun.” He leaned back into the couch and crossed his legs.

Kyu-Ri’s eyes, on the other hand, fell to the floor, darting about as her thoughts leaked. A small smile revealed itself on Merlin’s lips as he watched her, but he quickly hid it when her gaze shot upwards and pinned itself on him. Then she smiled.

“You nasty little brat,” she said. “Got me there, didn’t you?”

Merlin smiled and shrugged. “But you’re on board, aren’t you?”

“Damn right, I fuckin’ am.” She swerved around to face her system. “Let’s do this. Issa, is it? Any last name?”

Merlin jumped to his feet and hurried to join Kyu-Ri once again.

“None that I know of,” he said. “But I can describe him, if that helps.”

Chewing her gum, Kyu-Ri answered, “It will, after I narrow down every single person named Issa older than ten years but lesser than sixteen in the whole of Lagos, a city with over more than fucking twenty million residents.” She turned around with a raised brow. “That’s not counting the possibility that he might not even be registered in the system. You nuts? No last name? What nonsense.”

Merlin frowned. “He was born, wasn’t he? He definitely has a birth certificate.”

“Oh, yeah? I wonder how good you would be at converting whatever image you’ve got of the lad in your head into that of when he was a teeny-weeny baby.”

Sudden frustration broke inside Merlin like a dam, and he straightened on his feet, exasperated.

After almost a minute of Merlin pacing around, disturbed as he tried to think up ways to bypass the humongous wall before him at this very moment, Kyu-Ri sighed audibly, dragging his attention back to her just in time to see her swerve around to face her computer.

“The boy got a Grimoire?” she asked.

Merlin shook his head. “I didn’t see any.”

“Hmmm… Well, I’m not going to bother with it. That takes out the Consortium Guild’s database,” she said casually.

“Got an idea?” Merlin asked.

Kyu-Ri clicked her tongue, subtly telling him not to speak to her. But she replied regardless, “Yes. Gonna take far longer than I would like, but I’ve got one, yeah.”

Merlin hurried back to her. “Which is?”

“There’re a couple of things children in their early teens love to associate themselves with. Ninety percent of them involve recent pictures, and most times they don’t hide their real names from the internet too. Which is dumb, honestly. Who puts all of themself out there. My social media picture is that of a fluffy cat. A Siberian cat. God, I wanna have one.”

Merlin had heard this same story countless times, and he had asked her every time why she couldn’t just get one as a means to not leave her hanging whenever she brought it up. She would always say it was because she was scared for her cables. Cats were kinda mischievous after all.

However, he didn’t pose that question again now. He was beginning to think she had short term memory regarding her social media experiences. Perhaps she traded that part of her sanity for her computer skills.

Whatever the case was, he was more concerned with how she was going to find Issa. And he had no qualms letting that known.

“So social media,” Merlin interrupted Kyu-Ri. She glanced at him, her brows slightly pulling in. “That’s how you’re going to get him, is it?” Merlin went on, acting oblivious to the stare she was shooting at him.

Kyu-Ri clicked her tongue once again, transitioning the act into something of a ritual now, and answered, “Yes, Merlin. Well, mails are the most likely, since there’s every certainty it’ll have his name, but there’s no certainty of pictures. What I’m going to do is shortlist the most likely people to be our mystery lad from every section, then I’ll sieve and sieve, until we have our man—or boy, to be precise.”

Merlin hummed in response. Before he could say anything, Kyu-Ri reached for a paper and pencil that was on the edge of her table and handed them to him. He raised a brow.

“Draw the kid,” she said.

Merlin frowned. “What? I thought I was just going to describe him to you?”

“Nah,” she shook her head. “Draw him.” She turned back to her computer screen and cracked her fingers. “Just think of it as you being the profiler of our small group of vigilantes, eh? Make-believe. Don’t worry. It’s fun.”

Merlin could smell the sarcasm in her tone. But, whatever, he didn’t mind. It was his idea to seek out Issa, and he had dragged Kyu-Ri into it. He had no idea why she was adamant on him drawing the image he had in his mind instead of describing it, but he was sure that under all her sarcasm, there was a genuine reason. She wasn’t Nora. She wasn't petty. Thankfully, he had a little bit of experience in drawing. Those little times he’d logged onto Udemy to take manga lessons because he wanted to create comics would pay off today.

“Make sure it’s perfect,” said Kyu-Ri as Merlin found a suitable spot perfect for relaxing his body as he drew. “I’ll not accept anything less.”

He took his thoughts back. There were no genuine reasons as to why he was drawing Issa’s features.

The whole process took hours. It was not until evening before they finally got a hit on something.

Kyu-Ri had scanned the drawn image of Issa, which Merlin was especially proud of, created a realistic 3D image of it, and after shortlisting the closest people to who they were seeking, used the image as a reference for her system to scan through the list she’d created, seeking anyone with similar features.

She was sipping a cup of coffee, while Merlin played with a finger of flame dancing on the center of his palm, still amazed that he was able to conjure magic even though months had already gone by since that day, when a ding from the system shook them back to reality.

The finger of flame on Merlin’s palm disappeared into thin air as he hurried to where Kyu-Ri was seated. A smile appeared on his face immediately and he grabbed Kyu-Ri’s shoulders and massaged them gently.

“You’re a freaking genius,” he hooted.

Kyu-Ri growled and shook his hands away from her shoulders.

On the slightly curved computer screen was the portrait of a teenage boy with a gaunt expression and sunken cheeks looking straight back at them. Beside it was his name and age. Umar Issa. Thirteen years of age.
