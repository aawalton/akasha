The rot then proliferated in patches, leaving gaps that exposed the man beneath. His visor dissolved first, revealing grey skin and fever-bright eyes, scanning and yet unseeing his surroundings.

His left pauldron was gone as he closed the distance between us. His right gauntlet hung in tatters.

And he was all the swifter for it.

His sword came down in a wild arc, buzzing with fluctuating gold lightning. I rolled left as the blade struck stone, sending fragments scattering.

Olethros shot several black tentacles at him from behind, trying to restrain him. They hissed and burned away on contact with the holy knight’s armor.

“Maybe we can give him a few moments to tire out!” I shouted. “He can’t keep this up forever.”

Marcus’s aura pulsed erratically as he stumbled through his recovery—gold flaring bright, then drowning in black so absolute it swallowed the light preceding it. The cycle repeated, faster and faster. Each pulse made him scream louder, the sounds less human every time they erupted.

He then pivoted, raised his shield while shrieking, and barreled forward once more as unbridled rage animated his every movement. But underneath it was the same decaying agony that was claiming his armor.

Correction, he’s not tiring; he’s fully intent on spending the last of his vital energy on nothing!

I backpedaled, keeping Will raised as Marcus swung his sword like a sledgehammer. The blade whistled past my shoulder, burying itself in yet another pillar. Fissures spider-webbed up the column as he yanked it out.

I stepped in to catch the next strike on Faith, reasoning we had a better chance of dispersing the force behind his attacks.

“Marcus!” I shouted again. “Fabian’s right over there, breathing. See?!”

He answered with another unintelligible scream and several sword slashes.

I couldn’t keep this up. The ereborite jewel was already shattered, so there was nothing to remove. Which meant I’d have to use the old method.

Compassionate smackdown.

Physical contact to cut through the overload and realign his spiritual core. I’d done it before, usually with a well-placed punch or a hand to the shoulder.

I’d have to get creative somehow. The fact that his armor was corroding on contact did not bode well for me.

His Majesty materialized between Marcus and me, inviting his next attack. Olethros then sidestepped, shooting out another round of shadowy tentacles. This time, they aimed for the exposed parts of his body, successfully latching and hauling the hulking holy knight backward.
