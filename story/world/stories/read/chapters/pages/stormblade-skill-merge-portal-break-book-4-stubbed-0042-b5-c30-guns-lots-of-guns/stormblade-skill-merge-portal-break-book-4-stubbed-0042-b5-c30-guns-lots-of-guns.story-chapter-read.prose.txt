Robert Traynor had been busy.

His daughter was a full-grown, brilliant diamond. She no longer needed his protection—but she did, on occasion, require his guidance.

So, when the head of the Traynor Corporation’s weapons development branch called him asking about a delivery to Surprise, he’d been on high alert right away. He’d pulled up the invoice, looked up the buyer, and put a twenty-four-hour hold on the purchase. The Governing Council would have to wait if they wanted their Traynor Cannons delivered to the Desert Wind Guild.

Bob had already mentally erased the Overholz name from the guns. They were his, not Rebecca’s.

He leaned back in his chair, hands behind his head, and pulled up a recording of the next generation of cannons in testing. These were smaller, but his research and development team was pretty sure they’d figured out both rifling and explosives. If that was true, the Traynor Cannons were pretty much caught up to pre-Portal Blitz artillery. It was only a question of refinement. This would change the world—and Bob would be the man who’d done it.

A notification appeared on his screen.

He stared at it.

Purchase hold waived, by order of the Phoenix Governing Council. This purchase is vital to the security and existence of Phoenix, and must be filled immediately.

Further attempts to place this purchase on hold will result in immediate action.

Thank you for your cooperation.

“Fine. Fine.” Bob stood, pulled his jacket on over his shoulders, and buttoned the top button only. Then he checked his hair in the mirror and strode to the door. His trucks were already being loaded with his guns, even though he’d given the order to delay.

Fine.

Yes, this was fine. The delivery would happen right on time, just like the Governing Council wanted. He’d even be there when it arrived, to hand the weapons off to that Gerald girl. What she had in mind for them, he couldn’t guess, but if she wanted the previous generation of cannons, at bulk pricing, who was he to say no?

After all, the Governing Council had demanded it.

Ellen’s hand stayed in mine as she stared at the limousine. “He’s not here for me.”

“Why else would he be here?” I asked.

She shook her head slowly. “We’re legally separate entities, and have been since we signed those papers. I gave up all my stake in his company, and in return, he agreed to leave me alone. He wouldn’t compromise that agreement. Would he?”

Councilwoman Myers cleared her throat. “I’m not sure where you want all these, but we can have them unloaded onto the street. It doesn’t look like your guild hall’s in any shape to receive weapons.”

She was right. I closed my eyes and tried to let go of Ellen’s hand. She squeezed tighter, and I stopped. She’d gone from a confident, serious, S-Rank delver to a…scared…young woman in seconds. But right now, we needed to focus on getting these weapons. We could handle Bob later. “That should be fine. We can move them from the street to where we need them once they’re all dropped off.”

“Great. I’ll have the GC teams cordon off an area around your building to drop the crates off.”

I watched Councilwoman Myers go, then lowered my voice. “Ellen, I can handle Bob if you need me to. You can ask for a ride and get to the Portal Tyrant’s castle. That’s where everyone else is.”

She rolled her eyes.

“I’m serious. I know Bob’s a tough guy for you to be around, and I’m willing to take the heat for you.”

“Yeah, I know, and I appreciate it. But…no, I need to do this.” Ellen finally slipped her hand out of mine. She looked at the limousine for the first time, then raised a single, gore-covered palm toward it.

She waved.

The door opened, and Bob Traynor stepped out.

He wore a light suit, out of a material I’m pretty sure Ellen had told me was seersucker. He’d hidden his eyes behind sunglasses, but he was clearly fully focused on Ellen. Even I was only an afterthought.

“You know, Eleanor, I visited you when you almost died during the tournament,” Bob said. He leaned against the car’s body, arms crossed. “You said you didn’t want me there. Your boyfriend there threw me out. But now, you two come crawling back to the Traynor Corporation.”

Ellen coughed slightly. A little seawater trickled down her chin, and she wiped it on her battle robes’ sleeves. They were a horrific combination of faded red, slimy green, and mud-brown, and I doubted she’d made her face any cleaner. We’d fought hard against the Paragon of the Stillwave Path, and we’d both taken a beating. Her puncture wound had stopped bleeding; instead, it was an angry red color that, in a normal human, would have signaled infection.

In an S-Rank delver, it wasn’t even worth mentioning.

“I didn’t want you there. Daddy…no, Bob. Bob, we signed an agreement. You have no legal rights as my father. Why are you here?”

Bob Traynor smiled.

As he did, Ellen’s fingers touched mine again. Just for a moment, but as they did, a pressure built in my core—and in hers. I could feel it. Shadowstorm Battery, our Dual Skill Progression skill, wanted to push to the next rank. But now wasn’t the time for either of us.

“I’m here to make sure that this arrangement is what’s best for my daughters—for both of you,” Bob said. “Legally speaking, I have no ability to influence you or shape your path. But someday, you’ll realize that you made a mistake. The Traynor Guild is up and running again. We’ve brought in two new teams. A pair of S-Rankers would make us the most powerful guild in the city. We’d even be able to challenge the Governing Council itself.

All you’d have to do is—“

“I don’t want that. I’ve never wanted that,” Ellen said. “But you’ve never really cared about what I wanted. It was always about the Traynor Corporation with you. Always.”

“That’s—“

Ellen interrupted again. “I spent years trying to be what you wanted me to be. I learned everything you wanted me to learn. How to be pretty, charming, adorable, witty—all the things I needed to survive your cocktail parties and receptions. How to run a business. How to give orders. I hardly ever saw Mom, and I didn’t understand why. I still don’t. She’s around, but it always felt like she’d just…given up on us both.”

Bob stiffened. “She wanted nothing to do with the corporation, and I let her have her way. I regret that every day, Eleanor—just like I regret not forcing you to do the right thing.”

“The right thing for who?” Ellen asked.

“For us,” Bob said. “The world changed, Ellen. It’s not enough to make it. We have to be powerful, and until—“

Darkness welled around Ellen suddenly. Her aura rippled out, stopping inches from Bob; he couldn’t see it, but I could feel the anxiety increase inside of me. Her skin darkened as she cast Voidwalker, the energy pulling on our Shadowstorm Battery bond as Mana flowed into her to sustain it. “Daddy, I am powerful. I don’t need your money or your influence to be powerful. I earned this myself.”

“You earned that because your boyfriend carried you through it.”

“No, I didn’t,” I said. I didn’t summon the Storm’s Blade, but it was a close thing. The fury coming from Ellen was all but intoxicating, and I wanted to slip into it myself, to let the battle trance take over. But that would be a massive mistake. This wasn’t my fight. It was Ellen’s. All I needed to do was help. “Ellen would have reached A-Rank without me, and done it easily. We did it together instead.”

“But you wouldn’t gain power together, with me?” Bob asked. His voice had shifted. The anxiety aura hadn’t reached him yet, but something about him…something about him had gotten weaker as Ellen had unleashed a fraction of her strength.

“No.”

“Why not?”

“Because you’re just like the monster that destroyed our guild building, Daddy. You get your tentacles into everything, and you squeeze it for everything it’s worth. You never stop—not after you’ve gotten enough, not after you’ve gotten plenty, and not after you’ve gotten far, far too much.” Ellen took a breath. Her Voidwalker fell off, and she stared at Bob. “If I helped you with everything I’ve learned, with everything I’ve accomplished, it wouldn’t mean anything to you.

“You’d just add Kade and me back into the Traynor Family, and we’d be another feather in your cap, another part of your favorite daughter’s ever-growing, ever-hungry body. That’s never been what I wanted. And Kade gets that. He understands that there’s a limit to power, and a point where it has to stop.”

She was talking about ascension. About SS-Rank. I couldn’t let myself reach that point. Sooner or later, I’d have to have a conversation with the Crone about the process of reaching those heights, so I could avoid doing it. Because Ellen was right. There were limits to power. When one person—the God of Thunder, the Placid King, Retan, or Bob Traynor—got too much of it, it corrupted.

“Please have your people unload the delivery behind those cones,” Ellen said. She pointed to a line of orange cones between the still-burning guild building and the trucks. “Then you can go. Goodbye, Bob.”

We walked to the GC car, Ellen leading the way. Her fists were balled and shaking, and she didn’t look back. She slid across the seat before I could walk around, then patted it. “Get in.”

I didn’t argue. Everything about her body language, from her shaking to her too-straight shoulders all the way to the stare she had locked on the limo, screamed that she was ready to lose it. I joined her, hip bumping against hers as she slid over a little more, and shut the door.

“I know you’re probably upset about losing your—“

“That asshole!” Ellen interrupted. “That absolute asshole.”

“Ah.” A second passed. Two. Ellen’s relationship with her father was nothing like mine had been with mine. I knew that, of course, but it was hard to really believe it until I saw her reactions to him. “Do you want me to kill him?”

She stopped. Her eyes met mine, and I looked away first. “I’m joking, Ellen. Joking.”

“That’s too bad.”

More silence. It was only broken when the driver’s intercom flared up on the wall between our compartment and his. “Where are we going?” he asked.

“Uh, the Portal Tyrant’s castle.”

“Got it.”

I took a moment to decide it was safe, then pulled Ellen in and held her as the car started moving. The AC was almost too strong, but I was pretty sure that wasn’t why she was shivering—and I was equally sure that holding her wouldn’t warm her up. It was what she needed, though. I just held her as her anger slowly shifted from something out of control and enraging into something more…more like my fury. Still anger. Still dangerous. But focused.

“It’s not fair that we can’t kill him,” she said slowly. “I don’t know if I want to. It might cause more problems than it solves. But god dammit, it would have felt good to unleash my power back there.”

I didn’t let her go, and her head rested on my shoulder. We were still both covered in the remnants of our battle with the Placid King, so it wasn’t exactly a pleasant headrest, but Ellen didn’t seem to care, and neither did I. I didn’t move as I thought about it. “There are different kinds of power, you know.”

“Really?” Ellen asked.

I shifted just enough to jostle her, then stared her in the eye until she shrugged. “Sorry. That was uncalled for. I know Bob’s got a different kind of power, though. But, really, he’s no different than Retan or the Placid King.”

“Yes, he is.”

“How so?”

I waited for a few seconds, pulling my thoughts together. “Bob’s a lot more like the God of Thunder than like either of those two. Not in his personal power, but in his attitude toward it and how it affects his relationships with people. Eug—the God of Thunder…I think he really believed we were friends. He really, truly did care about me, in his own, weird way.”

“That way almost got us all killed in Yalerox’s tower,” Ellen pointed out.

Another pause. Then I nodded. “Yeah, it did. He didn’t care about any of you. As long as I kept moving forward, that was his priority. Bob’s a lot like him in that regard, too. Do you think he really cares about the Traynor Guild?”

“It makes a lot of money.”

“It’s a drop in the bucket for him, right?”

“True.”

“No, Bob only cared about one thing, and that was getting you back into the fold. If you’d given in, he’d have built the best team he could around you, because you being part of the corporation would have helped him more than the current Traynor Guild ever could.”

Ellen shifted, lying down so her head rested on my lap, looking up at me. Neither of us had buckled in; a car crash at ninety wouldn’t even phase us at S-Rank. I ran my fingers through her hair—avoiding the slimiest bits. “You need a shower.”

“I know. Shut up.” She glared up at me, and I stopped. “No, keep going. It feels good. But you’re right. Bob always wanted me in the guild. Not you or the others. Just me.”

“Exactly like the God of Thunder. But his ability to care about me only extended as far as I was useful and interesting, and Bob’s ability to care about you only extended as far as you were obedient and helpful. They’re the same.”

“Other than the fact that one of them has phenomenal cosmic power.”

“And the other one wants it.”

Silence. It hung for a moment, then Ellen started laughing in my lap. I joined her, scratching her scalp as I did. If she’d been a cat, she’d be purring. As it was, she melted into me just a little more.

After a minute, I stopped and rested my hand on her shoulder. She looked like she was asleep, but I wasn’t fooled.

“Jessie’s going to kill you,” she said eventually.

“Oh? Why?”

“Because you destroyed everything she’s been working toward. That guild was her baby. We talked about it a few times when you weren’t around. She loved every part of running it, and now she’s out a base of operations.”

I groaned. Ellen was right. The God of Thunder would have to wait, and so would Bob. As the GC car pulled up to the Portal Tyrant’s headquarters, I steeled myself to face a boss no amount of ranks, skills, or Laws could prepare me for.
