Alexander and Johnny made their way back to the pool. As they reached the clearing, they noticed the roots around the pool had twisted into various forms. They had been shaped into chairs, a table, and a pagoda to sit under. A soft glow now filled the clearing, accompanied by glowing insects that perched on the trees. Linda was still mesmerized by the pool, but she now sat on an armchair that seemed to have grown up beneath her.

The Widow of the Wood sat at the table on her hair and waved them over to her pagoda. As they approached, she greeted them.

“Alexander! What fortuitous timing you have. I have sat here pondering any need I may have for you, and then a circumstance presents itself in which you may assist me and win my favor.” She smiled ominously.

“Well, I am happy to assist.” Alexander attempted a smile in return.

“Please, sit. You may even take out your quaint contract, and we shall put promise to paper. Then seal our connection and destiny’s plan for the magic to judge.”

Alexander felt a small comfort as he pulled his clipboard back out.

“First is to make your request of myself.” she said, putting her hand to her chest solemnly.

“Yes. I believe what we want out of our arrangement are three things. First, for you to free Linda and Megan from their connections to this pool and yourself so they are able to leave without any entanglements with this world.”

“This is within my power to do.” The Widow nodded.

Alexander looked over at Johnny, who nodded as well.

“Second is to make sure none of the rest of the people—or the dog that we travel with—will be enchanted by the pool or the magic of this place, so that we can leave without complications.”

“I can suppress the power of the pool so that it will no longer impact the minds of your friends. I will grant them safety in this place.”

“Finally, your assistance in our safe return to our world. Whether that is knowledge of threats or difficulties we may face, how to open the portal to our home, or your full willingness to help us get home.”

“Agreed. Assuming no other agreements are made, your people should have no difficulty returning. Free will is a burden mortals bear, however.”

Johnny laughed before commenting, “So you’re saying you won’t protect us from the consequences of our own actions, is it?”

“Just so.”

Alexander wasn’t sure how to respond, so he simply took some notes for the contract.

“Now, what is it you will need in order to secure these requests?” Alexander asked, pen at the ready.

“I need your assistance in vanquishing an intruder in my lands.”

“Can you explain the nature of this intruder?” Alexander asked.

“I can only sense them dimly. They are an ancient power that I have never encountered before. It threatens my dominion by its very presence. They must not reach this place. All I have built will come to ruin if they do.”

“And you require all of us to accomplish this?” Alexander asked.

“No, I only require the assistance of Alexander. He will have the required skills to assist me in this task.”

Johnny looked at Alexander, concerned. “I think it would be wise if we all went together, begging your pardon.”

“You are pardoned,” the Widow said flatly. “But I do not want to protect a large group, as this agreement already requires much of me.”

Alexander and Johnny were silent as they looked at each other with concern.

“What about just adding me and Bubba, then? We seem capable of resisting the mental attacks of the pool, so we are less likely to be a burden. Also, we are confident in our ability to hide and escape danger.” Johnny pressed.

The Widow’s face scrunched up in agitation for a moment, then she smiled. “Very well, wood walker. You and your companion may also join in this crusade.”

“In the event we fail, will the protection still stand?” Alexander asked.

“Your full effort to assist me in defeating the foe is required. If I have to exert my full efforts, then my ability to protect your friends may falter.”

“A good-faith agreement.” Alexander nodded. “I shall add that to the contract.”

“Then we are agreed?” the Widow asked.

Johnny looked at Alexander. “I feel we are out of our depth otherwise. I don’t see much choice if we want to help Megan and Linda.”

Alexander felt this was a gamble; he didn’t like it. In the end, he didn’t see much other choice.

“We are agreed.”

Alexander spent the next thirty minutes crafting the contract. He didn’t leave room for the Widow to betray her word, but he didn’t have knowledge of her way of thinking. He had Brian and Johnny look over what he had written, and they shrugged, seeing it as the best of a bad situation.

“But why not bring me? I would obviously be an asset,” Cassidy said with a huff.

“Maybe she doesn’t want to burn down her territory. That might add more problems than solutions,” Judah responded.

Cassidy folded her arms and nodded, resigned. Her demeanor toward the party had relaxed somewhat.

Alexander nodded. “I will do my best, and hopefully we will all be on our way home within the next day or two.”

“Returning everyone home safely is the best we could ask for. Plus, we would at least have a non-aggressive relationship with one authority in this world. That would be a huge success, in my opinion,” Brian said to the group, who all seemed to agree.

Alexander and Johnny presented the contract to the Widow, who held it in her hand and stared at it blankly. She looked up at Alexander and asked, “Do you think I know how to interpret your scribblings? Read it to me, and I shall match the will you intend to place upon myself.”

“Ah, well. My mistake.”

He read it through, and the Widow nodded in approval.

“Very good, young magi. You would do well in my service.”

“Thank you, but I am not looking for employment at this time. If you would like to continue working together in the future, you can reach out to my direct supervisor.”

The Widow nodded, then reached out to touch the contract. “I accept this bond.”

Do you accept the contract of conquest between your party and the Widow of the Wood?

Alexander’s mind was filled with the contract, and he felt the binding that would be placed upon him. His full efforts to assist the Widow would be required of him, and she would be forced to protect his party in return.

“I accept.”

Three tendrils of magic moved from the contract to wrap themselves around Alexander, Johnny, and the Widow. It was noticeably less intense compared to the contract between Bru-haha and himself. Johnny shivered, as if a cold wind had blown across the back of his neck. Alexander felt the hairs on his arms stand on end.

“It is agreed,” the Widow said as she moved her arms and concentrated.

The roots near the pond groaned as they moved to cover it. Linda reached her hand out with longing as the roots covered the magic waters, and she whimpered as they were sealed. Megan’s face held a sad expression as the light dimmed.

“Your friends shall be safe from the pool’s temptations now. None shall dare enter my sanctum while we are away.”

“Thank you. We will talk with our group and be ready to set out shortly.”

“Make haste. The closer they get, the more they will subvert my authority.” The Widow scowled.

As they made their way back to the party, Big Bad sat on his haunches near a tree, a disconcerting grin on his face.

“Bold you are, and also foolhardy. Safety is not safe for you and your party,” the wolf chuckled.

Alexander didn’t know how to respond.

“We can do nothing but our best,” Johnny said, placing a hand on Alexander’s shoulder.

Alexander pulled out his tackle box as he walked up to Brian.

“I think we will take every precaution we can. No holding back,” Alexander said as he handed the box to Brian. “Before we go, let me share a few things with all of you.”
