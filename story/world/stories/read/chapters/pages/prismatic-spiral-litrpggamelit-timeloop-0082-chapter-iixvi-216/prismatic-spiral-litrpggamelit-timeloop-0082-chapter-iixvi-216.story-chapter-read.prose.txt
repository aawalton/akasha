Chapter II.XVI (2.16)

Thankfully Sterling kept Mitsuko from oversleeping. The cat leaped up onto her face and she jolted awake with a start, banging her head on the bed overhead. While she’d been sleeping, someone had moved her into the bunk room and placed her on a bottom bunk. She suspected Tori, as Holly had no spells suited for moving someone adult human sized with enough grace to not wake them. She flopped back down and stared up at the underside of the bed over her.

On the floor beside her bed, Sterling groomed himself, rubbing his ears with the back of his paw.

“I wonder if I could set up my Mends to activate without me directly commanding them,” Mitsuko said out loud. “I could trap myself in a state of dreamful bliss for the rest of eternity.”

“While hypothetically possible, that is not an especially productive course of action,” Sterling commented. “You’d need to train yourself to cast completely out of instinct to keep your body unconscious. Something that takes a great deal of skill and practice. Or perhaps I suppose you could attempt an enchantment of some sort. I personally dabbled very little in that side of our craft. I much prefer the raw power and reliability of real spellwork.”

Mitsuko sighed and tried to sort through her next few priorities despite the distractions around her. Beside her, she heard Holly’s familiar snores, like a lemur giving rhythmic raspberries. On the other side of the closed door, children argued with their mother. Mitsuko only caught snippets of the conversation but it sounded like Tori was insisting a toy named Chelsea needed a bath. Someone had dragged it out into the muddy street. One child sounded very upset with the other so Mitsuko suspected this might have been a plot of mischief or malice. Though it could just as likely be a misunderstanding.

The confrontation ended when Tori forced her children to apologize to one another and hug. Shortly after, the door into the bunk room creaked open and a blond child stuck her head in.

“Kitty cat!” the girl failed to whisper.

Sterling perked up and nervously glanced between Mitsuko and the girl peering in. He shuffled closer to Mitsuko.

Minding her head this time, Mitsuko sat up and stretched. Sterling immediately hopped up onto her bed, out of reach of the girl.

“Hey, what’s your name?” Mitsuko whispered to the girl.

“Jo,” she loudly whispered back.

“Nice to meet you, Jo. Mind if I squeeze past you? I could use some fresh air.”

Jo scampered off in the other direction, leaving the door half-open.

Sterling’s entire body relaxed with her departure.

“What’s with you?” Mitsuko asked, standing up. “Do you have a problem with her? Did she pull your tail or something?”

“Worse,” Sterling grumbled. “She tried to put a hat on me.”

“Huh. Surprising. You always struck me as an enjoyer of hats.”

“Tsk!” Sterling shifted his head in disgust. “A proper hat? Perhaps I would bear it upon my crown. But she attempted to subject me to a frilly blue bonnet! Like one donned by an infant!”

Mitsuko barely held back a burst of laughter. It turned into a choke and she darted out of the room before the noise woke up Holly. Once out in the main room and with the door closed behind her, she crouched next to Sterling and cast Mend on herself to return her body to normal, allowing her to take in a deep breath.

“Where did she even get a bonnet your size?” Mitsuko whispered to the cat.

“I believe she stole it from the articles of clothing meant for the soon to be birthed infant within her mother’s womb.”

“Mitsuko? Is that you?” Tori called across the room. “Someone arrived I’d like to introduce you to.”

Mitsuko left her crouched position and stood to her full height.

Sure enough, a sculpt sat on the couch next to Tori. Despite being bundled up like an easily bruised apple, Mitsuko could still tell the sculpt was shaped like a young woman. Her dark eyes, made from coal, examined Mitsuko from beneath a white fur hood.

The woman raised a mittened hand in greeting. Either fashion was extremely different in sculpt culture, or this sculpt was making some questionable decisions with her outfit’s color scheme. Mismatched patched mitten were only the start of the fashion disaster. Most of it looked like it didn’t even fit properly. Some cloth sagging loose while other bits were cinched tighter than a corset.

“Oh. Hello,” Mitsuko said awkwardly. She wished Holly was awake to speak to this woman. But she supposed that if she made a social faux pas and flubbed the conversation she could always just try again next loop.

“Hello. My name is Jacqueline. Pleasure to make your acquaintance, Mitsuko.”

“Likewise. Is that juhyo fur?” Mitsuko asked.

Jacqueline glanced at her white fur coat. “It is. They are designed to help keep us cool despite the sun outside. Please forgive the mismatched patterns. We do not have nearly enough at this time. I gathered articles from four different sets, loaned to me by many in town.”

“I didn’t even notice. It all looks like white fur to me," Mitsuko lied. The differences in patterns were obvious. But she at least knew enough to not press the topic.

“Word is that you traveled here in an attempt to discover the barrier’s source,” Jacqueline said.

“True,” Mitsuko confirmed. “I plan to depart as soon as my companion wakes up.”

“I have been sent by town to act as your guide into the mountains.”

Mitsuko held back a frown. Suddenly, this sculpt rocketed up on her list of acolyte suspects. She desperately wished she could trust and rely on a local to guide her. But, as it was, no one from this island was truly trustworthy. Not even the children in the house.

“I think we’ll be fine without a guide,” Mitsuko said slowly. “My friend is a diviner. Her spells will keep us safe and help detect any hidden lairs.”

Jacqueline shifted in her seat. “If I’m not suitable, I can ask for a replacement,” she said, her voice a bit hoarse.

Then Mitsuko understood. “You’re here on orders from King Lars.”

“I—I am.” Jacqueline nodded. “But only because he worries for your safety. If something were to happen to you, it would break his heart.”

Mitsuko almost asked if sculpts had hearts to break but held her tongue. Even if Jacqueline’s statement had been metaphorical, it might be a rude question for Mitsuko to ask. Instead, she studied the young sculpt woman.

It could be that King Lars was worried he might be blamed by the Hon Empire if she and Holly encountered trouble out in the wilderness of his island. This very well might be a scheme in which to insure their safety. Or… he could be hiding something. Whether a scheme to kill them as an acolyte to the island’s guardian or a more mundane secret buried out in the snow. Back on Verdant the goblin witchdoctor had been closed lipped on the locations of ancient sites in fear the Hon government might have plans for them. Something similar could be occurring here as this loop Holly had outed them as friends of Emperor Sasaki.

“Fine,” Mitsuko decided. “You can join us.”

While it might be a better idea to request to swap their guide, Mitsuko would like to know for certain if Jacqueline was an acolyte for future loops.

Tori soon sent her children off to bed and prepared a meal of bacon and eggs for them to munch on while they waited for Holly. After a couple hours of smalltalk, the gnome stumbled out of the bunk room and rubbed her eyes.

“Food?” she rasped, sounding like a zombie.

“There’s bac—” Tori cut herself off and her eyes widened. “A gnome! I completely forgot. Let me get you veggies. Give me just five minutes!” She scrambled back to the kitchen in a hurry.

“Thank you.” Holly flopped down on the couch between Mitsuko and Jacqueline. “Hello. Who are you?”

Jacqueline reintroduced herself for Holly’s sake. As the gnome nodded along to the words, her eyelids closed. When she stopped nodding, her head drooped and a bit of drool dribbled out of her mouth.

“Does she require additional rest?” Jacqueline asked. “We can postpone if she is not fit for travel.”

Mitsuko elbowed her friend who jerked to attention. “She’s fine. She does this every morning.”

Holly returned to the land of the conscious more fully once Tori set a plate of roasted tubers and a mug of herbal tea in front of her.

“Thank you,” she said around a mouthful of food. She swallowed and let out a satisfied sigh. “Bless. This is even better than Mitsuko’s cooking.”

“You cook?” Tori asked.

“No,” Mitsuko replied. “I can just make food edible. Something Holly struggles to achieve.”

Holly protested and acted wounded by the comment. The four women continued like that for a little while longer, enjoying the banter. But, soon after clearing their plates, the mission loomed. And the clock was ticking.
