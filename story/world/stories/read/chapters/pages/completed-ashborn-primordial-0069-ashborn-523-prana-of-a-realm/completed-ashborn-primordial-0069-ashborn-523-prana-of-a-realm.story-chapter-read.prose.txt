The machine was even more monstrous up close.

Vir would know, having entered it countless times in his nightmares. He’d ridden its central lift to the top, had inserted his body into its array of hoses and pipes, his body coming apart at the seams with pain Vir had once thought impossible.

Yet those visits had always begun mid-sequence the way dreams did, with no memory of having chosen to walk through the door. This was different. This was voluntary, and the difference was enormous.

He stood at its base in the Crown Chakra mindscape and looked up at it, at the black hull that seemed to swallowed the light around it. At the pipes and hoses running its length like veins on some colossal creature, bound by chains that shackled it to the walls and floor and ceiling. As though even its creator hadn't fully trusted what he’d built.

Ten stories of Imperium engineering to work the miracle of all miracles. His life for countless millions.

Vir didn't want to die. That much hadn't changed.

And yet some other force seemed to have taken over the task of moving his body, guiding him toward the lift at the center. Duty, he supposed, felt strangely like what he imagined possession by the Fateweavers must feel like.

As though his life had always been moving toward this particular door no matter his intent, and now that he stood before it, was powerless to do anything but enter.

Which was fortunate, because despite everything he'd said to Maiya, Vir felt nowhere near as certain on the inside.

The lift was a narrow thing, barely wide enough for two people to stand abreast, open on all sides, the machine's interior on full display as it carried him up.

Its frame, up close, was not entirely smooth. Tiny channels traced patterns across its surface. Prana conduits that would blaze with light when the device was powered.

The pipes that jutted out at haphazard had the same inscriptions. Inscriptions that put even the most complex Ultimate Art to shame.

He'd always wondered, in the nightmares, why the machine felt so familiar. Now, rising through its center, he thought he understood.

This was, as Janak had said, his destiny. The purpose he’d sought all his life. It was carved on his very skin since the day he was born.

He stood in the lift for a moment after it reached the top, just looking out over the machinery below him. And because this was his mindscape, the walls dimmed, and he saw further. To Kartara’s walls in the snowy north and Camar Gain’s arena in the opposite end of the universe.

Vast and still and, as he now understood, deliberately empty. He'd always taken the emptiness for an absence. Worlds that would come to life with a future upgrade, perhaps, or when he made the next breakthrough.

It had never once occurred to him that this space had never been a map of the world, but a practice ground for one that didn't yet exist.

A world Vir was supposed to seed with life, by collapsing the Ashen Realm and channeling the whole of its infinite prana through a mortal body that wasn’t even designed to survive such trauma.

That was a depressing thought, and so instead, Vir focused on the task at hand.

He activated the machine the way Janak had shown him, firing a twelve-step sequence of Ash prana into the main control panel in the precise order required.

Each step had to be exact in both timing and prana quantity, and without the sequence, Vir would not have had the faintest hope of making the thing respond at all.

Which was a good thing he decided. Without it, the machine might have been activated by accident in the millennia it sat dormant. Given that the machine's purpose was to drain an entire realm of all its prana, it seemed like exactly the sort of accident worth protecting against.

The machine responded instantly. Blue-white light flared to life across the jet-black hull, tracing up and down its length, as though the beast were waking after an age of slumber.

The pipes hummed and the chains rattled as the floor began to vibrate and the air in the chamber grew heavy, like air in the face of an incoming storm.

Then, slowly, the prana began to build.

So slowly, in fact, that had Janak not explained what to expect, Vir would have assumed something had gone wrong.

Even a mediocre mejai could accumulate prana faster than this.

But like a Mahakurma, like all great beasts, the machine took great effort to shift.

It began as a trickle, slowly doubling and redoubling its intake as time passed. Not collapsing the Ash so much as draining it the way a wound drained blood. Desiccating the realm until nothing but a dry husk remained. The collapse would then happen all on its own.

The plan was so brilliant, so outrageously audacious, that Vir scarcely believed it was possible, even now.

And yet, a thought nagged at him. The Ashen Realm contained a nearly infinite amount of prana in its air and its soul. In the fabric of its reality itself.

Yet that was not the sum of it. The Ash Beasts. Prana Swarms, Wyrms, Ash Biters, and everything in between all had plenty of it.

As did his Asura.

If the machine drained all the prana from the realm, what became of them?

Janak had assured him that, should he succeed in transplanting the souls of the realms, they would emerge unscathed. But that process would begin only once the prana of the Ash had been consumed by the machine.

Vir swore to confirm with Janak before attempting this in earnest, for his peace of mind, if nothing else. For all that he was the lynchpin of the procedure, he had ironically little choice in the matter. Even if saving the realms required sacrificing thousands, he would have to do it. He could only pray it didn’t.

The prana was amassing faster, building in great spiraling currents that moved through the chamber at speeds that made the air howl like wind.

Vir saw it all through Prana Vision well before it became visible, of course. But when it did become visible, when what had been an invisible force became a roiling maelstrom of dense energy that blotted out the walls entirely, spinning in layers that compressed against each other with the sound of something vast being slowly crushed, Vir realized this was so far beyond anything he had ever known.

He had witnessed prana in every state he'd ever believed it capable of, manifesting as flames on his own body, or as liquid in that Fateweaver controlled universe.

But he had never seen it become anything beyond that. Had not thought such a thing physically possible, and for a moment he simply stared at the churning luminous mass of it rotating around him as it kept going.

Past liquid, into a form as solid as the strongest steel. Surpassing it, even, to become a solid wall of compressed energy that rotated around him with speeds that ought to have torn him asunder, taking the entire machine along with it.

It's designed to handle this, he reminded himself, before suddenly remembering this was all merely a simulation. A figment of his Chakra Mindscape.

That he had to remind himself proved both how strong his mindscape was… and how absolutely terrifying this machine could be.

If his mere mindscape was capable of such a phenomenon, what would the real thing be like?

And if even a fraction of that prana escaped, if the shielding failed by even the smallest margin, he wouldn’t simply die. He, along with all the hope for the realms, would be erased before he knew what hit him.

There would be no body to bury. No part of him that escaped intact.

The prana was now dense enough to vaporize anything organic in an instant, and simply being near it was sufficient. The only thing standing between him and absolute obliteration was a machine built five thousand years ago by a desperate man trying to save his daughter.

And then, when Vir thought the prana could grow no more dense, it surged through the pipes, and he watched with mounting horror as he realized where those pipes led.

To the machine’s very core.

To him.

Time slowed, but no amount of Haste could prepare for the deluge that bombarded him.Panicking he primed every Ultimate Art in his body, but when the deluge inevitably touched his body, he met it the way a demon might meet a tsunami.

There was no pain. His world simply ceased to be, and then he was standing at the base of the machine again, looking up at the black hull and its breathing pipes and its patient chains, the absolute silence of the absence of roiling prana ringing deafeningly in his ears.

He stood there for a moment, his mind catching up with what had just happened.

He’d failed, his body vaporized and his mindscape reset. Had this been real, his ineptitude would have doomed the realms forever.

But it wasn’t, and Vir could try again as many times as he liked.

And so, he stepped back into the lift, rising through the machine's center and activating the contraption once more. The blue-white lights brought the machine to life, and below him, the empty realms stretched outward in silence, once again waiting to be filled.
