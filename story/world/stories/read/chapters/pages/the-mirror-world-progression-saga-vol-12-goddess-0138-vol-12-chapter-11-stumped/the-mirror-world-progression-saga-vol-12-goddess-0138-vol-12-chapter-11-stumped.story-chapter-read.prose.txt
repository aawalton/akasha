Hearing Ash’s words gave Samantha mixed feelings, as it perhaps helped explain why Ana would push herself so hard, too.

As if she hasn’t suffered enough.

Platitudes died without being voiced, though. They neared the end of all roads, including each of their own. There would be pain and sacrifice, however much she might want to spare them.

Instead of denying that reality, she said, “Only if necessary, Ash. I’m not convinced it was so with Ana, for instance. We use the power needed, exhausting all resources collectively. We’re smart in how we do it. Hmm?”

Ash nodded. “Yes, Mother.” The gleam in her eye remained determined. “True excellence is effortless, after all.”

Chuckling, Samantha rose. “Good luck with your spell. I’m off to make another, myself!”

“And luck to you, as well. Don’t forget to talk with Dax.”

✦•············•✦•···········•✦

Samantha indeed did touch base with Dax about strategy. The arguing ensued immediately.

“I know you want to save everyone, all the time, Samantha,” Dax said over the mental vectors, with barely restrained impatience, “but that buff is too much of a game-changer. You don’t want to frontload what could be an epic counter-surge. I want Ash’s spell going off at the right time, too, and the activation of your crown is one-time, five minutes. It needs to be a strategic pivot point.”

Samantha grimaced. “You’re asking for a hell of a lot getting held back. The buff in particular is widespread and has a long duration; we should use as much of it as possible, too. Don’t we have enough layers of big plays by now?”

“Never. We don’t know how many the enemy has. They will have things we won’t expect. If I somehow have something I never used in the back pocket, that’s a win of preparation. Samantha… I promise this will have its moment, and early-ish. Just trust the general you appointed to this position, and at least partially to make the tough calls you won’t. This battle more than any other must be focused on victory above all.”

Despite more argument on the tip of her tongue, Samantha restrained it. She’s right, damn her. “Fine. But the army gets Wreath of Clouds immediately, and the vanguard gets the Contempt For The End buff immediately, too. Probably.”

“I can live with that.”

Samantha disconnected abruptly and turned her attention back to her custom spell. The amorphous ideas shifted within her, going through a hundred tossed-aside ideas in a matter of minutes.

She soon realized one thing above all: she did not need destructive AoE, compared to simply spamming one of dozens of existing spells with slight benefits this way or that for each spell. [Wizards], and by extension a [Magineer] like her, were good at that already. Intensity increases were negligible against hard System limits.

The construct was especially a puzzle this time, because there were many contrasting influences and restraints. The primary idea was to link it to the tower, but waive the hour-long restriction, thereby making it particular to the 5-minute crown activation period. Overall, this provided oomph but had wince-inducing mana requirements not much different than the other ritual spell. As such, she had to pare it down.

On the other hand, during the crown activation period, I’ll be in a nexus zone halving all MP costs, and I can use the tower’s Draw Mana to refill.

These considerations made the pare-down not so bad.

She ultimately decided on another mass summoning spell, especially when she discovered she could bake in the immunity of the [Alter Spell: Pentagram] effect and even save time that way. The autonomy was just too difficult to pass up. Exactly what she wanted to do for these entities remained amorphous. She wanted something a bit different; wanted to fill a hole in their army’s needs…

Inspiration eluded her, the infinity of possibilities practically dazzling her into indecision. Time was always the limiting factor. There was never enough time to do all that could be done.

She was once again interrupted in her ruminations, as Azure sent a message: “She’s awake.”

Samantha rushed back over to the infirmary and the room Ana was in, as she saw Azure holding the hand of the blind elf girl, who still looked overly pale but was sitting up and looking at least marginally better. Right then, she was sipping a glass of water, and her ears twitched as Samantha entered. She turned her head in the direction of the door and seemed to quickly realize who was there.

Ana swallowed the water as her blind eyes nonetheless pointed downward, misery painting her features. “I’m sorry, Mother.”

Floating over to her side to sit and laying a hand on her daughter’s leg over the covers, Samantha said softly, “It’s okay. We just need to not let this happen again.”

Ana nodded. “In that case, what I hated to burden you with must come to pass. Such is our Fate, Mother. You must be the one to summon him, with me as the guide. You must call forth the king who sacrificed royalty and glory, who cast down his crown to be a slave to Fate. I suppose it's fitting, as only you could measure up to such cherished blood and legendary grandeur.”

“I see. Very well.”

Ana chuckled faintly. “Do you really agree so quickly and simply? You’re humoring me, Mother.”

“I’ll do whatever it takes to ensure you don’t kill yourself.”

“I accept that, as I would accept death. I know it would wound you too terribly, however. This is thus the walk we have to make.”

“At least we’re doing it together.”

Ana smiled and nodded. “Always, Mother. Always.” Her words, and subtleties in her expression, showed sadness, however.

“I suppose I’d be foolish to ask if I can help,” Azure muttered.

Ana sipped more water and shook her head. “Not foolish, but I’m afraid not. I’ll be alright, by the way. You can return to your duties at this late hour.”

Azure squeezed Ana’s hand insistently. “I’m not going anywhere.”

To this, Ana made a face of admonishment, though weak and betrayed by her obvious fondness. “Mother…”

Samantha sighed. “I’m the ultra-super busybody of the coupling, after all. Though I was getting nowhere in what I was doing, anyway.”

Ana turned to her curiously. “Oh? Tell me about it, please.”

“Bah! It’s nothing, don’t worry your pretty little head ab-...” She trailed off at Ana turning on her disappointed ‘puppy dog’ expression. “Well, ahem! On second thought, maybe you could help! Heh. Yes. I was just trying to come up with a role for a batch of powerful summoned entities. It’s quite special, as a custom spell that requires the crown and the tower, but bypasses the long casting time. I’m stumped by too many possibilities to consider. There are so many things I could create, you see.”

Azure snorted. “I’d assume you’d go with something that goes ‘boom boom,’ ‘scorchy scorchy,’ or ‘smashy smashy,’ maybe all three. It can’t be that hard to decide on the flavor with which it's done.”

“Yeeeah, that’s the thing… as tempting as that is, it’s too obvious, and we already have plenty of that.”

“Perhaps you should ask a different question, rather than seeking only solutions to the one,” Ana said.

Samantha’s brows furrowed. “What other question is there? I’m seeking the best option for our forces, simple as that.”

“Ask what represents you and the tower, what is an icon of these conceptually. Perhaps even your role in the battle? It may lead to a more potent conclusion.”

As Samantha pondered that, Azure suddenly snapped her fingers and pointed. “Countermagic specialists! If they were strong enough, they’d not only free up a rare core to cover more ground, they’d potentially even protect themselves from being dispelled. Maybe they’d even free you up!”

Samantha’s eyes widened. “Dispel entities? That’s fucking brilliant!”

Azure had a smug grin. “You’re welcome.” At Ana’s cleared throat and pointed look, she sheepishly added, “Good thing Ana had that advice, too. What a gal, right? She’s indispensable.”

Ana soon dropped the expression suddenly and then smiled in a way that made it clear she wasn’t offended and had been teasing. “I’m sure our inspired spellweaver needs to go act on her new ideas.” She handed Azure the glass of water. “I still feel fatigued and should rest, but I wanted to wake up to settle everyone’s nerves a bit and provide guidance as needed. Please do not linger, either of you. I’d feel less guilty if you conducted your duties and didn’t fuss over an unconscious body. It’s more than fine. There are plenty who can be assigned as guards to watch over me, and the healers are close and ready.”

Azure frowned. “Wait, you chose to wake up?!”

Despite being blind, Ana blinked. “Oops.”
