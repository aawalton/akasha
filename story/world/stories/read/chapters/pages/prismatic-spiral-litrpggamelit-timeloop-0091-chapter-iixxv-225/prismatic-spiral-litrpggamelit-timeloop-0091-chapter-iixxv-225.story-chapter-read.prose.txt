Chapter II.XXV (2.25)

Mitsuko had a list of objectives for her time on Amber Island, and the goal that umbrellaed them all was to complete those objectives while causing as few ripples in the loop as possible. That, naturally, led her to the library, where she planned to hide away in a dusty corner and read books. The plan was to have nobody bother her and for her to bother no one in return. She just had to get there first.

With a yellow hooded cloak concealing her identity, she spoke no words and crossed as few paths as possible as she traversed the island’s streets. Thankfully, there were a plethora of other distracting characters on the island to absorb the attention of pedestrians. As far as she could tell, she went entirely unnoticed.

The average age on the island was likely in the sixties and she saw far more people who looked to be in their nineties than in their thirties. The only people she spotted who were roughly around her same age were the dock workers and sailors. That unique attribute alone could cause her some grief in future loops if she tried investigating people.

However, if Mend Aging existed and Deteriorate was symmetrical with its abilities… well, that could potentially open up a few options for her. Though the idea of hobbling around as a crone for several loops held very little appeal to her.

A thought for another time. Mitsuko found the mound that Zeaqu had pointed out to her from atop his tower and ducked inside.

Down the steps into the main entrance, Mitsuko found the ceiling within was low and it felt more like a wooden tunnel than a proper building. An old man reading a tome at a desk looked up at her. Rather than studying her face, he scanned her cloak.

“Hm. Only three striped? Barely permissible for the main studies. Enter.”

Mitsuko blinked. Then she glanced at her cloak’s sleeves. When she’d taken it from Zeaqu’s guestroom closet, she’d noted that it had three rainbow stripes on its forearm sleeves, but thought nothing of it. Now that she considered it though, most of the wizards in the streets had likewise worn similar bands of colored fabric. It must denote some sort of ranking system to the island’s citizens.

She said nothing and lowered her hooded head as she passed the elderly man, who she noted had six bands on his arms. It raised a question though of why someone with such a high ranking was placed on library watch duty. Still, she reminded herself this wasn’t the time for an investigation.

Each of the library’s rooms was painted a different color to denote what island’s history they focused on. The largest of the rooms was obviously Amber’s. It had yellow shelves of books that stretched beyond the walls and onto the ceiling. Mitsuko had no idea what sort of elaborate gravity altering enchantment had to have been arranged to create that effect. It went far beyond her rudimentary understanding of enchanting wards.

“I should try experimenting with temporal altering enchantments,” Mitsuko mused as she watched an upside down wizard pull a book from the ceiling. “I’ve heard it said that enchanting is more a technique of spellcrafting than its own branch. That might mean I can create something with it.”

Of course, she had no education in the subject. So that added a layer of difficulty. But it wasn’t an idea to dismiss outright. One of the sages might have something to say about the topic. Unfortunately, Juniper was out scouting currently and Mitsuko doubted that her current sage would have much to offer on the topic regardless.

While Mitsuko was curious about Amber Island’s secrets, her priority at the moment was Porcelain Island. She exited the large, garishly yellow room and pushed open a wooden door painted a stark, glowing white.

Akin to the Amber library chamber’s yellow color scheme, Porcelain’s was entirely white in its aesthetic. The shelves, couches, walls, and floor were all a blinding shade of white. However, unlike the previous room, there were no wizards currently in here. No one browsing the shelves or on the couch thumbing through tomes. Just silence. Which was exactly what she wanted.

Mitsuko let out a deep breath of air, then squared her shoulders. She predicted she’d be spending a lot of this loop sitting at the table she spotted tucked away in the library’s corner.

“Where to begin?” There were less shelves in here than the Amber Island room, but still well over a thousand books and scrolls to peruse. After an hour or so of searching and glancing through a dozen texts, she found a book titled The Origins of Ice. She almost discounted the tome after skimming the first dozen pages, thinking it was a very basic instruction on how to form elemental ice magic. But then, on a whim she skipped ahead to the book’s center and frowned at the image sketched onto the page. It was a complete diagram of the anatomy of a sculpt.

She had plenty of other options. There was a book she’d found completely dedicated to the modern flora and fauna of the island which held some promise as well as another that detailed a war between the sculpts and the goblins of Verdant Island. Those could both definitely be treasure troves when trying to gain a better understanding of the island’s history and foundational make up. But something about the word ‘Origins’ snagged her attention.

Sitting down, Mitsuko got herself comfortable to begin to read in earnest. The tome’s paper had been magically preserved with enchantments and there was no date written inside the front, so Mitsuko had no idea how old the contents were. One of the first things she tried was casting Retrospection on the book to see if anything interesting would happen. But, besides a short wizard once pulling it off the shelf for a few minutes a century ago, she hit her maximum reach with the spell before anything notable occurred.

She turned the pages and read. Hours passed. She had no idea how long exactly since whenever she felt physical weariness, she’d Mend her body back into peak wakefulness. As she’d seen when first skimming the start of the book, a large chunk of the first portion was dedicated to magical theory involving ice. As someone who lacked any affinity with elemental branches, it was difficult for her to relate to. But the next section switched topics, instead focusing on golems and theories of how to integrate artificial body parts onto a human. It was a seemingly random shift in topic that confused Mitsuko.

Tap, tap, tap.

The noise broke her concentration.

Mitsuko looked up from her book at the white door. The tapping continued. Sighing, she pushed her chair back and approached, ready to flick her wrist and attack if this was a trap set up by the island’s guardian.

The door swung open to reveal… a gull with glowing brown eyes.

“Roosting underground of all places,” Juniper commented, hopping into the room and onto the table. She plopped herself down there among Mitsuko’s selected tomes. “I hope whatever information you’ve gathered makes it worth visiting this place. The doorman attempted to shoo me out four separate occasions. He singed my feathers with his last lightning bolt attack.”

“You’re the one who wanted to scout out the island,” Mitsuko said. “Did you find anything notable? Was it worth your time?”

“Is that—”

“Yes. I’ll use a question for that. I think information gathering while I’m still anonymous warrants the use of a question.”

Juniper nodded her head in approval. “Very nice choice! After this, it will leave you with four available questions.”

Mitsuko waved dismissively. “Fine. Get on with it. Also, you’d better not have been detected by the island’s guardian.”

“I was not, poppet,” she said sweetly. “Now, a bit of political upheaval seems to have seized this peaceful island of wizards. There’s a great debate of elders trying to decide who will rise to the position as their governor with the passing of their last leader.”

“Any sign of the guardian? Who are the major players in the succession?” Mitsuko asked. She leaned forward in her chair.

“Kouis Fribbolt is a leading player. He’s a wizard who appears hyper fixated on painting and art.”

“An illusionist?” Mitsuko asked. Usually art and illusions went hand in hand with mages. But she was quickly realizing that wizards tended to be a bit… different.

“Not as far as I can tell. Truthfully, I could not ascertain his specialty.”

Mitsuko wondered if she could go back to Zeaqu and ask him a few more questions about his peers. But crossing through the island again would be risky. “Where does Kouis Fribbolt live? I assume it’s one of the towers?”

“Yes, poppet.” Juniper nodded her head, her little beak bobbing. “His tower stands out, different from the rest with multicolored paints, rather than the usual standard yellow brick. He is often referred to as the Rainbow Wizard.”

“Interesting. I noticed that a lot of wizards wear some sort of ranking system on their arms. Colors seem to represent rank. So I suppose a rainbow would probably be considered the highest possible rank.” Mitsuko ran a hand through her hair as she considered everything. Her hand caught on a tangle and she instinctively used Mend to straighten it. “The Rainbow Wizard is in the lead, but what about the other front runners?”

“A wizard named Deelina Zuinn is likely the next most prominent figure, as far as I could tell. She’s spoken about less by the populous but has a lot more fliers in the street. Keep in mind that this scouting mission you asked of me only lasted half a day so I only have a snippet of the public sentiment.”

Mitsuko blinked. “It’s already been half a day?” Time was truly slipping through her fingers. She glanced at the open tome on the table. Books were more time consuming than she would have liked but at least she they offered a visual representation of her progress. A solid third of the way through the tome.

“And the guardian?” Mitsuko asked. “I doubt it’s one of those two, but do either have a sudden new advisor or anything?”

“I thought it prudent to avoid directly presenting myself in front of a potential acolyte,” Juniper replied “Though I believe your mindset is correct. Rather than the crisp leaves that flutter in the wind, look to the bitter roots for the source of nutrition and life.”

Mitsuko made a noncommittal noise then returned to her reading. She was staring at the words, she tried to figure out what prosthetic limbs had to do with The Origins of Ice.

She turned a page.

Then it all collided together.

The image of a wizard stared back at her, his body half flesh and half ice. A melded halfway point between human and sculpt. Under the sketch, the description read, “Wizard Eustace Hrim, the Father of Scults.”
