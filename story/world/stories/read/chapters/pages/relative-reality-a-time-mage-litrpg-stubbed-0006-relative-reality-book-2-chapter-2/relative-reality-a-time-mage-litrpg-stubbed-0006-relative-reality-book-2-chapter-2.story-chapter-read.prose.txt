Rowan lounged as he finished off his most recent drawing. It was a depiction of the old Dawn family manor, as he remembered it at least. He’d even included a basic version of himself for scale.

As Dawn himself had said would happen, neither he nor Helja had seen her parents since the day of their departure. With all the training they had been through since then, those days felt like such a long time ago. Despite that feeling, Helja hadn’t been able to rebuild or reclaim her lands since. The anthill emergence that had destroyed the manor had been cleared, but the mound of dirt itself remained.

Helja had simply not had the money nor the time to work on fixing up the grounds to rebuild, and Rowan wondered if she would do so even if she could. She was so focused on training these days that Rowan had begun to grow worried.

Sighing, Rowan flipped the page on his book. Without anything else to draw, he began working on his own version of the Dawn manor. A new style that was a mixture of architecture from both worlds, something sturdy that benefitted from the magically reinforced materials that Lyel used, yet modern and clean. Modern by his own standards at least.

The end of term tournament was near and Rowan was growing more and more excited. Along with all of the other rewards that came with winning the tournament such as prestige and unique opportunities after graduation, there were two things that Rowan was uniquely looking forward to, should they win. The first of those was the offer of a Stone of Power from amongst an exhaustive list of stones owned by the city's various nobility and higher tier individuals, one each for the members of the winning team. More important than that however, was the opportunity to punch Felir in the face.

A heated rivalry had kicked up between him and Helja during the adventure emergence near the start of the semester, and the misfortune of Helja’s house along with her newfound dedication to training had only caused Felir to try to punch down that much more. Both teams had needed to break up fights between the two on multiple occasions, one particular clash going so far that the instructors had gotten involved. Trying to follow Emir’s very first bit of advice in regards to Felir, Rowan had tried to stay out of things. Both Helja and Felir were making that a little hard to accomplish though.

And so as the tournament approached, Rowan found himself hoping more and more that their two teams would end up fighting over the course of it, if only for the opportunity to get at least one good punch in. Holding himself back when Helja was constantly fighting the guy was not fun.

Today was their team’s one rest day for the fortnight, even Helja admitting that they did need to rest once in a while, and Rowan found himself without any idea what to do. Both Arne and Melody were visiting their respective parents, while Elise was off doing Elise things. Helja was naturally trying to live her life in the most regimented and efficient manner and was spending the entire day sleeping so she could cut down on hours of sleep later in the week. As he wasn’t quite crazy enough to spend his one day off at work, that left Rowan without anyone to spend time with and nothing to do but lay around for a day.

His attempts at redesigning the Dawn family manor in a new style spent another half an hour of his day, but he soon grew restless. Hopping to his feet, Rowan put away his flipbook and left the academy grounds. While not wanting to train, meditation was always a safe play as it was restorative, rather than exhaustive.

Ever since Instructor Sky had taken Rowan out of the city and flown him up to the top of one of the pillars where a little pond lay, Rowan hadn’t quite found another place that was as good for meditation as that. Luckily, he hadn’t needed to.

Leaving Lyel entirely, the pillar Rowan was looking for was only a short distance more. Arriving at its base, he stretched gently before beginning the climb.

Had it been some six months ago, Rowan would have struggled to get even a few metres off the ground. He had put in a lot of work since then however, not just physically but mentally. Where he might have once been too scared to climb very high at all, he now found himself at peace as the ground shrunk below him.

Still not a particularly skilled climber, did find himself out of breath and tired by the time he reached the top. Heavy winds blew, whipping his hair around his face as he sat and prepared for meditation.

Light and sound faded as Rowan sunk within himself to the starry void he called a soul. Fracture was there, cheerfully greeting Rowan as his consciousness arrived. He still found that strange, the way that he didn’t consciously exist within his own soul unless focusing on doing so actively.

Time slipped away as Rowan meditated. He vaguely noticed Instructor Sky landing beside him but continued to meditate, knowing that she would prefer that he keep his concentration. Much of his focus today was on exploring the seemingly endless depths of Fracture. On the surface, Rowan’s familiar was a pane of shattered glass but as he delved closer to try to understand the strange being, that fracturing surrounded him and became everything. A two dimensional pane became a maze of broken reality that was impossible to navigate.

Still, Instructor Sky had said that inspecting the souls of others was one of the most important parts of advancing to tier three, and so Rowan did his best.

He vaguely understood the nature of Fracture’s existence as described by the familiar itself, something about existing outside of reality and looking in through a window. He even experienced that for himself when sharing Fracture’s perception. That was the limit of Rowan’s understanding however, and today would not be the day that he understood it any further.

Opening his eyes, Rowan leaned back and looked to the sky. It was darker, with the sun setting over the horizon.

“You’ve come a long way,” Lylc said.

She was hovering in the air as she tended to do, legs crossed and eyes closed. Instructor Sky was an avian and the soul facet instructor for the academy that Rowan attended.

“Doesn’t feel like it, when I’m constantly chasing the others in an attempt to catch up.”

“They have had many more years of hard work than you. You may never catch up to Helja seeing as she has her own familiar of all three facets. It is as good a motivation as any, but I trust it isn’t your only one.”

“Of course not.”

“Good. Observing others is important to your advancement, but comparing yourself to them is unhealthy for your mind. I would be careful doing that. You are your own person, and your advancement is singular to yourself. Besides, you have done impressively well considering your background.”

“And if we weren’t considering my background?”

Lylc shrugged.

“You are on track, which is much more than could be said for when you first arrived here.”

Rowan laughed, remembering his first days. It was a surprise that his origins were a secret to anyone considering how clueless he had been about everything.

“At least there’s that. Do you think we have a chance of winning the tournament?”

Lylc opened her eyes.

“Easily. A lot of the teams are barely trained nobility after all. Most of them will leave immediately after the tournament and not bother to do their second half year of training. Dawn has caused quite a lot of trouble for a lot of those families and now is not the time for them to send their scions out into the world, even for such a short time. I’d bet that some of the other teams will give you a bit of trouble though.”

Rowan nodded. It was the same thing that Helja had been saying for months. The one team that she didn’t seem particularly enthused to fight was the same one that had made it the furthest in the adventure emergence they had explored. They were incredibly defensive, with a level of scaling power that rivalled Melody’s songs. It was this combination that had allowed them to climb much further than the rest of them.

The best way to defeat them would be a blitz tactic, to try to break their defense before they had the chance to build in power. That was unfortunately exactly the opposite of Rowan’s team’s usual tactic. It was Melody’s songs that they usually relied on to make them stronger, as they granted slowly growing benefits to the entire team. They had yet to think of something that might work against that particular team.

They would figure out something.

“Mind giving me a lift back to the city?”

“Sure. Anywhere in particular?”

“The campus please.”

Lylc flicked her hand and Rowan was flung off into the sky, thrown across the landscape and over the city. He came to a floating rest above the campus, then slowly floated down to a gentle stop on the flagstones of the main campus path. He received one or two odd looks from students leaving classes to go home, but it wasn’t the most unusual sight most had ever seen.

When Rowan arrived back at the student accommodation, he found Helja going through a warmup routine of some kind of martial art.

“Helja?”

“Mm?” Helja hummed, continuing her fluid movements.

“Aren’t you supposed to be resting today?”

“I am.”

“Not what it looks like to me. You said you would be sleeping most of the day.”

“This is a routine used to calm the mind and relax the body. It counts as resting. Besides, I’ve slept enough.”

Rowan rolled his eyes, but sat down nearby to wait for her to finish. She eventually came to a stop, held her pose for a second and then came over to sit next to him.

“So, what’s on the schedule for tomorrow?”

Helja pulled out her little notebook and flipped through it, sighing in frustration.

“I wanted to get you to tier three before the tournament, but it looks like that won’t happen. Perhaps we can try to push your Body facet over the edge? Other than that, we still need to work on getting you to switch roles more efficiently. Arne does a good job protecting Melody, but he can’t do that on his own against a concerted attack.”

“What about that team,” Rowan said.

“That team?”

“You know, the one that we are going to struggle against. Have you thought of a way to beat them?”

Helja flicked over a couple pages.

“No, no I have not. I still think we can beat them at their own game, especially with Elise. Why? Did you have a new idea?”

Rowan shook his head.

“No, I was just talking about it with Lylc. There must be something we can do.”

“Maybe. It might just be a bad matchup for us and we should hope someone else eliminates them.”

“I somehow doubt that will happen.”

“Me too.”

“Well, I guess I’ll see you tomorrow morning. I’m going to catch an early night.”

Helja bid him a goodnight, and Rowan went to bed.

The next morning came with a knock on his door as Helja called for their team to awaken. Another early morning for a grueling day of training, led by their taskmaster of a teammate.

Rowan groaned, but still got up. Helja might be the most motivated of them all, led by the grief of the loss of her home, and the departure of her parents, but Rowan had just as much to prove as she did. They would win this tournament, and they would find William and Esmerelda again. They had to.
