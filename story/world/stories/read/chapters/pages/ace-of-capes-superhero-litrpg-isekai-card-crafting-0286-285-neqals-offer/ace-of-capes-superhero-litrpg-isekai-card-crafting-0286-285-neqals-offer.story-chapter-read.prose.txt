Lexie stiffened. She stared hard at Neqal, who had that mad grin once more.

“So,” he drew the word out. “Are you not going to ask?”

“If I ask, will you tell me?"

“For a price.”

Of course. It wasn't like she thought he would tell her for free. He wasn’t Naem.

But she had to be careful with Neqal because he could outright lie to her. Naem managed to deceive her without lies, but Neqal could do even worse.

“What’s the price?” she asked.

“You become my disciple.”

She snorted. “Forget it.”

“You haven’t heard the details.”

“I don’t need to. That’s the most outrageous offer you could have made. I can’t even list all the reasons that’s a bad idea.”

He gave a dramatic frown. "That's offensive.”

“And I’m offended you would even ask me something like that.”

He drifted around her, bringing his chaotic madness along with him. “A discipleship isn’t forever. I’m sure Naem taught you that it can be broken. And the mere fact that you were able to steal Pvilycht from me proves that you have better bonding abilities than I do, at least when it comes to other Eldritch. Thanks in part to your soul card.”

"And all that means what?”

“That means that once the arrangement no longer serves you, you should be able to detach with no problem. We can even sign a soul contract to that effect.”

“No.”

“Being my disciple will temporarily shield you from the demands of Lordship, and you will no longer have to take the test. You won't have to worry about any other Lords coming after you, or the Fae binding you. The terms are quite favorable to you.”

“That’s precisely the thing that makes me so suspicious. The last time we met, you tried to kill me, and now you’re trying to offer me the deal of a lifetime?”

“The last time we met, I was in a very volatile phase,” he said with a sniff. “I’ve changed.”

Lexie narrowed her eyes at him. He did look and feel different, a lot more stable than the last time, that was for sure.

She didn’t doubt that her card had the power to alter the psyche of Eldritch as it calmed their chaos, but a little card like that wouldn’t keep Neqal calm, at least not for long. Even now, when he was acting far too non-antagonistic for her liking, his madness lurked in the corner of his gaze.

“What do you get out of this arrangement?” Lexie asked.

“The chance to attain some of your human essence. When Naem was bonded with you, he became different. I didn’t necessarily see it at the time, but now I know he made choices that he would not have made if he were entirely himself. At first, I thought it was simply his obsession with humanity making him strange. I realize now it was magical feedback from you influencing his soul.”

Lexie’s mind worked fast. “So I’m guessing you want some of this feedback from my human essence, and you'll use that to create your very own soul cards. Is that right?”

“Correct."

“Not happening.”

“You realize I could simply take you right now, as you're still recovering from your fight, and forcefully experiment on you."

“No, you can’t."

"You're weaker than you used to be."

"Regardless, you can't, or you would have done it already.” She glanced at him. “The only reason you’re here negotiating with me is that you know that no matter how much you experiment on me, you can not get to my soul's essence if I don’t give it to you. Right?” It was a comforting realization to make. Neqal's gaze flashed with annoyance, and that relieved Lexie even more.

"The answer is no, Neqal."

"You ungrateful louse." His dakrness loomed toward her, the air shimmering in warning. "After everything I've done for you. I'm the one keeping the other Lords from devouring you."

“That’s because we had a deal, right? I’ll just give you another soul card if that’s what you want. That was our deal."

He hummed noncommittally, withdrawing his menace with a startling quickness and turning in that strange spiral pattern, like he was pacing in circles really fast, while his head remained in place.

"Think about it more deeply. I’m sure your mother would like to hear from you again.”

Lexie caught her breath. “So she’s alive?”

Neqal grinned, happy to have drawn the reaction. “That’s the thing, isn’t it? Without me, you’ll never know,” he said. “I will give you this clue, since I’m sure you must be thinking it. Your mother is not like Pvilycht.”

Pvilycht flinched at the sound of his own name, still on alert as though Neqal might change his mind and smite him right there.

“Did Naem know that you had her?”

“No,” Neqal responded. “It was one of the memories I managed to store away until I needed it.”

“How do you know when you need it?”

“Become my disciple and find out.”

With one final spin, he dissolved into black sludge and was absorbed out of the dungeon. Only then did Pvilycht start breathing again.

Lexie gave him a droll look.”You really need to work on the cowering. It's unbecoming of you.”

He cleared his throat and straightened. “Yes, I will work on it at my next session with Dr. Bossetti.”

"Right." Lexie assumed he was talking about the book, or to the Dr. Bossetti he'd conjured in his mind.  Not the real one.

As Lexie inhaled, she thought about Neqal’s deal again. There was no doubt in her mind that he’d concealed most of his presence in the ghoul's memory, but could he also have corrupted all the subsequent memories about her mother?

Was that why he was so confident that she wouldn't find out what happened without him?

She could check right now, but to be honest, she wanted to save her energy. There was already so much going on, and regardless of whether or not the memories had gotten corrupted, this was not the time to give a mad scientist Eldritch Lord access to her soul.

Then again, Neqal was right. She could probably break the bond if she wanted to. Or even reverse it as she'd done with Ryn. Maybe.

Maybe they could sign a contract for a limited discipleship. Like an apprenticeship.

But that was just one more thing on her plate, one more deal with an unsavory individual that might blow back in her face. She didn't need that right now.

What she needed was strength.

To beat the Lightlarks and to face whatever Neqal sent her way as a result of turning down his deal.

“Maybe that’s not his true goal," Pvilycht murmured.

“What?"

"I mean for Lord Neqal. Maybe his goal is not to have you become his disciple, but instead to become your disciple.”

Lexie paused. “That's... he would never do that. Why would he?”

“Because there are certain benefits to becoming your disciple, and that's the easiest way to earn your essence if he does indeed want to study it. By becoming your disciple, your soul will transform his more directly, and he'll discover what it means to be a humano-Eldritch. With that, he can then attempt to follow whatever steps you took to create the soul card. That sounds far more plausible than what he was saying."

“Okay, but even if he becomes my disciple, there's no guarantee that he gets any of my powers. I can cut off access, can’t I?”

“You can, but it's not easy, especially for a creature like Neqal. He’s very powerful and very good at getting around limitations, even those that the Fae place on him.”

“I thought becoming my disciple would suppress some of his abilities or make them go away forever.”

“Becoming your disciple will either suppress some of his abilities, or it will give you some of his, so it's a sacrifice he must be willing to make.”

She pursed her lips. “Then why didn't he just ask me to be my disciple? I might have been more likely to take that deal than what he offered.” Although now that she thought about it, maybe not. It was still too dangerous.

"He, as a Lord, can never admit anything like that, that he wants to be subservient to another Lord. Or rather, a young Eldritch who's not even a Lord yet, in fact."

"Hey," Lexie protested.

"No offense, but it's the truth. It's completely mortifying and unheard of for a Lord to do something like that, even for Neqal, who is more unconventional than most. Neqal is also very prideful. However, if you were to become his disciple and then seize control of the bond, reversing it, he would then be trapped as your disciple. He would get what he wanted without ever having to admit that was his goal in the first place."

Lexie gaped. That was so...strangely convoluted. So Neqal had just been playing coy the whole time? Did he expect her to try to negotiate? Was that what his show of frustration was?

Ugh, this was so confusing. It was already enough of a pain dealing with subtlety and subtext in human conversations; she didn't want to do that with Eldritch, too.

One of the best parts of being Eldritch was how straightforward the communication tended to be.

She'd been expecting that here, which was why she'd missed out on the purpose of that whole conversation.

"It is additionally suspicious that he didn’t press the matter further or try to threaten you," Pvilycht pointed out.

"I think he did threaten me."

"No. If Lord Neqal truly threatens you, you wouldn’t think it. You would know it."

"Maybe he couldn't threaten me well enough because the dungeon wouldn't let him."

Pvilycht shrugged. "As I said, he’s good at getting around limitations.” Lexie wondered how. Despite turning down his offer, there were a lot of things she wanted to know from and about Neqal. Like how he made those orbs he'd given Duru, and his link to the Emperor, and exactly what he'd planned with the Alchemist when it came to taking down the Fae.

If she made him her disciple, she could get the answers she wanted.

But it was still sounding a little too good to be true. She would think about it more when she had time.

Right now, she had too much going on.

"Did you get a list of the Lightlark's powers?"

"Yes. The ones he displayed in the fight were light bending, light entrapment, flashes of enlightenment, speed, light healing, banding–"

"Jeez," Lexie breathed. "He used all that on little old me."

"There’s more."

Pvilycht continued rattling off a list, and it told her two things: that Leo Lightlark was powerful and that she had caused him to push himself to the limit.

"Perhaps the key is to trap him before he moves," Lexie murmured. "We can get him with pathway control."

"Yes, but I will warn you, his pathways are protected with light against any Eldritch magic. It was so difficult for me to even take hold of his arm. I don't know if total pathway control will be feasible in this instance."

"I see." Lexie twisted her lips, thinking. What card did she have that countered light?

Kill the Light.

Well, that didn’t exist as a card on its own. It only existed as part of her soul card, which she could use to siphon or suppress his light.

But she'd only done that with Eldritch and Fae.

How well would it work on someone made of Light?

Pvilcyht didn’t think it would work all that well.

"You won’t be able to utilize his light if you attempt to wield it. It’s completely antithetical to your Eldritch framework. It'll be like drinking poison."

"What about suppression?"

"That might work depending on your strength. But keep in mind, if you're using a soul card against him, and he reverses the attack, he might be able to hurt your soul. That damage will be a lot harder to fix."

"Can he do that? Reverse the soul attack?"

He shrugged.

This was the problem. She hadn't used the soul card on a human, never mind on a human with so much light. Eldritch craved light because they didn't have enough of it, and so their souls made a facsimile of it for their comfort.

It was nothing compared to what the Lightlarks naturally had. She doubted she would be able to ensnare them much with her light, and it was probably better not to risk her soul right now.

Most humans didn’t have strong enough soul control to attack with it, but for all she knew, Lightlarks might have learned a lot of soul magic on planet Fae.

Creature cards wouldn’t work. Neither would her attack card.

"The best bet is to boost your physical capabilities while trying to repel his light," Pvilycht said. "And to boost your mana back to dungeon levels."

She nodded.

Earth's system wouldn't allow her to use her full capacity of mana, but she could craft extreme speed-boosting and strength-boosting cards from one of the dungeons. Maybe a mana-boosting card too, in addition to beefing up her constitution.

That way, she would have her old strength without having her old strength.

Alternatively, she would also bring Leo into her world and trap him here, but she wasn't sure how much Xena would like that. That would be plan B.

Plan C was to figure out a way to boost her human magic.

Eldritch magic fed off chacos. What did human magic feed off ot?

Hmm. She had some time to figure it out. After all, a few days in her dungeon were only like a few hours on Earth. She would try a lot of things. She would keep brainstorming, too.

All she knew was that by the time she left, she would beat Leo and any other Lightlark that came her way.

Now to the other order of business...

"By the way, Pvilycht," she asked. "How do you feel about spying?"
