Footsteps echoed as we ran down the stairs. Through that tiny office, we reached the main building.

The fire alarm was ringing. Water sprinklers sprayed from the ceiling. Files lay scattered across the floor, broken mugs spilled coffee, and the people who had been working here just a few minutes ago had haphazardly fled when the Rift opened.

“Move,” Toby shouted. “This way, down the stairs.”

“How many floors is it?” a middle-aged journalist asked.

“Seven.”

“Ah, shit. My knees.”

“Well, choose. Aching knees or becoming a monster’s meal,” Toby said.

The first person had barely taken a step onto the stairs when I sensed something climbing up at great speed.

I pulled him back by the collar.

A purple whip smashed into the exact spot where he had been standing, breaking half the staircase.

I dared to look down.

Halis Toad

Residing in the Great Forest of Baalti, this three-meter-tall toad is one of the apex predators. Its tongue moves at great speed, stabbing its prey and killing them in a single blow. Recorded in the Akashic System in the year 894.

The toad stuck to the wall. It covered an entire floor in a single leap.

The pouch beneath its throat swelled as it began preparing another attack.

“Move!”

Tina jumped down, directly at the toad. It opened its mouth, and its tongue shot forward. Tina’s whole hand glowed red.

That’s why her left sleeve is missing.

She held her glowing hand out. The tongue came at her like a whip, touched her hand, and split apart.

The toad cried out.

Tina kept falling.

Her glowing hand tore through the toad’s tongue, through its head, and straight through its back before she landed seven floors below on her feet.

The toad’s body split open.

Its innards spilled out from the massive wound, raining down around Tina. Her silver hair turned pink as blood and gore splattered over her.

The dead toad crashed onto the floor beside her with a deafening BAM.

Tina casually wiped some of the blood from her face.

“Come down!” she shouted. “I think I can take these toads on my own.”

Another whip-like tongue came at her. She raised her hand like a blade and cut it apart. The floor cracked beneath her feet as she leapt, and she disappeared.

“Why are we going down?” someone from the crowd screamed. “There are monsters there!”

“How else are we supposed to escape?” Toby frowned.

Until now, these guys had been afraid of the unknown, so they were listening. But now that they had seen a monster for themselves, the real fear had kicked in.

“If you didn’t lie to us and this really is the Phoenix Guild building,” another man from the crowd said, “there should be even more Silver and Gold-rank Walkers on the top floor.”

“This is a Gold-rank Rift break. There are flying monsters on the top floor,” Toby tried his best to keep his voice calm and even. “And I told you before. Phoenix Guild members will kill you the moment they see you.”

“Why?” a woman cried.

“Because you know their secret. The Rifts can be closed. You guys saw that with your own eyes, and they can’t let you tell the world.”

“But why?” the same woman cried again.

“You know the answer very well. Now move. We don’t have time,” Toby answered.

“Curse the guy who sent us the email,”

Someone in the crowd said. I didn’t notice who. But it made my heart ache.

These guys being here was my fault. If I hadn’t sent those emails, they would have never been kidnapped, never gone through that imprisonment, and never would have been here.

Toby tapped my shoulder, and I looked at him.

He said in a whisper, “You couldn’t have known.”

The people carefully trudged through the remaining part of the stairs, then ran the rest.

“Keep moving, but don’t push each other,” Toby guided everyone like a leader.

We had reached the second floor when I sensed someone rushing towards us.

It broke through a wall. Then another, and another.

“Something’s coming!” I shouted. “Run fast. I’ll check it.”

“Clark, wait—”

I vanished before Toby could finish his words.

I emerged on the path the monster had smashed its way through.

I looked straight ahead and saw just how fucked we were.

Galis Bull

The predator of predators living in the Great Forest of Baalti. It repeatedly smashes its horns into rocks to sharpen them. It can keep accelerating and reach speeds of up to 200 kmph. Its hide is so strong that nothing in the forest can pierce it. It can smell its prey within a hundred-meter radius, and it always goes for the strongest. Recorded in the Akashic System in the year 901.

To list how fucked I was: the bull was big enough that it was touching the ceiling. It could reach a speed of 200 kmph, and nothing in the forest could pierce its hide. I could see its black hide, which looked like obsidian. And it could smell its prey from a hundred meters away.

This beast was out of my league.

It must have smelled me, but it hadn’t stopped its charge. It kept moving straight. I was guessing that it could smell that Toby was stronger than me.

I don’t know why, but that pissed me off.

I channeled my Magika and focused on Shadowstride, emerging on top of its head.

I hung onto its horn and kicked it right in its left eye.

It stopped and cried out loud.

The building shook from the sheer volume of it.

I used Shadowstride to put some distance between us, moving away from the stairs where I could still sense people making their way down.

The bull stopped roaring and turned around.

Its hooves clanked loudly against the floor.

It stared at me.

Its left eye was bleeding, while its right eye watered.

If the bull could talk, it would say one thing.

I will eat you.

It charged at me.
