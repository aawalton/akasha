Interlude III - Retaliation

Part I - Cybelle

Cybelle hummed a happy tune while she removed another sheet of paper from the facsimilater and folded it. Her mother had taught her origami as a girl and the repetitious movements helped keep her mood up, despite all the chaos in the world. That same mother was currently in a Tross camp and her three older brothers were all enlisting in the revolutionary efforts. Their family had fallen far from their station these last few months. Her father, once baron of a large swathe of coastal land in Edgeland, was now little more than a political activist and advisory to a local mayor in Tross.

She folded the paper diagonally.

All those problems faded and Cybelle continued to hum. She was happy Emilia had remembered this skill of hers. Emilia was excellent at recalling their passions and always knew exactly how to improve their moods. Her friend was a social genius, manipulating scenarios to benefit those close to her while simultaneously punishing those who opposed her.

Finishing up the origami doll, Cybelle set it aside into a pile of those now ready to be enchanted. She examined the face of the lifeless paper doll. The girl who had earned Emilia’s ire was barely worth noting outside her prestige as the Student Council President’s sister. She was lazy, mean, and snotty. Cybelle had met her in the third year common room over the years and even been a part of a signed petition to make her stop sleeping on their couch during their first year at the academy. Honestly, Cybelle couldn’t fathom why a boy had left Emilia, of all people, for this drooling idiot of a girl. It didn’t make any logical sense.

“Boys,” she said with a sigh. She shook her head and got to work on the next origami animal. This one was a kappa. A bald turtle man. Cybelle liked this one because it genuinely felt like the best fit for the girl’s taste.

Kaga Kizu’s interruption of their initial batch had been horrifying. He’d burst into flames and been dragged out of the dorm by a blob of burning flesh. After that, Cybelle had been certain Emilia would pull back on her plan. Instead though, her friend had doubled down. She’d leaped beyond a dozen of their planned steps and spread the golems not only throughout all the dorms, but the town as well. And to a resounding success. People loved their golems. They’d blown up in popularity. The girl was doubtlessly now the biggest laughingstock of the entire island.

Kaga’s appearance had changed one thing though. From then on, everything was produced inside Cybelle’s room, never the common room like earlier. Since the facsimilater couldn’t be brought out of the academy and down to Emilia’s villa, Cybelle’s room was deemed the safest location. And now, her entire bedroom had been fitted for the production of these golems. There was a stack of papers next to the facsimilater. Each had a different monster design with one of the thirty or so cards plastered on it. The artifact only recreated images for about two weeks before the paper dissolved into goo, but that was more than enough for the golems to spread around the campus.

Carefully slitting the kappa’s eyes, Cybelle remained focused even when the door behind her opened.

“One minute, Emilia.” Cybelle carefully tore the bit of paper, giving the face depth. She smiled at her work. She knew it would likely be stomped on and not appreciated, but she still took pride in it.

“You’re quite talented.”

Cybelle’s heart skipped a beat. That hadn’t been Emilia’s voice. Slowly, she turned her head and felt the color drain from her face.

It wasn’t the sleepy voice of the Kajima girl they were targeting or the masculine tone of Kaga. No… this was far worse. In her dormitory doorway, stood the Princess of Hon. Kotei Aoi.

Arms crossed, the princess tapped a perfectly manicured finger to her lips. Her attention went past Cybelle herself and fell instead on the paper kappa in her hands.

“Your… your majesty.” Cybelle’s throat was dry. She’d heard rumors that the fourth-year princess was friends with the Kajima girl, but had dismissed them. It was ridiculous to assume someone in a noble house as prestigious as the Kotei’s would stoop so low as to associate themselves with a character like Kajima Ione. Now though… this sudden appearance sent ripples of doubt through Cybelle.

Rather than acknowledge her, the princess stepped forward and snatched the paper doll from her hands. Cybelle didn’t protest, simply letting it slip away from her. She stared at Kotei Aoi’s shoes, struggling to come up with a response as the princess turned the paper over in her examination.

“Do… do you want it?” Cybelle managed to squeak out. “It’s yours.”

“Thank you,” the princess said crisply. “You have talent. Pity to see it used by someone so pathetic.”

“Emilia isn’t—” Cybelle started to say, momentarily forgetting her station.

“She’s petty and vindictive. Not to mention she’s absolutely floundering. The Emilia of last year would never have been this idiotic. She’s desperate and lashing out.”

“She’s…” Cybelle stared wide eyed up at the princess. “You know her?”

“We’re in the same Politics S class. A class she’s been struggling in these last few weeks especially. Her merchant family threw themselves in with the Tross Senators immediately after the Dragon Invasion. A bad move considering that those same senators are now facing the threat of a civil war. And even worse since they’d just finished moving the majority of their remaining assets to the capital when the borders cut them off. Everything is unraveling for her at the moment. This is a desperate grasp at power, in an attempt to reclaim not Kizu, but rather her own confidence. She wants to prove that she’s still in control.”

Cybelle’s hands twitched. She reached for another paper and started to fold it. She didn’t really know why, beyond that she needed something to keep her hands busy.

“You, on the other hand, are in an altogether different position,” Princess Aoi continued. “Your family is instead siding with the rebels. A bold move, but the best one for advancement.”

Cybelle flinched, the movement ripped the wing of the origami bird she’d been working on. “How do you know my family?”

“Why would I wander into the third-year dorms without bothering to research who’d be here?” The princess pocketed the paper kappa in her jacket. She finally met Cybelle’s eyes. There was an intensity in those dark eyes that made Cybelle’s insides twist. “Do I look like someone who’d rush in without thoroughly thinking the situation over?”

“What….” Cybelle gulped. “What do you want from me?”

Princess Aoi laughed. Not a cackle, giggle, or a guffaw. This was a manic laugh of someone teetering on edge of both greatness and horror. A noise that sent a wave of shivers across Cybelle’s skin. It was the sort of laughter one heard from an evil mad mage. A necromancer’s laugh.

The princess knelt down and removed the origami bird from Cybelle’s grasp. The wing tore completely off, but the princess appeared not to care. She ripped off the card from the back of the bird, then reached into her pocket and withdrew a piece of paper the same size.

This card was remarkably similar to the one removed. This one, though, had the image of a rotting crow carcass with milky white eyes and patches of feathers missing. The way it was presented in the image gave the impression it was puppeteered by invisible strings.

“Night Raven,” Cybelle’s lips sounded out the script at the top of the card. “This description and these numbers… they’re just like Kajima’s cards.”

“Yes. I can’t let Ione get all the credit.” The princess’s hand caressed Cybelle’s cheek then her fingernail traced along her chin and lifted it so Cybelle once again met her eyes. “I’m poaching you for my own purposes. Your old friend will be left behind, alone and flailing about in the mud where she belongs. From now on, you work for me. And, under me, you’ll fly far higher than your former connections could ever dream.”
