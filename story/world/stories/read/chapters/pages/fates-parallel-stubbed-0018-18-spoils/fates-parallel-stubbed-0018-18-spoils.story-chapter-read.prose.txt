The entire training field was silent except for the ringing in Lee Jia’s ears, and Yan Zhihao lay motionless, a thin trail of smoke rising from his body. Lee Jia was overwhelmed by conflicting feelings of shock, elation, concern, and trepidation. She couldn’t believe it—she had actually won!

She wanted to check on Yan Zhihao to make sure he was ok, but between the blast of that lightning bolt, the exhaustion, and just plain nerves, she was already wobbling on her feet. Finally, the moment ended, and the crowd erupted into chaos.

The noise was cacophonous as people shouted excitedly, but Lee Jia couldn’t make any of it out. She began to shuffle unsteadily towards Yan Zhihao, but she was beaten to it by a group of people she didn’t recognize—probably some of his groupies. Lee Jia stumbled, nearly falling to the ground before someone caught her. She looked up to see Takeda Rika grinning down at her.

“Nice job! I guess it’s my turn to carry you, huh?”

Lee Jia looked around to see her friends all crowding around her and smiled.

“I still can’t believe I won...is Yan going to be ok?”

Dae adjusted his glasses and peered over at Yan Zhihao and his retinue.

“Hmm, I think he’ll likely be fine. It’s difficult to kill an immortal practitioner outright—the title isn’t just for vanity—and Yan’s given himself a double-edged sword by making such a spectacle of this duel. On one hand, his humiliation has been extremely public, but on the other, he’s receiving swift medical attention.”

Sure enough, on closer inspection some of the people crowding around Yan were treating his wounds, applying salves to his burns, and one person even seemed to be applying some kind of acupressure technique, their fingertips glowing softly.

Lee Jia relaxed a bit, knowing that she hadn’t accidentally killed her opponent. She turned to Dae with a complicated expression.

“Dae, what in the ancestors’ names was that spell?”

Dae scratched his cheek and chuckled nervously.

“Ehehe, I did say that I’d outdone myself. Still, that result was exceptional. It shouldn’t have produced a manifestation that intense, even if I had cast it myself.”

Eui crossed her arms and frowned at Dae.

“That doesn’t make any sense. When a mage casts a spell from a higher tier it’s supposed to be weaker, if they can even activate it at all.”

Dae looked surprised as he blinked at Eui and adjusted his glasses.

“You’re quite well read, Miss An! Well, what you said is true, but interdisciplinary practitioners are somewhat uncharted territory. I can only make conjecture, but I believe it was the natural attunement of the ki she used to power the talisman that made the difference.”

Lee Jia tilted her head and furrowed her brow in thought.

“Isn’t that natural attunement pretty weak at my level, though? When I used ki to infuse talismans they were always elementally neutral.”

Dae stroked his chin and nodded.

“Indeed, but high quality materials can intensify even a slight imbalance. Much like the attunement rod you used to discover your natural affinities, but much more intense in this case. Where an attunement rod simply magnifies any imbalance, every aspect of that talisman was tuned to bring out the power of lightning.”

Lee Jia sighed and shook her head.

“If I’d known all that I could have just saved myself the trouble and led with the lightning bolt. Now it kinda feels like Dae just won the fight for me.”

Takeda flicked Lee Jia in the forehead, scowling down at her.

“Don’t be stupid, Lee Jia. Spiritual artists can use their ki—er, qi I guess, to partially absorb elemental attacks. His flame shield wasn’t just for show either. If you had wasted that attack at the start, I guarantee he would have blocked it.”

Lee Jia wanted to protest as she rubbed at the sore spot on her forehead—Takeda’s flick was no joke—but her ears twitched as she heard the sound of Yan Zhihao groaning and turned her attention to him. He was sitting up now, with some assistance, and he grimaced as he met Lee Jia’s eyes.

“Tsk, damn it. Ow! I have no idea what you just did to me, but it’s quite clear that you’ve won our bout, Miss Lee. Very well, name your demand.”

Lee Jia frowned as she approached with her friends. Even now, Yan tried to maintain a veneer of prideful disdain. She almost felt sorry for him, and almost reconsidered the demand she has spent all week agonizing over. Almost.

“Everything.”

The area around them suddenly grew very quiet and tense. That one greedy, audacious word stunning everyone in earshot into utter silence. The crowd seemed to pick up on the tension and also quieted to a low murmur.

“I’m sorry? I must have misheard, or perhaps misunderstood. Could you please clarify?”

Yan Zhihao smiled stiffly as he spoke. Even her friends were staring at her incredulously, except for Eui who wore a playful smirk—she had known what was coming.

“I want everything you have, Yan Zhihao. Surely you didn’t come here empty handed? You must have brought riches, treasures of some kind, maybe even technique manuals. I want all of it. Oh, and also the secret to unlocking my qi. There is one, right?”

Yan’s smile faltered slightly as he cast nervous glances at the crowd around them.

“Miss Lee, don’t you think that’s a bit—”

“Unfair? Disproportionate? Perhaps you’re not aware, Yan Zhihao, of what kind of resources someone from my background has. I’ll fill you in—nothing. My only treasures in this world were the secret you tried to take from me, and the talismans I used to protect it. I wagered everything I had on this duel, and I’m asking you to match it.”

Yan Zhihao grimaced, now showing his disdain openly as he shook his head.

“That’s not how this works, Miss Lee. Your demands are excessive! Ask something else, or I will consider you to have breached the terms of our—”

SMACK

Lee Jia, her friends, and everyone in the immediate vicinity of Yan Zhihao all jerked back in surprise at the sudden appearance of Elder Qin Zhao, who had appeared from out of nowhere and slapped Yan across the face in the space of an eyeblink. Yan Zhihao stared aghast at Elder Qin sneering down at him.

“Have you not lost enough face yet, you foolish boy? First you embarrass yourself by challenging a novice to a duel, then you deepen your shame further by actually losing. Such mistakes I can tolerate, but by flouting the traditions of our nation, you bring dishonor to yourself, your family, your sect, and worst of all to the one who has deigned to take you in as a disciple—me.”

Yan fell to his knees and bowed deferentially to Elder Qin. His voice was desperate as he kowtowed to his master.

“I apologize, master! I have every intention of honoring our agreement! This beast knows not what she asks, I was simply trying to—”

“Silence! I’m not interested in your excuses. You were overconfident in your wager, thinking yourself invincible. Now you must suffer the burden of your failure. Now hand over your ring and get out of my sight. Meditate on your failure.”

Elder Qin held out his hand as Yan Zhihao clenched his fists, biting his lip so hard it drew blood. Without a word, he removed a ring that Lee Jia had never even noticed and threw it petulantly at her feet. Yan then rose to his feet, spat on the ground and stormed off as well as he could with a severe limp and the support of his nervous lackeys. Elder Qin raised an eyebrow at him, but said nothing further.

“Wait, what about—”

Lee Jia began to protest, but was cut off as Elder Qin held up a hand.

“I believe you will find your demands have been sufficiently met, unless you want him to undress for you.”

She didn’t understand, but Elder Qin had a physically tangible presence and she couldn’t bring herself to argue with him as she bent down to pick up the ring. It was silver, with strange red bands running through it. With her mana sense, she realized the bands were an impossibly dense formation script, running not only along the surface of the ring, but also in veins throughout the metal.

“What exactly is this thing?”

Elder Qin turned his gaze to her, the sneer he’d had when speaking to Yan Zhihao hadn’t entirely gone away and Lee Jia couldn’t help but wonder if the reclusive Elder looked like that all the time.

“The ring is an artifact of immense value. If it becomes widely known you are in possession of such a treasure, it is likely you will be killed for it within the year.”

Lee Jia’s eyes widened with panic and she reflexively dropped the ring as if it was on fire. Elder Qin caught it out of the air effortlessly and handed it back to her.

“Don’t be too alarmed. Since my arrival, only you, Yan Zhihao, and myself have been privy to our conversation.”

Now that Elder Qin had mentioned it, Lee Jia noticed that it had been eerily silent. Looking around she saw that the crowd was abuzz, but no sound reached her.

“Yan is unlikely to reveal the secret himself. The loss of that treasure brings enormous shame to his family—it wasn’t his to give. Just another consequence of his foolish actions.”

Lee Jia nodded slowly, not really feeling like her concerns had been assuaged. She’d known that she was asking for trouble with this demand, but she was starting to think she’d bitten off more than she could chew.

“What is it, though? It’s a priceless treasure, but I asked for everything he had, not his most expensive thing.”

Elder Qin chuckled, or perhaps scoffed. He didn’t smile and it was hard to tell the difference.

“You’ll find that they are one and the same. That is a dimensional storage ring, within which you will find all of Yan Zhihao’s belongings. You should also find the answers you were looking for with regards to unlocking your qi. Once you’ve done so, meet me at the eastern pavilion at noon if you wish to walk the path of true cultivation.”

“Wait, how do I—”

Elder Qin Zhao was already gone before Lee Jia could ask how to use the ring. She could only hope that it was intuitive enough to figure out by herself.

“Lee Jia, what was that? After Elder Qin appeared we couldn’t hear what you were saying anymore. What happened?”

Lee Jia was startled by sound suddenly returning to her world as Takeda approached, the rest of her friends following behind. An Eui covered her mouth with a sleeve as she snickered.

“Except that slap. We heard that loud and clear, hehehe.”

Lee Jia giggled at that. She had to admit it was pretty cathartic to see Yan bowing and scraping like that. She tucked the ring away in a sleeve as she responded.

“Elder Qin arbitrated our disagreement. I can’t really go into detail, but I think I am happy with how things turned out.”

Eunae stared pointedly at the sleeve where Jia had stashed her newfound treasure and smiled mysteriously.

“I should hope so. I’m rather surprised by Elder Qin’s appearance here. Nobody has seen him since his first ‘lesson’. There were even rumors that he had left the academy entirely or gone into closed door meditation.”

“I was pretty surprised myself. I had just assumed he was playing favorites, but now I don’t really know what to think.”

With the duel over and Elder Qin gone, the crowd had begun to disperse as the students returned to their daily activities, chatting animatedly about what they had just witnessed. Lee Jia let out a forlorn sigh. There went any hope of her remaining anonymous—by now there were already bound to be strange rumors circulating about her.

After saying their goodbyes Jia and Eui returned home and now sat staring at the ring sitting on the tea table between them.

“You definitely got scammed.”

Eui was skeptical about the value of the item, though she had to admit that it lit up impressively to her mana senses. Jia had tried wearing the ring, but it was far too big for her fingers and was awkward to wear around a thumb.

“I don’t think Elder Qin lied to me, and Yan Zhihao seemed really upset about losing it. I just can’t figure out how to use it.”

Jia grumbled in frustration. She had tried activating it like a formation or talisman, but that hadn’t worked with either mana or ki. Eui rested her head on one hand with a bored expression.

“Maybe you have to use qi. That would be a pretty cruel prank, but I wouldn’t put it past them.”

“Yan, maybe, but we don’t know anything about Elder Qin.”

Eui shook her head and smirked at Jia.

“You mean you don’t. Qin Zhao is a third generation member of Qin’s eponymous imperial clan. The Qin as a nation, but especially the Qin clan themselves, are generally xenophobic in the extreme. To them, the people of Yamato are savage barbarians, and us beastkin are no different from animals.”

Jia’s jaw dropped as she stared at Eui incredulously.

“How do you know all that!? We’ve been meditating together and sharing thoughts and memories all week and I never even got a hint of that! Also, third generation as in the God-Emperor is his grandfather? Isn’t that kind of a big deal?”

Eui snickered as she mercilessly enjoyed Jia’s confusion.

“Yeah, he’s the emperor’s grandson, but that’s actually not that big a deal. The old fucker’s been around for centuries and he has literally thousands of grandchildren. As for how I know...hehehe, that’s a secret.”

Jia stared flatly at Eui, who just giggled uncontrollably in response, clearly enjoying herself. She would extract the answer out of Eui one way or another later, but for now she had bigger concerns.

“Fine, whatever, I still don’t think that’s it. Mana, ki, and qi are all the same thing aren’t they? If it works with one, it should work with another.”

Eui calmed down from her incessant snickering long enough to respond.

“Eh, not necessarily. Really complex formations can be very picky about the energy used to activate them. Highly advanced—or clumsily made—talismans can only be activated by the person who made them, for example.”

Jia nodded. That had been why she hadn’t doubled up with Eui to write a bunch of extra shock spells. Eui frowned and scratched her chin thoughtfully for a moment, then looked up at Jia.

“What about making a circuit of ki through it, like with the attunement rod? Or with mana like when we meditate?”

Jia shrugged, it was worth a try at least. She slipped the ring onto the middle finger of her right hand, where it sat loosely. Closing her eyes, she focused on the flow of ki in her hand, concentrating on the smaller threads of ki that loosely followed her blood vessels. She attempted to push the ki from her ring finger into the ring while simultaneously drawing ki from the ring into her index.

After a few moments, a counterclockwise circuit of ki had been formed and the ring lit up in her senses like the attunement rod had, as if it was part of her body. At the same time, the ring suddenly shrank to fit snugly onto her finger without squeezing uncomfortably, and disappeared to both her eyes and her mana sense.

Eui blinked at the place on Jia’s hand where the ring had been.

“Uh, did it work? What just happened?”

Jia inspected her hand, she could still feel the ring on her hand, but it had become completely invisible. As her ki flowed through it, she felt the space within the ring as if it was a part of her.

“I think it did. It feels kind of weird, but it’s like there’s some kind of new space inside me like—I don’t know, an extra stomach or something.”

They both giggled a bit at the strange mental image before Eui looked expectantly at Jia, her eyes glittering greedily.

“So...can you see what’s in it?”

“Let me try.”

Jia focused on the space within her, intuitively forming a mental image of it within her mind. The contents appeared in her mind as vividly as if she had been looking right at them. Jia’s breath hitched momentarily as she saw what was contained in the dimensional ring.

Aside from several sets of extremely fine-looking and suspiciously sleeveless robes, the space was filled with piles of gold coins, dozens of the glowing gems that Jia now recognized as mana stones—all of them fire element—a wide assortment of pills and salves that Jia had no way of identifying, a variety of weapons—mainly swords, daggers, and other blades—and finally, several jade slips that Jia assumed were manuals of some sort.

Jia stared in silence for a moment before turning to Eui with a stunned expression of awe.

“Holy ancestors Eui, I’m rich!”
