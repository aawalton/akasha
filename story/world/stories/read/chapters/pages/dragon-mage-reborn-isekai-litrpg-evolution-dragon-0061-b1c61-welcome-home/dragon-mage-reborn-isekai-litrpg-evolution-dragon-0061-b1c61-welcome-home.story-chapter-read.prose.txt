It was frigid cold for even my scales—yet, I was burning at the same time….

I gasped, and then snapped my eyes open to a familiar scene. A second resurrection? No, I wasn’t dead. I was in my spatial domain.

The area had a complete makeover and I wasn’t sure how. It looked like the inside of a cathedral in the middle of a thunderstorm at night, the moon’s light pouring through tall arched stain-glass windows. Thunder rumbled, not from outside, but seemingly from within the very walls themselves, vibrating through my bones like a second heartbeat.

Tall stone pillars stretched upward into darkness so complete that I couldn’t see where they ended. Between them hung tattered banners bearing unfamiliar insignias that seemed to shift and change whenever lightning illuminated them. The air in here smelled like extinguished candles and wet earth after a storm, but there was something else I was catching, a familiar scent that moved my feet up a few rows down the nave of the unlit cathedral….

Before I could spot him, he said something to me, “I was wondering when you’d come here and pick me up.” I gasped. “My roommates are a real snoozefest.”

Zaiden?

I noticed his hunched back in the middle of a pew, with his arm stretched along the top of the backrest. Suddenly, he flung his head back and looked at me upside down, those cool-blue eyes making me catch my breath.

He smiled. “It’s a good thing they are caged, because I’m pretty sure they don’t like the fact that I’m here.” His eyes moved south, toward the depths of the altar in front of us. There, I heard the growling of Dusty, and the buzzing of my giant dire wasp!

‘What the hell is going on?’

“You tell me.” He shot up off his seat and spun around to face me. “One minute I’m falling into the warm abyss of death, and the next I’m here watching your memories play out like some twisted theater production.” He gestured to the stained glass windows where, I now noticed, scenes from my recent adventures flickering faintly like some cheap cinematic production!

‘What the hell?!’

“Fascinating place you’ve got here,” he continued, sauntering toward me. “Part temple, part prison, part….”He waved his hand vaguely. “Whatever this is. Your mind? Your soul? Hard to tell with dragons. I didn’t even know they could whip up a place like this in the spirit world.”

‘This isn’t the spirit world! This is my domain!’

“Is it? A house of thralls in an astral plane?” He brushed the knuckle of his pointer finger on the tip of his nose and chuckled. “So I’m seriously your slave now, huh?”

‘I had to induct you to save you. You were dying! And let me remind you that you caved into my conquering!’

“Not like I had much of a choice, Freeeya.”

Ew, I didn’t like how he’d just said my name.

It was a bit condescending, considering all I’d done for him.

“What now? You expect me to thank you?”

I rolled my eyes. ‘The pain of saying thank you must run in the family.’

That smirk on his face sailed a bit. “You know, these visions up in the windows are pretty damn choppy and inconsistent, but I noticed something really important about them.”

‘Oh yeah? And what’s that?’

“Typha,” he said, and my stomach tanked. “Where is she?”

I took a step back and jerked my head away from him. ‘She should be the least of your concerns right now.’

I walked away from him and closer to the altar, where my two monster thralls were being kept. Up close they noticed me, and instantly became less hostile. I couldn’t understand this—why were they caged? That could explain why I wasn’t able to summon them all the times I’d tried. Unless they were out already from being inducted, or that one time I’d summoned Dusty at the inn, they’d never come to me.

“To hell with that answer—try again,” Zaiden said as he marched right to me. “Where is Typha, Freya?!”

‘I don’t know!’ I said as I snapped around and shouted at him. ‘I don’t know where Lun is! Where Nolan is! Or where your sister is! All I know is that their souls were sucked out in some void of darkness, and I have no idea how to get them back!’ I breathed, Zaiden noticing how rattled I was, triggering him to stop asking me questions. ‘I just… I just don’t know, okay? I feel like every decision I’ve been making, good or bad, ultimately ends up with a shitty outcome! And I’m stuck! I’d just allowed a tier five dungeon boss, a piece of crap prince of darkness, to fuse himself to me, thinking I was doing the right thing, but instead, I’ve somehow trapped myself in my own domain! I didn’t even know that was possible! I have to figure out how to make things right.’

He raised an eyebrow at me and asked, “What the hell possessed you to think that was a good idea? I’m pretty sure he’s at a higher level than you. There’s no way you’d be able to—”

‘I have an infernal bloodline….’ I admitted. ‘It allows me to create this realm, and gives me an edge in necromancy. It also allows me to level up pretty damn fast, and interact with this world in a unique way.’

“Ah…” he said simply, pacing around me as he stroked his chin. “So, you’re a nether dragon?”

I’m a what?

He turned around and flashed me a cheesy smile. “Yeah, rare breed. Thought they were just legends until now!”

‘Wait a minute, I’m not a—’

“Most creatures with that kind of power wouldn’t advertise it, though. Makes them targets.”

‘I never said that I was a—’

“But I guess in this situation, it wouldn’t really matter, right? Seeing as I’m your slave?” He winked. “I’d hate for my mistress to rip me apart for spilling her dark secret!”

‘Can you cut that out?!’ I barked at him as I flailed my arms. ‘I’m not a nether dragon!’

“Oh, really? Then how do you explain that?” Zaiden pointed his finger upward to the swarming black tendrils in the void ceiling that started to slowly descend on us.

I gasped. ‘Theron! The Prince of Darkness!’

“Oh, that’s him?” Zaiden said nonchalantly as he tilted his head up and shrugged, then he looked back down to me. “He doesn’t seem that intimidating.”

‘Look, I don’t know if that unbothered personality of yours is a coping mechanism for when you’re in the deep end, but that’s a tier five, level 120 dungeon boss up there, and I bet he’s real pissed that I tried fusing his aura with mine! This is serious! He’s the one containing the souls of my friends, and your sister!’

He chuckled, surrendering to me with his hands up. “Ha, all right, all right. Let’s not lose our heads, here. I’ll reason with you. We don’t have much time, so give me the basics and we’ll work it out together, deal?”

I tried to calm down as I nodded.

“Heh, sorry, it’s just hard to take things seriously when you think you’re supposed to be dead.”

‘Apparently, you don’t want to be, otherwise, you wouldn’t have fought the way you did before I brought you here.’

“No, I don’t. Because I have a little sister on the other side who needs me.” His smile flattened a bit. “I’ve been through some shit,” he said, his eyes looking down at where his arm was supposed to be. “You start seeing things differently when you’ve been where I’ve been, things that you’ve taken for granted.” He looked up to me more seriously. “You say you’re not a nether dragon, which honestly, complicates things. It means that you might not have a conduit to dark energy.”

‘No, I don’t. I… couldn’t even manage harmonizing with my own darkness.’

“Well, you managed to have done something right, otherwise, that thing in the ceiling would have consumed us already.”

‘I don’t know how I’m doing that.’

“I think you’re not giving yourself enough credit. You aren’t conquering him the draconic way, because I doubt your willpower is higher than his. And you aren’t merging with him, because if you could, you would have done so already. So, what are you doing?”

‘I told you, I don’t know! I can’t even summon my thralls when I need them! That’s why they are in cages!’

“Think, Freya, think. This infernal bloodline, it gives you access to a unique domain that you can call yours. But you’re not controlling Theron—you’re containing him.”

As Zaiden spoke, I noticed the black tendrils hesitating, coiling like snakes preparing to strike but never quite descending.

‘Containing him? How?’

“Look at this place,” Zaiden gestured around us. “It’s not just some random pocket dimension. It’s a reflection of your inner nature—part temple, part prison. Pure and impure. Something inside you is holding you back, keeping you from reaching your full potential. Your bloodline isn’t just a level booster, or some means to summon a sub domain—it’s far more powerful than that. You’re a container of both halves of a hole—light and dark, a vessel of both entities, but you’re allowing your light to snuff the power of the dark, which turns out, is more powerful, just as long as you release it from its dormant state. You need to channel it and make it yours, Freya.”

The ceiling trembled as Prince Theron’s essence pushed against whatever invisible barrier was holding him back. His voice, when it came, sounded distant and distorted.

‘But, I don’t know how!’

“You do!” he retorted. “You’re scared! It’s your darkness, own up to it!”

‘No, I’m not like that!’ I cried, my inner voice trembling. ‘I don’t want to be a monster!’

“If you don’t do this, you’re dead! Your friends are dead! And Typha!” He choked. “Typha’s soul doesn’t deserve to be in an abyss….”

The unshed tears in Zaiden’s eyes made me feel even more terrible. I brought him here, and now I had no answers for him.

He paused for a moment, and then looked back up to me and snickered. “Wanna hear about the worst idea known to man?” He placed his hand on my head and instructed, “Use me as your conduit.”

I blinked. ‘What?’

'“You’re afraid of going dark, right? Well, maybe we can use this Prince of Darkness as a catalyst for your own darkness. Judging by that look up there, he’s made up of shadow energy, a total contrast to light, an extension of darkness. If we do this right, I can channel some of that energy through me. As your thrall, I’m already connected to you. You can use me as a buffer—I’ll take the darkness, filter it, and you can use what comes through.”

‘That’s insane! You could wind up being a vessel for Theron, or worse! It could kill you!’

“Hey, if it does, I’m already in the right place, right?!”

‘Zaiden, you don’t have to—’

“Actually, I do. I’m going out fighting for something I believe in, and I believe in my sister. Besides, if there’s even a small chance this works—that we can save them...” His voice cracked slightly. “I’m not giving up on her. Not now, not ever.” He paused for a moment and continued, “Just promise me one thing; if this goes south and I turn into some shadow-possessed freak, you’ll put me down before I hurt anyone. Especially Typha.”

I froze.

This was a bad idea!

“Come on, let’s go.”

Zaiden looked down at me and I was speechless!

“Freya?” He said, staring at me seriously.

I nodded reluctantly. ‘I… I promise.’

“Good. Now, conquer that motherfucker and make him yours!”
