Oswald walked in silence, "escorted" by two of the vampires. Neither of them spoke. They didn’t glance at him or at each other. Just moved forward with a slow, patient gate.

The chill in the air clung to him tighter the closer they got. Street lamps flickered in their sockets, casting broken pools of light that barely reached the cracks in the stones. A thin layer of fog curled along the ground, swirling around his boots.

When they finally stopped, Oswald’s breath caught. The house looked... wrong, rotten.

Doctor Cecilia’s estate had never been warm, but a few days ago, it had at least looked intact. Now, it barely resembled the same place.

The walls were streaked with dark stains, water damage, maybe, or something worse. Fissures ran along the base like the earth itself had pulled away in quiet protest.

Ivy had swallowed half the façade. Thick cords of it clung to the stone like veins, winding tighter, higher, coiling into cracks and gaps like it was trying to crush the life out of the building. This all happened in days? What's happening to this place?

Even the door had changed. It hung slightly ajar, but not like someone had left it open. More like the house itself had grown tired of keeping secrets. The wood looked soaked through, darkened to near black and warped along the bottom edge.

The iron handle had rusted to a sickly red-brown, corrosion creeping up the metal like a spreading rot. It hadn’t been there before. He was sure of it. This don’t feel right…

The vampires beside him stood still, but he could feel their eyes on him. Oswald took a breath and stepped forward.

The wood groaned under his palm as he pushed the door open. The air inside rolled out to meet him, dry and stale like a room shut tight for too long. He stepped in, and the sound of his boots echoed down the empty hall.

Behind him, the door creaked shut. Here I am.

He moved deeper into the house. The shadows stretched long inside, flickering with weak lantern light that painted the walls in trembling gold.

Then a figure appeared in the doorway ahead. Lily?

But Lily wasn’t how he remembered her. The nurse's skin had turned pale, like porcelain held too long in shadow. A black cloak hung around her shoulders, similar to that of the other vampires he'd seen.

But it was her eyes that stopped him. Where they were once brown, now, they were a deep, smoldering crimson. They didn’t blink. They just stared at him, still and sharp.

“Oswald, follow me.” Lily said bluntly.

She turned without a word and started up the stairs. Oswald followed cautiously. I gotta be careful, have a feelin' that things will go south real fast.

While walking, Oswald noticed how the halls were stripped bare. Furniture had been pushed aside, some toppled over entirely.

The walls bore fresh cracks, spidering out across plaster like veins. Doors hung slightly ajar, revealing glimpses of empty rooms, beds hastily stripped or overturned.

He glanced into an open room as they passed. Six beds lined the wall, four empty, two still stained with something dark and congealed. Instruments were scattered across a metal tray, some bent out of shape. A chair was turned on its side, one leg snapped clean off.

Oswald swallowed hard as they ascended to the next floor. At the landing, the hallway constricted, ceiling lowering ever so slightly, as if the building itself recoiled inward.

A faint sound threaded through the stale air. A low, rhythmic buzz. Oswald paused mid-step, chin lifting. One fly darted past his ear, then another, the sharp whine of their wings brushing against his skin. Not good. Flies don't gather for no reason.

Soon, Oswald rounded the corner and caught his first full view of the door to Cecilia’s office. Dark, tar-like streaks dripped from the top of the frame, trailing down the warped surface.

The wood had turned the color of rotted bark, swollen and uneven, as if water had soaked through from the inside. Cracks split the grain open like veins beneath bruised skin.

Clusters of flies clung to the molding walls. Shit, things really are only getting worse.

Oswald stopped a few paces from the door. “Mind tellin’ me what happened here?”

No answer came. Lily stood beside the door and opened it, becoming the boy inside.

The handle shifted with a dull click as the door creaked open. Lily’s pale hand slipped away, her glowing eyes never breaking from his face.

Oswald stepped forward. The smell hit him like a wall.

His breath caught. He staggered back a half-step, instinctively raising a hand to shield his nose and mouth. The air inside was saturated with rot. The scent clawed its way into his throat: soured blood, decaying flesh, and something sickly-sweet, like fruit fermenting into vinegar. It was wrong. Too wrong.

Each step inside brought a wet squelch beneath his boots. The floor had grown soft.

Black mold coated the boards in uneven patches, its surface pulpy and riddled with holes. It crept outward in branching patterns, trailing up the baseboards and walls.

Swarms of flies clouded the air. Clusters of pale eggs covered large swathes of the room, pulsing faintly. Some had already split open. Their contents gone. This isn’t just rot. This place... it’s infected.

The shelves stood empty, their contents long abandoned. Cracked glass vials littered the floor, crunched beneath his heel. What remained of the curtains dangled in strips, stained with mildew and trailing like wet cobwebs.

When Oswald looked at the desk. His stomach twisted as he saw Doctor Cecilia Or maybe, the thing that had once been her.

She wore a black cloak now, draped over her body like funeral cloth. The fabric shimmered faintly, like oil floating on water. Her once black hair had turned stark white, falling in fine strands over her shoulders. Not silvery like Selene’s, this white was lifeless, like bone dust.

Her skin had dulled into a pallid gray, as if the blood had been drawn out and never returned. Patches of her face had begun to rot—flesh sagging near the jawline, a long split curling beneath her eye where dark tissue crumbled at the edges. But she smiled.

Her lips, pale and cracked, peeled back just enough to show her teeth. Her eyes, once tired, now burned with an eerie glow. A sharp, unnatural green. “Oswald… you came.”

His fingers twitched near the hilt of his sword. That… that ain’t her. Or if it is, then she ain’t the same anymore.

He forced himself to meet her gaze, though every instinct screamed at him to turn and run. “What happened to you?"

Cecilia’s smile widened just slightly. As if she found the question amusing.

The door shut behind him with a final click. Oswald felt Lily’s presence shift across the room as she moved to the far wall, slipping into the shadows.

“I didn’t plan for things to go this way, but now that we’re here… I’m going through with it.” Cecilia said.

The thick stench in the air clung to the back of Oswald’s throat like oil, dragging downward with every breath he forced himself to take. His lips parted slightly, just enough to keep from gagging through his nose. “What are you?”

Across from him, the doctor tilted her head just a fraction, like a puppet adjusting to a new angle. “I am a vampire, just like my brother.”

Upon hearing this, Oswald peeled his eyepatch away and slipped it into the inner lining of his cloak. "Silas? Then technically, that guy killed himself.”

The woman’s smile faltered, the corners of her mouth tightening. Her eyes, once distant and unreadable, narrowed as she fixed him in her glare. “Tell me, boy, what do you hope to accomplish here?”

“At first? I was fine talkin’. Seein’ if there was still something human in you. But now… after what I’ve seen?”

He glanced at the rotted floor, at the eggs pulsing in the corners, at the flies crawling over the desk. “I don’t know if talkin’ is even on the table anymore.”

Cecilia stood. She placed one hand on the edge of the desk, smearing dark fluid where her fingers dragged. “I had a comfortable arrangement in Volstarn. The sanitation was appalling, and illness spread like wildfire. All I needed to do was provide treatment, and if some proved… beyond saving, well…”

She smiled, wider this time. “They served a different purpose.”

“That makes you a far worse person than I thought.” Oswald said.

Cecilia’s laugh broke the silence like a cracked bell, echoing through the decaying room. “Oh, Oswald, do you really think of yourself as a hero?”

The weight of her tone made it clear: nothing he said would change what was coming. No use talking. She’s not here for a conversation.

Oswald stepped forward, hand already moving to his side. He drew the blade, and in an instant, fire erupted along its edge, licking up the steel in golden-white arcs. “I ain't a hero, just a guy.”

Cecilia raised one arm with theatrical grace. Her palm opened, and from it poured a thick, viscous sludge, pitch-black and glistening like tar drawn from a corpse. It writhed upward in unnatural coils, folding over itself before hardening mid-air.

A blade formed in her grip, its surface slick and uneven, pulsing faintly with a black gleam. “At the very least, I appreciate your honesty.”

Suddenly, a sharp hiss sliced through the air. Oswald’s body moved before his thoughts caught up.

He threw himself sideways as Lily thrusted a dagger that whistled past his cheek, close enough to sting. That would’ve been my eye.

Another attack came, prompting Oswald to drop low and roll across the floor, his shoulder slamming into warped boards that cracked beneath his weight. Two more daggers struck the ground where he'd just been, their impact muffled by the rot but sharp enough to send flies scattering in a frenzied cloud.

He landed in a crouch. Zareth burned in his grip. Alright then... guess this is how it’s gonna be.

Meanwhile, Cecilia started to move. A thin trail of black sludge followed her blade, smearing across the ground like a wound opening in slow motion. Her pale fingers curled around the hilt, dragging the dark weapon beside her.

Each step she took echoed in the hollow chamber, measured and heavy, as though she walked not just toward him but toward something inevitable.

But Oswald couldn't deal with the doctor just yet. Lily was already onto him, twin daggers in her hands.

Oswald twisted, dropping low again. One dagger hissed past his neck, the air parting with a whisper. He turned the motion into a spin, momentum carrying him just out of reach as her second strike came in fast and low, aimed for his ribs.

He swayed back, just far enough. The blade kissed his cloak, slicing clean through the front layers but stopping short of skin. Not today.

Oswald dropped his weight, pivoted on his heel, and drove a kick straight into her stomach. The impact landed solid. Lily grunted as the force sent her skidding across the floor, crashing into a pile of broken shelves with a sharp crack of splintering wood.

Finally, Oswald got the chance he had been waiting for. Flames burst to life along his forearm, searing white-hot. Burning chains wrapped around his skin like living metal, coiling tight from elbow to wrist.

From the rotted floor, fiery links erupted in a violent spiral. They burst through mold and wood, splitting the boards as molten chains lanced toward Lily. She barely had time to cry out before they struck.

The first chain wrapped around her ankle, yanking her to the ground. The next coiled around her waist, pinning her arms. Then another, and another, each one burning brighter, tighter. Smoke rose where the links met her flesh, the scent of scorched cloth and sizzling skin thick in the air.

Lily screamed, struggling as the chains pulled her down and held her fast. The glow of her eyes flickered beneath the pain, but the hold did not ease.

Oswald’s chest heaved. At least she's down for now.

But movement flickered in the corner of his eye. Cecilia had stopped walking, instead, she lifted her arms and spread her black cloak wide. From the folds poured a swarm of what seemed like thousands of flies. They exploded outward in a wave, screaming across the space between them.

Oswald’s pupils dilated. His breath caught mid-chest. Not good!

Immediately, Oswald swung his sword as hard as he could. Light raced along the blade like a fuse catching fire. In the next instant, furious golden flames erupted. It blazed along the metal in a torrent, as if the sword had been holding its breath until now. The heat slammed outward, dry and stinging, pushing against his skin like a wall.

The fire curled and stretched, licking outward in broad strokes. It unfurled like wings, illuminating the decay around him. His grip tightened as he swept Zareth in a wide, punishing arc. “Back off!”

The fiery edge carved through the air, launching a blazing wave toward the incoming swarm. It hit the cloud of flies with a roaring hiss, the burst of heat igniting wings mid-flight. The swarm scattered, smoke rising in curling tendrils as charred husks fell like black rain around him.

But the fire hadn’t stopped Cecilia. She burst through the flames, sword raised, her pallid face twisted with something caught between fury and glee. Her feet barely touched the ground as she lunged, her blade cleaving downward toward his head.

Oswald raised Zareth just in time to block. Steel clashed with black sludge, the impact ringing through his arms. Sparks and embers scattered across the office like wildfire. He pushed back, shoving her off, but the tight space gave him little room to breathe.

Cecilia swung again with short, brutal strikes. Her corrupted blade hissed with each attack, leaving trails of black vapor in the air. Oswald ducked behind a half-rotted shelf, her sword smashing through it with ease. Moldy books and debris burst into the air as Oswald sidestepped and retaliated, slashing diagonally across her torso.

Zareth’s edge caught the hem of her cloak. Fire erupted in jagged streaks, racing across the fabric like lightning drawn in flame. The blaze crawled up her back, licking higher with greedy hunger.

The flames should have made her scream. But she didn’t.

Instead, Cecilia reached behind her with the same eerie calm she always wore. The sludge from her sword oozed across the flames, hissing on contact. Black tendrils smothered the fire, swallowing the heat and light until only charred tatters remained. She just put herself out with that stuff. What even is that blade?

Oswald staggered backward. His heel clipped the leg of a rusted tray, and it tipped. The metal clattered down with a shriek. Forceps, scalpels, and bloodstained scissors scattered across the floor in a mess of rust and dried gore.

Desperate, he snatched up the tray’s stand and threw it at the doctor.

Cecilia's sword cleaved through the stand with a single strike. The pieces flew apart, crashing into a nearby desk and skittering across the ground.

Oswald feinted right, shifting his weight to fake a step, then dropped into a roll. Zareth carved a blazing arc behind him as he moved, fire trailing from the blade in a gleaming streak. The wooden floor sizzled where it touched: mold, splinters, and filth combusting in his wake.

He kicked a scorched chair into her path, the legs screeching against the floor. She batted it aside with the flat of her sword, not even breaking stride.

Oswald lunged. Zareth came down hard.

Their blades met with a metallic roar, and the impact rattled straight through his arms and into his spine. Sparks burst between them in a violent shower, brief flashes of gold against the smoke.

Cecilia’s eyes didn’t blink. Her knee crashed into his chest. The shock exploded through his ribs, and his breath tore from his lungs in a silent gasp.

Then her boot found his sternum. The second blow sent Oswald flying.

The office door exploded around him as Oswald crashed through it, a spray of splinters following him into the hall. The wall met his back with a brutal slam, but the force hoved him further down the staircase.

Each step slammed into him, jarring pain through every joint until he hit the landing below with a heavy thud. The impact stole what little breath remained. His body screamed from too many places at once.

He groaned, the taste of blood on his tongue. The night was still far from over.
