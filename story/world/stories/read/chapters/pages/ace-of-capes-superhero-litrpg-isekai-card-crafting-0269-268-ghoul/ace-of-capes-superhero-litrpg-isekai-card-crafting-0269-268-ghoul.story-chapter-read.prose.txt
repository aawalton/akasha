Lexie knew how to find Vulcan. She was pretty sure that Yasycht would help her if she promised him another dungeon to control.

What she needed now was to find the best way to kill him.

The most satisfying thing to do would be to put a VOID bullet in his head and be done with it, but what about his memories? Those could be useful to the investigation, and he probably had a lot of memories from all his playthroughs, which might be useful to Lexie. So she needed to first figure out a way to extract them.

Lexie went to her bedroom and summoned Pvilycht. He came along with his self-help book, which he held against his chest like it was something precious.

“Are you enjoying it?” Lexie asked him.

He nodded.

“Great. I have a question. What is the quickest way for me to access and extract someone's memories?"

“It depends on how much memory there is to take out.”

Lexie figured that Vulcan had been using his powers for a while, and each run through could probably last years. She didn’t know how old that made him mentally, but it had to be at least a century.

“How about a few centuries?” Lexie asked.

“I don’t think there’s anything in the human world that can contain that, although I’m not familiar enough with humans to know,” He cocked his head. “The Fae do have memory orbs, but you would have to steal one, which could take time and cause far-reaching consequences.”

“Xena has one,” Lexie murmured. She wondered if Xena was done watching the memories on it and if she would let Lexie borrow it.

“They can’t be already used,” Pvilycht said. “The orbs are linked to the memories and cannot be unlinked. We should look to alternative means.”

Lexie sighed. She didn’t have time to look through alternative means. She needed Vulcan dead today, right now in fact.

“There is one way,” Pvilycht mentioned and rubbed his chin.

“What is it?”

“My former master did extensive experiments on ghouls, both Eldritch and non-Eldritch. There are types of non-Eldritch ghouls that can assume human form. They do not necessarily possess the human as much as they embody them and become clones of them, including mannerisms, memories, and more. But they do have life and are bound to the Shadow realm, unless summoned."

“Oh yeah?”

“Yes. It’s somewhat easy to access these ghouls, but it is somewhat hard to control them. As they steal the attributes of humans, they start to become them and can even surpass the human power level and sense of identity. If they gain enough intelligence, they might find their way out of the Shadow realm."

“I see. So, is there a way to have the ghouls simply copy the memories without the abilities, the intelligence, and the personality?” The last thing she needed was to create another Vulcan.

Pvilycht thought about it. "Perhaps with your card magic, by turning it into a creature card and restricting everything but the memory. You can also bind the ghoul in the Shadow Realm. I have experience with ghouls, so I can help you with that.

"I will warn you, there’s always a risk when dealing with these creatures. You must keep an eye on it. Non-Eldritch ghouls have no will and no malevolence, which is part of how they are restrained in the Shadow realm. But now you're introducing malevolence and ambition, and even if you suppress them using your card, the creature might fight back anyway and try to break free of their bond. It might escape it. Ghouls cannot be killed as they are already undead. They often reincarnate after being denatured as well."

"My father killed a ghoul once," Lexie remembered Aiden slicing off a V'Sala's head when it had attacked her in Hartville. "It was an Eldritch ghoul, though."

"And it likely reincarnated after," he said. "If they find their way out of the Shadow realm, it might be difficult to trap them back in there."

“So they're harder to control and harder to get rid of, but they can easily solve my problem, right?"

“Yes.”

Lexie chewed her lip. It was quite the trade-off.

Ghoul Vulcan would provide her with constant access to his memories. And if she could activate the ability portion, she might be able to tap into the Mountain without Dewie.

But she wasn't going to activate the ability portion. It was too risky.

Lexie was about to create what could possibly be a liability to her in the future. She would have to add a contingency for if the creature ever escaped the card, so it would automatically be trapped again in the Shadow realm or perhaps in her dungeon, where she could somewhat handle it.

She would need at least three cards for that, and a permanent attachment to her soul card could help. Even with the warnings, this was the best option she had on short notice.

"How does Neqal get rid of the troublesome ghouls in his experiments?" Lexie asked. "Just in case."

"He does something to them that binds them in the void. I do not know what exactly."

But Lexie could find out later. Either from Naem or Neqal himself. That made her feel better.

While she was deep in thought, the door opened suddenly, and Uncle Max stood at the doorway.

“Who is this?” Uncle Max asked, jabbing his chin at the Eldritch.

“This is Pvilycht. Remember I mentioned him before?"

“Hello,” Pvilycht greeted, sans bow this time. He was learning.

Uncle Max didn’t acknowledge him. “And what is this Pvilycht doing in my niece’s room?

“Nothing much.”

Uncle Max gave her an annoyed look. “Get rid of him, Lexie. We need to talk.”

Lexie nodded at Pvilycht, but Pvilycht didn’t move. He was regarding Uncle Max with a cold look, lifting his chin.

“Pvilycht does not like to be ignored," he said.

“Excuse me?” Uncle Max responded.

“I greeted you. You should have greeted me back, but you did not acknowledge my greeting or my existence. That is called stonewalling. When you stop communicating with me, it’s hurtful. Pvilycht is open to disagreement, but he won’t accept being shut out. If there’s an issue, he expects a conversation and mutual respect."

Lexie blinked at him. Uncle Max looked similarly confused.

Did the Eldritch just therapy-speak her Uncle?

"Since when do you refer to yourself in third person?" Lexie asked him.

“It's a stage in my journey to self-discovery," Pvilycht said. "Dr. Bosetti recommends self-observation. I am listening to myself and deciding who I am. I am also asserting myself and my boundaries." He held the book closer and nodded. "He has crossed one of my boundaries."

“Oh. Sorry.” The self-help books were working. Great. It would take some time to get used to it, though.

Uncle Max stared at both of them like they were crazy.

“What the hell is he talking about?”

“Just say hi to him, Max,” Lexie said. “And tell him to leave more nicely.”

Max stared hard at Pvilycht, but the latter wasn’t budging.

“Pvilycht,” he said with gritted teeth. “Can you please leave so I can talk to my niece?”

Pvilycht looked to Lexie once more for confirmation, and only after Lexie nodded did he vanish.

“Okay, hosting overly sensitive demons in your bedroom aside…where were you last night?”

“The dungeon.”

“Why?”

“You remember Naem, right?”

“Yes.”

“Well, he has a brother called Neqal, and he doesn’t like me because I kind of stole a dungeon from him. Also, I stole Pvilycht from him, too. I guess he was really pissed about that, and he was trying to kill me, so I went to him and offered him a deal.”

“I’m sorry," Uncle Max shifted his weight, peering at Lexie. " You said you went to a vengeful Eldritch who was trying to murder you? And offered him a deal?”

"Yeah.”

“Lexie–”

“I know what you’re going to say. That it was dangerous and reckless, and yeah, it was. But it was also dangerous and reckless to leave it alone because he would have kept making attempts on my and Pvilycht's lives. At least this way, I buy us some time. It worked out.”

Uncle Max breathed harshly. “I seriously don’t know how your dad deals with this crap. I feel like I’ve aged ten years just babysitting you."

“It's not babysitting," Lexie said. "We're teenagers now."

He glared at her, then added, “Next time, tell me before you leave, not after the fact.”

“Okay. I'll try and remember that."

“Have you eaten?”

“Not in at least twenty-four hours.”

“I thought so. Come down. I ordered–”

“Let me guess – pizza.”

He cocked his head, noting her tone. “What? You guys tired of the pizza?”

“A little bit,” Lexie admitted as she walked out of her room.

“What? I used to love daily pizza when I was your age.”

“You still do," Lexie’s eyes drifted to his widening midsection. “It’s starting to show.”

Uncle Max followed her gaze, then glared at her. “Oh, you mean little brat.”

Lexie giggled. “Sorry. It’s just that, without training and delving, you’re not as active as you used to be. You might want to cut back on the pizza.”

“I’ll cut something alright." He grabbed her and shoulder pressed her wriggling body, and then he hung her upside down while she squealed.

After she begged for mercy and he put her down, he said, “Pizza was the one thing I missed while being in place. So I guess now that I’m back, I’ve gone a little overboard.”

"A little?” she teased, and when he threatened to lift her again, she said, “Okay, okay, I’m sorry.”

“Yeah, right, you are.” He lifted her anyway, slinging her over his shoulder as he walked down the stairs.

He deposited her at the dinner table.

“Do you miss it?” Lexie asked as he slid her a plate of pepperoni.

“Miss what?” he asked.

"The dungeon."

“You’re asking me if I miss that godawful place where I was hunted for sport, and had to survive on dried animal carcasses and almost died about three hundred different times?”

“Yeah.”

Uncle Max thought about it. “A little.”

Lexie could relate to that entirely. There was something horrific yet simple about living in the dungeon. Not having to think past daily survival, and appreciating the little moments that life gives you. Another second of breath. Another food for the day. Another triumph over an enemy.

And of course, the silence was nice.

“You couldn’t pay me to go back in there, though," Uncle Max said.

“What about if you wanted to visit Sly?” she asked. “Don’t you miss him?”

“Yeah,” he sighed. “I didn’t get to say goodbye.”

“I can take you to him." Lexie's dungeon had pretty much closed access to Sly’s, but she was pretty sure she could find them through Yasycht and take Max there.

Max was staring down at his fingers when she said it, and he flexed them reflexively in response.

“Maybe another time,” Max said as he grabbed himself a newly acquired beer from the fridge. Lexie gave him a judgmental look, and he flipped her the bird as he sat down.

As they ate, Lexie finally remembered, “What kind of gun did Monty want you to make?”

“An automatic watchgun."

“What’s that?”

“It’s a gun that basically watches you while you sleep and can shoot anything that it perceives as trying to attack you. It can also be controlled by the mind and nervous system."

“Wow. Why would he want something like that?”

"He seemed paranoid about something or someone coming for him. Anyway, I told him I could make it, but it would cost him an arm and a leg, and they can be pretty dangerous to handle. I’d probably also need to recruit a few underground friends of mine. He's game, so I'm going to start on that."

Lexie nodded, wondering more about this gun. “So you’re going into full-time gunmaking now?”

“For now.”

“Luke must be happy.”

He made a noncommittal sound, and they continued the rest of the meal in silence.

After that, Lexie went back to work with Pvilycht, and they eventually had to go to the dungeon to summon the ghoul.

Lexie summoned the V'Sala first to give her an access point, and Pvilycht used his expertise to draw forth a ghoul from the Shadow realm.

It was a small, ghostly lifeform, a whispering, transparent orb thing that was the embodiment of emptiness just seeking life.

And it was aggressive about it.

Though they restricted its movements, it kept trying to enter Lexie's body regardless.

It certainly was more curious than voracious, though.

Lexie started by linking it to the blank card, then she turned off all its attributes except memory copying. Then they sent the ghoul back to the Shadow Realm, and Lexie continued crafting the control elements of the card and linking it to her soul card.

The ghoul would technically be her second disciple, although not really, since it wasn't Eldritch or alive.

When she was done, it was time to meet Yasycht.

She appeared in his domain and said, "I would like to offer you two dungeons in return for another favor today."

"What is this favor?"

"I would like to find this man." She visualized Vulcan. "He is currently trapped in a pocket dimension of my father's making."

"That will cost more than two dungeons. Let's make it five dungeons."

She swallowed. "Okay. But you still have to teach me the ritual."

"It's very simple," he said. "You go to a dungeon, and you access the heart. In the heart, you must assume control of the dungeon and find a doorway. Then you must use the dungeon Lord's essence to draw me forth with these words." He whispered the words of the ritual, which was a long poem in the Eldritch tongue.

Okay. That wasn't simple at all. It seemed like it would take a lot of effort, actually, not just stealing dungeons from Lords (and probably angering them in the process), but also remembering all those words of the ritual.

"Can I do it after this?" Lexie said. "It's just that my mission is kind of urgent."

"Of course. We have built trust between us, Lexie Sparrowfoot. But you should know what will happen if that trust disappears."

"Yes. I know."

He drew forth an image showing her where Vulcan was.

It was a white space.

The pocket dimension was very barebones. No sky. No ground.

Nothing there but Vulcan hanging up and restricted by the atmospheric bands.

His head was down, but when Lexie entered the domain, it snapped up. He looked terrible. Eyes were red and crazed.

His hair hadn't grown, and he hadn't lost weight, which meant time wasn't moving as fast here. But his face looked sharper. He was still wearing the same clothes from when Lexie last saw him, but he looked more haggard.

The nothingness was destroying him slowly.

"Lexie Sparrowfoot," he murmured. "Are you really here, or have I lost my mind out of boredom? Please tell me it's the latter. I don't know how much more I can take of this, and I would rather go insane."

"Sorry to disappoint," Lexie said calmly, even as she was filled with loathing. "I am really here. And I've come to kill you."

He laughed. Madly. "I really have lost my mind."

"If that makes you feel better."

Lexie knew that her cards probably wouldn't work here. Her father had outfitted this dimension such that magic didn't work. She could have very easily been trapped here, but luckily, her portal was still open, and Pvilycht was at the entrance. He'd learned how to create a corridor during their battle with the Alchemist, and he linked right to Vulcan.

With that, they drew him into their domain.

Into their dungeon.

Aiden could probably sense what they were doing, but he wouldn't be able to stop them.

Vulcan collapsed on the floor the minute gravity worked for him again, his whole body trembling. He coughed, like he couldn't breathe for a moment, and maybe Aiden had installed something that would kill him if he tried to escape.

But it wasn't killing him fast enough.

He coughed, and without giving him a chance to recover, she activated the ghoul card and drove it into his chest.

He gasped and then screamed, gargling as the card glowed, extracting everything from him.

Lexie hadn't known it would be this painful, but she liked that it was. He deserved it for what he'd done.

"What did you do to me?" he rasped after she took the card back.

"I guess you can say I stole your consciousness," she squatted. "That means every single memory you've ever had of every playthrough is officially mine."

He glanced up to glare at her.

She smiled. "You're not going to survive this. I was curious to know if you had any last words. John."

"You can't kill me," he said, reaching for her neck with his trembling hand. "I refuse. This is not how my story ends."

"But it is. You're going to die as pathetically as you lived. In the shadows. Unknown to everyone. Not in a blaze of glory, but wriggling like a little worm in the grass. All your allies are gone. Most of your victims don't know you. No one will remember you exist. No one will know everything you did. No one will mourn you. And your power will die with you. Game over."

"No," he screamed, then his voice cracked. “You’re not going to kill me!"

"Am I not?"

"No, the vision…you’re not supposed to kill me. Your mother." He grasped onto that like a lifeline. "If you kill me, you will never see her again. She’s alive, but I’m the only one who can find her and free her. If you kill me, you kill her."

"Are you linked to her?"

"Yes," he rasped.

"That's a lie," she said. "Only Eldritch, Alchemists, and necromancers can have soul links. You're none of them."

Panic boiled over in his gaze. He reached out for her ankle. "I'm still the only one who can help you find her. That's the truth."

Lexie thought about it. It was still a hard decision, even though she already knew she had to make it.

Her mother was probably dead.

On the off chance that Vulcan was telling the truth...a conflict wracked at her conscience, battling her will. What would Aiden think knowing she might have condemned his wife with her actions?

It didn't matter. It wouldn't make a difference. She'd come too far. Had done too much.

She needed to kill Vulcan to keep her loved ones safe.

Lexie looked down at Vulcan. There was so much she thought she would say to him. She wanted his suffering to be drawn out, wanted him to know how utterly worthless he was.

But that was probably what he wanted. A grand speech. And acknowledgment of grandeur.

In the end, she really had nothing for him.

There was nothing to say. Despite the lives he'd lived, he was so viciously empty, devoid of any good, any redeeming quality. He wasn't that smart or funny or evil or anything.

Just the average power-hungry ghoul who needed to be stopped.

She put her finger up and blasted a hole in his skull. He didn't have time to scream. Some of his blood got on her face.

Effectively brutal.

As Vulcan's body slumped down on the floor, Lexie straightened.

One down, one to go.
