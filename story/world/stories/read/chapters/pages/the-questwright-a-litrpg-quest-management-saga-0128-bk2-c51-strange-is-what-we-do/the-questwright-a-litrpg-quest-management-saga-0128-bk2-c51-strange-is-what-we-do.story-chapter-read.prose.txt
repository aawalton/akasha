Despite his wishes to the contrary, Dain carried Cass up the stairs, Adya close behind. It was funny to note that everyone else stayed below as a second neigh rippled down from above.

Dain, on the other hand, had a blissful smile on his face as they climbed.

The big man shrugged, bouncing Cass a little in his arms as he spoke in a deep timbre, “From what I could tell, you did some big things in the Dungeon. Maybe saved a few of us from some serious trouble. Figured a little bravery is the least I can offer.”

Cass decided right then…he liked Dain.

The last few steps cast a blinding light upon them after hours within the Underdark. He couldn’t see, but he heard a voice in his head nonetheless.

What hath befallen thou?

Cass blinked a few times, willing his eye to adjust, “I’m okay, Bella girl. Nothing a few good meals can’t fix.” Another few seconds passed before his vision cleared. Bella was absolutely enormous.

Dost thou like…?

After they’d bonded and Cass had found a tolerance for reading, he’d looked into things. Draft horses aren’t small. Most stand between sixteen and nineteen hands tall, whereas a normal horse would barely touch their lowest limit. Bella had already been larger than most, coming close to the maximum the book had offered.

Now? She was easily twenty-one hands, her head clearing the roof of the bar.

She turned in place like a strutting peacock, showing off powerful muscles across her rear and forelocks. As if he weren’t looking close enough, she reared back before coming forward to pound the Earth. Even held in Dain’s powerful arms, he felt the vibration in the ground.

“What did you pick?” Cass asked as Dain took a small step back, his bravery quickly forgotten.

Herald, naturally.

“What the hell is Herald as a Calling?”

“I guess I know what you spent your points on, now.”

Cass lifted his chin, “A promise is a promise, Alan.”

“Guess so.” He tapped Bella's chest, then looked behind her, “You owe me money.”

“What?” Cass looked where his face was pointing. Apparently, Bella had been quite excited to get her Calling, as she’d walked through the edge of the bar, knocking down several structural poles, judging by the slanted and collapsing roof. An angry Will stood just outside, rag in hand and a frown on his face.

Cass looked from the collapsing roof to Bella. Bella looked from the collapsing roof to Cass, then, very deliberately, looked away from his eye contact.

“Bella…”

I see nothing.

With Gary’s support, Orla stepped into a monetary battle on Cass’s behalf. Meanwhile, he asked Dain to move him closer to his horse. Bella had already gotten over her embarrassment as she preened for all the others gawking as they exited the tunnel.

Adya got there first, as she began massaging the massive horse’s muscles.

“Bella? What happened to you?”

Tell her I have at last received my Calling.

Cass dutifully spoke for her as they went back and forth while everyone finally escaped the darkness below. There were tears for the fallen, as they didn’t leave a body behind. But everyone knew what going into a Dungeon could bring. So while water was shed and burial plans were shared, it didn’t stop them from considering the expedition a success.

Liberty had one less dungeon.

In the midst of Bella explaining what had happened, Cass fell asleep from pure exhaustion. He woke up with a large plate of cold food on a table beside him, the lumpy feel of Adya’s uncomfortable bed pressing into his back.

Within the quiet darkness, he took a deep breath, then weakly leaned over and grabbed the plate. It was gone so quickly that he barely noticed his fork scraping against the off-white plate. A light shone from the gap at the bottom of the door, and suddenly, there was Adya with a smile and a candle.

She closed the door behind her, “Orla talked Will down to 100 Crests and using Bella for the heavy carrying. She’s very talented.”

“Or just mean,” Cass said, blinking, then blinking again.

Something was funny with his vision. He reached up and rubbed his bad eye, but the oddity was still there. Adya took one look at him, then quickly moved closer and held the candle up.

“Cass…your eye is healed.”

“What?”

She covered his good eye, and he discovered to his shock that she was right. He could see. Reaching over next to the empty plate, he picked up the dark eyepatch he’d been wearing since the war against the Dark Guilds. Holding it in his hands, he rubbed the material, thinking back to when the changeling had cut his sight of the world in half.

How strange to suddenly not need this anymore.

Then, he put it back on.

Adya tilted her head, “Why?”

“It’s a little too miraculous, Adya. I’ve been told that there are healers who can fix it, but I’ve also learned that they’re expensive. I don’t need Liberty to know what my abilities are.”

“Miraculous is the word I’d use for a lot of things lately. About ten minutes after you fell asleep, Bella shrank down to normal. About ten minutes after that, she shrank down even more.” Adya shook her head, “Strangest thing I’ve seen since a boy I kind of like lost a quarter of his weight in an hour. I’m sure there are some ladies out there who would kill for your abilities.”

She put the candle down and pulled him against her as he leaned his head down so they touched. They rested like that for a time, until she spoke.

“You’re already a skeleton of your former self. They’re going to notice some weird things happening with you at the Guildtower.”

“True.”

He explained his bargain and what he’d learned from the Source, then waited as she looked at him incredulously, moving further away on the bed. The feeling she sent his way was brittle and cold.

Cass sighed. ‘I was desperate. For all I know, Bella’s Calling wouldn’t be there the next time I close a portal. Or even if there would be a next time. Now, Bella’s a Herald, and she can level up just like us.”

Closing the portals and cleansing corruption is a step on the road, not the end goal.” He paused, looking up as he tried to find the right words. “I feel like this is something I need to do, beyond the geas. No more portals, no more dungeons, no more monsters, people in need-”

He leaned over and whispered in her ear as she first looked at him with confusion, then started to laugh.
