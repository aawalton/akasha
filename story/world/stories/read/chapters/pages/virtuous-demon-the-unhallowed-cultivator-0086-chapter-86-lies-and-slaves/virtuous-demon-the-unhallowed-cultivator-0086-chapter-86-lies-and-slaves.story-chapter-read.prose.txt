Shuren had been waiting on the grounds for the time it took for an incense to burn; around 10 minutes. His blond hair flowed as he donned an ominous crimson robe. It wasn’t a colour he usually wore, but he didn’t want the blood to be noticeable on his clothing.

Of course, the blood from when he was done beating Tez to submission, that is.

But there was one thing he had to clarify, to rectify the pathetic attempts of his enemy. Shuren enveloped the edge of his Ruyi Staff with his Qi, and with a simple tap on the floor, Wind Qi pulsated outwards, covering the entire Dueling Colosseum.

I should be able to do a mere parlour trick like this.

Leaving his Ruyi Staff on the side, with the power of wind, Shuren resonated his voice amongst the spectators. And with the clasp of his fists, he introduced himself, bowing his head down. A rare act unexpected for a typical young master.

“I thank my fellow disciples for being willing to take time out of their day to attend this insignificant duel. In truth, I’m glad this matter has spread throughout the sect. Perhaps some of you may know, my name is Liu Shuren, hailing from the Liu Clan. I am but one of their mere descendants, of no significance,” Shuren announced, and only after putting himself down did he raise his head to the spectators.

“I was once offered the position to enter the sect as a delegate, but I refused that offer. Why? Because I wished to join the sect through the same route as any of you would have. I wanted to see the Seven Clouds Sect for what it was, not from a sheltered position high above, but as one of you. I attend the same lectures as you, work under the same teachers and elders as you, and contributed to the sect by completing their missives, just like you all. Ignoring the status of my surname, I learnt to understand the meaning of the sect; to be free and open, as reformative as the clouds themselves.” Shuren ensured to make himself relatable to the spectators. If he wanted to achieve what he must; to bud the small issue at hand, he had to do this much.

He lowered the tone of his voice, to one of somber and injustice. “A fellow disciple of ours has been crippled, on the cusp of life and death. Over a mere squabble. Even if he is my cousin, he is also one of us; a disciple of the Seven Clouds Sect. I have alibis and evidence, and you may hear these from the students of Elder Harmony herself. I believe some of you were even there on that day, watching me carry his bloodied, bruised and battered body up Sensority Mountain.”

The spectators in the Duelling Colosseum quietened. Wudan had a soft smile on his face at the realisation. “He’s quite good with his words.”

Shuren raised his left fist, clenching it with injustice. He raised his tone to that of passion and hidden fury. “We are not like those demonic sects, willing to kill and maim their own kin as if it were a small matter. We are not even like the orthodox sects, that allow disciples to cripple each other on the basis of cultivating strength. The Sect Leader of the Seven Clouds Sect has made these rules in his grand wisdom, understanding that to grow strength and gain comprehension in the Grand Paths, those in the same sect must become sworn brothers and sisters. If we have our grievance, we settle it out in a public duel, not a scheme out of sight to kill and cripple one another! This is how the Seven Clouds Sect has risen to the top, overlooking every other sect and clan in the vicinity from the skies above!”

Amongst the inner disciples, a dull-eyed man with short silver hair held his chin up by his hand as he observed the performance. After a long sigh, he spoke out loud. “Someone has been fanning the flames, making this insignificant duel a matter involving the entire sect. Why is the blood of the Sword Saint here, being used without even realising it?” And though he said his words, it seemed as if none surrounding him heard it.

Shuren waited for a breath, lowering his raised fist against his chest. He spoke with the countenance of a truth seeker, pleading for righteousness.

“I had every right to seek vengeance, but as a disciple of the sect, all I ask is for my opponent to publicly admit to his wrongdoings. All of you as my witnesses, please see this duel to the end, and judge for yourself who is the one at the wrong!”

With the declaration of Liu Shuren, the crowd began to speak once more, speculating at the nature of the duel happening in front of them.

“Which idiot said the Liu Clan was using Shuren as a puppet to take over the Seven Clouds Sect? You think our sect would fall for something like that? Clearly someone’s been getting deep in his conspiracies!” An Outer Disciple yelled out with passion, asking the others on the identity of the outer disciple.

“It’s true, I remember that day. I saw him take an almost unrecognisable body up Sensority Mountain, begging for help. Why would he go this far just to bully some random Outer Disciple?!” A brown haired lady recounted, spreading the message to her friends around her.

“Who the hell is Tez anyway? He’s some guy that’s not even from the Cloud City, and he’s coming to our sect to cause this much trouble?!” One of the older inner disciples exclaimed, looking around for more information.

The Dueling Colosseum had various seats, and for those who paid the most with spirit jades, they could get the best rooms and seats with a clear view near the apex. These were usually reserved for delegates, those with deep pockets.

A girl with incandescent white hair sat, observing Shuren with molten eyes glowing with the hymns of the skies.

“Interesting. The last time I saw him, and even now, he has always shown the same, subdued colour. Of a dead green,” she commented, always retaining the serene smile.

The clink of a wine glass echoed across her room, sloshing around as footsteps came closer.

“Well, what do you think? Who you see in front of you is no mere disciple, but the grandson of the Divine Sword Saint himself,” an amusing voice said, from a young man with dark purple hair who continued to walk towards the girl.

“It’s quite rude to enter a lady’s room without invitation,” Celes quipped back, without even looking behind at the intruder. Not a single hint of panic was shown on her face, as if everything was going according to plan.

Su Kang took a small sip, savouring the wine and licking his lips. “Don’t worry. Your servant gave me permission to enter.” As if the world was in his palms, Su Kang continued to walk, stopping himself right behind Celes.

“Unchained Crown Sect. Your Conqueror’s Path is an amusing name. Why don’t you admit it? If it weren’t for the remnants of that fallen order, you all would’ve been branded as blaspheming heretics,” Celes exchanged sharp words with Su Kang, but she continued to observe Shuren out of her intrigue. “It is in your name after all. Conquering Path? That’s just an embellished name to cover up that you’re all just slavers.”

Su Kang glared at the neck of Celes, even considering dealing with her on the spot. To think some random girl dared to slander the name of his sect and even their very Path? She wouldn’t live long with an attitude like that.

“Someone from a mere backwater little organisation that excels in fortune telling has no place here. Blasphemy? You people are the one who try to play with fate itself. Even though I couldn’t find your background, you yourself are just a convenient tool for them. Haha, truly laughable,” Su Kang jested as he laughed, but his red eyes were narrowed with anger. Anger of his sect being insulted, yet unable to punish her.

To think a mere Rank 5 Sect could restrain him like so. But the mark of a righteous was their ability to hide their fangs and repay back enmities under the guise of face.

Su Kang walked to the edge, watching Shuren with bright eyes. “But, I expected a bit more, brother Shuren. You still haven’t even figured out the scheme set out against you. At least you still have that charisma to sway people with your words.”

Celes stood up, facing against the railing of the balcony aside from Su Kang. “Oh, am I hearing that right? The representative of one of the Great Nine’s is trying to play games in the domain of Aether Ul-Regen?”

“You mishear.” As if the mere presence of Celes was an affront to his very being, Su Kang began to walk away. “I’ll leave you with one message. Do not prey on Liu Shuren,” he said, taking one last sip of his wine glass.

“Prey? He’s not worth that luxury. I simply wanted to… amuse myself for a bit,” Celes enjoyed the wind as it caused her hair to flutter in the sky. It had been a while since she was able to enjoy simple pleasures such as this.

“You know nothing. A dull blade of an emperor is still the emperor’s blade, worn and rusted from his expeditions.” When Su Kang said these words, memories of a bygone time came fleeting in his mind.

He hoped to see the glory of that time once more.

But it all hinged on one person.

“You speak very highly of him, but I do not see it. If he is from the Divine Sword Saint’s lineage as you say, then he is, quite frankly, an utter disappointment.”

Su Kang couldn’t reply to the claim. When he walked back out of the door, he left with one final saying.

“I would’ve taken a different approach in handling this were it not for the rules of the Seven Clouds. I trust you understand what that means.” He left the room without waiting for Celes’s reply. As he exited, the guard to the door of Celes knelt down a knee to the floor, awaiting Su Kang’s orders.

“Lord, what would you have me do next?” He said, with an unfocused gaze akin to a puppet.

Su Kang ignored him, walking through the hallway. “You may be free, but forget this matter,” with a raise of his palm, he acknowledged.

The guard of the sect gained a clear clarity in his eyes, but soon that was changed to utter confusion as he raised his eyebrows. “What am I doing on the floor? I need to get back to work! Strange, how did I even end up like this?”
