After leaving the orphanage with a lighter Bag of Holding and considerably fewer sweets, I headed toward my next stop.

The Scribing Guild occupied a modest three-story building near Hano's Merchant Guild. The scent of ink and parchment greeted me the moment I stepped inside. Unlike the Freelancer Guild or the Merchant Guild, there wasn't much coming and going. A few scholars browsed notice boards, a courier delivered a stack of sealed letters, and an elderly woman carefully compared several maps spread across a reading table. There were also a few doors leading deeper inside to storage rooms filled with maps, books, and ledgers, while others opened into what looked almost like cubicle workspaces, where desks were manned by a few dozen hunched scribes working on various projects. Only the sounds of scratching quills and turning pages reached me. It was a strangely comforting melody to my scholarly ears.

A middle-aged man wearing the guild's dark blue robes looked up from his work.

"Welcome to the Scribing Guild. How may I help you today?"

"I'd like to subscribe to the Hano Broadsheets."

He nodded as though he had heard this request many times before.

"Name and title," he said.

"San Alice Hecate, freelancer." I flashed my Guild badge and my Bank badge. Both confirmed my title.

So it is true. Only nobles can subscribe. That's why he asked for my title.

"Weekly or monthly delivery?"

"Weekly."

"Five silver a year."

I placed the coins on the counter.

He opened a ledger, wrote down my name, guild affiliation, and room number at the Freelancer Guild, then stamped the page with a small seal.

"Your subscription begins immediately. Future editions will be delivered to your registered address unless you notify us otherwise."

"Actually..." I said, reaching into my pouch again. "I have another request."

The scribe looked up.

"I'd like copies of every broadsheet you've ever published."

His quill froze.

"...Every issue?"

"If that's possible."

"It is," he admitted slowly. "Though, we have only been officially publishing them for the last five years."

"Only five?" I frowned.

"The predecessor to the broadsheet was when a noble paid us to copy a letter or an announcement and send it to everyone important," he explained. "Those only evolved into a proper broadsheet after Lady Marina suggested that we centralize it."

"Can I buy copies of those letters as well?" My eyes lit up.

The scribe immediately shook his head.

"We do not archive letters for privacy reasons."

"I guess that makes sense."

He spent a few moments making calculations on a scrap of parchment.

"There are approximately one hundred and sixty-one weekly editions." He looked back at me. "Copying them all will occupy several scribes for quite some time."

I placed a gold coin beside the ledger.

"Would this cover the cost?"

The scribe stared at the coin for a heartbeat before carefully sliding it into the guild's lockbox.

"It certainly would."

"When do you think they'll be ready?"

"Within a few days." He paused thoughtfully. "Perhaps a week if we're particularly busy."

"That's perfectly fine."

"I assume you'd like them delivered?"

"Yes. To my room at the Freelancer Guild."

He added another note to the ledger before closing it.

"Consider it done."

"One last thing."

I gestured toward a shelf where several folded newspapers rested in neat stacks.

"Can I take the latest edition now?"

"Certainly. We also have the two previous editions ready as well."

He retrieved the three, tying the bundle together with a thin string before handing it over.

I thanked him and tucked the bundle under one arm when another thought occurred to me.

"Out of curiosity," I asked, "who actually writes these?"

"Various important people within the city share their thoughts," he said eagerly. "As a freelancer, you may find the column written by Lady Marina interesting. Or perhaps you have an eye for fashion, so be sure to check Madam Ciram's latest take. Or, if you're more interested in scandalous gossip, be sure to read the column written by the anonymous Miss J."

"Anonymous? She must be pissing a lot of people off."

"Oh yes," he nodded. "She doesn't shy away from the truth and is one of the boldest columnists."

I thanked him once more before stepping back into the street, flipping open the top newspaper as I walked.

If I wanted to better understand Hano, reading history books would only get me so far. Reading yesterday's gossip, however, was far more revealing.

An hour later, I found myself sitting on a bench atop Hano's outer wall, overlooking the surrounding farmland where elephant-sized cattle quietly grazed beneath the blue sun. The walls were open to the public during the day in times of peace, yet somehow I'd never climbed them before. Even during my training under Garo, when I had registered as a reserve wall guard, I'd never actually stood atop the walls themselves.

The final newspaper rested open across my lap.

The contents were... weird.

Maybe it was my Earth sensibility speaking, but they felt strangely unprofessional.

Madam Ciram openly claimed she was responsible for the latest fashion trend sweeping through Hano.

"Naturally, you all know that Petra was my former apprentice. I taught her everything she knows. I am sure you all recognized my personal touch throughout those newest designs. You know me. I am always open to sharing my experience with younger people."

The woman was taking credit for something she had absolutely nothing to do with. Petra's new designs came from my phone, my mother's WhatsApp conversation history to be precise.

Another article was even worse.

A merchant named Tan Ma'i had devoted nearly an entire column to attacking Je'e.

"A Tree-forsaken Soulit born in the Bloodline Realm who has never once felt the touch of the Motherland's soul."

The insults only escalated from there.

He even claimed that wearing jewelry sold by such a woman would somehow reduce the chances of producing Souldealer children.

"Her father had seventeen daughters. None became proper Souldealers, and only one awakened as a Soulseer. What greater proof does one need that the Mother Tree's blessing has forsaken that bloodline?"

I stared at the page for several long moments.

"Seriously? This is worse than the American media during election season..."

Mick Ira's column, thankfully, was far more grounded.

He mostly wrote about food.

Every week he recommended restaurants worth visiting, praised talented chefs, criticized poor service, and occasionally highlighted unusual ingredients he believed deserved more attention.

His reviews were surprisingly entertaining. One particular passage actually made me laugh.

"Had they always served food decorated with gold leaf, that would be one thing. Instead, only my own plate received such treatment, as though they imagined my integrity could be purchased with shiny metal. My stomach cares very little about the cost of a meal. It cares only about how good it tastes, which, frankly, was disappointing."

Then there was the anonymous Miss J.

Her column was infuriating.

The older issue contained an exposé detailing Commander Kitchi Agame's affair with Beatrice Amone, daughter of Lord Amone. It described how they had met, where they had secretly conducted their liaison, how they had eventually been discovered, and even Lord Amone's furious threat to stop exporting his famous honey to Hano unless the Commander wed his daughter.

I wasn't even sure how someone could possibly know that much.

The two following editions, however, shifted entirely toward coverage of the Saint rumors.

What had started as gossip among Dreamers and followers of the Mythic Faith had now escaped into Hano's Bloodline and Soulit high society thanks to those articles. The newest edition even mentioned me by name.

"I have personally observed Lady Alice and found her to be a remarkably reasonable individual. Humble, even, despite being one of only three teleporters currently residing in Hano, or four if one counts Ki'a Outspring's borrowed power."

I groaned.

Please stop. The last thing I wanted was to become the subject of citywide speculation.

The column I found most interesting belonged to Commander Marina.

Not because of what she wrote, but because of how she wrote it.

The difference between her articles was so dramatic that I found myself checking whether they had actually been written by the same person.

The first read like a military report.

It carefully documented the Cult engagement on the Pikar Steppe, the rescue of the missing civilians, and the subsequent battle against Todor. Every participant was listed by name. Several freelancers were commended for their achievements, including myself, who was praised for bringing reinforcements, and the report concluded with a brief but respectful eulogy for Lieutenant Lloyd. Even then, it remained thoroughly professional.

The second article, however, had a completely different tone.

It enthusiastically praised Captain Kerissa's new dresses, calling her cute, declared the old enchanted corsets horribly outdated, and argued that Hano should embrace lighter, more comfortable fashions.

It was cheerful, playful, and almost bubbly, nothing like the commander I had personally met.

Then came the latest article. It wasn't nearly as formal as the first, nor as carefree as the second. Instead, it struck a warm middle ground as Marina recounted how Captain Makai had intercepted a monster stampede before it reached several northern villages near the Colossus Dead Zone.

Reading all three back to back, I found myself wondering if Commander Marina secretly had multiple personalities. Or perhaps, more likely, she had hired someone else to write each one.

I wondered if the last one had been written by Nada. I would have to ask her the next time I met her.

I tucked the newspapers into my Bag of Holding before stepping down from the wall and back onto the street.

Three years' worth of newspapers would be delivered to my room over the next few days. That should give me plenty of bedtime reading.

I glanced toward the sky.

The blue sun still hung comfortably above the rooftops. Judging by its position, I still had five or six hours before I needed to meet Louis and Silnar for our trip into the Great Dream.

Plenty of time.

The question was how to spend it.

I briefly considered teleporting back to the observatory. Now that the paperwork had finally begun, I could start planning how I wanted to restore the place. The ancient tower had enough rooms to become a proper home if I invested the time.

Then another thought crossed my mind. I could start looking for an Earth mage, someone capable of repairing ancient stonework.

No.

I immediately dismissed the idea.

There wasn't much point in hiring a construction mage before Vena sanctified the tower. No sane craftsman would willingly teleport into the middle of monster territory without any guarantee that they wouldn't become lunch before finishing the first wall.

Even if I paid hazard rates.

"I suppose that project can wait."

Which left another destination: the Soul Emporium.

If the Merchant Guild sold goods, then the Soul Emporium sold magical conveniences, my Bag of Holding being one of the items they offered.

The building sat across from a plaza in the nobles' district, towering over the smaller shops. Its front was clean and understated, with smooth slate walls, but the glass door gave it an air of modernity absent from other establishments. The logo of a red fruit with a green snake biting it was painted above the entrance on a polished wooden sign.

I stepped inside and was greeted by a wide glass display. Each case showcased carefully arranged enchanted items resting on velvet cushions with small brass plaques describing each item's purpose alongside neatly written prices.

One display featured enchanted lanterns that changed color with a touch. Another contained self-heating cooking pots. A third displayed an assortment of cleaning enchantments, with enchanted brushes sweeping themselves across tiny wooden boards to demonstrate their usefulness.

It felt like walking into an upscale magical department store.

Deeper inside, past the lobby, were multiple corridors, each dedicated to different schools of enchantment: household items, camping equipment, communication devices, and Bags of Holding.

Unlike most stores, customers weren't expected to browse alone. Several receptionists dressed in elegant white and red robes stood ready to greet newcomers. Like car salespeople.

The nearest woman noticed me immediately.

"Welcome to the Soul Emporium. My name is Shina, and I will be your assistant for this visit."

She offered a practiced smile.

"Is there anything specific you're looking for today?"

"I'd like to see what sort of household enchantments are available."

Her professional smile widened.

"I believe we have quite a few things that may interest you."

She stepped out from behind the counter.

"If you don't mind, I'll give you a tour."

"Yes, please."

"Our displays only show a fraction of what we make. Most customers don't know what they're looking for until they see it."

That opening line sounded tailor-made to encourage impulse purchases. Oh well, what's a few wasted gold coins in the name of exploring magical enchantments? It's not like I couldn't afford it.

The receptionist led me down the Household Enchantments corridor at an unhurried pace.

"Most people assume enchanted items are luxuries," she explained. "But in reality, they save you your most important resource: time."

I smiled. That sounded like a rehearsed sales pitch, the sort of thing you had to say to every client or risk getting written up.

She stopped before the first display.

A polished ceramic bowl rested atop a wooden pedestal beside a glass pitcher. The pitcher had a blue monster core about the size of a marble attached to its base.

"This is one of our most popular household enchantments."

She poured the pitcher into the bowl until it was empty. Moments later, the pitcher began to fog over, and water started condensing along the inside before slowly dripping down toward the base.

"It converts ambient mana into temporary water," she explained. "The process is slow because of the size and quality of the core."

"How temporary is the water?"

"It lasts roughly six hours before dissolving back into ambient mana. Drinking it is highly discouraged, as it could disappear inside your stomach, leaving you in an even worse state of dehydration. Though some people have used it to delay death in the desert until they reached actual water."

That was actually interesting... though I doubted that pitcher would suit my needs.

"If I wanted one that could fill a bathtub in a few minutes, what would that cost me?" I asked.

"Around three gold coins," she said, "including the tub and the heating enchantments."

"Follow me," she added before leading me into a side room.

Inside looked like a luxurious bathroom, complete with a large porcelain tub big enough for six people. A blue monster core the size of a fist was embedded in its center, while several smaller blue and red cores had been set around its sides.

Beside the tub stood a copper disk covering a transparent glass container. It looked like a large shower head placed on the floor instead of overhead, with holes spread evenly across its circular surface.

"What does this one do?" I asked, pointing at the strange disk.

She picked up a damp towel from a nearby basket and dropped it onto the disk with a wet plop.

The engraved runes glowed.

Droplets of water lifted from the cloth one by one before flowing into the glass container beneath the platform. Within half a minute, the towel looked perfectly dry.

"It's a drying pad," I observed.

"Yes," she confirmed. "It uses water manipulation instead of fire or wind."

She pulled out the glass reservoir.

"The water can be reused for washing floors, watering plants, or anything else."

"Would it drain your blood if you stepped on it with an open wound?" I asked.

The saleswoman blinked.

"No... I don't think so."

"Why not?" I asked.

"Let me call my supervisor. I'm sure he could answer you."

She stepped away and returned about five minutes later with a cyan-haired Soulit man.

"My lady, my name is Zi'a." He bowed. "Allow me to answer your question."

I nodded.

"Your blood, along with every other bodily fluid, contains trace amounts of your soul. Even the water you drink becomes imprinted after a few minutes. The enchantment only interacts with unclaimed water. That's also why it wouldn't work on your sweat unless you washed it off first."

"Huh. Thank you, Mr. Zi'a."

"Would you like me to continue your tour, my lady?" he asked.

I noticed the young woman stiffening slightly.

"That won't be necessary. Shina was doing a great job, and I'm sure you're busy," I said with a conciliatory smile. "If I have any more questions, Shina and I will stop by your office, if that doesn't bother you."

"No, that's perfectly fine, my lady," he said. "Thank you for your consideration."

The manager left, and the saleswoman led me back into the display corridor. She couldn't quite hide the smile on her face.

The next display featured a bronze plate with a red monster core embedded in its center.

I had seen something similar in Nina's workshop.

My eyes drifted down to the price.

Three silver. It wasn't the most expensive item here, but it was still about the price of a dairy cow.

"This is a bathtub water heater," Shina explained. "The design I showed you earlier has one built into it, but this version can be used to heat any container of water."

"You can use it at home," she added, "but it's especially popular with freelancers who need to bathe while camping."

"We usually use Duke Raik Agame for that," I said with a grin. "Though having this enchantment might not be a bad idea."

She blinked.

"You have an Agame on your team?" Shina asked, her eyes lighting up before she remembered to maintain her professional composure.

She led me to another display, this one featuring a cube-shaped container.

"With an Agame on your team, you're actually more likely to need a freezing enchantment."

She poured water into the container, and I noticed it was divided into smaller compartments.

The magic activated.

Ten seconds later, she turned the container upside down, and perfectly rectangular blocks of ice slid out, each about the size of a stick of butter.

"Oh, I am definitely taking this one!"

I hesitated.

"Could I have it made with smaller cubes?" I asked.

"Custom containers are more expensive, and you would need to wait a few days for one to be crafted."

"Fair enough."

She continued leading me deeper into the corridor, showcasing more household conveniences: levitation platforms that largely replaced ladders, a disintegration garbage can that used decay magic to reduce the volume of organic waste, and a cloth duplication device. You placed raw materials into one compartment, and a sample garment into the other, and the machine produced an identical copy.

That last one cost far more than I could afford, especially since it consumed neutral Soul Essence rather than mana. The magical substance was so expensive that a single drop cost a gold coin.

Still, I couldn't help wondering whether a similar machine could be built to turn coal into diamonds.

The next enchantment I considered buying looked like an ordinary watering hose.

There was a chair beside the display, and the saleswoman, Shina, asked me to sit down. She then laid the hose around the chair in a circular formation.

The moment she completed the circle, a pleasant warmth surrounded me.

It wasn't too hot or too cold. It was simply... comfortable.

"Oh, I could really use this one," I said. "What is this? How does it work?"

"It's a Campfire Aura enchantment."

"Step outside the circle and you'll see."

I did as she suggested. The moment I crossed the invisible boundary, the pleasant warmth vanished, replaced by the cool afternoon air.

"Wow."

"It isolates a pocket of warm air inside the formation," she explained.

"How does it work?"

"It contains both Sky and Fire monster cores woven into the cord."

A thought crossed my mind.

"Do the cores eventually run out of mana? Do they need to be recharged?"

Shina grimaced.

"It depends on the quality of the cores," she said. "Here at the Soul Emporium, we use the highest-quality materials for our enchantments. Our Soulscribes sacrifice lesser cores to strengthen the final core we use. Even then, the cores gradually deteriorate over time, depending on where and how often they're used."

"Where?"

"Using a Fire core in a Water-affinity environment, for example, causes it to deteriorate more quickly."

I see.

That probably meant I should avoid Earth cores in the Sunless Reach because of the abundance of Sky mana.

Fire and Water enchantments, on the other hand, should be fine, as should this one, since it also relied on Sky mana.

We browsed through more household enchantments, but nothing else caught my eye before we reached the end of the corridor.

As we stepped back into the main hall, my eyes landed on a section labeled Communication and Information Enchantments.

Shina noticed where I was looking and, ever the professional, immediately guided me toward it.

The first item looked like a polished wooden music box.

She opened the lid, and a soft harp melody immediately filled the room.

"A music box?"

"Not exactly."

She lifted away a small velvet cushion. Beneath it rested a smooth white monster core engraved with delicate runes.

"If you place your finger here, it will begin recording," she explained. "However, doing so erases the previous recording, so you need to be careful."

"Oh, a sound recorder."

"Yes. It was originally created so people could send voice recordings through messengers before someone had the bright idea of recording music with it."

"How long can it record?"

"This one can do up to fifteen minutes of uninterrupted recording.”

“How long would the music continue playing?"

“Up to six hours, though the device needs several hours to recharge afterward."

She closed the lid, and the music stopped.

"You're telling me this world invented tape recorders before printing presses?"

"What?" She tilted her head.

"Never mind."

The next display contained what looked like two thin stone tablets with sheets of paper permanently attached to one side. Each was roughly the size of a notebook. At the top rested a tiny glass vial filled with black ink alongside a matching silver stylus.

"This is our paired correspondence slate."

She wrote Soul Emporium on one tablet. Almost instantly, the same words appeared on the second.

I had heard about these before, though I'd never actually seen one.

"So there's no distance limit?"

"Yes and no." She rocked her hand from side to side. "Both slates must remain within the same Realm."

That was still incredible.

She tapped the attached ink vial twice.

The writing vanished from both slates as streams of ink flowed neatly back into the vial.

"The ink comes back?" I blinked.

"It is reused indefinitely. You never need replacement paper."

"This is amazing."

I picked up one of the styluses and wrote Hello in English.

Writing on the smooth surface felt almost identical to using an iPad. The moment the silver tip touched the paper-like surface, ink appeared beneath it effortlessly.

"This..."

I looked over the remaining displays before letting out a quiet sigh.

"I think I'll stop the tour here. Otherwise, I risk bankrupting myself."

In the end, I purchased several water-summoning containers, a fully enchanted bath complete with a large tub and shower that both ran on summoned water, along with one of the drying pads. I also bought several heating enchantments: a heating plate for warming bathwater, another that functioned like a stovetop, and an enchanted kettle. To round things out, I picked up a few cold and freezing enchantments, including a cold box and several ice-making containers, one Campfire Aura hose, four recording music boxes, and three pairs of matching correspondence slates.

The total came to twelve gold coins.

Roughly half of that was for the bathroom alone.

Seriously... I might have gone a little overboard.

The entire pizza restaurant had cost us barely half of what I'd just spent on household conveniences.

I even had to pay using my bank badge because I didn't carry that much cash on me.

Strangely enough...

I wasn't regretting a single purchase.

I was in a magical world using inert metal to buy magical conveniences.

Back on Earth, I would have happily paid far more for even one of those devices.

I stored my purchases inside my Bag of Holding before stepping back onto Hano's streets.

The blue sun had already begun its slow descent toward the horizon.

It was about time I met up with Louis, Silnar, and Nina so we could dive into the Great Dream.
