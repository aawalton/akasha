Chapter 33

“The evaluation protocol cannot be delegated, bypassed or refused. It measures whether the structure will hold when the load exceeds design parameters. There is no partial credit.” - Codex of the Long Watch, Operational Directive 7

Donna stood after thirty minutes and brushed the dust from her legs.

None of them had talked much. Arthur had spent most of it with his back against a sarcophagus, testing the range of motion in his bruised wrist and gingerly exploring his ribs. Steve had filled three pages of his guidebook with sketches of the crypt’s masonry layers, his pencil scratching in a rhythm that was almost soothing.

Arthur followed Donna’s lead and got to his feet. The quicker they solved the last line of the riddle, the quicker they could leave this place and its smell of old death. “Right then, Steve. The riddle said, ‘touch the anchor where the shadows fall back.’ The anchor. Where do you reckon that is?”

Before Steve could answer, Archie unfolded in front of Arthur. The geometry’s rotation seemed uneven, almost hesitant, the facets catching the chem light and throwing it back in fragments. Arthur had never seen the rotation falter.

“The final chosen candidate will have to shoulder the responsibility of his planet and his species,” Archie said and Arthur could detect a note of hesitation. “It is no small feat, especially at such short notice.”

Arthur stared at the floating geometry. "What's that supposed to mean? Are you trying to tell me something?"

Archie’s facets locked into stillness. The artificial green light played across his surface, and he said nothing.

Steve glanced up from his guidebook. "Archie being cryptic. There's a first." He tucked the pencil behind his ear and moved towards the Black Madonna statue at the far wall of the crypt, stepping carefully around the edge of the mosaic. “Let’s find this anchor.”

***

Steve circled the Madonna statue twice, running a glowstick across the stone plinth and the surrounding floor.

"A light cantrip would come in rather handy about now," he said, squinting.

On the second pass he stopped, knelt and went very still. "Surely it can't be that simple." he said, more to himself than anyone else.

Arthur crossed the crypt floor and looked down at where Steve was staring. Cut into the stone some seven feet from the base of the Madonna’s plinth, half-hidden and barely visible by centuries of grime and candle wax, was a symbol of an anchor. The carving was precise, the lines worn smooth by time but still visible, the crossbar sitting where the fluke met the shank.

“It’s an anchor,” Arthur said. “A ship’s anchor.”

“It’s better than that.” Steve’s fingers hovered above the carving without touching. “This is a crux dissimulata. A hidden cross. Early Christians in the Roman Empire used the anchor shape as a disguised cross during the persecutions, before it was safe to display the faith openly. The anchor’s crossbar forms the arms; the shank forms the upright.”

He looked up at them. “Saint Victor was a Roman soldier martyred in Marseille during the Christian persecutions. Second…no Third century. A port city, a Roman martyr and a time when displaying a cross could get you killed. Of course there’s an anchor carved into his crypt floor. It was the oldest safe way to mark a site as sacred.”

Donna had moved from her post at the entrance to look. “The riddle said, ‘under the hand of the Lady in Black.’ The Madonna is the Lady in Black. The anchor is directly below her.”

Steve was nodding vigorously enough that the pencil fell from behind his ear. “The riddle solves itself once you know what you’re looking at. The early Christians carved their symbol over something that was already here.”

He picked up the pencil and nodded at the statue "We even know what was here first. It's in her name. Everyone calls her the Black Madonna, but her proper title is Notre-Dame de Confession."

"Confession," Donna said. "As in sins?"

"Nothing to do with sins. In the early church a confessor was someone who declared the faith out loud and was killed for it. A martyr. A martyr's grave was called a confessio, and the martyrs of Marseille were laid to rest down here. This crypt was the Confession."

"So, she's named after the graves she's standing over," Donna said.

"And this place had the name since before there was black Madonna here. Notre-Dame de Confession is in the records from the twelfth century, and statue after that. They placed the Madonna here and gave her the name of the ground." He looked back at the anchor. "Our Lady of the Martyrs' Grave, with the martyrs' cross, cut into the floor at her feet."

He traced the air above the anchor without touching the stone. “Layer on layer. Seeder substrate, then the Hellenistic necropolis, then the Roman crypt, then the Christian anchor, then the medieval abbey on top of everything. Four civilisations building on the same sacred point and none of them knew what’s underneath.”

He sat back on his heels. “Touch the anchor where the shadows fall back. This is it. The anchor is the activation point.”

Arthur looked at Archie’s still geometry. The construct hadn’t moved since his cryptic statement. Hadn’t offered a single data point.

He knelt beside the anchor and placed his palm flat against the stone.

***

Blue Ether, dense and bright, erupted from the anchor in vertical lines that joined above his head and sealed, and Arthur found himself inside a column of luminous energy two metres across. The air inside it was heavier and carried a charge. The hairs on his arms stood upright.

Donna reacted before the cage had fully formed. She lunged for Arthur’s arm and the Ether barrier threw her back, her hands sliding off a surface that looked like light but behaved like tempered glass. She hit the ground, rolled and came up with the axe.

“Get him out!” She turned on Archie. "What the fuck is this?"

“Archie, now would be a good time for explanations.” Steve said.

“This node does not have a guardian. This node has an evaluation.”

“An evaluation of what?” Donna’s voice had turned menacing.

“The Grid must confirm the candidate’s willingness to walk this path even when the path leads to physical and mental distress. The weight will exceed what his body was built to carry. The evaluation measures whether he chooses to stand anyway.” He paused. “The candidate will be given the option to terminate at any point. If he terminates, his candidate designation is permanently revoked.”

“Get him out, right now! Override it.” Donna shouted, her face centimetres from Archies turning facets.

“I do not have that authority. The evaluation protocol sits above my operational tier.” Archie’s clinical flatness thinned as he spoke. “He will have to hold till the end or give up and end it.”

Inside the cage, Arthur heard their voices as though through deep water. The world outside the blue light was muffled and distant. A system notification flickered into his vision:

CANDIDATE EVALUATION IN PROGRESS: 0%

You may terminate at any stage by transmitting cessation intent. if evaluation is terminated, candidate designation will be permanently revoked and cannot be reinstated.

Then the weight came down. It grew inside the space, as though the gravity within the cage was slowly being dialled upwards. Arthur’s shoulders compressed. His spine loaded and his knees flexed involuntarily under mass that had no source and no ceiling.

As the weight grew a section of the crypt floor, at the far end of the chamber began to slide. Stone ground against stone and the tiles separated along ancient channels, revealing a dark surface underneath. As the floor opened, threads of luminous energy began to trace across the exposed surface. A map was drawing itself one line at a time.

The notification updated: 5%.

The weight doubled and Arthur had to brace. His boots ground against the crypt floor and his quadriceps burned with a load he could not explain. A hundred kilograms of human experiencing three hundred of weight.

10%.

He could still stand. His back was straight and his legs were locked and his brain was performing calculations to divert his mind from the increasing pressure. Weight distribution through the skeletal frame. Compressive force along the femurs. Stress across the knee joint. His body was a column, and the column was under test and the load kept climbing.

15%. 20%.

Each increment increased the weight up and the floor opened further. The map was drawing itself across the surface in threads of blue light revealing coastline and connection points. Arthur couldn’t look at any of it because his vision was tunnelling. Blood vessels in his eyes were bursting, a red tinge creeping in from the edges. He could hear his own cartilage grinding. He clenched his fists and kept up the deep breathing.

25%.

He was barely keeping himself upright. His entire body was trembling; his thighs were tearing themselves apart under a load they were never designed for. Through the cage wall, he could see Donna. She had both hands flat against the barrier, her face close to the surface. She was saying something he couldn’t hear.

30%.

His left knee buckled and he went down on it. The impact sent a jolt of pain up through his hip and into his spine. He stayed on the floor, his teeth clenched, trying to fight the pain.

After what seemed like a few minutes, he realised that the notification had stalled.

The floor had opened perhaps a third of the way and the luminous threads hung motionless. He couldn’t explain how, but Arthur knew that the evaluation needed him vertical. Lying down with the weight of the world on you looked very much like giving up. A wall was a wall when it was upright. Horizontal it was rubble.

“For fuck’s sake,” he screamed and forced his body to stand. His knees protested but he stood because the alternative was to stay down, and staying down meant failure, and failure meant everything they’d walked through since London was for nothing.

40%. 45%.

His breathing was a conscious forcible act. Each inhalation required his diaphragm to fight against a compressive force that was crushing his intercostals. He breathed because he chose to breathe. He stood because he chose to stand. The thought that he could simply will the test over had not even occurred to him.

50%.

His left knee went, unable to stand the force any longer. The joint gave with a wet sound and he screamed. The sound hit the cage wall and died there.

Outside, Donna was hammering on the barrier with the flat of the axe. Steve stood behind her with horror on his face, his mouth moving, saying something Arthur couldn’t hear.

He collapsed, the ruined knee unable to carry his weight and the progress bar stalled at 50%, waiting in challenge.

Arthur got his shield arm under him. The Bullguard pressed against the stone floor, and he used it like a jack post under a failing beam, a temporary support to redistribute load while the permanent structure reset. He pushed himself up. His left leg dragged behind him, useless.

But he stood.

55%. 60%.

His eardrums burst at sixty per cent. The pain was white hot and then he couldn’t hear himself scream anymore. His own heartbeat became the loudest thing in his existence. Blood ran from his ears and his eyes, and his vision was a red-black tunnel. He saw Donna’s mouth open, he knew she was screaming, but his world was silent.

He could trigger Structural Reinforcement. The technique would lock his musculoskeletal system for perhaps ten maybe twelve seconds, giving his body a scaffold to lean on. But ten seconds was ten seconds, and the bar was at 60% and if he used it now he would have nothing left for the end.

So he held it himself.

65%. 70%.

The map was three-quarters revealed. Through his ruined vision Arthur could see the threads of light reaching across the Mediterranean, hundreds of connection points pulsing in the dark. Beautiful and useless if he fell.

75%.

Finally, his right knee refused the load and gave in and Arthur went down again. Crumbling on the ground, his head bouncing off the edge of the shield he was using as a crutch.

His chest hit the stone floor and the weight drove him into the crypt floor with a force that made breathing impossible. His arms were pinned. His legs were useless. And the progress bar had stalled.

He couldn’t move. The weight was a building on his back, a structure he could neither understand nor lift. He tried to push up with his arms only for left elbow to pop and he felt it through his jawbone.

Through the cage wall, he could see the world outside. A world of light and sound and air. Donna with both fists against the barrier, her mouth wide open. Steve beside her, with abject horror on his face.

Arthur screamed into the stone.

He would get up. He had friends on the other side of that wall who depended on him and he would not lie here. He would HOLD. A wall was only a wall when it was vertical. Horizontal it was rubble, debris, a failure someone else would have to clear.

He got his right hand on the shield and tried to push up. His left arm was broken at the elbow, and it dragged behind him as he pushed, his legs were dead weight. The force on his body was beyond anything the human frame was designed to carry.

But he pushed. His right arm shook. He got his chest off the floor by two inches and then four. His left arm hung at an angle that doctor would classify as failure, but he swung it forward regardless.

His knees were agony, but he braced them. He was fighting, his body would have too as well.

He was half-upright, most of his weight on one arm and a shield, when he triggered Structural Reinforcement.

The technique fired wrong. It was designed for a body with functional joints and intact ligaments and Arthur had neither. The reinforcement locked down on broken architecture and became another source of agonising pain. But the technique held. His muscles froze in their current torn configuration, his skeleton became a locked frame, and for ten seconds Arthur Ashworth was not a man but a structure.

The progress bar moved. 80%. 85%. 90%. 95%.

The map completed. The last threads of gold light connected across the Mediterranean and the entire network blazed. The progress bar hit 100% and Arthur felt the weight vanish.

The he knew nothing and dropped.
