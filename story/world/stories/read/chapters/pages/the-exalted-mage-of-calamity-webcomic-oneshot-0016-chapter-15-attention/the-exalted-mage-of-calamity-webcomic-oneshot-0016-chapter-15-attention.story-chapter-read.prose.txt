“Halt!” one guard barked.

“All of you—stand down!” shouted another.

More voices joined the chorus as guards converged from every direction, their hands near hilts, their steps quickening. The situation was escalating fast, and it wouldn’t take much more to spark an all-out street brawl.

“She’s a slave! I’m telling you! She’s been on the run and doesn’t even have any identification on her!” Jorren shouted.

“A runaway?” someone whispered in the crowd.

“That would explain the rags she wore this morning,” said another.

Veronica’s jaw tightened. She cursed that it would take almost a decade more to abolish the practice of slavery.

Although she wasn’t a slave, she also had no proof of her identity. If they claimed she was a slave, and she had no way to explain otherwise, then perhaps even the Baron would arrest her. That was the main thing she was worried about.

If the need arose, she might have to fight off the entire town to avoid being imprisoned.

She thought about it for a moment. Then, she reached inward.

Sage.

Hey! You are a part of me now, right? If you can gauge my mana levels, then do you know if my body can handle it if I forced my cores to synchronize?

[Entering your Exalted Form at Tier-1 may result in damage to both your mana cores. The damage would be repairable, but your mana usage will be chaotic for a couple of days. I would recommend achieving at least Tier-3 before attempting it.]

So it’s possible, she thought, exhaling softly. That’s a relief.

The technique that had earned her the title of Exalted Mage. It wasn’t from having dual mana cores or surviving Medusa’s curse. No—it was a signature ability only she had access to.

Veronica just hoped she didn’t have to use it yet.

For now, she kept her stance defensive, her mana carefully coiled just beneath the surface. Her eyes focused on their necks.

Jorren stepped closer, spewing more nonsense. “We mercenaries believe in an eye for an eye! This is what happens when you try to abuse generosity!”

Veronica readied her magic.

He attacked, pulling out a small dagger with his uninjured arm, his body angled forward and low. Shouts from nearby bystanders and even the guards mixed together. The other mercenaries joined him, taking a half-step forward, about to assist.

A single wing appeared behind Veronica’s hand.

Can’t aim for his neck in that position…

Veronica shot a concussive force of wind forward, one strong enough to send the man flying dozens of meters.

It shot directly toward Jorren.

“Duck!” yelled their leader, Telrick. He ran forward to assist, his blade drawn.

Jorren dropped to the ground immediately, narrowly avoiding the blast. It streaked past him, slowly dissipating into a normal gust of wind.

Kaela followed suit, stepping forward. She quickly drew two knives from her pocket and threw them.

Veronica ducked, dodging out of the way just in time. She was already preparing another concussive wind spell. Again, she targeted Jorren, who was now struggling to stand back up.

He managed to get onto one knee, but not out of the way. Veronica launched her spell—

—but it never landed.

“Enough!”

The voice cracked through the square like thunder.

Veronica’s focus snapped toward it. Her mana spiked, but then settled, her inner core retreating from the edge of bursting once she saw who it was.

The ground beneath her feet trembled. Then, with a violent rip, thick roots and twisting vines erupted from the earth, coiling upward like living chains in front of her.

Her magic exploded against a large root that extended between her and the mercenaries, completely dispersing the blow.

“W-what the hell?!” Jorren shouted—just before the roots wrapped around him and slammed him flat into the dirt.

One by one, the other mercenaries were yanked down, their limbs bound and weapons stripped from their reach. Their bodies jerked, then thudded hard against the cobblestones as the vines held them fast.

It was over in seconds.

Veronica blinked, caught between readiness and confusion. Then she turned.

She narrowed her eyes at the one who had shouted.

Hadrian. Greystone’s steward.

He stood calmly at the edge of the square, his posture composed and his hands folded neatly behind his back like a man out for an evening stroll. His demeanor made it clear that he wasn’t the one who had cast the spell, but the one who had ordered it instead.

Veronica’s eyes shifted to the left and spotted her.

Elise stood in the center of the square, one hand raised, lingering traces of mana in the air around her. She was still in her maid outfit, but Baron Welterman was nowhere to be found. Regardless, Veronica now knew which attunement Elise followed—one of them, at least.

The Path of Sculpting.

The surrounding guards recognized Hadrian, and most likely Elise too. A group of them rushed over, kneeling to apprehend the mercenaries. They all noticed that Veronica wasn’t a target of the spell and knew to spare her their attention.

Veronica relaxed.

Steward Hadrian walked over. “I say, it’s seldom that Greystone gets this much excitement in a single day. First, a mage comes to town, takes down an ogre, helps the guards at the barracks, and now a group of mercenaries comes into town to attack said mage.” He spoke loudly enough for everyone nearby to hear.

He turned to look at Elise. Her hand was still held out, but now her fingers moved, controlling the roots. They shifted around the mercenaries’ bodies, releasing them so the guards could cuff them.

“Ogre?” a bystander whispered.

“Mage? Is she the one who took down that monster this morning? I thought it was just a rumor.”

“That crater is still being filled in. That’s not something a guard could have done with an explosive.”

“Those mercenaries were threatening someone who could take down an ogre? What a joke!”

“She’s our savior, then, right? My children were so close to the gates when that happened!”

One by one, murmurs spread through the crowd upon hearing the steward’s words. Only Captain Luthen and a few of his immediate guards had seen her take down the ogre. There might have been a few townspeople hiding in the shadows who had spotted her, but it seemed many among the public hadn’t believed it.

Veronica realized what he was doing. Although he was exposing her secret, it was in her best interest. Her having handled the ogre was a simple truth, and Hadrian was using it to help her establish a rapport with the people of Greystone, especially after Jorren’s accusations.

Steward Hadrian continued, “Luckily, Lady Elise was at the edge of town. This is usually her break hour, so I must thank her for de-escalating the situation.”

He turned to face Veronica. “Now, may I hear your side of the story, Miss Veronica? I’ve already heard enough screaming from that man over there about what you did. And do tell me, as curiosity has stricken me quite hard—did you do the things he claimed?”

Veronica lowered her brows, offended by the question.

Hadrian realized his mistake. “Apologies—that was a poor way to phrase that. I wish to inquire whether there was more context to what he said or if he made everything up.”

She didn’t answer immediately.

Veronica held Hadrian’s gaze, studying him. Not just his words, but his posture, his eyes, and the slight lilt in his voice that carried a touch of courtly sharpness.

He wasn’t trying to antagonize her. That much was clear. But he wasn’t offering blind support either.

Good.

She didn’t want someone who trusted blindly. Acquainted or not, it wouldn’t help her in the long run. People in positions of power wore masks—first impressions were easy. It was what lay beneath that mattered.

“I didn’t make the first move,” she said flatly. “He did.”

She tilted her head toward Jorren. He was still groaning on the ground as two guards fastened steel cuffs around his wrists. His arm was still bleeding, the soaked bandages now dark with fresh blood.

“In the forest, I came across their group while they were resting. I was alone. I made it clear I wasn’t looking for trouble. I just wanted directions to the nearest town or city.”

Her tone remained steady. She didn’t plead. She didn’t embellish the story. The plain truth always carried further than shouting. She wouldn’t resort to what Jorren had done.

Her voice wasn’t loud, but it was enough for a few townspeople to hear her conversation with Hadrian. Enough to spread the rumors she wanted.

“They suggested restraining me. Cuffs—just in case I turned violent. I agreed.” Her jaw tightened. “Later that night, while I was asleep, Jorren tried to assault me. The others didn’t stop him.”

Hadrian’s expression shifted, hardening darkly. He gave a small nod, prompting her to go on.

“So I fought back. I already had a bad feeling the moment I saw they were carrying slave gear. I waited, hoping nothing would happen. But when he reached for me—” She sighed, irritated. “I used magic and injured him. I could’ve killed him, but I didn’t.”

Her arms crossed loosely. “I let them go. Didn’t want a fight—wasn’t looking for one at the time, either. I ran into the woods and made my way straight to Greystone. That’s why I was in worn rags this morning.”

There was no pride in her voice. No dramatization. There was only frustration and annoyance.

“And today,” she continued, “he saw me again and tried to twist what actually happened. Provoked me in front of everyone. Said I was… less than favorable things. Then worse.”

She frowned. “I warned him. He attacked me, and I defended myself. That’s the whole story. There is no other version.”

The square was still in motion as guards lifted the mercenaries to their feet while others urged bystanders to disperse. The tension had lessened, but it hadn’t left entirely.

Jorren continued shouting at the guards, demanding that they let him go.

Hadrian said nothing yet, but Veronica could feel the scales in his mind tipping.

He looked her over again. His expression was unreadable, but his eyes studied her closely, as if probing for deeper intent.

After a few moments, he gave a quiet, thoughtful hum. “I see. That does sound like a reasonable explanation for the events that just unfolded.”

His gaze drifted toward the guards, who were now hauling Jorren upright. The man snarled and twisted in their grip, his resistance futile.

“You don’t strike me as someone who uses violence to get what she wants,” he said. “And frankly, there was no clear gain in attacking them. No motive, no advantage.”

His gaze stayed on her, assessing her reaction. “As someone who’s met with the baron—and even helped train our guards—I find it difficult to believe you’d ambush a group of mercenaries for sport. Or out of spite. Especially since you didn’t even have anything on hand when you entered town.”

He paused, then added more quietly, “I also doubt you would have saved that little child this morning if you were as devious as he claimed.” His gaze flickered toward Jorren’s injured arm for a brief moment. “The strangest part is that you left them alive with only a minor flesh wound when you can take down an ogre.”

Veronica didn’t respond, but the corners of her mouth lifted. Just slightly.

Hadrian returned her look with a mild, diplomatic smile. Then his tone sharpened, his voice slipping into something more official.

“These men will be held in custody for the next few days while we investigate their movements. It is a serious crime to try to forcibly enslave others without proper process. Normally, we would also imprison you, but considering that you’ve spoken to the baron today, we will overlook this for now while I speak with him. I’m not privy to the details, but I am sure the baron requested your presence for something important. So consider this a special privilege for now. I’ll ensure that he also hears your account of what happened… and that the official report isn’t written by whichever guards were closest to Jorren’s yelling.”

Well… that won’t be an issue, since he’s guaranteeing Greystone’s support to me.

He glanced back toward Elise.

The maid stood calmly, arms lowered. The vines and roots had fully receded, the last of them vanishing beneath the cobblestones like they’d never been there at all.

“Elise,” Hadrian called, “thank you again for the intervention.”

She gave a small nod, voice level. “It was necessary.”

Then she waved her hand, dispelling the magic.

The stone beneath them shifted. Loose gravel slithered back into place, seams between bricks sealed shut, and the ground restored itself with quiet precision. Once finished, Elise gave a nod to both Hadrian and Veronica before turning and walking off as if nothing had happened.

She’s good with her control, Veronica thought, watching her go. Especially for someone only at Tier-2.

Hadrian turned back to her. “I may call for you later today or the upcoming days to verify some details. For now, you're free to go,” he said. “Unless you’ve taken any injuries. I can arrange someone to look you over.”

“No, I’m alright.” Veronica offered him a polite nod and turned to leave.

Her footsteps were quiet, swallowed by the resuming rhythm of the square behind her. But even as she walked away, she felt it.

There were eyes on her.

Whispers in the air all around.

Soft mutters brushed at the edge of her senses, like wind licking at the flame of a candle. Some were harmless. Curious townsfolk, no doubt, surprised to find another mage in Greystone. But the others… she had an ominous feeling about it.

There were gazes that lingered. Calculating ones. Watching her a little too carefully. And if the cultists were truly operating this close to the town, then it wasn’t unreasonable to assume some were already here. Intermingled and hidden in plain sight.

She couldn’t explain why, but she had a feeling something was going to happen tonight. She made too much noise now. If people hadn’t seen her use magic to take down the ogre—they definitely saw and knew about her now.

Still, no one followed her. No one dared confront her again.

Her thoughts came back to what Hadrian had said.

“You don’t strike me as someone who uses violence to get what she wants.”

That was where he was wrong.

It was why first impressions mattered—but also why they should never be trusted at face value. She had taken him for a sharp man, calculated and observant. But that one line showed that he still misunderstood her.

It wasn’t that she didn’t want to use violence.

It was that she couldn’t.

Not yet. Not now.

At Tier-1, she was too weak. Too limited, too restrained by the capabilities available to her.

This situation with the mercenaries couldn’t happen again. It was only luck that everything had stacked in her favor. Pure coincidence that she had helped several high-profile figures in town, giving them enough reason to doubt Jorren’s claims.

If something like this happened again, she needed to be more decisive. She didn’t have her old status here, nor her old power. Her mere presence no longer carried authority.

She should have killed all the mercenaries back in the forest.

If she fell into a similar situation again, she couldn’t hesitate. Delays would only create problems later. She had just seen that firsthand with Jorren.

Politics and prestige were one way to control others. But the other method, overwhelming power, was far more reliable. It had never failed her before. But she needed to be careful about how she used it. She needed to be just. The moment she was locked in prison, she’d have nothing backing her to get her out.

Veronica walked away, heading toward the inn so she could advance once more.

Murmurs continued to spread through the crowd. Many spoke about her composure just now. Others whispered about the ogre that had appeared that morning.

The last ones in the crowd, shifty but unassuming, spoke of something else entirely. Their eyes stayed locked on her from the shadows, but they dared not move forward.

Instead, they waited.

A clock had begun to tick.
