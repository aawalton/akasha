Chapter 111

Chase Guild, Part II (First Impressions and Follies)

Chase Guild headquarters looks like someone took a corporate campus, fed it steroids, and then wrapped it in magically reinforced glass. Multiple structures rise behind a high security fence, all dark glass and clean angles, reflecting the gray Columbus sky. The main gate is wide, with scanning pylons.

We roll up, get waved through after a quick ID check, and are directed to park off to the side of one of the larger buildings. The parking lot is already jam-packed. Cars everywhere. People standing around in workout gear, tactical gear, and more than a few outfits that scream, I picked this because it looks cool in a mirror. You can practically taste the ambition. The air is thick with it and the general buzz of nerves. I see at least one person bent over behind their car, puking their brains out.

“People really care about this open assessment, huh?” I mumble to myself.

We circle twice before finding a spot that technically counts as a parking space if you believe in miracles and don’t respect painted lines.

Veronica and I get out, join the flow of people moving toward the back of the facility. The crowd funnels between buildings and spills out into a huge field. It looks like a soccer field, except someone took a compass to it and drew three massive circles that eat up most of the grass—each circle marked with thick white paint and faint runes that shimmer when you look at them too long. What are these? Ritual rings scaled up for corporate talent acquisition? I muse, sniggering at my own silent joke.

The entire field is packed. Hundreds of applicants. You’ve got nervous chatter, laughter that’s too loud, people stretching aggressively, and the occasional glow of someone’s aura flaring like they’re trying to intimidate the entire field of applicants. I’m not focusing on my [Aura Sense], but further suppress it to avoid the distraction.

There are numerous signs that point toward registration. Veronica tugs on my sleeve, pulling me in the proper direction. We follow more of the same signs to tables lined up like a concert check-in, and immediately run into a line of people that looks like it’s going to wrap around the planet.

“Of course,” I mutter.

Veronica bumps my shoulder. “Relax. Worst-case scenario we die of old age before we get signed in and don’t need to worry about embarrassing ourselves!”

I’m scanning the crowd, trying to spot anyone familiar, when someone calls my name.

“Joe!”

I turn towards the exact voice I was hoping to hear—and there he is.

Clyde approaches with a smile that’s easy and calm, like he’s walking up to casual meet up for drinks, not a high-stakes Guild assessment.

He looks different. Still lean and wiry. But his shoulders are broader now, arms thicker. He’s got that “training arc” look: muscle carved onto him like he spent the last several weeks eating discipline and sleeping inside the gym.

I’m curious about his aura. Turning back on my [Aura Sense], I focus it like a sharpened edge, zeroing in on Clyde. His aura hits me like a low-frequency wave. It’s strong, focused, and dangerous. Its close to his body, a thin film covering his skin. I’m sure most people wouldn’t even notice how potent—and potentially threatening—his aura is. I re-suppress my [Aura Sense].

“Damn,” I say, stepping forward. “Look at you.”

Clyde’s grin widens. “Yeah? You too, man.” His eyes flick down and up like he’s taking inventory. “You look like you’ve been through some shit.”

“That’s… accurate,” I say, though I wonder how he can gather that much just by looking at me.

We clasp hands. Firm, familiar, the kind of handshake that’s half greeting, half test of strength. His grip is stronger than it used to be. Nice gains, I think.

Veronica makes a gagging noise behind me. “Get a room you two!”

Clyde chuckles, unfazed. “Hey, Veronica.”

“Clyde,” she says, nodding like a queen acknowledging a knight.

He looks between us. “Alright. Which group are you joining for the assessment?”

His gaze lingers on Veronica. “You’re Tank, right?”

Veronica tilts her head. “Obviously.”

Then Clyde’s eyes move to me.

And I suddenly feel like I’m naked again, which is a recurring theme in my life now.

“I, uh…” I rub the back of my neck. “Honestly? I don’t even know all the categories. Wanna give me a quick run-down?”

Clyde’s eyebrows lift. “You’re kidding.”

“Nope,” I say. “I’m totally winging it.”

Veronica snorts. “Joe’s brand.”

Clyde shakes his head, amused, and starts counting off on his fingers.

“Alright. Groups are by type of System User: Artificer, Alchemist, Healer, Tank, Scout, Assassin, Warrior, Mage.” He gestures toward the field. “Everybody does some general parts of the assessment, almost like a sports combine. Same activities, but you’re scored against your own group. Then each group has their own unique tests.”

“Right. Simple enough,” I say.

He leans closer, lowering his voice like he’s sharing a secret. “Chase is only accepting up to one applicant from each group.”

Veronica whistles low.

“Except Healers,” Clyde adds. “They’ll take more of them. Always need more Healers.”

“One per group,” I repeat, taking in the size of the crowd. “Not great odds.”

“Nope,” says Clyde.

“I feel pretty good about our chances,” says Veronica.

“Don’t get too cocky,” says Clyde. “Probably half the crowd are Guildless Users, like ourselves. I feel pretty good against them. But there are an equal number of applicants who have excellent experience at smaller Guilds, and are looking to upgrade. They’re the ones I’m worried about.”

“We’ve taken down a freaking Storm Dragon!” I exclaim. “I’m not worried.”

“A baby Storm Dragon,” Veronica corrects.

“Whatever!”

“In any case,” adds Clyde. “This isn’t a tryout. It’s a bloodbath with paperwork.”

I glance back at the field, then I look at Clyde. “So… which group are you?”

His smile turns sharp. Confident. “Scout,” he says.

“Not Assassin?” I ask.

Clyde shakes his head. “Nope. Think it’s my best bet for landing an offer. Assassins are lucky though.”

“How so?” asks Veronica.

Clyde taps the side of his neck like he’s referencing an internal loadout. “I’ve gotten pretty damn good with Glass Phantom. The silencing Skills are insane. But Scouts fit better for how I move. How I think. And a lot of my support Skills.”

Veronica raises an eyebrow. “Interesting.” Then, she elbows me sharply in the side. “And you?”

“I don’t know. Mage, I guess.” It’s the only option that remotely fits, other than Warrior.

We reach the sign-up tables, and the line finally spits us out at the front where a Chase Guild administrator sits behind the table in a crisp black polo with the Guild emblem stitched over the heart. Their expression is professionally neutral, like they’ve seen every kind of hope, desperation, and overconfidence already this morning and decided none of it is interesting.

“Name. System User ID. Group selection.”

“Joseph Sullivan. Er… Oh, here ya go! And… um, Mage.” The admin reviewed my ID, hands it back, nods, taps something on a tablet, then slides a bib across the table.

I take it. Big black numbers fill the bib in clean font. A little Chase logo accompanies the numbers at the bottom. Applicant number 342.

I pin it to my shirt, using the four mini bobby pins pre-attached to the material. I rejoin Veronica, who is number 345 and we push our way through the crowd. Clyde is tagging along on Veronica’s other side.

There’s another admin walking through the crowd. His voice cuts through the crowd noise, magically amplified. “Reminder. Items and Equipment are not permitted unless explicitly instructed by assessment administrators.”

Eventually, the crowd is instructed to join your assigned group. I turn to Veronica and Clyde, wishing them luck. We all bump fists.

“Good luck,” Veronica says, but there’s real warmth under the snark.

“Same to you,” Clyde replies.

I nod between them. “Yeah. Good luck. And… I hope I can catch part of your assessments. Not sure how this works, or if there will be a chance to watch the other groups.”

Veronica smirks. “You just want to see if I’m still stronger than you.” She sticks a tongue out at my playfully.

“That too,” I admit, then look at Clyde. “And you. I want to see how much stronger you’ve gotten while training. It’s all you’ve been talking about lately.”

Clyde gives me that chill smile again. The one that says you have no idea. “You’ll see,” he says.

We split off—Tank, Scout, Mage—each of us swallowed into our respective streams of applicants.

And as I head toward the Mage corner of the large, open field, I can’t help but think that even if I don’t get an offer to join Chase Guild, today will give me a good opportunity to compare to Harvest Guild, and also how I measure up against others who have yet to join a private Guild.

I peel off from Veronica and Clyde and follow the signs towards the area designated for Mage applicants, letting the crowd carry me like a slow tide of nerves and ambition.

The Mage candidates are clustered on the far side of the field, wedged between one of the large painted circles and the outer most glass tower of Chase’s headquarters building. The grass here is trampled flat from anxious pacing. People clutch water bottles. Many are flipping through spellbooks, practicing somatic spell components.

Two Chase Guild administrators stand at the edge of the circle in matching black-and-gold jackets with the Chase emblem over the breast. One is a tall, broad-shouldered man with a buzz cut and the kind of face that says he has never once been impressed by anything in his life. In fact, he looks absolutely bored, as if he’d rather be anywhere else. The other is a woman with a tight platinum blonde bob cut and sharp eyes, holding a tablet with skilled proficiency. A pair of scarlet-framed glasses sit on the narrow bridge of her nose.

“Alright,” the man calls, voice carrying. “Mage open assessment group, listen up!”

The nervous chatter dies down.

“This is the open assessment for Chase Guild,” the woman adds crisply. “Before we do the mage-specific portion, all candidates will undergo baseline testing in the following categories: Mana, Strength, Acuity, Speed and Agility, and Cognitive. You will be scored within your cohort of Mage candidates.”

A murmur runs through the group.

The man reaches down and produces a small pedestal from a case at his feet. He sets it on the grass with a heavy thunk. On top of it sits an orb of dark glass. It’s smooth, the size of a grapefruit, shimmering faintly. It reminds me of a fortune teller’s crystal ball. A cord runs from the pedestal to a standing screen, much like one of those roadside speed displays that flash numbers at you when you’re driving too fast. Except this one’s blank and waiting.

The woman taps the orb. It begins to hum softly.

“This artifact measures the power, control, density, and purity of your mana,” she says. “Place your dominant hand on the orb when called. The device will display your rating. Do not attempt to spoof it. It will know.” That last part is delivered with the calm threat of someone who has watched people try and fail.

Candidates start stepping forward one by one.

The first candidate is a short girl with ginger hair. She places her hand on the orb. Instantly, the screen flashes a number.

23.

Next.

A lanky kid with trembling hands steps up to the orb. His hand barely grazes its smooth surface when the screen lights up.

104.

The crowd reacts. A couple people whistle. The candidate smiles like he’s been waiting his whole life for that moment. He lopes back towards the crowd, no longer shaking.

Damn, I think. Though we only have the first number to compare it to. 104 could still be extremely average. I try not to think about how low my score will be, given I use Vitality instead of Mana to cast my Spells.

Next.

89.

Next.

60.

Next.

78.

Next.

50.

Numbers climb and fall in no obvious pattern. Some people look smug at their results. Some look utterly crushed. Some look like they want to argue with the orb. “That can’t be! I’ve got at least twice as much MP as that guy!”

While I wait, I take in the group. It’s a grab bag. There are total string beans who look like they’ve never met a dumbbell. There are out-of-shape guys with soft bellies and tired eyes, clutching their bibs like they wandered into the wrong line at the DMV, but it’s too awkward to change lines now. There are average folks in gym clothes. And then there are a handful who clearly train—broad shoulders, good posture, the subtle tension of people who know how to move their body. I wonder if they’re the ones who’ve come here with experience from other Guilds.

I consider flicking on [Aura Sense], just to get a read. Then I remember what that feels like in crowds, particularly with those who may not be suppressing their aura in such close quarters. I’m nauseous just thinking about it. Yeah, no thanks. I keep my sixth sense to myself.

Finally, the administrator calls my number.

I step forward into the little open space around the pedestal.

The woman gestures. “Hand on the orb.”

I place my palm against the glass. It’s cold. Like touching the surface of a frozen pond.

The orb hums.

The screen flickers.

And then it displays:

0.

For a second, nobody breathes. The crowd goes silent again.

Then, they erupt.

There’s shocked laughter. Snorts. A couple full-on cackles. Someone goes, “No way,” out loud like the orb is a prank.

The male administrator’s face betrays him—his eyebrows jump, eyes widening for just a flash before he tries to pull his expression back into professionalism. He fails. He stares at the screen. Then at me. There’s a switch of annoyance at the corner of his eye, as though he wants to screen at me for wasting their valuable time.

“Are you sure,” he says slowly, “you meant to be with the Mages?”

I nod, deadpan. “Yup.”

The woman’s eyes harden.  “If you’re trying to game the assessment by topping physical categories,” she says sharply, “you will be sorely disappointed. The general portion of the assessment is weighted different for each User Type. For Mages, Mana is one of the most heavily weighted portions of the assessment.”

I lift a shoulder. “Well, looks like I’ve got some catching up to do then.”

The man just sighs.

“Back in line,” the woman administrator snaps, already calling the next candidate.

I step away from the pedestal and walk back into the crowd. Jeers follow me like thrown tomatoes.

“Zero!” someone laughs. “Zero!”

“Wrong line, bro!”

“Go join the Tanks, Muscles!”

I slide into place, ignoring the noise, letting it wash over me like static.

This will be fun, I think, and I can’t help the small, crooked smile that pulls at my mouth.

The Mana test wraps up without any more surprises. The highest number ends up being a whopping 235.

“Strength,” the male admin announces, like he’s saying the word slowly in case the Mages have forgotten what muscles are.

We cut across the field to a corner that makes my heart do a stupid little happy hop.

In this portion of the practice field sits a very familiar sights: a squat rack, a bench press, and a hex bar deadlift platform. Each one is equipped with what looks like forty-five pound bumper plates made of rune-covered glass. For one blessed moment, the chaos of Guild assessments fades and my brain goes, oh. Home. There’s a screen here too, same style as the Mana test.

The female admin steps forward with her tablet. “These are controlled with gravity enchantments. We set the weight digitally. No plates. There is practically no limit to the amount of weight this equipment, when in this reinforced space, can handle.”

A ripple of interest goes through the crowd. More than I anticipated, given none of these to-be Mages struck me as the gym-goer type.

“Each exercise can be attempted three times,” she continues. “Bench press, back squat, and deadlift. Your highest successful attempt for each is recorded and added together. That total is your Strength score.”

A couple of the candidates closest to me swallow nervously.

I, on the other hand, feel my grin start to stretch my face.

“Oh,” I whisper. “Oh, we’re doing this.”

The first several candidates are a range of ‘has never lifted a weight before in their life’ and admirable attempts… In the pre-System world. One particularly average-looking guy almost casually tosses up 325 lbs. on the bench press, and not even with pristine form. He’s clearly invested a handful of points in his Strength, for whatever reason.

When it’s finally my turn, I step up to the bench station like it’s my altar.

The male admin watches me, arms crossed, expression guarded. After the shortest staring contest ever, he gestures to the screen. “Set your weight.”

I punch in my first number.

4,000 lbs.

A few people laugh, thinking I’m being cute.

The bar settles into place with a low thrum as the enchantment adjusts gravity around it. I lie down, grip the bar, and press. It comes up.

Clean. I rack it.

Wow, I haven’t had the chance to really see how much I could bench now since my Strength score has skyrocketed. Unfortunately, the high-end System User weightlifting gyms were a luxury and cost an arm-and-a-leg.

The crowd goes quieter.

I swing my arms in front of my, getting a little stretch before my second attempt.

10,000 lbs.

This time, the crowd is deadly silent.

Attempt number two. Here we go!

I lie back down. Grip the bar, squeezing it as though I want to pull it apart. I squeeze my shoulder blades.

Breathe. Press.

For a moment, it sticks. My elbows tremble. My shoulders strain. The weight tries to crush me into the bench like I’m a bug pinned under a book. Then, I exhale and shove.

The bar rises.

Slow. Grinding. Unforgiving.

But it rises.

Eventually, I lock out, quickly tossing it back and re-racking it. Instantly, the enchantment ends, removing the weight from the entire area of the bench.

“Final attempt,” the admin says, voice a little tighter now. “Proceed.”

I nod.

Alright. Let’s be dumb.

I type in my number.

30,000 lbs.

A sound leaves the crowd—half gasp, half prayer.

I lie back down. Grip the bar.

And the second I start the press, I activate [Muscle Mastery].

My shirt explodes off my body. Fabric disintegrates in a burst of threads like my pecs just dropped a nuke.

The bar moves. My shoulders drive. My chest tightens. And the bar rises. Up, up, up… All the way.

I rack it. The gravity enchantment eases again, the bar settling with a gentle hum.

I sit up, breathing hard, and for the first time in this entire assessment I’m… disappointed.

I could’ve done more, I think, kicking myself for leaving some gas in the tank. Not much more, but more.

I swing my legs off the bench and look around for my bib. It’s on the grass near my feet. I pick it up and, since my shirt is now dust, I clip it to my pants leg.

The crowd is frozen.

I swear I hear someone whisper—awed, horrified even—“Hydraulic press…”

I grin.

“Thanks,” I mutter. “I try.”

Squat is next up, and it’s the same story as the bench press.

I set ridiculous numbers. With [Muscle Mastery], the movement is efficient. Several tons on my back, and still, I get that shit up with pristine form.

Same thing with the hex bar deadlift.

I’ve earned some respect, now. But still, too many of my fellow applicants look at me like I don’t belong here.

“He’s just playing pretend spellcaster,” someone whispers, though not too quiet that I don’t pick it up with my [Perception].

“What kind of joke is this?” someone else says.

After the Strength station, the Mage group treats me like I’m radioactive.

Nobody stands too close. Nobody makes jokes anymore. A few people won’t even meet my eyes, like they’re afraid my weird, shirtless, zero-mana energy might be contagious.

The administrators don’t comment. They just move us along briskly.

Next up: Acuity.

They march us to a station that looks like a cross between an optometrist’s office and some strange carnival game. There are targets—small, shimmering glyphs on levitating metallic plates—floating at different distances and angles. A screen tracks response time and accuracy. The test is simple: identify patterns, call out symbols, track tiny movement shifts in peripheral vision, and react the instant the glyph changes.

When it’s my turn, I focus all of my energy into [Perception].

They start the test.

A glyph flickers. I call it instantly. Another shifts color. I catch it. A third tries to fake me out with a delayed change and a decoy symbol. I don’t even blink. My body reacts before my thoughts do.

The administrator’s eyebrows climb again, and a few of the candidates stare at me with… Is that jealousy?

Then: Speed and Agility.

This one’s on a rune-lined track laid out on the grass. Cones, hurdles, short walls, and a series of pressure plates embedded in the ground. The screen tracks foot placement, acceleration, deceleration, lateral changes, balance recovery… Basically every way your body can move without falling on its ass.

A whistle blows, filling the air with it’s high pitched scream.

I go, launching myself at the course with reckless abandon.

It’s not pretty, but it’s efficient. Even without the assistance of my cursed jorts. Fast cuts, low center of gravity, controlled breathing. I spring over hurdles, pivot on the balls of my feet, hit pressure plates dead-center without thinking, and recover from a forced stumble by turning into a roll and springing back onto my feet at almost a full gallop.

This time, I’m barely in the top quartile of performance. Still good, but not more on par with the field. At the end of this segment, the male administrator just nods, lips tight, and records my numbers without comment.

Then comes the final portion of the general segment of the assessment: Cognitive.

They herd us off the field and into the Chase Guild building.

The lobby is sleek and expensive, all dark tile and stainless steel accents. A corporate fortress vibe that would make my past life in finance very much at home. It’s nicer than most places I’ve ever worked… but honestly? It’s not that much nicer than Harvest Guild’s facilities. Don’t know how much I care about corporate aesthetics, but I make note of the point of comparison.

We’re escorted through security gates and down wide corridors lined with glass walls. Inside offices, I catch glimpses of people moving with purpose, some wearing Guild gear, some in suits who wouldn’t look out of place in the world before the System’s arrival.

We’re ushered into a room full of desks.

A proctor stands at the front with a stack of tablets. “Five logic games. Twenty-five questions,” she announces. “Multiple choice. Forty minutes. No communication. No Skill usage. This is a baseline cognitive assessment.”

The tablets are handed out. I take a seat.

The room falls quiet except for tapping fingers and the faint hum of HVAC.

The first question pops up and the block of text instantly elicits a groan. Someone hits me with a “Shhh!...”

Fine, I think. Let’s give this a go. Liv was always the one who liked these kinds of logic games.

I work through them steadily, not blazing fast, but not stuck either.  I think I do alright.

When the proctor calls time, I set the tablet down and exhale slowly, waiting for my score. The tablet eventually produces a number: 65.

Well, not sure what that means.

We’re herded out of the testing room and back into a hallway where the air smells like carpet cleaner and ambition. The same two administrators who ran the field tests stand there with tablets in hand and the dead-eyed confidence of people who’ve watched hundreds of applicants try to prove they matter.

“Alright,” the woman says. “Please split into your intended track.”

She points down two corridors.

“Combat-focused Mages on the left. Support-focused Mages on the right. You will undergo role-specific measurements at this time.”

A murmur ripples through the group. People shift, whisper, glance at each other like they’re trying to choose a lane on the highway right before a crash. Some candidates are clearly trying to suss out where their top competition are heading, and perhaps hoping to choose the opposite.

I consider it.

Support… I can do support, right? Like, if I really wanted to? I’ve got [Clean]. Oh, and [Light]! If I combine all of my utility spells, I’d be the most over-qualified official Guild janitor Ohio has ever seen!

Ah, who am I kidding?

My spells are fists! Utility turned into violence in magical form.

If I’m honest, I’m not built to stand behind the line. I’m built to break it. I sigh. Such is my lot in life.

I walk left with the combat group.

The hallway opens into a larger wing, and we’re taken down to an indoor combat facility that feels like a cross between a high-end sports arena and a massive containment unit. Thick, reinforced walls. Floor and ceiling lined with the same materials meant to absorb high-level System User Spells and Skills with ease. There’s a viewing platform behind a floor-to-ceiling window of enchanted glass. I can see silhouettes moving behind it—administrators, evaluators, maybe even other Guild members watching like this is entertainment.

The combat mages are brought in and then split again.

“Groups of three,” the male administrator says. “Random assignment.”

A screen flashes names and bib numbers.

The rules come next, crisp and cold. They’re accompanied by a neutral, almost feminine voice that rings over an intercom system:

“You shall participate in combat. Combat will end when the other two combatants sustain sufficient damage, at which point the combat will be announced over, or when two combatants forfeit. Failure to cease combat when announced will result in automatic disqualification of your application to join Chase Guild. Items and equipment are permitted, so long as their use does not violate any other rules of this engagement.”

Oh, I think. That last bit catches my attention. Items and equipment are permitted. I wonder how many of these other candidates are only half-baked without their gear. Even I feel absolutely nerfed without my jorts.

The field of applicants are ushered into a viewing room on the ground level, beneath the elevated viewing platform. We’re also behind reinforced, enchanted glass, though the view is definitely obstructed. There are wo groups go ahead of mine. I watch as they enter the combat room, the barrier sealing behind them with a soft whump.

Spells fly as soon as the voice over the intercom declares, “Begin!”

A massive shard of ice spears across the floor, shattering into glittering fragments. A lightning arc cracks against a shield, making the room flash white. Someone throws a gravity pulse that makes the other two stumble like they’ve been hit by a wave.

Some of these mages have power. Real power, despite their squat form saying otherwise.

But the way they move? The way they hesitate? The way they panic when someone gets inside their range, or forces them to go off script? They clearly don’t have experience.

I swallow a grin. Alright. It definitely makes me feel better about my chances.

The second group, however, is filled with three applicants who all have clearly had experience fighting other spellcasters, and probably Gate-borne monsters too. Their fight is a brutal contest of attrition, with the victor eventually being the last one standing with enough charge to still cast a spell.

My group gets called.

I step forward into the staging area with two others—a man and a woman. They both look like classic spellcasters. Robes, runed gloves… The guy’s holding a tall, wooden staff topped by a carved hawk with open wings.

They glance at me, then at my plain clothes and shirtless chest, and I see the faint, dismissive smiles.

“Careful, Conan,” the guy smirks.

The barrier opens.

I step into the combat room.

And as soon as I do, I swap into my equipment. My real clothes dematerialize in pixelated light, my athletic shorts dissolving away. In their place snap my enchanted gear: Behemoth Cap, boots, and, of course, my trousers.

The two opponents break into laughter. The woman doubles over, the man nearly drops his staff. I spare a glance towards the applicant viewing room. I can’t see or hear the reactions of the others, but I imagine it’s much of the same.

“Oh my God,” the woman says, amused. “Are those—”

The guy snorts. “This is gonna be easy. My guy’s wearing straight up Daisy Dukes!”

I just smile, calm and confident, like I’m about to start a workout set I already know I can finish.

The announcer crackles overhead, voice amplified.

“Begin.”

The woman lifts from her laughing fit and immediately launches a spinning sphere of water at my face.

I don’t flinch.

[Slimy Shield]

A blue disc snaps into existence in front of me, catching the water sphere with a wet SPLAT as it spreads and runs down the shield like rain on a windshield.

Too easy, I think, only to forced to leap back and out of range of the ice spikes formed by the busted water sphere stabbing right through my slime shield.

“Tsk.” I click my tongue, mentally kicking myself for being too confident.

Before I can even blink, the other mage—the guy—fires a ray of flame from my flank.

I duck smoothly.

The fire passes just over my head.

“Wait… Fire!” I breathe. I lift my head slightly, and my Behemoth Cap inhales the last tongues of flame.

The jet of fire had apparently been hot enough to melt the ice spikes, because when I look down I notice I’m now standing in a puddle of water. Water. Right…

“Oh fuuu—!”

The water shoots up around my legs, instantly freezing and locking me in place. I summon my Full Metal Staff just in time block a leaping attack from the guy, bringing his staff down on me. A dumb move for him to get so close. I use my Strength to break free from the ice and slam my staff into his shoulder with a loud crunch!

His body instantly turns to wooden vines that entangle my staff and stretch out to snare me in a large embrace. I dance away, only to land in the range of a torrent of water, joined by a beam of energy.

“Oh, come on!” I yell. I take the beam straight to the shoulder, letting it push me to the side. I use my momentum to roll and avoid the water blast, which slams into the floor so hard I’m sure it would have taken my skin off without issue.

Fine. If they want to make this fight two-on-one, then I’ll even the playing field.

I tap into [Multiplicity] and flex, the familiar pose snapping into my muscles like a loaded spring.

[Wizard’s Fist]

Puffs of silvery mist explode around me.

One fist.

Two.

Six.

Twelve.

A dozen spectral fists hover in the air, twitching like eager hounds straining at a leash.

The mages step back, alarm finally breaking through their amusement.

“Wait—” the guy starts.

I point.

“Get ’em.”

The fists surge forward in an angry swarm.

One fist grabs the man’s staff arm and yanks, spinning him half around. Another slams into his ribs. A third swats his legs out from under him like he’s a chair in a bar fight. The woman throws up a shimmering barrier spell—an arcane shield that crackles under the impact.

The fists pound it anyway. She tries to dodge, blinking sideways with a short-range warp. Two fists predict it and follow, forcing her back.

She tries a defensive fog next. Too bad my fists don’t use sight. A fist punches through it and plants itself in her stomach like a piston.

My Wizard’s Fists are relentless. A minute later they’re on the ground, bruised and coughing, arms up in surrender.

“I forfeit!” the woman gasps.

“Stop—stop!” the man chokes, rolling onto his side.

The announcer voice booms overhead.

“Cease!”

My fists freeze instantly, hovering like obedient monsters. I dismiss them with a silent command. Twelve spectral hands dissolve into silvery mist, vanishing like breath in cold air. The combat room suddenly feels too big without them—just me, two battered spellcasters on the floor. I walk over and reclaim my staff, pulling it back into my Inventory.

The barrier to the combat floor opens.

The evaluators behind the glass are quiet. Still shadows, looking down at my display of violence and effective brutality. I wonder what they think of me.

I’m about to step toward the exit, when a voice cuts in.

“Candidate. Wait.”

It’s the female administrator’s voice, crisp through the intercom. Not the guy, who had been the one running the combat group.

I stop mid-step, turning slowly.

“…Okay?”

The barrier door hisses open again.

The male administrator walks into the room from the applicant viewing area, abandoning his flock. His mask—that bored expression—has crumbled away, revealing a face carved with anger. And there’s something else there. Something deeper, more nuanced… Suspicion?

He looks at me like I’m a math problem that just told him to go fuck himself. He had likely built a very consistent worldview of what budding Mage candidates were supposed to look like. Then I stroll in, a hulk with a Mana score of 0, and overwhelm two very capable spell-slingers. I probably shattered everything. I know how that feels, to have your world crumble around you and leave you wondering what’s left for you to believe in.

“What kind of games are you playing?” he demands.

I blink. “Games?”

He jabs a thumb toward the two mages still on the floor. A group in Chase Guild colors have entered through a new doorway in the wall and are helping them to their feet. “

The battered man and woman are pulled to their feet, wincing, avoiding eye contact. They leave fast, trying to avoid the attention of either the administrator or myself.

The door to the applicant viewing area seals behind them. Now, it’s just me and the admin.

He stalks closer, eyes narrow. “You measured zero mana.”

“Yeah,” I say carefully. “That was—”

“And then you tore our strength stations apart like they were toys. Then you come in here and do… that.” He gestures vaguely at the empty space where my fists had been. “You’re either a fraud or you’re something we haven’t accounted for.”

I lift my hands a little, trying to disarm the situation. “I’m not trying to—”

“I want you to fight me,” he says.

I stare. “What?”

He takes another step, posture squared, like we’re in a bar and I just insulted his mother. “Fight me.”

I laugh once, awkward and disbelieving. “Sir, are you sure this is—”

“Yes.” His tone is iron. “You’re such an interesting candidate that we need to give you a different test. A proper one.”

I try to diffuse it, because the situation is absurd. “I really don’t think—”

“Fight me,” he repeats, and his voice leaves no space for argument.

I exhale. “Fine,” I say. “Okay.”

We each take a starting position on opposite ends of the small arena. And internally, I make one decision. End this fast.

The female admin’s voice returns over the intercom.

“Commence.”

The man moves instantly.

Spell symbols bloom through the air around the administator like a swarm of glowing insects—sigils stacked on sigils, complex geometry forming and breaking as he chains spell after spell. Mana gathers in streaks, compressing into projectiles, waves, lances, nets, and the forms of unfamiliar tools of war, forged from pure mana.

Magic slams toward me, tearing through the air. My eyes widen.

How the hell is he casting this many spells that quickly?

But I already had a plan of action in place before the fight even started. I trigger [Range of Motion].

The world fractures, and ten ghostly projections of me appear in silvery-blue smoke, each one weaving through the incoming spells. They’re ducking, pivoting, stepping where I haven’t stepped yet. One path ends right in the guy’s face.

I pick the last frame, and reality snaps. I’m suddenly there, bypass the mana-forged projectiles and inside his spell pattern, inside his breathing space, close enough to smell his cologne and the sharp ozone of his aura.

His eyes widen just a fraction. A moment of realization, just as I throw a right hook.

My knuckles connect with the back of his head like a hammer meeting a nail.

The impact is sickening. The admin’s face slams into the floor with a crack that isn’t just bone—it’s reinforced arena flooring breaking under the force of his face’s impact. A spiderweb of fractures spreads across the ground.

His spells die mid-air, collapsing into harmless sparks and motes of neon blue light.

He doesn’t move.

The room goes silent.

Then the female admin’s voice comes back, flat and controlled.

“That’s enough. Thank you.”

The door to the viewing area opens, just as the side doors in the walls of the arena also slide open. Chase Guild officials rush in—more than two this time. They don’t look at me. They go straight for the unconscious administrator, grab him under the arms, and drag his limp body out like they’re removing a piece of broken equipment.

I stand there for a second, staring at the cracked floor, my fist still clenched, and the faint realization settles into my gut: I may have fucked up.

When I rejoin the other applicants, they part around me like I’m a walking hazard sign. No more whispers. No more side-eye. Just… space. One thing is for certain. Even if I join the Chase Guild, I’m probably not making friends any time soon.

The remaining battles continue, but the viewing crowd is noticeably muted. Less cheering. More watching. Just waiting for the formality to be over.

Eventually, we’re dismissed back out to the field. The female administrator stands in front of the combat group again, face composed, voice amplified.

“Decisions will be made within a week,” she announces. “Those receiving offers will receive calls from the Chase Guild office, with formal invitations to follow in the mail.”

People start to disperse in clumps. A very anticlimactic end to the day.

I’m making my way out, when a voice catches my attention.

“Candidate Sullivan!”

I turn.

The female administrator is there, flanked by two officials in dark suits with subtle Guild pins.

One of the officials hands her an envelope.

She holds it out to me.

I take it automatically, like my hands are moving before my brain catches up.

“We’ve conducted an emergency consideration of your application,” she says. Her voice is still crisp, but there’s something different now. Respect? Concern? “The hiring committee made the decision to make a snap offer to you as one of our new Mages.”

I stare at the envelope.

It’s thick. Emblazoned with the official emblem of Chase Guild: an eagle soaring over a leaping hound.

My chest tightens.

I don’t say anything at first because I’m not sure what words fit in my mouth right now.

Then I swallow and manage, awkwardly, “Uh…Thank you.”

She nods once. “You’ll find all necessary details inside. Don’t hesitate to contact us if you have any questions.”

I nod back, still not committing to anything.

Then I turn and walk away. Out through the gate, into the jam-packed parking lot. I find my car and lean against it, envelope in my hand, waiting for Clyde and Veronica.

And as the sun glints off the dark glass buildings behind me, I can’t stop thinking about my new predicament.

Chase just offered me a spot… And Harvest is still waiting.

And I’m still broke as hell!
