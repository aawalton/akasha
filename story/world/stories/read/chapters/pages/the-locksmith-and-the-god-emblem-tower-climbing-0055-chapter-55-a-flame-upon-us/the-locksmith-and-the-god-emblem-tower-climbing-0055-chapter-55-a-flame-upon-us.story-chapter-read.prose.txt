"Get down!" A tall man, who for some reason was still wearing Nordic clothing, shouted. "...Now. Go!"

Manes, listening to Daniel's commands, was constantly shifting between fiercely determined and absurdly terrified. At that moment, however, he leaned more towards the courageous side. Following Daniel's quick, dense steps — known as Bodkin in his game — he failed to keep up with his speed, but tried his best.

He saw the warrior in his most glorious position; leaping with what looked like the tip of a knife in his left hand and a swarm of arrows coming from all sides. The fighter, now realizing his fatal mistake, seemed to beg for mercy with his terrified eyes.

Without hesitation, Daniel plunged the knife into the side of his left leg and knocked the man unconscious with a well-placed kick, just before Manes arrived. He looked back, containing his murderous instinct. ...Will I never be able to kill someone while someone is watching again? This is embarrassing... He wiped the blood from his mouth as Manes checked if Daniel had any serious injuries.

It's an arrow... That makes sense. Manes had noticed the strange shape of the "knife" and quickly understood what had happened. They had been fighting this man, supposedly one of the nobles' royal guards, for only a few minutes, which was enough to drive Daniel crazy; every second counted.

The unconscious man, in turn, possessed the Iceberg Emblem, strange as it seemed. He could summon and control small clumps of ice in the air, and even summon them from the ground. He didn't have as much control as Daniel with his arrows, due to the weight and density of the objects, but his strength compensated. They both knew that any blow they took from the mini-icebergs could take them out of the fight.

What the man had learned too late, however, was that he should have used them more... sparingly. Because they were heavy and difficult to control, when he made a mistake, he opened up a huge weak point around himself. This eventually tired him too much and left him easily defeated.

Daniel then fell to his knees and coughed violently; Emblems tire you out.

"Hey! Old man!" Manes said, approaching the viking, who refused his help.

"...I'm fine, I'm fine." He said as he stood up, brushing dust off his coat. "Have you found Harry yet?"

Manes looked at the battlefield below, the creature's body still covering half the arena and making a clearer view impossible. "Not yet."

"Then let's go to Brun." He said, his tone grave. "We've wasted too much time with these scoundrels. I have to make sure she's alright. I can't even imagine..."

His voice was then consumed by an eternal buzzing, along with a brutal impact from behind them, throwing them against the stands, tumbling across the hard ground. Besides sparks, they saw only ashes; the nobles' cries were also muffled.

"...Manes!" Daniel shouted, but he couldn't hear his own voice. "Boy...! I—"

He spotted the fallen youth and, propelled by his arrows, grabbed him and turned around. Another explosion followed, almost in the same spot, but smaller. Manes got up, coughing but appearing to be alright.

From within the ashes emerged a man missing a front tooth, his hair disheveled and his pants falling down. It was Carga. With an insane grin, he took each step with the patience of a fisherman on a quiet day. He clapped lightly, each clap creating a small kinetic explosion that pushed away the ashes and dusty air around him.

"Hey, don't run away!" The man chuckled. "The games have only just begun!"

"...Games?" Daniel asked, paying attention to his enemy's every move. "The games are over... buddy."

He thought that if he added "buddy" at the end, he would have a better chance of resolving this diplomatically. There was no denying it was an attempt.

"No!" he shouted, but then laughed again. "Of course not! They're just starting! I heard it from down there, it's all part of the plan, the creature, the chaos... It makes it more fun for the audience, you know? To be honest, I even like to add a little drama if it makes things more interesting..."

He really thinks this is all part of the game!? Daniel thought to himself, unsure how to convince him that none of it made sense.

"Do you see any audience here?" Daniel said, pointing around the stands. "Just stop doing... that, and let's... talk?"

"You don't even believe what you're saying." The man said, taking another step forward. "And of course they're not here, but they must be using someone else's Emblem to see everything! Some, of course, like the dynamism, and so you might see some members of the audience running around, but it's all part of the plan! I heard that... Well, I'll say it, screw it; one of the members of the royal guard has an Emblem that allows him to transmit any images to the audience."

"Look," Daniel began as Manes took another step back, prepared to flee and begging his companion to come along. "I don't know anything about nobles or royal guards, but I know this wasn't planned. Maybe your mother let you hit your head when you were a kid, but look at this damn arena. There's nothing but chaos. You understand... buddy?"

But even he knew he'd gone too far; the "buddy" wouldn't work anymore. Realizing they still had another fight to go on, Daniel sighed deeply and got into a fighting stance. He glanced sideways at Manes, who also understood the answer and moved aside, covering his body with shadows.

"Oh. Oh no..." Carga wore a terrified look as soon as Manes used his Emblem. "Y-You're the devil! You're the one who said that! That day, it was you, wasn't it?"

"...I, um..." Looking at Daniel, who also seemed confused, Manes had an idea and took another step. "Yes! It was me, um, the devil. The very one. I made you do all that and say all those things. Now, before I'm torn apart, I order you to leave us in peace and... I don't know, drown in the sand—"

"I have to break the rules." He said almost to himself as he looked at his hands. "This is my chance. I have to kill you and the patron of hell. It's even possible that you're one of his commanders, isn't it!? Damn you. You're going to leave this earth, even if I die trying!"

Shit. At the same moment, Daniel and Manes whispered to themselves in disappointment.

On the opposite side, also still at the barricades, Goldman and Vela were searching for Harry. Giving up on going after Madeline, the two were trying their best to avoid fighting. The combat, however, chose them.

"We don't want anything, mate!" Goldman shouted at a man with torn clothes and bandages wrapped around his body. As if he had just emerged from a war, scars covered his right eye, half of his hair also slightly charred.

The man ignored Goldman's pleas and turned to Vela with a curious look. "...A candelabrum?" he asked. "So that's what they had to do to copy me?"

Although both Vela and Goldman were from older times, in an era where information took longer to arrive, the tone of his voice sparked something in the former monk's mind. Perhaps it was his appearance, or his arrogant tone. Either way, it was familiar.

Vela narrowed his gaze and analyzed the wounds and appearance of his enemy. Taking a step back in surprise, the former monk almost dropped his weapon. No... I don't believe it.

After the Astronomer unleashed the "Emblem Theory" upon the world, the first Symbols used were the most common ones, such as water, wind, earth... and fire.

"Don't come near, Vela..." Goldman said to his friend. "I feel immense pressure coming from him. I don't think—"

"Ogien." The man said, his voice subtle but broken. "He has the Flame Emblem..."

"Ah, so I'm well-known, aren't I?" Ogien said, his tone emanating pomp. "Tell me, copy, how can we resolve this?"

You, here!? Vela added inwardly, perhaps afraid to speak further. The fire is as hot as it was that day...

"It's quite obvious, actually." As if reading the monk's mind, he replied, breaking his train of thought. "Apparently, the people in the Tower still hold the rights to their Emblems. This means that the power of a glorious flame can only be wielded by me... Seeing you and your candelabrum, it's clear there's a pathetic attempt to replicate my fire. For having this audacity, you must at least have the confidence that your flame is stronger than mine, right? That's why I asked; how can we resolve this?"

"You're very confident for someone who's imprisoned here," Goldman said, taking a step forward.

"...Is that how you see this place? As a prison?" The man said, opening his arms and smiling, his face as scarred as an ancient tree. "No, no... It's a blessing, don't you see? An attempt to harbor power the world wasn't prepared for. This place is the future! Don't you see those nobles? They came here to take refuge... That should tell you how bad the world is out there."

"You're literally fighting in an arena... in a Colosseum," Goldman replied, a hint of anger escaping his words. "I can assure you that most of the prisoners here would rather be out there—"

"That..." He closed his eyes and clenched his fists, as if trying to restrain himself. "There are no prisoners. There are only those whom the Emblems have chosen, and the rest. There is only us and them, understand? I see, however, that you are not prepared for the Emblems that belong to you..."

Vela, all this time, held tightly to his candelabrum while watching small flames emanate from Ogien's wrists. No... Please no. He ruminated as his mind was drowned in disturbing screams and a dense air. No. It was smoke, black smoke. Roofs collapsing and windows foaming with death. The place falling to pieces; the monastery.

"Enough!" Vela shouted to himself, startling even Goldman. "You can't even handle your own flames! Look at your face, you worm. A tough skin for a man with a heart of stone, inflexible. Emblems are parasites, and I see you've already entered into symbiosis with yours."

"Vela, what are you—"

"You think you know what it's like to feel heat, huh?!" Vela took a step forward and pointed his candelabrum at the man who seemed to growl with rage. "Well, I do! I've seen more than you'd see in a thousand lifetimes. No, you're not worthy of this Emblem... If you want, I can forge your soul and leave it with a scar. It would suit your cracked face."

Goldman, startled by Vela's behavior, could see that this was a matter of great importance to his colleague. Swallowing his surprise, Goldman remained confident as he dropped a weight from his back; one of his metal plates twisted from a previous combat. But it will never be as heavy as yours, will it, Vela? He observed as small tears fell down his cheeks.

I hope you burn away that sin you carry, Vela. Just don't forget that even a small flame can cause a fire.
