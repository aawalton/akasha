Time marched on for the Samanthian realm, and soon enough, its armies marched, too. Samantha was perpetually busy just managing things, or as she typically thought of it, ‘helping Jeeves manage things.’ In Itha Ranon, her growing expertise and talent for magic allowed her something she cherished when she got the opportunity to do it: tutor. Furthermore, she found herself in the groove of creating in-house quests for others to accomplish such things as completed rituals, rare component acquisition, and spell designs.

The last thing, she was particularly often consulted on. As the unusual high-level [Magineer] class, she was practically the foremost authority on the subject of custom spells on Calrenazzod. The rapt attention of students to her ‘pearls of wisdom’ was just a little embarrassing to see, and made her careful not to screw around quite as much as she was used to. Wondering about theories aloud was also something she had to watch.

In any case, this combination of things accrued minor quest completions and simpler credits, most often toward [Magineer], which was a damned good thing considering how close she was getting to leveling it up.

She was even more proud of what she earned for her time spent with students who needed her. It was a badge of honor, and echoed across time and past lives. It felt like a special destiny, but not like the world-shaking events of Fate, no. This path was entirely optional. Something she chose, again and again. In this world, it seemed unlikely she’d reach the sort of culmination she’d reached in others. But it still felt damned good.

Teaching

You’re good at teaching others what you know. Add +1 Effective Levels to your total value for these purposes, and your students learn 10% faster than normal while you’re dedicating your time to them.

Sadly, this relative joy was all too rare with a war to wage. Consistently, she was interrupted out of the warm, comforting embrace of the relatively mundane, to zip back to Heavenpeak to unravel some puzzle and make a snap decision related to logistics, supplies, or troop movements. Most of the time, this was just picking from the options presented to her by arguing sides. Nothing could make Jeeves on the same authority level as her, so this was perpetually a big role for her, even if she knew what Jeeves would do and agreed. She was also far more diplomatic about making the selections.

Sometimes, diplomacy was the entire task, such as when some of the touchier minotaurs were arguing — politely but insistently — about their roles and placements, with equally stubborn commanders. The order needed some powerful guards for such things as protecting supply runs coming up after the army, and one single minotaur champion was golden for that for a number of reasons. Despite this, a handful were balking about staying behind, even for the ‘river run’ period of the march. Thankfully, not all were so picky, so she smoothed things over by giving the gloryhounds what they craved and replacing them with their humbler counterparts.

Similar crap cropped up among the Naugites constantly, who often used primitive duels to solve things. She was not having that shit, not with what was on the line now. Bad things could happen in duels; very bad. In any case, her involvement was substitution enough.

Clout and authority really were something else. Every time the [Goddess] showed up in the situation, bringing the literal ‘divine decision,’ things got resolved amiably, sometimes even with scowls and insults turning into smiles and reconciliation. She strongly questioned whether it would’ve all held together without a non-hothead figurehead around to manage prides and tempers. Several times, she wanted to lose it right along with them, because of the sheer stupidity of this or that asinine problem… but she always refrained. She understood her role.

I’m practically a glorified nanny sometimes! Yeesh. I wish I were Rallindin. I could just glare at them, and they’d scamper away in shame with their tails between their legs, to even bother me with nonsense.

Things held together with her particular style of things, in any case. The actual march to ensue would almost be a relief compared to the complexities of the lead-up.

Soon enough, her ballooning realm recruitment netted her a long-awaited quest completion.

The quest “Major Deity: Pillar of Society” is complete. 5000 FE gained. +20,000 Maximum FE added. 14(10) exp gained in [Goddess]. [Immortal Coil] added as a Special Trait.

New Quest unlocked: “Major Deity: Key Icon” — Reach 22,222 total Followers.

● Rewards: +10000 FE, +80,000 Maximum FE, +12 exp in [Goddess], [Assurance] upgraded.

Righteous! Goddess is only going to get harder to level. That’s a pretty good start toward 12. Now, let’s see about the new Special Trait…

Immortal Coil

You have innate supremacy in your very being, against those lesser than you, in terms of divinity. Add +4 Effective Levels to any contest or comparison against a mere mortal, +3 Effective Levels against a lesser spirit, supernatural entity, or ‘immortality obtained’ mortal, +2 against greater spirits or similar immortal entities, and +1 against demigods.

Samantha’s eyebrows rose. Did that mean she had a bonus against the Sage? It had to. He was not a god or demigod. It seemed he was either within the +3 or the +2 category, depending on exactly what he’d accomplished for himself.

Tightening the gap on him was really good news, and it was spectacular news against everyone else lesser than him. This was gain on most of her enemies. She had to have eclipsed most of them, even in basic comparisons, by now. Tracking every variable was difficult, but at [Magineer 13] and [Goddess 11], she was Effective Level 17 as the barest minimum, and this bonus climbed that to 20 or 21 against most feasible threats, offensively and defensively. Likely, at a minimum, some collection of bonuses would make true offensive and defensive minimums ten over that. Her mental and spiritual defense, specifically, was already ten over that. Her caster levels or lethality grades were pretty damn close to the same under most situations.

Feasibly, any given high-level Dominion bozo was maybe 17-18 in [Wizard], and something highly variable in their second class, which was designed to support [Wizard]. Presumably, they could get it to something like 14-15. With multiclassing, that would be a base of 21-23. Maybe the best of them were at 24-25, she’d guess. At most, a few could exist like that. There would be all sorts of bonuses to worry about on top of it — including offensive specialists such as would exist in Matrazere among the war wizards — but she was certainly in-range.

She’d already been, if her enemies’ reliance on circles or giant summons to deal with her during the battle for Rivermount was any clue. It was going to be even worse for Matrazere. They’d throw everything and the kitchen sink at her. Their best would be waiting, barring only the Sage himself and his protege. The Inquisitors would strike from afar… She wouldn’t be rid of them, either, not until completely breaking everything for good in the Capital. Unless they would abandon it out of fear? She wasn’t certain. A matter for another time, in any case.

Fresh with the morning dew, the armies of Rivermount marched upriver en masse, everything under an altogether very expensive enchantment system to move faster. So they began the extensive, grueling, but largely sure task of taking the fortresses that dotted the approach to Matrazere. The immediate fortress, Swiftpoint, was arrived at without issue in the morning, as they’d already occupied it.

Skirmishing had pushed well ahead of this point, and the next significant fortress, arrived at by evening, fell rather easily. Too easily. As they copiously scanned and searched it, the whole fortress blew up spectacularly, succeeding in more casualties than were dealt in taking the damned place, including a merc wizard that had not properly protected himself.

While he wasn’t super-essential, it was annoying. When it was discovered he’d been an alcoholic who was drunk the whole time, new rules were made about inebriation while the campaign was on. Dax didn’t like it and told her it was pointless because it would just get sneaked around, but whatever. Any degree it cut things down was better.

As intended, the armies pushed on through the evening, once again relying on mass enchantments at copious expense, to reduce fatigue. Nonetheless, after delays from heavy skirmishing and harrying from mobile hit-and-run specialists of the Dominion, they made camp in the night, utilizing semi-conventional sleep medications to deep-sleep through the explosions and such in the distance. They relied mostly on Servitors, spirits, and minotaurs to keep the fighting pushed away from the main camp. As such, there were few casualties except on the Dominion side.

Samantha assisted directly here and there, primarily to help move troops around for a sudden overwhelming force from unexpected angles. But she knew that they would have nothing capable of threatening her much out here, and besides that, she drenched herself in every protection, always had backup, and did not expend any auto-successes for anything, giving her a huge amount of failsakes. This meant she could go hog on ‘default offense,’ and so she did.

She roasted mounted flyers and gargoyles, even the occasional wizard, though the latter usually just burned through defenses, contingencies, and the like, to beat a retreat before she could end them. Usually, but not always. She counted a handful of wizard deaths thanks to shredding through defenses quickly enough.

She essentially threw continual monkey wrenches in their plots to use cheap wizardly tactics. She was a hard counter against that, especially from the mid-tier expendable losers they were willing to field, so she exploited the dominance for all it was worth.

However, she didn’t want to get too predictable or allow them to expect her to be here or there, lest they forge a plot to try another assassination. She didn’t think they could pull it off, but there was also no point in pushing super hard and risking it just to wipe out a few more expendables. She tried to stay unpredictable.

If she were honest, she just wanted to play around with new spells and power levels. The boost from her new trait was exceptionally noticeable, as she was bullying everyone she faced. They just couldn’t deal with her offense now.

Meanwhile, the other armies from Burnstire practically raced to make for Matrazere in sequence with the river-route forces. As expected, they were eventually slowed by intercepting Dominion skirmishers, but these were severely outclassed by the mobile Daenriar trolls and specialist ranger-knights. The Dominion, having grossly underestimated them, quickly retreated from an initial attempt, giving the marching army a great pace that would allow some leeway in otherwise tight timing across the varied, sometimes difficult terrain. It was rough for mounts, in particular.

And so the advance went largely the same, for the next couple of days through the foothills. A gruelling hell of attrition, but they kept the losses negligible. Hopefully, the flatter terrain would speed things up considerably going forward.
