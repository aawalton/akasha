The auxiliary tower was something much easier to load up with a spell and then unleash in the field, at-will, at full strength. Five seconds casting, that was all. After she forged the mana portal, it didn’t even need her further action economy, as one of the secondary stone users could execute it through the crown. The entire tower would essentially be weaponized in the field, which was a tad scary.

Might’ve been a good thing that the old wizard king discarded this thing and stayed home. That guy on the loose, especially in his maddened state, wouldn’t have been pretty.

The trouble with the auxiliary tower was that she didn’t know how to make it, and the old, quickly deteriorating and fading king had already been tapped on it, to no avail. He didn’t even remember the thing, much less how it was fashioned.

Maybe now that memory is restored? Eventually? Eh. Probably not.

Jeeves had already gone far and wide to check various entities about the matter, including the old king, as well as Redberry, dark fae, and various known spirits. If there was any current effect, it was tiny. It made sense, after all — damage done was damage done. All Glae had done was stop further deterioration. New memories were needed, making the whole thing a symbolic victory more than anything, at least based on what she knew.

To Glae, the world might not be ending soon. He’d done something aimed at helping the long-term future generations. He cultivated young heroes like Hyginos with care and mindfulness. Despite the debuff to the Sages thrown in, she believed the memory thing was wholly personal to him. He wanted to restore the giants, who’d been among the most regressed of all peoples due to what the Sage did.

The thought made Samantha sigh. She could see the perspective he’d come to in the days ahead, now that the harrowing, all-consuming ritual was behind him, his obsession blinding him…

All of my allies see things the same way: we herald the end. Regardless of our mutual foe and the necessity of vanquishing him, Glae is not going to be on board with an apocalypse. He has his own designs for the future, and his head, well… it’s just not in the game. Or too much so, I suppose.

In that way, he was similar to the Sage. It felt like he’d backstab her more than ever before. Her guess was currently that it would be too late, after she had already done what she needed to do, and finally defeated her nemesis. Possibly with his help.

But what if it wasn’t true? He could fuck everything up.

The definitive answer eluded her senses and intuition like nothing else. It had even in the beginning, when she’d first laid eyes on ‘Devero’ and knew absolutely nothing about him. Even then, the uncertainty and ambiguity were there. The desire to keep him close and the desire to kill him, wedded like night and day, making for a perpetual twilight. It was there in the future, linked to Fate, just as it was lying in the past. It was simply them.

But there was nothing to do about it. If she wanted to be ‘pragmatic’ enough to strike him while he was weak, she could have, but it flat-out wasn’t the answer — not backstabbing him before he did it to her. It just wasn’t correct, and if she’d even tried, she’d probably have locked up, or even puked on herself in revulsion and totally ruined it.

Their ambiguity had to be ridden out to the end, whatever his wrong-headed perspective, and this wasn’t the time. There was more to do, more road to cover before that could be true. The time and place of the ritual couldn’t be it, not ever.

Not in the midst of proving what magnificence we could achieve together. It proves something. That something must remain whole and sacred in this time. How could I call myself the Goddess of Fate if I can’t even do that?

Aside from those realities, the ‘opportunity’ had been on Glae’s home turf. Not so pragmatic to attack him there.

Casting aside the intrusive, dark thoughts on Glae, Samantha finally took a deep breath and smiled at her current happy place: the crown. It was time to put it on.

It signified accomplishment, or a ‘stage reached,’ like nothing else — not even rewards from the system.

Ozra’s third door. It’s ready.
