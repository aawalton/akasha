Longclaw had just spent hours going over the theropod deity’s burgeoning business affairs and was ready for a break.  Rising slowly from her desk, she emerged from the office she shared with her boss and made her way over to her preferred spot in the living room.  Grimfalk was napping on the couch, having fallen asleep while watching a feed titled “The 10 Best Undersea Battles”.  On the screen, a swarm of sea monkeys engaged in a pitched battle against a bloom of pteropods, more commonly known as “sea butterflies”.  Oddly enough, the background music was quite calming.

Settling into her chair, the theropod watched as the sea monkeys fought with savagery, attempting to close the distance between the two groups with sudden bursts of speed.  Once they latched on to a sea butterfly, they used their modified antennae, empowered by Skills and Special Abilities, to pierce through the heavily armored pteropods.  The sea butterflies, on the other hand, remained in a tight formation, deploying nets of poisonous mucus that hindered their enemies’ movement while draining away vitality.

Longclaw took a long sip from a Stoat Water as she watched the battle unfold.  At first glance, the sea butterflies were clearly on top.  Relatively few had fallen victim to the ravaging sea monkeys, while thousands of the latter were stuck in mucus, either dead or too weakened to fight any longer.  But looks could be deceiving.  As the two groups continued to drift through the ocean, more and more sea monkeys were left behind.  Eventually, they were far enough away from the battle that the System determined they were no longer in combat, allowing them to receive an influx of Skills and levels.

A few of them developed a resistance to the mucus.

That was the beginning of the end for the sea butterflies, as they had no answer to the small but growing number of sea monkeys that were able to evade or ignore the nets.  It would take a long time for the scales to finish tipping, but Longclaw had seen enough to know the outcome.  The sea monkeys would eventually overwhelm the sea butterflies, so long as another party did not intervene.

Longclaw had no wish to watch a massacre, so she picked up the remote and was about to change the channel.  A snort from her left surprised her so greatly that she jumped out of her chair, sending the remote clattering to the floor.  She shot an annoyed glance over at the source of the noise: Grimfalk.  The larger theropod’s head was thrown back and his mouth gaped open as he napped, revealing his dagger-like teeth and enormous tongue.

Despite still feeling annoyed, Longclaw couldn’t help but chuckle to herself.  The other theropod was clearly dreaming.  Grimfalk was mumbling nonsense in his sleep, his stubby arms waving around enthusiastically.  Despite her keen hearing, Longclaw couldn’t make out exactly what her deity was saying, but it sounded like he was giving advice.  She smiled to herself as she retrieved the remote and looked up at the screen, ready to change the channel to something a bit more suited to her taste.  The sea butterflies had finally broken ranks, attempting to scatter in every direction to escape certain death.  Small knots of sea monkeys were chasing them down one at a time, moving swiftly to mop up the battle.  Then an enormous whale moving faster than a pre-integration torpedo zipped through the area, catching both the sea monkey and the sea butterflies in its baleen plates, which had been upgraded from flat, immobile structures to rounded cylinders that continually ground against each other.  Both sides were equally pulped by the whale, providing nourishment to its body and a small amount of progress towards its next level.

Longclaw snorted and shook her head as she changed the channel, which was on a commercial break.  The abrupt transition from the soft background music to the loud, obnoxious jingle touting customized Quest rewards caused Grimfalk to stir in his sleep again.

“Just…just follow your heart.  Wait…” he mumbled.

Grimfalk was having the strangest dream.  In it, Charlemagne, the Champion who had turned his back on his Patron’s genetic heritage, had appeared to ask him for advice.  The very temerity of it all should have filled the theropod deity with rage, but, for some reason, Grimfalk found that he was inclined to take the request seriously.

The rooster appeared before his Patron in a strange new form.  Even Grimfalk, who was strong enough to defeat the strongest mortals with nothing more than a sneeze, couldn’t help but notice both the power and the potential that the avian now exuded.  Also, Charlemagne was practically on fire, which was pretty cool, no matter how much the deity tried to deny it.

But the most interesting thing about Charlemagne was that he had an enormous spear of ice sticking out of his torso.  The thing was massive and radiated a strange mana signature.  It was also clearly the reason that the rooster had contacted him in the first place.

“Bawk,” Charlemagne said, unnecessarily indicating the spear in his chest.

“Yeah, that looks like it hurts,” Grimfalk responded.  “Don’t most mortals die when that happens?”

The rooster shrugged.

“I’m pretty sure they do,” the theropod deity continued.  “In fact, why aren’t you dead?  Wait, maybe you are dead, and that’s why you’re in my dream?  Huh, can you get a Skill that lets you invade other creatures’ dreams?  That would be cool!”

“Bawk!” the rooster interjected.

“Hmmm, so you want my advice on what to do?  Well, talking is free, and I’m feeling generous today.  Plus, to be honest, you’ve been a pretty decent Champion, despite not following in my footsteps.  But don’t tell anyone…if you do, I’ll deny it!”

Charlemagne said nothing.

“So,” Grimfalk continued, “tell me about your situation, and let good ol’ Grimfalk see what he can do.”

“Bawk, bawak, bawwk,” Charlemagne explained.

“Wow her powers seem totally broken!” the deity exclaimed when the rooster had finished going over his fight with the Empress of the Forest in detail.  “Not only did she somehow use earth magic to meld into the ground, she also bypassed your mana shields.  That’s not supposed to happen!”

“Bawk,” the rooster agreed.

“So what are you going to do about it?” Grimfalk asked.

“Bawk?” Charlemagne inquired in response, once again indicating the icy spike impaled through his torso.

“Yeah, but like, can’t you just ignore it?”

“Bawk?”

“Yeah, I mean like, if that had happened to me as a mortal, I totally would be dead.  But you’re not dead yet, are you?”

The rooster took a moment to consider that statement before finally responding.

“Bawk.”

“Exactly!  You’re still alive, so that must mean there’s something you can do about your situation.  Now, let’s see if we can figure out what that ‘something’ is.  Tell me everything you’ve gained since you left Earth.  Actually, no, first tell me where you are and why you’re there!  And what happened with the canine and the human that were with you?  I just heard that they were back on Earth.  Is that pangolin still with you, or did he die?  And then tell me everything that happened to you since you left your farm.  After all that, tell me about your new powers so we can figure out how to get you out of this jam.”

Charlemagne thought furiously, trying to find a starting place that would make sense.

“And hurry up!  I want to get back to my nap.”

The demand opened the floodgates, and Charlemagne explained everything as quickly as he could.

“Bawk bawak bawwk bawk, bawwwwk, bawak, bawk bawk bawk, bawk bawwwak bawk buwk bawwk, bawk, buck-cawk!”

When the rooster finished, Grimfalk’s head was spinning.  There was a whole multiverse out there, some of which possessed a System and some didn’t.  A host of questions had sprung up on the heels of that revelation.  If the System had taken Charlemagne from Earth and placed him somewhere else, where he competed against other beings from different universes that also had a System, then that likely meant that the System on Earth was the same System elsewhere.  And if that were true, then it stood to reason that…

Grimfalk shook his head and refocused on the task at hand.  His Champion needed his assistance.  He couldn’t afford to spend time or brainpower thinking about such trifles.

“All right, so you have a new type of Skill called a ‘physique’, and the name of your Skill is ‘Inferno’s Embrace’?  Huh, I guess it stands to reason, since you somehow acquired the Skill ‘Pyro’.  I mean, I heard some legends back in the day about a dinosaur that could shoot fire, but that seemed to be an exaggeration.  Now, I recently learned about the bombardier beetle, when one of them cooked…well it’s not important how I learned of them.  Those would probably get the Pyro skill.  But I have no idea why you obtained that Skill.  Maybe it was a byproduct of getting burnt to metaphorical cinders by that amoeba guy?”

“Bawk,” Charlemagne begged.

“Right, on to the point.  If your body has literally been transformed by fire, can’t you, I don’t know, create body parts out of flame?  Or maybe even mana?  Like, think of the possibilities!  You have a Core that stores mana right?  What if you made a heart that pumped both blood and mana…out of mana?  You could attach it to your Ember Core…it still is an Ember Core, right?”

“Bawk,” the rooster confirmed before adding a much sadder, “Bawwwk.”

“Well, yes, the spear is a bit of an issue, isn’t it.  But just follow your heart.  That’s what I always…wait, sorry, bad analogy for the moment.  Just trust your instincts.  This ice, it’s just cold right?  Just make your fire burn hotter than the cold of the ice.”

“Bawk?” Charlemagne asked.

“Well, yeah.  In the end, I think that fire is always going to be equal to or better than ice, at least in the short run.  What is ice, really?  It’s just water that has lost enough heat energy to become solid.  That’s all.  Ice slows you down, and it can kill you slowly over time.  But it preserves as it does so…it fights change with a ferocity that few can match.  But fire, fire is a whole different story.  Fire is flashy, fire kills quickly, and it changes anything it touches.  Fire destroys and leaves nothing but charred husks behind!  Like that time that…oh no…no no…I don’t want to think about that right now.

Charlemagne watched in confusion as his Patron wrestled with the traumatic memory of all his followers across the world dying: most due to the fiery explosion that flash fried the prehistoric Earth, and the stragglers to the seemingly endless ice age that had followed it.  The internal struggle, while intense, thankfully lasted only a few moments.  Then the enormous predator’s eyes flashed with a strange emotion, and he straightened himself to his full height.

“Look, you’ve not even begun to explore just how strong you truly are, Charlemagne.  So what if you’ve got that toothpick shoved through you.  Marshal your inner fire, melt the ice, and reform your body so you can continue fighting.  You are my Champion!  When I was a mortal, I was wounded many times during hunts, or when fighting over mates, but I never stopped fighting!  And even after I became a deity, I never gave up.  Not even when all my followers died, Longclaw and I never gave up…we…we found a way to survive all those millions of years, even when the other dino deities gave up and passed on.  I still miss Saurion, Ceras, Hadrian, and even Steggo, but I had to find a way to keep going on my own.”

Charlemagne felt new courage flood his heart as Grimfalk urged the young rooster to keep trying.  Despite the heavy weight of the icy spear through his chest and the accompanying numbness that threatened to overwhelm him, Charlemagne puffed up his torso and flapped his wings before crowing defiance against the world.  Then, he suddenly vanished.

Grimfalk startled himself awake with a snort.

“I’m awake, I’m awake!” he yelled.

“Oh, good, your snoring was getting a bit overwhelming,” Longclaw remarked from her spot.  “I’m bored…can we do something that doesn’t involve napping or watching a stream?  Dealing with all your non-gambling business gives me a headache.”

The theropod deity heaved his bulk up and offered Longclaw one of his claws.

“Of course, why don’t we go for a hunt together?  It’s been far too long since I’ve had a chance to stretch my legs.  I don’t want to get rusty.”

As the pair made their way over to the hunting grounds, intent on terrorizing whatever they could find, Grimfalk’s subconscious churned furiously.  There was something about his dream that seemed important, but he couldn’t remember any details beyond it involving Charlemagne in some way.  His conscious mind wasn’t too concerned, however.  Hunting, even simulated hunting, always made him feel better.
