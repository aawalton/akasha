As soon as General Valen’s transport cleared the airspace, the sirens started howling.

It was a synchronized wail that tore through the bruised twilight. On the ground, within the massive, temporary airfield constructed near the Crawler build sites, the klaxons were deafening. Above, inside the cavernous hangars of The Aegis, The Vengeance, and The Retribution, the same frantic, mechanical scream echoed off the bulkheads.

It was a full-scale scramble.

The Cinderfall infantry, shivering in their camps across the valley, looked up. They watched as the sky above our lines filled with metal.

First came the Wyverns and Strike Fighters, launching from the carriers in a blur of blue-white thrust, forming up into tight, predatory squadrons. Then came the dark, lumbering shapes of the Centurion C-52 bombers ascending from the ground bases.

They flew straight over the Cinderfall vanguard.

The Hegemony soldiers watched the armada pass overhead, a cold dread settling in their stomachs. They knew in their guts what it meant. The mechanical demons they feared most were turning their fangs on the heartland.

We held unquestioned aerial dominance. I intended to push that advantage until it broke them. All Cinderfall could do was sit in the mud and watch the carnage fly past.

Tes had spent the night mapping out a network of strategic bombing runs targeting the arteries of the Hegemony's war machine.

We targeted key choke points. A bridge over a deep gorge in the southern mountains was vaporized by a P-2 Phantom, severing a critical supply route. A mountain pass, essential for the movement of heavy siege equipment, was collapsed by a focused C-52 strike, burying the road under thousands of tons of rock. We hit their logistical hubs, incinerating hoarded rations and turning stockpiled magical crystals into smoking craters.

The goal was simple. Delay. Force their reinforcements to take the long way around. Starve the army already at my doorstep.

The Cinderfall commanders on the ground didn't know I was keeping casualties to a minimum. They just saw the sky catch fire behind them. They panicked.

They decided to move.

On the tactical feed, I watched as the red icons of the Cinderfall vanguard shifted. They began forming up into the old, traditional shield walls. Small, tight clusters of infantry—five or six men at a time—raised heavy iron shields, interlocking them to create a rudimentary, moving bunker. Behind them, spearmen crouched low, preparing to thrust through the gaps.

They began a slow, cautious advance into no-man's land.

"They're testing the waters," Nyxia noted, leaning over the console. "Seeing if we'll engage with infantry."

"Let them test," I replied, watching the lead groups creep forward.

The first squad crossed the invisible boundary of the three-kilometer kill zone.

The ground beneath them didn't care about their shields.

A smart-mine registered the foreign mana signature of a Cinderfall boot. The firing pin engaged silently. The soldier stepped down, shifting his weight. He lifted his foot to take the next step.

The mine detonated.

A focused, directional blast of kinetic force sent a sheet of steel ball bearings ripping upward at supersonic speeds.

The impact shredded the soldiers' legs and lower torsos before the sound even reached their ears. The shield wall collapsed. The men were thrown backward, their armor offering zero protection against the upward trajectory of the shrapnel.

Another squad triggered a mine a few yards away. Then another.

Within seconds, the tentative advance turned into a screaming, bloody mess. A probing force of several thousand men was halted and decimated before they had even crossed a hundred yards into the kill zone.

The Cinderfall lines recoiled. Combat mages were hastily pushed to the front. They began casting detection spells, their hands glowing as they swept magical resonance fields over the ground, searching for the traps.

I watched them work on the high-resolution feed. A cold smile touched my lips.

They won't find them.

Their spells were designed to look for magical signatures—the hum of a loaded rune, the latent energy of a conventional magic trap. The detonators on the Claymores and smart-mines were strictly mechanical and microcircuit-based, technology pulled directly from Earth. The magical element—the explosive core—remained completely inert and shielded until the physical trigger was engaged. To the Cinderfall mages, the ground looked perfectly normal.

Frustrated, the commanders tried a different tactic. They ordered heavy wooden supply barrels brought to the front. They filled them with mud and rock, intending to roll them across the field to trigger the mines safely.

"Do they really think I'm going to let them play bowling in my minefield?" I muttered.

The heavy barrels began their slow, lumbering roll across the ash.

They made it exactly fifty yards before the automated defense network decided it had seen enough.

The rapid-fire suppression turrets, sitting squat and ugly behind our berms, spooled up. The rotating barrels blurred.

BRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRT.

Streams of azure plasma tore across the valley floor. The heavy, mud-filled barrels were shredded into sawdust and baked earth in a fraction of a second. The covering fire continued for a few seconds longer, chewing up the dirt just ahead of the Cinderfall lines, a very loud, very clear warning.

"Amusing," Nyxia said, a rare, genuine laugh escaping her.

The Cinderfall formation changed again. They realized brute force and rolling barrels wouldn't work. They integrated the mages directly into the small shield squads.

They advanced again. This time, the mages projected shimmering, golden domes of force around each small group, protecting them from the turret fire.

"Tes," I commanded. "Hold the mine detonations. Let's play with their heads."

If every step triggered an explosion, they would simply stop and try to find another way. But if the detonations were random—if one squad walked a mile unharmed while the squad next to them was vaporized—the psychological toll would be devastating. Even if we ran out of mines, the soldiers would second-guess every single step they took for the rest of the war.

The rapid-fire turrets opened up on the advancing groups. The unshielded squads were chewed apart instantly. The mage shields held. The golden domes flickered under the plasma hail, but the mages gritted their teeth and maintained the barriers, pushing slowly forward.

"They're adapting," Nyxia observed.

"Then we escalate," I replied.

The automated defense network shifted its priority targeting. The massive Fortress Breaker cannons, sitting silent behind the main line, hummed to life.

Unlike the rapid-fire turrets, these weapons didn't spray plasma. The long, heavy barrels began to glow with a deep, furious blue light, charging a massive, localized capacitor.

VWOOM.

A single, concentrated beam of raw, azure energy lanced across the valley. It was a spear of light as thick as a tree trunk.

It struck the leading mage shield dead center.

The golden dome shattered like cheap glass. The beam punched through the barrier, through the mage, and through the knights cowering behind him, leaving nothing but a smoking, glassed trench in the dirt.

Another cannon fired. Then another. The shielded squads were systematically targeted and obliterated.

Every attempt Cinderfall made was pathetic. They were throwing medieval tactics at a modern defense grid.

"Tes," I said, leaning back in my chair. "Ping Commodore Theramore. Have him fire a volley from the main batteries."

...

On the bridge of The Axiom, Theramore cracked his knuckles.

"Finally," he grunted, pointing a scarred finger at the tactical display where the order from The Aegis had just flashed. "The Lord Commander remembers I have guns."

Trisa suppressed a sigh. "Targeting parameters, Commodore?"

"Aim for the back lines," Theramore ordered. "The command tents. The supply depots. The places where the fat lords are sitting and drinking their wine while the poor bastards in the mud get shot to pieces."

He grabbed the comms receiver. "All destroyers, this is the Commodore. Spin up the main railguns. I want a coordinated, staggered volley. Show them what real thunder sounds like."

The twenty-seven destroyers of the 3rd Fleet reacted instantly. The massive railguns on their foredecks swiveled, elevating their barrels toward the distant shoreline.

They fired.

The sound was a rolling, continuous crack of thunder that shook the ocean itself. Twenty-seven solid adamantium slugs, accelerated to a fraction of the speed of sound, tore across the sky.

They flew in a devastating, parabolic arc. The Cinderfall mages on the shore, frantically trying to track the incoming projectiles, found it impossible to cast deflecting shields in time against weapons moving at that velocity.

The slugs slammed into the Cinderfall rear guard.

The impact was cataclysmic. The heavily warded command bunkers, designed to withstand sustained magical bombardment, survived the initial kinetic shock, though their golden shields flickered dangerously close to collapse.

The unwarded supply tents, the reserve infantry camps, the staging areas were decimated. The earth erupted in massive geysers of dirt and shattered equipment.

It wasn't a total wipeout. Compared to the fifty million soldiers in the valley, the casualties were a drop in the bucket. The message was clear though.

Do not toy with us.

Theramore lowered his binoculars. He adjusted his collar, a look of profound boredom settling over his features. "Send a message to engineering. I think barrel number four on the Vanguard is firing a fraction of a second late. Tell them to tighten it up."

...

Back in the CIC, the tactical map flashed with a new, urgent warning.

A small cluster of red icons had detached from the main Cinderfall force. Approximately thirty thousand troops. They were moving west, attempting to scale the steep, treacherous mountain ridge that bordered the kill zone. It was a flanking maneuver, a desperate attempt to bypass the automated defenses and strike at the Crawler construction sites from the rear.

"They found a goat path," Nyxia noted, her eyes narrowing.

"They think they did," I corrected.

I tapped the console, opening a channel to the Air Force commander.

"Captain Ivela. We have a flanking force attempting to scale the western ridge. Grid Four-Charlie. Erase them."

The Vengeance, hovering high in the stratosphere, responded immediately. A flight of four P-2 Phantoms and a dozen Wyverns broke formation, diving toward the mountain ridge.

The Cinderfall troops, exhausted from the climb, heard the scream of the engines only seconds before the plasma rained down. There was nowhere to run on the narrow mountain pass. The bombing run triggered massive rockslides, burying the survivors under thousands of tons of stone.

The flanking maneuver was crushed before it even began.

...

Cinderfall seemed to finally get the message. The probing attacks stopped. The valley grew quiet, save for the crackle of localized fires and the groans of the dying.

They had an army of millions, including tens of thousands of archers. They could loose enough arrows to blot out the sun. There is a physical limit to how far an arrow can fly. They would just land harmlessly in the empty, ash-covered expanse of no-man's land, miles short of our trenches.

In the Cinderfall command redoubt, panic was setting in.

I watched the live feed from the fly-drone on the ceiling.

A heavy-set general slammed his fist onto the table, his face purple with rage and fear. "It feels like the enemy knows every move we are about to make before we even make it!" he shouted. "We cannot flank! We cannot charge! We are cattle being led to the slaughter!"

He wasn't wrong.

I had a live feed from their most secure strategy meeting. The Oracle gave me a bird's-eye view of every single soldier on the battlefield. I had complete, uncontested aerial dominance, and artillery that could strike from beyond the horizon.

Modern warfare relies on information and technology.

It was time to welcome the Hegemony to the new world.

"Tes," I said, my eyes fixed on the panicked faces of the Cinderfall generals on the screen. "Send orders to Commodore Theramore. Have him fire a second and third volley. Stagger the timing. Keep them on their feet."

I turned away from the screen.

"Let's see how long their morale holds when they can't even sleep without the earth shaking."
