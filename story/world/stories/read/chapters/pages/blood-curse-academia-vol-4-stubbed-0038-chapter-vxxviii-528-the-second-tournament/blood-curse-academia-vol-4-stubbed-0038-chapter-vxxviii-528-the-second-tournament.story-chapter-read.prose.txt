Chapter V.XXVIII (5.28) - The Second Tournament Challenge

Kizu’s feet touched down and he steadied himself, taking in his surroundings. He was in Professor Grove’s astronomy tower. Stacks of pillows were to either side and the ceiling was lit up with constellations.

An older Tainted student to his side collapsed on the floor and slammed a fist against the carpet. The impact shook the entire tower.

“They can’t be serious! The professors are hunting us? We’re meant to just scurry about and hide like rats from a cat? What does this prove? It’s the opposite of combat!” the boy loudly ranted.

Like Kizu, his fellow student wore an academy uniform. Unlike Kizu, said uniform was bursting at the seams as it strained to contain his muscular form. Blond sideburns grew on either side of his face accompanied by crescents of golden scales. It took Kizu a second to place the young man. It was one of Harvey’s pals. The second placed combat student at the academy. Or… at least he had been. Kizu couldn’t remember seeing his name at the top of the list Arclight had just shown them. If he’d failed the first challenge then he could have been tossed back dozens of ranks.

“Weston, right?” Kizu stepped up to the pissed off fifth-year and offered a hand.

Weston glanced from the hand  up to Kizu’s face. “Oh. You’re the kid who kneecapped Ulric. Friend of Harvey’s.”

He didn’t take the offered hand so Kizu let it fall to his side. “Yes. Kaga Kizu.”

Weston grunted and stood up. He walked to the closest wall and quickly began drawing on it with a piece of summoner’s chalk. A few seconds later, he conjured a wicked sword for himself. Resting the massive blade on his shoulder, he turned and frowned at Kizu.

“Why are you still here?”

Kizu rolled his eyes. While he’d hoped to form a plan with the fifth-year, he wasn’t going to fight Weston for the opportunity of teamwork. Kizu opened a side curtain and glanced down at the campus below. One perk of being sent here was that the high vantage point offered him a complete visage of the academy grounds.

Students scrambled across the campus like ants. Most ran in the direction of cover, likely hoping to get out from under the open air. Which was wise, because Kizu spotted Professor Oasaji hovering in the sky over their starting point. The flying turkey pointed down at stragglers with a wing and cages of fire burst into existence.

The curtain slammed closed in front of him.

“You’re going to give away my position!” Weston hissed.

“You already did that when you shook the entire building in your tantrum earlier,” Kizu pointed out. “At least what I’m doing gathers information.”

Weston grinded his teeth and then took a deep breath. “Fine. Keep doing whatever you’re doing. You’re right. I’m in a bad mood as a result of how the last challenge played out and it’s leading me to rash decisions. Not fair to get you caught up in them. Sorry about the ground punch.”

Kizu blinked. That was a surprisingly reasonable response.

“But I am not going to just hide up here,” Weston announced. “I’m going to the ballroom to face the professors on my own terms. You can stay here peeping on the battles. That’s your prerogative. I will prove my worth through combat.”

He strode over to the stairs and disappeared from sight.

Kizu took a moment to debate about his next course of action. His current situation wasn’t amazing. Weston had shaken the entire building when he punched it, so the professors definitely knew at least someone was up here. But likely not who exactly. While explaining the rules they’d said that in a gesture of fairness, Professor Grove wouldn’t be a member of the staff actively hunting them. Now that declaration made a lot more sense. Her absence removed the faculty’s most powerful reconnaissance asset.

Kizu was tempted to make a fort out of the room’s cushions and hide inside an illusion to become just another pile of pillows among many. As far as he understood it, the goal was simply to survive as long as possible, not to actually best the professors in combat. Weston was being extremely rash with his decision to confront them in a fight. But Kizu also didn’t love hiding in a place where they already knew someone was at. There were ways to dispel illusions, even ones that blended anti-divination into their spell formulas.

“If only I had my necklace,” Kizu lamented out loud. But he’d gotten in the habit of leaving the powerful anti-divination artifact with Anata as a precaution for if her father ever sought her out.

Kizu decided to take a small risk and test his spatial spells. He jumped across the room. Or… at least he tried to. An oppressive aura beat his spell down. He only cleared a third of the distance and when he reappeared, he fell to his knees as vertigo swept through his senses. When the world righted itself, he shakily regained his footing. It seemed Wakino had locked down this area. He could probably fight her control if he seriously needed to. But fighting both it and the constant pull of the academy’s nearby beacon would be tough. And he had no doubt if he let himself get carried off to the beacon that he would be caught within minutes.

“Okay, so no jumping except in dire emergencies,” he muttered. He pulled out a potion from his spatial ring and downed it. Immediately, the dizziness cleared from his head. The fact his ring still worked was a good sign. It meant that Wakino’s presence wasn’t able to disrupt all spatial spells. Just jumping. But there was still a question of the perimeters of that disruption.

Kizu closed his eyes and focused on his bond with Mort. The monkey cracked an eye open in his little nest in an upper cupboard of Taroe’s kitchen. It only took a few seconds for Mort to get the jist of his situation.

The monkey then jumped across the island and popped into existence on Kizu’s shoulder.

Kizu grinned. So an outside force could still come in. This was excellent. He now had an ally and scout on hand.

“Alright, Mort, you go down the stairs and catch up with Weston. Stay out of sight and I’ll be behind you in a minute.”

Mort bit his earlobe and then leaped to the floor. While Kizu rubbed his stinging ear, Mort scampered off.

Deciding to risk a peek outside, Kizu cast a shadowy illusion over himself and pulled back the curtain. He blinked. In less than a minute, everything outside had changed. Scattered across the academy grounds were the still bodies of students. Even from afar, Kizu could see the brilliant blue print on each of them. The exact paint he’d seen on the professors’ hands.

“So that’s the unspoken final rule,” Kizu said to himself. “All they need to do is touch us and we’re out. I guess it could be worse. At least we’re not out the moment they see us.”

His eyes naturally drifted to the single professor in the center of the courtyard. Arclight wrestled with a familiar multi-headed bear while Sene floated above her, blasting the combat professor with bolts of lightning.

Professor Arclight used the summoned bear to shield herself from the lightning, but the electricity still clearly circulated through the beast as her patches of hair stood on end as her body stiffened. Still, with enhanced strength, the woman gripped the bear with her bare hand while her prosthetic arm formed into a fist. Her punch slammed into the summoned beast. The impact punctured through.

The summoned monster flaked away but Ione was nowhere to be seen. Even flashing his spellsense, Kizu hadn’t any idea where his girlfriend was hiding.

Her twin, though, remained very visible as she pulled something off her belt and hurled it at Arclight.

The professor dexterously caught the projectile only for it to detonate in her hand, engulfing her in thick vines. It all happened so quickly that Kizu never even saw the vegetation grow. Simply a blink of the eye and all that remained of the combat professor was a mound of writhing vines in the rough shape of a woman.

“Was that a potion inside an enchanted vial?” Kizu wondered out loud. That was smart. The enchantment was likely one time use but could definitely see the utility in using a trigger to shatter the glass rather than force to smash a vial. Offensively, it could be very effective. He thought it likely would be worth the effort of individually enchanting potion bottles.

Arclight’s laughter boomed. The vines that held her loosened and withered.

Sene already had another potion in route to Arclight but the professor was no longer there. She moved in a blur as she dodged around the vial and leaped into the air with enough force to leave behind a crater in her wake.

Sene blasted herself backwards in a whirlwind. But Arclight obviously had foreseen the maneuver. The professor pushed off an invisible wall and altered her trajectory, her blue hand glittering in the sunlight.

Kizu’s breath caught as Arclight clamped down on Sene’s left arm, spun in the air, and hurled the top student into the grass below.

As Sene tumbled across the courtyard, a potion slammed into Arclight’s back and detonated. This time the potion erupted in purple flames.

Its sudden appearance seemed to catch even Arclight off guard as the professor flipped herself in the air and rolled across the grass in an effort to put it out.

“Was that… how did it follow her?” Kizu fruitlessly strained his eyes in an effort to see the shattered glass from the vial. He was fairly confident that it was the same vial Arclight had just dodged moments before. “Is it… heat seeking?” He vaguely recalled reading something on the concept once ages ago back when he’d been studying enhancement theory. A way to enhance one’s eyes to see people through their body heat. His enhancement capabilities weren’t nearly good enough to put something like that into action so he’d never thought much about the niche spell. He’d never even stopped to consider it could be used in an enchantment. Sene had very likely read the exact same book as him and utilized the theory with her other skills.

Then, as Arclight rolled in the grass, the professor’s posture stiffened, the flames still flickering with life on her as she went still as a board for a moment.

It only lasted a second. Arclight sat up and peeled a sea urchin-looking creature out of her thigh. One of Ione’s caldrops.

Again, Arclight threw her head back and boomed with laughter. As she did, the flames on her spluttered out.

Then Arclight stood up and dusted off her dirty and burned clothing. She raised one foot, her heel waist high. Then she stomped down.

Kizu nearly fell over as the shockwave shook the tower. His darkness illusion flickered as his concentration waned for a moment. But he caught himself on the windowsill and refocused.

In the moment he’d been distracted, Arclight had pushed through the field and somehow found Ione in one of the tunnels below.

Ione, now sprawled on the grass next to her twin, had a glittering blue handprint across her face.

Kizu shuddered at the sight of Arclight’s smile. This woman was a monster.

Then, she turned her attention away from the twins. She rolled her shoulders and cracked her knuckles. Her head slowly twisted on her muscular neck and the professor looked up.

And met his eyes.

If possible, the woman’s smile somehow managed to grow even larger.
