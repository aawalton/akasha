Syrene nodded enthusiastically. “That is something that feels more reasonable to me. You still want to pay me but I don’t need the money right now. But if I can help the village with that money, even if that money is kept in my name, that’s more than fine with me.”

"Great!" Kivamus smiled. “Obviously, the wages we will pay you or anyone else will be after deducting their expenses, that is, the cost of food and shelter, unless they live by themselves and buy their own food, like Cedoron or Taniok. But my plan is to pay master craftsmen or researchers like you 25 coppers a day. Even after deductions it will be above 20 coppers a day for you in savings. That’s more than six gold in savings every month. So if you really decide that you do not need any money, then for now you can tell Duvas about it and he will keep it for you, and in return you will earn a small interest on it. We will continue using it for the regular expenses, and if needed, you can take that money whenever you want.”

Syrene smiled. “Fine by me. I think Ma would also like to invest some of her wages, and there would probably be others in the manor who would like the same. Leah lives with Cedoron, so she doesn’t have to pay for shelter, while her food comes from the manor. Same for many others.”

Kivamus nodded, thinking about it. He couldn’t open an official bank right now, and he didn't even know what the rules and regulations of the kingdom said about that, but he could still let the manor residents keep their excess savings with Duvas if they wanted, for a small interest. It would be helpful for everyone. He also felt glad about talking with Syrene, since he hadn’t thought of this idea himself.

“That’s a good suggestion,” he said. “I’ll talk with Duvas about it. Thanks for the idea.”

Syrene grinned. “Oh, it’s nothing, milord.”

Kivamus glanced at the outer door, wondering where the majordomo was. He glanced at the work table, and knew that he needed to think more before he continued the sketch, and he didn’t have anywhere else to be right now. So he decided to talk more. Who knew, maybe he would get another workable idea after talking with Syrene.

Back on Earth, he had a habit of talking with his colleagues when brainstorming ideas, and many times, it helped him to think of a good solution which he wouldn’t have thought of by himself. But here in Tiranat, he didn’t have that luxury. Gorsazo and Duvas knew about his history, but both of them were set in their ways and couldn’t easily think out of the box. However, Syrene was just 21, the same age as him in this world, and she could help him think of some new usable ideas, even without knowing his past.

He looked back at the girl with dark red hair sitting in front of him. “So how are others in your family doing?”

Syrene chuckled. “Ma talks for hours with Gorsazo as you know already. They both seem happy with the company, so that’s good. And... Lucem is being a brat again these days and goes bragging to anyone who cares to listen now that he can read the alphabet properly, but Clarisa puts him in line every time he starts to get a big head. She is a smart one, and is always the top ranker in any tests in the classes of Gorsazo.”

“Yeah, I heard about that. And how are your own classes going?”

Syrene shrugged. “Good, actually. Teacher Gorsazo was saying that he wouldn’t have much to teach me after a few months at most.”

Kivamus raised his eyebrows. “Oh, that soon? I can see who got the brains amongst Madam Helga’s kids.”

Syrene snorted. “Lucem is also clever, and he can do much better in his studies if he just wanted to, but he doesn’t want to concentrate and just plays around with his friends in the village.” Then she frowned. “As for me, I hardly know anything compared to what you know, and I don’t even have any idea of how much knowledge you have...”

Kivamus shook his head. “Not at all. You have done great with the limited education you got. You are the one who found the process to make that medicine, and I’m sure it’s not going to be the last invention by you. But yeah, I think that after your classes are over with Gorsazo in a few months, I could start teaching you a few things myself. I don’t really know too much about biology or botany, but what I know will still be enough to give you a good foundation and allow you to understand things much more easily.”

Syrene looked away. “You had mentioned doing that earlier, but if I’m going to be living at the encampment, it’s not like you can teach me anything there. I’ll only be visiting the village for a few days in a month, at most. If only there was a way to talk to someone from a distance... then you could teach me even from here...”

Kivamus barely stopped himself from laughing, thinking about phones. Yeah, it would be a long time before even primitive phones could be made here, but it was possible alright.

Syrene tilted her head, looking at him. “You are making fun of me in your mind, aren’t you? I know it’s stupid and that nobody can talk from a distance!”

Kivamus shook his head. “No, it’s not stupid. I have told you earlier that basically all discoveries and inventions happen because people thought of and dreamed of things which were considered impossible by others. You wouldn’t have discovered that method of making acelos medicine if you firstly didn’t believe that it was possible. So don’t call yourself stupid for thinking of something like that. Even if it seems impossible today, one day it might just come true.”

Syrene frowned. “But such a thing is not even possible... I can’t even imagine something like that where people could talk from here in Tiranat to the encampment, or to anywhere else. You know I don’t believe in witchcraft or such stuff, but a person’s voice doesn’t go on for more than a few hundred meters no matter how loudly they shout. It’s impossible for you to shout from here and me to listen in that encampment—which is like a full day’s journey away in those hills.”

Hmm... Kivamus gazed into the distance as he thought about it. While Gorsazo and Duvas knew about his past, Syrene didn’t, even if she was much smarter than those two. She was also progressing tremendously in her education, and assuming he hid everything about his past right now, there wouldn’t really be too much he could teach her even if she stayed here in the village. But the fact was he needed more people to know about how things were like on Earth.

Obviously, they had to be people he trusted implicitly, but his dream of having a lot of smart people in Tiranat researching and inventing one thing or another—simply because there weren’t enough hours in the day for him to do everything by himself—would never come true if he never trusted them and didn't tell them about what the possibilities were, and where the ceiling was. By knowing for certain that something was possible, even if the process was unknown, would make someone smart find a way to get there.

He looked at the girl sitting in front of him. Could he trust her with something like that? He knew that if any nearby nobles found out about even her current knowledge and her ability to make those long-lasting medicines from those leaves, they would try everything in their power to kidnap her or force her to work for them. They might even attack Tiranat just to get to her—whether to capture her or kill her. So if she knew more, she would be in even more danger from people like Zoricus.

On the other hand, it would be a complete waste of her talents if she didn’t know about the possibilities of science. By now he knew her well enough that she would never betray him intentionally, just like the other advisors and people close to him at the manor never would. So could he trust her with that kind of information...?

He was thinking about it when the outer door opened. Kivamus looked at it, expecting the majordomo to be back, but this time it was Gorsazo—one of the only two persons in this world who knew about the full truth. Was it the world giving him a sign that it was the right time to include someone else into the inner circle?

The teacher looked at Syrene, who was like a daughter to him now, because of his closeness with Madam Helga—both of whom were widowed. Then he looked at Kivamus, before he grinned and sat nearby.

“You’re thinking of telling her about that, aren’t you?” Gorsazo asked.

Kivamus was surprised by his former teacher’s intuitiveness. “How did you know?”

Before Gorsazo could reply, Syrene looked at both of them one by one. “What’s going on? What were you going to tell me about?”

Kivamus looked at her curious expression, then back at his teacher. “What do you think? Is it the right decision?”

Gorsazo shrugged. “I think you can trust her as much as you can trust me or Sir Duvas. And unlike the two of us, she is the one who will benefit from that knowledge the most, and could actually make some good use of it. But in the end, it's your decision.”

“What are you both even talking about...?” Syrene asked with annoyance. "Stop ignoring me!"
