Baphomet’s blood froze in his veins. Had his puppet just… threatened him?

That was new. His victims didn’t always beg for mercy or break into tears, but he couldn’t recall any of them daring to promise retribution. Perhaps, he really ought to squash the insolent bug and worry later about establishing a new karmic pathway to the planet.

Just as he was considering his options, something changed inside the mortal’s body.

Warmth surged within his sternum for the briefest of instants, before dissipating.

His abdomen heated up after that, right where his second core was, though that didn’t last very long either. Whatever had caused this odd feeling had clearly failed to find whatever it was looking for. Baphomet could almost taste its disappointment.

But it didn’t give up.

A third rush of heat blossomed like an arcane flower from the mortal’s glabella, its centre right above the bridge of the mortal’s nose.

Unlike the previous two attempts, the magical seed finally found a good spot to plant itself and spread its magical roots within the mortal’s skull. The forming growth pressed against bone and brain alike, causing the softer tissue to shift inwards and part, creating a strawberry-sized cavity.

The mortal’s mind and memories churned underneath Baphomet’s magical restraints as a wave of searing pain burned through puppet and puppeteer alike. However, the mortal’s body seemed to adapt to the new structure within seconds, leaving no lasting damage – almost as if he had always been destined to experience this transformation.

A dozen needles of mind mana shattered as their fragments sank into the mortal’s brain, disappearing inside the magical seed. The corresponding threads linking back to Baphomet’s zealots grew thicker for a moment, seemingly drawing more mana than the god’s subjects could possibly provide. Then, they snapped.

Several crisp sounds, like a series of crystals shattering in quick succession, reached Baphomet’s ears, drawing his attention back to his chamber in the real world.

Many of his zealots collapsed to the floor, splashing into pools of blood that had poured like rivers out of their own orifices. Baphomet failed to spot even a single drop of mana within his subordinates’ lifeless bodies, and their chests appeared to have caved in where their cores were supposed to be.

More cracking sounds followed as zealots kept dropping dead one after another, while the bottomless pit inside the Green’s head showed no signs of stopping. The crimson threads grew wider as they fell into the karmic compass, before eventually disappearing inside the mortal’s head.

“STOP THIS AT ONCE!” Baphomet roared, his fury shaking the entire continent.

He tried to clamp down on his zealots’ mana with his internal world.

The massacre slowed down, but didn’t stop. Baphomet felt like he had grabbed a fistful of air. His grip was more than strong enough to hold the threads, but they kept slipping through the gaps between his metaphorical fingers.

Somehow, the mortal had found a way to feast upon his zealots’ cores, ignoring the vast distance separating the two worlds.

No. Not somehow. Baphomet’s own spell was being used against him – by connecting his mind to the mortal, he had delivered the lives of his subordinates like a sumptuous meal on a silver platter.

He counted thirty zealots dead… then forty… fifty…

Baphomet’s fur stood on end as he realized what the mortal had been counting earlier.

The source of this strange ability was still a mystery, though Baphomet was willing to bet that it was a Decree. Nothing else a mortal could do would have produced such an effect, and if his allies had managed to request help from actual deities, they would have found more effective ways to fight back.

But if this was truly a Decree, it had to be an extremely powerful one.

Baphomet was no titan, yet he was still very close to the upper end of the Elemental Acquisition stage. Decrees were just spells that other gods had cast a long time ago – it was generally impossible for a Decree to win against a living, breathing Concept mage.

Yet, this one was doing just that. Baphomet felt many ancient and powerful wills resonating within the mortal’s last resort, demanding their unholy tribute.

‘A collaborative Decree?’

There were very few of those out there, and most were well-known. Could this be the fabled Void Decree?

If the mortal’s faction had recently and secretly joined the alliance, it would explain many of the strange things that Baphomet had seen today. But the mortal already possessed a second core, so how was he forming a third?

And why would one of the most celebrated champions of a peak faction bother – or be allowed – to personally auction the secret of the cyan powder off?

‘It doesn’t matter! NONE OF THIS MATTERS!’ Baphomet shrieked. ‘THESE MORTALS ARE MY PROPERTY! MINE! STOP THIS AT ONCE!’

He didn’t care if this was the Void Hand, or even the Moirai. And why should he? Nobody dared to mess with Baphomet!

Even the peak factions would rather not deal with the headache of constantly testing their demigods for mind control or worrying that they would randomly start wreaking havoc on their planets. If it hadn’t been for Baphomet’s infamous spell, somebody would have stolen his Elemental Source a long time ago.

The Arahnians were giving Baphomet strange looks, as his outburst had nearly destroyed their fragile puppets. But he had more pressing things to worry about than the opinion of his weaker guests.

Who did this insect think he was, to take what was his?!

Baphomet lashed at the insolent mortal’s brain, trying to fry it into charcoal. Unfortunately, the deaths of his subjects had greatly weakened and destabilized his spell. The distance wasn’t helping either, and the other minds linked to his target had a much easier time shielding and reinforcing him.

Baphomet screamed, causing reality itself to wobble and twist around him. He tried to pry the projection of his internal world off his chamber, and to retract it back into his body to interrupt the spell.

The black walls and ceiling crumbled as the pair of overlapping realities fell out of alignment, revealing patches of a dark and moonless sky. The immense pressure crushed most of the surviving zealots into meat paste, but Baphomet had stopped caring about their lives. He’d have to spend centuries – if not millennia – to build a new order of trained mind users up from scratch, but he could worry about that later.

First, he had to punish this slight.

The final handful of threads snapped as the mortal tried to devour the mana cores they were linked to.

Baphomet didn’t let him. Wrapping the full might of his internal world around the crimson strings, he finally managed to hold them in place like a vice, stopping them a mere finger’s length before they could vanish into the karmic compass.

He tried to pull the mind mana back, or to crush the spherical object entirely, but he found himself unable to do either. The connection to the distant planet persisted, and it looked like Baphomet could not sever it until the Decree was either satisfied, or its owner dead.

That was fine.

A savage grin formed on Baphomet’s lips as he tasted the mortal’s sweet agony and felt the warm blood trickling down his nose, eyes, and ears. Whatever the bastard had tried to do was still incomplete. The mortal needed those last threads of mind mana and would probably die if he failed to absorb them. Baphomet just had to hold out for a few more seconds.

He was about to taunt the foolish mortal for daring to challenge him, when another invisible force manifested out of seemingly nowhere and tugged at the magical threads. It helped the mortal steal another pair of strings, leaving only two left.

‘A second collaborative Decree?! Just where is he getting them from?!’

No. This one was slightly different. It felt like a mental-type Decree that had been cast by multiple titans, though the influence of one of its creators was clearly dominant. Baphomet had never encountered anything like that either, though he guessed that this had originally been a single Decree that had somehow assimilated several others.

Whatever it was, it was hellbent on helping the mortal consume the cores of Baphomet’s remaining zealots.

The seven minds linked to the mortal’s were helping him as well. The four replicas were among them, as was the Blue with the icy body – who no longer had to worry about holding his companion down. The mysterious mind deity who was no longer a deity, and the sentient Ring of Sacrilege that had reestablished its connection to its owner were also fighting tooth and nail to assist him.

Baphomet couldn’t accept this outcome.

He was an Eighteenth Circle deity, while his opponents were just a bunch of mortals aided by a couple of Decrees. Sadly, he had lost most of his zealots and butchered his own spell, while also being forced to fuel the connection against his will and fighting a losing battle on hostile ground. Invading the minds of others had always been risky, though nobody had ever managed to take advantage of that before.

The first thread fell into the compass, leaving only one left.

The second – or seventy-third, if he counted the previous ones – soon followed.

Baphomet refused to let go. He pushed a tendril of his internal world through the karmic compass and into the mortal’s head. He ignored the overlapping spatial forces grinding against his internal world, clinging onto the tip of the crimson thread with every fibre of his being.

Baphomet would NOT let the mortal steal it. It was his.

He had painstakingly bred, raised, and trained his zealots like cattle. They were his property. He could lose them, he could kill them, or hell… he could eat them if he wanted, but he would never let somebody take them from him.

They were his.

HIS!

The edge of his internal world lingered just outside the gluttonous cavity, preventing the red string from fully entering it.

The mortal was on his knees, his whole body shaking from the pain and effort as mortals, deities, Decrees and Rings of Sacrilege warred within his skull. His third core was almost complete, but the final sacrifice eluded it.

‘JUST DIE ALREADY… YOU INSOLENT WORM!’ Baphomet screamed with maddened fury.

The mortal’s fingers dug deep into the spongy cap of the giant mushroom, carving deep trenches into its colourful surface. He gritted his teeth hard enough to crack a couple, though his will remained firm like iron and his resolute gaze pierced through time and space to meet Baphomet’s own.

‘Fuck you too… asshole.’

The mortal, his seven companions, and their two Decrees pulled at the magical thread one final time, dragging it to the depths of his glabella.

Like a devil’s maw, the Void Decree snapped shut atop both its final offering and the chunk of Baphomet’s internal world that was still coiled tightly around it, severing the connection once and for all.

The god was sent reeling backwards, crashing against what was left of the chamber’s wall. Blood poured out of his own orifices as a series of wrinkles formed on his skin. Random patches of fur fell off and his body visibly aged – an experience wholly alien to a deity like Baphomet.
