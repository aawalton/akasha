Information always has a buyer.

Caden Boggs was not a small man.

Doorways, ceilings, chairs—all were too narrow, too low, and too small. Yet with his size came a great strength. When he was a teen, he’d bested men twice his age. Much of that strength had faded along with his youth, his hard edges softed by life’s small comforts. He was still burly with hands big enough to palm a cutpurse’s skull and legs stout enough to kick out the knees of anyone that tried to cheat him.

"I want thirty."

The ratlord's voice was shrill and loud. Boisterous, as if to make up for the greasy strands of black hair that hung in his face.

Boggs considered the boy’s knees. “Do you, now.”

Young but not inexperienced, the ratlord didn’t quail under the merchant’s gaze. A shrewd one. And still a saltstained fool.

What else could he expect from the dregs of the Shroud?

“Listen, Boggs—I need it. This is good stuff, taken just today—”

“So it’s hot.”

“The former possessors are fresh off their boats and sodden with ale by now. These’re as hot as a drowned flame.”

It was cleverly said, Boggs had to admit. The boy couldn’t have been more than seventeen, but he didn’t look nervous. Not yet.

“Thirty is untenable for such paltry goods.” Boggs kept his voice calm and low. It was a technique he’d learned across a great many years—speaking softly and with enough authority will make everyone listen all the harder.

The trumped up cutpurse leaned in, placing his greasy hands on Boggs' freshly polished counter. “Thirty, or I take my business elsewhere.”

Boggs lifted his eyebrows. He wasn’t the only fence in the Sunken City by a long shot, but few would actually deal with the ratlords. Their take was always far too little for the risk they presented, and the Shroud had bigger concerns. This ratlord—Rags, if memory served, and it always did—seemed to either forget that fact or was bullheaded enough to ignore it.

"This is all quality," Rags insisted. “Reagents—”

"Dry," Boggs interjected.

"Jewelry—”

“Pasteboard.”

“...And gemstones. What do you say about those?"

Boggs said nothing. His Talent had already assessed the topmost layer of the pouch, and it bore three of sufficient quality to consider his demands. With enough skill and the right sucker, Boggs could move the lot of them for twice the price. But the Abyssals would wake before a merchant would walk away with only fifty percent margin.

Silence stretched for several long seconds between them. A small clock ticked away, its gears accompanied by the sharp plunk of Water. Seconds turned to minutes and Boggs neither broke the quiet or eye contact with the boy. At two minutes, the ratlord shuffled his feet. At five, his upper lip was sweating beneath the haze of mustache hair. Boggs stayed still, hands folded neatly atop his paunch. This was his domain.

Let the boy squirm.

“Look—” The words came out of Rags like a leaking wineskin, fast and sweet. “You’ve a good place here, Boggs. Quality. Which is why I brought you all this. The herbs will fetch you something, so will the jewelry, pasteboard or not. I’ve seen the locals come in here. The gems, those are the prize. Lifted them from a first mate with at least three scar tags.”

A veteran of the Southron Bloodsea. Boggs considered the pouch with the gemstones again. “No doubt a conscript.”

“Maybe. Maybe not.” Rags took a breath. “Twenty-five silver.”

Boggs kept his expression smooth and unimpressed. It was almost endearing how the ratlord wriggled. A worm on a hook.

Boggs decided to reel him in.

He smiled. “Ten.”

“Twenty.”

“Twelve.”

The ratlord gritted his teeth. “Eighteen.”

“Fifteen. Final offer.”

“Deal!” The boy snatched up Bogg’s hand and clasped it. It was like a child pulling on an adult’s arm. “Fifteen for the lot.”

“For the gemstones. I have no need for the rest.”

Rags blinked. "What'll I do with them, then?"

"Feed them to your little rats. I don't care.” Boggs started counting out fifteen silver coins in neat stacks of five. “I don't peddle in trash."

While technically part of the Shroud, to Boggs the ratlords were an unfortunate pest that had to be endured. It never hurt to remind them of their place.

The door opened to a sharp chime, admitting a tall stranger. At least six inches taller than the stout merchant, his rangy form was covered in a cloak that draped from sharp shoulders. It gapped, revealing a simple jacket and trousers and a set of belts holding a few knives. None of that was of any concern. To talk the streets unarmed was foolish at best. Far more eye-catching was the man’s face—it was covered by a sheet of solid, uninterrupted steel.

No visor or eye holes. How does he see?

The stranger moved into the room. He didn’t walk so much as stalk, his movements oozing an eerie steadiness. As if it wasn’t him that moved, but the shop itself. His head never pivoted—it was fixed on Boggs like a steel-plated hammer aimed for his heart…

“Boggs!”

The merchant flinched. “Abyss! What is it, boy?”

The ratlord pointed at the counter.

Boggs glanced down, surprised to find his hands wrapped around the piles of silver like claws. Clearing his throat, he released it all. “Take it and go.”

“Gladly.” Rags scooped his coins and sketched a mocking bow. “A pleasure doing business with you, Boggs.”

He swept out, his slight form almost bumping into the tall stranger. The ratlord barely came up to his shoulder, but he walked with the confidence of a fool. “Watch your step, beanpole.”

The stranger didn't move, merely stared down at the scrawny thief, forcing the ratlord to step around him instead. Rags wasn’t stupid, though. He glanced back at Boggs. “You’re lucky we’re on neutral ground, elsewise we’d need to have some sharp words.”

The stranger said nothing, only pivoted his blank gaze toward the ratlord. Rags swallowed. “Whatever.”

The little ratlord showed uncommon wisdom. He left.

"My apologies for that," Boggs said, voice raised to reach across the cluttered shop. “Ancestors punish the prideful.”

The masked man watched the door for a measure longer than Boggs thought was necessary. He walked, not looking at the merchant, but at the walls. They were covered in samples of his goods, all of them set behind glass.

He reached out, his dusky fingers almost touching the cases. His voice was a resonant rasp. “Interesting collection.”

Boggs offered a professional smile. He couldn’t quite get a read on this man. “Those are rare. A selection of powders. Itching, stinging, rusting, and standard.”

“Standard?”

“Simple chalk dust. It’s useful for a great many things.” Boggs eyed the man. “Are you seeking goods…or services?”

"A little bit of both.” His voice was muffled behind his odd helmet, but the harsh rasp did not relent. "Reagents, if you have any. Weapons. But mostly I’m interested in information.”

Boggs smiled. He even let it touch his eyes. “Information is a specialty of mine. Little comes through the city without me hearing about it. What are you looking for?”

“Chartermarks.”

The merchant’s smile faltered. The Chartermarks were only handed out during Threllsnacht and everyone knew that, so the man clearly didn’t mean that. “Are you after a particular person that has taken the mark?”

That curved metal helmet turned to regard him. “If I am…?”

Boggs licked his lips. “If you are…I would be remiss if I didn’t remind you that the Chartermarked are protected by the Academy. The moment they take their oath, they are watched. Interfere with the marked, and you risk the weight of the Nine Spires falling on your head.”

The masked man nodded. “I see.”

Boggs spread his arms. “There is little I can do on that front. Are there any other items you wish to know about, or shall I pull my selection of reagents out?”

The stranger pointed his finger at the wall. “Tell me what’s there.”

Boggs lifted an eyebrow. “Tonics? I have an assortment, though most are relatively—”

“No. Behind the tonics.”

A chill ran down Boggs' spine. "What’re you talking about?"

“I’m speaking of the recess you have hidden behind the wall. The items there are quite interesting.”

Boggs paled. "Who are you?"

The stranger tilted his head, and the merchant could make out his own warped face reflected back at him. “Someone looking for your true merchandise.”

“My…I see.”

“There’s no reason to reach for a weapon.”

Bogg’s hand froze inches from the haft of his mace. “How—?”

“You can trust me.” The stranger reached into his cloak, and before the merchant could seize his mace and lift it, placed a wide vial on the counter.

Boggs swallowed. He knew that vial and the roiling purple liquid within. “Wild Tincture.” His eyes shot back toward the masked man’s face. “This can only be gotten from…”

The Shroud. Boggs had no interest in saying the words aloud. Neither, it seemed, did the masked man. No. He correcred himself. A Phantom Blade of the Shroud.

That sort of poison wasn’t allowed amongst simple ratlords, fences, or even the lesser lordlings amongst the elite. It was reserved for the inner circle…and their fixers. Blades in the night. Assassins.

The stranger calmly replaced the vial in his cloak. “I’m looking to expand my collection. Now, if you please?”

Boggs swallowed and did as he was told. He cringed at the thought of putting his back to the man, but he had no other choice. To deny a Phantom was to bargain away his own life.

He all but scrambled to the wall, thick finger finding the hidden latch hidden along the casement. The tonics rattled as the panel swung free of the wall and revealed a recessed cabinet. It was filled with bottles, vials, and jars, all of which were teeming with mixtures, solutions, and thick bubbling unguents.

“What would you be interested in?”

That empty face considered the wall of potions with an inscrutable eye. “Quite a wide selection. Foul Fester. Ambrosia. Blightburn. Sheencry.”

“Only the best for my true wares.” Boggs managed a smile and it was only half a shade off his usual. “Are you looking for a specific effect?”

“Hm. I will have to consider things…While I do, what can you tell me of the Chartermarks and their movement?”

Boggs swallowed. “As I said—”

“I am not hunting any of them. Fear no reprisals from the Spires.”

“Very well.” He couldn’t gainsay a Phantom, not to his face. “All of them have left for the Southern Reach.”

“South of the Gnarl.”

“Aye. From there they travel the riverlands to Antrim, where the Spires have commandeered the waveskim that crosses the straight to Vodaten.”

“I see.” The Phantom tapped his fingers on the counter. “How long from here to Antrim?”

“A few hours to take the lift up to the Southern Reach. From there, it’s only a day or two on foot to Antrim itself.” Boggs hesitated.

“Merchant.”

“Yes?”

“What are you not saying?”

Boggs blanched. How could—? “I’m not…I only worry because further details are not verified.”

The Phantom leaned closer. “Tell me anyway.”

“The path to Antrim, through the riverlands, has been complicated. There was a Horizon Break.”

The masked man pulled back. Not flinching, but… What sort of reaction is that? A Phantom wouldn’t fear a Break.

Boggs let none of it show on his face. He nodded and lowered his voice to a whisper. “The riverlands had plenty of threats normally, but it’s been flooded with the Sea. Now everything’s gone mad. Rumor is the Spires are cleaning up the Break, but Bestials are infesting the path to Antrim.”

“I see.”

The man seemed steadier now, but Boggs had seen through him. What sort of assassin had hands so thin?

“If you don't mind me asking, sir. What business does the Shroud have with the Chartermarked?"

The man went still. "Some things you will regret knowing.”

Boggs bowed his head. "Of course. Forgive my curiosity."

Things weren’t adding up. The man carried himself and spoke like a threat, but it felt off. That metal plate… I’ve seen plenty of fixers in my time, and none of them have worn such a thing.

It was clear the man could see through it…or it was a trick. Knowing about his hidden compartment, “seeing” through the wall—none of it was outside the realm of a clever thief. If he was wrong, if the man did bear a relationship to the inner circle, then the merchant would pay dearly. Yet if he fell prey to an imposter, the Shroud would do worse than kill him. He’d be made into an example.

Trust your gut. Don’t let this man out of here alive.

“Is that all you know of the riverlands and its threats?”

“Unfortunately yes. As an apology, let me offer you this soporific.” He pulled a bottle off his hidden shelf. It was a pale lavender with swirls of bright green through it, and was sealed by an infused wax. “It's made of Lady Bell, Kelfer's Drop, and Teaswell caps.”

“A sleep draught? What’s the dosage?”

"Simply place two drops onto a rag and have them inhale it. Alternatively, it can be imbibed through a drink, but the draught does have a slight coppery aftertaste. I’d advise a strong drink to hide it.”

The man held up the bottle, as if examining it through his blank mask. “How fast does it act? Is it metabolized quickly?”

He knows his terminology, I’ll give him that. Boggs kept his smile fixed. “It depends on the individual, but generally speaking it’s anywhere between ten and thirty seconds. Anyone down to First Depth will find themselves sleeping for at least an hour.”

The masked stranger tucked it into a pouch. “What of weaponry?”

"I have a selection in the back. Much like my potions, some are not meant for casual observers. I also have a practice range to test them if you're so inclined."

"And infused items?”

“I have some. My selection is limited, of course. My clientele are not able to pay for prime infusions. Everything I carry was taken and trade.” Or stolen, but that went without saying.

“Hmm.” The man reached to his waist again. "What can you tell me about this?"

He pulled free a coin. Or so Boggs thought at first, but as soon as he set it on the counter, the merchant’s pulse quickened. It was a circle as black as pitch, giving no reflections at all, as if it were a piece of darkness set against the wood. A sudden wave of potency rolled off of it, tingling against his Talent.

Boggs trembled, but stopped himself from snatching it up. “May I?”

The masked man inclined his head.

Boggs picked it up. Goosebumps immediately rippled up and down his arms. “First Depth—Assess The Earth.”

His Talent was an esoteric focus not intended for battle. Instead, Boggs had developed it to ascertain hidden qualities of the inanimate. Water rushed from his palm, enveloping the coin…and vanished.

“Ancestor’s breath.” He turned it over in his hands. “It’s thrumming like a freshly-filled reservoir. But there’s no way to access it—!”

He hissed as the edge of it sliced into his fingers. It stung, but the pain was forgotten the moment Boggs saw his blood vanish into the coin. It grew hot in his hand. “Astounding. I’ve never seen something like this.”

The man snatched the coin back, quicker than the merchant could react. “I suppose I was expecting too much.”

“No!” Boggs took a breath, trying to calm his hammering heart. “No, I was merely overwhelmed. Give me but a moment.”

From beneath his counter, Boggs pulled out several thick tomes. Each one was bigger than the masked man’s head, with leather bindings that had been worn by countless years handling them. He flipped them open, turning the thin pages with reckless abandon. The reference tomes carried the record of a great many thaumic devices, but what he was after was the index of commonalities.

“The coin is clearly phagic.”

“Meaning?”

“The fanciful would call it a devourer, but really it’s just absorbing things. My Talent couldn’t read it because it took in my Water. The fact that it also absorbed my blood could indicate many things, but most likely it’s attuned to the life elemental focus.” Boggs tried to keep his breathing steady. Controlled. “It even warmed after absorption. That indicates a transfer of potency…but there’s no release. I cannot determine its purpose. Where did you get it? That might tell me more.”

The stranger said nothing. Boggs tried not to let his irritation show. "I see. The phagic property is not common. Tidal gems use it, of course, but they have ways to release the Water back out. If I had to guess, everything absorbed is being used to fuel something inside the coin.”

“Like what?"

"I haven't a clue, but you'll find out before long if you keep giving it Water. Or blood, I suppose."

Such an item would sell for thousands in auction. The mystery alone would fuel the bidding war. If it actually blossoms into something worthwhile, it could be priceless.

His Assess The Earth hadn’t been entirely useless. Boggs could still feel its potency, even tucked into the man’s palm, and it felt stronger now. He’d learned long ago to trust his Talent.

I must have it. Whether this man works for the Shroud or not didn’t matter anymore. Having the coin was worth whatever pain followed.

The stranger put it away, tucking it into the odd pockets at his waist. "Well, that's good to know." He gestured to the back of the shop. "Show me this practice range."

Boggs tamped down his frustration. It was far easier now that he had a goal before him. "Of course. Right this way, sir."

The practice range was perfect. Testing weaponry was a dangerous thing, of course. Anything could happen.

Anything at all.
