The entire valley froze as ice seemed to grow from the rock itself, clinging to and caressing anything it touched. Warriors halted mid-stride, encased in cocoons of ice, while the air itself became daggers in Devar’s lungs, making it an agony to breathe.

Devar knew as the ice crept up his legs and the cold penetrated his flesh that attacking the humans had been a foolish mistake. That they had underestimated their foe, and that they would now pay for the folly with their lives.

Closing his eyes while he was still able, he waited for the end.

At least, he planned to, but his body refused to listen.

A sudden weight pressed upon his mind. A presence that told him all would be well, so long as he yielded himself fully to the voice.

Devar hastily accepted, and not a moment later, he was channeling prana into his art.

Aspect of the Stone Sentinel activated, and a wall of mud surged up in a ring around him and a dozen fellow Chitrans nearby, sealing them inside a dome.

Demonic Overlord, of course! Devar thought, his mind finally understanding what had just transpired.

Adiman was not quite a Raja. Not yet, but he’d been foremost among their military commanders, and everyone knew he had inherited the Chitran Ultimate Bloodline Art after Matiman’s passing.

Only, no one thought he had mastered it enough to use it in the field.

Devar thanked the Akh Nara and Janak and every other god there was.

The elation, however, soon gave way to shame. He’d needed Adiman to shake him of his panic. In truth, Devar should have activated the ability straight away. Had he not trained the art night and day these past months? All for this moment?

The ice’s touch retreated only a moment later and Adiman allowed Devar’s art to fall, the earthen wall cracking and crumbling around them.

What he heard first when it did was laughter, and it came from none other than captain Harsa himself.

“They sought to trap us! They sought to freeze us in ice!” he was shouting. “But they forget we are demons! We are kothis of the Chitran, and we will not fall!”

It was true, all around him, Devar found his Chitran brothers alive and well. Either breaking free of their ice cocoons under Demonic Overlord or having been protected by barriers similar to Devar’s own.

“We… survived,” Devar muttered, able to speak despite the Ultimate Art’s influence. Whether intentional or a limitation of Adiman’s lack of mastery over the ability, he could not say, and as he stared at his own hands in disbelief, he did not care.

For they had taken the highest magic the humans had… and they had lived.

Battlecries sounded off all around him. Devar joined them. All across their army, demons liberated themselves and pressed on.

But not all, Devar noted. For every four demons who moved, one remained entombed in ice forever. Mostly among the red and gray demons of the Aindri.

It made a sad sort of sense. A Warrior’s prana capacity affected their strength far more than what species of demon they might have belonged to, but the Aindri and the Garga were among the physically weakest demons, aside from the Iksana.

Devar was none too strong himself, yet the kothi blood that flowed through him granted him an edge.

An edge strong enough, it seemed, to overcome human magics. As expected, not a single Bairan had succumbed either, and the Iksana were nowhere to be seen, likely having sunk into the shadows the moment the magic deployed.

With an almighty roar that was half Adiman’s influence and half of Devar’s own volition, he rejoined his warband and pressed the charge.

Several demons, it seemed, had thrown up defensive barriers. Either walls of packed Earth like Devar’s own or rings of fire that melted the ice, protecting not only themselves, but the demons nearby as well.

Compelled by a will that was not his own, Devar ran past one such wall, the far side melted to glass, the demons behind it already up and running on.

The grand ice working had been impressive magic, to be sure. No demonic art could hope to cover such a vast area. Not even Ultimate Bloodline Arts.

Against humans, the magic might well have cut them down to the last, but their foe had learned the hard way that demons and humans were not the same.

The Chitran among their warband moved with a supernatural grace that was impossible unless under the influence of the art. Devar knew not how great its reach was, but it was clear that Adiman had some measure of command over the Ultimate Art.

And so they ran in an organized fashion that would have otherwise been impossible. They ran through that vast open field in the dead of night, past all manner of things hidden to Devar by the darkness.

The earth opened in front of him, revealing gleaming steel tips of spikes in a pit full of them.

Under the influence of Demonic Overlord, Devar and his brethren rushed forth with no heed to their survival, unable to check their run.

Ahead, an orb buried in the dirt burst, blowing the legs clean off a nearby demon.

The ground could not be trusted, and were Devar able to control his own body, he would have surely halted this mad charge.

The Iksana had done their best to find these traps, but they could not possibly have found them all. Not with the humans firing down at them from their wall.

Devar’s legs carried him forth regardless, and he could only watch on, horrified, as he ran towards his death.

That was, until an Ash Mule and its mounted Warrior came roaring past with such force that the wind from its passing nearly caused him to stumble.

It was followed by a hundred more, all rushing past, talwars and spears raised, riding to the sound of their great battle drums.

And yet, as impressive a sight as Raja Jagath Aindri and his mounted elites were, it paled to what came next.

It began with the lowest ants and insects all forming lines, all rushing the same way the army was bound. Devar almost missed it until the rodents began emerging from their burrows, racing ahead.

Then came the birds, swooping low, joined by hares and foxes and small creatures Devar had never seen.

All ranged ahead of the army, sensing what the demons could not.

They found the buried orbs and the false ground, piling up in front of them. Crying out to alert those nearby.

There was only one ability capable of such a feat, and Devar never thought he’d see it with his own eyes.

Clan Aindri’s Ultimate Bloodline Art, Jungal Beckons.

And unlike the fledgling Adiman, Raja Jagath Aindri had centuries to master it.

Several rodents formed a ring around a hidden trap ahead, allowing Adiman to guide the Chitran under his control to safety.

Two Rajas, each wielding their Ultimate Arts in beautiful harmony.

And ahead of them all, at the speartip of the vanguard, rode Jagath himself.

The Raja weaved his mounted Aindri through the gauntlet with unbelievable grace, deftly avoiding the thousands of fireballs, ice lances and wind blades the mejai atop the wall hurled relentlessly.

It reminded Devar of a waterfall, so dense were their spells. So numerous that they lit the wall entirely on their own. Only this waterfall wasn’t made of water, but every pranic element in existence.

In the face of all that, Jagath didn’t slow a single pace, urging his mount to push through and reaching the wall before everyone else.

The army roared in cheers behind him, and Devar had no doubt the history books would remember this glorious act for millennia to come.

The Aindri riders spread out across the length of the wall, securing it for what was to come.

And what came behind were Bairan giants. Giants who bore unfathomable power, and who could not die. Charging hot on the heels of those Ash Beast mounts, roaring their battlecry at the top of their lungs.

It was no imagination on Devar’s part when several humans disappeared from the ramparts of their wall, their posts abandoned in terror.

Devar could not blame them. Between the Ash prana that flowed through all the Samsara and their Giant’s Hide and Giant’s Grace bloodline arts, Roshan and his hundred and seven nullified every spell the humans launched, dodging elegantly when it suited them, but more often than not simply taking them against their bare skin, not even slowing in the face of that magical onslaught.

They reached the wall in Jagath’s wake. Half their number vaulted themselves halfway up the wall in a single leap, slamming their fists into the wall, creating handholds where there ought to have been none.

And through it all, they shrugged off the incoming bombardment of magical spells as though it were nothing but cold water.

The other half massed at the gate and began breaking it.

Devar arrived behind them with the Chitran, and the entire demon army behind him.

They’d reached the gates.

Before very long, they’d break through them.

Demonic might would prevail this night… and maybe, just maybe, Devar would be reunited with his little sister once again.

Princess Tiyana Matali watched from the rampart as the defenses failed one after another. Her defenses, for she’d come up with them herself. Had convinced the others that fighting from the wall with traps and magic barrage was their best course of action. Every preparation had been made to her exacting standard.

She’d believed in the plan. She’d believed in it so thoroughly that she’d completely disregarded Maiya’s warnings.

Now, all she could hear was the Saint’s voice in her head, warning her of the blood that would be spilled.

She wondered briefly if the situation might be different had she stationed soldiers in front of the wall… but that would have been the height of stupidity.

The wall was eighty paces of every advantage human beings had ever built into it. Only a fool sent men out from behind a fortress like this. Every trick they had, every magic they possessed had been used to its full potential.

Even now, Altani Mejai of Realms fired attack after attack with every affinity under the sun, the Talent wielders among them firing their arrows and manning the ballistae from the ramparts as support staff poured boiling oil onto the demons rushing the wall.

It was everything they had. It wasn’t nearly enough.

And that wasn’t even beginning to consider the situation behind the wall.

Where chaos reigned in the camps before the very first attack had even begun. Tents burning and ammunition stores set ablaze as their Iksana infiltrators sowed one crisis after another, forcing her to fight on two fronts.

Not for the first time, Tia lamented humans’ absolute lack of such arts. To infiltrate the enemy using shadows… it was a power she would kill for.

They’d been foiled from the outset, and of their four great S rank spells that were supposed to decimate the enemy and leave behind a sundered wasteland, only one had fired successfully.

Even one ought to have decimated them, or at the very least significantly eaten into their numbers, crushing morale.

The issue was not the demons’ physical strength. Tia had accounted for that. It was why she’d convinced the Altani—after lengthy debate—that four S rank spells were required at once.

Absolute overkill, they’d called it.

Tia had seen it as insurance against exactly this sort of sabotage, though even she could not have predicted demons appearing from the shadows to snap subjugation Artifacts on their strongest mejai.

It was genius, in a way. Those Artifacts could not be so easily removed once placed. In a single move, they stopped her greatest weapon and sidelined her greatest mejai for the rest of this battle, rendering them little more than pranascorned farmers.

She’d watched on in horror as Ravan, Prime Mejai of the Altani, had been flung off the wall screaming as he fell to his death.

Even still, one of their S rank spells had launched.

Maelstrom ought to have done far, far more damage than it did.

The issue was that demons did not march in ordered ranks as the armies of humanity did.

They rushed in a haphazard way, each clan marching to the beat of their own drums—and often, not even then.

The spell had killed a full quarter of the demons it had touched… only that it hadn’t touched even half of the overall army.

And then there was her own S Rank Meteor. A spell she’d thought impossible to defend against had not only been stopped midair, it had been redirected, falling behind their own walls, wiping out a quarter of their army in the blink of an eye and only that because Maiya had intervened, breaking up those tons of rock before it hit them.

If she hadn't... Tia shuddered. There would be no army left. There would not even be a wall.

Her own attack. Used against her to slaughter her own people.

Tia fumed with barely contained rage. She never wanted this war. She never wanted to fight demons, and she and Maiya had both done what they could to avoid it.

Now, she imagined herself slaughtering every last demon in this army limb from limb. She imagined herself ripping that giant limb from limb, waiting until he revived, only so she could do it again. And again. Until he lost his mind.

She forced herself to take a deep, calming breath. She'd done all anyone in her position could. She’d hatched the best plan with the knowledge she had at the time.

Fate, or demonkind, she supposed, simply had other plans.

Below her, Raja Jagath of the Aindri reached the base of the wall on his Ash Mule and was joined by the Samsara moments later.

Tia didn’t need to look down to know when they’d started climbing. The tremors beneath her feet said it all.

Time seemed to slow as he looked around her. At the panicked soldiers who hurled spells and arrows, desperately fighting for their lives. At their ruined camp behind them, still burning from the aftermath of her Meteor spell.

If the demons broke through, and at this rate, they surely would, there would be nothing to stop them.

No organized ranks of humans funneling demons through the narrow kill zone that was the fortress’ gate.

No barrage of magic and Talents to turn this into a fight of attrition.

The demons would cut through them like a flood, and if she did nothing, it wouldn’t simply be a fraction of their army that perished.

Every single human here would die.

Unless she gave them a chance.

A fighting chance.

Unless Tia broke her promise to Vason and Haymi when she’d sent them away earlier to deal with her S Rank spell’s fallout.

Sorry, you two.

“Sound the horns,” she ordered, climbing atop the battlement. “Announce a full retreat. The position is lost! Abandon the wall and retreat to safety!”

“Milady, what are you doing?” someone nearby asked in panic.

“Buying time.”

Then, before anyone could argue, Tia gripped the battlement and jumped off the wall, Artifact blade in hand.

Directly upon Raja Jagath Aindri’s unsuspecting skull.
