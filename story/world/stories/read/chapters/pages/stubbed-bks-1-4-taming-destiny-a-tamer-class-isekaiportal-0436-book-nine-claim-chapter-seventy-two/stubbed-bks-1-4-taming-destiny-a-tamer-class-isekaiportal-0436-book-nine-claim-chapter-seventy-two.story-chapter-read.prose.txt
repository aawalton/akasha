The tidal wave hits the ship like a hammer blow. Josiah stumbles as the ship lurches hard to port and spray explodes across the deck. Around him, the crew members stagger, some going to one knee, others already gripping lines or railings. This isn't the first blow they've suffered, though it's one of the hardest so far.

"Hold fast!" Josiah barks, already summoning his magic. "Water Mages, deflect the attack!"

Already the remnants of the wave on the deck are shifting with clear intent, drawing into tendrils as thick as a man's torso and clawing their way towards the centre of the ship.

Josiah doesn't hesitate. Thrusting a hand forward, he forces his magic into the closest tendril, reforming it into a spray of rain that lashes harmlessly across the planks. Around him, the other Water Mages and Water Mage-adjacent Classers with him deflect, repel, or disintegrate the remaining tendrils, driving the seawater back where it belongs.

It's not an easy task. The water resists their efforts, fighting them like it is a part of the living beast controlling it.

His eyes catch a glimpse of silver scales off to starboard, and his jaw tightens.

"Should have killed you the first time," he growls grimly. Then, raising his voice, "Archers, fire at will! Bleed it every time you get an opening."

Arrows fly, and bolts of magic follow from those not fighting the water tendrils. Ice, lightning, compressed air, and even some sound attacks hammer into the serpent as it surges up into the air once more. It's enormous, larger even than the last time they encountered it. The effects of the manastorm, he can only conclude.

Uncaring of both projectile weapons and magical strikes, it coils with unnerving speed in the water and strikes the side of the ship again with precision, hammering its head into the same section each time.

"Starboard midships," his captain mutters beside him. "It's spotted our weakness there."

Josiah’s jaw tightens. He wishes now he’d agreed to the deep inspection of the ship that the captain had recommended before setting sail. But the cursory inspection had been positive and the need to bring food back to the settlement seemed more urgent at the time.

"Then we make it regret that choice," Josiah answers grimly. "Reinforce that section," he calls to the fighters, directing them with an arm gesture and a shaped cloud of raindrops. "Shields there. Now."

With battle-tested efficiency, the fighters move fast. This isn't the only beast they've fought at sea, though it is the only one that's attacked them twice.

Another mass of tendrils surges up the side of the ship, worming into the cracks and spreading across the deck. Once more Josiah calls his mages in to defend against them. He drives at the mass with a focused gale, his coat snapping around him with the backdraft. The water twists, resists…then shatters into sea spray, blowing back into the face of the serpent.

It hesitates for a moment, not expecting its own attack to be returned. Josiah spots the chance, and so do his fighters. A coordinated volley of arrows and magic strikes home. A lance of lightning cracks across the serpent's flank, and ice begins to wrap it in a constricting collar. The beast rears back with a sound that is neither quite a roar nor quite a scream, fighting against the chilly bindings. For a single moment, it falters.

"Fire!" Josiah snaps.

More attacks follow, his fighters emboldened by the momentary success. They drive it back down into the water, leaving only bloodied, churning waves behind. A ragged cheer breaks across the deck.

Josiah doesn't join it, but something in his chest eases. Perhaps they'll manage this without losing anyone else. He's tired of losing people.

"Good," he praises his crew, using his control over air to carry his voice across the deck. "Again like that, and we'll–"

The sea explodes and they draw back instinctively as they’re showered by painfully-hard droplets.

The serpent surges back up, faster than before. Fewer of its wounds are in evidence compared to when it dived in, and even those are already knitting. Its eyes burn with fury, and spikes of water bristle around its entire body, forming a dangerous armour. It coils and surges forward with a speed it hasn't shown before.

Josiah's heart rises into his mouth even as he calls out a warning. Despite his expectation that the serpent would change tactics, it goes straight for the same spot again, fresh cracks forming in the wooden hull at the powerful strike.

"Stubborn bastard," he mutters, then exchanges a glance with his captain. "If it's determined to hit us there, we'll make it pay for every attack."

The captain nods grimly. Josiah shouts instructions to his fighters.

“Secure the catch!” he half-hears behind him as the deck pitches once more. Darting a glance sideways, he sees the nets from the last haul lying tangled amidships to starboard, some of them dangling precariously close to the edge, the fish trapped inside flapping with renewed vigour as if they know that their salvation lies close by. If they lose this catch, the whole journey will have been for nothing.

Perhaps that's why the sea serpent has been attacking – an easy meal of fish, ready caught and still thrashing weakly in the nets. Josiah's determination hardens. There are people back home who need that food. No sea serpent is going to steal their catch.

"Get it clear," an officer calls. "We need that space."

"Leave it," Josiah cuts in sharply, his mind racing. They won't be surrendering the catch…but perhaps they can use it as bait. "Just secure it. Make sure it won't slip over the edge accidentally."

His plan forms quickly and orders flow from his mouth. Reinforce the deck, layer the shields, pull back the attacks just enough to draw it in.

And then, when it's committed, hit it with everything they have.

It's a risk. They've been holding back some of their heaviest strikes, either because they're single-use attacks or because they take so long to regenerate that they're effectively once-per-battle. But if they can finish the serpent or wound it fatally in a single coordinated strike, it'll be worth it. Certainly it will be cleaner than a battle of attrition, which they are sure to lose.

Even as the serpent coils to strike once more, a shadow flashes over Josiah. He glances up and feels some of the tension ease from his shoulders at the sight of the expedition leader overhead – and that large Tier three larnatis of his beside him. With them here, the odds are tipped decidedly in their favour.

While communicating with Markus to update him on the situation, Josiah doesn’t take his eyes off the serpent. Frustratingly, despite having seemed so ready to strike before, it now hesitates. He can only hope it hasn't sensed the trap – because this time, he's determined the creature doesn't make it out alive.

Would a Tier three put this much effort into a simple meal? I ask her.

It depends on the meal. Perhaps there is something more than ordinary, Unevolved fish in that catch. If they accidentally caught something of value, then perhaps the Enlightened is willing to risk its life for it.

If it's a parent urgently trying to retrieve its hatchling, perhaps it will leave us alone once it has it back, I comment to Bastet.

She sends me a cautious optimism.

It’s possible – especially if we present a strong front that makes it wonder if it won’t survive an attack. However, we must be prepared for it to want revenge, especially if the hatchling comes to any harm.

Then we had best ensure that it doesn't.
