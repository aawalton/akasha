James’ axe lit up with a blue glow as it launched itself toward the approaching monster. It crashed into the lava monster with a plop. At that moment, it burst into pure white steam, the lava freezing solid into black obsidian rock as a white, translucent ice covered it.

James strolled through the flaming city, letting his parallel thought cast ice magic toward nearby monsters. White steam rolled over the street as ice magic poured onto the lava. One monster, unable to bear the brunt of the ice magic attacking it, made a desperate charge at him, but with his new wings, James jumped over the ten-foot monster. Its body cracked and shattered as it cooled down.

“The boss is nowhere to be seen,” James said as Andrew and the other survivors trailed behind him. Unlike him, they didn’t have any protection against fire, so they were cautious, keeping a good ten-meter distance from the front line.

Most of them didn’t have ice magic either, so they knew they couldn’t contribute even if they wanted to.

After the area was cleared, Silas and a company of soldiers arrived with their tanks, welcoming Andrew and his team. “Greetings, Mr. Andrew. While you were busy touring Australia, we came here risking our lives, bearing the brunt of these dungeons. I am glad you didn’t miss the show. We almost nuked this city.”

“Well, you guys never supported the guild. Look, we cleared the city with only ten men while your army of five hundred got wiped. How did we do that? From dungeons and magic!” Andrew replied. James could feel the hostility rising in his tone.

“I see. Your insight was very useful. Now that we know their weakness, I shall arrange for helicopters to pour liquid nitrogen onto these monsters,” Silas said.

“So, our job here is done?” Sanders asked.

“We will relegate ourselves to being your support. Please finish the boss.” Silas bowed. His face curved into an incomprehensible smile. “Please understand that we would have let you—and even supported you—to dive into all dungeons if they had not come out and attacked us.”

“Tch!” Andrew scoffed as he rallied his men and walked toward James. “Don’t believe him. If he really supported us, he would let us borrow all artifacts and weapons registered with the system.”

“You were the ones trusting him. I never cared about him in the first place. I would still dive dungeons with or without you,” James said. “I only helped here because this is Ryan’s hometown.”

“What happened to you after I was gone, James? You were not so detached before.” Andrew asked, bewildered.

“Why did you accept a trial dungeon, Andrew?” James smirked. “Well, I don’t want to talk about it. Let’s go. We have a boss to kill. Oi, government spy, where is the boss?”

“They are on a mountain seventy kilometers from here,” Silas said.

James then rode his raptor toward the mountain. The liquid lava wiggled, chasing him in vain. Fortunately for him and the UN soldiers, these monsters were slow and had no ranged attacks. A tall Hexco barrier or concrete wall was enough to block or trap them for a time.

The UN was only confused about the magical nature of these lava monsters. After they learned the monsters’ weakness, instead of guns, they began using fire trucks and drums filled with liquid nitrogen. Andrew stayed behind, coordinating with survivors to cull the monsters that had spread to other areas. He only had one water mage, so he stayed put.

Now that the survivors knew the monsters were weak with ice and water, they could change their upgraded beginner weapons and switch their enchantments to that of ice magic. Unlike the elemental weapons James had, enchantments offered far less elemental damage. They were not very effective against the lava monsters.

Galloping across the city, James scouted the area, trying to find the boss monster, but he had yet to locate it. Unlike the red dungeons that James had culled before, this one had spread its destruction to nearby villages and towns because the boss had not been killed. James saw how these monsters rampaged through the land, spreading destruction wherever they went.

In less than two weeks since this red dungeon broke out, the monsters had spread out, encompassing dozens of kilometers of land. It was no longer contained to just one street like the one James defeated. James knew that if things were left alone, they would grow and even conquer the very earth he lived on.

Moments passed with the raptors galloping, eating James’ mana to hop on rocks and climb the hill next to the town. There, James found a stone statue, its body encased in silverish armor. It stood on its legs, towering over him by a good three feet. In its hands were a sword and a shield—the kind of weapons James hated to go up against.

Before James could take a shot at it, a helicopter armed with a missile fired at it. The silver rocket flew toward the golem with a trail of smoke and exploded on the metallic body, enveloping it in hellfire. The golem stood from the licking flames, unscathed. It strode on the hill, pointing its finger at the fleeing helicopter. A glowing fireball gathered, condensed into blue flame, then shot toward it. Like a bullet, it left a trail of blue light in the air.

The helicopter exploded in a blazing trail of fire.

James marched on, treating the fallen helicopter like an NPC as he went up the hill to challenge the silver guardian. It saw James and shot a fireball, which was deflected by James’ lightning bolt.

James dismounted and snapped his bow into his hand, drawing it to full length before activating his skill. The arrow drew a straight line and plopped against the armor on his chest. James tilted his head at the bizarre event. The arrow that had pierced the silver knight plopped out of its chest as if it had struck jelly. The hole it left behind mended together, erasing the damage and returning the gleaming armor to brand new.

The knight fired several fireballs toward him, but James parried them with his lightning bolt and an ice wall. Knowing further missile exchanges were useless, the knight dashed forward, pointing its shield and sword ahead. Likewise, James charged with his sword. His blade glowed red, signaling that he had prepared a sword skill with his Sword of Khine.

The knight’s sword smashed against James’ like a sledgehammer. If not for James’ sword skill, Lunar Counter, he would have been pulverized.

Alas, when James’ sword skill hit the knight, the blade passed through it like a knife through butter. It slipped through the knight’s armor as if it were made of water. The wound bubbled and recovered to its former luster.

James used his new wing and hopped far when the knight swept its sword toward where he had been a few moments ago.

“Elemental weapons are becoming important. Are ice elemental weapons the only way?” James muttered as he drew his axe. His axe glowed blue, then James charged toward the knight. Seeing the sapphire glow, the knight no longer acted recklessly. It braced with its shield held upfront. The towering shield was firm, and the knight was confident it could withstand all attacks.

James smirked. He would have had trouble surpassing that shield a few days ago, but with the new golem wings, he could leap above it, aiming his sapphire axe at its helmet.

It was just a graze, but the elemental weapon spread its damage. The head hardened from the frost. Then, it cracked and shattered.

But the knight was not done with James yet—not by a long shot. The body undulated, and its head reformed within moments. James blinked at the regenerative monster. The pestilence blob in the medium-level dungeon hadn’t behaved like this. What was the difference?

Alas, James had to find the core or freeze it solid, and that was what he was going to do. He activated his magic, and ice shards bombarded the knight relentlessly. But the knight was firm. Its shield blocked most of the damage as it inched forward.

Its great axe pummeled the ground, and James had to hop onto a nearby rock while engaging it. This knight might not have any sword skills, but James knew better than to assume he was weak. His experience with golems in the trial dungeon told him it had the strength to ward off any weapon skill aimed at it.

“How do I strike it from the back?” James frowned. He dominated the field—the knight was slow, its attacks were easy to dodge, and its fireball skill was easy to parry. But he couldn’t land a decisive blow. The knight’s defense was solid. Even if he could get a glancing blow, it would just remold its body. The knight could liquefy itself, preventing James from dealing fatal damage. So, the battle turned into a stalemate.

James then had an idea. He used his ice magic to cover the ground in an icy sheet while taking a potshot at the knight with his horn.

Then he stopped bombarding the knight and stood still. The knight was baited. He charged with his giant sword in hand, ready to crush James with a decisive blow. But as he stomped on the ground, his steel boots slipped on the ice, and his face kissed the muddy ground.

A blue glow shone from James’s axe. It had been a long time for James to use his axe’s double-hit skill, V-slash. The blue axe hit the knight square on his back. The elemental weapon worked its magic, converting the physical damage into ice damage.

Ice covered the knight’s armor, and with the temperature dropping, the knight could not use his liquefy skill. James broke his armor with a stomp. There, at his stomach, a green jewel glowed with liquid metal sloshing around it. When James picked it up, the liquid stopped sloshing and plopped onto the ground. It had turned into harmless mercury.

Another helicopter flew to where James had dueled the knight, and James waved at it with the green glowing cube in hand.

Then, a notification from the system lit up his view. James had leveled up.
