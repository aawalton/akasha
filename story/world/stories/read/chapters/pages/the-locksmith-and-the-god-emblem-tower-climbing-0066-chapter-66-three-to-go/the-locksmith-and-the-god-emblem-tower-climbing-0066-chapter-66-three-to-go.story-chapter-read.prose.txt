Despite the dread he felt the moment he saw the floating figure blending among the stars, Matthews trusted his brother. He believed Harry would make the right choice and, even after everything he had done, loved him enough not to stand in his way. Still, there was no denying that punching a creature that could fly, whose voice was strong enough to shake the room, was not the wisest of ideas. But trusting someone every once in a while led to exactly this, didn't it?

What followed was possibly the greatest mix of sheer terror and adrenaline the Locksmith had ever felt throughout his entire journey. Not only had he landed a blow on Raven, but he had also rejected his proposal. Knowing the entity, he imagined the latter was the part that irritated him the most.

Raven was slow to react, almost as if he himself couldn't believe what Harry had done. He turned slowly and touched the side of his mask where he had been struck. He stared at the young thief, who kept forcing his smile even though his heart didn't share the sentiment. He had no idea how the others behind him were reacting, but he could imagine it wasn't with great joy.

"...You would rather lose the chance to see your mother for a bunch of strays?" The figure took a step forward, nearly pushing Harry aside. "Do you have any idea what I have been through all this time? Because of something that wasn't even within my control, I found myself forced to watch men lose themselves in pursuit of power, aggressively climbing the tower as if it proved something. I'm sure he never felt the passage of time, but I did. I tasted a piece of infinity. And what was my sin? Existing? No. I will not stay here, waiting another million years."

"You..." Harry found himself lost in the words after Raven's sincere reply. "This was never about the others. You always had the ability to change things; you let it happen because it fed your—"

"My what?" He cut him off, taking another step and reducing Harry in stature. His voice had now returned to what the Locksmith was used to. "My arrogance? My entertainment? No. I am past that phase. I am simply tired now, Harry." He sighed deeply. "I can say that being human is difficult. All these... emotions and feelings. This passage of time that seems to stretch even further as the years go by. It is a privilege to be indifferent in a world that seeks purpose, don't you think?"

As intimate as his words seemed, Harry noticed the apathetic tone he had first heard back on the first floor. This was the calm before the storm.

"We can help you with that." Harry said without much enthusiasm. "All of us understand a little of what it means to be human; it is pain that moves us."

"I appreciate that, Locksmith." He whispered in Harry's ear. "But it is too late. I believe logic serves better than anger or joy in situations like this."

The Locksmith's eyes seemed to leap from his face when he realized what Raven was about to do. In an agile movement, he turned and conjured a door as a barrier between Raven and the group.

"Watch out!" Every ounce of air in his lungs was used in a single terse phrase.

Gleaming, an object with the properties of a rare mineral was reflected in the young thief's blurred eyes. The projectile, moving at a dizzying speed, tore through the door, shattering it into dozens of pieces as though it were made of cereal.

"NO!" Harry screamed again as he grasped at the air. The projectile had struck Goldman in the chest. Only the young thief's scream echoed through the unnecessarily vast room. Like an audience applauding, the books trembled on their shelves.

Blood sprayed in a jet behind Goldman's shoulder, his smile already gone. The event, though sudden, did not destabilize the group, who quickly fell into a combat stance. Matthews and Vela stepped forward, holding the swaying body of the gentle giant. Madeline then painted a semicircle around the three of them, shielding them from further attacks. She added another layer to reinforce the barrier.

Manes, in an attack that combined melancholic fury with the recklessness of someone mentally unstable, covered himself in shadows and hurled one of his bands at Raven. A sound like paper being cut rang out, followed by the sharp cry of a fallen Manes.

"Don't attack him alone!" Harry used one of his doors to reach the group, but the moment he opened it, he saw Raven standing on the other side, tilting his head slightly.

"We have all seen what you are capable of, Locksmith." The figure said, aloof. With the palm of his hand, he pushed Harry away, sending him falling backwards against the telescope.

No... Not again... He thought to himself, his vision giving way to black stars that left him hazy. He was losing consciousness.

"What did you do?" The Astronomer helped him up with a panicked expression. "Don't you understand the control he has?"

"...What was I supposed to do?" Harry fumbled a response, slowly reclaiming himself. "Leave all of you behind while I take what I want? I don't think I could live with that on my conscience..."

He could then see the violent clash taking over the room. Raven stood at the center, deflecting the attacks of Vela and Madeline. In truth, it was the attacks that deflected from the figure, who seemed to have an invisible force surrounding him. In a show of light, he saw colors shifting from a burnt yellow to a deep blue, losing their effectiveness the moment they touched Raven; like some ancient prophet, the acid was turned to water upon contact. To his left, flames from a candelabrum bent in the air and seemed to fall back on the user. It was chaos.

Goldman coughed blood for the first time, which produced the opposite effect his body intended; he went back into the fight. He raised his head, grabbed his sphere with one arm and began to levitate. He was now at the same level as Raven.

"Look at that." The figure said. "Almost as if he weren't insignificant."

With one arm, he hurled the sphere with all his force, but instead of waiting for it to rebound back to him, he used his Emblem to orbit it around Raven. Quickly repositioning himself behind the figure, Goldman threw himself at him and pulled the sphere with the intent of crushing him between the two forces. Raven dodged the attack with minimal difficulty. Goldman, anticipating this, used the sphere as propulsion and launched himself at the masked figure. He then grabbed the mask and squeezed as hard as he could. A crack rang out; it seemed to fracture when the figure shoved him away before he could do more damage.

"Do not touch that." He said, his voice now sharper. "It is mine."

Goldman, falling to the ground again, coughed a black, coagulated blood.

No, Goldman. Please no. Harry remembered the herculean feats of the golden-hearted pirate. Harry then realized that even the greatest of men have limits. His optimism, however, seemed not to falter, as he smiled having almost managed to tear the mask from the figure's face.

Raven, as if done with the pirate, turned his attention to the skeleton, who seemed reinvigorated and ready to fight.

The Locksmith used one of his doors, against the Astronomer's advice, who lost his grip on the young man's shoulder. He reached the fallen man.

"Goldman!" He held his large hand. "Hold on, we're going to finish him and then—"

"No..." He whispered, his voice growing weaker. "He is a child, Harry. Can't you see? His... petulance and easily... irritated behavior... He is a child being punished."

The realization seemed to give Harry an idea, and he did not let go of Goldman's hand. Smiling calmly, Goldman squeezed the Locksmith's hand with a steadiness that only a true friend could give. Harry, his eyes blurred, was not startled by the cold sensation of tears running down his cheek. He embraced the man, who seemed to have been infected by the earlier attack.

Still crying, he closed his friend's eyes and, with a determined rage, turned to face Raven, who continued fighting two figures; a boxing skeleton and the son of darkness.

Matthews was trading blows with the figure, who seemed to enjoy letting him land one every now and then, as long as it wasn't to the face. Even so, he dodged most of the strikes with both hands behind his back, making Harry's brother even more enraged. Manes, when he could, lashed the figure in the gaps between exchanges.

If this keeps up... Matthews added inwardly, without showing his concern. Despite the occasional hits, whenever Raven landed one on him, he felt his skeleton crack slightly. It was a strange sensation, like a pressure building from within that could shatter him entirely. How long had it been since he felt pain? How long since he had cried or genuinely laughed with happiness? He couldn't blame it all on his brother, of course. Especially now that death itself was staring into the empty space where his eyes once were.

Manes was now pulling back, sensing the pressure was too strong to stay close. Madeline and Vela also kept their distance, as each punch and block between the two was accompanied by a sonic boom. Now that the room continued to tremble, more and more books kept falling from shelves that seemed to be an endless source of literature.

A crack rang out; a tiny fissure was visible in his knuckle when he clenched it. Another crack. This time it was not only his knuckle, but several other parts of his body; he would never win this fight. Despite his earlier conviction, he had reached a dreadful conclusion: he was not fighting an opponent, but a demon. One that was as resilient as it was powerful. This was the fight of an unstoppable force against an almost immovable object.

"Harry!" Matthews called out to the young thief, who remained close to Goldman's body. "I absolve you!"

The shout derailed the Locksmith's thoughts entirely. "...What!?" He shouted back between the cracks that were now audible.

"I forgive you, brother." He said. "I know I put a lot on you. I know that... Ouch... I know this wasn't the life you wanted, but I understand what you tried to do with it. So I forgive you. I just wish we'd had more time together before—"

One last crack rang out before Raven struck him in the face, splitting the skull in two.

Harry felt the entire world around him go silent. The only sound was the violent Thump! Thump! of his heart. All over again. It was the only thought he had. He remembered Devon, his mother, Prism, and Petrikov. He remembered Goldman. Everything was crumbling so fast. How much time did he have left to fix it all? How many wishes could he make to set it all right? Would he hold out? But he knew that even if he couldn't, he had to try.

Goldman had died. His brother had died. Now it was personal.
