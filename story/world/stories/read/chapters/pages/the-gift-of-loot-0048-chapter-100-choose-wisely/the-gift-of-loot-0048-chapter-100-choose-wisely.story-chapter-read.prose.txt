One week later

_____

Thomas sat on a pretty uncomfortable plastic chair behind a cheap Costco card table. His booth at the LA Free Marketplace was nothing special and blended in with the mishmash of other vendor setups in the same row.

Still, he was doing better than the people who'd just brought out blankets to display their wares. There was even a guy a little down the way who was selling low-grade, level 1 mana crystals out of a beer cooler.

In Thomas's humble opinion, his setup stood out to those who knew quality at a glance. No one else had Level 3 Light, Electric, and Nature mana crystals for sale.

People who were wandering by, window shopping, constantly dropped by to ask about prices and maybe catch a hint about which dungeon had dropped the mana.

No hints required. Thomas had a stack of flyers with maps that constantly needed to be refreshed. As word spread, more people were coming by.

Because Thomas was nobody's fool, he only kept the lower D and C grade crystals out on display. A few had gone missing here and there, anyway. The thefts pained him but he had a lot more where that came from. The crystals worked to catch eyes and attention, though.

Prices in the LA Marketplace had gotten a little weird since the last time he was there. Cash was still king, but more and more people had signs at their booths stating "Mana Preferred."

Thomas had watched a woman dressed in scrubs trade a gold necklace for some Ocean mana crystals a few booths down. It seemed like the barter system was seeing a resurgence.

It was kinda fun being a part of it.

"Okay, so we're cutting Duke out," Twitch said from a few feet behind him. As usual, the guy was completely unable to stand still and was pacing back and forth while talking rapidly on his cell phone. "He promised to bring a team of professional drywall tradesmen, like union grade, and instead he brought one guy who said he knows drywall. Fuck him, who else you got?" He paused, waiting for an answer, then fished a spiral notepad out of his pocket. "Yeah, I've heard of him. He'll work for mana? Wait, hold on, I got another call." He quickly took the phone away from his ear, punched a button on the screen, and replaced it. "Hey Manuel, speak of the devil. You looking for a job?"

Twitch was practically a new man. He'd taken to managing the Light Dungeon Facility with gusto and was happy to use all his local contacts to get people back to work.

He'd also reached level 3 and undergone the purge. As Thomas predicted, the process had completely scrubbed him of his tattoos, leaving evenly tanned, clear skin. It was weird how the shape of a face changed with the addition or subtraction of ink. If he hadn't seen the process himself, Thomas would have had a hard time identifying him as the same guy.

Twitch was still thin, but now he looked like a lean ultramarathon runner. He had lost the overall skeletal look, and braces (for some reason) in the process, and had shaved his head to a neat stubble. With the addition of some new pressed clothes, he now had the sharp air of a put-together, corporate shark. He acted like one, too. Thomas was glad he was on his side.

Finishing up his call, Twitch dropped into the plastic chair next to Thomas.

"Okay, here's the status: I got another drywall crew coming in, and they should be competent this time. The demolition team said they're almost done pulling out the jail doors and grinding more stone out to deepen the rooms for privacy and shit. I got Manuel and his cousin on remapping the electrical through the new areas—yeah, and I know it's not really electricity, it's Light mana bullshit, but he says he got a Gift for it. So." Twitch paused and flipped a couple of pages in a small spiral notebook he had taken to carrying with him. "Oh, and the vending machine guys are coming on Wednesday."

That took Thomas by surprise. "Vending machines?"

"Yeah, Manuel said he got a line on some of the real fancy rigs, like the ones in Japan. We're creating a common room where people can stop in for snacks or whatever. Oh, and all the trade guys accept mana at the standard payment rate."

"Cool," Thomas said after a moment, grateful beyond telling that he didn't have to manage most of this. Twitch's endless energy was being put to good use.

Not that Thomas had been sitting around doing nothing. It occurred to him that he had valuable contacts with the Ross-Astor family, who happened to really hate the de’Montvallons.

After a quick call to Robert Ross of the Ross-Astor family, he was able to get a few guards from that family to help them out with the facility, for the price of letting them dive the dungeon a few times a week. Thomas got the impression part of it was the Ross-Astors thumbing their noses at the de’Montvallons, but the guards came in handy when another de’Montvallon branch family showed up a few days after the team's boon wore off, looking for answers.

They were overwhelmed and sent packing, thankfully without casualties, but the message was relayed.

Zach didn't think the main family, who were the actual powerhouses, would get involved. The real danger was the Light elves. So far, there hadn't been any sign of them.

Thomas hoped they were still wary enough of the System to not attack overtly yet. He suspected they had shifted their energies to other avenues of takeover. One low-leveled dungeon by elven standards wasn't enough to risk open conflict.

At least, not yet.

Thomas's attention was quickly diverted when a woman wearing leathers, like she was in some kind of Amazonian cosplay, came up to the table.

"Now, where did you boys get all that Light mana?" she asked, looking them up and down. She smiled a man-eating smile. "And are those really level three crystals?"

Thomas smiled back. He'd noticed a definite uptick in attention since his Purge, though not as much as Zach got after his Purge, damn him. "Sure is. We're selling here, but I also have a location if you wanna try the dungeon yourself."

Her eyebrows raised. "No catch?"

"Only the entrance fee to the facility. It's out in the middle of the desert, so you'll want to be under shade." He started his now well-worn spiel, and once she saw he knew his business, she dropped the overt flirtation. That was fine; she still walked away with a map and a promise to talk it over with her team. Word was spreading.

"We should have more information packets out on the table," Thomas said absently. "Stuff about the Integration deadline and what happens to people who don't get their Gifts before the countdown ends."

"Except we'd be speaking to the choir. Most people here have their Gifts. That's why they're shopping for mana," Twitch pointed out. "Plus, if you push too hard, you look like a conspiracy theorist. That’ll freak people out and then won’t listen."

"We need to get the word out, though," Thomas said. "The government isn't stepping up. Someone has to."

"Yeah, yeah." Clearly unbothered, Twitch turned his phone screen Thomas's way to show a colorful Instagram account full of tattoos. "What do you think of this guy's catalog? He says he mixes his inks with mana."

Thomas wouldn't soon forget the feeling of Corruption mana being jammed into his back. He shuddered. "If he uses something you're not compatible with, you're going to have a bad time."

"Yeah, but if I ever go through the Purge again, I'll go back to a blank slate. It's boring," Twitch whined, then was distracted a few seconds later as his phone rang. He picked it up with an obnoxious, "Talk to me."

Thomas rolled his eyes and then stood to help another customer who had questions about the Electric mana.

_____

"Heads up, dudes. I got lunch."

Zach popped in from out of nowhere, as was his habit, carrying three bags that smelled like greasy deliciousness.

Thomas, who'd been flagging under brutal SoCal heat, perked up. "Food?"

Twitch put away his phone, just having ended another call. "What's up, pretty boy?"

"Takes one to know one," Zach said easily, setting down the bags on the table. Twitch chuckled at the scored point.

Zach had gone through the Purge, but the effects were different for him. He'd always been good looking (Thomas had working eyeballs), with blonde hair and athletic body. Now he’d lost his hints of early wear from years of busting in the sun and living rough. However, the true change had been to his blue eyes. They now carried an inner glow of light and fire that was so striking it gave him an ethereal, fey look. Thomas had seen several women literally stop in their tracks and stare at him.

Zach either didn’t seem to notice or care about the attention, but seemed delighted that he now had a cleaner connection to his mana, too, now there were no toxins plugging up the works.

Considering Thomas' new physique and Jo's show-stopping radiance, the team as a whole looked like they belonged on the cover of a magazine. Out of all the weirdest, most unexpected things that had happened to Thomas since first stepping into a dungeon, that was near the top.

He hid his discomfort by opening a bag to see what Zach had brought him. The answer was a truly massive burger and thick-cut steak fries.

Definitely time to start reintroducing some of those good old-fashioned toxins back into his body.

"Where did this come from?" he asked.

"A little place I know in Napa," Zach said. "Absolutely choice wine there, usually, but sales have been off the charts with people stocking up like it's doomsday, and everything that goes great with red meat's sold out."

Thomas did not care a bit about wine. He ate a thick fry with an appreciative sound. "You Teleport there or Light Speed it?"

Zach sighed. "Teleportation, all the way. I still haven't cracked the light speed problem yet."

Twitch looked up at that. "What's that mean? You can't use your skill?"

"Nah, I can." Zach casually waved the worry away. "It's just the light speed stuff is really short-range. Like... okay, it's hard to wrap my mind around what was going on at first, but guess what also travels at the speed of light? Light does. So if I—" in less than a blink he was standing on the other side of the table, "—use my Light Speed skill to move, it's like the world is in total pause while I travel. I'm moving with the photons. If I try to move past them or around them, I can’t see. I only get that moment to, like, take it all in."

"I... guess?" Twitch said doubtfully.

"It makes it hard for him to use that skill for long distances, like running up to Northern California," Thomas translated. He was getting good at Zach-speak.

"Right." Zach nodded. "I’m basically moving blind. But it's even worse than that 'cause from my point of view, moving around the table still took a few seconds. It's instant to you, but still the normal travel time for me."

Twitch squinted at him for a second then surprised the hell out of Thomas by saying, "I thought special relativity worked the other direction? You experience less time the closer you get to light speed."

Zach threw his hands up. "I know, right?"

"Magic bullshit," Thomas concluded and bit into a burger. He'd never been a physics guy, either.

"So you can use Light Speed to zip around a room, and teleportation to go for burgers across the state?" Twitch asked, and at Zach's nod said, "That means you're still good to go with the dungeon tonight?"

"Hell yeah. Jo said she's probably going to hit level four today, right? Now she bought the last of the Shadow and Poison from the Dark Elves?"

"Right," Thomas agreed. "We've got a few adventurer customers paying to dive the dungeon, even though there's all the construction around it. I think it's time to see if the System thinks our quest is done or what."

Zach slanted him a sly, knowing look. "Have you thought about what the reward might be?"

Thomas grinned back. "That’s all I've been thinking about."

_____

Jo definitely had that 'just leveled up' glow about her when Zach popped them all in front of the dungeon later that evening.

"Congratulations on level four," Thomas said with a smile. "How do you feel?"

Her return grin was sharp. "Like I'm ready to stab straight through some bosses."

"I think you'll get your wish tonight," he said. "You saw Mariah when you were shopping over at Aether, right? How is she?"

Instantly, Jo lost her sharpness and instead rolled her eyes, all mom-exasperation. "Same as Ashton, just obsessed with completing Elf Dungeon School before her fifteenth birthday. She says she's learning a lot, and if it makes her safer and more ready for her to dive a dungeon..." She trailed off and shrugged, though unwillingly.

Thomas knew how she felt. It had been like a fire had been lit under Ashton's ass. He'd never seen his nephew more enthusiastic about classes. Then again, while it had been a while since his school days, he didn't remember them ever allowing him to handle weapons there. Maybe that was the difference.

Ashton had flat out refused to go back to Earth until his term was up. Learning about dungeons and training was all he wanted to do, and the Dark Elves were happy to provide him the opportunity.

Kingston and Braxton weren't as single-mindedly focused as their big brother, and while they had enjoyed their classes, they were willing to return to Earth and their 'normal' lives.

Talia had pulled Thomas aside and told him that Braxton had indeed tested as a potential healer. That was something they did upon enrollment for all children, apparently. Braxton was still unaware, but he would have to face a decision when it came time to unlock his Gift.

Thomas hadn't told Derek about that yet. He’d just spoken to him today, and the whole incident had been ugly.

His brother had finally returned from who-knew-where, acting like he'd just come back from vacation. The full story about how his children had taken shelter on a whole other planet had been a shock, but... not enough. Thomas couldn't put his finger on it, but Derek's reactions had been weirdly muted.

Then he instantly snapped into his normal self by demanding to know why he and each of the boys didn't get their own room in Thomas's new house instead of sharing. There had been no talk about the apartment, all they left behind, or the fact his children had been in danger. Thomas didn’t mind if Derek was angry at him, he just wanted him to show a little concern for his kids.

But no, Derek just started going off about how Thomas should use his powers to help others, instead of wasting it dungeon diving, and something in him snapped. Thomas had come scarily close to just hauling off and punching Derek in the nose. The Burden backlash would have been worth it.

Except, at that moment, he was dead certain it wouldn't cause a backlash. The second he pictured himself hurting Derek, he felt... nothing. Like Derek was a dungeon monster.

Or maybe Thomas was becoming the monster. There was, after all, Corruption in his core.

That thought had been unsettling enough to make him hesitate.

He remembered Derek stepping forward, probably to drive home a point. Maybe just to push him, like they were kids again. There had been light in his eyes and more passion about bossing Thomas around than concern for his kids. He hadn’t even asked where Ashton was.

With a screaming neigh, Problem Child had rushed around the corner—it had come back with the children, which meant Braxton and Kingston were nearby, probably listening to the argument on the sly—and rushed right at Derek with needle teeth gleaming.

Derek had screamed, "What the fuck?!" and kicked at the unicorn toy, which deftly avoided the blow and bit him just above the ankle.

It hadn't been anything close to a fatal wound, though Derek screamed like it was and threw a complete fit which culminated in him leaving the house. Meanwhile, Problem Child circled and rubbed against Thomas's own legs, purring like a cat. Or maybe growling. It was hard to tell with that thing.

The upshot was Derek was gone, again, and Thomas had left the unicorn to guard Braxton and Kingston while he was out of the house. Had that been the right thing to do? Should he have tried to go after Derek? He was obviously having a rough time, but Thomas was getting so sick of his shit—

"Thomas?" Jo touched his arm, and he realized he'd been lost in thought.

"Sorry." He smiled down at her. "Just thinking about my nephews." And their asshole father. "Anyway, are we ready?" He looked around at the others.

He got a nod and a cheery thumbs-up from Twitch and Zach, respectively. Jo looked at him with concern for a moment, but then seemed to shrug it off. "I'm ready when you are. Let's speed run this dungeon."

After all, the real prize would hopefully be in the reward room.

_____

Maybe it was because Thomas knew what to expect, but the Wisp dungeon was no biggie.

Nah, it was definitely his team. Everyone had upgraded in some form or another, and they were ripping through the wisps like they were tissue paper.

Twitch had always been hard to kill by virtue of his Gift of Endurance, but now with the Earth Receives All Blows skill, he caught hits with muted thuds like the wisps were striking an unmoving sandbag, not a person.

The catch was he had to see the strike coming, but Twitch had never had tunnel vision. His attention darted all over the place, and so it was hard for enemies to sneak up on him.

Jo had apparently absorbed her Toxin of Everything skill. Thomas saw it work firsthand when she struck back at a Nature mana-aspected sub-boss. When she stabbed it, the sub-boss began to boil from the inside.

"I turned what they hit me with into poison, and it returned back to them," she explained, at Thomas's look. "I think the skill should've been called 'an eye for an eye'."

Her pure joy at being able to poison an enemy with its own magic was a little disturbing, but Thomas kept that thought to himself.

Of course, these were level three creatures, and as a level four, she way overpowered them. He wondered how well the mana poison would work on same-leveled opponents.

Zach's fire came out burning clean with no trace of smoke behind it. He dodged blows with an eerie grace, and when that wasn't enough, used his Light Speed to either get out of the way or right into the perfect spot to strike. At some points, he seemed to be in several places at once. He was turning into a fire speedster.

His issue, of course, was that Light Speed and Teleportation ate up a lot of mana. He had to slow down halfway through to save some for the boss fight.

Thomas didn't have an empty slot to add another skill, but he had received a hell of an overall boost from the Purge and his renewed core. It was like his thoughts ran clearer. He could see more details with his Combat Foresight and react to them faster than ever before. Any minor injuries he took were a breeze to fix with his Living Architecture and Wellspring. The new rainbow of colors wasn't nearly as confusing to his Eyes of Riches sight, and he made sure to identify and target standout wisps, which always dropped higher-level mana.

Once, he even got a Nature mana potion, which seemed to affect plant life. He planned to either give that one to his mother or Zach, who still had his plant from the last dungeon’s drops.

Everything just came easier. He moved faster and was barely out of breath. When he struck with his halberd of the electric knuckles, he hit like a god damned tank. It was as if this dungeon had been switched to easy mode.

At last, the bosses were defeated, the final bits of loot collected (it was a good haul of mana, but everything seemed to pale after his System boon had run out), and they faced the exit reward room.

Thomas was practically vibrating with anticipation.

Catching his eye, Zach laughed and gestured for him to open the door. "You first, Mana Man. Maybe your Gift will give us that extra edge."

"Pretty sure it doesn't work that way," Thomas said, but opened the door anyway. He walked through. "My Gift never affected the reward chest--"

He stopped.

There was only one ornate chest inside, and it had ‘Thomas Coldstrike’ emblazoned on it.

"That isn't right--guys?" He glanced back towards his team only to find there was no one behind him.

The door he'd just come through was gone as well.

Heart thumping, Thomas turned in place. "Guys?" he called again, and his voice echoed in the small stone room.

He was alone in here, except for the final exit door and the single chest that had his name.

Was his team in danger? No, they might have rushed through the dungeon, but they'd completed it. Plus, Thomas had held them back at the entrance for the required time for the instance to close. That was non-negotiable. No one had followed them in.

They were fine, but he had been separated from them.

Why?

His eyes went to the reward chest with his name on it.

"I guess the System wants to talk?"

Thomas took a hesitant step toward it, then another. Heart thumping, he opened the chest.

A heavy vellum scroll lay inside, sitting on a pillow. He unrolled it.

Thomas Coldstrike,

Congratulations, you have met the terms of your quest. The Light Eleven colony of Aether has been barred from this dungeon, and the facility has been returned to the native population of your planet.

In recognition of your success at great personal risk, you have been granted a reward. Choose one of the following:

The twelve-hour boon granted at the start of your quest, which amplified your Gift of Loot, shall be made permanent.

One existing skill of your choosing shall be upgraded.

You shall receive a new skill, Master Level Prism Eye of Illusions, which is available only through the System and selected specifically for you.

Choose with wisdom.

Brow furrowed, Thomas read through the scroll again, just to make sure he had it right.

Well, good news: He'd completed the quest. Those choices, though...

The first option was tempting, if he was only focused on the short term. The loot rewards had been amazing, and since they worked outside dungeons, they were loud and really obvious. How often did he get in a life-and-death fight outside dungeons?

He grimaced: Certainly more nowadays than he ever had before the System.

The point was, the boon was great but also made it very easy for outsiders to guess what his Gift was. Could he afford that risk? How often would he use the boon?

It almost physically hurt, but he had to look seriously at the second and third options.

The second was to choose an existing skill to upgrade.

The fact that skills could be upgraded was news to him. Which would he choose? Living Architecture and Wellspring skills were both powerful in their own right. Maybe Combat Foresight? It had saved him more times than he could count, and seeing more moves an opponent could make was always a good thing.

Still, as a reward it was a little 'bleh.'

The last option, though...

He didn't think it was his imagination: The wording and detail felt like the System was pushing for it.

A skill unique to him. One that, apparently, the System felt he needed. Oh yeah, and he'd get it without having to reach level 5 first.

Master Level Prism Eye of Illusions.

That would be his third vision-type skill. So, would it allow him to see illusions?

No, he decided after a moment. He got the strong feeling that this would allow him to cast illusions. He did have Light mana in his mix, after all.

Just off the top of his head, Thomas could imagine defensive and offensive applications to using illusions. That was real mage-stuff. Crazy magic on par with being able to heal at a touch.

The System had certainly picked something that spoke to him on a deep level, something that, to his surprise, he didn't realize he wanted until it was offered to him.

Was that why the System had offered it? It saw the shape of his soul and had given him the Gift of Loot. Could it see his, what, future? Or had it seen how he fought and had picked something he could use to defend himself that easily fell within bounds of being a healer?

Pressing his lips together, Thomas's gaze flicked between the boon and the free skill. Both were good. On a purely greedy level, he wanted that boon permanently. He could be careful, right? Make sure that he wasn't closely involved in any conflicts that could give away his Gift.

Right. Like he could control that.

If the Light elves or anyone got wind of his true Gift, he'd be so screwed.

Thomas didn't want to choose out of fear, so that's why the third option was so nice. He wanted the free skill just as much. It was a whole new well of power without the drawback.

Besides, his Gift would evolve on its own after level 5.

This was why he had been separated from the others. He needed to make the choice for himself. He wanted to be a combat healer. The ability to trick enemies with illusions brought him that much closer.

Thomas touched his finger to the third option. "I choose the skill."

The scroll flashed golden, and the letters flowed up to his pointer finger. Then, just like a mana crystal or skill shard, they sank into his skin.

Gentle warmth bloomed behind his eyes for a moment, and Thomas felt a bright line connect down to his core. He became aware of knowledge that was suddenly there in the back of his mind: this skill would run off both Light and Wellspring mana.

It really was meant for him. He couldn't wait to test it out. Would the team kill him if he suggested they rerun the dungeon again?

Smiling, Thomas glanced down at the now empty scroll, then out of habit at the chest.

A second scroll lay on the velvet pillow inside the chest.

He'd bet every bit of loot he had ever collected that had not been there a few seconds before.

"Uh," Thomas said intelligently, then bent and picked up the second scroll. He unrolled it.

Thomas Coldstrike,

Once again, the System congratulates you on facing and overcoming tribulations far above your level. In addition, each choice you have made through the duration of the quest indicates an unwavering willingness to defend the peoples of Earth.

Healer, the System does not further Burden you with the mantle of Champion. However, extraordinary circumstances require those who are willing to stand between your planet and those who would wish to illegally subjugate it.

You are offered the position of System Warden and charged with safeguarding the integrity of Earth's Integration.

Should you accept, you will be granted immediate choices of a class commensurate to your new station.

Your choice is strictly voluntary, with no penalty should you decline.

Oh. This was why he had been separated from the others.
