Now that he was kind of coming back to himself after having his shoulder and half his chest blown away, Luke could see some similarities between the greater demon standing in front of him and one of his more recent friends. Something about the eyes and the way the side of his mouth twisted upward in amusement.

"You're Lemira's brother?"

"Half-brother," the greater demon corrected. "Zorath. Pleased to make your acquaintance."

Luke, not minding the pause in fighting in the least, kept it going while he struggled to find some way to beat Lemira's half-brother into oblivion. "You made quite an entrance."

"A good way to get someone's attention, I've found. But I bear no ill will against you. It was unfortunate, but I could not join those who made the transition over to Earth. Father thought Lemira would leap at the chance to come here, but I can see that she stayed behind."

"She did," Luke said, eyeing the mass of demons surrounding them. "Your father is still alive, I'm guessing?"

"He wishes to see you."

Luke nodded. "Figures."

"Will you act like a gentleman and come of your own volition?"

"I'll come," Luke said. Biding his time was his best bet at the moment. Every second that passed replenished his mana. If he wanted to have a chance at winning against someone like Zorath, or even his father, he needed to be bursting with mana. The problem was that he wasn't sure that would be enough. And for Ezezal, Lemira's father? Well, Luke would just have to cross that river when he got there. At least he'd been a reasonable man, demon, at their last meeting, maybe they could come to some sort of understanding? Then again, that had all been a ploy to get Lemira to Earth and use her as an anchor, bringing about this whole mess. Yeah, this mess was far from over.

The one thing that made him feel at least a smidge of optimism was the fact that the quest didn't say he had to kill a boss. Going through the hole to Infernal Flux, that was what he'd expected, and what he'd readied himself for. What hubris to think himself capable of doing this on his own.

"Penny for your thoughts," Zorath said, walking next to Luke after having gestured for him to go on toward the massive city.

Luke scooped up his staff as they went, ignoring the gory remains of his arm. "Just considering what your father might want."

"Earth."

"Great," Luke said, clearing his throat. Best change the subject. "Are you Lilineese, like Lemira?"

A smile played across Zorath's face, and he nodded, "I am. Few races are compatible with demonkind. The Lilineese is one of the few we've discovered."

Luke glanced over at the half-demon, a terrible realization dawning on him. "And humans?"

Zorath's smile changed. It was difficult to read, but Luke thought it looked eager, and the answer came in an almost purring voice. "Yes, humans. Progress."

Doing his best, and probably failing, to mask his disgust, Luke turned to look at the city. "Is this Infernal Flux?"

"I have a proposition for you," Zorath said without even looking at Luke.

"A proposition?"

"Do you believe that I am strong enough to kill you?"

Luke forced his hand to remain at his side rather than scratch at his new shoulder. "Maybe."

"What about my father?"

"He's strong," Luke said, remembering their previous meeting.

Zorath stopped near a rock outcropping and turned around, surveying the land behind them as if making sure the other demons weren't following. They weren't.

"Your meeting with my father will not end well for you, I believe. I would like to offer you an alternative to a protracted and... unpleasant end."

"I'm listening," Luke said, unsure of what to expect. Making deals with demons was a famously bad thing to consider, but Zorath was a half-demon, like Lemira. Maybe he could be trusted to some extent? No, who was he kidding? Looking at Zorath, Luke didn't need Weaver's Perception to tell that there was no way in hell that trusting this creature would turn out well for anyone, but he didn't have much choice other than listening.

"My father is old. I believe his strength has waned over the years, especially after having part of himself confined to the home of my late mother for such an extended period of time."

"So he's weak?"

Zorath showed small but pointed teeth through a weak, thin smile. "No. Far from it, but I am strong. Do you see where I am going with this?"

"I think so," Luke said. "You want to help me kill your father."

The words made the half-demon's skin turn a paler shade of red, and the slight widening of his eyes told Luke everything he needed to know. Zorath was afraid, but the set of his jaw also showed determination.

"Can you do it? Kill your father?"

"Together, we might stand a chance."

"No," Luke said. "I mean, he is your father."

Zorath looked at Luke, not even a hint of understanding in his eyes.

"Never mind," Luke said.

"Will you consider my proposal?"

"You help me with Ezezal and, what? You'll want something in return."

"The same thing you offered my half-sister."

Luke raised an eyebrow. "You want to stay in my system? On Earth?"

"I have reason to believe my physiology would be able to adapt, like Lemira's. It would take more time, and the pain would be... something to behold, but yes. That is what I want."

"Let me think about it, yeah?" Luke said.

Zorath set them into motion again, heading straight for the tall gates of the dark city. "Time is a luxury. Do not ponder my offer until it turns into grains of sand and sifts through your fingers."

They made it to the gate, and it opened without a single demon showing up to greet them. Beyond, an empty street waited for them.

"The air itself has eyes and ears beyond this point," Zorath said, his eyes fixed straight ahead. "Be wary."

"Will they attack us?" Luke asked, looking up at the buildings. They were far more human than he would have expected.

"No."

Considering Zorath's deal, Luke wondered about something. "Hey, Z-man, how are the other demons able to be on Earth without dying?"

"The system event provides allowances."

"And the demons up there? They are avatars of actual demons from here. What about Lemira?"

"I don't understand the question."

"Is it really Lemira on Earth with my friends? Or is it an avatar, like the other greater demons?"

"I'm not sure what you mean by 'greater demon', but Lemira is herself. She was born to a Lilineese woman in her own world, and she has never set foot here. In a way, she was lucky."

Luke breathed a sigh of relief. "Lucky? How so?"

"Once you come here," Zorath said, turning to grin at Luke while gesturing at a large mansion at the end of the street, "It is most difficult to leave."

"That's Ezezal's house?"

"It is not, but he is staying there while in this part of our homeworld. I believe he intended to gift the city and its inhabitants to Lemira if she had been the one to come."

"That was his plan?"

"She is of Earth now, and he needs her there, but she would be able to make brief visits."

"I'm not sure I'm following."

"We have arrived," Zorath said, eyeing the mansion. "I would have your answer before we enter."

More demons had shown themselves now. These were stronger than those out on the plains, far stronger, and Zorath did not want their attention on them for longer than necessary. Many pairs of eyes regarded them, from inside buildings, by the street, and even from the gloom above. Not wanting to tip them off, Luke nodded to the half-demon, signaling that he accepted the deal.

If Luke wanted to make it out of this place, he would need all the help that he could get, and Zorath was strong. There was no doubt about that. Remembering Ezezal, he just wondered if even that would be enough. That instance had been an avatar that didn't even possess the demon's full strength. Luke shuddered at the thought. There would be no holding back if it came to blows. He would have to play all his cards, and even that might not be enough.

Perhaps there would be a peaceful way out of all of this. Luke held on to that foolish hope as he entered the mansion through gilded doors and stepped into a lit hall with rooms off to both sides, and a double set of stairs leading up to a second floor. A powerful presence clung to the air, making it difficult to even breathe. Cold hatred filled the entire house. Ezezal didn't even try to conceal it. Which was why it was a bit strange to see his ever-pleasant smile as he approached the railing at the top of the stairs.

"Luke Quinn, Lifeweaver, pleased to see you again. Won't you join me in my office? We have much to discuss."
