Ethan walked along the top of the Fable Train, which had grown truly enormous. Looking back, the sun glinted off over a dozen chrome cars, each over a hundred feet long. Ethan’s spirit had been pumping more power into Fable than she’d been able to handle, and so they’d started adding more cars, these ones now containing a good portion of the army. Only the most powerful Twilights stayed out, walking in two columns, one on either side of the train, which still bristled with weapons.

The reason for this growth loomed over Ethan as he strolled along the train with Apollo and Selina. His spirit glowed with power like a beacon, and the range of its corruption devouring aura now spread for several miles in every direction. The larger the range, the more powerful it became. The more powerful, the more it grew, and the wider that range became.

Ethan was continually annoyed that he didn’t have a more productive way to spend that power, but he’d been warned of the consequences of this path. His spirit ultimately kept the entire army moving by virtue of fueling Apollo, but Ethan couldn’t benefit from this mind-boggling power. Selina was experimenting with rune combinations with Fable, but for now, the spirit was stuck where it was, and beyond the reach of his army, Ethan was on his own.

Not that he wasn’t growing used to that. Strategus still appeared every few days to remind him of that fact, and the promise he’d made. Still, they weren’t ready yet.

“A day of natural healing has helped,” Ethan said, stretching his recently reattached arm gingerly.

“I’ll keep healing it every half hour until you leave,” Apollo said. “The last thing you need is for it to get a bit loose mid-fight.”

“You’re sure you’re okay with me leaving again so soon?” Ethan asked.

Apollo laughed. “Believe it or not, I find your company somewhat superfluous. Besides, I have Zak if I need entertainment, or Strat if I want an Olympic-level bummer.”

Ethan smiled. “Perfect. So what about recruitment? You mentioned there was news yesterday, but I passed out.”

“We know now that we need to speed things up, to get back ahead of the Followers. Thankfully, Selina and I were already working on something like that–although we hadn’t planned for the sudden time-pressure.”

“That’s what Walter is for,” Selina said brightly. “We’ve identified a few demons that are especially capable of capturing other Twilights. Right now we have two crab-things, a lobster-thing, and some kind of monkey. What we really want is a gigas–but a live one, this time.”

Ethan nodded. “Right, so something big and durable that can drag another Twilight without dealing out, or taking too much damage.”

“Exactly,” Selina said. “Anyway, the plan is to send them out with their own little squads of weaker demons. Those small ones run around, and get the attention of other Twilights, drawing them back. Then our big boys carry them home.”

“Excellent,” Ethan said. “That’s basically what I was doing. Might be even be faster in some cases. I’m surprised you have that level of control, Apollo.”

“I don’t,” he said. “Not for anything I can’t see, and my hold is weaker the farther away one of them gets. Thankfully, Fable has come up with solutions for that, with Selina’s runes to help.”

“We were talking about relay towers to communicate better,” Selina said. “Like cell towers, back on your world. That got us thinking about Apollo’s own signal getting weak.”

“So you’re making towers to help broadcast Apollo’s mind-control?”

“Not what I’d like to call it,” Apollo said. “But essentially, yes. They’re costly, and complex, and take a long time for Fable to create, but they allow me to extend my range and control a considerable distance. Combine that with some basic cameras, and suddenly I’m playing a weird, crab-grab video game.”

“That’s fantastic,” Ethan said. “How many do we have?”

“Only three so far,” Selina said. “Walter, Stephen, and Gary. We should be able to field another one every week or so.”

Ethan nodded. “Great work. Keep doing this kind of stuff while I’m gone. We have too much power stored up. It’s great that we don’t have to worry over-much of an attack from the Followers, but I wish we could be more productive with it all.”

“We’ll keep working on it,” Apollo said. “Just stay safe out there. I hate to remind you again, but this all falls apart without you.”

Ethan sighed, glancing down at his recently severed arm. “I know.”

***

“You really didn’t need to give me a ride,” Ethan said.

“I wanted to!” Selina said. “Plus, Walter is doing all the work. We just sit here in our cozy Fable Shack, watching the woods for anything suspicious…”

Ethan chuckled, looking at the tall woman bent over the many monitors lining the walls. To Fable’s credit, the so-called shack was quite comfortable, and well-protected. It was a Twilight construct, albeit a small one. “What are you hoping to find out here?” Ethan asked.

“I’m not sure,” Selina admitted. “But I’m convinced we were attacked by more than one something. Whatever attacked was willing to take on a Twilight, so at the very least, we might want it on our side. Maybe it’s a pack of demons that hunts together? We haven’t seen that much at Twilight rank, but it could end up being even better than Walter at hauling back new recruits.”

“If you say so. Just be careful,” Ethan said. “Don’t leave the bunker, and call if you’re in trouble.”

“I will! And I’ll be fine. You’re the maniac disappearing into the jungle, alone, and in search of something that can turn you to stone.”

“My rune can fight it, don’t worry. Now that I’m Twilight myself, that attack shouldn’t be as effective. Abilities like that only seem devastating when aimed at weaker targets. If he could just look at anyone his power level and insta-win, he’d have been a lot more dangerous.”

“Didn’t you mention him doing it to some other Twilight, back where Michael found the bad god mojo?”

“I can’t believe you said mojo. Stop watching movies with Zakiel.”

“He only wants to watch Austin Powers. Every. Single. Day. I’m fascinated by it. He laughs in the same exact way, at every joke, every time. I’m writing a paper on it.”

“As long as it makes him happy,” Ethan said. “Wait,” he added, leaning forward. “Is that one?”

Selina squinted. “I think so. I’ll steer Walter in that direction.”

Ethan felt the crab-demon shifting under him as it shifted toward the odd-looking tree Ethan had spotted. As hoped, it burst into dust as the enormous creature crashed into it.

“This is the place,” Ethan said happily. “Alright, don’t follow me, we don’t want to risk Walter. Or you.”

“I’ll be good,” Selina said. “The ambush was farther north anyway, I’m going to head back that way.”

“Be safe!” Ethan called, then flashed outside with a rift.

He jumped from the crab, landing on ground still soft from the last storm. It didn’t take long to identify a path of stone trees, and he hurried in that direction.

***

The dense forest wasn’t particularly difficult to navigate for someone of Ethan’s rank. Wrapped in illusion and barely visible, he moved from tree branch to tree branch at a reckless pace. The limbs were thick enough that they barely reacted to his weight, and he moved erratically enough not to fear an ambush.

The miles disappeared behind him and he followed the trail of stone. The entire forest hadn’t been turned, far from it. But there was an obvious path that had clearly been used numerous times. It was easy to follow, as Ethan could almost always see a stone tree in the distance, and he simply teleported above the canopy for a better look if he ever lost track.

Still, it took two more hours to find where the trail led, and he paused on a particularly tall tree, looking down on the wide mouth of a dark cave.

“That has to be it,” he said.

“The trail ends here,” Tomo agreed.

The cave was well-hidden, despite its size, but the area directly in front of it had been cleared. Ethan inspected the entrance, eyes narrowed.

“That…is a big cave,” he declared.

“Your wisdom astounds me,” Tomo replied from the Astral.

Ethan chuckled. “I mean, it’s way bigger than I’d expect. I figured the Basilisk would have found something smaller and more defensible. A place he could hide out and not worry some demon could just wander in.”

Tomo nodded. “A curiosity. As you say, a smaller lair would make more sense, as would blocking the entrance.”

Ethan dropped from the tree, creeping in for a closer look. There were some oddities. It looked like something had been dragged across the ground, and there were a few broken trees, which looked too healthy to have fallen on their own.

“Well, I’m not surprised things aren’t exactly as I hoped,” he said to Tomo. “We both knew this would end up with me going into the spooky cave.”

“Be cautious, Ethan Bishop,” Tomo said. “He may not have been the most powerful Twilight rank you’ve faced, but he’s had centuries to master his abiliites.”

“Good call,” Ethan agreed, then he moved toward the looming cave, which appeared to angle downward steeply into the darkness. “Here we go,” he said, remaining a Trick of the Light.

The sun disappeared behind him as he crept inside, moving to the ceiling for added safety. He expected to need to navigate around stalactites as he continued inward, but the tunnel was strangely smooth. Not enough to make him think it was man-made, but still an oddity.

Ethan moved deeper, increasing his pace as he continued to find nothing but more tunnel. The cave branched a few times, but Ethan stuck to what seemed like the main chamber for as long as he could. His night-vision rune was working overtime, but there was nothing to see but more cave.

After nearly a half an hour of plodding forward, a trail of dimensionally-charged daggers leading the way back, the cave finally opened up into a large chamber. It was circular, and had numerous other passageways leading indifferent directions. Ethan had to be a hundred feet belowground at this point, and he figured that these underground tunnels were how the Basilisk must be getting around.

Ethan dropped to the ground, moving into the circular chamber and looking around.

“Still nothing,” he said.

“There are many other tunnels,” Tomo said. “Searching them all could take considerable time.”

Ethan nodded, tapping his foot as he thought. The floor of the cave was oddly uneven, and seemed to be coated in a thick layer of stone-dust. “It really does seem like he’s been here. The magical dust in the air is the same we found under Athens. Maybe he’s out hunting for food?”

“Perhaps,” Tomo said. “So what should we do?”

“I’m thinking of collapsing the whole damn thing,” Ethan said. “A few explodey daggers and no one can use this place anymore. If he’s inside, it should force him to the surface. If he’s not, he’ll lose his hidey-hole.”

“Are you certain you want to take such destructive measures?” Tomo asked.

“We can’t even be really sure it’s Basilisk,” Ethan said. “There’s only so much time and finesse I’m willing to put into this search.”

Each moment that passed gave the Followers more time to build their army. Ethan was frustrated, and in a hurry, and that was a dangerous combination for someone as powerful as he’d become. The simple truth was that he was getting very tired of being quiet.

Ethan cycled to flare, then charged a dagger. “I’ll make this quick,” he said, tossing one dagger into the ceiling above. It split into five on contact, the other four raining down into the cave floor. Ethan laughed. “I’m still not used to that,” he said, then began moving back toward the tunnel he’d entered from.

After only a few steps, the ground began to churn and rumble beneath him. Ethan had a brief moment of panic, as it seemed like the cave had chosen that moment to collapse on its own.

The truth proved far worse.

Ethan made a desperate leap to the wall, looking down with wide eyes as the “floor” continued to move. He watched the unusual ripples and waves he’d thought were uneven stone resolve into something else. A massive body was uncoiling itself from where it had rested, dust falling from it in waves as the enormous serpent slowly rose up, a head the size of a delivery van searching around for what had woken it.

“I believe you have found the Basilisk,” Tomo said.

Ethan swallowed, looking at what had to be a Master rank creature, given its alarming size.

“He’s put on some weight,” Ethan whispered. He trusted Tomo to block the sound as he always had. Yet, the gigantic snake turned in an eye-blink to face Ethan, its jaws splitting open to reveal fangs taller than Ethan himself, and white eyes searched for him as a tongue darted out to taste the air.

Then it lunged for him.
