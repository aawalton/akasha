Vir’s mind was still reeling from the multitude of revelations the tapestry of reality had piled upon him when she appeared.

Not physically, of course. There was no way for anyone to safely approach Vir while the machine was active, and his eyes were shut besides. But through the Crown Chakra’s expanded awareness that showed him everything in every realm, Vir saw himself, Janak… and now her, and what it showed him made his heart lurch in his chest.

“Ashani?”

Physically, she was standing just inside the machine’s radius, closer to the process than any living being should have been, and had this been moments earlier, she would have been torn apart. Only the fact that the machine had already finished collecting the prana of the Ashen Realm saved her, but even still, the density was lethal.

But then, Vir supposed that wouldn’t matter to her, would it? He needed only take a single glance at the tapestry—the revised tapestry—to know.

Her expression was as composed as ever, and in her eyes was something he had never once seen from her before.

Peace, he realized. Complete and total peace.

“What are you doing here?” Vir asked, sending his thoughts directly to hers through the machine, though he already knew the answer.

Aided by the enhanced perception of the machine, Vir’s mind ran as quickly as Ashani’s, and an entire conversation occured within the span of a heartbeat.

“Being close to you, for one,” Ashani replied playfully. “Altering Fate, for another.” She tilted her head slightly. “You did not see this on the tapestry, did you?”

Though phrased as a question, there was no doubt in her voice. Though it should have been impossible, she somehow knew.

“No,” Vir said. “But I do now... You knew!”“But of course! Being an agent of theirs for millennia grants one certain perks, so to speak.”“No, you can't do this! Don’t do this!” Vir begged, finally grasping her plan.

“They anticipated Janak’s sacrifice,” Ashani replied, a small smile the only sign she'd heard his words. “But they did not expect mine. How could they? Their foremost agent in the realm, about to do something they believe impossible. They've never seen it before. That is their weakness, Vir. They can only use what they know. Which might be vast and nearly infinite, but omniscient they are not.”

“Janak knew...”

“Oh, no, not quite. He knew I planned something, but not its contents. Nothing was safe to be spoken, after all. Not if we wished this to work.”

“You're telling me he sacrificed himself on a hope that you had a plan!?” Vir asked in disbelief.

Ashani's smile widened. “That is who he was, Vir. That is why I love him. And though it will do little to ease your mind, know that I planned this since long before we met,” she said. Though physically they were nowhere close, she reached a hand out, and Vir felt it on his cheek as if she were there in front of him.

“No,” Vir said, more confident this time. “No! You'll be throwing your life away. The machine is compromised. The process cannot finish!”

“Oh, that?” Ashani laughed. “Yes, I am aware. We knew not what form it would take, but both of us knew they would compromise the machine somehow. It was the simplest option.”

“But it worked!” Vir blurted. “The tapestry…”

“Shows what they see. That you yourself saw no other avenue only validates the merit of our plan,” Ashani said, smiling.

“I don’t understand,” Vir muttered, not bothering to blink back the tears welling within his eyes. “If I see this changed future, so will they. What difference will this make?”

“You will understand, in time. But time is what we are most short of, and I must first apologize for leaving you without the means to create Ash Gates of your own. Though,” she added with a chuckle, “I suspect you will no longer need them.”

“Is there no way?” Vir begged, madly searching the tapestry of reality for any possible alternative. “There must be some way to save you! Some reality in which you live! I... I can't lose you!” He blurted, his true thoughts coming through unfiltered.

“All the time you gave me,” Ashani said gently. “All those wondrous sights I would never have seen without you... Trust in my words when I say that these past several years have been the most joyous of my solitary life. So thank you, Vaak Thank you for showing a pitiful creature who knew nothing but Ash and loneliness a world full of color and magic. No god could bestow any greater treasure.”

The tears now streamed down Vir’s face and he let them, sobbing uncontrollably, unable to form a single coherent response, his lips failing him when he needed them most.

They said nothing for a long, long time, though in reality was less than a tenth of a single second.Eventually, the sobs subsided as denial gave way to acceptance, and Vir reshouldered the mountain of responsibility he'd always carried. Except now, it was all the heavier with the weight of Ashani's sacrifice.

“It will not be easy, you know,” she said softly. “They will continue to hound you, even in a fresh universe.”

“It doesn’t matter,” Vir said, shaking his head. His voice was hollow and spent, as though all the emotion he had in him had been wrung out. Now, there was only emptiness.

“My actions here will draw the full weight of their attention upon this universe. Remember Janak's words. Time and attention are the true enemy. My actions will reset the clock, so to speak, but I can do nothing against the chaos their attention will bring.”

“It doesn’t matter. Whatever it takes, I will not squander this chance.”

“I know,” Ashani said, still smiling. “I have but one wish to ask of you.”

“Anything,” he whispered. “Everything.”

She smiled. It was both the saddest and most peaceful smile he had ever seen on any face.

“Please, give Janak’s daughter the life Janak and I never could. You’ve no idea how I longed to see her again. To wrap her in my arms and tell her everything will be alright... My one regret is that I must leave this world without seeing her whole and healthy again.”

“I'll tell her for you,” Vir said, nodding hard. “And you have my word. I will look after her as though she were my own daughter. But... she will be heartbroken by your loss. Nothing I say or do can change that.”

Vir had blurted the words as a last, pathetic attempt to get Ashani to stop. Despite knowing this was the only way. The only salvation for Siya and for the realms. The only correct decision that any Raja in any time would make a thousand times over.

“No,” Ashani said, and for the first time since he had met her, tears streamed down her face. Her shoulders shook and her voice broke. “No, my Akh Nara. She won’t. No one will.”

In all the time he had known her, Vir had striven to call her a friend. An equal when everyone else thought of her as a god. He felt she had appreciated it.

And yet, now that he reflected upon it, there had always been a distance between them, even if he was the closest to her of anyone. A chasm between two people from different eras, never to be bridged.

Yet now, in this moment, for the first and last time, Vir saw the being before him for who she truly was, with no distance to separate them.

Not a deity or some mythical creature. Not even the last remnant of an advanced race. But a soul. Just like him. A terrified soul about to sacrifice everything that she was and had ever been for the world.

A mortal. A hero. An irreplaceable friend.

Vir opened his mouth to say something. To tell her how much he cherished her. To pray for her, or even just to thank her, but nothing came.

In the most crucial moment of all, his voice betrayed him again, and she turned away before he regained it, moving toward the place where Janak’s soul had burned.

Her soul began to burn, and one by one, her thread on every tapestry winked out.

“ASHANI!!”

It made no difference. He watched her soul go the way Janak’s had, screaming her name again and again, his voice drowned by the din of the machine, and for all the power currently moving through his body, for all the prana of an entire realm cycling through his awareness, he could not touch what was happening. Could not reach her. Could not rewrite Fate.

Ashani… was gone.

He thought back to that fateful day when they first met. When Shan had leaped through a barely-stable Ash Tear to drag Vir back to help. She’d been bleeding out to a Garuda attack.

But had it been her?

Her face was blurry. Indistinct. He couldn’t remember.

Who was she, really?

Panicking, Vir searched his memories, trying desperately to remember.

Why couldn’t he?

Who was she? And why did not remembering terrify him so?

The details were gone. Memories he knew he had just had were missing, as though removed.

“No!”

It was only then, and far too late, that Vir understood.

Her final words.

The true weight of her sacrifice.

Something deeper than grief, melancholy, or regret.

Something more sinister than death.

Ashani wasn’t simply burning away her soul as Janak did. That would not have worked against an enemy who saw all.

She wasn't simply fueling the machine.

She was burning away her existence. The very memory of who she was, in every mind that had ever known her, across every thread on the tapestry going back to her birth.

Permanently altering the tapestry, past, present, and future in a way that couldn’t be undone. In a way the Fateweavers had no framework to counter, because there was nothing left for them to manipulate. No thread to find, no record of her to rewrite or learn from.

For there was only one Fate worse than death, and it was to be forgotten.

Vir hadn’t known it was possible. Wasn’t certain even they had known.

Which was why it worked.

After a lifetime of carrying the moment of her death carved into her soul, after millennia of serving as the Fateweavers’ instrument, this was her final act.

Her rebellion.

Vir would never know how, but she had found a weakness. The one thing they couldn’t counter.

He fell to his knees as the last memories slipped from his mind.

Death he knew. Had dealt with so many times before. But how did one deal with this? How could they?

Ashani had been with him since the beginning. She had guided him and traveled with him and fought beside him and through her Gates, she had singlehandedly ushered in an era of prosperity in both realms.

And now, one by one, those memories—those precious, irreplaceable memories—were being erased and all he could do was weep for each as they left. Because he knew that when they were gone he would not even know what he had lost, and that was the most terrifying thought he had ever had.

Vir stayed there, all thoughts of the machine set aside to focus on his memories of her. Until the last one disappeared, and only emptiness remained.

The machine glowed brighter than ever, and through Demonic Overlord, Vir felt the great contraption fire.

All the prana that Ashen Realm had ever held, channeled into a single spot.

Into the illusory realm, ready and waiting to be made real. Every soul had been transferred. Every city, blade of grass, and creature copied faithfully across. Except one.

Only Siya was changed. Given the life her father had spent five thousand years trying to bestow.

The great machine engaged and the world went white.

Vir wiped his face. His hands came away wet, and he wondered why.
