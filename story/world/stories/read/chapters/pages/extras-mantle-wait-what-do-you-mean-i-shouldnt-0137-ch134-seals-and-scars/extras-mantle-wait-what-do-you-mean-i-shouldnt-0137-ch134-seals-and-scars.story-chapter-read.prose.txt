The first thing Jin registered when he came back to his senses was an intense ringing in his mind and his body ridiculously sore.

He tried to open his eyes, but they stubbornly decided not to listen to him, and all he could do was stare at the endless darkness while pieces and fragments of what happened floated around.

What the fuck did I get myself into? All I remember is me trying to piece back my memories and Angel saying something…

« That wasn’t me, Jin. » Angel’s voice resounded in his mind, quiet and slow.

Angel!

What’s happening, buddy?

And what do you mean it wasn’t you? I remember you saying we need to remember…. Oh. Jin paused as he remembered. The odd circumstances, the distorted voice, the insistent tone… he should have known better.

« Yeah. What’s done is done. Both me and the Z.A.C. code were suppressed, which means whatever that entity was… it was way beyond anything we could have done. »

Still, Angel… what’s my condition? Just from the fact I can’t feel my body… Am I in a state similar to a coma?

« Yes, the entity widened the fractures in the soul, dealing severe soul damage. Fortunately, before that thing could take over, Joe had us sealed and driven the entity away. »

« For now, sleep. »

For the second time, Jin woke up slowly, this time facing the flat white ceiling rather than the endless darkness.

The second thing he registered was that he still couldn't move, but this was not in the way sleep-weight held you down; no, no… this was something else entirely.

When senses came back, they came with the cool press of metal across his wrists, the same weight across his chest, his shins, and his ankles.

He stared at the ceiling for another moment. So he was still restrained and couldn’t be good.

…Angel.

« Good morning. Or afternoon, technically. You've been unconscious for fourteen hours and some change since our last talk. »

Huh?!! That long?

Fuck… how am I?

There was a pause. A deliberate one, the kind Angel only used when he was deciding how much to give him at once.

« Bad enough that I'm going to ask you to stay calm before I show you the status effect screen. »

That's never a reassuring opener.

« I know. Ready? »

Jin exhaled through his nose.

Hit me.

The screen materialized at the edge of his vision, and even though he braced for it, the list was longer than he'd hoped.

•••

NEGATIVE STATUS EFFECTS

◈ [Soul Identity Degradation] — CRITICAL → FADING (32hrs remaining to drop to Severe status)

◈ [Essence Pathway Disruption] — SEVERE → FADING (21hrs remaining to drop to Major status)

◈ [Cognitive Dissonance Residue] — MAJOR → FADING (11hrs remaining to drop to Minor status)

◈ [Containment Seals — External] — ACTIVE: Holding back further intervention of foreign powers to manipulate your soul. (Degrading: 11%/day)

◈ [Void Poisoning — Residual] — MINOR.

◈ Soul damage sustained during ??? Possession incident exceeds standard recovery parameters.

◈ WARNING: Any use of soul-linked power during the recovery window risks accelerating degradation.

◈ WARNING: Containment seals require external maintenance within 72 hours.

•••

Jin read the ominous status screen twice and let the silence stretch.

‘The Containment Seals’, I’m assuming those are Joe’s doing?

« Yes and no. He managed to access Bastion functions. They're holding the damage from spreading, but they're not a fix, Jin. They're a lid on a pot that's still boiling. »

And the Soul Identity Degradation.

« Almost worn off, as I said. But the damage it did while it was active… that's not going away on its own. »

He closed his eyes. The ceiling could hum all it wanted.

What about the thing that was inside me? On the screen, it's just ???.

Another pause, shorter this time.

« Gone. But I heard Joe when he was ordering protocols to the bastion. He said it was a “Heavenly principle”. I don't have more than that. »

Great, and let me tell you there was nothing heavenly about that piece of shit monster.

« Agreed. »

He would have laughed if his ribs didn't feel like they would burst beside the bindings, which were way too tight.

He tilted his head to one side—the only direction the bands allowed him—and took stock of the room. It was a standard medical setup, much better than what they had going down on the 30th level. A table to his left with a half-full water pitcher and an empty glass. A door to his right, solid oak, currently shut.

And in the chair directly across from him, arms folded, legs crossed at the ankle, watching him with the flat but murder in her eyes was Reyana.

Their eyes met. She held his gaze for a long, wordless moment.

“Umm, hello?” Jin offered with a smile.

He saw Reyana's eyes twitch, but spoke nothing and walked to the door, opened it, stepped through, and closed it quietly behind her.

“…Was that bad?” Jin said. “And why didn’t you tell me about her!”

« On a scale of one to ten? » Angel said. « She didn't slam it. And your reactions were pretty fun. »

So a six.

« At least seven. »

He heard voices in the corridor—it was Reyana's voice. Low but her, and one he didn't recognize—and then the door opened again. She came back in first, and behind her followed a middle-aged man in a white lab coat with the slightly rumpled, chronically unhurried look of someone who had been woken up for emergencies so many times that emergencies had simply become his natural habitat.

He had wire-rimmed glasses perched low on a broad nose and was already flipping through a thick, dark tome before he'd fully crossed the threshold.

Maya came through next—quiet, steady, her dark eyes finding Jin immediately and giving him a pointed look—and behind her, ducking slightly through the doorframe, came Joe.

He gave Jin a small, tired wave.

Jin, still strapped to the bed, waved back as much as the wrist band allowed. Which was not much.

"Mr. Winters." The man in the lab coat finally looked up from his tome, and the mild, assessing look he gave Jin over the rims of his glasses was the look of a man cataloguing a problem. "I'm Dr. Campbell, the only lead medical personnel alive. I've been working with your friend on your case since they brought you in." He set the chart down on the table beside the water pitcher and pulled a small runic device from his coat pocket, rolling a stool toward the bed. "How do you feel?"

"Like something used my skull for a drum," Jin said.

"That's accurate," Dr. Campbell said and pressed the device against Jin's temple. It hummed. Numbers scrolled across its face that Jin couldn't read from this angle, and the doctor's brow furrowed incrementally with each line. "Deep breath."

Jin breathed.

"And again."

He breathed again.

Dr. Campbell moved the device to his sternum, then to the inside of his wrist just above the metal band, studying the readout with the quiet, concentrated focus of someone working a puzzle they'd encountered before but never quite in this configuration.

Joe crossed to the wall and leaned against it, arms folded loosely. Maya had positioned herself near the foot of the bed. Reyana hadn't moved from just inside the door.

"Well," Dr. Campbell said at last, setting the device on his knee. "The good news is your body itself is remarkably intact, all things considered. Soft tissue, bone, essence pathways—all operational, the latter will need time, but they'll recover." He paused, adjusted his glasses. "The soul is the problem."

"I figured," Jin said.

"Well," Dr. Campbell replied, pleasantly enough. "Most people, when they say 'the soul', are thinking of it abstractly. Spiritually. Even practitioners who work with essence their whole careers treat the soul as an idea rather than a structure." He picked up the tome and flipped to a page, and turned it so Jin could see the page—a diagram of something that looked like a layered lattice, dense and interconnected, with several sections shaded in red—and spoke again. "Your soul is a structure. Specifically, it's the architecture that defines the relationship between your identity, your existence, and your essence. When it's damaged—not wounded, damaged—those three things stop agreeing with each other."

Jin looked at the diagram. The red sections were not small.

"The curse you were carrying," Dr. Campbell continued, "attacked the identity layer. The most fundamental part. The part that answers the question: " Who is this?" He tapped the outermost ring of the diagram. "At its worst, if it had run its full course without intervention, you would have reached a point where your essence no longer recognized you as its host. Where your existence, on a conceptual level, would have begun to… dissolve."

The room was quiet.

"The curse is fading," Joe said from the wall. "We confirmed that."

"The curse is indeed fading," Dr. Campbell agreed, and looked at Jin directly over the rims of his glasses. "The damage it left behind is not. Those red areas are not recovering on their own. They need time, carefully managed conditions, and—most critically—they need you to not do anything stupid."

"Define stupid for him, please, doctor," Reyana said coldly, and Jin shivered from her gaze.

« Hoho… someone’s in trouble, and after what you put me through… It's karma! »

Shut up, Angel.

"Anything soul-linked," Dr. Campbell said, in the tone of a man who had anticipated this question and was not entertained by it. "That includes abilities that draw on your identity as a component, skills that interact with your existence as a principle, and any use of that." His gaze moved briefly and deliberately to the silver chain of fate visible at Jin's collar, a seal Joe had placed on him. "Anything in that category risks destabilizing what the containment seals are holding together, and if those seals degrade before the soul structure has time to stabilize—" He stopped. Consider his phrasing. "You won't die quickly. That would almost be preferable.”

"Oh," Jin looked at him. "That’s not reassuring."

"No," Reyan said simply. "It’s not."

Dr. Campbell ran two more tests—a runic sweep that made Jin's molars ache, and something involving the chart, a thin silver stylus, and what appeared to be Jin's own ambient essence responding to stimuli Jin couldn't feel—and made notes.

He then frowned at those notes and made more notes. Then he straightened, pocketed his device, and fixed Jin with the look that doctors reserved for patients they were not entirely confident would follow instructions.

"Rest. No essence use. No soul-linked ability activation. The seals need maintenance within seventy-two hours…your friend has assured me that's being arranged." He glanced at Joe, who nodded once. "Eat. Drink water. Sleep." He picked up his tome. "I'll check in tomorrow morning. If you feel any slippage—a sensation of discontinuity, like you're not certain where your thoughts begin—you ring for me immediately. Do you understand?"

"Yeah," Jin said. "I understand."

Dr. Campbell gave him one last evaluating look, apparently decided he'd done what he could, and left, giving Maya and Reyana a brief nod.

Maya stayed a moment longer. "I'm glad you're awake," she said, “But please stop with the self-harm.”

"I know," Jin said.

She squeezed the metal band over his hand, and then she too slipped out, pulling the door halfway shut behind her.

Which left Joe.

And Reyana.

Joe reached into the inside pocket of his jacket and produced, with the air of a man performing a small but deeply considered act, a bottle of orange juice and a wrapped sandwich. He crossed to the bed, set the sandwich on Jin's chest since his hands weren't available, and held the juice out at an angle Jin could reach his neck toward.

"You should eat," Joe said.

"I'm strapped to a bed."

"I'm aware." Joe uncapped the juice and held it closer. "I can tip it."

“Really?”

“Yeah. You wan’t it or not?” Joe shrugged.

"…Fine."

Joe tipped it. Jin drank. The juice was cold and startlingly, aggressively good in the way that meant he was hungry.

"How's Veric?" Jin said when he pulled back.

Joe's expression shifted—not much, just a fraction, a small compression around the eyes—and gave him a look. "He’s alive."

"Okay? All I remember are bits and pieces."

"Well… He’s got three cracked ribs. Concussion. Both eyes hemorrhaged from the pressure." Joe recapped the juice and set it on the table. "He'll be fine. He's done worse to himself on purpose in the field, apparently, and he won't stop telling anyone who'll listen that it wasn't your fault."

The guilt landed in Jin's gut like a stone dropped in still water. He turned his head toward the door, the impulse to get up immediate and physical and completely immovable given the bands around every limb.

"I need to—"

A hand pressed flat on his sternum, and Jin found Reyana standing over him, her eyes leveled at him.

"No," she said.

"Rey—"

"No." The word was quiet and absolute. "You are strapped to a bed, your soul looks like something took a hammer to it, and if you rattle those bands, I will ask Joe to sit on you worse, I’ll tell what happened to Rudy and have him sit as well."

"I'd do it," Joe confirmed, from across the room. “So would Rudy, I bet.”

Jin looked at Reyana. She looked back at him. He let his head drop back against the pillow.

"…Fine."

The tension in her shoulders dropped by a fraction. That fraction was the whole of it, and she let out a slow breath before she did something that surprised him. She sat down on the edge of the bed and, without a word, wrapped both arms around him in a hug.

Jin didn't say anything. Neither did she, for a moment.

Then she pulled back, looked at him, and the fond part of her expression did a rapid, almost seamless transition back toward homicidal.

"What," she said, "were you thinking?"

"I wasn't, probably."

"That's the problem!" Her voice finally cracked through the careful control, just for a moment, and then she pulled it back. "You ran down into the lowest level full of enemies, Jin. Alone. Then you fought with an Underlord cultist, a fucking mind mage Underlord. Alone! Then you give me that crystal without any other words…”

“I did say it was from Trish—”

“You will shut your mouth while I’m talking, or I’ll have something to shut it for you.” She threatened.

Jin quickly shut his mouth. There isn’t any point in arguing with her… an argument that he knew very well from his experiences with his sister that he can never win.

I wonder how Ame is? Jin mused.

« She… no, they should be okay, Jin. »

“Jin!” Reyana flicked her finger to his forehead.

“Ouch!”

“You do all that shits and then when you finally rest… no, you don’t rest, do you! You came back with whatever it was riding you—Joe was out there, I was right there—and you didn't—" She stopped. Dragged a breath in. "You just vanished into the dark."

Jin only gave her a tired smile.

She looked at him for a long moment, something working behind her eyes that she didn't say. Then she unfolded herself from the bed's edge and sat back in her chair, arms crossed again, though the edge on it was different now.

"This isn’t over, you got that!" she said.

Jin nodded.

Joe stirred from the wall. "We've kept it quiet. What happened in that chamber—the Keeper, the possession, all of it. The people who needed to know know. Anyone outside that circle thinks it was a difficult fight with the cult's upper command that took a toll."

"Rudy?" Jin asked.

"Knows the bones of it," Joe said. "We told him and others we were testing something."

Jin nodded slowly, working through it.

"The thing that was in me," Jin said. "I heard you called it a principle?" He shifted against the pillow.

“So you heard?” Joe asked with an expression unusually serious. “And no, I’m not telling you anything right now.”

“What—”

"Don't follow that," Joe said. "Don’t follow that line of thought yet. Not while the seals are fresh."

Jin held his gaze. Joe held his back.

"Joe," Jin said.

"Jin—"

"What do you know about it. I’m okay now. Every status effect is fading."

Joe looked at the ceiling, then at the juice bottle he'd left on the table, then at Reyana, who had been watching the whole exchange quietly with eyes burning with questions.

He pulled the stool out from where Dr. Campbell had left it, reversed it, and sat down with his arms folded across the back, and was quiet for a moment in the way of someone organizing something that didn't organize neatly.

“Okay,” Joe sighed and then looked at Jin. “I’m guessing you already have a fragment of the code?”

Jin paused and then nodded. No point in hiding anything at this point. “Yes, I have the Z.A.C code. I got it from my sponsor.”

“That's what you gave me, right?” Reyana interjected, and Joe turned to her in surprise. “You got a sponsor?”

“No,” Reyana frowned. “Although it does say sponsor available. What is all this, Joe?”

Joe turned to Jin. “How in hell did you give her a branch?”

Jin shook his head, at least shook how much the bands would allow him. “It wasn’t me. Trish wanted that to be given to her bestie.”

“Trish?” Joe asked. “The imposter girl?”

“Oh, Joe, you missed so much fun,” Jin smiled, and when Joe only gave him a raised eyebrow, he knew Joe wasn’t in the mood for jokes. Which was odd.

“Well, to put it simply, Trish isn’t human. She is, I dunno, the system said ‘Kin of the darkened one’ or something when the pact was made.”

“Wait, pact?” Joe asked.

“Pact?” Reyana mirrored.

“Yeah. Soul pact? She said one half of the promise was already delivered and…” Jin started.

“You made a pact with the daughter of an outer god?” Reyana asked, her voice rising.

“Oops?” Jin said.
