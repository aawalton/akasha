Jumo made his way through the shadow-ridden slums, frowning at the changes that had occurred during the handful of days since his last visit.

While Lowtown had always been… fragrant, the stench of unwashed bodies and rotting garbage filled the air, competing with the ever-present reek of torch smoke and beast shit. There was a wild look in people’s eyes—tension so thick he could have cut it with his spear. There was still enough food coming in for the time being, but other resources were in short supply, and morale was souring by the hour.

Even without the Ancient fanning the flames, the simmering discontent was perilously close to boiling over. The cavern below the mixing bowl was where the poorest and weakest of the free city’s residents dwelled, along with those who preferred to conduct their business as far away from the city watch as possible. They weren’t bad people, but they were growing more desperate by the day, which made them dangerous in a different way than the rugged hunters, bloodthirsty jailbirds, and battle-crazed gladiators living in the levels above.

By now, Jumo was familiar with the places where the Syndicate’s lookouts were watching from the rooftops, along with some unsavory characters who were sizing him up for a mugging before deciding that taking on a state-two warrior for a backpack full of gear wasn’t worth the risk. He listened to the chatter as he walked down the street, sifting through rumors regarding recent events and petty grievances between neighbors.

His ears picked up everything from conspiracy theories linking the Magisters to the anomaly to claims the Wardens were planning to take over the settlement. It wasn’t all doom and gloom. He smiled when he heard people talking about Edge’s generosity, revealing a guarded hope that the Hunters Association would provide additional supplies going forward.

He waved to several people he recognized as he headed for the One-Stringed Puppet, the bar the organized crime ring known as the Syndicate used as their base of operations. Jumo had helped their leader Frederick—also known by the ironic title, the Best of the Worst—a short while back and had been recognized as an ally of the organization who was welcome to visit anytime.

He had also gone on several dates with Frederick’s second in command, a delightfully dangerous woman named Kira. He hoped to spend some quality time with her soon, as their personalities were a good match and the sex was amazing.

Work first, Jumo. Then you can play. He tipped his hat to the bouncer on duty and entered the establishment, which was bustling at this time of day. The crowd wasn’t as feral in here, a testament to the order the Syndicate worked hard to establish during these chaotic and trying times.

When he sat down at the bar and ordered a drink, an unassuming man standing in the corner left through a back door. Kira came walking out a few minutes later. He ran his eyes across her muscular body, enjoying her athletic curves, dark eyes, and angular features. She met his gaze and licked her lips, sending a thrill running down his spine. When Kira gestured, Jumo paid for his drink and followed her into the room Frederick used as an office.

The leader of the Syndicate was sitting behind a desk filled with paperwork. He was a dapper gentleman whose pristine gray suit was a stark contrast to the dingy attire his associates wore. Frederick had pale skin, ice-blue eyes, and a petite frame. Wherever he had placed his attribute points, he didn’t prioritize his physical stats like most of the Syndicate’s elites.

He poured all three of them a glass of something a great deal stronger and more expensive than the liquor served to uncored residents.

“Jumo,” Frederick began, “it’s always a pleasure to see you. What brings you to my office today? If you’re looking for work, Kira might have a job or two we could use some help with, but most of our efforts are devoted to keeping the free city from falling apart.”

“Actually,” he replied, “that’s why I’m here. I’ve become aware of several disturbing developments. I wanted to share what I’ve learned and get your take on the situation before deciding my next steps.”

He told them about the near riot Edge had prevented. They had already heard about the confrontation but didn’t know about the mysterious interloper throwing rocks at the protestors to incite them to violence.

Frederick’s expression darkened as he told the story. By the end, the Syndicate’s leader was seething with fury. He visibly regained control over his emotions, considering Jumo’s words for several minutes before offering a reply.

“Tell you friend he has my thanks for preventing an incident that might well have set flame to the tinderbox this settlement has become in recent days. I’ll investigate the instigator myself, as they might be responsible for a series of disturbing developments plaguing Lowtown. They must be remarkably skilled at covering their tracks. This is the first concrete evidence I have of their existence, although I’ve suspected something strange was going on ever since people started vanishing.”

Jumo wasn’t ready to discuss the Ancient until Sakura examined the Syndicate, but he liked these people and didn’t want to lead them into a confrontation with a creature that would slaughter them all if its existence was revealed.

“Be careful,” he replied. “Based on what we’ve learned thus far, this individual is incredibly dangerous. If they suspect we’ve caught on to their schemes, the odds are high that they will eliminate anyone they perceive as a threat. There’s no way to be certain, but I would assume they are late stage-four, possibly even stronger.”

Kira’s eyes widened on hearing those words. “We don’t have the resources to deal with someone like that. Even the Magisters would have trouble dealing with a rogue stage-four.”

“Indeed.” Frederick nodded his agreement. “Thank you for the warning. I will only bring in individuals I have known since before arriving on this world. I will deal with this dilemma in the most discrete manner possible and contact you if we learn anything. Now that we know someone is deliberately inciting violence, I can position my personnel in places where they can prevent something similar from happening.

“On that subject, I have another problem on my hands, which might be related to your mysterious instigator. People have been disappearing in Lowtown since long before the disaster. I didn’t think anything of it at first, since knives in the dark were just another part of the game we were playing. I thought they would stop in the aftermath of the anomaly, but that hasn’t proven to be the case. In fact, the situation has only grown worse in recent days. I’ve suppressed the information, but the Syndicate has discovered bodies stuffed into hard-to-reach places—horribly mutilated to boot.

“If you can do anything to put an end to this threat, you will have my eternal gratitude. Right now, we don’t even know if a monster, beast, or serial killer is responsible. It doesn’t make any sense. Any of those should have left traces behind that our magic could follow to the source. I have several skilled trackers in my employ, and other than some microscopic bloodstains, the areas were swept clean.”

Sakura should be able to learn more. Her Unique skill is better at reading trace magic than anyone else I know, and her powers are stronger than ever now that she’s mana sensitive.

“I know someone who might be able to help,” Jumo said. “If you can give me a list of places where people have disappeared, where those remains were found, and anything else relevant to the investigation, I will bring her down here and see if we can uncover any clues. In the meantime, be on guard. I have a funny feeling that this is only the first patter of rain in the deluge that’s headed our way. Eliminating either of you would be an excellent way to destabilize the free city’s precarious balance of power.”

He spent an hour drinking with Kira and Frederick, speaking of small matters while enjoying their company. After agreeing to go on a date a little later in the week, Jumo walked back to the mixing bowl, eager to share what he’d learned during the crew’s nightly meeting.
