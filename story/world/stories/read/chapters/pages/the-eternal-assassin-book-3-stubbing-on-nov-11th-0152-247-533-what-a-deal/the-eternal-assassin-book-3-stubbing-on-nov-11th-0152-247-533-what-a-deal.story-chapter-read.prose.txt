The rest of their day with Brian was spent training. While Brian practiced with his bow against the same targets Samantha usually used for her own training, she and Asher spent their own time sparring. Asher still found it entertaining how often Brian wanted to work on his archery on his days off, but he had told him they would do whatever he wanted. The three of them kept that up until late in the evening, until it was time to drop Brian off once more, with a promise that they would be back in another week to check in on him. Once he was safely returned within the walls of the truthseeker program, Asher and Samantha Recalled back to the palace to check in with their team.

Now that Rulfar was back in action, it was time to discuss their next steps.

“So, what have you learned?” Moxy asked, lounging in the beanbag chair with her arms crossed behind her head. “I’m assuming the sparkly books didn’t melt your eyes when you tried reading them?”

“No, eyes are both still intact,” Asher confirmed, summoning the thirteen journals from his rift and placing them on the table between all of them. “It took me some time to go through everything manually, but most of it was just as helpful as we’d hoped. Zygrul managed to uncover quite a lot of useful information about the other members of The Council of Death, and we’re going to use as much of it as we can to take them down.”

“I know we already went over this… but you’re sure we can trust Zygrul’s notes?” Samantha asked, frowning as she gazed down at the shining journals. “Part of me still feels like this is just some sort of elaborate trap.”

“I know what you mean, but Zygrul is the only reason why I managed to kill Hylm,” Asher said, picking up one of the thirteen journals and tapping the cover. “Based on what it knew about Hylm, I think she would have ended up being one of the harder liches for us to take down. I’m not sure if you guys have really experienced any of these yet, but skills that affect your mental state are nasty to go up against. I couldn’t even use Complete Resilience to purge Hylm’s freaky love skill. I wanted to, but her skill prevented me from doing it, because she didn’t want me to. It was rather unpleasant.”

“Audrey’s Guilty Conscience is the only mind-altering skill I’ve ever experienced, and I’d rather not go through that again if I can help it,” Samantha said, shuddering at the memory of nearly biting off her own tongue to keep herself from spilling all their secrets to the truthseeker.

“I once fought someone with a Rage Aura,” Moxy admitted, chuckling at the memory. “Things did not go well for them.”

“I’ve experienced my fair share of mind-affecting skills, but Love is a new one,” Rulfar said, leaning back against the wall as he frowned at the journal. “I have to agree, I think it would be a tough one for us to counter. Still, I wouldn’t put it past the Council of Death to execute one of their own just to lure us into a trap. If your element is as important to them as you claim it is, perhaps killing one of their own is a worthy trade.”

“Again, I see where you’re coming from, but I really think this is genuine,” Asher argued, annoyed not for the first time that he couldn’t share his memories of his encounter with Zygrul with them via Incorporate. “Zygrul didn’t seem like it was bluffing. Hell, I don’t even know if it can bluff. It really gave off the sense that it didn’t feel any emotions whatsoever.”

“Let’s just all agree that even if this is a trap, we’re going to spring it anyway, because we want to punch more liches in the face,” Moxy grinned, bringing an end to the argument in her own special way. “With that in mind, who’s our next target?”

“I’ve narrowed it down to two choices,” Asher said, storing the rest of the journals and leaving the two he thought were their best options on the table. “Most of the liches remain in hiding, scattered around the continent, but staying out of the public eye. That’s what Zygrul did, and it seems to be the more popular option. That said, two of the liches never bothered to go to ground. Their locations have been known for centuries, and they’ve survived through their own sheer power rather than through hiding.”

“So your first choice of who we should go after… are the two deadliest ones?” Rulfar asked, raising an eyebrow. “I assume you have a good reason?”

“They’re not necessarily the deadliest ones so much as the most well-suited to keeping people away from them,” Asher explained. “And I think these are the ones we should start with because we want to try and hide the fact that Zygrul gave us this inside information for as long as possible. If we start going after the locations of the secret bases it left us, that’ll tip our hand to the Council that we know where to find them. But if we start by going after the two liches who everyone knows about…”

“Then we get to keep our upper hand a while longer,” Samantha finished, giving him a warm smile. “That’s genius!”

“Strategically, it’s a good call,” Rulfar nodded, looking impressed. “Not bad. So, who are the two liches? I’m assuming one of them is the King of Death?”

“You’ve heard of him?” Asher asked, blinking at the bloodhound.

“Don’t forget, I’m not a native citizen of the Noala Kingdom. I’ve been around the continent a bit,” Rulfar grunted. “I remember hearing rumors of a supposed King of Death off to the north-east. Of a land that no living being could even approach without losing their lives and becoming one of the King’s undead minions. The rumors all said it was some terrible monster, but I suppose it would make sense if it was one of the liches.”

“It is a lich. And a quite deadly one indeed,” Asher nodded, tapping the thicker of the two journals. “Akius. Owner of the Death, Flesh, and Life elements. Apparently, he was rather infamous sixteen hundred years ago before becoming a lich, so Zygrul has a good number of notes on him.”

“Wait, he has the Death and Life elements?” Samantha asked. “How does that work?”

“Not a clue, but it would seem rather well,” Asher admitted, opening the journal and reading from the first page. “The most important thing to know about him is that he has a skill similar to Draken’s Death Aura, only turned up to eleven. Somehow, Akius has an aura that stretches out dozens of miles in all directions. Anyone who sets foot in his territory will slowly begin to die, and the effect only grows stronger the closer one gets to him. Most people can’t even make it a mile into what has effectively become known as the Deadlands; a land where nothing grows and only zombies roam about.”

“Alright, I guess I’ll be the one to state the obvious,” Moxy said, sitting up and fixing Asher with a pointed look. “In case you’ve forgotten, the rest of us don’t have your Complete Resilience skill. I can probably counteract the weaker aura with my Life element, but we won’t be able to get near the guy without dying. I’m not afraid of death, but that doesn’t mean I want to drop dead for no reason. I want to go out punching!”

“It’s definitely tricky, but all we’d have to deal with is the death aura, and the random zombies running about,” Asher said. “Pretty straightforward compared to what a lot of the Council members seem capable of.”

“You mentioned there was a second lich who the public already sort of knows about,” Samantha said, looking like she wasn’t a fan of the death aura one any more than the others were. “Who’s the other option?”

“Zygrul doesn’t have nearly as many notes on her, but our second option is Iphiane,” Asher said, shifting gears and picking up the far thinner journal. “According to Zygrul, she lives in the south-eastern portion of the continent, deep within an infamous cave system. For generations, the locals there have warned travelers of ‘The Lost Lady,’ the soul of a woman who chased after her husband into the caves one day and never managed to find her way out. Apparently, she loved her husband so much that her soul clung to her rotting corpse even when she eventually died. To this day, if anyone ventures too deeply into the caves, they risk running afoul of The Lost Lady. The story is completely made up, according to Zygrul, but it’s true that’s where Iphiane is located.”

“I like the sound of a creepy and potentially monster-filled cave system a lot more than an automatic death aura,” Moxy said. “What’s the catch?”

“The catch is that we have almost no information on what she’s actually capable of,” Asher admitted, scanning through the sparse notes for the umpteenth time. “Zygrul mentions that Iphiane was extremely private, even more so than the other Council members. She practically never spoke during their meetings, and doesn’t seem to have any sort of minions either, which is apparently unusual for them. When they gathered everyone’s phylacteries to try and create the Eternity element, Zygrul had to personally go to her cave for hers, and even then, she didn’t show herself to him. She just left her phylactery, her own soul, lying there rather than risk Zygrul seeing her in person.”

“Is she even evil?” Samantha asked. “I mean, I know she’s a lich and all, but if she just hides in her cave, do we really need to worry about her?”

“Part of the legend of The Lost Lady is that every few years she emerges from the caves in the dead of night to find someone resembling her husband and drag them back into her caves, never to be seen again, so I don’t think she’s exactly innocent by any means,” Asher explained. “Zygrul confirms that part of the legend is true, minus the whole bit about the men she takes looking like her husband. He believes she goes for the strongest people she can find because they have a better chance of surviving whatever rituals she’s using them for.”

“So we basically have to decide between the lich with abilities we know about, who is going to be a pain to get to, or the lich we know next to nothing about, who should be a lot easier to reach,” Rulfar said, looking like he was weighing their options. “I can’t say I’m a giant fan of going after the unknown… I vote we start with the King of Death. He’s also more well-known, so it’s the least suspicious lich for us to target.”

“So long as we can figure out an actual counter to the death aura, I’m down for beating up some zombies,” Moxy shrugged, grinning as she no doubt began trying to figure out how to best punch the head off a zombie. “Should be fun!”

“I guess that’s that then,” Samantha sighed, before standing up straighter and nodding at him. “Let’s go for the King of Death.”

“I’m glad you guys already decided you wanted to start with him, because I actually had one more thing I wanted to share that I was saving for dramatic effect,” Asher admitted, laughing at the annoyed looks his team shot at him. “Don’t worry, it doesn’t change anything about his abilities! But it’s definitely weird, and it makes the King of Death an even better choice for us to strike at first.”

“You fail on the dramatic reveal, if you ask me,” Moxy snorted, waving her hand at him to get on with it. “What else do we need to know?”

“You know how the Council is made up of fourteen liches?” Asher asked. “Well, now twelve I suppose, seeing as Hylm is dead and Zygrul sort of ran away. As it turns out, not all twelve of those liches are in agreement surrounding the plan to create an Eternity element.”

Pulling out one of the other journals, Asher grinned as he tapped the loose page. “One lich was adamantly against the idea, and the others forced him to go along with it. They worked together to secure his phylactery and bound him against his will. For the last six hundred and thirty-two years, he’s been trapped. And his jailor?”

Asher slapped the journal on top of the larger one, nodding as he saw understanding click in his teammates’ eyes.

“Exactly. Akius was put in charge of keeping the dissenting lich both bound and in one piece, and according to Zygrul, he’s still trapped deep within the Deadlands. If we go after the King of Death first, we can take out two liches with one stone.”
