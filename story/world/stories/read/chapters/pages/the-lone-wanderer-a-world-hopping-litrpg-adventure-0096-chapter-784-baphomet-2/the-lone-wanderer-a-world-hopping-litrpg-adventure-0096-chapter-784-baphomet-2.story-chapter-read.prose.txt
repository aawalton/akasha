The more Baphomet learned about his latest puppet the more rattled he felt.

If the two cores, inexplicably partitioned mind, unnaturally powerful communal domain, and unexpected ownership of a Ring of Sacrilege hadn’t been baffling enough, the potent flow of mana coursing through the mortal’s channels and his exceptionally powerful body certainly added to the mystery.

‘This… thing… is it truly a person, or a damn weapon?!’

Whatever it was, it was clearly too strong for its grade. It felt more like a secret project that its faction had decided to pour all their resources into. Baphomet wasn’t sure how powerful the creature was at the moment, but the thought that it might attain divinity one day sent a shiver down his spine.

But why would anybody send such a valuable tool to participate in an auction? Shouldn’t the local deities have locked their creation inside the deepest dungeon on their backwater planet?

And why had they equipped the mortal with a Ring of Sacrilege?

Baphomet could understand the urge to grant the creature a blessing – it was merely the tip of the iceberg when compared to its many other gifts, but still worth the trouble. However, the mortal had internalized the concept already, since its mana sported an odd colour despite not circulating through the artifact.

Shouldn’t the foolish gods have removed the ring as soon as the creature was done with it? Didn’t they understand the importance of keeping their most valuable assets apart?

‘It doesn’t matter. It’s good that I uncovered their plot early, otherwise a new player would have shot up the cosmic hierarchy in a few millennia.’

Baphomet’s shock first turned into relief, before morphing into greed.

How much could he take from this place?

Back in the real world, he subtly glanced at the Arahnians’ puppets, examining their expressions. They didn’t seem to have realized what they were dealing with, otherwise they would have never brought such an unprecedented treasure trove to Baphomet’s attention. This great prize was ripe for Catharis to seize! He just had to give his guests a suitable scapegoat afterwards, to shut them up and get them off his planet.

All of a sudden, keeping the secret of the cyan powder under wraps or making an example out of those responsible for sharing it fell to the bottom of Baphomet’s priorities. If he managed to extract even half of his new puppet’s secrets, very few factions in the universe would stand a chance of catching up to him.

He just had to figure out how to accomplish that goal without damaging the goods.

The Green was still pressed against the spongy ground by his other half, his joints locked in place by the Blue’s frigid flesh and willpower. Baphomet forced him to resist, but all he accomplished was to crack his bones and bruise his muscles – both of which were regenerating faster than expected. A cyclone of colourful motes spun around them, mostly driven by the leakage of the Blue’s powerful willpower.

Baphomet tried to take back a portion of his victim’s domain – hoping to strengthen his puppet and weaken the other mortal at the same time. It was theoretically possible, since the resource was clearly shared between the two bodies. Unfortunately, Baphomet’s mind control didn’t grant him proper access to another’s willpower, so wrenching the domain away from the Blue proved difficult.

Worse still, the mortals had more tricks up their sleeves.

The Green stood no chance of breaking out of the mental restraints by himself, but Baphomet felt many others probing against the magical prison.

The two-cored Blue appeared to have identified the source of the spell, and was currently lashing against it, like a tiny goopendal struggling to tunnel through a layer of bedrock.

Six other minds joined him.

Four felt identical to the Green puppet itself, thinking similar thoughts and resisting in the same exact manner. Baphomet didn’t know how they had done this, but the mysterious faction had obviously made attempts to mass-produce their secret weapon.

The other two minds felt different. More experienced. They fought against Baphomet’s control with vastly inferior tools, but they wielded the pitiful trickles of soul mana they had access to more skilfully than the puppet and his four replicas.

One of them knew exactly where and how to push against the spell – the spots where Baphomet’s control was weakest. They were trying to unravel the spell from the outside, like an expert hand untying the world’s most intricate knot instead of hacking at it like a brute.

Whoever that person was, they might have wielded a mind affinity before, though they didn’t seem to have access to it at the moment. Baphomet had no idea how someone could have possibly lost their affinity. If he didn’t know better, he would say that this person had even attained divinity with the element once, and possibly mastered all its auxiliary concepts.

Still, that couldn’t be the case. Had this truly been a Twelfth Circle mind deity, they could have helped the mortal break out of Baphomet’s hold in an instant.

The final mind slamming against the magical prison also demonstrated skill on par with a deity’s, though their expertise manifested in different ways. Baphomet realized that this was the only one who wasn’t connected to his puppet in the same way as the rest.

‘Is that coming from the ring? It’s sentient?’

Strange. So at least one of the seven helpers had been a Concept mage before, though the power they wielded was but a shadow of a forgotten past. The element was all wrong too – soul, most likely, rather than mind.

Either way, severing the connection was relatively easy. Baphomet just had to keep the Green’s mana from touching the ring. The other six were much harder to block, however.

‘Stop resisting and yield your secrets immediately, or I will destroy your comrade!’ Baphomet demanded. ‘No matter what you do, I’m still going to find a way to your planet later. Don’t make this harder than it has to be.’

Fortunately, the mortals didn’t have the strength, nor the means to break Baphomet’s spell any more than he could extend it to affect the rest of their minds. The problem was that they were still free to seek out the local deities and get one of them to dispel his magic. The main advantage of his spell was that it could control somebody without anyone realizing, but it was already too late for subtlety in this instance.

Baphomet couldn’t afford to give them time to consider other options.

Scanning the Green’s surroundings, he tried to gather as much information as he could about the planet, hoping that it would be enough to form a new karmic connection if he lost his puppet. The Blue swiftly covered his friend’s eyes upon realizing what Baphomet was trying to do. Hopefully, the glimpse of the giant mushroom and the colourful motes were unique enough to lead him there.

He’d need to commission another karmic deity’s help, since telling the Arahnians anything was out of the question, but that was fine. Catharis had a couple of decent trackers of their own.

He would have loved to sift through the mortal’s memories and all the priceless secrets concealed inside them, but it would take too much time and focus, which he was quickly running out of.

‘It doesn’t matter. I can return to this planet and interrogate its pitiful people at my leisure, so long as I figure out where it is.’

The Green’s mind slammed against Baphomet’s directly. The other six had loosened the mortal’s magical restraints just enough to allow him to shift within his binds slightly, but he couldn’t do much more than that.

‘Give it up,’ Baphomet gleefully advised him. ‘You and your planet were doomed the moment you decided to participate in that auction.’

A pang of guilt and worry rose to the forefront of the mortal’s mind, though he remained surprisingly calm, despite being targeted by one of the most infamous and feared Eighteenth Circle deities in the universe. Baphomet felt a sea of rage simmering beneath the surface, but there was no fear, nor panic.

If nothing else, he could at least respect that. He couldn’t recall any of his previous victims maintaining their composure after finding themselves at his mercy.

The Green tried to say something. Baphomet loosened the restraints just enough to let him, as he was genuinely curious.

‘Forty… Fifty… Sixty… Seventy… Should be enough,’ the mortal said with a mental sigh, seemingly talking to himself.

Baphomet raised an eyebrow. ‘Huh? Have you lost your mind? What are you counting?’

Only then did he feel the puppet’s attention turn toward him. The mortal’s body was battered and imprisoned, as was his mind, yet that did very little to prevent the wave of cold fury from gushing through the connection like a glacial river.

‘You must be Baphomet. I was told that you’re the only one who could accomplish something like this,’ the mortal said, a tinge of regret finding its way into his voice. ‘Pray. To your comrades, to the Ancients, or to yourself. Pray that I end up dead, or I’ll hunt you down one day.’
