Eric jumped in shock and backed away. The ravine creaked shut, closing like a book. He thought he heard a distant scream before the walls squeezed shut.

“What the…”

There was a pop, and a boot dropped out of the slit. A timer appeared on the wall just over where they’d appeared from. Red numerals in the sports-scoreboard font spelled out:

0:09:02:09

The numbers counted down before his eyes, ticking away the seconds. 08. 07. A timer? Until what? Until the dungeon refreshed? Until something new spawned? He made a note of the time, checking it against the time in his System interface. It was right around three pm, which meant the countdown ended at midnight tonight. Is this why Joe told me to finish the dungeon today if I could? He’d thought that was connected to the first episode of the Great Game show airing tonight, but maybe it had more to do with this timer. Or maybe the dungeon… refresh, for lack of a better word, happened every night at midnight after a clear. He didn’t know enough to really start speculating, so he tucked the fact away in the back of his head and made a note to ask Joe.

At least I have my boots. Eric picked them up and gave them a quick examination to be sure, but they were his Leather Boots of Bonus Damage Against Insects. They even had the scuffs on the toe from where he’d stumbled a little. He dusted off his feet and slid the boots back on, feeling the warmth of bonus damage against bugs settle onto his body. Not really. It didn’t actually feel like anything, but just knowing it was there made him feel warm and fuzzy. The easier it was to squash cockroaches, the better.

He had a bunch of loot to unpack and lots of things to ask Joe about. Eric hurried back to the gas station, eager to check out his drops.

Joe wasn’t there when he returned. Disappointment panged through his chest, but not surprise. He had the feeling that Joe couldn’t manifest physically very often. Again, he didn’t know enough to say for certain, but the Admin’s relatively rare appearances had him guessing in that direction. Maybe he was just a busy man, but he kind of doubted that. How much did a Regional Admin have to do when there was only one Player to administrate?

He didn’t need to be in a safe zone to receive his prizes, but given that he was heading back to the gas station for the night anyways, it only made sense. Settling down in his little bed-slash-nest in the small clothing and trinkets section of the gas station, he opened one achievement after another, starting from the ordinary rewards, then the rare one, then the ultrarares. Thanks to the rewards from finding the dungeon earlier, which he’d nearly forgotten about, he had four ordinary-rank rewards. The ordinary rewards were as underwhelming as they’d been the first time. He received a second tent, two health potions, and a mana potion, all of which went directly into his inventory. As he added them, he noted that his inventory had rearranged itself.

Inventory: 58/200 lbs | 105R

Consumables

2x Tent

2x Potion

5x Airplane Pretzels +5% float for 3s on consumption.

15x Blackscale snake skin

12x large snakeskin roll

1x enormous snakeskin roll (boss)

1x Wafer bar

999x Grandma Candy

Non-consumables

1x I Love Truckin’ bag of snacks

1x Vehicle Key Ring

1x Blackfang Sabre

He raised his brows. I forgot about the key ring. I guess it ended up in the inventory at some point. His red shop tokens had added up while he wasn’t paying attention, stacking up along with his snakeskin rolls. Interestingly, the snakeskin rolls were in the consumable part of the inventory, while the bag of snacks was not. I wonder what I can use those rolls for? And the bag… is that because it’s in a bag, and the entire bag is not consumable at once? He shrugged. It didn’t really matter. What was important was that he now had two health potions, which was better than zero.

Antidotes and potions. Dropped loot. Items from monsters. Now it’s really starting to feel like a game. A Great Game, if you will. He chuckled to himself, then stopped. It wasn’t a good joke.

He had one rare reward from killing the boss and two ultrarares for first-clear overall rewards left. With no particular expectations, he opened the rare reward, leaving the ultrarares for last.

A large green token tumbled out. This one was bigger than the red tokens, about the size of his palm. He turned it over in his hand. One side was blank, while the other had a white disk in the center of the token which read: Shop Summoning Token: Armorer.

The boss dropped… this? He looked at it, then shrugged. He’d already made crude armor from the snakeskin; was this the System rewarding him for his actions by giving him a much nicer-looking armor?

There was only one way to find out. “Summon,” he said aloud.

Smoke billowed out from the checkout, completely obscuring that end of the room. Eric coughed, waving his arm. The smoke quickly cleared, revealing not a gas station checkout desk, but an old-fashioned wooden table laid with all sorts of hammers, gauges, and so on; everything a medieval armorer would need to make armor. A burly woman dressed in functional, head-to-toe leather stood behind the table, hammering metal studs into thick leather. A tag appeared over her head when he looked at her, listing her as: Sadie, Armorer. Abruptly, she looked up, then frowned. “Already? They shouldn’t be done with the Tutorial yet.” Her frown deepened. “Hello? Anyone there?”

“Um, yes. No. Er, hello.” Eric walked out from behind the shelves, raising his hand half to say hi, and half to draw her attention. He was tall enough to easily clear the shelves—they barely made it up to his chest—but it wasn’t like whoever this was had been expecting to get dropped into a gas station; he understood her missing him.

First Shop Summon! Reward granted.

First to Summon a Shop! Rare reward granted.

She looked at him, and then her gaze shifted slightly, as though she were reading the messages that had popped up to the bottom right of his HUD. “Oh. Hey. Your first time summoning a shop, huh?”

“Yes. Wait… you can see that?”

“Sure can. Most support staff can, in fact.” She cleared her throat. “You might want to open those. One of them’s usually shop tokens.”

Eric did as she recommended. One was 50 more red shop tokens, taking him to a total of 155 red tokens, while the other was a few ingots of a coppery-silvery metal. 5x Argent ingots, the description read. “Uh…”

“Oh, I can hold onto that. And all your armor-crafting materials. Takes some weight out of your inventory if I bank ‘em. Downside is, you don’t have them, but what’re you going to do with a bunch of hides and some ingots?”

If only you knew. Still, he didn’t see a future where he needed a makeshift snakeman disguise at the drop of a hat again, so he nodded and handed over all his drops. The armorer held her hand out, and he held his out toward her. There was a faint buzz, and a message appeared in his HUD.

Handed over:

15x Blackscale snake skin

12x large snakeskin roll

1x enormous snakeskin roll (boss)

5x Argent ingots

“So… how many tokens do you have? I hate to break it to ya, but most people summon the shop before they have enough tokens for anything. Not that you should feel bad about that! Shop summon tokens will become pretty common later on. Just think of it as the cost of doing business.”

“One fifty-five,” Eric said.

Sadie raised her brows. “Not huge, but not nothing either. You’ve provided the materials, so cutting down the costs thanks to that, I should be able to make you a coat.”

“Er, Sadie—sorry, I read the tag—”

“Yeah, me too. Hi, Eric.”

“That is, is this just armor? The snakes could hide from mana and the big one could turn invisible. Is there any chance I could get in on that?”

Sadie laughed. “Gods, I love mages. You guys are all the same. Oh, don’t get me wrong, I love hacking together some real tough hides to make some brute’s first leather armor, but there’s a real soft spot in my heart for mages. Hey Sadie, can you make this magic please? All of you, I swear.”

“Sorry—”

“No, I can,” Sadie said, suddenly dead serious. She leaned forward, resting her elbow on her table and her chin on her fist. She wore all leather herself, and her top was little more than a leather vest. Eric quickly averted his eyes. “I can, but it’ll cost more than you can afford.”

“Oh. Sorry.”

“Nah, don’t be sorry. Listen; I’ll use the big guy’s hide to make the coat, and I’ll save the rest for a full set later—vest, pants, so on. Shoes. Gloves. Helmet, or a hat, whatever you want up top; for mages, I can get a little artsy with the headware. Point is. When you have enough tokens, or you find the materials, find me or summon me again. I can upgrade something made from the big guy’s hide to turn invisible, though it’ll only work on the parts of your body covered by clothes. Don’t expect to put the gloves on and go full-body invisible. Not going to happen.”

“That would be awesome! Wait… what about the mana-blockage?”

“Oh, that? That’s an inherent property of blackskin snake hide. I don’t need to upgrade that; it’s already built in.”

“Awesome,” Eric murmured to himself.

Sadie waved her hand. “Don’t get too excited. It only means anything against mages and magic beasts, and you’re not going to see any of those for a while. Still, it’s a fun trick. Means this armor has niche uses even at the highest levels; never fully loses relevance. Finding the snakes off the kick was real fortunate for you… either that, or someone’s already got their eye on you.”

“O-oh, you think so?” he said, a little flummoxed. Already? He was the only one already in the Game, but even so—

“Eh. Could just be luck. The snakes are a pretty common enemy type; the real fortune is finding that shop token while their hides are still relevant for general-purpose armor as opposed to niche cases. By the time most people get to me, they’ve got some far stronger boss skin they want me to turn into armor, and they don’t want to waste tokens on the snakeskin unless they’re planning to take on a magic-type dungeon. And even then, there’s better skins out there…” She glanced over at Eric to find him drooping, and quickly added, “But this is a really good draw for this early stage of the Game! It really is. I’m serious.”

Eric just nodded.

“My bad habit of talking about armor this guy isn’t even going to live to see,” Sadie muttered under her breath. Louder, she said, “So? You still want that coat, or—”

“I’ll take it.” Right now, he had nothing. A coat that never fully lost relevance and could turn him invisible when it was upgraded sounded way better than ‘nothing.’ Also, and this wasn’t a huge part of it, but—as gaudy as a snakeskin coat was, he at least wouldn’t be stuck in a gas station t-shirt and shorts set that he wouldn’t be caught dead in under ordinary circumstances.

“How long do you want it?” she asked, eyeballing him. She traced lines in the air, then ticked off measurements on a greasy sheet of paper.

“It’s going to turn me invisible, right? Sweeping the floor.”

“Nice. I’ll give it a three-inch clearance, and we can close the gap with some ankle boots later. I assume you want long sleeves and a high collar, too?”

Eric nodded. Invisibility didn’t mean anything if it didn’t cover his whole body. The more he could cover with this coat, the less he had to worry about later.

She nodded in approval. “Leave it to me, man. You’re going to look so cool.” And then she held her hand out. “That’ll be 145 red tokens, please.”

Not knowing the value of red tokens, or having anything to spend them on but Sadie, Eric touched her hand again. There was a buzz, and the tokens left his inventory. “Can I buy anything with the last ten?”

Sadie thought for a moment, scratching the back of her head, then shrugged. “Handkerchief? It’d basically just be a finished square of snakeskin, but I can make it flexible enough that you could tie it up like a bandanna. Cover your neck or face with it.”

“Works for me,” Eric said.

“That’s that, then. I’ll save the rest of the boss’s skin; I’m assuming you’re gonna want a full set?”

He nodded.

“Excellent. If that’s that—and you’re out of tokens, so it is—then you can expect your coat and kerchief right here tomorrow morning.” She drummed the surface of the table and pushed back up to her full height. For a second, Eric stared, realizing just how tall she was; he cleared 6’, and she towered a good 6, 7 inches over him. Then black smoke poured out again, and he staggered back, waving his hand in a futile effort to clear the air. When he stopped coughing, the checkout counter had returned to its usual configuration, without a big woman waiting behind it. Did that really happen? He checked his inventory and confirmed that he had no red tokens or snakeskin left in it, then shook his head. Seemed like it had.

This Great Game is insane.

Static crackled. Eric looked around, startled, and put his ultrarare rewards back, not wanting to be distracted when he got them. Behind him, a television had materialized on the upper corner of the wall at some point. He didn’t recall it being there, but the big flatscreen looked as though it had always sat there. A thin patina of dust touched its top, and there was a stain on the wall where it was mounted. Despite it being a very modern, very large flatscreen, the surface flickered with old-school, analogue static, the true-random flickering of black and white bits determined by cosmic radiation itself.

I wonder if that means anything, he thought, and then theme music started to play, a backdrop appeared and a logo proclaiming The Great Game flashed onto the screen with a fanfare. He looked around, then, failing anywhere else to sit, hopped up onto the checkout counter to watch. It was time. Despite himself, his stomach clenched with anticipation. Not knowing if he wanted it, or feared it, one thought swirled in his head:

Did I make the cut?
