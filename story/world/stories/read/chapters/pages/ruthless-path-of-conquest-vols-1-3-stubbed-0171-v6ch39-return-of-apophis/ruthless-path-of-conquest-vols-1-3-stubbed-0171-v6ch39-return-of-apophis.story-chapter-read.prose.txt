The Panther Queen circled with her opponent, the dark-skinned man with dreadlocks, wearing an odd, mismatched armor set.

“You don’t talk much?” she said quietly, waiting for an opening.

Claws extended like swords, all she needed was one little weak spot to drive her blades into. The problem she had was that the opponent seemed full of weak spots. He was hardly in a defensive posture at all. He strutted around, weapon at his side, head tilted at an odd angle, dreadlocks hanging in front of his eyes, as if he was posing for pictures or something.

“I’ve taken a vow of silence,” he said, then clapped a hand over his mouth. “Oops!”

I got a weird one. Seems like I always get the strange ones in these situations.

“A joker,” she said. “Remind me to laugh over your corpse!”

She rushed in, claws slashing. They cut into the shadows that lay beneath his armor, jabbing deep into the shoulder and knee regions, where there were exploitable gaps. The blades went in—and then nothing.

I was supposed to feel something. Flesh, preferably accompanied by blood.

The man hadn’t moved. He just looked her in the eyes, smiling, his expression slightly wild. It didn’t feel performative now. It felt like a warning, the same as coloration on a poisonous frog.

Where did these people find this guy?

The Panther Queen’s fur stood on end. Acting on instinct, she jerked her claws back just as the tentacles shot forward, appearing seemingly out of nowhere from within the armor and reaching out for her through the same gaps she had just exploited.

She danced back, pivoted, leaped out of the way, lunged for his throat.

Her claws were blocked by the shadow tendrils, operating openly as a weapon this time.

“Shadow Magic?” she said. “I thought you were eccentric, but I never would have thought you were a Mage, wearing that armor.”

“Expect the unexpected,” the man said, his smile spreading into a creepy grin.

He raised a hand and fired a quick burst of flame. The Panther Queen threw herself backward again.

He’s toying with me, she thought, annoyed.

She leaped up and used the Skill that allowed her to walk on the empty air. With more space and a position above him, she could hopefully come up with a good counterstrategy.

A tornado? It won’t dispel the shadows, but might be good as a distraction.

She evaded a couple of the whip-like tentacles and jumped to a greater elevation as they lashed the air where she had just been standing.

Then she sensed him behind her. Somehow he had outpaced her. She spun around, claws raised, ready to parry—and there was no one there.

She looked down. The armored man was still on the ground. He gave her a little friendly wave, as if the two of them were not trying to kill each other.

Then he pulled a small object from his pocket. She tried to focus on it, and after a moment, she discerned that it was a small piece of metal. It shone brightly in the sun, which seemed to be intensifying slightly—though perhaps that was just her imagination.

It’s a coin.

He tossed it, caught it, put it over one hand, and then looked at which side was up.

“Heads,” he said aloud. “You’re an unlucky one.”

He looked up at her and shook his head, the smile shrinking and turning icy cold.

She felt him behind her again, jabbed blindly back with her claws, though she could still see him, and of course, she hit nothing.

Or… do I feel something on the ends of my claws…? No, damn it, don’t fall for this again. James used Illusion Magic in your fight with him. This clown is just doing the same. I need to throw him off his game.

She began chanting, eyes locked on her opponent, waiting for him to move and gathering Wind Mana around herself.

He stayed put, but the shadowy tendrils shot up toward her again, trying to grab hold of her.

This time, she allowed them to get a grip. A tentacle, like a rope or a chain, was a leash at both ends. She had no reason to think his Strength was greater than hers. After she unleashed her next attack, she might need an easy way to grab hold of him.

The Panther Queen ignored the tips of the tendrils, biting into her ankles, she plucked a couple of hairs from her neck, and she used the Wind Mana she’d gathered. They formed a tornado with the sharp hairs intermingled with the wind, turning the violent speeds even deadlier.

The tornado shot down toward the dreadlocked man.

He just stood there for a moment, then reached out through the tentacles wrapped around her. He jerked her forward, hard, pulling her off-balance and dragging her suddenly, with unexpected force, through her own tornado. The hairs could not injure her; they had come off her own body. But it was a little frightening to be yanked through her own attack.

As she went flying out the other side, he was there waiting for her.

“Good night,” he said. He slammed a fist into her stomach.

The Panther Queen felt the gauntleted hand impact against her abs, but she only smiled.

“You’ll need to do better than that,” she said.

She slashed diagonally, aiming for the figure’s neck through a gap in the armor, and she scored a direct hit. He tumbled backward through the air, his neck visibly ripped open, blood gushing out.

She did not pursue. She felt the shadowy grip of his tendrils still as strong and firm as ever around her ankles. That Shadow Magic shouldn’t remain so steady if he was really badly hurt. He was faking, either to trap her or for his twisted amusement.

The armored man stopped his fall.

“What, you weren’t feeling aggressive?” he asked in a mocking tone, floating about a foot above the ground. The opening in his neck had not begun to close, but it stopped bleeding as soon as he spoke.

“What are you?” she asked simply. “You’re not a man, at least not the man I can see in front of me. What am I fighting?”

He gave her a low chuckle. In the middle, his voice seemed to change and go from deep and gruff to soft and delicate.

“Well, I never expected you to notice,” he said. “I guess the Fisher Emperor is surrounded by some pretty good people. He might be fun, after all.”

The armored man reached with both hands for the place where the Panther Queen had cut into his neck. He stuck his fingers into the wound, and with a terrible sound of tearing flesh, he wrenched the wound open further. He pulled the skin of the neck up until he removed the skin and face like a mask.

Below the dark-skinned, dreadlocked face was an extremely pale visage with a snake-like aspect. He shook loose a mane of long, dark, straight hair that was moist from having been squeezed beneath the mask of flesh he wore over his own features.

“Hello, Queenie,” he said, smiling brightly and showing all his teeth. The canines were far too sharp for a human. “Nice to meet you more directly. Did that answer your question?”

Did he use Race Evolution to become some sort of snake person?

“What Race are you?” she called, trying to keep her heart calm. Everything felt wrong about this interaction. Why had he bothered hiding his appearance at all?

“Just a human,” he replied. “Pure and fresh.”

But nothing in his manner was trustworthy in the slightest.

It’s fine. Keep your secrets. Let them die with you.

She cracked her neck and used her Ruler powers. The slender feline body instantly swelled to three times its normal size, muscles almost exploding through the fur, height increasing by almost fifty percent.

“So now we see the beast behind the beauty,” the human said. He sounded amused.

“Now you die!” the Panther Queen growled. She shot forward, putting on a burst of speed and crossing the distance between her and the enemy at an almost impossible speed. She had trained more in this form since her fight with James. It had always been slower and clumsier than her usual body, and it still was less agile.

But with her vastly increased Strength, her lunge speed rose dramatically. She couldn’t change directions easily, but she could attack hard and too fast for most opponents to avoid.

She struck the enemy full on, claws fully extended, ripping through his abdomen.

Blood gushed out under her claws.

“Oh, yeah,” the human groaned, almost moaning. She couldn’t tell if the sound was one of pain or pleasure. “Good shit.”

She began to pull her claws out of him, but his flesh clung to the blades like tar. Then it began to creep up the length of the claws, stretching out from his body and growing toward hers.

“I told you the truth, by the way,” the pale man said. “I’m just a human. A human who was touched by a god.”

A thick, heavy divine aura suddenly surrounded the man, and the Panther Queen felt a powerful urge to fall to her knees.

“You—what do you want with us?” the Queen managed to ask.

“With you? Well, I flipped a coin to decide your fate. Like I said, you turned out unlucky. As for my god, he likes it when random shit happens, so I try to keep him entertained. Letting you impale me with both hands wasn’t something any normal person would do, and the damage would be lethal for most humans. Fortunately, my Job is Alchemist King. I took so many potions before this fight, you could probably chop my head off, and I’d just say, ‘Thank you, kitty, may I have another?’” He giggled sickly.

“And… James?” she asked.

The Panther Queen had a sick feeling that she was about to die. It might not be permanent, but it would be unpleasant to die without doing anything to help her Ruler, the man who kept her people safe within his territory.

“James insulted my god,” the man said. “Spat on his blessing, a blessing that was meant for James, of course. There are no accidents in chaos. I got a quest to destroy the Fisher Emperor’s soul. I tossed the coin earlier, before I got here, and it said go for James’s soul. Apophis isn’t always reliable about whether he honors promises, but getting rejected for Anansi really stuck in his craw. So I think he might actually give me a boon if I complete the quest. I really do.”

“Why choose a monstrous god who wants to go after someone he formerly blessed, for nothing more than a perceived insult?”

“Because his sensibilities fit mine to a tee!” the pale man exclaimed. “Did you think I was acting this way because it’s just in character for someone who follows a God of Chaos? Nope, it’s just me, enjoying myself.”

James, I need to tell you, there’s a man blessed by the god Apophis coming for you, the Panther Queen began sending. Then she stopped. Why don’t I feel the connection—

She looked the enemy in the eyes again. His flesh had crept all the way up her claws, and now it slipped up her hands and onto her arms, an improbable mass of meat like clay that had physical control over more and more of her body. The shadow tentacles around her legs had also tightened while she wasn’t paying attention, and now her legs were clamped tightly together.

She couldn’t run away. Not easily. Not without doing something to break this hold.

“What’s wrong, Queenie?” the pale man asked, visibly on the verge of giggling again. “Just now realize that you’re on your own?”

“What did you do?” she asked through gritted teeth.

“A Ruler should know they’re always on their own,” he said. “You shouldn’t expect there to be anyone else you can rely on. The world is chaos and suffering and pleasure and twisted life, trying to spring up through the cracks. You can only count on your own strength. But I guess you forgot that, ever since you got on your knees to that Emperor. He has a lot to answer for, doesn’t he?”

The Panther Queen let loose a terrible, Mana-infused roar. It blasted the flesh he had wrapped around her from her body and shattered the hold of the Shadow Mana around her ankles.

For a moment, she was free.

She turned to escape—she knew she was out of her league. She lifted one foot as she moved to run.

And then she felt it. The oppressive presence of the god that this man served fell on her like an anvil, pressing her into the dirt. She lost her balance and tumbled face-first to the ground.

“Queenie, you disappointed me,” said a voice from behind her. “I was hoping we could have some more fun together. That was a good trick, though. Guess I can’t really restrain you for long. We’ll have to say goodbye early.”

She turned her head back and saw Mana the color of a burning photograph. It was a halo of energy she had seen before, wrapped around James.

Soul Magic.

The Panther Queen let loose a final, horrible scream as she died.
