Asher nodded to the royal guards as they stepped out of the way and allowed him access to the appointed meeting room. He’d never been in this part of the palace before, but he supposed that made sense. His team had been given a private lounge to use as they saw fit, so anytime they needed to strategize or figure out their next move, that was where they congregated.

The new room he found himself standing before was located at the center of the palace, in a much more convenient location. As Asher stepped in, he briefly took note of the elegant, round table set up in the center of the room, as well as the row of fancy snacks sitting out on a side counter. There were a few stacks of thin books waiting off to the side, and the entire room was illuminated via a large chandelier dotted with rubies, each one giving off a warm, gentle light.

“Asher!” Donvath grinned, the High Prince walking over and taking his hand before clapping him on the back. “Look at you, actually being one of the first ones here. You remember Lanra, from the Eight Crests?”

“Asher,” the old wizard said, giving him a warm but professional smile as she stood up and nodded toward him. The last he’d spoken with her, she and her team of realm wanderers had taken him on an expedition into the wild realm to show him how the professionals handled journeying between realms. Then Asher had shown them what he was capable of, and she’d all but demanded he join their team. “I don’t suppose you’ve given any additional thought toward our kingdom’s last offer?”

“Unfortunately, my stance hasn’t changed in the last few months,” Asher admitted as he returned her smile. “Though I did greatly enjoy our time together. In fact, that tritessence-filled crystal that we found during the expedition ended up being the only reason I survived my fight with the King of Death. One could argue the Eight Crests played a pivotal role in defeating that lich.”

“Seeing as everyone has agreed to pool funds and place a bounty on each lich slain, one might want to be a little more careful about tossing credit around,” Donvath muttered, causing Lanra to laugh as she took her seat.

“Don’t worry, we’re not planning to try and argue that we deserve any of that particular bounty. We’re trying to convince Asher to join us, after all. Stealing a portion of his hard-earned shards would be somewhat counterintuitive in that regard.”

“Let’s hope everyone else is as understanding as you are,” Donvath sighed. “I know it was my idea in the first place, but there are quite a lot of futures where it becomes a point of contention and causes us no end of problems.”

Asher could only shake his head as Lanra began asking Donvath questions about his Time element. The fact that the High Prince was capable of rewinding time in short bursts or viewing potential futures was something that had taken him quite a while to get used to, but it was true that anything became mundane given enough time. Now, he was far more hung up on the fact that Donvath’s third element was Mind, which was forbidden according to his own rules. He understood why the man had done it, but even so, Asher couldn’t help but keep a sliver of his focus on Donvath as the High Prince conversed.

It wasn’t long before the next representative appeared, and Asher blinked as another familiar face walked into the room. He had to keep himself from summoning a dagger to his hand and preparing himself for an attack, because the man standing before him looked less like a human being and more like one of the lesser demons he’d slain.

“Groln?!” Asher gasped, getting to his feet as he stared in shock at the grinning slayer. “You’re the representative for the Hojon Empire?”

“Indeed I am,” the large man laughed, his black horns nearly scraping the ceiling as he turned toward him. His blood-red skin was just as eye-catching as the first time Asher had met the man, and his rows of jagged teeth sent a shiver down his spine as Groln smiled. “Seeing as I had met you prior, it only made sense for the Queen to send me. You have made an even more impressive name for yourself since we last spoke. I suppose I should welcome you to the ranks of the greater powers.”

“Thank you,” Asher said, feeling a lot more confident in their ability to hunt down and finish off the rest of the Council with Groln of all people at their side. The man’s physical appearance was beyond intimidating due to his Demon element bleeding over into his physical form, but it was his skill as a royal slayer that had allowed Groln to make a name for himself. Famous for his ability and willingness to take on any challenge that threatened the Hojon Empire, Groln was one of the continent’s greater powers, just like Asher. Though while Asher was still rather new to the concept, Groln had been one for almost three decades now.

“And I hear that the Eight Crests are also trying to poach you for themselves,” Groln added, slowly turning his impressive bulk toward Lanra as he stared down at the old wizard.

“Your Queen wants to turn Asher into a barn animal, harvesting him for his Spatial element in order to churn out artifacts,” Lanra said bluntly, not looking the slightest bit intimidated as she matched the man’s piercing stare. “We want him for his skills as a warrior. To join our realm wandering team and explore the realms with us.”

For a single, tense moment, Asher was worried the two of them would come to blows. But before he could intervene, Groln let out a deep, rumbling laugh as he nodded.

“You are not wrong. In fact, I told Asher myself that if I were in his shoes, I would not take my Queen’s deal either.”

“You did?” Lanra said, clearly taken aback by Groln’s honesty. “…That was stupid of you.”

“Maybe so,” Groln shrugged, selecting the seat beside Asher and sitting down. It was a testament to the quality of the furniture that the wood didn’t so much as groan as the giant, armor-clad demon got settled. “I suppose we shall see in time.”

The next person to arrive was the first one Asher didn’t recognize. A tall, handsome man with flowing locks of hair draped down his back and gleaming white armor walked into the room, his face impassive as he looked around at everyone. He looked like he didn’t really want to be there, and he quickly took his seat without a word. He had either a skill or an artifact that prevented Asher’s Identify from working on him, but Judgement wasn’t blocked. His aura was like that of a silent snowfall mixed in with jagged icicles, equal parts soft of calming as well as dangerous and deadly.

“Representative Hounth,” Donvath said, giving the man a curt nod. “Thank you for joining us. Around the table we have Lanra, from the Eight Crests, Asher, representing himself, and Groln, from the Hojon Empire. Everyone, this is Representative Hounth from the Lambit Republic.”

“Everyone,” Hounth acknowledged, before going silent once more. Asher didn’t miss how the man would occasionally throw him sideways glances when he thought he wasn’t looking, and he couldn’t help but wonder if this mysterious new figure was one of the men responsible for sending the Shadow Squad after him. He was currently in possession of a ring enchanted with the Honest Ears skill, but it wasn’t as if he could just come right out and ask if Hounth had tried to kill him. Even if he did, there was every chance the man had his own skills, artifacts, or resistances in place to prevent truth magic from working accurately.

Asher listened to Lanra and Groln chat about more mundane topics, such as demons and their own preference for how to go about fighting them. Despite Lanra’s team focusing most of their journeys on the wild realm, apparently demons showed up every so often in that realm as well. That very fact had resulted in countless debates between scholars as they tried to figure out if there was any sort of connection between the wild realm and the nine hells, but nobody had been able to come up with anything definitive. While the two of them chatted about that, Donvath busied himself with making a plate of tiny snacks, happily nibbling away at them while they waited for the last representative.

“Sorry I’m late!” Amanda said, the paladin rushing into the room and giving them all an apologetic smile as she quickly took her seat. “My city is far smaller than this one, and I got a tad lost. Going for an early morning run was probably not the best idea.”

“Amanda?” Asher asked, surprised to see yet another familiar face. Amanda was the paladin he’d met in the heart of the Deadlands, who had personally welcomed him to her small city of Preaset and hosted a ceremony thanking him for taking out the King of Death. After Donvath, Amanda was the only one here who was actually the ruler of an entire nation. Technically, it was her Order of paladins that commanded the Nunait Kingdom, but seeing as she led the Order, she functionally led the kingdom as well. “Alright, what is this? How is it I know almost everyone here?”

“Is it really that surprising that the people willing to join up against the Council of Death are those who have gotten a first-hand look at just what you’re capable of?” Donvath snorted, munching away at a chocolate-covered chip. “Though this is exciting. It is the first time the Noala Kingdom has ever entertained even an emissary from the Nunait Kingdom, let alone the head of their Order. It is an honor to have you, Falling Sun.”

“The honor is mine,” Amanda grinned, tilting her head in acknowledgement. “Seeing as The Reaper and my kingdom have formed an alliance with one another, I couldn’t very well not come when I heard about the coalition. Not to mention my Order’s entire purpose is devoted to ridding the world of undead monsters. Now that the King of Death is gone, we are more than willing to help ensure the rest of the Council of Death follows suit.”

“This is a rather curious occasion,” Lanra smirked, looking around the table at the gathering of faces. “The Immortal…” she said, looking at Donvath. “Hellspawn…” she added as her eyes landed on Groln’s grinning face and jet-black teeth. “The Reaper…” she nodded toward Asher. “And Falling Sun,” she finished, eyeing up Amanda. “I wonder just how long it has been since four greater powers representing four individual entities gathered under a single roof like this. It’s not often I feel inadequate after everything I’ve seen and done. But I’m certainly getting there.”

“We’re going to need all the power we can get our hands on if we are going to take down what remains of the Council of Death,” Donvath said, popping the last of his food in his mouth before taking on a much more serious look. “Which seems like as good a segue as any.”

Clearing his throat, Donvath stood up, somehow changing from the silly, laidback man that Asher knew him to be into the ruler of his kingdom in an instant. His silver eyes turned sharp as he looked around the room at everyone who had gathered there for a singular purpose, evidently liking what he saw.

“Each of us is gathered here for one reason, and one reason only,” Donvath said, pausing as if daring anyone to say otherwise. When nobody did, he nodded. “It’s time to put an end to a force that has plagued our continent for the last few thousand years. It’s time to wipe out what remains of the Council of Death.”
