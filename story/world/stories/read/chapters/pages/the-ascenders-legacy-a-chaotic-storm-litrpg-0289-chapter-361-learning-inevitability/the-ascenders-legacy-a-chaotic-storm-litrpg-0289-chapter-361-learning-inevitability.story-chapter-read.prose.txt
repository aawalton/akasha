Dawn came with a surge of energy, Aodhán's muscles spasming as they drank in the revitalizing wave of power erupting from his core.

He groaned softly as pleasure washed through him, energy diffusing into his bones, tissues, and veins like warm lightning.

Tier 50. Finally.

For the past ten hours, he had replayed every major scene of the war in his mind, meditating on each one to unearth whatever inspiration it offered—anything he might have overlooked before.

And it turned out there had been many.

From the deaths of Calamities to the echoes of rules and decrees ringing through battlefields as the cries of Mythics split the skies, every clash had practically overflowed with inspirations of ruin and authority so concentrated it had been impossible to focus on just one.

Formations thrived on authority. Rules, commands, decrees—they all demanded it. Even the activation of skills required it.

Authority was the lifeblood of any magical warfare.

And when imposed through force—when deployed with the express goal of shedding blood—the end result of all that authority was devastation. Catastrophe.

Ruin.

Aodhán had spent the entire night sifting through that overwhelming flood of inspiration, searching for the insight that resonated most deeply with him.

It had taken a while. But eventually, he found it. And the approval of the Origin Plane in that moment couldn't have been more rewarding.

The world had collapsed around him like a house made of mist, and for the first time in two months, Aodhán had entered the Origin Plane of Storm.

This time, however, he hadn't entered as just any cultivator. He had entered as the reason for its current expansion. A wielder of Ruinous Authority.

The difference in his reception had been immediately apparent.

The winds had been fiercer than ever before—their howls deeper and more violent. Lightning had flashed brighter. Thunder had roared deeper. The rain had fallen heavier, denser, more deliberate.

Aodhán wasn't sure whether it was a reaction to him or a result of the plane's expansion. Either way, he had loved every second of it.

The loud whispers of chaos. The rage of the storm around him. Its authority as it pressed down on the world with a weight so tangible it felt physical.

It was a welcome fit for a herald.

But beyond all of that was the reception he had received from the consciousness of the Origin Plane itself.

Pride.

Aodhán doubted he had ever felt an emotion from it before. The all-encompassing consciousness of the plane never spoke, never advised. It simply ravaged through minds, judging those worthy of seals and gifts.

His own mind had been ravaged countless times.

This time, however, there had been no ravaging. No judgment. Just acceptance and pride.

The Origin Plane of Storm was proud of him.

And that alone felt far more valuable than the skill, titles, and seal he had just gained.

Eyes still closed, he placed a hand on his chest, fingering his latest seal. It was different from the others—larger, heavier, as though the others were a cheap imitation of it.

But Aodhán knew that wasn't the case. This one was just special.

He opened his eyes, a smile blooming on his face as soft dawn light pierced into the room, painting the wooden floor in hues of gold and silver. The air was fresh, like a new beginning, the sounds of the world as crisp as breaking ice.

His smile widened—then stuttered as his gaze landed on Crixis, head bent, soft snores emanating from his lips.

He was asleep.

Aodhán stared at him for a moment, not quite sure whether to laugh and let him be or wake him up.

He chose the latter.

With a flick of his fingers, he summoned a crackling bolt of red electricity, brought it close to Crixis's ear, and exploded it.

The bolt was too small to do any damage. The noise was like a gunshot.

Crixis tumbled, his robes tangling around his limbs as he jerked awake, arms flailing in terrified disorientation.

"Get up!" Aodhán snapped, feigning anger as he rose to his feet—every bit the no-nonsense instructor he knew Crixis needed.

Crixis's eyes widened in fear, and he jumped immediately to his feet. "Good morning, elder brother."

"Good morning to you too." Aodhán's tone was clipped. "How did you enjoy your rest?"

"I…" Crixis stuttered. "I didn't rest. My eyes just closed."

"Is that so?" Aodhán raised a brow, looming slightly.

"Yes," Crixis nodded, gathering a bit more confidence. "I was not sleeping."

"Good." Aodhán leaned back and folded his arms. "Then you must have completed the task I gave you."

Crixis's eyes went wide, but Aodhán continued as though he didn't notice. "A dozen imbuement threads. How many have you perfected?"

"I… uh. I… seven. I was only able to do seven."

Aodhán paused. Real disappointment crashed down on him.

Crixis wasn't an inheritor or double inheritor, so he knew better than to expect the extraordinary. But it was as though the boy hadn't even bothered to try. If he had perfected four imbuement strands in three hours, then ten hours should have been enough to at least triple it—or take it to ten. But to stop at seven and then sleep?

It was disappointing.

Before Zatya, Aodhán had had no mentor, no tutor, no one willing to teach him anything. He had pushed himself. Strained his own limits. If he had been given an opportunity like this, he couldn't imagine sleeping through it.

Let alone failing it.

Yet Crixis had been comfortable enough to do both.

That had to change.

His gaze hardened slightly as the silence stretched into a minute. Then he spoke. "You're a talented cultivator, Crixis. Perfecting seven strands of energized will in one night might be considered genius by many. But to me, you have failed." His eyes narrowed. "You have failed terribly."

He took a step closer. Crixis instinctively took one back.

"I am not doing this because I want to torture you or make fun of you. I am doing this because you cannot be by my side and be weak. The people who come after me will not be weak. They will be transmigrants and double inheritors—geniuses with abilities so rare and powerful that even I might have trouble with them."

Crixis nodded. "I know—"

"No, you don't." Aodhán cut him off gently, his anger subsiding for a moment. "I cannot be on a battlefield and be worried about you, Crixis. I need you to be an asset, not a liability. That is the point of this training. And you will not take it with levity."

"I'm very sorry, elder brother. I appreciate the time and the effort. I will do better."

Aodhán regarded him for a moment, then shook his head. "First, you'll pay the consequences."

Crixis's eyes bulged. "Con… consequences?"

"Yes." Aodhán smiled. "I promised you yesterday that if you failed, we would duel. You have failed. This is the consequence." His smile widened—slightly, threateningly. "You have ten minutes to land an attack on me. It doesn't matter how minor. I will only evade—I will not attack. But at the end of every minute without a successful hit, I will attack you. And it will not be gentle."

Crixis swallowed nervously as Aodhán teleported to the opposite side of the hall, crimson-violet gaze piercing.

He raised a hand and brought it down in a swift arc. "Begin."

Crixis unleashed everything he had at his mentor.

***

Crixis's heart sank as the first wave of lightning spears hit empty air, Aodhán evading them all without the barest hint of effort on his face.

Once again, he was too fast.

Crixis had never seen anything like it. He moved like a phantom in the wind—not running, not teleporting, yet a blur to every sense.

"Limit yourself, elder brother," Crixis shouted. "You're too strong. I'm still at the evolved class. You can't possibly expect me to bridge thirty tiers of gap."

"Thirty-two," Aodhán corrected with a smile. "And I'll have you know I'm not running. I'm barely even moving."

"Still," Crixis protested. "Lower it."

Aodhán regarded him for a moment. Then his entire presence seemed to change—collapsing on itself until he looked almost mundane. His bloodline manifestation remained, the advanced rings still circled his irises, but the looming weight of him was gone.

"Tier 10," Aodhán said. "What do you think?"

Crixis stilled. His heart stuttered, then fear settled in its place. He had expected Aodhán to lower himself to his tier—but Tier 10? That was eight tiers below him. Transmigrant or not, advanced or not, if he failed to land a hit now, then he truly was a failure.

Aodhán's smile widened. "Let's start again, shall we?"

Crixis forced a nod, heart pounding as he watched Aodhán raise an arm and bring it down in a swift arc.

"Begin."

This time, Crixis didn't rush to attack. He took a moment to think.

The Path of Ruinous Inevitability was powerful—granting him the ability to attack, land hits, and cause ruin with absolute certainty. It was a subversion of fate itself. It made him a writer of destinies, able to pick and choose who fell and be absolutely certain that no matter how hard fate fought back, they couldn't evade it.

That was his path. It was so much more than mere superiority. It was anti-evasion. It was psychological warfare.

He couldn't afford to make a fool of it.

Eyes narrowing in determination, Crixis conjured another barrage of lightning spears and activated one of his newest skills: Inevitable Strike.

The spears shot out—a dozen of them tearing through the air, locking onto Aodhán with brutal intent.

Aodhán smiled. And this time, he ran—dodging, jumping, bending, twisting—his movements suddenly as fluid as liquid. He had limited himself to the tenth tier, yet his reaction time was still off the charts.

Crixis growled, determination surging within him as he poured more willpower into the spears.

Their tips sharpened. Their edges burned. Their speed doubled—and yet not one of them struck Aodhán. They barely even grazed his robes, bending and slashing and skewering through empty air.

Then one bent very close to Aodhán's head. Their gazes locked. And then—

BOOM!

The spear slammed into the wall where Aodhán had just been and exploded, taking out half a dozen others with it.

Gritting his teeth, Crixis gathered the dispersing lightning to form new spears—but before he could, Aodhán was on him. Crimson-violet eyes burning. Arms already drawn back.

Pain exploded in his jaw an instant later, and the next thing Crixis knew, he was flying through the air. He slammed into the opposite wall hard enough to rattle his bones and collapsed to the ground, eyes wide.

“Get up!” Aodhán called, his tone sharp. "Your attacks are inevitable. Not you. Standing in one place will only get you killed. Again."

Crixis pushed himself to his feet, taking that lesson to heart. Without hesitation, he attacked again, only this time, he didn’t use spears.

He moved, lightning exploding from beneath his feet as jagged chains of crackling electricity burst out of his back and palms, all directed at Aodhán.

Aodhán barely dodged in time, but he still dodged it, twisting between two chains in a way no human body should have been able to manage.

But Crixis had more chains.

Two stabbed down from the top. Another two from the left. Two from the right. Two from below. The rest worked on support—a web of crackling inevitability closing in from every direction.

It was his second strongest skill—Chains of Fate—and with unerring convergence, they struck, tearing jaggedly into the space where Aodhán should have been.

But Aodhán was no longer at the center. He was—

Crixis looked around and his eyes widened.

The chains surged again—coiling, twisting, slithering like snakes as they fought to bind Aodhán and skewer him. They reached him almost immediately, faster than the spears had been.

But just a moment before they struck—

Aodhán’s smile widened once again.

Crixis felt his stomach drop, and then he was flying again, slamming against all even harder than the last time. Light flashed before him as his bones reverberated, but once again, Aodhán was there before he could even catch his breath.

“You are interfering.” He said, not even winded. “Inevitability is certainty. It is an absolute. Trying to assert control over it is will not work. You have to trust it.”

“You just said I needed more control.”

“Yes,” Aodhán exhaled. “But in your case, for your path, control isn’t about manipulating every step or guiding every chain. Inevitability doesn’t need your help to be. It simply is. And the moment you try to guide it… you make it something else.”

Crixis frowned. “But how, should I just close my eyes?”

Aodhán chuckled. “Again.”

“How? You haven’t told me how?”

“It’s your path, Crixis. Figure it out. Now again.”

Crixis pushed himself to his feet, and attacked again, trying his very best not to manipulate the outcome or guide his attacks, yet failing. And in the end, he was sent flying once more, this time so hard he saw stars.

He collapsed to the floor, groaning as pain racked his entire body.

Aodhán came to stand before him then, his gaze sympathetic. “You’re not getting it are you?”

“If you explain it to me, I will.”

“I can’t do that,” Aodhán shook his head, realizing now why tutors never gave free answers. “If I explain it to you, you’ll miss out on what is likely to be a major inspiration for you. You’ll miss out on the seal, the skills, the experience…”

He sighed. “You have to figure this out yourself. You keep trying to aim inevitability. But nobody’s aiming change and change is inevitable. Think about that.”

Crixis did, truly, but he still didn’t understand.

Groaning, he pushed himself to his feet. “Let’s go again.”

“No,” Aodhán shook his head, his gaze hooded. “I know I said ten minutes, but… no. I’d rather not train you the same way I was trained. I’m not too comfortable beating you up like this.”

“I can take it.”

Aodhán smiled at him, but it was a little sad. “I want you to be strong Crixis. I want you to be driven. But my ways are not your ways. And to understand this, you need to learn it your own way.”

So, tell me,” He lowered himself to Crixis’s eye level. “How do you learn?”

Crixis hesitated. “Uh… I learn better when I’m in danger. Actual danger.”

Aodhán nodded slowly. “A rift then. Something to put you in that space of danger.”

Crixis hesitated longer, swallowing nervously. “A rift could work.”

Aodhán smiled. “Alright. Come on, let’s go.”

“Go where?”

“The outer sect.” Aodhán’s smile widened. “There are no evolved rifts here in the special sect. but there’s plenty there. Maybe we’ll even see Rhetta and the others.”

He left without looking back, and after a moment of hesitance, Crixis scrambled after him.

More than Aodhán knew, he too wanted to be strong.
