“Noah?”

Tony’s voice dragged Noah out of the fugue of concentration and he barely kept hold of the distant thread of Life Siphon keeping him alive. And, more importantly, showing him where their enemy was. Looking back through his memories, Noah kept losing track of the man, even when they were directly confronting one another. So long as he held the man’s life on a tether, he could point to the other end and say enemy even if all his senses pretended to show him an empty room.

“Noah, help?”

I’m coming. Noah gradually shifted his focus away from sustaining and back into Abyssal Awareness. At first, all he found was a distorted blur of power that he failed to comprehend, but as his awareness expanded out to its full sphere, he recognized the mana crystal cavern. You made it! You’re the best, Tony.

Their enemy was far away, and moving even more distant. Noah could only hope that meant his gamble was right, the power surrounding them on all sides was enough to hide him from their pursuers. He might stand out as a small B-Ranked thing in a place where almost everything was A-Rank at least, but if he was a tiny pocket of lower power within a crystallized clump of an existing power? That might buy him time to reconstruct himself.

“Noah is also the best,” Tony replied smugly. “And will become even better. Noah will be the greatest dragonoid ever.” Tony’s voice hesitated, as if ashamed to admit it. “The only thing lacking is usable mana. Tony has tried everything, but this place is too strong.”

Everything Noah was draining through Life Siphon went directly into Wyrmblood, which Tony was directing into very, very slowly creating a functional spine and ribcage to protect his heart.

Want me to try meditating? I’m not sure how much it relies on having a body.

“It does. Tony has already tried.”

Noah considered the problem a moment, running through their available resources, and came up with nothing he hadn’t already tried.

But then Bun Bun’s words came back to him, the simple obliviousness that let him do what Noah would have considered impossible.

How does eating a mana crystal work? It's not physically digested the same way as food, dissolves quickly.

“All that is required is to contain the crystal within yourself and intend its absorption.”

How completely does it need to be contained?

“Tony doesn't know.”

Then I think it's time we find out.

Noah scanned the massive growth of crystal, searching for anything protruding away from the rest that he could more easily break off.

Take me to that one.

“Tony understands.” His symbiote unmerged himself from Noah, revealing a long scaled body that vaguely resembled Noah’s bloodwyrms, but obsidian-black instead of blood-red. Hellfire gleamed from between the scales and Tony’s six wings were veined with shadow so dark it made midnight look bright.

Wow, Tony, looking good.

Tony vibrated happily, picked up Noah’s incomplete body, and deposited him on the designated crystal outspur. After a minute of careful direction, the unfinished network of flesh and half-formed bone enclosed the crystal he'd chosen on most sides.

This was going to really, really hurt.

“Noah is strong,” Tony said reassuringly. “We will become stronger.”

That’s the plan, at least.

Aurelia’s warnings from back when he first arrived still echoed in his mind. The danger of using mana above his level was great even when at his full capacity. As he was, he fully expected to straddle the tightrope between life and death by attempting this, but he didn’t see any other option. If he waited for his mana to recover naturally, relying only on the trickle from Life Siphon to empower his Wyrmblood regeneration, he’d be lying here helpless for hours if not days.

Being helpless wasn’t something he wanted to prolong under any circumstances. Especially having seen how fast and unstoppable their enemy was, they needed something exceptional.

You ready for this?

“Tony is ready.”

Noah focused his intention. It took a moment for the absorption to take effect, accompanied by a hum of warning that almost vibrated his fragile new partial body apart.

Noah ignored the warning. He couldn't let anything distract him, no matter how hard this became. He wasn't going to get another chance at this.

Then the A-Grade mana started to force its way through what little of him existed.

The overwhelming power instantly shredded everything but his heart into dust.

He shoved the power into all his abilities, activating everything in an effort to use the power instead of letting it rampage.

Bloodwyrms erupted into existence, poured out from him in a flood that dissipated against the mana crystal that surrounded them. Abyssal Inferno raged hot enough to melt the crystal nearby and send it dripping or evaporate it entirely. Weapons from his Runeforged arsenal appeared and disappeared, flickering in and out of existence too quickly to capture. Phantom limbs sprang out from him, human or inhuman, draconic or shadowed, only to be overloaded and explode into mist a moment later.

Even then, there was too much power to handle. His body reformed visibly, then was torn apart again just as rapidly.

Force it to submit.

It felt like trying to hold lava in his hands, burning through him while also sliding away from his grip, but he was past caring about how much it hurt. He survived his entire body being pulverized by an Ascendant. He could survive this.

Even if it just kept going.

If this didn't work, he was pretty sure dying painfully would be the preferable outcome. The idea of being a test subject for whatever the cults wanted him for as they searched for some way to tear the shards from his soul made him violently recoil. The determination not to let that ever happen was enough to sustain his focus.

The alternative is worse. The alternative is worse.

Power lashed through him and out, a tiny fraction of it turning into spectres or bloodwyrms or crackling void lightning, most of it simply releasing wildly into the air around him, making the ambient area even denser and shattering his conjurations as quickly as they appeared.

Only one thing survived the onslaught, his Dragon's Heart sturdier than anything else a B-Ranker could have accessed. He knew now that was the only reason he'd survived the previous A-Rank crystal either.

His body kept trying to create itself, and the power too strong for him to contain continued to destroy it just as fast.

He had only consumed around half of the crystal and was already nearing the edge of what he could endure. He felt like he was going to fall apart, not just his body, but everything that made him him. Like he was shaking apart on the soul level.

Enduring far beyond sanity was Noah’s specialty. He clung to his purpose and refused to back down.

An angry void at his heart drew everything in, consuming the crystal's power as quickly as it could, which only made it harder to control. If he'd been any worse at absorbing the power, he might have been able to guide it in a reasonable way.

This was, as Sev had said, like trying to leash a hurricane.

If he'd been anything but a burning heart, he'd have screamed. This silent perpetual destruction didn't even allow him that release.

He couldn't bear it, but he had to. The only way out was through, even if through was unbearable.

Force it to submit. Force it to submit. Force it to submit.

But he could barely even vaguely guide the A-Grade power flaring through him, much less rein it to that extent.

Wyrmblood's regeneration would rebuild from its standard template, and that template wasn't something Noah even knew how to change.

Yet despite feeling like he was making no progress, he wasn't alone. Even if Noah himself was overwhelmed fully with trying to manage the power he'd absorbed, Tony had the mental speed to keep up with the entire process without difficulty.

Slowly, ever so slowly, the valves and vessels and veins began to expand out from his heart with a new form, a new substance. No longer merely attached to his heart, they carried the same stormy shadow substance as the heart itself. The resilience of the dragon that had formerly been only in the heart and blood and scales began to infiltrate through every tiny fraction of his new true body.

Tony hadn't even made it two inches out from the heart before the crystal Noah had enveloped ran out and dissipated fully. There was nothing left behind that he could draw on, only a smooth nub against the mass of the rest of the crystals.

Noah's heart was beating so fast it felt like a single unending vibration, tension beyond anything he could have imagined feeling, and that was just the start. If he was going to do this the way he envisioned, he'd need to do this dozens more times. Hundreds.

He didn't want to. He wanted to stop, to rest, to sleep, to do anything except put himself through that again and again and again.

The alternative is worse. Force it to submit.

He shoved down the resistance and pointed Tony to the next crystal sticking out far enough to be used, and started over again.

Severence, Lunar Judge, and several other titles that were growing less relevant by the moment, stared down at the pile of pulverized Noah that he'd recently created in a brief fit of temper.

It'd been only minutes since his doomed attempt at infiltration began, and Sev was already tired of the whole affair. He liked playing with his targets, but this one was both boring and dangerous. Not a fun combination.

He'd probably be reprimanded for destroying the target so thoroughly, but that was one advantage of being more powerful than your co-workers. They couldn't do anything but frown and make snippy comments.

Assuming they didn't realize how much weaker he was now, that is. The Negator had been his keystone path, The Unwatched provided supplementary survival and infiltration toolset, while Lunar Judge represented the moral core of who he wanted to be.

Without access to The Negator, he could escape, disappear, non-be, but that left him with a gaping hole in his powerset when it came to non-lethal conflict.

He should have invested more in his secondary Lunar Judge abilities, he saw that now. He'd never seen Moonstrike or Moondust as particularly important, and focused on leveling Watcher's Witness and Lunar Judgment to the exclusion of the others. Without The Negator to weaken his adversaries, his only option was to smite them at full strength, and assaulting his alleged allies with an Ascendant expert finisher ability would be an unpolitic thing to do.

Demolishing the creature he was supposed to be capturing probably wasn’t much better, though in his defence, it was fascinating to observe. Even dead, the body was trying to rebuild itself. The blood was sticking to itself, instead of scattering apart in droplets. The mass clumped up around bone and muscle, and he could swear he felt it still drawing on his own health despite everything.

The boy's familiar had flown off, he could feel it flitting through passages as it tried to find somewhere to hide, but he was significantly less interested in the creature than in the bloody mess he'd just scattered across the chasm.

He fused some of the local stone and crystal into a hollow cube, then scooped the remnants into it until there was not even a stain left behind uncontained. This took him nearly a full minute, he was so meticulous, and when he finished he sealed the box and placed it into his own storage for future study. No need to leave evidence for anyone of what actually happened here.

He'd chosen to prioritize keeping the body samples fresh, which he maintained was the correct decision.

They could track the familiar down later. Severence cursed the lack of an observation path in his build. Being forced to rely on his teammates grated on his pride, but tracking was not his specialty.

He didn't have any perception abilities to aid him, so it took him an irritatingly long time to even detect his allies' location. He'd crossed more than halfway across the Middle Depths before he sensed them, and when he did it wasn't anything he wanted to involve himself in.

The Lord of Death had awoken.

Severence quietly backed away. He did plan to survive the day, and tangling with another Ascendant in his own territory was not something to be undertaken at half power.

He whispered a quiet prayer of regret for Eileth's loss, but if the Void Huntress and Crystal Cataclysm weren't enough to protect her, then throwing his own life into the fray would have added nothing. He was the infiltrator, informant, assassin. Not frontline.

Sev turned his attention to hunting down the B-Ranker's familiar. That would be a much safer use of his time than entering an altercation with the Valley's king. After all, it would be remiss of his duty if he left any part of his target unaccounted for. Even if it was just a few scraps.

Definitely better than joining the battle unfolding behind him. There could be no winners in a brawl like that.
