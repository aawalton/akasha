“We’ve got a decision to make,” Rulfar said, leaning over the table as he gestured to the nine journals laid out before them. “No matter what we do, there’s going to be consequences.”

“Come on, it can’t seriously be that big of a deal, right?” Moxy drawled, looking up from her sprawled-out position in the largest bean bag. “We killed three of them already, and they haven’t done a damn thing.”

Moxy had long since recovered from having a hole in her chest large enough that Asher could have stuck his hand through without touching the sides, and was already back to her usual, slightly aggravating self.

“We’ve gone over this,” Rulfar frowned, tapping the table as if that would make her be any more serious. “For all we know, the only lich the others are aware we’ve taken out is The King of Death. The Lost Lady and Hylm both kept largely to themselves, so unless they call for some sort of meeting and realize they aren’t joining, they won’t have any way of knowing they’re gone as well. We need to remember that we’re dealing with entities that are used to plotting and moving at the scale of immortality. The King of Death hasn’t even been dead for two weeks. It’s only a matter of time before they put the pieces together and figure out just what’s going on, and we need to strike before they get the chance.”

“And when we do, they’ll know for a fact that we’ve got intel on them we couldn’t have gotten from anyone but Zygrul,” Samantha finished. They’d all discussed this before, but Asher appreciated that Rulfar was at least trying to make Moxy care.

“All I’m hearing is that we’re going after another lich, and that’s good enough for me,” Moxy said, causing the bloodhound to sigh. “So, which one’s next?”

“That’s the whole point of this meeting,” Rulfar said through gritted teeth. “We’ve got nine options, though some of them are definitely better than others. Asher, you’re the one who’s gone over the journals the most. Want to explain?”

“Happily!” he said, standing up and clearing his throat as he pulled a map of their continent out of his rift and attached it to the whiteboard. “So, we’ve got nine liches. Of those nine, Zygrul was able to figure out at least a rough location for four of them, which means he didn’t have the faintest idea where the other five are located. Obviously, we can strike those five from our list of potentials.”

“I approve of this choice!” Moxy said, waving a hand lazily and getting a tired sigh from Rulfar.

“Of the four other liches, each one has pros and cons that we need to consider,” Asher continued, wheeling over another whiteboard as he began jotting down notes. “Option number one. Ploryn. Zygrul’s got quite a lot of notes about him, so we’d have a pretty good idea of what we’re getting ourselves into. Not to mention he’s the lich responsible for having devised the ritual of extraction they can use to rip elements from people’s souls.”

Asher didn’t miss how Rulfar took in a sharp breath at the news, but the bloodhound’s face remained firm. Even if he’d once had the desire to undergo the ritual himself, Asher felt confident that Rulfar truly had moved on from that wish.

“Sounds like a dangerous one to go up against,” Samantha pointed out. “Why not leave him for the coalition so we don’t have to risk anything like that ourselves?”

“Mainly because of the fact that his defenses are something we’re already familiar with,” Asher admitted. “Zygrul noted that Ploryn is similar to the King of Death in that he mainly works with undead. However, he’s a bit more specialized. Because elements are his focus, he’s managed to figure out a way to create undead minions that still have their elements from when they were living, or even graft elements onto them that they didn’t have while they were alive. But even if such experiments make his minions more powerful, we got in plenty of undead hunting practice during our attack on the Deadlands.”

“I was a bit annoyed we missed out on all the fun while you were off fighting those Servants of his,” Moxy said. “What else you got?”

“Option two is Flunx,” Asher continued. “The Council’s grandmaster enchanter. While it sounds like most of them are highly capable, she can do things beyond any of their wildest dreams.”

“I’d be a little worried about going up against an enchanter,” Rulfar admitted. “We already know the Council can prevent you from slipping into the astral realm or even accessing most of your spatial abilities. It sounds like going up against her would run a real risk of us all getting trapped and being taken out one by one.”

“She’s not my first pick either, which is rough, because she’s the easiest one to find,” Asher said, tapping at the map. “Zygrul figured out she lives in an old abandoned castle and even noted its position on the map. I thought we might be able to carve our way through her enchantments with the void dagger and get the jump on her, but I’ll admit, it’s risky.”

“I’d rather not leave our lives in the fate of the evil dagger we got from the evil lich, but that’s just me,” Moxy said. “I can’t exactly punch an enchantment. Not well, anyway.”

“I’m with Moxy and Rulfar, she sounds too dangerous,” Samantha nodded. “Kroult? What do you think?”

“I have next to no experience with true enchanting,” Kroult admitted, the undead sitting quietly in one of the seats with his hands in his lap. “Unfortunately, I would be of no help.”

“Then I guess we’re onto option three!” Asher continued. “Jormon. The lich we know arguably the least about, but we have a pretty good idea where he’s located.”

“That was the one with only three paragraphs of information, right?” Rulfar asked. “I remember reading about him. Didn’t Zygrul say he wiped out his entire country to keep his own origins a secret?”

“That’s the one,” Asher confirmed. “All we know is that he dabbles in diseases and plagues, and that he’s almost certainly located in a large stretch of forest that isn’t really owned by any one country. It’s a giant, overlapping zone of some rather deadly monsters, so nobody has tried to claim it for themselves.”

“I like the idea of getting to take on a lich in an actual forest for once, but a disease specialist…” Samantha said, frowning at the thought. “How would we go up against something like that? I actually have an evolved resistance to disease, and I still don’t like the sound of that.”

“It would be dangerous, that much is certain. But, I figured if we prepared well enough, we might be able to pull it off,” Asher said. “On my world, there are people who end up going into diseased locations wearing things like biosuits. Basically an outfit with no openings to the outside for diseases to get in. If we could fashion something similar, and solve the air problem with an artifact, we might be able to negate his diseases entirely.”

“That sounds way too simple,” Moxy snorted. “You don’t think he would have come up with defenses against that kind of thing by now? If he’s living in a monster-infested forest, I bet he’s got monsters riddled with diseases just waiting to bite and tear into people. That way even if they survive the monster attack, his diseases do them in afterward.”

“I had a similar thought,” Asher admitted, remembering the swarm of mold-covered rats he’d gone up against while fighting Eight back in Whikoga’s sewers. Mold didn’t sound like such a threatening element if one was careful not to inhale its spores, but evolving an element could make it capable of truly wild things. “Still, I think he’s a solid pick if we can’t decide who to go after.”

“Who’s next?” Samantha asked. “Is Lethinal on the list? He’s the leader, right?”

“Unfortunately, Lethinal isn’t,” Asher admitted. “He did such a good job of keeping his location a secret Zygrul didn’t even have a guess as to where he was located. I don’t know how we’re going to flush him out when we get to him, but I suppose we’ll have all the resources of the coalition at our disposal once we do. Last but not least, option four is Krotao. Another lich located out in the middle of nowhere, though this one has… unique tendencies.”

“Unique in what way?” Moxy asked, sitting up as if she already knew exactly what he was going to say. “I sense fighting!”

“It’s seriously uncanny how you can do that, for the record,” Asher chuckled. “Krotao is a golem specialist. And like The Lost Lady, he has a bad habit of kidnapping people. Though unlike her, he does it a lot more frequently, and he specifically goes after adventurers, slayers, people who know how to fight. Once he gets them, he pits them against his golems, using them as functionally a live test run to see how he can better improve his creations. If they survive, he has them fight harder, deadlier golems, increasing in lethality until they eventually die.”

“That sounds awesome!” Moxy laughed, jumping to her feet. “Why didn’t you start with him?!”

“Mainly because we’re not exactly the best team to go up against a bunch of golems,” Asher pointed out. “Without my void dagger, which constantly eats at my tritessence the longer I have it out, there’s not a ton I can do against them. They’re probably too sturdy for Samantha’s arrows, and your Air Blasts and pelts won’t have any flesh to tear off. Rulfar is the only one here who could probably face them on his own due to his Strength element, and we can’t exactly send him in on his own.”

“I’m with Asher on this one,” Rulfar said. “I’m tough, but I don’t think I can take on an army of golems by myself. We need to choose a lich we can all fight together against.”

“Fine. But when we get the coalition together, I’m going with them when they take that guy down,” Moxy declared, plopping back down into her beanbag. “If not him, which of the other three? Undead with elements shoved into them, an enchanted castle probably rigged with traps, or a disease-ridden forest of monsters? I’m fine with either the undead or the monsters.”

“I’m also fine with either of those two options,” Rulfar agreed, looking relieved Moxy hadn’t tried to fight him on the golem idea.

“I don’t want to be the deciding vote… but I’d probably prefer the forest with monsters,” Samantha admitted, giving Asher a sheepish grin. “It would be nice getting to utilize my Forest element for a change.”

“I’d rather not have another Smiles scenario so soon after the last one,” Asher admitted as he returned the smile. “And yeah, maybe we don’t risk running straight into the lair of the lich who knows how to enact the extraction ritual. So the forest is more than fine with me. Kroult? Any preferences?”

“I am content with either option,” Kroult said simply. “Though if we are going up against diseases, I do have a spell to bolster the immune system. I doubt it will do much against magically enhanced diseases, but it will be more than nothing.”

“Guess that settles it,” Asher said, picking up the thinnest of all thirteen journals as he tapped it against the table. “We’ll have to run by Opal and Polly to figure out if either of them have any ideas as to how best to counter something like a magical plague, but once preparations are done, we’ve got our next target. And as soon as Jormon falls, that brings the Council down to eight members. With any luck, that’s low enough that Donvath can get started on assembling the coalition.”

“Not to count our griffins before they hatch, but we’re actually doing it,” Samantha said, gripping the edge of the table as she gave him a determined look. “One by one, we’re taking down the monsters responsible for so much pain and bloodshed. To think that in only another few months the Council might be no more… It’s crazy to me.”

“Months? Try weeks,” Moxy snorted. “We’ll crush those bags of bones faster than they can even ignite their eyes!”

“One lich at a time,” Asher said, shoving the journals into his Personal Rift before holding out a hand. “Now, who wants to go shopping?”
