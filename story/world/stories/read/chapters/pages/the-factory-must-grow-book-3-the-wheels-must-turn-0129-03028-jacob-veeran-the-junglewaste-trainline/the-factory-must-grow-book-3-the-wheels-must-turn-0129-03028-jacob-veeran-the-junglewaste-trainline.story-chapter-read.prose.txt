Veeran meditated upon a grand beast of steel.

The Song had been most interesting to listen to, as of late. Particularly since they had begun to build permanent constructs which reached beyond the bounds of their First Tower, there had been a strong case of dueling melodies competing for expansion. The dragons, the Calamity, had become far more fixed upon facing against them than against a general expansion into The Jungle as it had previously been wont to do.

It was less a matter of intelligence, from what Veeran could ascertain, and more simply a reflection of aggravation. Their actions were perceived by the draconic plague as being a direct form of attack, and therefore was being responded to accordingly. Tactically, that was a combination of desirable and not. It was less desirable because any enemy focused upon them was an enemy not spending its resources elsewhere. Yet it was more desirable on account of a known enemy being better than an unknown one, and with its focus upon them its abilities were very known. Furthermore, the gradual rise of power they had been experiencing would undoubtedly be far quicker were it not spending so much of its resources attacking them.

And even within its blind attack upon humanity's artifice, it overwhelmingly preferred to meet him upon the field of battle, that it might come to their prepared battlefields rather than attacking where they were weakest, at least most abundantly.

It was, if he had understood Smith properly during their occasional conversations on the subject, a form of ward Veeran had created.

To say it was unintentional was a mischaracterization. To control the flow of battle, to draw attention to one's self, was a vital skill for any proper warrior to possess, and Veeran had ensured he dictated the terms of their dueling symphonies as much as was possible. But those actions had woven a spell beyond that which Veeran had though himself capable of, one which provided protection well beyond where he physically acted, that diverted draconic fury away from the Ironroad and Coppermine, towards First Tower and himself.

The enchantment was not absolute, certainly. They still often had various wildlife or dragon-inflicted failures on their remote locations, something that would surely become more prevalent when they had more remote locations, but for now, Veeran was where he was most content - serving as the bulwark which enemies broke themselves upon. The tank, who was attacked such that the others would not be.

He felt the Song change slightly, and then a ballista fired and the Song returned as it once was, allowing Veeran to return to his meditations and the spider-bots to their construction.

The Song changed once again, and this time the ballista did not solve the issue. Veeran allowed his hands to fall to his sides, noted that the ballista had missed its target, and leaped.

He was no Ride, who flew through the battlefield like a missile. He possessed no Skills which changed the base truth that moving an arm was quicker than moving an entire body, and as such usually relied on the same basic principles that mankind had since time immemorial - get a solid position, adjust as needed, and make every move count for as much as was possible.

However, for fighting dragons, he had found a slightly altered approach worked better.

The beast rose from the ground, red sand cascading off its many spikes and spines. It was the size of the traincar itself, and its deep, fiery rage clearly reflected from within eyes as large as Veeran's own head, and it began to open its mouth in a mighty roar.

Veeran never gave it the opportunity. His leap carried him to the creature's snout, and while his weight scarcely impacted the dragon's actions, he smoothly drew a dagger from his belt, grasped one of the beast's needlessly large snout-spikes, and used [Piercing Strike] to sever it, giving him the perfect object which he could [Empowered Interdiction] and ⟨Rimeforge⟩ into what he had anticipated being a spear but manifested instead as a halberd.

Veeran's eyes flicked over its somewhat more ornate head, ensured it would still serve its function, mentally shrugged, and used it to half blind the dragon with a quick jab. It reeled back, and Veeran slipped off the side, landed in Giant Stance, and peeled back the creature's scales, exposing its flesh for a [Piercing Strike] augmented with [Sweeping Cut] such that Veeran's attack went through the creature, bursting out the other side.

The body slumped to the ground, and Veeran used his polearm to maneuver the corpse off the Junglewaste path and into The Jungle beyond.

It truly was considerate of the ravenous personification of uncontrollable wrath, consumption, and power to create a perfectly straight and level corridor through what was otherwise all but trackless and immensely thick wilderness. It even somehow crossed over the First River despite, if traveling along that particular waterway, the Junglewaste corridor was nowhere to be found. Smith had mentioned he was curious as to how such a thing functioned, yet Ride's cursory investigations into the matter - none of the rest of them were as equipped as she to find their way back home from the middle of nowhere, should the corridor vanish behind them - had been inconclusive, and had been otherwise too preoccupied to give it a proper study. It was a shame, as Veeran likewise found such more... subtle magics fascinating.

He had nothing but the utmost respect for those people who had taken 'magic' from a byword for the mysterious and unknown as it was in his grandfather's day, into the rigorous and understand toolset of today. Yet the modern analysis of magic preferred its usage as a discrete augment to that which already existed over a pure study of the esoteric.

Perhaps he was simply being a nostalgic old man.

Veeran returned to the build platform traincar, pulling himself up its attached ladder and situating himself within his meditation point yet again. He worked through the feel of his Skills, attempting to work with them in the ways Ride had described and incorporate them into his Stances. Twice more he was needed to drive off an opponent either beyond the defenses of the car, or due to a failure with its inbuilt defenses, before the machine finished emptying its payload and began the return journey.

As was typical, it was this return journey where Veeran was most needed. The tracks went largely unmolested, yet their slow traversal through dragon-infested wastelands ensured they faced far more foes than the handful of ballistae mounted upon the machine could solely handle.

When they did return, he hopped down from the traincar as new spider-bots began the process of refilling it, and gave his greetings to Haleford as the man was giving some attention to the emplaced defenses, performing the maintenance they so frequently required. The newest version of his steam cannon lay on the ground nearby, small enough for Haleford to lift it by himself, and ready for further practice or usage.

Upon his return to the Hearth, he found the food Haleford had mentioned to him and began to eat his dinner, while observing Smith working on his latest project.

It was a glider, perhaps some ten feet long with a thirty foot wingspan, and it had a most graceful and elegant appearance. Wings of wood and cloth curved out gently, looking like the sort of device the geniuses of old thought might enable humans to fly. As it swooped through the air, it did so with the sound of either mechanical birdsong or that of wind chimes, and while the seams between the wood and cloth were clearly visible, it did not seem to hamper the overall function of the device.

Beneath it, Smith directed it from place to place with the grace of a conductor and the absolute focus of a laser. He flicked his wrist, and the glider flew high, then he flattened his fingers and it swooped down to the ground below.

At that point, the Artificer noticed Veeran. They exchanged waves, and then Smith did some form of magic with the burst of tinkling bells and came over.

"Track laying is going well?" Smith asked with no other preamble.

Veeran nodded. "It is all proceeding as we anticipated it would. The track is largely unmolested, and I believe we have passed the First River, though it is challenging to confirm."

"Yeah. I get it. I need to remember to try and [Cogniprint] your ward at some point... when I have more time."

"Is your project likewise cooperating?"

"It is!" Smith happily exclaimed, then frowned. "Almost suspiciously well. I keep expecting something to go wrong. But I... actually no, I'm not going to jinx it. I'll just say that I think I'm finally finding my feet."

"That is most excellent to hear."

"Oh! Right, I almost forgot. I need your opinion on the helmet I made for you," Smith cast a hologram of a helmet, and a minute later a spider-bot came by and deposited a matching piece of gear.

"It's got room for cosmetic styling, so don't mind how it looks too much. It will actually need a buffer layer to help protect the enchantments, so if you do want it to look exactly like this it's not actually less work, so don't worry about that..." Smith nervously grew quiet as Veeran picked up the helmet and began to study it.

It was fully face-enclosing, a steel structure leaving a Y-shaped gap in the front that was itself filled with blue-tinted glass. Yet it was far from plain, as copper plates covered the cheekbones and a copper strip ran from the nape of the neck over the top and ending on the bridge of the nose. Bronze trim and detailing filled in much of the remaining details, and magic circles covered each ear. Many more glyphs covered the bronze, some of which pulsed with faint inner light, and the blue-glass visor shone with a light not attributable to their surroundings.

It was remarkably sleek and smooth, a construct which - were it not for its obvious material composition - would hardly seem out of place upon any modern warrior. Nary a toolmark could be seen upon its surface, and the metals were integrated with one another with perfect machine precision.

The inside was black leather, and whispered soft songs of knowledge and protection.

Veeran put it on, and was briefly unable to see. The blue tinge of the glass was too great, and it greatly constricted his field of vision besides. Then he blinked, and the coloration and view hindrance vanished. In its place was a crystal-clear view of everything around him, possibly even expanding his peripheral vision. Off to the side, Veeran's [Status] floated, showing that he had gotten a new level since he had last checked.

It was nothing he particularly needed to see at this time, but he was unable to dismiss it.

"It is not as uncomfortable as some helmets I have worn," Veeran commented.

"Yeah... it could be better," Smith said.

"I wish it did not interfere with my senses, however," he continued. "The magic around me is far more muffled than I would prefer. My sight is excellent, though."

"Good to know. I might need to work with you to make sure your arcanoception isn't hindered."

"There is a part of the casing which digs into the back of my neck," Veeran indicated, and Smith leaned over to look.

"Huh. I would have thought you were referring to the pokey arrow. But yeah, I can get that fixed."

Veeran pulled it off. "It is not as free-feeling as I would prefer," he apologetically stated.

"No comfort enchantments, unfortunately," Smith shrugged. "I've been trying to leverage some Air enchantment to at least prevent overheating, but that's a work in progress."

"What does it do, then?" Veeran asked. "Its comfort is second to its utility."

"It's got a divination engine based on the Window of Words and Winds as its primary function, but it has a standard suite of material reinforcement on top of just being tempered. Then a bit of [Unblemish] to keep it clean inside and out. But... mostly it's just the divination engine, and the internal sensory abstraction. Put it on for me?" he asked, and Veeran complied.

"It should allow you to Identify anything about as well as the Window can, and at slightly better range. It does take a bit of a spell, but it's super easy to cast."

"Would that enable me to dismiss my [Status] now that I do not require it?" Veeran inquired.

"Oh. It shouldn't be pulling that up automatically," Smith frowned. "But yes, all of those controls are pretty easy."

Smith's definition of what constituted as simple was not an accurate one. Hopefully, he had worked with Inq for developing the commands.

"But yeah. It can give identifications, directions including targeting assist, even leveling notifications. Those are the base functions, but I also tied it into the Network so that..." Smith grew quiet, but continued to speak, [I can do this! Or any of us can, really. Well, technically this is a spell that I don't know how easy it is, but there's a messaging system that will translate words spoken but unheard into a text message. This obviously only works when connected to the Network, but I isolated the connection features such that it safely fails when outside our territory. No message caching though, they just get lost if you don't catch the active broadcast.]

The words appeared as blue letters in Veeran's vision, slightly below Smith's head as though he were speaking with captions in a movie.

"Because I'm acting as a bit of a root node, I can also set up various Autonomous Divinations to give alerts to anyone wearing a helmet, which is definitely more useful than what we've got now. Still working on audiovisual streaming or recording, but I'm hopeful I can get those working. But yeah! Still a bit rough, but I think it works."

"It does," Veeran pulled off the helmet. It was hardly an impressive slate of abilities by the standards of what he was accustomed to, yet it simultaneously restored a great many capabilities which the System itself natively provided, and that was no mean feat. "I am rather fond of the current appearance, you are welcome to leave my helmet looking akin to this for the final configuration."

"Awesome. I'm planning on making the glass larger on mine. Probably black?" Smith speculated aloud.

"Is there any particular advantage to one form over another?" Veeran inquired. He was certainly not about to make a decision entirely on aesthetics if such a thing might compromise performance.

"Eh. Kind of, but not excessively," Smith shrugged. "The kinds of things I'm implementing don't have super stringent requirements on the actual visor shape. Broadly speaking, the more glass the stronger the divinations will be, and the more metal the better it'll serve at protecting your head. Maybe as we go forward, the differences will become more pronounced, but modern wearable-type enchantments have a pretty good flexibility of form, and with Clark's [Unblemish] I can get that working a lot sooner than otherwise."

"How long do you anticipate before the helmet is complete?" Head protection was quite valuable, and though Veeran was confident he did not require armor, having armor enabled a far broader suite of tactics. The distinction between being capable of defending against an attack, and not needing to defend against an attack, was a great one.

"Depends on how annoying the audial arcanoception issue is. Are you back for the night?" Smith asked.

Veeran shook his head. "I intend to oversee one more trip of the train before I retire today."

"That's fair. I suppose there was still some tracks in stock. I'll tinker with it while you're gone when I'm not working on the glider, and when you get back we'll see if I've fixed it."

That was remarkably fast. "Would it be preferable for me to remain here?"

Smith declined. "I need some time to poke at it now that I know there's an issue first to figure out what might be going wrong. Do your guard run, it'll be here when you get back."

Veeran finished his meal, bade his goodbyes, assisted the spider-bots in loading up the final few railway ties and tracks, and set out once more into the Junglewaste

They were but halfway to where new track would be laid when [Empowered Interdiction] flared into full power.

First Tower was under attack.
