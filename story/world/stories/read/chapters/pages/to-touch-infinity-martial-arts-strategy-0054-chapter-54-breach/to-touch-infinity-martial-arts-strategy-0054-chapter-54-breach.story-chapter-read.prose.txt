The sour fumes of wet quicklime were making Tae-seok’s remaining good eye water.

The old general stood on top of the wooden frame their men had constructed, slightly to one side of the gatehouse, resting his palm on the rampart. Just behind him, Chae-ha was returning from her inspection of a lower section of the wall, wiping sludgy gray paste from her fingers into various cracks. She had spent the last thirty-six hours obsessively overseeing modifications to the masonry beneath them. Wagonloads of materials had disappeared in rapid succession as they had worked.

Down the road, the continued quiet felt ominous. Chae-ha had ordered the two hundred Tai-shan disciples serving as a decoy force to retreat hours ago. Nature abhorred a vacuum…and so did an invading army. A few hours of stunned surprise after the decoy column vanished, the Meng-shan began their advance. After all, by withdrawing her troops, the young Inspector had practically begged them to do so.

Mu-cheon paced back and forth along the timber walkway, the handle of the Dae-jik-do on his back swinging dangerously close to wooden support poles every time he turned.

“They should have been here by noon,” the Inner Disciple muttered, glancing out over the empty, sun-drenched road stretching towards the southern bend. “The Meng-shan march fast. Why haven’t our scouts spotted them yet?”

“Their strategist is checking for traps that aren’t there,” Chae-ha explained. “To him, this whole situation smells like bait, and he doesn’t know for what. That caution is what bought us the time we needed to finish the fill.”

Tae-seok turned his gaze away from the road, looking back at the girl. “Are you sure this madness is going to work?”

“It doesn’t matter what I think. It has to work, or things are about to get very, very bad, Elder. Are you ready?”

Tae-seok didn’t bother answering. He adjusted the leather strap of his new eyepatch instead, feeling the familiar, dull throb of the scars beneath.

“The road curves just before this space,” Chae-ha continued, pointing towards the mouth of the clearing. “Their siege engine needs to pass that curve to be able to take a clean shot. They’ll have to drag it into range before they can try to line it up against the gatehouse. It’ll work.”

Tae-seok grunted in reply. He turned his sweeping gaze slowly down the line of the rampart they’d constructed, making one final appraisal of their preparations.

Dozens of local archers were hunkered down behind the makeshift wooden merlons, clutching nervously at their longbows. Commander Shin was pacing near the stairwell, wiping a continuous sheen of nervous sweat from his neck. The engineers had all left to go hide. Everything seemed to be in place.

The day was cool, but despite the weather, the air in their manufactured glade felt suffocating. With every breath that passed in silence, the anxiety on the wall ratcheted higher, like a bowstring being drawn past its breaking point. Heartbeats raced as everyone waited for the inevitable arrival of the enemy.

Finally, just as the afternoon sun began to dip behind the western crags, the ominous silence collapsed.

Softly at first, but then progressively more loudly, the sound of thousands of men marching in unison began to echo off the canyon walls. The noise was followed by the exertions of straining oxen and the regular boom, boom, boom of a man beating steadily on a small drum.

As they watched, the first rank of Meng-shan spearmen rounded the corner and spilled into the clearing. They moved in a tight, perfectly synchronized formation. Their bucklers were raised, their weapons were ready, and their eyes kept darting warily towards the tree line.

Into the space behind them rolled the star attraction.

Six burly oxen strained against heavy leather harnesses, their nostrils flaring as they hauled an imposing apparatus of iron-braced oak into the mouth of the clearing. The metal hasps on the siege ballista's three linked composite bows gleamed dully under the mountain light.  The device's foreboding winch mechanism bristled with iron gears.

And beside the guard detail, atop a sleek black stallion, was the enemy strategist – comfortably insulated from danger by several ranks of spearmen.

Tae-seok’s eye narrowed. The Lesser Elder was keeping his distance. His fan was held high, his posture radiating a mix of caution and triumph as he pointed down the lane.

Tae-seok walked up to the rampart, stepping on a small crate to raise his upper body fully into view of the Meng-shan army across the clearing. He took a deep breath, and invoked the fourth mnemonic of the Chuk-ap-gyeol. He felt the pressure building up in his throat, until he finally released the seal on his Tian Tu point, and allowed his voice to come thundering out across the artificial clearing.

“WELCOME BACK, BOY! MISSED THE FEELING OF MY BLADE AGAINST YOUR NECK?”

Wi Mo watched with partial disbelief as the scenery opened up into an engineered clearing, right in the middle of what was once dense forest.

His scouts had warned him of this, of course, but a part of him had refused to believe it was true. Yet, there it was, right in front of him: an oval clearing carved out of the mountain timberland, anchored in the center by a gatehouse wall made of wood and stone.

So this was what the Tai-shan had been doing! This was why they had let him waste days clearing felled pines and sweeping for caltrops. This was why they had abandoned the junction without a fight. It was all to prepare this surprise.

Well, none of it mattered. They’d spent weeks sweating over stone and timber, but what they’d built was nothing more than an oversized wooden coffin. He had with him the solution to this problem.

“Bring the ballista to the front!” Wi Mo commanded, his fan snapping shut with a sharp clack. “Line it up to hit the gatehouse!”

His gaze drifted across the field. The path forwards was interrupted by a maze of sharp, carved tree trunks called abatis. Their butts were buried in the earth, they jutted up at an angle, and there was no way for a column to march through them. Any invading force would have to split up to approach the wall.

He continued scanning the clearing, his gaze wandering upwards and across the ramparts. And then, his eyes widened.

Standing proud against the afternoon sun, and looking taller than the Lesser Elder remembered him, was the disfigured, one-eyed old monster himself. The man who had stolen the glory of an untarnished victory straight from his grasp. The man who had almost ended Wi Mo’s life.

The old brute was staring across the field at him – and it was glaringly obvious that it was deliberate. The veteran's voice vibrated through the canyon like a gong had been struck:

“WELCOME BACK, BOY! MISSED THE FEELING OF MY BLADE AGAINST YOUR NECK?”

Wi Mo’s hands trembled, but he forced a cold, condescending sneer onto his face. He would keep his cool. He was a strategist; he wouldn't succumb to petty taunts.

More importantly, he had already seen what the siege ballista could do in trial firings. One bolt – just one – would punch clean through that makeshift wall of timber and stone, exploding it into a cloud of shrapnel and flying rubble. If he survived at all, the old monster would be left collapsed on a pile of debris, just in time for hundreds of Meng-shan spearmen to swarm into the breach and butcher him.

“Turn the cranks!” Wi Mo ordered, pointing his fan directly at the part of the wall where Tae-seok stood. “Prepare to fire! Aim for that section of wall!”

The ballista crew scrambled into action. Forged iron cranks creaked noisily as four men threw their weight into the winches, and gears began to turn. Clickety-clickety-clack.

The mechanical ratchets slowly pulled back, storing immense kinetic force in the three linked composite bows. The machine’s iron-braced, oaken frame creaked under the strain. Sweating, muscle-bound disciples pushed ever harder, their faces turning bright purple as they forced the handles around one final time.

Click.

The last ratchet step locked into place. The iron ballista bolt, as thick as a young pine tree and tipped with forged steel, lay locked in the firing groove.

“Loose!” Wi Mo screamed.

A soldier dropped the release trigger. The three connected bowstrings snapped forwards with a deafening FWOOSH. The bolt rocketed out and across the clearing like the divine rage of an angry god, carrying enough force behind it to smash stone and obliterate wooden ramparts in a single heartbeat.

Wi Mo braced himself for the imminent explosion.

There was a sharp, initial crack as the outer wooden façade broke open – immediately followed by a squishy, wet squelch.

Wi Mo’s victorious smile faltered.

Instead of a cloud of flying debris, the wooden cladding ruptured to reveal a thick, gray, sopping mess. Fresh, un-cured, runny mortar spewed outwards as the enormous iron bolt was swallowed whole by a reservoir of grainy, viscous muck. It disappeared inside the pile of goo with a moist plop.

The mortar slumped slightly around the protruding tail of the bolt, weeping gray slurry down the thin pillars that were actually holding the walkway in place.

Wi Mo stared on in utter disbelief. His mouth hung open. This was…an illusion. A trick! It couldn’t be real.

The Tai-shan had hollowed-out a section of the wall and filled it with sludgy, damp mortar? The strategist looked back at his weapon in disbelief. This was a strategy designed to do precisely one thing: absorb one single bolt from a siege ballista.

His eyes frantically darted back up to the top of the ramparts. The wooden walkway still stood. But…

Eom Tae-seok was gone.

A suffocating feeling of horror gripped Wi Mo’s chest. Not again! Where…was he?!

“NOW!”

The booming voice of the Tai-shan’s Young Master echoed from the ramparts above.

Before Wi Mo could process what the command was supposed to mean, rows of Tai-shan archers stood up in unison from behind the wooden posts. Bowstrings twanged in a synchronized chorus.

A dark cloud of steel-tipped arrows rose into the air, cresting over the clearing before descending like holy rain upon the clustered Meng-shan ranks.

Blood rushed to Wi Mo’s ears as he tried to stop sheer panic from breaking his composure. He reined his horse back wildly, and screamed the first thing that came to mind:

“The shields, you fools! GET THE SHIELDS UP!”

Behind the thick timber frame of the half-open gate, Chae-ha watched as their archers loosed another volley into the afternoon sky.

Across the clearing, a hazy blur tore through the dust. Eom Tae-seok moved with supernatural speed, propelling himself with alternating blasts of pressure towards the enemy forces. A white mist trailed in his wake.

At the entrance to the glade, bedlam gripped the Meng-shan ranks. Disciples who had been trying to re-crank the winches ducked for cover as steel-tipped pine shafts rained down on their heads. Bucklers were thrust skywards in a desperate attempt to put up some protection, resulting in a few stuck arrows among showers of splintering wood. A discordant symphony composed of thunk-thunk-chink-chink echoed across the valley as shafts slammed into shields, many finding gaps and sinking into flesh.

Chae-ha watched Tae-seok blur across the field behind the projectiles. Right as he arrived, the hail of arrows abruptly ceased.

Without breaking stride, the elder leaped into the air while the Meng-shan were still crouching. His boot landed squarely atop a buckler that was bristling with arrows; the spearman beneath it let out a harsh gasp as the force of the impact crumpled him into the mud. Using the man’s falling body as a launching pad, Tae-seok kicked off the buckler, soaring high above the front ranks and straight towards the siege engine.

The veteran general shot past the exhausted team of oxen. He could probably have cut one of them in half, if he had wanted – but draft animals were not his target today.

Even from where Chae-ha stood, the young girl could hear the words he screamed as he approached the machine.

“Dan-hae-gyeok!” he roared, his voice ringing through the valley and echoing off the canyon walls. “Sea-Splitting Slash!”

In the heartbeat that followed, reality seemed to fracture. Time ground to a halt, and the vibrant colors of the valley drained away into a familiar black and white. For a fraction of a second, the screaming men, the debris from the arrows, and the drifting dust all froze mid-air. Meanwhile, across the front of the siege ballista, a single, razor-thin black line appeared. It passed through the top of an iron wheel, through the central gear housing, across the first of the three composite bows, and straight into an iron crank.

Color flooded back into the world in a sudden rush, and a thunderclap echoed as the air displaced by his strike collapsed to fill the void.

All the tension stored within the machine by the panicked attempts to re-crank it was suddenly released. The composite bow limbs fractured into messy splinters. Iron gears spun off their axles, flying across the field and striking one unfortunate soldier in the head. Bits of iron and wood shook themselves off the siege ballista’s frame as the device disintegrated into a shower of twisted metal and kindling.

Chae-ha’s heart raced in her chest as the dust settled. This would be the decisive moment. How would their strategist react? Would the destruction of his weapon trigger a sensible retreat? Or…would his bruised ego force a reckless gamble?

Through the haze of flying splinters, Wi Mo stood high in his stirrups, his face contorted in obvious rage. The man whipped his fan forwards, pointing it first at Eom Tae-seok, and then directly at the oozing mess coming from the lower section of the gatehouse wall.

He was splitting his troops – trying to surround Elder Eom, while ordering a full-frontal assault on their most apparent weakness.

The Meng-shan column advanced, locking their bucklers over their heads as fresh lines of men pressed through a renewed rain of arrows, aiming straight for the gap in the wall.

That answered the question of which plan to deploy.

From their perches above, the archers had an unobstructed view of the clearing. As long as the Meng-shan were at least thirty paces away, they would be sitting ducks in the open field. But the base of the wall created a blind spot. If the purple-clad soldiers reached the gate itself, they could gather against it and be mostly safe from arrows. To prevent them hacking at the wood, tearing apart the joinery, or just marching through the breach, the battle needed to happen out in front of the wall.

That was why the abatis had been anchored so far out – to split the Meng-shan column into narrow channels, which their allies could defend while the archers continued to fire over their heads.

The young girl turned towards Commander Shin, meeting his anxious gaze with a nod.

Shin drew his sword and screamed out his orders. “Open the sally ports! Don’t let them get through!”

With a chorus of roars, the Tai-shan infantry surged past the gates, pouring into the abatis-filled field to meet the Meng-shan charge. Lines of swordsmen formed at the end of each of the lanes, waiting for the moment the first surviving spearmen made it through.

Positioned safely near the threshold of the gatehouse, Chae-ha watched the choke points form. Beside her, Sergeant Noh Gu-shim and his three veterans adjusted their shields, standing ready to receive her command.

Chae-ha put a hand into her sleeve, feeling the cold steel of one of her needles for reassurance. Soon, it would be their turn. But for now, the Tai-shan line was holding. And there was still one more thing that needed to happen.

The young Inspector turned to the side, cupping her hands around her lips for emphasis, and screamed at the top of her lungs.

“SENIOR BROTHER…NOW!”
