“Where is she!?” Tressa’s father, Altex, roared as he slammed his hand down on his table. Seven guards stood there with Aldmern, shifting their feet uncomfortably. The anger of their master was very much justified at the loss of his daughter.

“We don’t know, my lord. She is beyond the borders,” said the captain, who knelt as he spoke, one arm resting over his bent knee and a fist against the ground, his head bowed in respect to his lord.

“I said find her! It’s been three days and my daughter is still missing! Aldmern, you have one chance to redeem yourself.” He growled through gritted teeth. Aldmern had been tasked with watching her, and he had failed in doing so as she took off into the massive forest around the capital, filled with dangerous creatures that took a hunting party to ward off or take down.

“I’m so sorry, my lord,” he said with great sorrow. He had informed his lord and mistress that he had an argument with Tressa and how she had run off afterward. He claimed he had lost her trail and couldn’t find her.

“I don't want excuses. I want my daughter home. Get another search party together and find her! The council said they will dispatch as many elves as needed. Just bring her home.”

“Now, now, my dear, please do calm yourself,” Lucyia said as her teacup clinked against the saucer.

“Three days, Lucyia. Three days and there hasn’t been a sign of her,” he said, pinching the bridge of his nose as he thought of Tressa lost in the forest or possibly killed. He then looked to the men gathered before him. “What are you still standing around for! Go on, keep searching.” He waved his hand in dismissal to his men and nodded at Aldmern to go as well.

They all bowed. The captain rose to his feet and ushered his men out, going off to plan and coordinate another search party. After the door closed behind them, Altex fell into his chair, holding his head.

“Why would she run off?” he muttered. “I don’t understand how she could be so angry with Aldmern. Clearly, there is something he isn’t saying.”

“Perhaps, but let us not jump to conclusions, my love,” Lucyia said soothingly. “You know he cares for her like a sister, after all.”

He only huffed at that, tapping his finger against the table in agitation.

“My love,” Lucyia continued, setting her tea down and walking over to rub his back, trying to soothe some of the tension from his body. “Worry not, she will return home, safe and sound. Tressa is quite a powerful mage and so skilled at her age.”

“Yes. But you know as well as I how reckless she can be.” He let out a breath, his eyes narrow and intense in thought. “Why would she run off? We should have Aldmern pulled from the search party and properly interrogated. I know he is hiding something. I just know it.”

“My love, perhaps this argument was merely a final straw? I don’t see Aldmern alone causing her to decide this. I think we should actually look at ourselves.”

“What are you saying?”

“You try to hold too tight of a leash on our daughter.”

He instantly looked at his wife, who moved back to her chair and sat down with her legs crossed, picking up her cup once more and finishing the last bit in the cup, and went to pour more, but found the pot empty.

He was appalled at her comment. He did know that she had a point. But how could he not hold her so close when she had almost been taken from him as a child?

“I do not!”

“You do.”

A knock came to the door, and Altex called for them to enter

“Ah, Lytin,” Lucyia said.

“My lady, would you like some fresh tea?” the maid said with a bow. She held a tray with a fresh pot, light steam coming from its spout.

“Of course, thank you,” she said as she set her empty cup down, smiling sweetly at her. “In truth, not a moment too soon.”

She set the tray down on the table in front of her and poured fresh tea into her cup.

“Thank you. I can take it from here.”

She bowed deeply, and left the room.

Lucyia sighed, smelled the tea, and took a sip.

“How do I keep a tight leash on her? She never listens. All she wants to do is wander around the woods and run off at the slightest drop of a hat. I do think it's best to send her to the temple. I almost lost her, and you, and I don't want that to ever happen again."

Lucyia took a long sip of her tea, closing her eyes and taking a moment to breathe in its scent. When she set it down again, she looked to her husband.

“You have no clue what your daughter is capable of. All you see is what you want.”

“How can you say that?” he asked angrily.

She let out a sigh. “My love, Tressa is a brilliant child. And need I remind you, you were just as bad as her at that age.”

“I was not. I listened to my father and mother and was a respectful son. I did as they asked.”

She raised an eyebrow over her cup and took another sip before she spoke.

“Need I remind you of the incident with the Everwine?”

He intently sucked in a breath and looked away, unable to meet her eyes as he rubbed the back of his neck.

“Or should I bring up the demolished building in the western courtyard?”

“That was… a long time ago. And it’s been repaired since!” He sighed. “Alright, alright, I get your point. But at least I listened.”

Another eyebrow raised, and she gave him a look.

“Most of the time,” he corrected. “But that is besides the point. Tressa needs to learn that her best option is to join the temple and study there.”

He looked at his wife, who finished her cup of tea and set it down before she gave him that look that made him feel he had missed something of extreme importance.

“Perhaps you should take more interest in what she’s actually been up to. It may impress you.”

“What do you mean? She is completely immature for her age. Yes, she is strong at magic, but she is turning into a crude barbarian! Running around with not a single care in the world. She is a daughter of the high council; that means the eyes of our people are on her. And with her trials coming early, it will be even more so. Now is the time to leave her childish ways behind!”

“She’s highly intelligent in ways that cannot be learned from books or in classes. Having her be some obedient child would ruin what she is. Have you seen any of her notebooks? She may turn out to be a scholar, just not the type that sits behind a desk. She may even stand among the great Guiders who have brought all our knowledge to the library.”

“A Scholar? A Guider? Lucyia, at the rate she’s going, she’s unlikely to fit into far simpler roles even.” As he saw his wife’s expression, he cleared his throat. “Notebooks? What notebooks?”

“Come, I’ll show you,” she said, getting up, setting her teacup down and leading the way out of the room.

He stood, confused by what she was talking about. There was no way Tressa was a scholar; it didn’t seem right. No, she would join the temple like he wanted and remain safe within their kingdom. There, she would finally be forced to learn discipline and her role as a noble high elf. Then, after a time, when he thought she was ready, he would have her join the council.

She led him down the hall and into Tressa’s room, then went right to a bookcase on the far wall. She scanned the shelves until she found what she was looking for and pulled out a beat-up, leather-bound book.

“What are you doing?” Altex asked.

“You’ll see for yourself in just a moment,” she said, walking back over to him, her heels clicking softly on the carpeted floor.

He took the beat-up leather-bound book as she passed it to him. Its spine was cracked from great use and smelled of ink.

“Go on, see for yourself what your daughter has been up to.”

Looking between his beloved wife and the book multiple times, he sighed and opened to a random page in the center. His face instantly turned from skepticism to astonishment.

“My word…” he muttered under his breath.

His daughter’s detailed sketch showed a bird from several different angles. Next to those drawings were detailed sketches of its eyes, beak, and wings, all with notes written close to them, detailing wingspans, colors, and other observations. The words were written in neat, organized handwriting. He held it in his hands as he went from page to page, his shock growing to respect. Page after page were more of those incredible sketches.

Finally, he stopped on a page and read the entry.

Blue Mimic

A species of monster that is somewhat similar to mockingbirds, though larger, and may have an ancestral relation. These may be one of the greatest links between monster and animal species that supports my theory of monsters being evolved forms of animals and more closely related than tradition would have us believe.

Adult Blue Mimics are large enough to fit both hands around their bodies. They have a wingspan of about three feet and ultra-sharp talons.

I’ve observed their peculiar hunting strategy of pairing up with larger predators. By mimicking the sounds of prey, they lure them into traps to be eaten. Though with some close calls, I have also observed that besides their need to eat flesh, they also have the ability to copy songs. They are quite gentle if taught a song and will aid the person in gratitude for learning a new sound to mimic.

They are consumable, though poisonous if prepared improperly. My experiments have proven that boiling the flesh for three minutes is sufficient to remove the poison, and then they can be roasted and consumed.

If consumed without proper cleaning, one will experience extreme stomach cramps and hallucinations. Though the poison has been shown to be non lethal, caution is still advised. In truth, I would list this monster as a “Class D threat,” as they don’t pose much danger if you can distinguish the sounds and notes of their songs and mimicked cries.

Update: I’ve now consumed five while using the boiling and roasting method. Their meat is quite tender. I have concluded this is an effective method to safely eat them. As such, they shall fall under “Consumable.”

Future research will be focused on developing an antidote and on how to safely extract their poison without harming them, so that I might use it to create a hallucinogenic cocktail.

Altex froze after reading that. His gaze shot to his wife. “She has been eating Blue Mimics!? And she plans to create poisons!?” He paled as he set the book down and shook his head. “Goddess… what is wrong with this daughter of mine?”

His wife suppressed a chuckle. “That one, I did not know about. Still, I’m sure she would never use such a potion to harm anyone. But that is not all she has done. She has filled notebook after notebook full of things like that. She has seen things neither you nor I have. She writes down detailed descriptions of monsters, birds, animals, and plants. Some of her research is even more detailed than our own records, and she has discovered things nobody else knows.”

He let out a breath, continuing to flip through the pages. “This is…” He let out a deep hmmmm as he flipped a certain page back and forth. “Well, it may be rather impressive… but…”

“My love, that isn’t the only notebook,” she replied.

“What?” he asked, eyes coming up to meet his wife’s.

“They are all over the house.”

“I have never seen them before. And what do you mean all over the house?”

“Come with me,” she said, leading him out of the room and down to their personal library. “Look around, look for more of those leather-bound books,” she said, finding a chair to sit in while he searched.

After about twenty minutes, he returned with two of them.

“How many of these are there?” he said, sitting down across from her and opening one of the notebooks, flipping through the pages, eyes widening as he discovered it was just as detailed as the last.

“About a dozen that I’m aware of. I tasked Maikia with searching for them one day, but had her promise not to let Tressa know we know. Tressa even put cloaking spells on some of them. As I have said before, Tressa is like none other. She is talented, bright, and adventurous. She would surpass any scholar of the temple or library. She is skilled beyond her years. I have also found books where she tried to break down the concept of a wind spell and combine it with a fire spell.”

“What? But that’s… that’s against our laws! She is not qualified to…”

“It was just some harmless musing, Altex,” she said, using his name. “But think, Tressa may have brought the attention of the gods because of these skills. Why else would the statue call upon her so early?”

He thought about it. It was true. Upon hearing of the dragon’s supposed interest in his daughter, he first only thought of fears that she wouldn’t stand up to the responsibility it implied. He never stopped to think, to really think, why she would have gotten the dragon’s attention in the first place. Even with that revelation, his instinct was not to trust in his daughter.

“I am such a fool,” Altex said, placing a hand over his brow.

“Hahaha. That you are, my love.”

“You don’t have to rub it in, Lucyia.” He shot her a playful glare, causing her to smile ever wider. “I’ll speak to her when she returns home. There is now much more to discuss.”

“If you plan to keep her caged after learning this, it may backfire on you and not in the way you would think.”

He shook his head. “No, that is not my plan.”

Lucyia took his hand and kissed it. “Oh, how you have grown in such a short moment. I hope you are wise enough not to hope to leash her, but let her soar as she needs to.”
