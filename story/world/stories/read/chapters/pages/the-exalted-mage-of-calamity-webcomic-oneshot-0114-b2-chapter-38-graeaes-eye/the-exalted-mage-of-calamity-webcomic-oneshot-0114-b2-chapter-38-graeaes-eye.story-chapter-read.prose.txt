Harris watched Veronica and her two aides leave the Starfault Archives.

Only a few hours ago, he had suffered a humiliating defeat. Not only to his pride, but to his position as well.

He was a sixth-tier mage at only thirty-two years old, mere steps away from reaching the seventh tier. That was the result of hard work and talent. Enough talent that, had he been younger, he might have been admitted to Valeborn. Instead, he had been appointed as one of the senior mage officials overseeing the Starfault Archives.

Within its boundaries, he was granted near-unlimited freedom. He could study and even borrow any book or record, including ancient texts from the fifth floor. He was someone meant to be respected and praised. And yet, even his own juniors, the ones he had painstakingly taught, had betrayed him. They had stepped back and left him alone to suffer the indignation caused by that woman.

Just who exactly was she?

A commoner carrying a nobleman’s signet. A viscount’s signet, at that. She had the gall to insult and antagonize him. But even more surprising was the way she carried herself. It was as if she had been trained to speak like a noble.

But she clearly was not one. Not only because of her clothes, but because she carried another noble’s signet. Such items were gifted to individuals of lesser social standing as a means of temporarily raising their status. Rarely were they ever given to lesser nobles. Most often, they ended up in the hands of commoners.

“Senior Harris?”

Marcen’s voice came from beside him.

Harris glanced back. His two assistants stood near the appraisal table, both pretending not to look as uncomfortable as they were. They had been quiet ever since the apology. Perhaps that was wise of them. Perhaps cowardly.

Most likely both.

“I’m going upstairs,” Harris said.

“Now?” Marcen asked, slightly confused, but still wary.

“You and Farcie are to reorganize the shelves of the first floor tonight. With no magic,” he said, walking away without even glancing back.

Marcen and Farcie looked at one another, then silently obeyed. They both knew they had wronged their senior. Perhaps simple cleaning duty was a more lenient punishment than they deserved.

The stone golems of the first floor watched Harris as he passed, but did not stop him. His presence was known to every guardian inside the archive. He had been granted proper authority upon receiving the title of senior scholar.

He continued up the stairs, passing the second floor, then the third, then the fourth.

By the time he reached the fifth and final floor, the guards had changed from stone and steel puppets to elementals. They stood in rows beside the entrance, each one formed from a different element. Fire burned quietly in the shape of a broad knight. Lightning flickered within a thin, spear-carrying figure. A water elemental stood motionless beside one made of pale wind.

All of them turned slightly, their attention pointed toward Harris as he arrived.

He walked past them casually and entered the fifth floor.

The air felt older there, carrying the scent of parchment and dried ink from works written hundreds of years ago. The shelves were darker, and the silence was even deeper. Not many people had access to the fifth floor besides great contributors or recognized nobles.

A single clerk sat at the desk near the entrance, an elderly man in gray robes with a thin chain attached to his spectacles. He looked up from a ledger as Harris approached. “Senior Harris,” the clerk said. “Is something wrong?”

“Perhaps. Perhaps not.” Harris placed both hands behind his back. “Three guests came through here earlier under Lord Vaun’s authority. A woman with slightly tanned skin, a boisterous girl, and a red-haired woman.”

The clerk nodded. “Yes, I remember them. They just left.”

“Did they do anything odd?”

“Odd?” The clerk thought for a moment. “No. They read quietly in the study section. They did not damage anything, remove anything, or attempt to cause any issues.”

Harris’ lips dipped into a frown.

That was disappointing to hear.

“What did they read? Any specific texts?”

“The younger girl asked whether we had any additional blooming-related works. Spellbooks, to be precise. It seemed she was carrying some books that referenced Fia Twindale.”

Harris recognized the name. Most did. She was the Phoenix Archmage from the prior generation, known for summoning spells related to the immortal bird of eternal flame.

“Was there anything else?”

The clerk hummed softly. “Hm… the red-haired woman, I believe, took out some cultivation manuals. One primarily focused on aura manifestation.”

Cultivation manuals? He couldn’t care less about insignificant aura users. “And the last woman?”

The clerk glanced toward the deeper aisles. “She looked through several shelves herself. I believe she searched for older historical texts. One book in particular caught her attention, though I cannot recall the title.”

Harris’ eyes narrowed. “You cannot recall?”

“It was not a restricted spellbook,” the clerk said, his tone rather patient compared to Harris’. “She didn’t speak to me directly, so I do not know what she read. I can confirm she placed it back in the aisle, however.”

Harris took a slow breath. “Show me where they sat.”

The clerk hesitated, but only for a moment. “This way.”

The clerk led him past several rows of shelves and into a quiet study section near the back of the fifth floor. A few tables sat there, each spaced far apart to preserve silence and privacy. No one else was there now.

“This is where they spent most of their time,” the clerk said.

Harris looked over the table. Nothing remained on it. No papers. No ink stains. “Leave me,” he said.

The clerk bowed lightly and returned to his desk.

Harris waited until the man was gone before reaching into his robe. He withdrew a small, round crystal the size of his thumb. It was clear at first, but when he turned it in the light, faint gray lines shifted inside it like smoke trapped within glass.

Graeae’s Eye. An artifact carried by revelation mages to assist their magic without spending too much mana. It was one of many bound artifacts a revelation mage could choose to forge. Some chose to enchant their eyes directly. Others preferred external items, such as Graeae’s Eye or even prophetic books.

Holding it up, Harris poured in his mana. The eye brightened, and the mist within began to swirl. Pale gray light spilled across the study table.

For a few seconds, nothing happened.

Then slowly, three figures began to appear. They were faint and hazy, like ghosts beneath moonlight.

It was the three women who had humiliated him. Each sat at the table, reading the books before them. Occasionally, they leaned over and spoke with one another, or flipped through the pages of their books. Besides that, there was little else to see.

With Graeae’s Eye, he could project a scene that had happened recently in an area. But sound was not something he could recreate. Not until he reached a higher tier.

Harris shifted his bound artifact and accelerated the scene, all the way until they finished reading. He followed Veronica’s image to the exact section where she placed the book back on the shelf. Then he pocketed the eye and reached up, pulling down the old book.

It was thin, an aged volume with a faded brown cover.

The Isles of Avalon.

He stared at the title.

Avalon?

What exactly was this?

He opened the book. The first few pages were nonsense. A fairy tale. A poetic rambling about saving some hero on a hidden island while he was chased by otherworldly enemies. Swords, white trees, and treasures. Something one might read as a bedtime story to a child.

The fifth floor archive was filled with such things. Ancient folktales and myths. Lies so old that almost no one could verify their authenticity.

Rubbish.

He nearly closed the book. But then his hand stopped.

Why was she reading this? So thoroughly, too.

He pulled out Graeae’s Eye again and looked through the scene once more.

She had come to the fifth floor as though searching specifically for this book. The moment it was found, she took it and began reading it for the entire duration of her stay on the fifth floor. Was there… some merit to the text in front of him?

She had surprised and humiliated him earlier. But he couldn’t deny that she was deeply mysterious. An enigma against everything he knew about mage tiers. It was ridiculous. A third-tier mage casting a seventh-tier spell? She must have obtained something fortuitous, or perhaps had an expert formation and array mage as her master.

Her spell was unlike anything he had ever seen. Even the way she warped the spell circle to contain the lightning was strange.

She was strange.

Harris stared down at the book cover. His eyes narrowed as he reread the title. “Fine. I’ll entertain this. Let’s see if there is any merit to it.”

He kept the book at his side and walked back toward the staircase. The clerk glanced at him, then at the book in his hand. Because Harris worked at the archives, he was permitted to take any material with him, including texts from the fifth floor.

“Have a good day, Senior Harris.”

Harris simply glanced back and nodded, saying nothing more as he descended the steps.

The next day, around lunchtime, Veronica and Gwen left the Maristar Gold Inn and headed toward the east side of the city.

Nasha didn’t come with them.

She said she had business in the city. Something about meeting old friends and checking a few places she used to know. Veronica had looked at her for a moment, but Nasha’s expression was the same as usual, and after some light… flirtatious teasing, Veronica let the matter go.

She wasn’t Nasha’s mother, and she didn’t want to come off as too clingy.

Most of yesterday had been spent studying. She had Sage memorize each page inside The Aisles of Avalon. That, however, did not mean Veronica could afford to be complacent. She still needed to study and memorize the text herself. Sage could remind her of the more convoluted parts or specific details, but Veronica also needed to become an expert on the island of Avalon.

The book itself contained multiple drawings and symbols, along with locations of traps and even secret passages. The island was covered in countless arrays, and some areas even acted as labyrinths to trap invaders who did not know the rules.

Gwen, meanwhile, had spent most of her time learning from several books written by Fia Twindale. They weren’t perfectly aligned with her Path of Longevity, since Fia Twindale followed the Path of Splitting, but they were useful enough to study. She would just have to try to apply the principles to her own path.

“You said they’re open every day, correct?” Veronica asked.

Gwen, walking beside her, nodded. “Yeah. I heard people talking about it each time I passed the building. It should have opened two days ago.”

Back when they first arrived in Maftem and Veronica had sent Gwen and Nasha off to explore, Gwen had discovered where the Pelotema Grand Auction would be held. In the process, she had also learned about something called the pre-auction showing. A public viewing where interested buyers could see some of the items that would appear in the actual auction.

Not all of the items would be shown. From what Gwen had heard, only around a third or fourth of the total lots would be on display, all randomly chosen. It was meant to primarily gather interest and draw a larger audience once the actual auction took place in three days.

Veronica didn’t have anything specific she was looking for. However, all sorts of treasures appeared in auctions like these. More likely than not, she would find several worthwhile items to bid on.

She just needed enough money.

As the two of them walked, a sudden shout came from the street ahead.

Several people had gathered near the mouth of an alley. Not many. Maybe a dozen or so. Most stood at a cautious distance, whispering among themselves while two men in white medical uniforms carried someone out on a stretcher.

The person on the stretcher was giggling. Not too loudly, but not softly either. It was breathless and broken, like pained laughter forced from someone with a bone stabbing into their lungs.

Veronica and Gwen both moved a bit closer to see what was happening.

Strangely enough, as the two blooming mages carried him away, Veronica noticed that the man wasn’t exactly in pain.

No.

He was smiling. Completely blissful.

They moved farther away, but Veronica’s enhanced senses could still hear the man whisper. Or perhaps it was more of a gasping struggle.

“So… beautiful,” he muttered, staring straight up into the sky. “It’s all white. All of it. So beautiful…”

A few people in the crowd looked on with concern, while others shook their heads and walked away.

A woman nearby spoke in a lowered voice. “That’s the third one this week.”

“Fourth,” another man said. “Cathie from the floral shop became like that yesterday, too. That’s why her shop was closed.”

The crowd began to disperse. Some people left in groups or pairs, speaking more privately about what had happened.

“What was wrong with him?” Gwen asked, confused.

Veronica’s eyes narrowed, and her lips pressed tightly together. She recalled what Galland had said before they reached Maftem.

So it was true. Someone was actually distributing it around.

“Snowfell,” she murmured.
