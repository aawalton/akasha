After some last-minute preparations and a careful shuffling of his inventory, Alexander felt ready to set out. He hoped the other members of the team had taken up his suggestion to pack various-sized socks.

Brian stepped up to him and raised an eyebrow. “I assume you don’t have a backpack because you have a new storage ability?” he asked.

Alexander frowned and looked down at his fanny pack. “It is a gaudy solution, isn’t it?”

“As long as I’m not the one who has to tell you.” Brian chuckled. “I do like the hat change. Just don’t go to any clubs or you’ll get some unwanted advances.”

“What do you mean?” Alexander asked, genuinely confused.

Brian chose not to explain, for his own amusement. “Do me a favor on this trip and let me know if you see anything I don’t, will you? I’m depending on you, and I’m more confident in our safe return with you behind us.”

Alexander hesitantly nodded, feeling the weight of Brian’s expectations.

The team lined up, and Johnny took the lead, stepping confidently into the tree stump. As Alexander waited his turn, he decided to activate his glasses as he concentrated on the gateway, curious whether its nature matched the one that had led to the orcs’ world back at Congo.

A prompt appeared in his vision:

Would you like to attempt to identify the anomaly?

“Yes,” Alexander said aloud.

“Yes, what?” Cassidy asked, standing ahead of him in her ornate red gown.

“Yes, I want to attempt to identify the anomaly,” Alexander replied matter-of-factly.

She raised an eyebrow and looked him up and down, pausing at his waist a little too long for his comfort. After a moment, she turned away, deciding to ignore him.

As Alexander turned his attention back to the portal, that familiar wave of information crashed over him. This time, however, instead of pieces clicking together to provide a clear answer, his mind looped through the same fragments endlessly. They seemed broken and stretched thin—as if a painting on a rubber canvas had been pulled thin across the room, deforming the image so nobody could see what the original was. After a few seconds, the thoughts faded, swept away.

Identification failed. You are unable to comprehend the phenomena before you.

So, not the same as the airstrip portal, he thought. He wondered if Brian had realized that.

When his turn came, Alexander climbed down into the portal. He felt the strange sensation of gravity shifting as he went from upright to crawling on his back, feet first. Watching the paper-mâché prop, he saw it transform into natural wood as he progressed. He cleared his head of the wooden surface and stood to take in his surroundings. Cool, fresh air hit his face, and he breathed it in deeply. The trees were healthy and looked like ash trees but not any variety he was familiar with. The sun was near the horizon, but he wasn’t so sure that night would fall since they had said they had never seen night through the portal.

Forcing himself to relax, he took another deep breath of clean air. It had been far too long since he had done any real activities in nature—though whether this could truly be called nature was questionable. Perhaps the inhabitants of this dimension considered this part of their world deeply unnatural. Something to keep in mind for his new position.

Johnny and Brian conferred briefly before Johnny bent down and let his dog sniff a piece of cloth that appeared to be a feminine pillowcase. The beagle trotted off, snorting and sniffing the ground every few yards. Johnny himself headed in a slightly different direction, eyes fixed closely on the earth.

Brian turned to the group as the last of them cleared the portal, which now stood within a giant knot of a massive tree—no glowing outline, just a dark black hole in a species Alexander did not recognize. Speaking in a hushed voice, Brian gave instructions.

“We need to remain quiet until we are more familiar with our surroundings.”

The others nodded without hesitation. Alexander, however, raised his hand to indicate a question.

Brian approached and whispered, “What is it, Alexander?”

“The portal changed colors,” Alexander whispered back, matching his volume. “I was wondering if it would be prudent to check whether we can return through it from this side.”

“That’s silly,” Cassidy quipped. “Why wouldn’t we be able to get back?”

Brian looked uneasy at the suggestion. He marched to the knot and tried to push his hand through, but it stopped at the entrance as if against a thick sheet of glass.

Judah sighed. “Well, this was totally foreseeable. My bad.”

“How would you have known?” Brian groaned, still probing the knot. “This didn’t happen last time.”

“Fifteen years of playing TTRPGs.”

“TT what?” Cassidy asked.

“Tabletop Role-Playing Games,” Judah explained, nodding thoughtfully. “You didn’t have a quest with a clear objective last time. It would’ve been too easy if you could hop back and forth for safety and provisions.”

“Nerds,” Cassidy scoffed.

“We prefer the title geek,” Judah retorted.

“Okay, that’s enough.”

Judah pushed against the portal—his hand passed right through. “Well, it looks like this world only cares about people with powers.” He sighed.

“Are you complaining because you’re not trapped in another world?” Cassidy asked, crossing her arms.

“No…” Judah replied, unconvincingly.

“We continue the investigation and find a way out once the girl is safe,” Brian said firmly. “The quest implies there’s an exit. If not, someone will likely gain a relevant ability once we level up.”

“Someone’s been reading their player handbook!” Judah said, impressed.

“Let’s go catch up with Johnny,” Brian said, waving them forward as he headed in the direction the tracker had gone.

Alexander had to admit Brian was proving a competent leader—redirecting the team to the task instead of letting them dwell on things beyond their control. Still, most of the others seemed woefully unprepared for the expedition. Their shoes told the tale: everyone except Brian, Johnny, and himself wore brand-new hiking boots provided by special investigations.

A sharp, deep howl from the beagle echoed ahead. A few hundred yards on, Brian waved them forward slowly. They stalked up to find Johnny kneeling over a spot in the grass.

“Good boy, Bubba. She was here, alright,” Johnny said, scratching the dog’s ear and pointing to a dress-shoe print. “Thing is, she wasn’t the only one. Lotsa prints in these… this place. Prints whose owners I don’t particularly care to meet.”

Alexander glanced at one of the prints Johnny likely meant—a large canine track. He watched as Johnny spread his fingers and placed his hand next to the paw print and was aghast as his hand was smaller.

“Assuming proportions are similar, this thing’s shoulders could be the same height as our own. We could be lookin’ at five to six hundred pound canine,” Johnny muttered to the group.

Oddly, the team remained calm despite the implications. They accepted Johnny’s findings readily and pressed on. Alexander thought about his situation and the likelihood that everyone would remain calm and thought back to Emily’s concerns. The implications once again pointed to the idea that the system was influencing their behavior and thoughts.

Following Johnny, it seemed he and the beagle were easily keeping sight of the trail through the woods. They hiked for several hours without incident, breaking at the top of each hour. The lightly wooded area they had started in gradually thickened into dense forest. Something bothered Alexander the whole time—he never settled into the hiking rhythm. A subconscious tug pulled at him, but he couldn’t place it.

After a few miles, as he had predicted, complaints about sore feet began. Alexander smiled inwardly and reached for his moleskin, but Linda from Naturoleum spoke up first.

“I have an oil that should help with that!” She jumped up and pulled out a small cloth bag with dividers holding a plethora of oils. “Eucalyptus oil for blistering and our proprietary joint blend for foot cramping.” She went around, having people remove their boots and applying the oils with a Q-tip.

This did not sit well with Alexander. Blisters built character and taught proper preparation for the future. He held his peace, however, and when Linda reached him, he pointedly stated he was not in need of her services. He decided to quit his internal grumbling as he didn’t want to dampen the mood that the thick canopy was likely already adding to the group.

They continued a few more hours as the light dimmed, though Alexander struggled to tell if it was the sun or the thickening canopy. Just then, Johnny and his dog jogged up to Brian, whispered something, and Brian peered ahead before waving the group forward.

“We’ll go ahead and make camp just up ahead,” Brian announced. “It seems there is an open patch just ahead of us.”

The others sighed in relief—they were clearly unconditioned for such long hikes. The thick roots they now climbed over slowed them considerably. Rounding a tree whose base was the size of Alexander’s apartment and likely near a hundred feet tall, they stepped into a near-perfect circular forest glade. The clearing spanned about fifty yards in diameter, with roots pulled neatly back to create a flat area covered in soft, thick grass. The canopy remained relatively dense overhead.

“Ohh, I don’t like this at all,” Judah whispered.

“Would you rather sleep on the roots?” Brian asked.

“Honestly… I might.”

Alexander looked to see Johnny on hands and knees, smelling the grass. The tracker then pressed the side of his face to the ground to listen before—astonishingly—licking it. Brian watched, bemused; Cassidy grimaced at the sight.

“I don’t know,” Johnny said. “This clearing’s perfect for campin’, and I don’t much care for them trees. I’d right burn ’em off my land if I found ’em.”

“What? Why?” Cassidy asked.

“Dunno, just something about them is agitatin’.”

“That’s foolishness,” Cassidy said. “How on earth can trees be agitating?”

Alexander had to agree with Johnny, though he couldn’t pinpoint why.

Johnny turned to her. “How many birds did you hear on that hike? Seen any squirrels or any other forest creatures? Not sayin’ squirrels even exist here, but somethin’ should be there.”

There it was. Alexander had seen no fauna all day, despite early evidence of it.

As Alexander turned to investigate the circle, he looked up at the canopy. The light breaking through seemed a different shade than before.

“Brian, I’m going to look up there and check the position of the sun.”

“Go for it,” Brian said with a thumbs-up.

Alexander used his sign language for advanced floating disc and elevated himself straight upwards.

He heard Cassidy muttering from below but paid it no attention. Suddenly he saw Cassidy flying up beside him, her face annoyed as she pushed past him. As they neared the canopy, Cassidy stopped. She had difficulty pushing branches out of the way. As he got close, he put a shield above his head and found a thinner point of branches. Effortlessly, he was able to push through with the strength of the floating disc.

As he cleared the canopy, he turned his head to take in the sea of trees spread before him. He saw a dawning sun from the direction they had traveled. Rather than a glowing orange sunset, the light was tinged purple. The stars, defying all logic, appeared strongest near the sun, and on the other side of the horizon the sky was pitch black—as if the stars themselves didn’t want to be associated with the dark. It was unsettling to think they were hiking toward that darkness. If what Judah said was true, that the daylight near the portal never changed, then as they walked away from the sun they were moving toward perpetual night.

Cassidy pushed aside the branch underneath Alexander and flew up to meet him. Then she slammed her face on the bottom of the floating disc.

“OUCH! DAMMIT!” she shouted, holding her forehead. “What! What is this?” she asked, pressing her hands on the invisible surface.

She flew around the disc and stopped when she hovered just above Alexander’s head.

“Floating Disc,” he answered. “I am standing on it.”

“So not true flight?” she asked.

“No, I initially got this spell to lift some furniture.”

“Ha, that seems almost disrespectful to your powers.”

“I don’t think magic cares how it is used.” Alexander pulled out his cell phone and took several photos.

Finally realizing where she was, Cassidy gasped, entranced by her surroundings. Alexander dropped back down to the ground. When he found Brian, he seemed so exhausted that he was barely on his feet. He had just finished setting up a tent that Alexander recognized.

“Temporary Tent?” Alexander asked.

“Oh? You saw this at the Outfitter?”

“I did. It’s a one-man tent, but I had wondered if the effects would work on two people if they fit in there closely enough.”

“I’m not willing to cuddle with you to find out if that’s what you’re asking,” Brian smirked. “What did you see up there?”

Alexander pulled up the photos. “A beautiful if disturbing view.”

Alexander explained his hypothesis with Judah and Johnny getting closer to see the photos. As he talked about the idea that they were marching toward night, Judah yawned and said, “I called it!”

“Called what?” Johnny asked.

“Well, yawn, the land of fey is split into Day, Evening, and Night. Gosh I’m tired. Can I explain after I sleep? I’m having trouble thinking here,” Judah said, rubbing his eyes.

As Judah spoke, a sudden wave of exhaustion struck Alexander as well. Perhaps he was more out of shape than he thought. He pulled out his ground pad and sleeping bag; the others followed his lead. Everyone looked ready for sleep.

“We can take shifts for watch, two people at a time,” Brian said. “Rock-paper-scissors for first watch, or volunteers?”

“I’m good for the first shift, boss,” Johnny offered, still energetic.

Despite his exhaustion, Alexander volunteered too, knowing a full block of sleep would serve him better than a broken-up night.

After settling the watch order, they laid out the night’s equipment and ate lasagna-flavored MREs—adequate, if not the best-tasting.

“Fire or no?” Johnny asked Alexander.

“No,” Alexander replied. “I don’t want to draw attention to our group while they sleep.”

“Agreed,” Johnny nodded.

“Then why offer?”

“If it made you more comfortable. Bubba could smell anything approachin’, and I got good senses as well.”

“I see.”

They sat in silence for a time, gazing into the near-dark woods, before Alexander asked, “What do you think of the day-to-night cycle here?”

“The light changed when we were movin’. Not when more time passed. Weird as it sounds, but that’s the type of place I think we’ve found ourselves. Mushrooms growin’ up to my knees in odd places, grass too perfect for the light, roots look like they grew in a day over the land.”

All true—and all things Alexander should have noticed. It felt like a haze lay over his mind. He yawned as another wave of exhaustion rolled through him.

“You can catch some sleep if you want,” Johnny said. “Bubba and I can count for two. Won’t be long before the next shift neither.”

The beagle lay beside Johnny, tail wagging and thumping the ground at his name. Alexander started to protest, but a dizzy spell nearly toppled him. Both dog and man looked sharp-eyed and alert.

“You know,” Alexander said, “I think I’ll have to take you up on that.”

He settled into his sleeping bag, which felt more comfortable than even his bed at home. Before another thought could form, Alexander fell fast asleep.
