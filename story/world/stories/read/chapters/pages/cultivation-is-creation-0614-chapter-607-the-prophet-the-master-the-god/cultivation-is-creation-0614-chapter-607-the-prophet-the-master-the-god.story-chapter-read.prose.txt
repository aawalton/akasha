During our climb up the stairs, I had imagined over a dozen times how this confrontation would go, in fact, which one of us hadn’t? The first words I had expected to come from the manifestation’s mouth were some cryptic philosophies about dreams and reality. Hell, maybe even a direct offer to join him or die. The typical final boss behaviour type of thing.

What I had never expected was for him to not only know we were not true natives of the Dream World, but to actually know our identities as disciples of the Azure Peak Sect. That brought about a ton of questions about what Shen Mohai understood about the outside world. But more importantly, what the hell did he want from us because from my understanding of webnovels, it couldn’t be anything good?

Clearly, I wasn’t the only one having such thoughts, because I could tell from Wu Kangming’s face, even though it stayed neutral, that he was reaching similar disturbing conclusions about what Shen Mohai knowing our sect's name actually meant for us.

Honestly, neither of us expected the dreamgod to have this knowledge. Surely, if the citizens of the Dream World were aware of the tournament, it would have made sense for the elders to include that in the briefing?

As for Xie Ling and Li Na, they were confused for a completely different reason, to them we were just Hou and Tian, fellow dream cultivators who had joined this expedition, so it must have come as a shock to them that the dreamgod’s manifestation seemed familiar with us.

Hearing Li Na's sharp intake of breath and sensing Xie Ling's spiritual pressure flicker, I wanted to tell the truth that Wu Kangming and I were opponents in a tournament, and that we weren’t here simply on an expedition for a legendary treasure.

But with Shen Mohai standing there, watching us with those mad eyes that seemed to contain infinite dream realms, I knew better than to give up more information about ourselves freely. Wu Kangming seemed to have the same thought, or he had forgotten about them, I couldn’t tell because his attention was fixed on the manifestation before us.

"How do you know about Azure Peak Sect?" he asked directly since he wasn’t the type to beat around the bush. “And how do you know we are its members?”

"Isn't it normal for the prisoner to recognize his warden after fifty thousand years?" Shen Mohai replied as his features shifted to one of amusement. “As the years passed, I was able to sense disciples from your sect enter into this world one after another, but unfortunately with my power being restricted to the palace, I could only resort to subtle manipulations, small nudges to probability and circumstance that accumulate over time to guide them toward this location so that I could finally speak to them.”

I had known all along that the Dream World and countless other inner worlds were under the Azure Peak Sect’s control. I’m sure some were acquired in a noble manner and others not so much, but that wasn't related to anything I had personally done. It didn’t matter which realm that I visited, I had always gone out of my way to treat the people well. Whether that be by helping Du Yanze try to save his world, leaving Mo Yuhang stronger and more capable, or giving Jinghui a purpose beyond revenge. Sure, I might not have succeeded in all of them, but I did my best.

So, I was really hoping that he wasn’t about to blame me and want to take revenge for things that took place thousands of years before I woke up in the Cultivation World. But with the insanity swirling within his eyes, I didn’t have the highest hopes of Shen Mohai being a reasonable person.

"But it always failed,” he continued, disappointment flashing across his face. “Instead of coming here together, the disciples would get distracted and fight each other. One of them would kill the other. As soon as that happened, they would be pulled back to their world. The tournament structure worked against my goals every single time.”

The frustration was clear in his voice, but it amazed me how it passively distorted the palace around him. This…this was true power.

“But this time it failed,” a smile appeared on his face. “I gathered all the power I had for one final attempt, and it worked. You are both here."

"What…what did you do?" I asked, though part of me already dreaded the answer.

Before my very eyes, Shen Mohai’s ageless features melted away, and his elaborate robes transformed into something older and more mystical. White hair flowed down past his shoulders, and he now wore traditional prophet's robes.

Wu Kangming took a step back like he had been struck, and I understand why, the person standing before us was Elder Lu Fazhen – the wandering prophet who had appeared at Tian’s parents' wedding all those years ago.

"I needed to shape your very identity from the moment of your creation," Lu Fazhen said in that raspy voice that had delivered prophecy at the wedding feast. "I needed you to grow up with purpose, with destiny, with a sense that your life had cosmic significance."

His arms spread wide in a theatrical gesture.

"When dream and flame unite as one, a child of destiny shall be born," he recited. "With eyes like sapphires, bright as dawn, who'll lead the world to greet new morn."

I recognised these words. This was the prophecy the main body had seen. It was these words that had defined Tian’s entire childhood by forcing him to carry expectations that no child could possibly bear.

"Two souls shall rise from fate's design, bound by prophecy's ancient line," Lu Fazhen continued, his eyes fixed on Wu Kangming. "In battle fierce where none can flee, only one shall claim victory."

Wu Kangming's breathing had become ragged, and his hand trembled on his sword hilt.

"Through trials dark and perils deep, when ancient powers wake from sleep, the dreamer's son must face his bane, where death alone shall break the chain."

Each line of the prophecy fell like a hammer blow, revealing layer after layer of manipulation stretching back to the very moment of Tian’s creation.

"In him the past and future meet, making the circle whole, complete. The world shall bend, the heavens ring, when rises up the Dreaming King."

Lu Fazhen's smile widened as he delivered the final lines.

"But know this truth, through joy and sting: only one can claim the crown that battle brings."

This was not something anyone could have predicted.

"I chose those words carefully," Lu Fazhen laughed. "They needed to be vague enough to seem genuine, but specific enough to create the right psychological pressure. I needed your parents to raise you as the prophesied Dreaming King. I needed everyone who heard the story to treat you differently, so that every challenge you faced, you would be measured against that prophecy. Your ambitions, your fears, your very sense of self, they were all shaped by those words."

He gestured with one hand, and I felt spiritual energy shift around us.

"But a prophecy alone wasn't enough if there was no miracle to follow it. So, I manipulated probability just enough to prevent your parents from conceiving a child naturally. Nothing that would draw attention to me, just small accidents and coincidences that seemed perfectly natural but ensured no pregnancy would occur. A spiritual fluctuation at the wrong moment, or a minor deviation in your mother's cycle. That was enough to make your parents desperate."

Wu Kangming's spiritual pressure was starting to flicker erratically. This was the first time that I had seen him this rattled. Not even Wu Lihua could do this to him.

"Eventually, your father drove himself into a corner to create the Genesis Dream technique, a technique he thought he created, but it was actually inspired to him by myself," Lu Fazhen finished. "By sacrificing his cultivation base, he would be able to create you from pure dream essence and spiritual will. A crippled father was what you needed to drive you to seek power, to eventually make your way to this palace seeking the Celestial Dream Lotus."

We thought he was done, but that hadn’t been enough, his form shifted again.

The prophet’s robes and mystical bearing melted away into a weathered face, practical clothing, and a plain sword at his hip. Seeing the figure standing before me made me feel like I had been punched deep in the gut, and Wu Kangming made a strangled sound beside me. This…this was Master Jian.

"Hello, my students," the old sword master said in that familiar voice we'd both trusted for years. "It's been quite some time since I saw you."

How…how could this be happening? Master Jian had been real. I was sure of it. The old man had trained us, taught us, traveled with us. He couldn't be Shen Mohai. That didn't make any sense. This must be an illusion. Psychological warfare to break us down. But even as I told myself that, the aura of the old man I had come to respect and love was too familiar, a part of me knew that this was the truth…

"But if only one of you had a reason to come here, that wouldn’t be enough," Master Jian said. “So, I once again manifested outside the palace, created the identity of a wandering swordsman who just happened to find a young boy named Hou Hongyun after his grandmother died. Who just happened to take him in as a student. Who just happened to travel to Moonhaven City years later where another young man named Tian had just been rejected by his people."

The pieces were falling into place, and each piece made me feel more sick.

"I trained you both. I kept you together for five years. I made sure you bonded, became friends, formed a connection strong enough that you became sworn brothers."

“The demon attack, you…you let Hou die,” Wu Kangming's voice came out hoarse.

"I let you believe that Hou died, but I knew the tournament would never actually allow that," Master Jian corrected. "I needed you to experience that feeling of loss where your sworn brother sacrificed himself so that you could live. It made the bond stronger, more meaningful, so that when you were reunited here in this palace, you would work together rather than fighting to the death like every previous pair of disciples."

He spread his hands as if the manipulation was perfectly reasonable. Then Master Jian's form dissolved back into Shen Mohai's ageless appearance, and he turned to face me.

"And you, Hou Hongyun," he smiled. It was a small victory that he didn’t call me Ke Yin, or worse – Kane. "The circumstances that brought you here were also carefully arranged. Do you remember that Nightmare Enforcer? I am sure you do. After all, it is because of it that you had to come here, is it not?"

My hands clenched into fists, knowing I was going to hate whatever he revealed.

"You see, I cannot control those beings directly, at least not while my power is restricted, but I can still influence the currents of nightmare essence that they follow to lead them to particular targets. So, when you and Lu Chenyang entered that cursed realm, I made sure the enforcer would pick up your spiritual signature."

Throughout this entire expedition, I’d been carrying the guilt of Lu Chenyang's injuries, how he was no longer able to dream walk because he chose to sacrifice himself for me, a stranger, so it made my blood boil to hear that all of it had been orchestrated intentionally by someone.

“Yes, it was me,” Shen Mohai declared. “I was the one responsible for every loss, every moment of growth and revelation. I did all this so that you would stand before me today."

Wu Kangming’s spiritual pressure exploded outward, causing cracks to spread through the black stone beneath his feet, and his eyes were wide with a mixture of rage and devastation that I'd never seen from him.

"My…my entire life in this world," he whispered with a shaky voice. "Everything I am… everything I've done. It…it was all you."
