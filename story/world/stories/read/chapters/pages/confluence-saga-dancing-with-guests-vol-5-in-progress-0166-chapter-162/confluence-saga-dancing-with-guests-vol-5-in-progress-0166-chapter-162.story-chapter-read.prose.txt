Adjustable Density Constructs - Available Models

Important considerations prior to working through installation plans.

Adjustable Density Constructs come in three versions, each exponentially more expensive than the prior. Few are ever able to afford any but the simplest model, the most advanced is only known to be installed in the large arenas used for the Progression Invitational.

The simplest model converts a single room into one capable of Variable Energy Density. The larger the room the greater the expense of the model as well as the running cost. Good for testing ability to withstand increased Density as well as for individual or paired training.

The next model covers an entire set of Constructs. Everything physically connected retains the same Density, allowing a set of training rooms or small arena to be adjusted as needed.

The final model provides variable Density within a defined area or areas, allowing for changes without any visible dividers. Due to modular, and borderless, nature of this field, Energy costs are compounded by the definitions of the areas to be affected.

Cara leaned back against the wall as the conversation continued to circle around on itself. Part of her was surprised that Gwen hadn't gotten frustrated and decided to storm off, although if Sandra asked about why Cara had mentioned fairies one more time she suspected that might do the trick.

Nothing about Anya and Seb's family made sense to her. Where was the scheming? Where was the bickering over the smallest detail? Where was the visible deference to those who were more senior and important?

For that matter who was more important?

Clearly Anya and Seb were important, it was their Holding. Sandra was important, she was a princess, the Heir to the Holding that Anya had grown up in, had been Heir to herself until she'd gone off and founded Holding Syl. It wasn't a cadet branch though, it was completely independent.

The ones she couldn't fully place were Fay and Briana. Fay was clearly a fairly Senior Seer. She'd managed to explain more about the Fae than Cara would have expected any Human to know, and made it interesting. The type of knowledge she had, the ability to share it with others, those weren't things you managed by level 15, or even by level 20. Yet she clearly was a family and Holding member, not in charge in any way. Well other than the fact that it sounded as though she had made more than one decision without asking permission first.

Briana was clearly higher level as well, yet equally content to follow. Didn't they care about power for themselves? Authority?

"… fairies are a group within the Fae, Sandra. Cara is part of that group."

She could hear the irritation creeping into Gwen's tone.

"I understand that, but-"

"Sandra," Anya said. "Are you actually asking? Or is this you trying to see if you can get a different answer because you don't see what's funny about the joke."

"I don't get the joke."

"You trained with the guard. If someone like Sam saw you sparring and complimented you-"

"He wouldn't, I don't spar anymore."

"Let's say you were, and he saw you," Anya continued.

Cara thought she caught the hint of a blush on Sandra's cheeks as she nodded, momentarily silent.

"And then he turned to one of the Captains and asked if they were all as good fighters as you were, because he was assuming you were a good example of their talent."

"The captain would correct him very quickly."

"Would you find their frustration at the comparison a bit funny?"

"Probably, but Sam wouldn't-"

"Sandra…" Anya sighed. "This isn't about Sam."

"Then why did you mention him?"

"Because I don't know any of your friends in the Holding who haven't trained."

"Just don't use Sam next time," Sandra said before storming across the field.

"Who's Sam?" Cara asked.

"The younger of the two Engineers who did most of the initial work on our Holding," Seb said. "His aunt is training them both."

"Does she…" Cara hesitated. "Get along with him?"

"They've worked together for a few years now."

"She's said he seems more focused on work. That he's made a point of showing her he knows things she doesn't," Fay said.

"She likes complaining about him when she comes to get coffee," Briana added.

Cara saw Anya and Seb lose focus for a moment, barely a flicker but she'd made a point of trying to catch them when they slipped. The answer was obvious, why were they bothering to discuss it rather than simply stating the obvious.

"You don't think..?" Gwen said.

"Maybe," Anya answered. "He's probably one of the few Awakened she knows who isn't obsessed with her position in the Holding and what her interest would lead to."

"Does it matter?" Seb asked. "She'll figure out how she feels, or doesn't feel."

"Will she have a choice? She is the Heir," Gwen asked.

Cara caught herself tensing at the look on Anya's face as she turned towards Gwen. She trusted Anya, she was certain she wouldn't actually attack, but it didn't stop her from reacting.

"Mother will let her make her own choice," Anya said, her tone cold yet not hostile. "She understood that Sandra wanted to be an Engineer not a warrior, that she wanted to have a birthday party that was for her and her peers not for the nobles. She'll understand that Sandra will make her own choices."

"Apologies, I didn't mean to imply…"

"No," Anya said with a frown. "I over-reacted. You didn't mean anything by it."

Cara shook her head and settled back down against the wall. Holding Syl was definitely a different type of place. They'd been right about bacon though. She hadn't seen Gwen that happy in a long time.

Seb closed his fingers around Anya's as Fay followed Briana and Sandra through the portal back to the Doorkeeper. The visit had been exactly what they needed. They'd spent time with family, they'd eaten bacon, they'd laughed and teased one another. Sandra and Briana knew that Anya was part Fae and had eventually run out of questions to ask about what that meant.

He'd caught the surprised looks Gwen had given Fay a few times during the questions. She'd gone into details he wouldn't have even considered about how similar Fae and Human Awakened cultures were. How outside the Royals and the few groups that were directly beholden to them such as the Fairies and the Warborn, Keldren in other words, someone wandering their Ponds might not even notice a difference. Or rather would notice plenty of small differences but not be able to figure out why.

Gwen had corrected her on a few details, most of which had gotten Cara's attention and even a question as to whether she should be sharing that information. Apparently the Royals were only one of three ruling families, or maybe it was five? Gwen hadn't clarified the exact number or how to recognize the others, simply stating that they existed and that it was unlikely they'd ever meet them.

No one had gone into detail about Names, the Rule of Three, or even Host and Guest rights, except to warn Sandra to be careful not to make any agreements with a fae. Of course Sandra had wanted to know why Briana wasn't given the same warning, only to grumble when Cara chimed in to ask who was more likely to.

They'll be back in six months, he sent.

I know. I'll miss them. It's like when we Dive, I miss them but then after a while it just-

-is so much that we're ready to go back into the Depths, he finished for her.

Is it bad that I'd almost prefer if Sandra didn't visit each time?

"How old is your sister?" Cara asked. "I didn't want to have her get upset at the question."

"Twenty-four," Anya said after a moment.

"She's less excitable than she used to be," Seb said. "Most of the time."

"I didn't mean to imply she was-"

"Immature? Energetic? Exhausting to be around?" Anya said.

He could feel Anya's desire to tease, to provoke, to test and see what exactly Cara would say in response. He could see the tension and worry filling Cara's face as her eyes darted to Gwen and back.

Anya…

"She is all those things," Anya said when Cara started to fidget. "I love her, but I'm glad she's gone home."

"I think she knows it about herself," Gwen said. "She'd storm off and come back looking determined."

"Maybe," Anya acknowledged. "We'll see next visit. So will you if you're still our Guests."

"Speaking of visits," Seb said. "Did you want to see your parents off?"

He knew they'd spent more time with Gwen's family, both at meals and at gatherings that Gwen had refused to speak about. Cara's hair had made it clear how badly those had gone, Gwen's mood sometimes nearly as brittle as her Sworn's hairstyle. The only time they hadn't come back seeming ready to explode had been when they'd met with her father privately. The private meeting with her mother on the other hand had left the pair locked in their room for hours, skipping dinner and barely speaking until after their second cups of coffee.

"No," Gwen said. "Everyone has said what needs to be said. Nothing will change for the better if we speak to them again."

"Are you sure?" Seb asked.

"Yes," she snapped, glaring at him. "Lady Phila has made up her mind. My brother's views are hers it seems."

"I'm sorry," Anya said.

"It's not your fault."

"Her mother tried to force the issue, threatened to impose her will," Cara said.

"Cara!"

"She did."

"It was a family matter, a personal matter."

"I can't help you if she pushes in the future. I can't do anything if she decides. I can't keep you safe from-"

"Cara!"

Seb felt Anya tensing as she focused on the pair, Cara visibly shaking as her words became so soft and jumbled as to be incomprehensible. Gwen seemed to have forgotten they were there, wrapping her arms around Cara and holding her, her answers lost in the sounds of Cara's reaction to the moment.

Any ideas? he sent.

None you haven't also realized aren't right.

They stood there, both turning away to give the pair the semblance of privacy despite being in the open. No one else was around, well not unless Lord Percivus was somehow imperceptible once more.

"Anya? Seb?"

Seb turned at the sound of Gwen's voice.

"Yes?"

"Can we go back to the dorm? Cara's right, we need to talk. I need your help."
