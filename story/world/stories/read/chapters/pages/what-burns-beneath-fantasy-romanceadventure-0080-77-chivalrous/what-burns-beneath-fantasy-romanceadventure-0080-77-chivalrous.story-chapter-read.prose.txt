I whirled, and my fear was immediately swallowed by confusion.

The man before us was clean-shaven, with fair skin, a strong jaw, and piercing blue eyes. Every inch of him shone with pristine silver armor and another white, intricately emblazoned cloak hung draped over one shoulder. A breeze swept the fabric out to one side, ruffling his already-tousled crop of black hair. The very picture of a dashing, gallant knight.

One gauntleted fist was closed over a silver-hilted sword. The blade was half-drawn. This would have been very concerning, except that his handsome face was alight with a sportive, delighted grin.

Renner folded his arms, seemingly entirely unconcerned, and this eased the tension from my shoulders. “Wow.” He snorted, looking the stranger up and down. “Playing hero, Jareth? I’ve never seen you this… shiny.”

Jareth, presumably, belted out a laugh. It rang off the pines, filling the air with mirth. “Hah! This is what real armor looks like, killer.”

I shifted back a step. “Er, friend of yours, Renner?”

“Friend’s being a bit kind. More like a half-wit I happen to know.”

Straight, white teeth flashed in the sunlight. “I think you mean, ‘struck dumb in the presence of!’ Now, c’mon. Draw.” His fingers tapped against the swordhilt.

Renner snorted. “You sure? I’d hate for all that fancy white to get soiled.”

“Broken in, more like.”

He chuckled. “Uh-huh. What do I get when I win?”

“You won’t win.” Deep blue eyes glittered beneath the canopy.

“Always do.”

“Not this time.”

“Every time.”

“You’re not really going to fight, are you? With real swords?” I looked between the two men incredulously. “What if one of you gets hurt?”

Jareth’s broad, gleaming shoulders shook. “Don’t fear, sweet lady. I may look fearsome, but I’m quite capable of being gentle.” He faced me, one mailed hand over his chest, and dipped into a little bow. Then he winked at Teela.

She nearly choked.

The smile turned positively rakish. “With more than one foe at a time, if the occasion calls for it.” This time she received a bow, and the wink was directed at me.

Heat pooled in my cheeks, though I couldn’t quite help but grin. The man’s smile was infectious. “And I thought you were bad.” I nudged Renner, who was chuckling.

Teela started giggling, too. “I thought you holy warriors were supposed to be all… y’know. Chivalrous.”

“And what’s more chivalrous,” the man strode forwards, fully sheathing the sword and dropping one hand to catch hers, “Than boldly acknowledging the presence of such beauty?” Jareth raised her hand to his lips, not quite gracing the skin with a kiss, beaming at her all the while. “Jareth Paltier, your ladyship. Here to serve, to protect, and to be… chivalrous.” He winked again.

Her cheeks flushed pink. “Uh… Heh. Hi.”

He swept to me, next, and gave a similar display. “At your service, your ladysh-”

“Just Brin, please.” I withdrew my hand, feeling quite shy in the face of this brazen charm, and he immediately stepped back with another bow. A moment later my skin prickled beneath a gaze just boring into me from the side, and a flick of my eyes showed that it was a very pleased gaze, indeed. I turned quickly away from Renner, aware that my blush was deepening. “You’re a member of the Order, Jareth?”

“I am indeed.” He dipped his head towards Royce. “At your service, your lordship.”

“Er… sure,” my friend seemed unsure of what to make of the man. Thick blonde brows drew together. “Are you paladin?”

Jareth hesitated, and Renner offered, “He’s a templar. Trained, but not blessed.”

I looked his way. “A templar. Is that what you were?”

“Yeah. Although…” he trailed off, eyes narrowing, then rubbed his jaw. “I could’ve sworn I saw… draw again?”

The black-haired man’s grin flashed once more, and with a low chuckle he unsheathed his sword fully.

I gasped. The blade was a graceful, intricate weaving of gleaming steel and proud silver. Polished, glassy witchwood wrapped through razor-sharp metal. It was a thing of beauty.

Renner whistled. “They’re just giving those away to anyone, huh?”

My brow furrowed at that. His tone was deliberately light, as if he was maintaining the facade of good-natured mockery the two men seemed to bear one another, but there was an edge beneath it. As if he was biting down on anger.

Jareth held the blade aloft, letting it catch and reflect the sun’s light. His blue eyes sparkled, and for a moment he seemed fully captivated by the sight of his own weapon. Then, with a more serious set to his jaw, he murmured, “Times are changing, killer.”

‘Killer?’ I mouthed, nudging the man beside me again.

Renner met my eyes, then looked quickly away. He seemed to think for a moment before, all in one motion, he undid the clasp of his cloak and dropped his pack. “Fine. I’ll play. But,” his eyes narrowed, “I want that if I win.”

The templar snickered. I felt certain he would refuse the suggestion, given how enamored he seemed by the magnificent blade, but he just shot my companion another sly grin. “You won’t win.”

“Awful cocky for a man who’s armor isn’t dented.”

“Maybe no one’s managed to hit it yet.” Jareth’s smile widened. The blade lowered, falling into the shadows beneath our pine canopy, though even in that dim its luster was undiminished. If anything, the witchwood sword seemed to give its own silver glow.

I hurried to give the men space, Teela and Royce at my side, and we turned to watch with wide eyes.

“Uh-huh. Don’t worry, I’ll scuff it up for you.” Metal hissed as gloved hands drew a wicked, dark knife and a familiar broadsword. Unlike his opponent’s, Renner’s blade didn’t look quite so glorious. It was smaller by a good margin, and the dappling sunlight highlighted each nick, scratch, and tarnished edge.

Still sharp, though. And he certainly knows how to use it. I wrapped both arms around my middle, trying to keep my pulse steady. It sounds like they used to do this all the time. So surely there’s no danger. It’s just sparring. They won’t actually hurt each other.

“You’re agreeing to this?” Renner’s eyes narrowed as he began circling the larger man. “Your sword when I win?”

Jareth spread his boots apart, knees bending. One heel dragged back and forth, slowly grinding into the dirt. “You won’t win.”

He paused, looking the templar up and down with a scowl. “Huh. You aren’t usually this cocky.”

Jareth grinned. Both hands settled around the witchwood hilt and brought it to one shoulder, with the blade tilted skyward. A breeze swept his white cloak back, carrying with it the scents of pine and earth.

Renner frowned, then resumed his circling. The movement reminded me of a wolf closing in on its prey. “Usual rules?”

“Sure, killer.”

He gave a little growl. “And if you somehow win? What do you want?”

The black-haired man’s expression became positively devilish. “Oh, nothing much. I’ll just… show you something.”

Brown brows rose. “Gonna start getting chivalrous on me?”

That earned a laugh. “Hah! Nah. Just something I think you’ll like.”

“Doubt it.” Renner paced around, boots silent on the carpet of dirt and fallen pine needles. Every step drifted in, closing in on the other man.

Jareth turned in slow circles. Sword held high, breath even. Waiting as calmly as if Renner was about to hand him a waterskin.

“I don’t think I can watch!” Teela squeaked, clutching at my waist. Her eyes were the size of saucers.

The templar grinned, but he didn’t look our way. “Don’t fret, sweet lady. I’ll be the very picture of restraint.”

Copper eyes rolled. Renner opened his mouth to retort, then lunged instead. The dark sword flashed in and was batted wide with an earsplitting ring. Jareth slammed a plated elbow into the smaller man’s shoulder, earning a grunt and keeping the dagger at bay, then shoved back to create distance. He swung down, still grinning. The other man skipped back as the blade chopped again, then again and again.

Renner danced away from each swing until, when the final one sank just a bit wide, his own sword flashed up to parry. Then he practically stepped into the blades, dagger slicing towards a silver thigh. Jareth freed a hand, caught his wrist, and wrenched the smaller man sidelong. They struggled, clearly off-balance, until Renner jumped away. He resumed circling as the templar fell back into his stance. Both eyed each other warily.

“It’s all about closing the distance,” Royce whispered, watching eagerly. “If Jareth can keep him at range, he’ll win. His sword’s much bigger, and I bet he’s stronger, so as long as he can keep space he’ll-”

“Royce, you sound like you want Renner to lose!” I hissed back.

He shot me a look that clearly said, ‘well, what did you expect?’ and spread his hands wide.

“Not just distance, farmboy,” the drawl sounded entirely unconcerned. “One of us is gonna get tired a lot sooner than the other.”

“That’s what you think,” Jareth shot back, though there was a heavy breath behind the words.

Renner smirked. “Can’t wait for my shiny new sword.”

Jareth started to speak, then grunted and parried as the broadsword sliced in. Just as before, the dagger followed low. He sank to one knee and rammed a shoulder into the smaller man’s chest, knocking him off-balance. Then he straightened into a lunge, silver sword glittering as it rose-

“What do you boys think you’re doing?!” A sharp admonishment split through the keening metal. It held the distinguished air of a mother who’d just found two children misbehaving.

Both men froze. Then, as one, they stepped away from each other.

The blonde Thalessian had climbed up from the lakeside. She stood a short distance downhill, hands on her hips and green eyes blazing. Now that she was closer, her features were even more distinctive. The carefully wound blonde bun was threaded with noble gray, and her eyes and mouth bore little webs of fine lines at each corner.

“Um… fighting?” Jareth supplied. He let the echo settle, then winced and sheathed his sword.

“I can see that! In front of two young ladies, and in the middle of the salted road! When we’re out on a job!” She stormed up the path, eyeing Renner fiercely, hands still on her hips. “And you… without even saying hello, first? Have you forgotten all your manners?”

The broadsword and dagger quickly disappeared. Renner straightened at the woman’s approach. “No.”

Blonde brows shot into her hairline.

Silence crawled between the two. Then, in a more abashed tone than I’d even imagined him capable of, Renner ducked his head and said, “Ma’am.”

Teela burst out laughing. I clapped a hand over my mouth, fighting not to do the same, as he shot us a lethal glare.

Her face smoothed into a warm, though still quite stern, smile. “I’d hoped not. Stars, it’s been a while, hasn’t it?” Without waiting for an answer she stepped forwards with one hand out. He closed his own around it and they pulled each other in, fists to chests, in a quick embrace. “You’ve been missed.”

“Only by some people,” Jareth sniggered.

“Hush, you,” she tutted, examining Renner’s face. Her eyes were a deep, bottomless sea-green, and fixed on him with the air of someone who saw much more than most. “You’re well?”

He gave a crooked smile. “Not bad, Dame. Didn’t expect to see you this far south.”

She snorted, swinging an arm out towards the distant city. “Oh, I’m not ready to be out of the field just yet. Certainly not when there’s breathless roaming a tenday from home. Are you here on a job?”

“Escort mission,” he said smoothly. “Coming to you all, actually. Those two,” he indicated me and Royce, “Are from Fellbrook, bringing news of an attack.”

She gave a low whistle. “In Fellbrook? Stars, is nowhere safe anymore? I’ll want the details of that.”

“We’ll give them to you,” I promised, stepping forwards. “I’m Brin.”

The woman gave me a curt nod, examining my face with an almost unnerving weight. “A pleasure to meet you. I’m Dame Joselia Deron, but you all may address me as you please. I’m here to serve, and was never one for formality. Now,” she brushed her hands together, giving Renner and Jareth a stern look, “If you two are done trying to kill each other, I’m keen to hear details about this attack. And we’re due to report back in a few hours, so may as well get moving. Shall we talk and walk?”

That settled it. Introductions were finished and the six of us continued down the hill. The city grew steadily closer as Royce and I gave details about the night Fellbrook had been attacked. I pointedly left out any mention of the shard, and even more pointedly ignored the glare Renner gave me as I did so. He cut in when my tale began to fumble, filling in my inconvenient blanks with the warnings of a mysterious, deadly ritual about to take place in Sedgewick Vale. This was followed by descriptions of our other harrowing experiences; the fog, the burned inn, Baltha, the escaped pacted, and the breathless woman from the inn.

Jareth listened to it all, offering little beyond the occasional exclamation or oath. Dame Joselia walked in contemplative silence, and once our tale concluded she gave her head a sad shake.

“What a sorry time this is. Innocents on the run, homes destroyed… and I shudder to think there might be some weight to this warning of a ritual. ‘The most dangerous place in the mortal realm,’ you said?” Her gaze cut to me.

I kept mine fixed straight ahead, to the city’s looming wooden gates. Several men and women stood stationed just outside, wearing leathers and holding bows. One of them was hurrying up the path towards us. “Those were his exact words.”

“Anything unusual going on here? We heard there’s breathless.” Renner was scowling at nothing in particular.

Anytime I glanced his way I was met with stony, displeased silence. It wasn’t hard to guess what he was angry about, but I wasn’t mentioning the shard. I wouldn’t use either of these people, nor any of their fellows, as bait.

Joselia swept her attention skyward. “Quite a few, and a missing troop, as well. We only just arrived yesterday.”

“We?” Teela piped in. She was walking behind us, between Jareth and Royce.

“A dozen Thalessians. Quite a few blessed, like myself.” Her features twisted, as though she felt pained. “The Order sent down a troop some time ago. Mostly templars, though they had two paladins to oversee. Sir Fenwick and Dame Avetta. It seems…” she swallowed, giving her head a little shake, then parceled out evenly, “It seems we underestimated the danger. Now there’s two paladins and a dozen fine young templars missing, so we’re here in greater force. Jareth and I set out to ask if anyone on the outskirts noticed anything strange lately, or if they gave the last group leads. We’re hoping to pick up their trail.”

The air seemed to grow colder with every word. “Ashes,” I whispered, looking at her in horror. “You’re saying there’s a bunch of trained warriors just… missing? Disappeared?”

Her lips thinned. “As of now, yes. I’m very keen to find them. And I fear there may be some weight to that warning you heard. Perhaps there is something going on in this city.”

I shuddered, and as we continued the sounds of heavy breathing and boots scuffing along dirt grew louder. One of the gate guards came jogging up. He was a very thin man, with a red nose and large ears.

“Dame, Sir!” He panted, looking between the Thalessians. “Begging your pardon!”

“At ease, sir,” Joselia instructed, handing him her waterskin. “You have news?”

The guard took a long drink. “Aye. A member of the Watch came by this morning, said to alert you on your return. There’s been an attack.”

“That would explain your haste. Details, please.”

The man gulped a deep breath, straightening. “Sedgewick’s temple, my lady. There’s a shrine inside, a monument to the blessed hero.”

My blood ran cold. I stepped back, eyeing the man in fresh dread. Certain of what he’d announce, and praying that attack had been an exaggeration.

‘I don’t kill things needlessly, Brin.’

He continued, “Two of the clerics woke last night and found a monster at the shrine. He tortured them and stole a holy relic.”

My feet dragged backwards. I raised one hand, stifling a pitiful little whimper, and-

“Ladyship.” His voice was so soft. Barely a vibration at my ear. The pressure of his hand at my back was a warning as much as an attempt to steady me.

Don’t give anything away. Breathe.

Joselia’s voice was crisp. “Thank you for bringing the news. Where are these clerics now?”

The man nodded. “Of course, my lady. They’re at the Watch.”

“They’re alive?” I breathed. My lips felt numb.

“Aye. Aye, the husband is in fine shape. But the wife… it sounded like she took the brunt.” He winced, looking gravely between the Thalessians. “You’d better go at once.”
