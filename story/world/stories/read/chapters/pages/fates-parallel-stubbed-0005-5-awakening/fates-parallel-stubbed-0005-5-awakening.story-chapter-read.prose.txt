Lee Jia froze while Takeda nearly choked on her biscuit. In her panic, Lee Jia forgot herself for a moment and stared at Seong’s face in surprise. Luckily, Seong Eunae was staring into her own teacup. Takeda recovered from her coughing and stared at Lee Jia in surprise.

“What!? Lee Jia, you’re unawakened?”

Lee Jia averted her eyes awkwardly and nodded. She glanced up nervously at Seong Eunae.

“How did you know?”

Seong put one finger to her chin and tilted her head slightly.

“Hmm...well, you don’t seem to have any control over the mana in your aura, and I don’t sense any more ki than normal for a half-spirit in your body.”

Lee Jia had no idea what that meant, but picked up on the important part.

“You—you can tell just by looking? Can anyone just figure it out at a glance like that?”

“Not me. I had no idea.”

Seong nodded at Takeda’s interjection.

“Different disciplines have different specialties. Mages are particularly good at sensing the flow of mana, and my mana sense is particularly well developed for a mage of my level. I wouldn’t expect most disciples to notice, at least. Though the instructors will likely see through you right away.”

There went Lee Jia’s plan of keeping a low profile until she could become a mage herself. Lee Jia wanted to know what mana was, but opted for a more pertinent line of questioning.

“Is—is that bad?”

Seong and Takeda had complicated expressions as they considered Lee Jia’s question. Takeda was the first to speak.

“Not...really? In theory at least. Everybody started out unawakened at some point. It’s just…”

“This place was meant to take the most talented of each nation and craft them into a new generation of interdisciplinary elites. Your ability is unconfirmed, and there are those who may take exception to you being freely granted the opportunity they had to work for.”

Lee Jia sighed and grumbled.

“It’s not like I didn’t work for it either.”

“Oh? That reminds me, you never answered my question. How did you end up here?”

Takeda agreed with Seong and they both looked at Lee Jia expectantly.

“Uh, it’s kind of a long story…”

“Yes?”

Seong indicated for Lee Jia to continue. It was obvious that neither of them were going to let Lee Jia go without hearing her story so she relented. Lee Jia described her entire story, though she avoided mentioning An Eui—not wanting to betray her roommate’s confidence, such as it was. By the time she was finished, the tea and biscuits were gone and the sun hung low in the sky.

“Sounds rough. What a crazy plan, though. Stealing from a mage? What if he went after you when he noticed the theft?”

Lee Jia averted her eyes at Takeda’s comment. She hadn’t really thought of that—it hadn't been the most well thought-out plan. Seong was mumbling something to herself with a thoughtful expression.

“Tae In-Su…”

“Do you know him?”

Seong shook her head.

“No, that’s the strange part. I’m familiar with most of the Goryeon disciples. There weren’t very many of them. But I’ve never heard of this Tae In-Su. An administrator for a border city? That’s an unusual candidate—just what is that Grand Magus scheming...?”

Lee Jia stifled a yawn as Seong Eunae continued to mumble to herself. She was curious, but it was getting late and the first lessons were scheduled to begin tomorrow. After she politely excused herself, Seong and Takeda saw her out.

“Bye Jia! It was lovely meeting you. Feel free to visit again sometime!”

“Thanks for helping me out, Lee Jia. I’ll do what I can to repay the favor, ok?”

Lee Jia waved goodbye and bowed politely before beginning to walk back to her dorm. Seong Eunae had not been what she expected. She didn’t have the air of superiority or arrogance that Lee Jia expected from nobles and mages, nor did she seem to look down on Lee Jia for her background. Still, Lee Jia couldn’t help but feel as if there was more to Seong Eunae than she let on—sitting in her presence still felt like facing her own execution.

It was almost entirely dark by the time Lee Jia returned. The fire in the stove had burned down to mere embers and there was no sign of An Eui in the living area. Lee Jia casted a glance at the bedroom door and wondered what An Eui had been doing all day. Had she just been sitting in there sulking? Had she gone out? After a moment’s consideration, Lee Jia decided that the answers weren’t worth a knife to the gut.

After adding the last few remnants of the crate to the stove and stoking the fire, Lee Jia curled up on the couch again. It was more comfortable than most of the places she’d slept before, and with the fire it was even pleasantly warm. Still, as she drifted off to sleep she couldn’t help but hope she’d be able to sleep in an actual bed without fear of impalement soon.

The next morning, after an awkwardly silent breakfast of plain rice, An Eui and Lee Jia left for their first lesson. Lee Jia had been hoping their first teacher would be Hwang Sung, but instead she found herself back in the central courtyard before Elder Qin Zhao, who regarded the crowd with a steely gaze.

“Disciples, you are each and every one of you inadequate. As an elder of the imperial family, I will not lower myself to directly teach those who have yet to prove themselves.”

His speech did not begin on a promising note.

“You have each been provided a slip of jade. Encoded within is the foundation building exercise passed down by my illustrious ancestor and used by the great sects to initiate their disciples on the true path of cultivation. Complete the technique within, and I will deign to accept you as my students. Not a moment before.”

Lee Jia stared down at the engraved stone in consternation. What was she supposed to do with this thing? Was that going to be the entire lesson? ‘Here’s a magic stick, good luck’? Before Lee Jia could wonder further, Elder Qin continued.

“You should understand just what a boon you have been granted. Spiritual cultivation is the only true path to immortality. While one can extend their life by training their mind or body, the true self exists only within the soul. Once the spirit has been strengthened enough, shackles like the mind and body can be discarded entirely. You now have a rare opportunity to follow the same path the great God-Emperor Qin took to do exactly that—you need only the will to take it. There will be no further basic lessons. Come find me when you have completed your foundation.”

Without another word, Elder Qin turned and vanished from the courtyard. The crowd erupted into excited commotion. Lee Jia turned to ask if An Eui could read the engravings, but found that the girl had once again disappeared somewhere.

“Of course…”

Lee Jia sighed and decided to go looking for Seong or Takeda. Perhaps they could help her make sense of the thing. After a few minutes of searching, Lee Jia came up blank, but she did find another promising option. A half-spirit boy, around the same age as her, was sitting at a small portable desk that Lee Jia wasn’t sure how he’d carried.

He was surrounded by books, and seemed to be excitedly writing something down in one of them. Lee Jia was a little bit nervous at the idea of talking to mages, but Seong Eunae had been nice and if it was between that and a foreign stranger—Lee Jia decided to risk it.

“Um, excuse me? What are you writing?”

“One moment...let me just finish this passage…”

The boy didn’t look up as he responded. Up close, Lee Jia got a better look at his features. He had the ears and tail of a dog, with light brown hair and fur. His bespectacled eyes were green, and even his nose was a complicated fusion of a human nose and a dog’s snout, indicating a fairly strong bloodline. After a moment he set his brush down and turned to face Lee Jia, jerking back with a start when he saw her leaning down, with her face next to his.

“Gah! Oh, h-hello. Didn’t notice you approaching, hehe. I guess I got a bit caught up in things. I’m Hyeong Daesung. Nice to meet you Miss…?”

“Lee Jia. Nice to meet you, Hyeong Daesung. So what are you writing?”

“Ah right! Hehe, sorry. I was transcribing the jade slip Elder Qin provided.”

“Why?”

Hyeong scratched his head as he pondered her question.

“Habit, mostly. And I like to add my own notes. Can’t do that with a jade slip, since it just kind of throws all the information at you at once, you know?”

“Not really.”

Lee Jia bit her tongue when she realized what she’d just said.

“You never—wait a minute.”

Hyeong Daesung seemed to focus more closely on her for a moment, his eyes glowing green for a moment. Lee Jia averted her eyes awkwardly and scratched her cheek.

“It’s kind of embarrassing when you stare like that.”

Hyeong’s eyes widened and he quickly turned away, chuckling nervously and blushing.

“M-my apologies Miss Lee, hehe. I just noticed that um, you are—or rather aren’t—”

Lee Jia sighed. Didn’t Seong say that most disciples wouldn’t be able to tell?

“Ancestors damn it. This is why mages are—”

“Sorry! I didn’t mean to pry. It’s fine! I’m not judging or anything. I was just surprised. Actually if anything it’s a good thing. Master’s research suggests that—er, no I probably shouldn’t talk about that.”

Lee Jia frowned at the rambling mage boy. She had no idea what he was going on about, but she decided that at this point it was probably best to just be direct with what she wanted.

“Actually, Mage Hyeong, I wanted to ask for your help with something.”

“Please, no need for honorifics Miss Lee. You can just call me ‘Dae’.”

Lee Jia wasn’t sure she was comfortable with that kind of familiarity. Come to think of it, Seong had insisted on given names too. Were all mages this weird? Lee Jia decided to just go along with it, since she wanted his help.

“Ok, Dae. I was hoping you could tell me what to do with this.”

Lee Jia held up the jade slip. Dae adjusted his glasses and peered at it for a moment before shaking his head.

“I’m afraid that’s useless to you until you’ve awakened. It doesn’t really matter which discipline, but you need some kind of mana, ki, or qi control.”

“Uugh, mana this, ki that. What are you even talking about? It was the same with Seong Eunae. You mages are always so cryptic.”

“Eh!? You’ve met Princess Seong!?”

Lee Jia narrowed her eyes at Dae. She really needed to learn to watch her mouth.

“Pretend you didn’t hear that, please.”

“Uh, r-right, hehe. Anyway, to answer your question, mana is the energy of life. It’s everywhere, and it exists in everything. Qi and Ki are just different words for the same thing, the difference is context. ‘Mana’ is used when speaking of the free-flowing energy found throughout the world, while ‘Qi’ or ‘Ki’ are used to refer to the refined energy contained in the body and soul of a living being. Collectively, they are called Spiritual Energy—though the terms are ultimately interchangeable.”

That was a much bigger answer than Lee Jia had been expecting. And still didn’t really mean anything to her.

“Ok. How can I learn to control it, then?”

“Well, the jade slip has one such method inscribed within. From what I understand of Qin history it should be quite a good one so if you just study it…”

Dae trailed off when he noticed Lee Jia’s flat stare.

“O-oh, right hehe. You can’t.”

“You were transcribing it right? Could you lend me a copy?”

Not that she could read it, but Lee Jia could probably get An Eui to help as long as she wasn’t in one of her moods. Dae shook his head.

“Sorry, my transcription is going to take quite some time, and I had meant to use it myself. Although—hmm, I might have a better idea.”

A loosely bound sheaf of papers appeared in Dae’s hand from thin air. Lee Jia blinked at it as Dae held it out to her.

“Sorry about how shabby it looks, hehe. These are my personal notes on the meditation technique Master Do Hye taught me when I first awakened. The mana here is very dense thanks to the mana gathering formation, so even this should be enough. It should be enough to teach you a basic mana sense and control your aura. Not much, but enough to read a jade slip, I think.”

Lee Jia regarded the papers skeptically. She supposed it was better than nothing. More than she had expected, at least.

“Thank you, Dae. Please tell me if there’s anything I can do to repay you.”

Lee Jia bowed politely as she thanked the young mage. For some reason, Dae blushed and shook his head.

“Nonsense! A-any time! I’m more than happy to assist a fellow peer in the pursuit of scholarly arts, hehe.”

“Uh, right. Well thanks again. I guess I’ll leave you to your writing. See you later, Dae.”

“Of course! Nice meeting you, Miss Lee.”

Lee Jia looked around once more for An Eui, even trying to use her enhanced sense of smell as a half-spirit to track her familiar scent. Unfortunately, with so many people around, she wasn’t able to pick out any trace of An Eui. Lee Jia gave up and decided to just return home and wait for her. A few moments after she left the courtyard, Lee Jia was startled by a familiar voice right next to her.

“Nice moves, charming that poor little mage boy. Couldn’t have done it better myself.”

Lee Jia glared at An Eui.

“You could have done something yourself, instead of just disappearing like that. Actually are you even real? You’re not a figment of my imagination are you?”

An Eui laughed and shook her head as Lee Jia tentatively poked her shoulder.

“Oh, I’m very real. I’m just smart enough not to stick my neck out. Especially when I’ve got a naive little kitten to do it for me. So what did you get? Looks kinda like he just handed you some garbage.”

Lee Jia held the sheaf of papers to her chest defensively.

“Why should I share it with you? I did all the work getting it.”

An Eui rolled her eyes.

“Nice try, but you can’t read it without me.”

“Maybe I’ll get Dae to teach me.”

“‘Dae’? Wow, you really did charm him. Still, I’ll call that bluff all day. If he was going to teach you himself, he would have done that instead of dumping that chicken scratch on you. Now what is it?”

Lee Jia blushed. She hadn’t meant to use the nickname, but she’d gotten used to it during their conversation.

“Fine! It’s his personal notes for awakening or something. I didn’t really get what he was saying, but it’s basically a manual for sensing mana.”

“Aha! And if we can sense mana, then we can control our aura enough to read the jade slips. Nice going, Lee Jia.”

“How do you know that?”

“Eh, this much is common knowledge among—”

An Eui cut herself off, and glared at Lee Jia as if she’d done something wrong.

“Never mind that!”

Lee Jia decided not to pursue that line of conversation any longer, annoyed that she had managed to step on another one of An Eui’s invisible snares--she wished the girl wasn’t so easily offended. Once they were back home, An Eui read through the notes with Lee Jia. The bulk of it was a description of breathing exercises and meditation techniques that would allow one to separate themself mentally from their physical sensations in order to focus on the mana around them.

It also had various side-notes from Dae stressing that everybody could sense mana to some degree. The feeling of being watched, the secure feeling of being close to loved ones, or an uncanny intuition were all signs of an active mana sense.

Once they had read through it all, the pair sat down across from each other and began to meditate. Following the instructions from Dae’s notes, Lee Jia found herself dropping into a meditative state quite easily. She enjoyed the quiet peace as she slowly allowed herself to let go of her worldly concerns and feelings.

Even the physical sensations of the couch below her and the warmth of the fire on her skin seemed to fall away. She could still feel them, but distantly, as though it was part of someone else rather than her own body. In their absence, she felt something else, somehow more real for all it was intangible. She tried to grasp at that feeling, to hold onto and keep it, but it evaded her like an itch you couldn’t quite find the right spot to scratch.

She gave up on chasing that fleeting sensation, instead just relaxing and allowing herself to sink into the serene feeling of the meditation. After what might have been a few moments, hours, or even days, she felt that strange ethereal sensation again. Instead of chasing after it, Lee Jia focused on that familiar intuition that had guided her so often in life.

Without being able to feel her body, she instead had the odd sensation of her awareness floating on nothing. If she focused on that awareness, she felt like she could start to sense whatever it was she was floating on. Like flexing a muscle she’d never known she had, she reached out with that awareness.

All at once, a new sense flooded into Lee Jia. It was as though she’d been born without a nose and had suddenly been granted the sense of smell. Mana was all around her—she could feel the dense cloud swirling around her and another nearby. That must be An Eui, she realized.

Her awareness extended outside of her body, floating on the cloud of mana like a boat on a river. She reveled in the new sensation, allowing herself to drift on the cloud of mana. Idly, she sensed another energy nearby. It felt familiar, but strange. Not mana, yet not something else either. Curiously her awareness reached out towards it and—

...terror, desperation, nowhere to go, she had no choice…

...blood on her hands, bile in her throat, she had no choice, it was everywhere…

...pain, screaming, fire burning on her forehead, it wasn’t fair, she had no choice…

...starvation, desperation, a man alone on the road, she had no choice…

...blood on the knife, blood on her hands, she had no choice, but it got easier…

Lee Jia’s eyes shot open as she gasped for air. She was drenched in sweat. She met An Eui’s eyes and saw the other girl staring wide-eyed back at her with an uncharacteristic expression of shock and fear.

With trembling hands, Lee Jia attempted to wipe the sweat from her face and found it kept coming back. Not sweat, she realized—she was crying. The two girls sat like that in silence for a moment before An Eui was the first to speak.

“What...the fuck was that!?”
