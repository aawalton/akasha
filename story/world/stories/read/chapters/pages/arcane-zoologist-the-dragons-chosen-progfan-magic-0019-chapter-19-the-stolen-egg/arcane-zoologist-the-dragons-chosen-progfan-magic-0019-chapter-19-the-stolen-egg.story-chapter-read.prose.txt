"Do you have some twine? Something that could be used to make a shelter with?" Callen asked the merchant.

He had been sent on an errand by Teacher Blackthorn, who was preparing the necessary materials for the next survival lesson. However, as he stood by the merchant's stand, Callen couldn't help but feel a twinge of envy.

This merchant was a young man, no older than thirty, yet he already had such a wide selection of merchandise. All the sugar and salt he had on display were already worth a fortune, but the merchant definitely had more in stock. He had set up in Greyfen for the whole summer, renting out a room and part of the shed from the seamstress's family, Gerda's mother.

"Of course. How much do you need?" The merchant asked with a pleasant smile, his silver eyes gleaming at the chance of selling his wares.

"One coil should suffice," Callen replied.

"Very well. Wait a moment, I need to bring it from my storage."

The merchant went inside his travelling wagon, which stood right behind the booth, rummaging through it. Once again, Callen was filled with envy. This man was able to travel the world, see new towns and cities, meet various people, and make a lot of money while doing all that.

Callen would love to live a life like that. But alas, that was not something that could ever happen. As a strong young man, he was expected to become a hunter or, at the very least, a guard in the village. His parents were steadily pushing him towards that path, and he had been training with Blackthorn for years to hone his fighting prowess.

But in reality, he didn't want that life. He couldn't imagine the horror in Callie's eyes if he became a hunter. She loved arcane beasts with all her heart, and each morning she would spend playing with her mireling friend.

Callen had been worried at first. What if the monster decided to attack Callie? Burn her with acid as he did with Aurelia and Gerda? He always remained near the coop while she was playing, ready to protect her at a moment's notice.

But nothing had ever happened. The slimy creature appeared… harmless. It went against everything he had been taught, yet he couldn't deny what he was seeing with his own eyes.

"If only I could become a merchant…" Callen muttered. Then he would never have to become a hunter and witness the disappointment in Callie's eyes after he killed an arcane beast.

"That's unexpected. I never thought anyone in the village would be interested in this lifestyle," the merchant said with a smile and offered the coil of twine to Callen.

Callen hastily gave the merchant the coins that Teacher Blackthorn had given him and took the offered coil. "It's nothing more than a passing thought. I don't think it's possible…"

"Why not?" The merchant inquired curiously as he counted the coins.

"Nobody in the village needs a merchant. Not to mention that I couldn't possibly leave my family to travel the world," Callen shook his head.

"That's a shame. It really is quite something travelling from city to city. I was hesitant too at first, and my family objected, but it was better to say that they were furious when I told them of my intentions. Nonetheless, I decided that I needed to carve my own path," the merchant said.

"Your parents objected, and you still pursued this profession?"

"Indeed. It wasn't easy in the beginning, let me tell you. I was selling flowers and firewood, which I had to gather with my own hands. Animals hide from creatures I caught and skinned myself. But each coin I earned, I put towards improving my merchandise. I bought sugar as soon as I could afford it, travelling far south to acquire it."

For a while, Callen listened to the merchant's tales with awe. He described the beautiful cities in the south, the people he'd met there, and the hardships he had gone through. Callen felt… He didn't know what he felt, but the feeling filled his whole being. Perhaps it was a sense of adventure. Or perhaps it was something akin to hope.

Only when one of the elders came to the merchant's stall did Callen realize that he had been chatting with the merchant for far too long.

"Excuse me, sir, but I need to get the twine to my teacher," Callen said hastily.

"I feel like I'm too young to be called sir. Call me Silas," the merchant said and offered his hand for a handshake.

"Silas then. I'm Callen," he said and shook the man's hand.

"A pleasure. Do stop by again. I can always tell you more of my stories," Silas said.

Callen nodded and hurried away to the training grounds. Predictably, he got a scolding from Teacher Blackthorn for being so tardy and was sent to clean up the obstacle course by the swamp. Some of the branches had to be reset in the proper place, and marks cleaned up, as many were covered in mud.

But he didn't even get halfway through the course when he heard a scream from somewhere within the forest. Callen instantly recognized the voice. It was Callie.

Without hesitation, he tossed his task aside and sprinted in the direction of the scream.

"CALLIE!"

What trouble had she gotten into now?!

"CALLIE!"

There was no response, and the forest was eerily silent. Callen's whole being filled with dread. What if the girl had fallen into a mire? Or had encountered an arcane beast of some sort? It would be just in her nature to get too close to a dangerous one just out of curiosity.

Callen's stomach suddenly lurched as an unpleasant thought crossed his mind. What if it was the glauxis? It was confirmed to be prowling somewhere in the area. What if Callie had gone to seek it out?

"CALLIE! Answer me!" He yelled so loudly that his lungs hurt.

"Brother, don't come!" Came a reply from deeper within the forest.

Callen rushed towards the source of her voice at once. Before long, he witnessed the most horrifying scene in his lifetime. Callie, lying on the ground on her back, while a huge, white beast stepped closer to her.

"Don't come closer!" Callie said, but not to the monster, but rather to Callen, her eyes pleading as she looked at him, then at the monster, then back at him.

Callen's whole body instantly filled with adrenaline as he ran at the beast. He didn't have any real weapons with him, only a small pocket knife, which he used for marking the trees on the course. But it was better than nothing. Callen would do anything in his power to protect his little sister.

"GET AWAY FROM HER!" Callen roared.

"No, no! Stop!" Callie screamed.

The beast thankfully turned towards Callen, stopping its advance. He was a bit surprised to see that it had an owl face, but didn't falter. It didn't matter what this monster looked like. He would protect Callie.

Callen ran at the beast, holding the pocket knife firmly in hand, and the beast outstretched its wings, letting out a warning hoot.

"STOP!" Callie screamed, the mark on her arm glowing so bright that it shone through the tunic.

All of a sudden, a few small roots emerged from the ground and wrapped themselves around both Callen's and the monster's legs.

"What?!" Callen exclaimed in shock, nearly falling to the ground from being suddenly constricted like that. The monster folded its wings and looked at the roots at its feet as well.

The glow on Callie's arm subsided, and she fell limply to the ground.

"Callie!" Callen hurriedly broke free from the roots—they weren't very tough—and ran to Callie's side, while looking firmly at the monster.

The monster didn't move. It looked down at the roots entangling its feet and then pulled itself free with ease, just as Callen had. He then looked at Callie.

"Don't you dare to hurt her!" Callen snarled, extending his arm protectively in front of Callie while holding the pocket knife in his other hand.

The monster looked at him, then at Callie, eerily silent. Callen's heart beat so fast, he thought it would break his ribs. It looked at Callie for a long while, its head tilting slightly to the side. Was that… curiosity?

To Callen's surprise, the monster turned and walked away. He blinked a few times, and the next moment, the monster had already disappeared, nothing more than a white speck zooming through the forest.

Callie woke up with a start, nearly headbutting Callen, who was holding her firmly in his embrace.

"Callie, are you alright?" Callen asked nervously.

"I… Where's the glauxis? What happened? You didn't hurt him, did you?" Callie asked.

Callen frowned. "That monster tried to hurt you, and the first thing you ask is whether it is fine?"

"Did you hurt him?!" Callie persisted.

"No, I didn't. It… It just ran away for some reason."

She sighed with relief.

"What were you thinking, Callie? Coming out here to seek out the glauxis. That thing nearly killed Mom!" Callen snarled.

"It only attacked Mom because she attacked him first. You can hardly blame someone for defending themselves," Callie said, trying to get up but feeling strangely drained.

Noticing this, Callen panicked. "Did it hurt you after all?!"

"No, it didn't hurt me. I… I think it's because I used magic. When I saw you two about to attack each other, I wished to stop you both," Callie explained, although she wasn't particularly proud of her magic display.

This time, she had managed to constrain it at the last moment, by imagining the moment she had tripped up Tristan with the root and the moment when the flowers grew near Snowglow. But initially, she had felt her magic swelling with fear and anger, burning hot like fire. Her emotions were so high that she wasn't able to hold them back.

Frankly, she believed that if she had not imagined these things, both her brother and the glauxis would have been burned. It was scary… No, horrifying even that she wasn't able to keep her magic contained.

"Thank the heavens…" Callen sighed with relief. "But seriously! Why do you come out here seeking out the glauxis? Even you should realize that being so close to the village is dangerous. We should tell the adults!"

"NO!" Callie objected. "We can't!"

"What? Why not? Callie, we can't risk it. Somebody could get really hurt!"

"He won't hurt anyone! He only wants to get his egg back."

"Egg?" Callen cocked his head in confusion. "What are you talking about?"

"Yes, somebody stole his egg. That's why the glauxis has been coming near the village. His egg is somewhere here."

"How do you even know that?!" Callen demanded.

"It said so."

Callen rubbed the bridge of his nose. He clearly was finding it hard to believe. "Callie, egg or no egg, that monster is dangerous. Mom is right. It's come too close, we're going to have to call the dragonbloods to deal with it."

"NO!" Callie shrieked. "They're going to take me away!"

Callen flinched back. "What?"

Callie took a deep breath and then explained to Callen what Granny Ethel had told her. That dragonbloods believed that magic was something only the chosen few could have, thus they would likely take Callie away should they discover her magic mark.

Callen listened intently and groaned. "B-But… ARGH! What are we supposed to do? Even if that creature truly only wants its egg, which frankly, I'm not so sure about, how long do you think it'll just remain in the forest? Look at how close it has come. We're just going to keep you hidden at home when the dragonbloods come, and they won't discover a thing. Nobody else in the village knows after all."

"No! What if they can somehow sense if other people have magic?"

"You don't know that!"

"Neither do you. And I don't want the glauxis to be killed. He only wants to get his egg back. It's not fair that his child was stolen. What if I were stolen away? Would you be fine with it?" Callie countered.

Callen's expression twisted with a flurry of emotion, unable to settle on a single one as he pursed his lips, gritted his teeth, furrowed his brows, and eventually exhaled.

"Okay, fine! But you know this only works until somebody else sees and reports it being nearby."

"We just have to find the egg and return to the glauxis before then. Then it will leave!" Callie announced.
