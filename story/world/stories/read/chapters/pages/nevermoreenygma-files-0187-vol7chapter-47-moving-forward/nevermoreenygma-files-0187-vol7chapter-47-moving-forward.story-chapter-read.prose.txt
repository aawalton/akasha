Chapter 47

Moving on

Mai sat lost in thought, staring out the window of her room for some time.

For days she had waited for the New Year, expecting the party in the section and others celebrations to which she had been invited in the following days. The hidden world never missed an excuse to celebrate. But now she was pondering the new situation, feeling the weight of her past actions on her shoulders, as if the shadows of her previous decisions were waiting to be faced again. Was she ready to dive into Wishper, knowing what it entailed again?

The first few nights, she barely slept.

She spent hours reviewing old Inoue Section reports, reading between the lines the names of those who never returned, and wondering if one day she would join that silent list—just a number among the losses, as had happened with Miho.

That month only one deceased agent in the section. And any day she could be the next one. It wasn’t exactly fear. It was more a kind of lucid exhaustion: the awareness of what it means to say “yes” when the rest of the world is still sleeping. It was part of belonging to that hidden world.

But even though it was hidden, it didn’t mean there weren’t bonds and friendships. She had met many people over more than seven years since that first May in 2004 when Miho had found her.

Perhaps her friends and acquaintances would help her make a decision that seemed so ambivalent in her mind.

So in the following days, she spoke with some of her acquaintances.

First with Sakamoto, from the Shinjuku Police forensic lab, who she visited when he was still working. He was the kind of guy with dry humor, always smelling of coffee and disinfectant and with a slight hump due to his poor posture when sitting for so many years in the lab. Mai had turned to him for help on more than one occasion, and despite his gruffness, he was a good man at heart. If she needed a favor, he would do it—provided she invited him to lunch to recount her adventures. He was only prickly with strangers or with his teenage daughter’s boyfriends.

“Wishper, huh?” he said, sealing a blood sample. “They’re the ones who do the work nobody wants to face directly. I heard that sometimes they do mercenary stuff related to the hidden world.”

“Yeah, but someone has to do it. Same with us,” Mai replied, sticking her tongue out. He had offered her a bitter coffee can from the vending machine, which she actually hated.

“And it’s always you, Izumi. You always end up being that ‘someone.’”

She didn’t answer, just shrugged.

“Don’t get me wrong. Honestly, I feel safer with you out there. But it will feel strange not having you around.”

“Thanks,” Mai said, accepting the compliment and drinking the rest of her coffee.

“Just be careful. Tokyo may be big, but working in a group like that will be different.”

“I know.”

“This is your home. Don't forget to come back if you do. This old man will feel lonely,” said Sakamoto, trying to rub his back while putting on an old man's voice.

“Just try to straighten that back and get more rest,” Mai said with a smile.

Next, she went to see Aika in the afternoon, a wolf priestess at a small temple in Ueno, with whom she had worked in in some cases, when appeasement rituals were still part of the emergency protocol. Now they were standard primary protocol when was something related to the spirits. A few years ago, Mai had even helped out at the temple as a priestess at the beginning of the year.

The temple was empty that afternoon, save for Aika in her priestess robe, Mai, and the spirits of the wisdom wolves moving invisibly about the place, unseen by ordinary humans. The temple was dedicated to a study kami, and since it wasn’t exam season, there wasn’t much activity. Aika's house was built in classic Japanese style and was located behind the temple. Only those very close to Aika were invited there.

Aika greeted her with a smile that seemed to know all the answers before hearing the questions. She revealed her wolf ears and tail, hidden by magic, while preparing tea, and they sat near a pond where some koi swam. Mai liked watching them glide carelessly through the water, even in winter.

“It’s been a long time since you came, Mai.”

“I had no reason to, being really busy these months.”

“And now you do?”

Mai stared at the pond, where a fish moved slowly among the yellow leaves.

“Maybe. Wishper wants me to join. I’m not sure it’s a good idea.”

Aika nodded slowly, pouring tea.

“Wishper, huh? Do you want my opinion?”

“That’s why I came.”

“Then I’ll give it to you: you’re not afraid of dying, you’re afraid of standing still. In that sense, you’re like all of us.”

Mai let out a small laugh, bitter but sincere.

“Sounds nice, but not very practical,” Mai said drinking the tea.

“Nothing practical is ever really important,” Aika said with a smile, while some wolves jumped around the pond, playing with the koi as they swam away quickly.

Mai looked at her a little confused, but Aika just smiled.

“If you're interested, a few months ago someone from that group also came here asking if I was interested in working as an informant.”

Mai raised her eyebrows. “What did you say?”

“Well, I already reported it and I'm connected to the Inoue section. It's not like I can just go around informing anyone in case some weird happens.”

Mai sighed, as a wolf floated above her head.

“I'm not sure if I should do it.”

Aika drank her tea. “Whatever your decision is, make sure it's something you decide for yourself. Even if you ask everyone you know, in the end it's all your decision. Or are you trying to do a poll?”

“Something like that. I'm just weighing the pros and cons.”

“Everyone has different opinions. As a famous fey once said, opinions are like butts, everyone has one,” said Aika as she wiggled her tail playfully.

“I am pretty sure was not a fey the one who said that.”

Mai drank the rest of her tea as she watched a koi swimming in circles in the pond.

She also stopped by the Nakano Section office. Everyone was already aware of the news, and opinions were divided. During a lunch of more than thirty people in the Section’s dining hall, the topic of her decision came up. Mai smiled nervously, letting them debate as she ate. Those who were fondest of her preferred she stay in Tokyo to help them.

Old Satoko at reception said absolutely not—she should stay. Jumpei from resources, a slightly chubby fey with a baboon-like face and long hair, said she should be careful wherever she was sent. Kitahara, technically from the Section’s foreign affairs, said that while he didn’t like the idea, it could help build more connections and perhaps, in a few years, she could return as a trainer when the Section expanded.

Everyone had an opinion. Jeon, from Korea liaison, Asuka from the Anomalies office, Momoka from the registry office, and many more.

Mai sighed, smiling as she looked at them all. In one way or another, she really knew something about each of them, and they knew about her. Beyond the dark nature of the hidden world, there were genuinely intertwined bonds here too. Just slightly different realities.

Since she was in Nakano, she once again visited her father’s grave. It was clean once more.

Aya had surely been there again. She would have to visit her.

Over the years, she had gradually met and formed a close friendship with Aya.

Thanks to those meetings, she had learned more about her parents’ lives, while Mai also had to invent a few stories about herself. She didn’t like lying to the poor woman, but she couldn’t tell her she was the daughter of Quincy and Hisui Izumi. After all, that feeling of menace, that she should never say it, was always there, like an invisible hand around her neck.

Aya was already older, with an adult family. Yet, in the times they had met, they had enjoyed their conversations, and soon Mai would share her own stories as well. The actual ones at least. Aya was aware of the hidden world, after all. Still, there were things she had to keep from her, like the truth about what had happened in the Shinjuku disaster, or the time she had been away from Tokyo working with Wishper in the north.

She went to Shibuya Cemetery and visited Miho's grave to talk for a while. She knew that if Miho was right about souls, she wouldn't be able to hear her, but she wanted to do it anyway. She went to visit her every two weeks, and it always seemed that the people who had known her hadn't forgotten her either. A beer was always in the tomb. Surely, if Miho's spirit were still around, she would have found some way to empty those cans of beer that she liked so much.

She went to Roppongi and Kabukicho to visit the looser, more fluid communities of the hidden world in Tokyo. Among gangsters, and feys who worked or used their abilities in hostess clubs, she sought advice. On more than a dozen occasions, she had saved many of the people there.

It was a community of hustlers, petty thieves, tricksters, and seducers of both sexes, straddling the line between the ordinary world and the hidden one. To navigate the hidden world, knowing those gray areas that formed networks of information was also essential.

In Mai’s case, many in that network would have come to her aid without hesitation. Cosplaying feys with real cat tails, using their charms to draw customers to bars. Fey men doing the same in host clubs for women. Casinos where the dice would become magical if the house lost too much money. These, and countless others, were just examples of the nocturnal world Tokyo submerged itself in after the sun went down.

The following days were a succession of conversations that seemed to go nowhere and yet, in some way, brought her closer to something. Each word, each gesture, seemed to accumulate in her mind like layers of dust on a decision forming on its own.

The next week, the new envoys from Wishper arrived from Kyoto.

A fey from Okinawa, small and slender, with almost white hair and deeply tanned skin, speaking in a soft tone but with a gaze that could cut steel.

And two men with strong Kansai accents, experts in tracking and espionage. Efficient, trained people, but with a certain charm they used in their investigations.

Mai observed them from a distance, while the Inoue technicians welcomed them and handed over their credentials. She felt out of place, as if the city she had sworn to protect were silently seeing her off already.

Yet, something in that scene reassured her. It wasn’t the end of anything, she thought. Just a change of scenery.

She could still visit Tokyo between missions, go to the temples, visit her father’s and Miho graves and see Aya, have coffee with Sakamoto, speak with Aika, see friends and rogues. Nothing tied her down, and at the same time, everything waited for her.

“Just let it flow, huh?” Mai thought.

That night at home, as she stored files on her portable drive and checked her recent cases once more, she realized that the reasons to stay were the same as the reasons to leave: routine, friendship, and an irredeemable need to also see the world outside the bubble in Tokyo.

Finally, after a month of reflection and preparations, Mai made a decision.

It was time to move and leave behind what had been and embrace what could be.

She resolved the matter of her house for the time being—she wasn’t planning to sell it at all. Fuck that! She loved that house and had the money and could keep paying taxes on it and the other one. She had saved a fair amount recently, courtesy of anomalies called Dark Events and other paranormal problems she had solved that the Section had paid for. The shikigami wolves would feel a little lonely, but Aika would always come by to visit.

She fixed a few extra issues and left everything ready.

So, on a cold winter afternoon in early February, she packed a few belongings, checked her equipment, and said goodbye to her life in Tokyo, at least for a while.

It was going to be the test. Who knew? There was a chance she might not like it at all and next week she'd be back drinking tea with Aika or Aya again.

The next morning, Mai took the Shinkansen and later arrived at the Wishper team headquarters in Kyoto.

Jinat greeted her in the middle of the operations room. The sound of the sliding doors opening made her look up, and when their eyes met, Mai saw in them a reflection of her own feelings: a mix of hope and determination.

“I’ve decided,” Mai said, her voice firm.

Jinat nodded with a smile. “I knew you would.” Jinat looked her in the eyes. “That’s all we need. Welcome to the team, Mai.”

Mai didn’t know exactly what the future held, nor what sacrifices she would have to make along the way. But one thing was clear: what had started as a personal struggle to find her place can now become something much bigger. And she would be ready to face it, alongside the Wishper team, hoping that, perhaps, in the end, they could make a difference.

Thus, Mai officially joined the Wishper team.

But first things first. She would have to prove for a while that she could work there in a team.
