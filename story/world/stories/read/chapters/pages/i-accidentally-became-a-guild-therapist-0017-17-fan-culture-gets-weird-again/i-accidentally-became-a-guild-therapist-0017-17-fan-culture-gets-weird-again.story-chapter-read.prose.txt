I ACCIDENTALLY BECAME A GUILD THERAPIST

Chapter 17 – Fan Culture Gets Weird (Again)

ARC II: THE WORLD OUTSIDE THE CAMP

SYNOPSIS: The subreddit has hit 100,000 subscribers. There is now an official appointment system, a merchandise store Livia did not authorize, a thread accurately psychoanalyzing every guild member, and one comment that disappears before Livia can finish reading it. The guild has opinions about all of this. Livia has opinions too. Some of them are about the comment.

The subreddit hit 100,000 subscribers on a Tuesday, which Livia discovered not through any notification she had configured but through Alaric, who was waiting at her door at 7am with the expression of a man who had found a betting opportunity and a news item simultaneously.

“One hundred thousand,” he said, without preamble..

“Good morning,” Livia said..

“The mods are running a subscriber celebration event. There’s a poll. Fastest growing emotional support community on the server. There’s a trophy. I bet on whether you’d accept it.”

“What did you bet?”

“That you’d look at the trophy like it personally wronged you.”

She looked at him.

“Current odds on that outcome?”

“Three to one.”

I am going to look at that trophy like it personally wronged me.

I am going to do it on purpose now.

Alaric cannot profit from my authentic reactions.

[Achievement Unlocked: Your Emotional Responses Are Now a Betting Market. +0 EXP. +10 Notoriety Points. The system notes this was inevitable.]

The subscriber celebration event had, it emerged, come with several features that the subreddit moderators had developed without consulting Livia, which was in keeping with how the subreddit operated generally.

The first feature was an official appointment request system.

Players could now submit structured session requests through the subreddit’s dedicated portal, which auto-sorted them by category: Existential Dread, Relationship Difficulty, Gambling-Adjacent (a specific sub-category that had been added after Alaric’s sessions went viral), Combat Burnout, Grief (General), Grief (Guild-Related), and a catch-all field labeled Other which currently contained 847 entries.

Livia read through the category list twice..

They built a taxonomy for my work.

Without asking me.

The taxonomy is accurate.

That is somehow worse.

The second feature was a merchandise store.

Livia found this one by accident, after a player at the Nexus Bazaar approached her to say he had bought the shirt and was she going to sign it. The shirt, which he showed her, read: "Breathe in for four." — Livia (Probably) on the front and "Not your NPC. Not your therapist. Not your waifu. (Okay maybe your therapist.)" on the back.

She stared at it for a long time.

Not your NPC.

Not your therapist.

Not your waifu.

Okay maybe your therapist.

Someone wrote that about me.

Someone printed it on a shirt.

He paid actual gold for it.

He wants me to sign it.

“I’ll sign it,” she said, because the shirt was technically correct and the man had clearly paid for it in good faith..

[Achievement Unlocked: Signed Your Own Merchandise (Unauthorized). +5 Brand Equity Points.]

[The system suggests consulting legal. The system acknowledges there is no legal in this game. The system suggests consulting Miles instead. The system acknowledges this will be worse.]

The third development was the thread.

Not a single thread. A thread that had been pinned to the top of the subreddit for three weeks and had accumulated 4,200 comments, titled: "Deep Dive: B&B Guild Member Psychological Profiles Based on Available Footage — Thesis-Length Analysis (Updated)" by a user called u/StructuralEmpathist.

Livia opened it expecting embarrassment.

What she found was considerably more unsettling: it was good.

The analysis of Bron identified his core dynamic as the fear of irrelevance in an existence defined by invulnerability, which was accurate. The section on Phina described her relationship with Glimmer as an object of secure attachment in a life history defined by abandonment, which was also accurate. Nyx was characterized as avoidant attachment presentation with specific environmental phobia— a combination that produces high functional competence and low relational reciprocity, currently in a transitional phase, which was so accurate it was uncomfortable to read.

The section on Miles was three paragraphs of “insufficient data but probable betrayal history” which was, Livia thought, the most accurate possible conclusion given what anyone outside the guild could observe.

The section on Livia herself was titled "The Therapist: A Special Case" and read, in its entirety:

Standard analysis does not apply. Livia does not fit any established NPC behavioral framework. She responds to stimuli non-procedurally, demonstrates genuine affect, and shows evidence of internal state continuity between sessions. She is either the most sophisticated emergent AI ever written or she is something the game’s taxonomy has no category for. I have been thinking about this for six weeks. I genuinely do not know. I am going to keep watching.

She is either the most sophisticated emergent AI ever written or she is something the game’s taxonomy has no category for.

Six weeks.

They’ve been thinking about this for six weeks.

Livia closed the thread.

Opened it again.

Read it one more time.

Closed it.

Filed. Somewhere I am not going to look at immediately.

The guild ran the official appointment day with the organized chaos of a guild that had, despite everything, functional logistics when it wanted to.

Miles had approved a single scheduled day rather than open-ended ongoing access. Livia had set her own session parameters. The subreddit mods had built a queue system. Alaric had, without being asked, designed a waiting area management protocol that also happened to double as a betting opportunity but was otherwise genuinely functional.

Twelve sessions. All booked. Mix of guild members from other guilds, a handful of players who had traveled specifically, and two NPCs whose presence in the queue required a brief system clarification that resolved itself when the system noted they had submitted requests through the official portal and were therefore eligible.

NPCs used my official portal.

The system had to think about it.

The system decided yes.

I am going to come back to what that means later.

The sessions were varied. A tank from a mid-tier guild who had not been able to sleep since a raid wipe six months ago and had not told anyone. A healer who was afraid she was too slow and had been taking combat stimulants without disclosing it to her party. Three people with versions of the same thing: they had been here for a long time, they were still here, and they did not know how to want things for themselves anymore rather than for their guild standing.

Session nine was a player who had driven four hours in real-world terms to reach this server. He sat down, said nothing for three minutes, and then said: “My daughter watched your ASMR. She’s eight. She has anxiety. It helped.”

Livia did not say anything for a moment..

“Is she okay?”

“Better,” he said. “She’s better. I just—” He stopped. “I just wanted to tell someone.”

Same as Request #12, Livia thought. Just wanted to tell someone.

There is no quota for that.

She gave him thirty minutes. He did not need therapy. He needed someone to hear that his daughter was better..

He left with the expression of someone who had set something down that they had been carrying without noticing..

[You held space for someone who needed to be heard, not fixed. +400 EXP. The system notes: this is also the job. Sometimes it is the most important part of the job.]

After the last session, Livia sat in the empty appointment room and opened the subreddit to write a brief thank-you post for the appointment day. Standard practice. The mods had asked for it.

The subreddit was active, as it always was after a major event. New posts, comment threads, reaction clips from players who had been in the waiting area. Someone had compiled a highlights reel. Someone else had already written a follow-up analysis to the existing psychological profile thread.

She scrolled through it in the particular way of someone who was very tired and not quite processing everything she was reading..

And then she saw it.

In the comments of the profile thread. Not near the top—buried about two hundred comments down, posted four hours ago by an account with no post history and a username that was a random string of characters. The comment read:

Has anyone noticed she doesn’t have a player ID? She’s not in the NPC registry either. She’s been checked. She doesn’t match any known entity classification in this server’s taxonomy. I’m not saying this to be alarming. I’m saying this because I think she should know that people outside this server have noticed. Some of them are not friendly.

She read it once.

She refreshed the page to find the full account username.

The comment was gone.

Not deleted with a mod note. Gone. As if it had never been posted. The comment thread numbered the same as before she had refreshed, and the surrounding comments were on a different topic, as if there had never been a gap.

That was four seconds.

The same deletion speed as the quest board message.

The same thing that deleted T.S.’s message deleted this one.

Livia sat very still.

She wrote in her notepad, quickly, before the memory faded:

Comment: no player ID. Not in NPC registry. Checked. Doesn’t match known entity classification. People outside this server have noticed. Some of them are not friendly. — Account: random string, no history. Gone in under four seconds. Same deletion speed as T.S. message.

Connection: T.S. said “some people are looking for you.” This comment said “some of them are not friendly.”

I don’t know if T.S. is one of the friendly ones.

She looked at the notepad for a moment. Then she closed it.

From outside the appointment room, she could hear the guild returning from whatever they had occupied themselves with during her sessions—Bron’s armor, Alaric’s voice, Phina’s commentary, the occasional flash of pink light from Nyx’s flashlight under the door gap. Normal sounds. Guild sounds.

I am going to go sit with them.

I am going to eat dinner and not think about this until tomorrow.

Tomorrow I will think about it.

Tonight, I am going to eat dinner.

[Mental Supporter Lv. 7: 2,100 / 7,000 EXP.]

[The system notes: you have had a long day. The system suggests rest. The system is aware you will instead stay up writing notes. The system respects this but does not endorse it.]

She did not stay up writing notes.

She went to dinner. She ate. Bron described the afternoon’s entirely optional sparring session in detail, which was lengthy but grounding. Phina had updated Glimmer’s jiggle chart. Alaric had not lost any bets today, which he considered a personal tragedy and discussed at length. Nyx ate without participating in the conversation but in the manner of someone who was present rather than absent, which was different from how he had been three months ago.

At some point Miles appeared at the end of the table, sat down, and poured himself coffee without explaining where he had been.

After a while, when the table had gotten noisier and nobody was paying specific attention to either of them, he said quietly: “How were the sessions?”

“Good,” Livia said. “Twelve people. One of them came to tell me his daughter was better.”

Miles nodded.

“And the subreddit?”

“Complicated,” she said..

He looked at her. Not the evaluating look. The other one, the one she had started to recognize as genuine attention without agenda..

“Anything I should know about?”

Yes, Livia thought. There is a comment that was deleted in four seconds by something that is also deleting messages to me from someone named T.S. who says people outside this server have noticed me and some of them are not friendly and I do not know what I am in this game’s taxonomy and I am very tired.

She looked at her coffee..

“Not tonight,” she said..

A beat. He did not push..

“Okay,” he said..

They sat in the noise of the guild for a while. It was not uncomfortable.

Not tonight.

But soon.

I am going to have to tell him soon.

Filed.
