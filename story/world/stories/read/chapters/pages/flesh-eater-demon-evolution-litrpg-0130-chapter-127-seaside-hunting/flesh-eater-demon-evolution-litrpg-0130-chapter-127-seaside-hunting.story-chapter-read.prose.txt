The night air was crisp and cool. Pyre strode idly along the water’s edge, taking in the bright moonlight reflecting off the waves. She was careful to remain around the rockier areas, lest her weight cause her to sink too far into the sand. The demon’s grey eyes scanned the sea, but she couldn’t detect any movement beyond the churning tides.

After Lord Alardice, Agnes, and Henry had left, they’d studied the poorly drawn map of the Rotham estate for a bit before the weariness of the day had slammed into the human members (“member,” more accurately) of their group full force. While Daphne had insisted that she was fine, she’d begrudgingly agreed to go to bed and continue their planning the next day after Callan had admitted that he’d also like a break.

Now, the noble was asleep on the second floor and Callan had also gone to his own room, probably to read books or meditate or whatever else ghosts did to recharge.

With several hours left to kill until morning, Pyre had exited the manor and left for the shore. Now, she peered up at the silver moon, listening to the sound of the water. She felt the vibrations shift in the ground below, the sensation a bit dulled from the loose sand. She turned to see Llyr striding forward and arched a brow at him.

“Back to stare at the water?” she joked. He smiled.

“You seem to be doing the same thing.”

“I’m looking for fish.” The demon paused. “Other than you, I guess.”

Now that they had an actual plan and direction, she’d been studying her current in-progress subtraits and decided that it wouldn’t hurt to get a few more [Aquatic Adaptation] shards. She didn’t think she’d ever be very good in the water, considering her true form’s size and her density in general, but she’d rather not instantly die if their fight against the cult led them by the sea. She only needed 6 more shards.

She said as much to Llyr, albeit a highly abbreviated version, but he seemed to understand. “Anyway,” she finished, “if you know of any kelpie pods or whatever, that’d be nice.”

While it would be very convenient if she could just devour any old fish and get the shards, she suspected this was similar to [Volant] in the sense that she’d only get the subtrait from specific types of creatures. That cave fish back in the Lightning Pass certainly hadn’t had [Aquatic Adaptation].

Llyr raised an eyebrow. “You seem very confident that I would know where they are,” he said dryly. He ended up proving her assumption right, though, because he then turned to the waters and hummed consideringly. “Kelpies are usually found near the ocean floor. They only come to shore when they’re hunting or in special circumstances, like the ichor quake.”

It figured. Pyre strode forward, close enough for the spray of the water to hit her, and squinted down at the churning waves. Yeah, jumping in seemed like a bad idea.

“If you’d like,” Llyr said, amused, “I can try and chase a few closer to the shore. I need to hunt myself—I’ve neglected my leveling recently.”

“Very generous,” the demon drawled. “But that’d be nice, yeah.”

“Alright, please give me a moment.”

Llyr carefully removed his outer cloak and set it down, weighing the cloth down with a rock so it didn’t blow away in the wind. Now that Pyre thought about it, she’d never actually seen the siren’s full monster form. She’d gotten a brief glimpse during the ambush, but that had only been partial and obscured by the light pillar.

Llyr seemed to sense the demon’s eyes on him, because he glanced back with a raised eyebrow. “Is there something wrong?”

“Just realized I’ve never seen your true form.”

“Ah.” Llyr chuckled and waded into the ocean, unbothered by the waves crashing against him. “I’m sorry to disappoint you, but this will not be my full form, either.”

Before Pyre had a chance to ask what the hell that was supposed to mean, the siren suddenly dove fully into the waters. In the brief second before he hit the waves, blue light illuminated the area. She caught a glimpse of his lower body transforming into a long, silvery-blue fish tail before he’d vanished into the depths.

The demon found a particularly large boulder and settled down on it while she waited for the siren to return. Based on her vague Earth memories, which apparently had their own legends about merpeople, that looked about what she’d expect. Maybe his “true form” was actually just a really big fish, no human section at all. It was a funny thought.

Several minutes passed before Pyre detected movement again. Further out in the sea, the pattern of waves broke. If she focused, she could see two dark shadows swimming rapidly below the surface, one long and slim and chasing the other, which moved up and down almost in a galloping motion.

Pyre stood just as the kelpie broke through the surface. She spared a brief glance at her surroundings through her back eye, and once she’d confirmed that no one was around, she rolled up her coat sleeve, took one step forward, and warped her right arm.

A web of flesh coiled around the horse-like creature before it could make it further than a step forward. It released a low guttural sound and attempted to disentangle itself, but it couldn’t move.

Not missing a beat, Pyre grew a mouth in the center of the flesh mass and bit straight through the kelpie’s chest to its heart.

Same shards as last time, Pyre noted. She released her coiled flesh and let the limp body sink back below the waves.

Llyr was watching from a few feet away, and while he was mostly submerged, she could very clearly see the fish tail behind him. It was quite long, scales shifting between blue and silver depending on the angle, and fanned out in wide, sweeping tail fins at the end. He gave the demon an amused look.

“Was that what you were looking for?”

“Yep.” She pronounced the ‘p’ with a pop. “Don’t suppose you could send, like, five more of those this way.” She waved casually. “Doesn’t even need to be kelpies. Any ocean monster above level 10’ll probably be fine.”

Llyr chuckled. “I’ll see what I can do.”

They fell into a rhythm after that. The siren herded sea creatures close to the shore, where Pyre would promptly devour them. It wasn’t as quick as that first kelpie had been, since Llyr had to travel further out each time to find new victims, but the whole process went pretty smoothly, if the demon did say so herself.

In the next few hours, she devoured a wide assortment of sea creatures, some which had the [Aquatic Adaptation] shard and some that didn’t. She hadn’t even known that this many kinds of ocean-monsters existed, to be frank. Some of them looked pretty damn weird—not that she had any room to judge.

At that point, she’d gained another 4 [Aquatic Adaptation] subtrait shards, 1 more [Adhesive Skin] shard, 3 [Scales] shards, 2 more [Scent Tracking] ones, and an [Elemental Resistance] shard to boot. Plus a decent amount of experience.

Honestly, this was probably one of her most efficient leveling sessions to date. She just needed 1 more [Aquatic Adaptation] shard, and she’d finally get the subtrait.

“Anything else?” she called out. Llyr popped his head out from below the water, long dark hair floating around him. His earrings had stayed miraculously on this whole time, which Pyre chalked up to some sort of magic shit. A full thirty minutes had passed without any new creatures appearing.

“I suspect every semi-intelligent being in the general vicinity is aware of what’s happening,” Llyr said wryly. “Most have fled by now.”

Pyre whistled. “Damn, you must be scaring the shit out of them down there. Couldn’t be a little more stealthy?”

“I do not think you should be the one lecturing me on stealth,” the siren remarked with no real bite. His eyes did narrow slightly as he thought, considering. “I suppose if you’re willing to travel further north, we may find more creatures there. The ocean depth increases rather dramatically once we leave this area.”

Pyre turned the direction in question. The shore did indeed grow rockier up north, and the ground rose in sharp, steep cliffs, leaving only narrow paths close to the water’s edge. She thought she could see various cave entrances as well, and the ocean looked a bit darker there. She turned to Llyr again.

“Sure, why not?” She shrugged. “Not like I’ve got anything better to do.” There were still a few hours before sunrise, anyway.

Llyr nodded. He flicked his tail in a gesture to follow, then promptly dove below the waters. He was visible only as a shadow beneath the surface swimming parallel to the shore. Pyre turned and strode after him.

In addition to the ground becoming rockier, the waves were also more violent, Pyre noted. She stepped a foot down, testing how firm the boulder below her was before continuing forward with her full weight.

Llyr had continued at a steady pace, occasionally slowing to give the demon time to catch up. She had to be more careful here, lest she crack a rock and fall in or something, but so far the ground had remained impressively sturdy.

Pyre glanced back briefly. They’d traveled far enough that the mansion was a dot in the distance, and the angel titan above Serindell was no more than a silvery blur in the rough shape of its wings. She imagined not many humans from the city ventured this far north. The waves and sharp rocks were probably a recipe for drowning.

“This should be far enough,” Llyr said, popping his head out again. Pyre turned to study the area.

Yep, the waves were definitely more violent, and while it was hard to make out at night, the sea was also darker. If she focused, she thought she could detect a particularly dark, almost circular shadow further in, too. There was probably a divot in the seafloor there or something.

“Okay, go wild.” Pyre settled back, leaning casually against the steep cliff behind her. She was currently standing on a narrow path sitting just an inch above the waves. Rocks jutted out from the ocean’s surface all around. It probably wasn’t the most ideal place for a fight, but she figured with her fleshwarping, she could grab onto a boulder if she slipped.

Llyr snorted, but he did, in fact, dive. Pyre was pretty sure he’d been hunting himself in-between herding stuff towards her. That was probably the real reason everything was avoiding them, actually. Definitely wasn’t her fault.

Llyr didn’t return for half an hour. He likely had to swim further out since most creatures wouldn’t be hanging around this rocky area. Pyre was vaguely curious to see what kind of monster would appear next. The big jellyfish after the first kelpie had been cool.

She got her answer five minutes later. Far out, a shadow appeared beneath the surface and began moving towards the shore. She watched as it approached, the size of the shadow increasing rapidly the closer it got. It just kept growing larger and larger. And larger.

A deep rumble sounded from below the depths. Pyre warped her arm, splitting the amorphous flesh into separate appendages that she used to grab onto nearby rocks just as the surface of the ocean split apart.

From beneath the waves, massive curling tentacles thrashed violently, sending saltwater spraying everywhere. One tentacle slammed into the cliffside further north, and a loud crack reverberated throughout the area. Fallen rocks crashed into the sea, further disturbing the waters.

Pyre spun around to face the ocean again, and she had a vague thought that maybe she should’ve thought this through a little better. In her defense, she really hadn’t expected Llyr to herd a giant octopus-thing over.

Speaking of Llyr, the surface broke closer to her. She saw the siren’s familiar face appear just as another tentacle swept out, leveling several of the boulders jutting out from the water.

“You couldn’t find anything smaller?” she called out. Between the crashing waves and the whipping wind, she had to raise her voice to be heard. Llyr’s lips thinned.

“It interrupted me.” He sounded vaguely annoyed. “It seems it was hibernating in the sinkhole.”

One tentacle barrelled towards Pyre. She promptly converted her arm into a bone shield. The appendage slammed into it, and she heard another crack as more nearby rocks broke from the force of the impact.

Her bone plate held strong, the blow glancing off of her, but at this point she was more concerned about falling into the water than about whether or not she could beat this thing. The latter wasn’t really in question. It was, however, a little hard to devour something when you couldn’t see or reach its main body.

Llyr seemed to realize this as well, because he muttered something under his breath. Blue light glowed from the center of his palms. With a swift sweeping gesture, the light burst forth, shooting out across the ocean’s surface. In its wake, the waves trembled, and Llyr slowly moved his still-glowing hands. Pyre watched as the water began to split apart.

Massive waves shifted away, instead climbing into churning peaks that were suspended by the force of the magic. Where the displaced water had once been was now a distinctly shallower area just in front of Pyre. Within that divot, the demon could now clearly see the main body of the creature.

It was not, as she’d first thought, a giant octopus. Not entirely, anyway. The tentacles certainly were, but where she’d expected a bulbous, squishy head was instead a wedge-like shape complete with a wide mouth filled to the brim with razor-sharp teeth. Her hazy Earth memories noted that it kind of resembled a shark.

She didn’t have much time to contemplate what the heck this thing was, though, because it immediately released another one of those rumbling sounds. Apparently angered by the sudden loss of water, it shot its tentacles out in a wild frenzy.

One crashed into the demon’s bone shield, and she vaguely noted Llyr dodging a tentacle nearby. She studied the creature again. Now that the water was shallow enough to reveal its main body, she could see how massive it was. Her own flesh rippled.

From her warped arm, she formed a long chain of bones that she shot straight towards the monster’s shark-like head. It howled as the sharp end dug straight between its eyes, but the impact wasn’t quite strong enough to kill it on its own at this distance.

Instead, Pyre focused, continuing to warp the bone below the creature’s skin. She contorted the point out in curved crescents that would hook onto the monster’s flesh and remain secure.

The monster roared and thrashed, whipping out more of its tentacles in a wild frenzy. A wall of water appeared to her side, blocking one before it could reach her. She noted in her peripheral vision that Llyr had moved one of his raised hands. His palms were still glowing, his magic keeping the water suspended.

Pyre gave the siren a nod, then focused on the monster in front of her again. She gave the bone-chain an experimental tug to make sure the hook was secure. It was.

Bracing herself against the cliffside, Pyre promptly yanked back with all her strength.

The octopus-shark lurched violently, its massive form barrelling straight towards the demon from the force of the tug. It made another screech, barely managing to get the hook loose, but at this point it was too late to stop its momentum.

Pyre converted the bone chain back into flesh and grew a mouth large enough to cover the entire span of her warped arm. She thrust forward, jaws stretching as wide as possible. Gleaming teeth clamped over the monster’s head just before it could collide with her.

Brief images of the ocean flashed through Pyre’s mind, but she was more preoccupied with absorbing her mouth, then stretching her warped arm out to grab onto the nearest cliff ledge to pay much attention to the creature’s memories.

No sooner had she done so than the entire rocky area she’d just been standing on collapsed. A good chunk of the cliffside went down with it, too, the cracks from the monster’s impact splitting through the stone in web-like lines.

Pyre glanced down and watched the fallen rocks collide with the churning waves. Llyr had released his spell at some point, and the water was back to its usual height. The rest of the lusca’s corpse sank down below the depths.

Still hanging from the cliff, the demon met eyes with the other reincarnator.

All things considered, that could have gone a lot worse.
