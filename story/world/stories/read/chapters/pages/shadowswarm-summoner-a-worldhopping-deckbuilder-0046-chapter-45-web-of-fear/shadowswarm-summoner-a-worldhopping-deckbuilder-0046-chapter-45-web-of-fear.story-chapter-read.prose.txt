Chapter 45: Web of Fear

It took only a couple of minutes of light bickering for them to decide that the ghoul threat really was taken care of, for now, and that they’d have to go deeper in the spider’s cave if they wanted to get out. Or, as Ambrose snarked, they could pray to find a tunneling card for one of the Ransas to equip and make an exit.

The problem was, no one really wanted to venture deeper.

“Are we sure about this?” Jessica asked for the tenth time in as many minutes. Outside of the bare ten feet radius at the entrance of the cave, webs covered everything. She wasn’t too keen to go deeper, and who could blame her?

“Well, thanks to Ms. ‘I’m going to blow up all our enemies and trap us here’, I don’t think we have a choice,” said Ambrose. He grinned at an unimpressed Reava.

“Tell you what, Mr. ‘I had to steal to cheat in a tournament and get us all exiled’,” she started, but Luthor and Raeleq got between them before they could make it a thing.

“Look. I know getting into a fight and crashing onto a foreign world wasn’t exactly how any of us wanted to start the day,” said Luthor, looking pointedly between them. “But we need to work together, and throwing blame isn’t going to do anything. Let’s get out of here, get to safety, and then repair our damned ship.”

“If the ghouls aren’t waiting for us at the ship,” Ambrose muttered.

Now there was an unpleasant thought. “If they are, I am pretty sure that tunnel collapse must’ve killed a lot of them, on top of all the ones we got before. What I want to know is, why didn’t SNAC warn us about a bunch of ghouls?” Raeva didn’t actually hold it against the golem, but it was nice to have someone else to blame.

“Must be new information,” said Jessica with a shrug. “He told us about Bedrock Spire, and some of the villages along the way, but maybe he didn’t know about the ghouls.”

A hacking cough and clearing of a throat caught their attention. Dexi’Lynn had finally put away her pipe, and looked impatient for a change. “Are we going to keep yapping, or get going? I’m okay with a yap. Just wanna know if I have time for a smoke, snack, or a nap.”

That spurred them into action. With one last, appraising look at the dark, windy back of the cave, they got into formation. Raeleq, with his incredible senses and awareness, led the group. The prisoners were in the center, flanked on either side by Ambrose and Jessica, Luthor right behind them. Raeva, being a bit of a liability in a cave full of highly flammable spidersilk, stayed in the back.

The problem with all of the silk was how reflective it was. Their lights rebounded off of every wall, the completely covered floor, and the ceiling. It was almost like being in a hall of mirrors, but with the flickering of a flame making everything dance. Raeva put out her fire and relied on Luthor’s golden orb for more stable vision.

Despite how it looked and their collective dread, the webbing on the floor wasn’t sticky at all. In fact, it had a spring to it that was downright pleasant to walk on, especially after climbing their way out of the rocky Forlorn Pit. Raeva found herself almost hopping from spot to spot, and wondered what running full speed would be like, if the cavern wasn’t more like a carnival funhouse the further in they went.

The ceiling sloped upwards and got higher, while the webs thickened and formed twisty paths and dead ends among them. It was there they found the first signs of danger: dried out bones behind a silken screen.

“How long do you think they’ve been in there?” Raeleq asked, stopping to point the bones out. They looked to be from something big, human-sized, but with big claws of their own, and also strange teeth. Not quite human, but not far off either. “Is this a ghoul?”

Jack, who had been quiet for a surprising amount of time now, let out a whimper. “That’s a molekin. Oh gods, what if the spiders are close?”

“Then I guess we throw you to the spiders and run away,” said Ambrose, smiling unpleasantly. The kid whimpered again, all of the previous fight completely gone.

“Don’t be a dick, Ambrose. He’s already scared enough without you making it worse.” Raeva didn’t particularly care for the kid herself, but kids were too vulnerable and stupid to bully. It wasn’t any fun.

The boy flashed her a look that was part gratitude, part embarrassment. Raeva nodded once in acknowledgement.

“There’s…I hear something,” Raeleq said, looking up. His eyes held wariness, but not fear. The only ones scared were Jessica and the boy. “It’s up ahead. The only way to go. Are we ready for a fight?”

“Yes,” said Raeva.

“I guess,” Ambrose sighed.

“No,” Jessica groaned.

“Then we’re ready,” said Luthor. “More or less. Be careful, and you two,” he addressed Jack and Dexi’Lynn, “don’t get yourself caught or eaten.”

The kobold laughed, her strange, throaty vibrato reminiscent of bleating. “Don’t have to tell me twice.”

A hush fell over the group as they padded over soft webs and tried to avoid the sticky, dangling threads coming from the ceiling and the walls. Their pathway narrowed and twisted, until Raeleq and Ambrose had to duck to avoid getting cobwebs in their hair. Luthor’s golden light stopped being as helpful when it was impossible to see around corners. For all Raeva knew, everything they did just alerted any remaining spiders to their approach.

More bones clung to thick webbing. Raeva really wished she could just burn it all and be done with it. This cave was big enough, they could be fine. And it would go up so fast. She kept grumbling to herself, and nearly jumped when the line stopped moving.

“I think I see a way through,” said Luthor, voice barely above a whisper.

“Oh thank the gods,” Jack groaned.

“However,” he continued to the sighs and curses of his friends, “I don’t trust it. This next area is completely open, and then there’s a tunnel out.”

“So what’s the problem?” Raeva snapped. “Let’s get the hell out of here.”

“There are holes everywhere,” Raeleq said before Luthor could explain. “And I hear sounds coming from them. Should we run, or fight?”

“Run,” said Luthor, looking over the group. “Stay low, keep moving, and if combat starts, focus on threat displays and crowd control. Raeva, how precise can you be with your fire?”

She pushed her way past the kid and kobold, past Ambrose and Jessica until she could see out the opening behind the web. It did open up considerably, to the point where the top of the cave wasn’t visible with their limited light. That said, there were dozens of patches of baby blue lights, coming from algae or something. It lit up enough.

There were more bones everywhere, and also wrapped up morsels for later, dangling from lines. Some looked dead or near enough to not matter, while a couple still wriggled and fought against their bonds. Poor bastards. She also didn’t like how many of those small, personal sized holes there were. If a fight broke out, they could be surrounded easily. But right ahead was a massive tunnel, and no doubt their way out.

“Enough to buy us some room and time,” Raeva finally said, bouncing from foot to foot on the thick web. Impatience rocked her, and she pushed Raeleq. “Come on, let’s make this quick. We’ll be gone before anything can get onto us. We don’t want to end up wrapped up for dinner.”

Her brother looked to Luthor for an answer. He nodded. Jessica whimpered, and the rest of them got ready.

One, big deep breath in, and Raeleq took off. For such a large man, he could move pretty quickly, and Raeva took off after him, Luthor hot on her heels. She trusted the others to keep up, and abandoned herself to the thrill of running for dear life.

The web beneath their feet thrummed, first at time with their footsteps and then nonstop. The vibrations grew as they went, and a wave passed from the center of the room, up the walls and into those countless caves and crevices. They made it to the center of the room when Raeva heard two things.

The first, unfortunately, was the dangling victims, calling out, “Aw hell, please help! Don’t leave us here, like the others did.”

At the same time, a bestial hiss echoed through the cavern, joined by countless responses. Then the webs thrummed from top to bottom, and spiders poured out. They skittered down the walls, with bulbous black bodies the size of dogs, and fangs big enough that even the normally fearless Raeva shuddered.

Behind her, Jessica practically screamed and put on a burst of speed, overtaking them all. While the walls crawled with dozens of oncoming arachnids, there was still hope. They had a good lead, and the tunnel out was right ahead of them. Maybe Raeva could collapse this one too. What was it Ambrose liked to say? Always double down.

They’d almost made it when a dark shape emerged from the tunnel, blocking their way. Eight legs, each as thick around as Raeva’s waist spread out, stamping down the webbed-up floor. She slowed to a stop just in time for the shockwave to trip her up. The next thing she knew, she looked up into eight eyes emitting an odd emerald gleam.

The Spider Queen loomed over her, fangs the size of javelins, coated with a sticky substance the color of her eyes. She seemed to consider Raeva for a second, before her body twitched violently and she struck.
