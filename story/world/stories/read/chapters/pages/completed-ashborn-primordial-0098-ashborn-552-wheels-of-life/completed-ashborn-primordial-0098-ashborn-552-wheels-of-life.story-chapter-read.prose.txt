“Why?” Thaman asked after having listened to Maiya’s plan for the upcoming meeting with demonic Rajas.

“Because if I tell them his soul is rupturing, the first thing they’ll do is panic and start preparing for a future without him. Which is why we’ll say only that he is busy quelling the chaos and is thus unreachable.”

Thaman considered this. “Some of them will press the issue regardless.”

“Oh, I’m sure. But I’m no stranger to handling rowdy leaders.”

He chuckled. “That you are not.”

The doors opened again, and Hema appeared, bowing deeply.

“Rajni Tara of clan Naga and Raja Matiman of clan Chitran have arrived, my Lady, as has Raja Sagun’Ra, who is being escorted from the main deck. Raja Jagath sent no representative, I’m afraid. There has been no message from the Aindri.”

“None at all?” Maiya asked incredulously.

“Correct, my Lady. Our hails have gone unanswered.”

Thaman exchanged a look with Maiya, their expressions both grim.

“Bring the others in,” Maiya said defeatedly. Yet another loose end for her to chase down. To say nothing of the slight this was clearly intended to be.

The Raja probably wanted some concession for his involvement, as usual. As if she didn’t have enough on her plate already.

Tara entered first, accompanied by four aides and just as many guards. She was draped in her ceremonial Naga half-form which left her serpent half unclothed and her upper covered in silver.

“Maiya!” she beamed, slithering over to wrap her in a tight hug. “It’s been too long!”

“That it has,” Maiya said, smiling as she embraced her friend. As the only female rulers in the demon realm, they’d grown quite close over the years.

“While I’m dying to catch up, I’m guessing it’ll have to wait,” Tara said, nodding to Thaman, who returned the gesture.

“That it will,” Maiya said with a sigh.

Tara patted Maiya’s shoulder with a knowing smile. “Chin up, now! The Akh Nara’s wife doesn’t get to sigh.”

That just got her another sigh from Maiya, which, of course, made Tara laugh heartily.

Matiman entered next with only a single aide and bodyguard as was his style.

“Greetings, Maiya, Thaman,” he said with a warm smile. “It is good to see you.”

“And you,” Maiya replied. “I’m glad you could make it. And thank you for sending those relief forces.”

“Truly,” Thaman said, nodding. “We appreciate the help.”

Matiman waved away their gratitude, though Maiya and Thaman both noted the swishing of his tail.

She rather liked Matiman. He was a straight shooter, and though they disagreed now and again on policy, they both had the realm’s best interests at heart. Aside from Tara, she got along better with him than any of the other Rajas.

Sagun’Ra, of course, arrived last, and instead of walking through the doors like any normal demon, he appeared in a shadow behind his chair, crawling over the backrest before bringing his knees up and sitting hunched over in his chair.

As always, he did not greet anyone, instead silently scrutinizing every face.

Clearing her throat, Maiya called the meeting to order, beginning with a summary of the situation. She informed them that the Vimana threat had been repelled for now and the city saved, albeit not without some damage.

“What of the casualties?” Matiman asked. “How bad is the situation?”

Maiya cleared her throat, meeting each of their gazes. “Thanks to Vir’s efforts, we’ve only injured to worry about.”

“You expect me to believe nobody died?” Tara asked incredulously.

“On the contrary. Hundreds died. They were just raised afterward.”

The room went silent for a long moment.

“Raised…” Tara said blankly. “You can’t seriously mean the rumors are true!?”

“As hard to believe as that is, yes. My husband has developed a new power,” Maiya said, glossing over the details. “He can now resurrect the dead.”

Tara looked at Thaman incredulously, as though expecting him to refute her words. “This is true?”

“Would that it weren’t,” Thaman replied somberly. “My own son is among them.”

Maiya went on to fill them in on how the Akh Nara had sequestered himself to meditate. Of Roshan leading the revived and the fallout already in motion across the realm.

Tara spoke first, as Maiya had expected.

“These… revived,” she said. “Tell us what you can.”

“I don’t have all the information. As you can imagine, Vir was somewhat indisposed. I can, however, say that they are tethered to the Akh Nara and that they do not seem to require food or sleep… or even healing. As you saw, they can rise from the dead. From what Siya understands, they cannot die. Not unless Vir is killed.”

Tara snorted. “More likely the realms’ll split into three again.”

Maiya replied with a small smile, happy to let her believe that convenient lie.

“Whether they age or not, I cannot say, but my guess is that they do not.”

The room absorbed this in silence.

Matiman cleared his throat. “What of their souls? Are they alive in the sense we understand?”

“Siya hasn’t yet been able to answer that,” Maiya lied, “but they certainly seem alive.”

“Not in the cycle,” Sagun’Ra rasped, splendidly ruining everything.

“Sagun’Ra?”

“The risen,” he said, rolling his head towards her as though it took immense effort. “Not in the cycle. Pulled out. Will not return.”

“What do you mean they will not return?” Tara asked with a scowl.

“Samsara,” Raja Sagun’Ra said. “The Akh Nara reached past the great wheel and removed them. They will not be reborn. Whatever happens to them is final.”

Matiman shifted uneasily in his seat, looking considerably less composed than he had upon entering.

Great, Maiya thought. So much for assuaging the realm’s leaders. She should’ve known the Iksana Raja would throw an Ash’va into her plans.

“Sagun’Ra,” Maiya said sweetly and without a hint of the frustration she felt. “Are you saying that what the Akh Nara did was unnatural?”

“Unnatural,” Sagun’Ra repeated, as if hearing the word for the first time. “No. Unprecedented. The wheel does not move backwards, and yet, the Akh Nara has.”

“And what of Vaak himself?” Tara asked.

Sagun’Ra’s eyes moved slowly toward her.

“Unknown.”

This time the silence was longer, and Maiya didn’t bother to interrupt it.

There was no use refuting his words now.

“Even if this is true, there is little we can do about it,” she said at last. “Let’s set this aside for the moment. What concerns me more immediately is the political situation. Hema?”

“While we cannot hope to stop the rumors at this point, we can, however, attempt to counter those rumors with a public address.”

“I’m sure I speak for all here when I say that such addresses are easily arranged,” Matiman said. “There is, however, a more pressing concern I would prefer to address while we are all here.”

She turned to him. “Yes?”

“I understand that the Akh Nara is occupied for the moment, but how long can we expect this state of affairs to last? We could truly use his help right now.”

“I honestly wish I knew,” Maiya replied. “He’s extremely busy right now, as you can imagine.”

“Of course,” Matiman said. “Then, what is our plan if he does not return in time to help us deal with the fallout of these events?”

All heads turned to Maiya, who met their gazes.

“Right now, all the revived demons have congregated around Vir. Which, conveniently for us, allows us to delay whatever fallout occurs. Rumors die away in time, so long as people aren’t seeing these demons walking amongst them. Rather, we should be more worried about how this news will be taken in the Human Realm, and what we can do to curtail any degradation of human/demon relations.”

“I agree,” Thaman said, coming to Maiya’s aid. “And I would suggest we focus on that task, for it will no doubt prove to be monumental.”

“I’m already assembling a team for this,” she swiftly replied. “With Cirayus, Thaman, Ira, and myself as its core pillars. We’ll draft speeches for all of you to quell whatever anti-human sentiment might be propagating around demonkind, and we’ll do the same for the leaders in the Human Realm. I’ll plan for the worst case and present them to this council within the week. Is that acceptable?”

The Rajas all nodded, save for Sagun’Ra, who showed no reaction whatsoever. From a ghael as eccentric as him, though, Maiya supposed that was the best she’d get.

“Then we’re all in agreement,” she said. “I’ll brief each of you privately on the matters specific to your clans later. For now, I advise you to return to your clan. Please do not issue any public statements until we’ve all agreed on a unified message. The last thing we need is for every clan to have their own story.”

With the meeting adjourned, the Rajas began to filter out one by one.

Sagun’Ra, of course, sank into the shadows without even leaving his seat, leaving only Thaman at the table.

“Well,” Maiya said. “If things go this smoothly in the Human Realm, I might even celebrate!”

“I would not celebrate just yet regardless,” Thaman said. “Jagath will be tricky to handle.”

“Right,” Maiya said dryly. “Almost forgot about him.”

“You think he means to lobby for some material gain?”

“I think that’s exactly what he’s done in the past,” Maiya replied. “He doesn’t act on anything unless he gets some tangible benefit above and beyond everyone else. I’ll send a messenger to him tonight.”

Thaman nodded and rose from his seat. “Then I wish you the best of luck, Maiya. I fear you will need it.”

“That makes two of us,” Maiya replied, rubbing her eyes tiredly.

With a final nod, Thaman ducked through the entrance, leaving her alone.

Maiya remained there for a moment, looking at the seat where Vir would have sat.

“Well, you were right,” Maiya muttered, recalling his words to her many years ago. Of how ruling over a single realm was already far too much of a burden. That the last thing he wanted was to rule both humans and demons.

She thought he was being modest at the time. Perhaps a little unambitious. Now, however, she understood the wisdom of his words.

Maiya was burning her flame as hot as she possibly could, and yet, the world still moved faster.

She didn’t doubt she’d falter. The only question was when. But until that time came, she’d fight with everything she had.

“Hema? Saanvi? Have our Altani friends prep the skyships. We head for Sonam with all haste.”

Not one second after she’d finished giving that order, her communications orb lit and a familiar, if unwanted face, appeared.

“Well,” Saunak said, grinning from ear to ear and rubbing his palms in greedy anticipation. “We’re about to intercept the enemy airship. Would you prefer to be present? Or shall I have the honors?”

Maiya sighed. “Belay that,” she told her aides. “We’re going to the Vimana first.”

With some luck, at least one of her problems would disappear today, and hopefully for good. And while she’d never destroyed a Vimana before, there was always a first time for everything.
