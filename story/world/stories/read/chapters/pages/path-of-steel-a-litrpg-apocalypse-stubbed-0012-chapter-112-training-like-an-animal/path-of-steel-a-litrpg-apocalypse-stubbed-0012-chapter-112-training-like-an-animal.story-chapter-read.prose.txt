Nine days until the tournament,Twenty-six days until the invasion.

A spear cluttered to the ground. “Not again!” came a cry. Gary sighed as he massaged his wrists.

The waterfall echoed nearby, subdued but present as water struck rock. The residue of Gary’s breakthrough had already disappeared from the flat stone in the middle of the pond. Serenity dominated the ear. Gary felt at peace, or he would have, if he wasn’t constantly dropping his spear.

“Worst training ever,” he muttered. Picking it back up, he settled it between his hands, then span it around. The Golden Bolt’s golden spearhead blurred as it span rapidly, creating what seemed like a golden circle in front of Gary. The black spear body also left afterimages, making the technique resemble a gold-and-black wheel.

“Whee!” Goldie exclaimed.

Gary resumed the movement. He tried to stay focused. Holding a rhythm was hard after a while—he’d only need to last a second in combat, but he wanted to make it ten in training. Unless he truly mastered the spin, it would eventually slip, and he might suffer for it.

Preparation was the mother of success. Diligence was its father.

Gary stayed next to the waterfall from dawn to noon, spinning the spear until his wrists were as flexible as stone. When he couldn’t take it any more, he hopped to the flat rock where he’d once broken through and sat down cross-legged. The pain in his wrists flared as they relaxed, but his regeneration would fix them right up. In the meantime, he took deep breaths and focused inward.

Through the eyes of a Birth of Aura cultivator, the world was more than material. It was also spiritual, flooded with vibrating energy. He could sense it everywhere around him in the scope of his four-foot aura field, as well as inside himself. His body was a vessel of aura. It filled him up, vibrating as rippling golden clouds. Black lightning brewed occasionally.

Deep inside him, in an area he could only marginally perceive, crimson tentacles connected him to the tiny red orb that was the Seed of Eternal Flesh.

Whenever Gary gazed at the space containing the seed, he got the feeling it was something he wasn’t supposed to be seeing yet. It was too abstract, too mystical. Like a book he’d accidentally picked up, whose language he could read, yet the text made no sense. He couldn’t even tell whether that was a physical space or not. Was aura physical? Was he?

Whatever that place was, it existed. That was for sure. He’d felt it when the crimson tentacles latched themselves onto him, and when the puppeteer tried to fight him through them. He could see the walls of that space flickering with black lightning, which passed between the tentacles with neither harming the other.

“Any news, Goldie?” Gary asked.

“Nope! I can sense that place, but I have no idea. I just know that I like those tentacles. They’re friendly.”

Gary shook his head. Back at the third floor, the only reason they’d discovered the seed was that Goldie had felt a resonance with it from all the way across the underground ruins. There had to be a connection between them, and Goldie herself had confirmed it, but that connection seemed nonsensical. She represented black lightning. The seed gave Vitality. The heck?

They certainly synergize well, but where’s the connection?

Friendly, Goldie had said. Whatever connection there was, the Seed recognized it, and maybe that was part of the reason why Gary had been able to absorb it. Anything beyond that, however, Gary was ill-equipped to investigate. He could barely sense his soul, let alone plumb its depths.

As for the Seed of Eternal Flesh, that was a creepy little piece of shit which he’d much rather leave alone for now. It held tremendous power. Maybe messing with it could make him stronger, but maybe it could also make his soul explode.

Gary finally turned his mind to what he could control. His aura. It pulsed inside his body, vibrating the ambient aura around him to its tune and, in doing so, sending out ripples which spread for four feet around Gary. Those aura waves were part of him. He was aware of everything they touched, be it matter or energy, bringing that four-foot-radius area under his complete surveillance.

Expanding his aura field was the main task given to him by Master Colorful. To do it, Gary observed his whole body at once, seeing how his vibration was slightly inconsistent. Different body parts vibrated in different ways, and the resulting clashes weakened the aura ripples he sent outside his body. He only had to smoothen out his internal vibration.

A simple concept, with an almost impossible execution.

All of his aura vibrated to the same concept—the Black Lightning and singing spear, subtly influenced by the Seed of Eternal Flesh—but there were more factors. His body density played a part. His subconscious patterns played a part. Bones vibrated differently from muscles, which vibrated differently from skin.

Those minute deviations were what he was called to eliminate.

Gary tuned out everything to focus inward. His body became his world. He saw his arm as a kaleidoscope of vibrating aura, and though he could impose his will on it to iron out the differences, he knew that was not the way. He’d already gone through his body in the Energy Harnessing realm. The next step was to lighten his aura, make it strong and springy, so that the influence of his body density and other factors lessened in comparison.

Master Colorful had taught him an exercise to achieve just that.

Gary grabbed his aura and started playing with it. He made it vibrate harder, lighter. He skewed its frequency. He pulled and squeezed it and drew it taut. He forced the deviations to increase, straining his body. The few feet of water around him were blanketed by irregular vibrations which made the fish dizzy. Gary himself was the most affected. Sweat appeared on his forehead, and though he was sitting, he felt the world spinning. Mental exhaustion crept in. He persisted, shutting down the rising sense of panic.

Aura workout, his master had called it. A straight-forward but challenging method. How long one could last depending on their willpower, and Gary would be damned if he let himself slack off. Ranking in the top sixteen was not enough. He needed to get stronger.

Sui Duang is training too, he thought, teeth gritted. I thought I had three months, but the tournament starts now. Our Duel of Honor is coming up. If I cannot surpass him until then, he’ll kill me. I cannot rest for even a second.

He pictured the man, with his slim features and unlikeable goatee. Those contemptuous eyes. The imposing physique. How he’d made his aura flow like water to corner Gary in the third floor, quickly earning an overwhelming advantage.

Gary knew that he couldn’t beat Sui Duang right now. He could resist, but he could never win. The water would get him eventually. The only reason he’d survived their battle in the third floor was the frog general’s appearance.

And for fucking what? Why are we fighting?

Anger welled up inside Gary. He remembered Sui Duang threatening him the first time they met. He’d talked back then, humiliating Sui Duang in front of a small crowd, but that only made the other man hate him more. He had Alicia’s grandmother arrested and sent a henchman to mess with Alicia and Cog. That henchman took their spears. Gary killed the henchman despite Sui Duang’s threats. During the expedition, they crossed words before crossing spears.

In every interaction they shared, the enmity between Gary Steel and Sui Duang deepened. And he was just part of something larger. The entire, massive Sui family was against Gary now. The Brothers were, too, because he’d killed one of them after they killed his mentor. The authorities would pursue him for selling Muscle Sap.

The Network had slaughtered his planet.

Gary dove deeper and deeper into the shit hole that was revenge, but he didn’t care. First Sui Duang, then the brothers, then the rest of the Sui.

Give me enemies. Give me hardship. They are all steppingstones on my path. The Network ruined Earth and they think they’ll get away with it. They will, if I don’t go up there to demand justice. I won’t let that happen. They can’t do anything they want and remain unpunished. They can’t keep ruining people. I will feed off the enemies they throw at me, I will grow stronger, and I will make them pay.

They won’t fucking get away with it.

Gary snapped into focus, realizing he’d stopped training. Reining in his thoughts, he returned to testing the limits of his aura control, sticking to it with dark, steely determination. He ignored the fatigue and persisted. He wouldn’t really harm himself—it was an illusion. The limit was his motivation, and boy did he have motivation.

When Gary finally stopped, the sun was halfway to the horizon, and he could barely think straight from the exhaustion. This was good. All battles ended in exhaustion, one way or another, so being able to operate through it was essential. Gary raised his heavy hands to grab the spear. He more stumbled than jumped ashore, then started spinning the Golden Bolt again.

“Faster, faster! Whee!” Goldie said.

He practiced spinning until long after the sun had set, then crawled to bed. By the time he woke, his mind was heavy, but his body was as good as new. His aura was a bit more flexible than before.

He arrived at the pond by dawn, span the spear until noon, then worked out his aura into the afternoon before resuming the spinning. It was harder than it looked. According to Master Colorful, most cultivators got stuck at this stage for weeks. Gary needed to perfect it in days. Only then could he begin to learn the offensive technique, Nine Songs of the Spear.

His wrists were swollen and bruised despite the regeneration, and his eyes were sunken from aura fatigue. He became a single-minded machine. Obsession filled every fiber of his being.

I’ll make them pay.

By night he would rest, and by dawn he awoke. The days bled together. They passed in a flash. Until, one day, Gary awoke as usual, but he would not train. This was the first day of the tournament.

And Gary Steel was ready.
