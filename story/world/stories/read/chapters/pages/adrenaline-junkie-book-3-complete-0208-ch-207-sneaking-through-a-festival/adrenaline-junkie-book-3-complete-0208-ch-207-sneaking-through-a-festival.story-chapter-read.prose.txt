The thought clung to him like a wet cloak as he approached the West Gate, as he waited in line, and up until he passed through the first round of interrogations to enter the city.

The guards didn’t even blink at his heavily armored appearance or the fact that he was arriving on foot – most people did when they were poor, especially physical fighters such as Archie, judging by his physical appearance. The grime, soot-covered edges, and half-dried mud caked over his armor told a story on its own.

To them, this wasn’t a noble or a knight, nowhere near that level of status, he looked like just another unfortunate wanderer who’d been caught on the wrong side of bad luck – judging by the fact that he looked to carry no belongings other than the armor on his back, they assumed he’d been robbed on the way to get here as they could not detect any Spatial Storages on his person except for a small one on his armor’s left leg that housed a couple of potions.

More specifically, they seemed to have settled on a theory before he even opened his mouth: a traveler from a caravan that had met its end in the crossfire of two powerful D-Grades. Tragic, sure, but also mundane enough not to warrant much more than a glance. This was, non-surprisingly, not an uncommon thing to see as a guard, given how common D-Grades were roaming the lands, especially with the increasing hostility with the Ooh-Rah Warbands growing in size as of late.

“So,” a mustached man in chainmail began, squinting at the oak brown card he’d taken from Archie, “what was the name of the caravan you were traveling with again?”

Archie’s eyes didn’t so much as flicker to the mustached man’s question. “The Wandering Grove,” he said evenly, letting a subtle tension creep into his voice as his gaze moved from the guy and towards the dirt beneath the man’s boot. He even clenched his right hand just enough to make the leather of his glove creak, selling the image of a man suppressing grief.

“And what did you and this… Wandering Grove do? How did you get entangled with them?” the mustache man asked, still watching him with the dull patience of someone who’d rather be drinking this very moment with his friends instead of guarding the West Gate.

Archie let a beat pass, just long enough to make it look like the words caught in his throat and wet his throat before stuttering, “We are… were…” he hesitated, letting three heavy seconds hang between them for mood development before continuing, “A group of orphans that grew up in the same rundown church in the slums of Marl’geth. Once we were old and able enough, we set out to explore… to escape the cesspool we grew up in.”

Please don’t ask me the name of the church, Archie thought grimly. I am more or less giving you the background to every single Dungeons & Dragons campaign I’ve ever been in. And I don’t think it’ll be realistic if I say the church I resided in was called the church of the Spaghetti God.

The mustached guard didn’t ask. Instead, he gave the oak brown card one last glance, shrugged, and handed it back to Archie, who’d quickly tucked it into the small Spatial Storage on his left armored leg. A second guard, this one with an enormous beard jutting out from the bottom of his full helmet like it was trying to escape, gave a bored grunt and a nod.

Archie – no, Creed – was waved into the city.

“There’s a tavern called the Burrowing Badger that serves good ale and is connected to a good bathhouse. I recommend it,” the bearded guard said in a gravelly tone that suggested to Archie that he’d been smoking and drinking from the very moment he’d been born. “And don’t do anything stupid while in Creavenboor. Enjoy the New Year Festival.”

Archie offered a polite nod as he stepped past the gates, though in his head he was already wondering how long he could keep the “orphan caravan” story from completely collapsing.

Unbeknownst to them, the oak-brown card tucked in Archie’s small Spatial Storage shimmered, fading back to its original white. At the same moment, the thin membrane of spatial mana he’d wrapped around his Spatial Rings dissolved, releasing the masking effect. The faint trace of mana he’d been bleeding from the small storage on his leg, enough to disguise the presence of the rings, faded with it.

The moment he crossed into the city of Creavenboor, the smell hit him. Not the stench of garbage, though that lingered faintly in the background along with the scent of them being burned off in the distance, the blessing and curse of his enhanced senses after evolving into D-Grade – but the scent of many types of perfume, smoked wood, and the faint, greasy scent of street food being cooked somewhere nearby.

Archie adjusted the strap of his gorget and kept moving, already cataloging the streets and the faces. Every set of eyes that lingered a little too long, every building that looked just a bit too guarded for its size – it all went into the mental map. Because if his Handler’s “17.8% chance of escape” was anywhere close to accurate, he’d need every advantage he could get.

The streets were narrow, cobblestoned, and lined with multicolored, swaying lanterns and streamers – decorations for the festival, it seems, he mused to himself as he eyed the roads and streets that were packed with couples and families celebrating.

The wind bore a series of perfumes, warm bread from an oven along one street, curl of chimney smoke into the night, and somewhere along a bit further on, the greasy, mouth-watering aroma of spiced meat sizzling in a fire.

There was a distant hum of music drifting from a tavern one street away, the sort of bardic playing that sounded far better than any sort of music that he’d heard while listening to any Medieval Tavern Ambience on the web back on Earth. It spilled into the stillness of the lane like someone had left a record player running in the wrong room.

He didn't desire to be the spectacle, but he could already sense people's notice extending out to snag a glance at him longer than what could be considered a passing one. And it was not the armor nor the headdress that he wore; people were accustomed to seeing much weirder-looking armor, but rather it was the amount of filth that had been caked in.

Mud crumbled into rough edges along the plates, soot burned into the crevices, and there was still dust stuck to the gorget and pauldrons.

I most likely overdid the whole "battle-worn traveler" appearance, Archie considered as he saw a D-Grade human woman pulling her kid in a little closer as they passed by in an attempt to not get her child’s clothes dirty when they went past him. I guess now I know why that bearded guard suggested that tavern and why everyone in line gave me a large berth and let me cut in front of them every now and then.

Turning to face one of the thinner alleys, a black slash between two slanted brick buildings and one of the few emptier once, he glanced back around him once more to make sure nobody was giving them too much attention. The street behind him curled on around without a hitch, the people flowing by like a slow current.

Good enough.

With a brief amount of focus and spatial mana, Archie cast Blink Step and appeared inside the alley without needing to nudge his way through people, letting him slip into the shadows of the alley and away from the bright lights of the lanterns. Sounds of celebration began to die down behind brick and wood.

“Now to untangle this mess,” Archie muttered, right before activating Forgesmith’s Flame and letting it roll across his entire body – armor, headdress, hair, and all.

The warm heat from Forgesmith’s Flame spread across his armor and skin, turning all the mud, filth, and grime that clung to him to ash before even that ash was purified by its flame. After deactivating Forgesmith’s Flame, Archie’s armor and body were now completely clean, no longer looking like a murderhobo.

With the filth burned away, he exhaled slowly and pulled out his Compass of Desire and watched as the bright crimson needle spun aimlessly for a brief moment before steadily pointing down the alley. Walking down the alley, he was greeted with the needle pointing right.

Creavenboor was practically a maze; downtown Pheonix could not hold a candle to how windy the back alleys were. Archie followed the needle’s point religiously, while sprinting through the cramped alleys at top speed, pushing Adrenaline Rush to 15% whenever he was out of sight, and Blink Stepping several times to dodge through the crowded lanes between alleys that spilled into the main streets.

“The city’s festival was both a blessing and a curse,” Archie muttered to himself. With how distracted everyone is and how practically everyone is using magic to do something, I’m practically a ghost in the city and not bringing any attention to myself… The only downside is that because I’m only using alleys and not the sidewalk, reaching Camilla is a lot slower than what I would have liked.

Not to mention rooftop hopping is out of the question: it would draw way too much attention to myself, besides, I dunno if that’s even allowed in the city, Archie mused as he didn’t see anyone hopping from rooftop to rooftop.

Unlike Greeber, which was ravaged by civil war and earthquakes that toppled thousands of buildings and threw the city into chaos, Creavenboor stood steady – its guards alert and its streets crowded with curious onlookers.

With only the needle as his direction, Archie grew progressively more restless as time passed. And as much as he would have preferred to use Vital Sight and zero in on Camilla’s Vital Outline and Sparks of Vitality, he couldn’t.

Because her vital energy was practically the size of a squash, and barely a spark amid the sea of life pulsing through the city. It would be invisible within this city, considering that it housed over a million people, each with their own unique Vital Outline and Sparks of Vitality. It did not matter that he’d memorized both Henry and Camilla’s  Vital Outlines and Sparks of Vitalities, and that normally they would stand out to him as the sheer number of people within the city simply overwhelmed his sight.

Trying to find even one in particular was like trying to pluck out a needle in a wheat farm - impossible.

Archie followed the needle’s every changing point through winding alleys and twisting streets, uncaring of the sound of laughter and celebration growing more distant behind him.

His boots clicked quietly on the stones as he neared the Workshop District, which was comprised of many different types of production shops ranging from carpenter shops to smithies. All of which were closed and with the lights of their shops out – possibly out celebrating with everyone else.

The needle suddenly pointed just to the left of an unassuming wooden shop door nestled between a tannery and a smithy. The said shop door had the words “Woods R Us” hanging atop it, and the only one around that was still lit up.

Blinking suspiciously at the name of the shop, he shook his head before practically teleporting in front of the shop door and putting away his Compass of Desire and knocking on the door of said shop.

After waiting a couple of seconds and not hearing a response, Archie focused on his own vital energy and activated Vital Sight, concentrating fully on limiting his senses to only surround both himself and the shop in front of him.

Not a second after activating the skill did Archie immediately body slam open the door and burst inside the shop.
