The party marched away from the shallow grave in silence.

The deeper they pushed into the forest, the more it seemed to actively decay around them. The towering pine trees were entirely dead, their bark peeling away in long, grey strips like rotting skin. The ground beneath their boots had turned into a foul, freezing slurry of black mud and stagnant, necrotic water.

Josh walked at the very front of the formation. His jaw was set like a block of granite. The weight of his heavy steel plate armour was nothing compared to the crushing psychological burden he currently carried. He had just executed a dying man to save his soul. That singular, horrific act had stripped away any lingering illusions about their mission. They were not just clearing wandering monsters. They were marching into a warzone.

"Josh, we need to stop and evaluate this," Brett stated quietly, his voice cutting through the heavy crunch of their footsteps.

The mage was walking directly behind the shield-bearer. Brett was holding his hands close to his chest to conserve his body heat. He looked pale, and his sharp, intelligent features were pinched in deep concentration.

Josh did not slow his pace. "There is nothing to evaluate, Brett. We heard Fenn. The undead are amassing an army in the basin. We have to cut the head off before it marches on Ashenfall."

"That is exactly what we need to evaluate," Brett argued, quickening his step to walk parallel with his friend. "You are assuming this General is simply a high-level boss that spawned from the broken Crypts. But dungeons don’t manufacture military commanders with a profound understanding of strategic troop assembly overnight."

"What are you getting at, spell-slinger?" Bhel grunted from the rear flank. The dwarf had his twin axes drawn, his eyes constantly scanning the rotting treeline.

"I am suggesting that this General predates the dungeon break entirely," Brett explained, his voice taking on a grim, academic certainty. "Think back to the night the portals surged across the continent. Think about the massive, tearing purple and orange light we saw in the sky. If an entity with the power to organise an army already existed in this world, then the dungeons did not simply overflow by accident."

Carcan gasped softly, her knuckles turning white around her amber-topped staff. "You think this creature forced the portals open from the outside?"

"It is the only logical conclusion," Brett nodded, looking at the dead trees surrounding them. "It flooded the local ecosystem with chaotic mana to kill the native forests. It created so much confusion that this forest would go unnoticed. If we are walking towards the entity responsible for the Shattering, five people cannot simply cut its head off. We are walking into an organised, fortified military position."

"We should turn back," Perberos announced from the shadows on their left flank. The elven scout did not sound afraid, merely pragmatic. "We must return to the main camp. We need to share Fenn's intelligence with the guild. They need to mobilise the city guard and the higher-tier raiding parties."

Josh finally stopped walking. The heavy mud squelched loudly as he planted his boots. He turned around, his storm-grey eyes meeting the concerned faces of his friends.

"If we go back right now, we go back with nothing but the delirious ramblings of a dying man," Josh said, his voice hard. "Torin will demand proof before he sends anything back and risks pulling the city guard away from the walls of Ashenfall. He will argue that Fenn was hallucinating from the necrotic infection. We need to see this army with our own eyes. We need to know exactly how many numbers they have, and we need to locate this General in the basin."

"You are asking us to conduct a reconnaissance mission against an overwhelming force," Brett sighed, rubbing his temples.

"We get eyes on the basin, we confirm the numbers, and then we pull back," Josh promised, tapping the heavy steel of his breastplate. "We do not engage the main force. But we cannot retreat blindly. We need actionable intelligence."

Bhel slammed the flat of his iron axe against his leather-padded thigh. "I agree with the lad. We did not march all this way just to turn our tails at a scary story. We get to the ridge, we count the rotting bastards, and then we go back. Simple."

Brett looked at Carcan and Perberos. The elven twins exchanged a brief, silent look of communication. Perberos gave a slow, reluctant nod.

"We push to the basin ridge," Perberos agreed softly. "But we do not engage."

"Understood," Josh said, turning back to the dark path ahead. "Keep your eyes sharp."

They resumed their march, the atmosphere growing increasingly tense with every passing mile. The ambient necrotic energy in the forest was becoming so thick it was almost suffocating. The air tasted of rust and old graves. Carcan had to constantly hum a low, cleansing melody just to keep the foul magic from seeping into their lungs.

An hour later, the terrain began to slope upward. The dense, dead pine trees thinned out, revealing a steep, rocky incline that led to the rim of the mountain basin.

Perberos instantly raised a hand, his long, delicate fingers splayed wide in a silent command to halt.

The party froze. Josh slowly lowered his centre of gravity, bringing his deeply gouged shield up to cover his chest.

"Movement," Perberos whispered, his silver eyes fixed on a cluster of massive, jagged boulders near the top of the ridge. "It is a heavy patrol."

"How many?" Josh asked quietly, his hand hovering over the hilt of his longsword.

"Fifty," Perberos reported, his voice entirely devoid of emotion. "They are not mindless ghouls. They are heavily armoured skeletal legionnaires. They are maintaining a strict defensive formation. And they are not alone."

Brett squinted through the gloom. "I see it. The magical density is terrifying."

Standing behind the tight formation of fifty skeletal warriors was a true nightmare. It was a Bone Goliath. The creature stood twelve feet tall, constructed from hundreds of fused, human-sized skeleton bones. It wore crude, rusted iron plates bolted directly into its bone structure, and it carried a massive, spiked mace that looked heavy enough to level a small building. A violent, swirling vortex of green necrotic magic burned within its hollow ribcage.

"We cannot sneak past them to see the basin," Perberos stated flatly. "They cover the only viable path up the ridge."

Josh looked at the massive patrol. He looked at the sheer drop on either side of the rocky path. They had to clear the patrol to get their intelligence, but a noisy fight would undoubtedly draw attention from the army below.

"We hit them fast, and we hit them with everything we have," Josh ordered, drawing his longsword. The steel sang a deadly, crisp note in the freezing air. "Brett, clear the small fry. Bhel, you and I will break the Goliath."

"Gladly," Bhel growled, his beard bristling with sudden, violent anticipation.

"Carcan, keep us shielded," Josh continued. "Perberos, take out their archers before they can signal the basin."

Brett stepped forward, a cold, focused intellect replacing his previous hesitation. He raised his hands, his drake-scale gloves glowing with an intense, blinding white heat as he activated his Aura of the Hearth-Forge.

A wave of intense, dry thermal energy washed over Josh and Bhel. Instantly, Josh’s longsword and Bhel’s twin iron axes erupted into roaring, magical flames.

A tiny, serpentine Cinder-Sprite familiar then materialised around his wrist, chirping sharply. Brett pushed his hands forward. A massive, roaring torrent of highly compressed, white-hot plasma erupted from his palms. The fire did not just blast outward. It moved like a living, hungry predator. It swept up the rocky slope, completely engulfing the front two ranks of the skeletal legionnaires.

The rusted chainmail of the undead troops instantly superheated, turning glowing red before melting entirely. The necrotic magic holding their bones together was violently incinerated by the elemental plasma. Twenty skeletal warriors collapsed into piles of brittle, burning ash before they could even draw their rusted blades.

The ambush was blown. The remaining thirty legionnaires turned their glowing green eye sockets toward the party and charged down the slope in perfect, terrifying unison.

"Now!" Josh roared.

He triggered his Juggernaut's Tread skill. Blue lightning sparked violently from his heavy steel boots as he ignored the treacherous, icy rocks. He closed the distance with unstoppable momentum, becoming a human battering ram. He slammed his heavy shield directly into the leading edge of the skeletal formation, crushing three skulls into powder instantly.

Bhel was right beside him. The dwarf moved like a boulder rolling downhill. His flaming axes were a blur of lethal iron. He hacked left and right, cleanly severing skeletal spines and shattering rusted shields with brutal, overwhelming force.

Perberos had already scrambled up a sheer rock face to their left. The elven ranger found a narrow ledge and unleashed a rapid, devastating volley of steel-tipped arrows. He targeted the skeletal archers in the rear of the patrol, pinning them to the stone before they could even nock their rusted arrows.

For the first thirty seconds, the fight was a masterpiece of coordinated destruction. The party tore through the lower-tier undead with the practiced, ruthless efficiency of a level twenty-five team.

But the Bone Goliath had finally closed the distance.

The massive, twelve-foot amalgamation of bone and rusted iron let out a deafening, hollow roar that shook dust from the surrounding cliffs. It ignored the heavily armoured Josh entirely. It locked its glowing green gaze onto the dwarf who was currently hacking its smaller minions to pieces.

The Goliath raised its massive, spiked mace high into the air, the weapon glowing with a sickly, necrotic green aura, and brought it down in a devastating, overhead strike aimed directly at Bhel.

"Bhel, move!" Josh yelled, desperately trying to parry a skeletal spear thrust to reach his friend.

Bhel saw the shadow fall over him. He did not try to dodge. He planted his thick boots firmly into the rocky earth, crossed his flaming iron axes above his head, and braced his incredibly dense musculature for the impact.

The collision was catastrophic.

The spiked mace crashed into the crossed axes with the force of a falling meteor. The weight of the twelve-foot monster was simply too much for Bhel to absorb. The heavy iron hafts of his axes held, but the brute force shattered Bhel's defensive stance completely.

The dwarf was driven to his knees, the rocky ground cracking beneath his boots. The spiked mace slid past his crossed weapons, the rusted iron spikes tearing brutally through the thick boiled leather and iron plates protecting his left shoulder.

Bhel let out a ragged, agonising roar. The spikes bit deep into his flesh, cracking his collarbone and tearing a massive, bloody gouge down his chest.

The Goliath yanked the mace back, ripping the dwarf's armour completely open. Bhel collapsed sideways into the freezing mud, dark red blood pouring rapidly from the horrific wound, mixing with the black dirt.

"Bhel!" Carcan screamed.

She abandoned her safe position in the rear line, rushing forward into the chaotic melee. She slammed the base of her amber-topped staff into the rocks right beside the fallen dwarf.

"Light protect us!" Carcan chanted desperately.

A translucent, golden dome of magic flared to life over her and Bhel. The remaining skeletal legionnaires immediately began hacking wildly at the barrier with their rusted swords, their weapons hissing violently against the holy energy.

Josh saw red. A terrifying, blinding fury erupted in his core. He did not care about the minor skeletons anymore. He locked his storm-grey eyes on the Bone Goliath.

"You rotting piece of filth," Josh growled.

He activated his Adamantine Breach skill. He launched himself across the rocky slope, ignoring a rusted blade that scraped uselessly against his breastplate. He closed the distance to the Goliath and slammed his heavy shield directly into the monster's kneecap.

The impact shattered the fused bones of the joint. The twelve-foot Goliath stumbled heavily, roaring in confusion as it listed to the side.

Josh immediately triggered Stormforged Adamantite. Raw, unadulterated blue electricity surged down his right arm, interlacing with Brett's roaring magical flames on his longsword. The air filled with the sharp, acrid stench of ozone.

Before the Goliath could recover its balance, Josh leaped into the air. He brought the heavy, electrified, flaming blade down in a brutal strike aimed perfectly at the glowing green vortex of necrotic magic spinning within the creature's exposed ribcage.

The blade sheared cleanly through the rusted iron plates and shattered the thick ribs. The static discharge exploded outward with a blinding flash of blue and white light. The lightning completely scrambled the dark magic holding the massive construct together.

The Bone Goliath went entirely rigid. The green light within its chest flickered violently and died. The massive creature collapsed backward, exploding into a shower of hundreds of individual, disconnected bones that clattered uselessly down the rocky slope.

"Clear the rest!" Josh roared, landing heavily on his boots and spinning around.

Brett was already finishing the job. The mage stepped forward, his eyes burning with cold anger as he saw Bhel bleeding on the ground. Brett unleashed a highly compressed, continuous stream of plasma that swept across the remaining skeletal warriors, turning them all into a massive cloud of glowing golden dust.

The rocky path fell suddenly, terrifyingly silent, save for the ragged, bubbling breathing of the fallen dwarf.

Josh dropped his sword and shield, rushing to Carcan's golden dome. He fell to his knees beside Bhel.

The dwarf looked awful. His deeply tanned skin had turned a sickly, ashen grey. The wound on his shoulder and chest was horrific. The iron spikes had torn through muscle and scraped bone, but that was not the worst part.

The wound was completely lined with a thick, pulsing black sludge. The necrotic energy from the Goliath's mace had infected the injury instantly.

Carcan had her hands pressed firmly over the wound, her golden magic flaring brightly. She was weeping silently, her face pale with extreme exertion.

"I cannot close it," Carcan gasped, looking up at Josh with panicked silver eyes. "The necrotic rot is fighting my magic. It is eating the healthy tissue as fast as I can repair it. I can stabilize his heart rate, but he is losing too much blood."

Bhel coughed, a thick splatter of dark blood coating his braided beard. He forced a weak, incredibly stubborn grin onto his face, looking up at Josh.

"Just a scratch, lad," Bhel wheezed, his heavy hand trembling as he reached up to grip Josh's gauntlet. "Wrap it tight. Give me a minute to catch my breath. I still have some swinging left in me."

"You are not swinging anything today, you stubborn fool," Josh said, his voice thick with emotion. He grabbed a roll of clean linen from his pouch and began desperately packing the wound to stem the bleeding, ignoring the foul, hissing smoke that rose as the holy magic fought the dark rot.

Brett and Perberos stood over them, forming a tight defensive perimeter.

"He needs a proper triage centre," Brett stated grimly, his pale face reflecting the dire reality of the situation. "He needs high-tier restorative potions that can purge the necrotic infection from his bloodstream. If we stay out here in this ambient dark mana, the wound will fester and kill him before sunset."

Josh tied the bandages off tightly, his hands slick with his friend's blood. He looked down at the dwarf. Bhel had finally closed his eyes, slipping into a troubled, pained unconsciousness.

Josh stood up slowly. He retrieved his heavy shield and his longsword. He walked the final ten yards up the rocky slope to the very edge of the ridge, looking down into the mountain basin.

Fenn had not been hallucinating.

The basin was massive, easily three miles across, completely surrounded by sheer, impassable mountain peaks. And it was full.

Josh stared down in absolute, silent horror. There were tens of thousands of them. It was an endless, perfectly organized sea of skeletal infantry, heavily armoured wights, and massive, lumbering bone constructs. They were not wandering aimlessly like a feral horde. They stood in rigid, immaculate military formations. Banners woven from human skin and bone hung limply in the freezing, stagnant air.

At the very centre of the massive army, standing atop a raised platform of black stone, was a figure that radiated an aura of pure, suffocating darkness. Even from this distance, Josh could feel the sheer, overwhelming density of the creature's power.

Brett stepped up beside him, looking down at the terrible sight. The mage did not speak. He simply let the impossible mathematics of the scene sink in.

"You were right, Brett," Josh whispered, his voice sounding hollow and defeated. "This isn't a dungeon overflow. This is an invasion. We are five people. We cannot cut the head off this snake."

Josh turned away from the edge of the cliff. He walked back to where Carcan was kneeling over Bhel's unconscious body. He sheathed his sword with a heavy, final click.

His arrogance had nearly cost Bhel his life. He had let his anger over Fenn's death cloud his tactical judgment. He had pushed them into a fight they had no business fighting.

"Perberos," Josh ordered, his storm-grey eyes hardening with absolute, uncompromising resolve. "Help me lift him. Keep his shoulder elevated."

"Where are we going?" Carcan asked, wiping a tear from her cheek as she stood up, her healing magic keeping Bhel stable for the moment.

"We are retreating," Josh announced, his voice ringing with the heavy, unyielding burden of leadership. "We are going back to the main camp, and we are going to tell Torin exactly what is waiting in this valley. We cannot win this war alone."

Perberos slung his bow across his back and moved to support Bhel's legs, while Josh gently lifted the dwarf by his heavy leather belt.

They turned their backs on the massive, silent army gathering in the basin below. They began the long, agonisingly slow march back through the rotting forest, leaving a trail of Bhel's blood in the freezing mud. The mission had changed entirely. It was no longer about hunting stragglers. It was simply about surviving long enough to deliver a warning that could save their world.
