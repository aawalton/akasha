In the pre-dawn gloom of Day 9 in this bloody new life, Ace sat on a boulder just outside of the cave. When the others woke up, they would head out for their first hunt.

Meanwhile, it was his turn to keep watch, and though he kept an eye out for trouble, he mostly stared down at the Crimson Sword in his hands. It was the weapon he had been given to survive, apart from Duskblade Manifestation, and yet he had almost no idea how to use it.

“Swing and hope you hit stuff” was hardly a reliable strategy, after all—even if it had worked thus far.

He rolled the hilt of his Crimson Sword between his palms, the blood-channeling grooves catching the perpetual twilight of the Highlands. Cold stone pressed against him as he sat cross-legged atop the massive boulder outside the cave. From this vantage, he had a good view of the vine-covered entrance as well as the barren hills beyond. It was also close enough that he could drop into the fray if something else tried to eat them.

Strange, really, how quickly he had normalized the idea of a monster trying to gnaw off his face.

Whatever.

This was his life, now, and he wasn’t about to run from it.

From up here, Ace could see the soft rise and fall of his companions' chests through gaps in the ivy hanging over the cave entrance. His new squad of misfits laid in various positions across the cave floor, their breathing too steady to be feigned.

Good.

The granite outcropping jutted from the hillside like a sentinel, offering both isolation and oversight. Perfect for a former scout sniper whose instincts still demanded high ground and clear sight lines, even when all he had was a sword instead of his old Mk 13. The rough surface beneath him still held the day's warmth, a stark contrast to the chill that had settled into his bones since his transformation.

Still not entirely used to his newfound powers, he was trying to figure out how to use Abyssal Armory. Unfortunately, his magic hadn’t come with a manual, and this was going to be mostly trial and error.

Fun.

The sword's weight—still so unfamiliar—lightened briefly as he attempted to channel it into his Abyssal Armory. Ace grimaced, his muscles remembering a different kind of combat entirely. As a Marine, his weapons had barrels and triggers, not blades and hilts. Military training had drilled him relentlessly on rifle handling, pistol marksmanship, and tactical positioning—not medieval swashbuckling.

They didn’t exactly teach swordplay in basic.

A flash of movement at the tree line caught Ace's attention. His enhanced senses picked out the silhouette instantly, even in the shimmering twilight.

Victor.

The man stood at the edge of the forest, his form unnaturally still as he stared directly at Ace.

“Damn it,” Ace muttered under his breath.

Victor took his first steps into the Highlands as Ace studied the man’s gait. The former merc strode toward him with a predatory grace that had become even more pronounced since they'd last crossed paths.

As Victor drew closer, the physical changes in him became apparent. His face had grown sharper, more angular, as though the last vestiges of humanity had been carved away. A silver scar ran from his left temple down to his jaw, the mark gleaming with an unnatural light of its own. His fingers had elongated slightly, the joints more pronounced, giving his hands an almost skeletal appearance. Most disturbing was his smile—too many teeth crowded his mouth when he grinned, and his skin seemed to absorb rather than reflect the faint light.

"Blackwell," Victor said, his voice carrying the same arrogant confidence it always had. "Fancy meeting you here."

Ace didn’t stand, but his grip did tighten around the Crimson Sword’s hilt. “Thought you were dead.”

A lie, but Victor didn’t need to know that.

“Sorry to disappoint,” Victor replied.

“Cute,” Ace said. "How did you find us?"

Victor's smile widened, revealing those too-many teeth.

He didn't answer.

The tension between them crackled like electricity. They'd been rivals from the moment they were both summoned to this hellscape, but something about Victor had changed beyond the physical alterations. There was a coldness in his eyes that hadn't been there before—a calculating emptiness that made Ace's instincts scream in warning.

"Are the others inside?" Victor finally asked with a nod toward the cave.

“Cut the bullshit,” Ace ordered. "What do you want, Victor?"

"Same as you. Survival." Victor gestured toward the cave. "And there’s safety in numbers. Wouldn’t you agree, Sergeant?”

For a moment, Ace simply glared at the former merc. That cocky smile pissed Ace off, but there hadn’t been an overt threat made. Even though his intuition warned him against letting the man back into their circle, Ace had no proof that Victor would do anything.

Reluctantly, Ace nodded toward the cave, giving the man unspoken permission to pass. His stony expression hardened, however, with each passing second.

Victor chuckled as he walked past. "Still don't trust me?"

Fuck no.

Ace didn’t reply, even though the thought echoed through his skull like a scream.

As Victor disappeared into the cave, Ace remained outside, and he wondered if he should head in, too. He didn’t like the idea of Victor walking around his team’s sleeping bodies, while they were unaware of a potential threat trudging through their midst.

A moment later, Ace could hear the distant murmur of Victor’s baritone as he spoke to someone in the depths of the cave. A silhouette shifted behind the ivy, and Ace recognized Tara’s blonde hair as she stood. The others were stirring, and with a wave of relief, he assured himself Victor wouldn’t be able to catch them off guard.

There were no screams from inside the cave, which was always a good sign.

Ace debated heading back there to see who Victor was speaking to—and what they were discussing—but this was his chance to finish what he’d sat out here to start. And what he needed to do required privacy.

Ace glanced down at the Crimson Sword in his hands. He needed to figure out how to use Abyssal Armory before things got worse. He’d been trying to get it to work for his entire watch shift thus far, and no luck. The sword should be stored there—it would be safer. Harder for someone like Victor to get his hands on it.

But with Victor possibly watching from the shadows, now probably wasn’t the time to reveal another ability.

Ace weighed the risks. He'd already delayed too long. He couldn't afford to waste more time, especially now that Victor had returned.

With a determined exhale, Ace turned away from the cave entrance. Whatever Victor's game was, it would have to wait. Right now, Ace needed every advantage he could get.

His fingers twitched with muscle memory, reaching for triggers that weren't there. He'd spent eight years learning the intimate details of modern weaponry—the precise pressure needed to squeeze a trigger without pulling off target, the controlled breathing techniques for long-range elimination, the tactical positioning that kept death at a professional distance.

Those weapons made sense. They were extensions of himself at this point. Squeeze, breathe, adjust for wind, fire. Done.

But from the looks of things, he would have to adapt to new weapons in his new life.

Fine.

Adapt. Improvise. Overcome.

He could do this.

The weapon in his hand flickered as his Abyssal Armory tried to absorb it yet again, but it ultimately re-solidified in his palm.

"Goddamn it," he muttered, his grip shifting uncomfortably around the handle.

His fingers found an awkward, uncertain position against the hilt. As he concentrated on the blade, Ace felt the familiar cold sensation of the shadows wrapping around the weapon. The sword resisted, and moments later, it slowly began to dissolve into wisps of darkness. A chill crawled up his forearm, twisting around his elbow and burrowing into his shoulder—the Shadow Realm's greedy fingers reaching through him to claim the first trophy for his collection.

The broadsword vibrated against his palm, fighting the transition like a living thing. Blood-red runes etched along its length flared briefly in protest, ancient magic warring with his vampiric orders. Unlike the daggers that came naturally to him—simple steel with straightforward purpose—this sword carried history, intention, perhaps even a twisted form of sentience.

"Come on, you stubborn piece of shit," Ace growled, doubling down on his focus.

Shadow energy coalesced around his fingers in pulsing tendrils, probing the sword's defenses, searching for weaknesses in its magical resistance. The metal flickered again, its silhouette blurring as reality and shadow competed for dominance. The crimson blade dimmed, brightened, then dimmed again—like a dying heartbeat surrendering to the inevitable.

Sweat beaded on Ace's forehead. His fangs extended involuntarily, a physiological response to the exertion of channeling so much magic at once. The taste of copper flooded his mouth as his own blood mingled with saliva.

"I didn't survive three IED attacks and a fucking vampire transformation to be beaten by an oversized letter opener," he muttered through clenched teeth.

Eight years of military discipline kept his frustration from boiling over. Marines didn't quit because something was hard.

He closed his eyes, steadying his breathing as he would before lining up a sniper shot. The difference between a marksman and a target was often just controlled breathing. Ace pictured the sword dissolving, bit by bit, into the dark pocket he could feel within his shadow.

This time, darkness bloomed more substantially around the weapon, climbing its length like living ink. The blade began to lose solidity, wisps of its form streaming into his shadow like water down a drain.

With one final push that felt like tearing muscle from bone, Ace channeled the full force of his stubborn grit into the blade, reaching through it and into the shadows of a realm beyond what he could see. The sword shuddered violently, a high-pitched keen cutting through the night air before it finally surrendered. Darkness consumed the steel in a violent implosion, and the weapon dissolved into his Abyssal Armory with a final, reluctant crack of shadow that curled around his wrist before vanishing.

“Fucking finally,” he said to himself.

The sudden absence left him light-headed. Ace flexed his empty hand, the phantom weight of the Crimson Sword still pressing against his calluses.

Ace stared at his empty palm, wondering if he was storing weapons in his own personal pocket dimension, or if he was collecting ghosts—pieces of reality that would never truly rest.

Now, he had to inspect his Soul Meter to make sure he hadn’t majorly fucked himself over.

Soul Meter. It was something he was still getting used to—the idea that his abilities had limits beyond what his body could handle. He’d pushed himself past his limits plenty of times before, but SM didn’t work that way.

At least not from what he could gather, anyway.

He had studied it on his Stats screen whenever he had a moment to himself, and it was starting to make sense. The essence of his Soul Meter hummed beneath Ace's skin like a living current—energy with consciousness, power with consequence. It wasn't just magical energy—it was the quantified essence of his vampiric transformation, measured in cold, clinical numbers that appeared on a stats screen he could already summon with ease.

Exhausted, Ace pressed his fingers against his temples, feeling the still-unfamiliar throb of Soul Meter depletion.

A glance at his Stats revealed the truth:

189/1,125 SM remaining.

Dangerously low.

"Great," he muttered, watching the numerical display pulse an angry red—the System's version of a critical fuel warning.

In all his attempts to access Abyssal Armory—along with the slow regeneration of the Soul Meter—he'd burned through his reserves like a rookie firing on full auto. The sensation wasn't just exhaustion, either. It was existential emptiness, as if pieces of his consciousness were being systematically deleted.

SM wasn't just a resource. It was a currency in this new reality. He didn’t know what would happen if it hit zero, and honestly, he probably didn’t want to know. If he had to guess, it would feed on his HP. And based on his experience in the arena, the way to restore his HP was to drink blood.

“Man,” he muttered again to himself. “HP. Stats. SM. I’m starting to sound like Tara with all these gaming references.”

“Talking to yourself, Sergeant?” a familiar voice asked.

With an annoyed sigh, he peered over one shoulder to find the System hovering behind him with a wide grin on her face.

“That’s a sign of insanity, you know,” she said with mock concern.

“Noted,” he replied dryly.

“I can see that mind of yours racing,” she continued. “You have questions.”

“Always,” he admitted. “But do you have answers?”

“Of course, silly.” She laughed, as though the mere idea of any other option was ridiculous. “But will I share them? Now, that’s the quandary.”

Fair point.

It was worth a shot.

“Explain the Soul Meter to me,” he ordered.

She tilted her head, smirking as she raised one eyebrow. “Come on now, Sergeant. We’ve been over this. What’s the magic word?”

Again, he sighed in resignation.

Maybe he didn’t care enough about this Soul Meter thing to play her games.

“You can do it,” she prodded. “I believe in you!”

Ugh.

“Please,” he said under his breath.

“Yay!” She clapped happily and closed her eyes as she cheered him on. “You did it!”

He gritted his teeth to keep himself from saying something that might’ve gotten him in trouble.

“The Soul Meter is determined by your Intelligence Stat,” she explained.

The System hovered midair, pacing back and forth despite the fact that she was three feet off the ground. She clasped her hands behind her back and circled his rock, as though she were a professor giving a lecture.

With a sidelong glance, she caught his eye. “Because of that, you have higher SM than the others. Smart move, keeping that stat high.”

He didn’t reply.

“The relationship between vampirism and the Soul Meter was brutally elegant in its design. I’m quite proud of it,” she added with a cocky little grin. “Physical hunger drives you to consume blood, which restores your HP. We’ve gone over that, of course. But spiritual hunger—SM depletion—drives you to harvest life essence through self mastery. Meditating, sitting in silence for hours, all the fun stuff.”

“That sounds boring as hell.”

“Oh, my, yes,” she agreed. “Now, Intelligence determines your maximum Soul Meter capacity, right? But it’s Wisdom that governs its regeneration rate. You have to balance both of them.”

“What happens if I run out of SM?” he asked.

“Your magic starts eating away at you,” she said cheerfully. “Basically consuming you from the inside out by depleting your HP. Don’t let your SM get to zero now, Sergeant. I need you to level up a lot more before you die.”

He met her gaze with a glare, but that only made her smile wider.

“That’s what I love about the Soul Meter,” she continued. “When it’s low, it will strip away emotional barriers first. Empathy, restraint, measured judgment—they evaporate like morning dew once your meter drops below 10%. That pesky humanity you insist on holding onto will succumb to the predator within—because the only things that can fill your Soul Meter are vampire blood and time.”

Huh.

Noted.

“Yours is low, Sergeant,” she commented. “At that rate, and with your current Wisdom stat, you’re looking at a full day of recovery time.”

Fantastic.

Soul Meter wasn't just a magical battery. It was the leash the System used to tug them toward moral compromise, one depletion at a time.

He opened his mouth to speak, but he was interrupted by a sound that didn't belong—namely, the splintering crack of massive trees being shoved aside. He stiffened, scanning the forest as birds erupted from the canopy in panicked flight.

A cluster of oaks toppled, crashing to the ground, and through the trees came a living nightmare—a towering entity that merged elements of ancient trees and primeval deer. Its body rose fifteen feet high, its bark-like armor plating its massive form while golden, twisted antlers formed a crown that snagged the first rays of dawn. Glowing pulses of energy flashed beneath its wooden skin, and its emerald eyes fixed on Ace with calculating intelligence.

———

Thornhide Behemoth

Core Racial Trait: Regenerative capabilities; their blood acts as a potent acid.

———

“Oopsie,” the System said coyly as she tapped one finger on her pouting lips. “Did I lure that thing here? My apologies, Sergeant.”

“You—” With a growl, he stopped himself, opting for silence instead of what he really wanted to say to her.

“What the hell?!” Tara asked from behind him.

She stood at the mouth of the cave, her hand holding aside the hanging ivy like a curtain as she stared out at the approaching monster.

“Get the others,” Ace ordered as he returned his attention to the looming threat. "I'll buy you time."

“But how?” the System asked. “Your SM is too low to summon any weapons without going crazy and killing everybody else in a bloodthirsty daze. Too bad you just put that sword into Abyssal Armory, huh?”

“God, I hate you,” he muttered.

“I know,” she said sweetly.

The Behemoth's massive head swung toward them, its emerald eyes narrowing as it processed him. Without warning, it lowered those lethal antlers and charged, tearing into the Highlands with the force of a runaway train.

Ace's muscles tensed, his combat reflexes taking over. Whatever this thing was, it had just threatened his team. The familiar cold calm of battle settled over him as he dropped into a fighting stance.

“Show me what you’ve got,” he said under his breath.
