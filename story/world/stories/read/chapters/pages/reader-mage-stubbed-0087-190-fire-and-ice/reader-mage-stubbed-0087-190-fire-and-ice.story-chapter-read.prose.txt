Merlin’s eyes were dull, his insides quivering as he sat in total silence in the waiting room. He clutched his sweaty palms while his mind tried to make sense of what had just happened a few moments back.

Fire and ice. The images were as clear as day, so he couldn’t disregard them as illusions. And even if he tried to fight back the fact that he had used magic, tagging it as a trick his mind had placed upon his senses in his fit of pain, perhaps borne from his deep desire to be a mage, the stares from the crowd and Instructor Park was enough proof that he wasn’t dreaming.

If he was to be completely honest, at this very moment, he could feel a certain strangeness in his body. His mana channels were no longer the same as they had been when he’d woken up this morning. They felt larger, wider, and seemed to conserve a lot more energy than they used to. As for his mana itself, he could feel it far more than before. Compared to the day of his evaluation—no, compared to even getting the System—his mana had become more dense. It had more volume, and a multitude of knowledge seemed to be streaming through it.

Merlin lifted his eyes towards the floating screen before him, staring at the notifications he had received from the System.

[Intelligence has leveled up!]

[Intelligence Level 40 → Level 41]

That was all. There were no new Perks he had gotten, or some quest he had unlocked without his knowledge that had suddenly rewarded him with the ability to breathe out fire from his lungs and produce ice from his hands. He had simply just leveled up while he was trying to deal the final blow to Marcus.

Merlin knew this wasn’t a joke of some sort by the System. It was real. And when he put the pieces together, specifically what his visit to the Consortium Guild had entailed a few months back, it was fairly obvious that what had happened now had been long coming. And even though, technically, this was all he had wanted a while back, he couldn’t feel excited.

His heart thumped hard in his chest as he dug his nail into his skin.

He was scared.

A soft chime came from the door, drawing Merlin’s attention towards it. Then a voice made its way into the room through the intercom.

“Merlin!” the voice called. It was Nora’s. “Are you all right? Instructor Park said you wanted to be alone, but I insisted. Can I come in?”

Merlin sighed.

The spontaneous effects of the System was something he had always feared, and how he had reacted in the arena only fueled that fear of his even more. The change he was undergoing, he had no idea what it was or how he could control it. What if it was triggered by his emotions or something similar? Pain had made him breathe fire and conjure ice. What if seeing his mates made him do something else he had no control over.

He was too risky to be around, and he was not planning on doing something he would regret.

“Open this door while I’m being nice,” Nora’s tone shifted from the calm one it was a second ago to a stern one at Merlin’s silence. “Don’t make me kick it down!”

Merlin’s nose twitched, but he simply lowered his head in further silence.

He was not letting her in.

“Ada’s here, by the way,” she said after a few seconds passed. “Are you really going to keep us out forever? I want to talk to you. Let’s talk.”

Merlin wasn’t keeping her out. He was just trying to come to terms with the changes he was undergoing. And, maybe, that was him being too childish.

Sure, his body was the one undergoing the changes, but why hadn’t he considered how that would feel to his friends? To Nora?

Merlin shook his head. This wasn’t the best way for him to handle such delicate matters. Isolating himself was not the way.

He stood up with a deep breath and walked up to the door. As he unlocked it he said, “It’s Blue, not Ada.”

As the door slid open, Blue sped into the room and into Merlin’s arms, before quickly climbing up onto his shoulders and snuggling into comfort there. A small smile crept on Merlin’s face as the intense anxiety he was feeling calmed briefly.

“Missed you too, buddy.”

Nora walked into the room, still in her patient’s gown. When the door closed behind her, she immediately asked, “What the fuck is going on?”

Merlin’s lips twitched as he walked back to his seat. “Beats me.”

“Don’t be so nonchalant about this,” Nora started.

“I’m not,” said Merlin. “I don’t have any clue what’s going on. All I did was level up my Intelligence during my fight with Marcus. That was all. The System didn’t give me any warning, and it’s still not telling me anything even now.”

Nora scratched her head. “Mom called Aunt Zuzu immediately after your fight,” she said. “I didn’t know what to say so I had to put my phone on DND. Merlin, do you understand what this means?”

Merlin frowned as his heartbeat hastened; he caressed Blue’s head as a means to calm himself.

“I know, Nora. Fuck, I know,” he said, his voice heavy. “Why is the headmaster not here yet? He knows about the System; if I explain things to him, do you think he’ll be able to keep me safe? I’m scared, Nora. God, I’m so scared.”

“Hey. Hey.” Nora walked forward and sat beside her twin brother, taking hold of his hand. “Listen to me. I won’t let anything happen to you. Remember? I promised. They’ll have to go through me first.”

Merlin appreciated Nora’s words, but what could she do in reality? It wouldn’t take much to subdue her when the Council of Mages eventually came for him. So it did nothing to calm his raging heart. All he could hope for was that the headmaster got here in time and proposed a reasonable solution to keep him calm.

That was all he could do. Hope.

The door to the waiting room chimed again, and this time it was Professor Jung’s voice that came in through the intercom.

“Merlin,” she began. “You have someone here to see you. I’ll be unlocking the door.”

The fact that she had gone straight to the point gave Merlin pause. It was not completely unlike Professor Jung, it was just a part of her that only came about when the situation was serious. And, yes, this situation was tagged as serious, but she had claimed that someone was here to see him. If it was a family member, then surely she would have asked for his consent first. That, coupled with the fact that there was a cold chill crawling down Merlin’s spine as a result of his Perception, made him think the worst.

He jumped to his feet as the door unlocked, startling Blue, while Nora immediately stepped in front of him.

Professor Jung walked in first, dressed in a white shirt and black suit trousers. Then she shifted out of the way and a woman Merlin had never seen before appeared. She was dressed loosely and held sunglasses in her hand; her light brown hair was cut into a bob and her probing green eyes shot past Nora and settled onto Merlin without even a second of delay.

Merlin’s throat clogged as his heart skipped a beat. Blue shifted uncomfortably, sensing Merlin’s fear, but did well to keep his presence hidden.

Behind the woman was also Kang Do-Won, but Merlin was too occupied with his fear to pay his respects.

In the next second, Nora was riled.

“Who are you and what do you want, old lady?” Nora asked, her voice layered with extreme animosity.

The woman shifted her gaze to Nora, and Professor Jung stepped in immediately.

“Nora!” she voiced. “Show some respect.”

“Like hell I would show any to someone I don’t know,” Nora scoffed. “Who are you?” she insisted her question be answered.

The woman smiled, raising a hand before Professor Jung could reprimand Nora once again.

“Greta Kaufmann,” said the woman. “Nora Tyrrell, I see. The records weren’t wrong. You’re extremely hot-headed.”

Nora’s brows twitched. “Why, thank you. Now answer my second question.”

Greta took a deep breath and turned back to Merlin. “I’m here for your twin brother,” she said. “The Council of Mages has ordered that he be brought in.”

Merlin’s heart skipped a beat and then pounded intensely in his chest to the point that it felt like it was trying to escape. A sudden dizziness slammed into him, almost rocking him off his feet, and all his muscles twitched in response. Subsequently, ideas popped into his head.

Getting out of this dilemma was bound to fail. The Council of Mages was the highest denomination in the world of mages. Now that they wanted him, they would have him.

Unless… he ran away.

Merlin’s eyes shifted from Greta’s gaze towards the door as several methods he could use to push past the mages before him and whichever might try to stop him on his way popped up in his head.

“I’ll advise against what both of you are planning,” said Greta, drawing Merlin’s attention back to her and then to the traces of mana leaking out of Nora. He frowned. She didn’t even have her Grimoire present. Was she out of her mind? “Just come quietly, Merlin Tyrrell,” said Greta with a tone bordering between pleading and threatening. “We’re not villains trying to harm you.”

Nora scoffed. “Oh. And we’re just meant to take your word on that?”

“Precisely.”

Nora shifted her attention to Professor Jung. “Are you just going to stand there like some thick-headed bouncer? She says she wants to take Merlin away!”

Professor Jung inhaled softly and said, “I’m aware.”

“You’re aware… You’re aware?” Nora's face squeezed. “What the…” She turned back to Greta. “I’m not letting you take my brother away.”

“That’s not up to you to decide.”

“Then you’re either going to have to rip my head from my neck or bring the heavens down to earth.”

Professor Jung stepped forward with a sigh. “Neither of that will be happening.”

“You stay out of it!” Nora voiced. “Just stand in the corner like you were before and stay out of my way!”

Professor Jung pressed her lips together as a flash of annoyance crossed her face.

“Listen to me, Nora Tyrrell!” she voiced. “We do not have the authority to go against the Council of Mages. Merlin has to go with them. We…can get him back after.”

Nora breathed heavily as she turned to Professor Jung. “Is there any guarantee of that?” she asked. “Is there?” Professor Jung was silent and Nora bit on her lower lip. “Well, go on. Let me hear it.”

Professor Jung’s expression loosened as her gaze shifted slightly from Nora to Merlin.

“Don’t you trust me?”

Merlin’s heart skipped a beat. Before Nora could argue anymore, he stepped forward and placed a hand on his sister’s shoulder.

“It’s fine,” he said, and Nora turned even more red than she already was. “I’ll be fine. I trust her. So, please, stop. There’s no sense in hurting yourself. Nothing good will come out of it.”

“Your safety is good enough for me,” she argued.

Merlin tightened his grip on her shoulder, drawing her eyes to him, then he softened it. With a gentle expression he said, “Nora. Please.”

Nora fought back the anger bubbling to the surface, and in its place found words instead.

“Oh, come on, Merlin,” she began, her voice breaking as she shook her head quietly. “Don’t do this.”

“We don’t have a choice. I don’t have a choice.” Merlin walked forward and stood before Greta Kaufmann. The woman regarded him with a smile.

“You’re a smart kid,” she said and turned around. “You’ll brief me on the changes ongoing within you on the way. Mr. Do-Won. Can I count on you for that ride one more time?”

Do-Won stared at Merlin for half a second before nodding. “Certainly.”

Greta was satisfied with the answer.

With a nod, she and Do-Won proceeded onwards, allowing Merlin the grace to follow behind her, trusting him not to do anything stupid.

As Merlin was about to leave the room, Professor Jung grabbed his arm.

“Stay safe,” she said. “The headmaster has a plan.”

Merlin licked his lips.

He would have preferred it if the man was here, but he guessed that would be greedy of him.

He nodded and turned to Nora once more.

“Tell mom and dad I’ll be fine, okay?” he said. “And Aunt Zuzu too. I’m sure she’s worried.”

Nora stood there with her teeth pressing hard against each other. She didn’t say a word, and Merlin was fine with that. When he left the room, a resounding “Fuck!!” echoed behind him.

Halfway down the corridor, with just enough space between himself and Professor Jung as well as Greta and Do-Won, Merlin turned to Blue. He rubbed the dragon’s head and whispered, “You fly off now. Stay with Nora or my roommates. You can’t come with me.”

Blue purred in disagreement, but Merlin shook his head.

“It’s dangerous,” he said. “I have no idea where I’m being taken, so don’t be stubborn and get off my shoulders.”

Blue hesitated for a bit, but finally succumbed.

With a low growl, he loosened his tail which he had wrapped around Merlin’s arm, as he had intended not to let go, and pushed himself off of Merlin’s shoulders. Merlin smiled at the dragon as he walked out into the Open field.

The arena was nowhere to be found, and so was the whole structure that housed the crowd. Everything was back to how he recalled it to be, just a wide expanse of grass with tall trees painting the horizon.

However, half the crowd was still present, and as he stepped into the rays of dimming sunlight pouring down, they all stared at him, murmuring. First years, second years, and even their families. It was as though they had been waiting for him to emerge, and that made Merlin heave out a deeper sigh. He lowered his gaze, succumbing to the intense pressure pressing down upon him.

It was upon arrival at Kang Do-Won’s car that Merlin finally raised his eyes and glimpsed his friends.

Chima and Hakyun were standing to a corner with pursed lips, a mix of emotions painting their faces, while the rest were spread about with expressions that made it seem like he was a lamb heading to the slaughter.

He chuckled and waved at them, trying his best to shift the whole sorrowful outlook into something less despondent. It didn’t work, obviously, but he assumed that was enough.

He hoped he would be seeing them soon enough.

“Get in, Merlin,” said Greta as she noticed Merlin’s slight hesitance. “We don’t have the time.”

Merlin turned his attention away from his friends and got into the car.

At this moment he realized that he had never been more afraid in his entire life.
