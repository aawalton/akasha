All my allies, to me, James sent telepathically.

It was time for them to strategize again in person for the Victors Tournament.

They had the time now, and who knew how long they would actually get to spend in the waiting area?

The voice of the System’s representative continued discussing the unfortunate losses due to unscrupulous use of Soul Magic.

[Our rules will be tightened next year to prevent a recurrence of these events. You have our sincere apologies for the oversight. We ask that surviving participants not attempt to circumvent the spirit of the rules that apply to the remainder of the Victors Tournament.]

James sensed a tension in the voice that hadn’t been there before, almost a warning. He knew it was directed at him. But there was no threat, nothing explicit anyway.

Of course not. Because I didn't break the letter of the rules. I’m technically okay.

He kept his mouth from forming a smug grin, because that just felt like tempting fate. He had gotten away with what he’d done so far, and he hadn’t even been the only one there using Soul Magic, so there were excuses one could make for his behavior, to some extent.

It was even possible that other people had been using Soul Magic, completely unrelated to the Fisher Empire versus Holy Kingdom conflict. But it wasn’t like this was coming down to a court proceeding or something.

[Please enjoy this respite from the action while we consult with the melee champion about his preferred rule set for the remainder of the Victors Tournament. We estimate we will resume the proceedings in fifteen minutes.]

Oh. Darn, they already have him. Fifteen minutes isn’t very long, either.

As the System representative finished with the explanation, James felt the nonhumans gathering around him. Almost all those former Rulers who had surrendered to him were waiting for his guidance on what to do next. A few had not been strong enough to make it to this competition, but for the most part, Rulers were by default among the strongest in the world.

There was a notable absence, though.

Luna, do you know where the Panther Queen is? James asked telepathically.

My Emperor, she… Luna quickly relayed what had happened to the Panther Queen to the best of her knowledge.

A few seconds later, Samuel confirmed that he had no information that contradicted what she had said.

The Fisher Emperor was taken a bit aback.

So, she’s really gone? James thought. I didn’t get through this without losses after all. She was quite something, too. The world feels just a little bit smaller somehow. She wasn’t that close to my heart, in the sense that Luna is, but still, I felt like I really knew her. What will the other Pantherfolk think? Such a shame…

He shook his head and sighed.

Thank you both for letting me know, he replied telepathically to Luna and Samuel.

He announced it telepathically to all of the monsters and then out loud to those close to him.

“We lost the Panther Queen back in the melee,” he said simply. “Someone besides me was using Soul Magic. I’ve dealt with him permanently, but I just wanted to address that sad news. We’ll do our best for the Pantherfolk now that they’ve lost their original leader. There will be a proper funeral for her when we return, though unfortunately we don’t have a body to bury.”

The group expressed their sadness at the loss of the Panther Queen, then awkwardly segued into discussing strategy for the Victors Tournament.

It was quickly agreed that they would attempt to kill off the Holy Kings as they had been before, with the difference that Soul Magic was no longer a necessary component of that plan. The conversation stalled out as to specific strategies, because James had not actually seen much in the way of specific Skills from the surviving Holy Kings. He had restrained them so quickly that they hadn’t been able to show what they could really do.

The other piece of information that James learned was that the bright light and heat that had killed him had also killed everyone else who had been in the melee. It hadn’t been a localized area of effect attack but something far more powerful that affected seemingly the entire area the melee took place in.

“All right, I really want us to find out what that was,” James said. “Whatever power that is, I want it in my hands yesterday. Besides killing the Holy Kings and acquiring that power, the other priority is that one of us win the Victors Tournament, if possible, of course. There will be a prize for that, and whatever it is, it could benefit our country if we secure it.”

“You mean, that you win the Victors Tournament,” said Damien matter-of-factly.

Several of the monsters nodded with amused expressions at James’s apparent modesty.

“I don’t care,” James said bluntly. “If one of you won, it’s fine. Different people are good at fighting different enemies, so it’s possible there’s someone I couldn’t defeat who one of you could.”

“You are by far the strongest of us, my Emperor,” Luna said affectionately. “There is no place for modesty in a competition like this. It is only fitting that you should place your boot on the world’s throat here and establish your dominance as the master of Earth.”

Mina squeezed James’s hand and smiled, a little hint of mischief in her expression.

“My thoughts exactly, skapi. Show the world who’s boss.” In his peripheral vision, he saw her other hand running over her stomach. She wasn’t showing yet, but it was nice sometimes to think about the life growing in her. Apparently, Mina connected her husband’s strength with that aspect of their future in her mind, even if subconsciously.

She’s probably thinking about how I’ll keep our second child safe just like the rest of our family, James thought a little uneasily. The little girl I saw in my vision… or maybe I’m reading too much into it, because of those damned Holy Kings. I hate unknown variables that could affect my family.

His desire to kill off the Holy Kings intensified. He could see them occasionally in his peripheral vision, talking to their allies, both fellow Rulers and the members of the Holy Kingdom entourage. They were probably making new alliances, too.

Anything as a part of their effort to desperately counter him.

Enough of this grouping together with people I already know. I need to use this period in the waiting area like it’s a second, miniature Summit.

“My primary concern isn’t who wins the Tournament, honestly,” James said. “It’s a nice to have, not a necessity. I think it’s more likely than not that it won’t be me, for the record. First and foremost, right now, we need to go around and try to make new allies. As a secondary priority, I’d like it if we could figure out what happened at the end of the melee. How did we lose? What kind of power was it that wiped us out? As a distant third priority, I guess you can try to figure out weaknesses we can exploit for the Tournament itself. But the more I think about it, the more it’s not something to worry about.”

“We will secure your victory, Your Imperial Majesty!” declared Damien, throwing up a salute.

While the others followed the Werewolf’s lead by also saluting, James just gave him a wry smile and shook his head.

All right, you have fun with the role playing, Damien, he thought.

“You’re all dismissed,” he said, trying not to laugh.

“Any special assignment for me, sir?” asked Jeremiah Rotter.

“Same as everyone else,” James said.

The Emperor stepped away from the group as well, taking only his wife with him. He pulled his hand from hers and wrapped his arm around her waist instead, gently pulling her along beside him.

“I wish we didn’t have to talk to other people,” he whispered in her ear. “But at least I’ll have you next to me.”

She almost literally glowed as she smiled at him.

“We’ll be alone again soon,” she whispered back. “Remember that this chance will not come again for some time.”

So James took the time in the waiting area seriously.

He went around and socialized like his life depended on it. Perhaps it would.

He made informal alliances—plans to be solidified later by Rotter and the other parties’ diplomats or functionaries—and planted the seeds of genuine friendships.

The Fisher Emperor had never been more charming than he was following being in some good fights and having had his social Skills unsealed again. Even some of those who had judged him negatively before seemed to come around a bit.

“My reaction to you was a bit strong,” Cara said when they spoke to her. “Things didn’t really work out for either of us in the melee as I understand it. Perhaps we could form a more… equal partnership going forward?”

“A bilateral defense pact,” James said.

She nodded, and Mina smiled too, though not without a hint of nervousness. It was impossible to forget that this Wendigo was the same person who had massacred huge numbers of people in her Orientation, though Cara now wore her gentle human face.

James also asked questions of the various people he spoke to. He learned that his experience with the ending of the melee was matched by what others had experienced, all over the arena.

A bright light, intense fiery heat, instant death. It had been almost exactly the same in every area, as if someone had an area of effect spell that had a range large enough to wipe out a state—yet somehow with enough power to destroy every other Ruler, including the eldritch monstrosity that had been hovering in the sky throughout the melee.

There was a common theory going around already.

“A nuclear weapon?” James asked. “Really?”

If I hear this theory one more time… Well, hell, maybe it’s true. If it was a nuclear weapon, it only increases the urgency that I acquire it for myself. And given the way the System reconstructed everyone and restored every weapon and piece of equipment to how it was before the melee, that would also mean this guy still has the nuclear weapon…

“I’ve heard that some people saw a mushroom cloud,” replied King Umberto in a dutiful tone, as if he was reporting this because he felt he had to, not necessarily because he believed that.

“Yeah, I heard that same thing, too,” James said, “only I have yet to meet anyone who claimed they saw a mushroom cloud personally.”

“Same here, actually,” said Umberto.

“Which makes sense, even if there was a nuclear weapon, because I don’t know who would have actually survived it long enough to see a cloud. It just reflects how much people believe this actually happened that they’re coming up with the cloud idea.”

“We may never know the truth,” said Umberto.

“Not if the winner doesn’t want you to,” Mina said softly.

It would be nuts if not only is the nuclear thing true, but it was actually perpetrated by the leader of some place known for being peaceful. ‘Greetings from Gandhi… our words are backed by nuclear weapons!’ A little smile came to his face as he thought of the meme.

“If I were the winner, I don’t think I’d mind the world knowing I had nuclear weapons,” said Umberto. “It seemed to do a lot for the respect everyone afforded the United States over the last century.”

James nodded and opened his mouth to agree.

Then the System’s agent spoke again, and the entire room seemed to jump at the sudden interruption.

[Everyone, we have finished consulting regarding the rules for the main part of the Victors Tournament. Now that the process of giving him his prize is concluded, please turn your attention to the winner of the melee, Lev Kazanov!]

A spotlight without an apparent origin landed on a man James did not recognize at first.
