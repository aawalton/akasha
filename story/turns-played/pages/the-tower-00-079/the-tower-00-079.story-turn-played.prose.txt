You set the lantern down on the cold stone beside the maul, unhood it so its honest light spills out clean, and you sit.

This is not the threshold, where you had only your own fire to give and a heatless gold pressing all around that offered nothing back. Here there is real warmth in the world again -- small and caged and steady, the one true flame on this whole climb -- and you reach for it. You feel for the lantern's heat the way you have learned to feel for any heat, and you take hold of it, and you draw it across the little gap into the dark core sunk in the maul's head. You are not making fire from nothing; you are only moving it, hand over hand, the lantern's burning into the cold reservoir of the weapon, and because the heat is the lantern's and not your own the work costs you only the carrying of it. The core drinks. You feel it warm and bank and fill -- low to a steady working glow, a held charge with weight to it again, enough to feed your fire-work from its own store and spare your focus when the next room asks for fire.

You do not fill it to the brim. The well runs deeper than it used to and the lantern is only the lantern; to pour the last of it in you would have to drink the light dry, and you need the light -- to see by, to shield your eyes by, for the truth it shows that the gold up there hides. So you stop while it still burns. The lantern sinks to a lower, quieter flame, dimmer but alive, and the maul rides warm and ready in your hands once more, banked to a solid moderate fire. It will do. It will more than do.

Then you try the thing you came down here turning over in your mind.

You take up the dense winding-drum core -- the heavy tension-wound iron you dragged out of the Colossus's wreck, the soundest weight you own -- and you lash it fast to the loose end of the chain, the end opposite the great head of Burning Anger. Now the weapon runs strange in your grip: maul at one end, dead heavy core at the other, the long chain slack between. You set your feet, you take the chain up near the core, and you begin to turn.

The maul comes off the ground and swings wide, and at full chain-length it is a different animal entirely -- a head of forged ember whirling out at the end of all that reach, the spin building on itself, the momentum piling up heavy and wide until it sings in the air a body's-width past where any sword of yours could reach. It is enormous. It is also very nearly past holding. The mass wants its own way; once it is moving it does not want to stop, and it drags at your shoulders and your stance, hauling you a half-step off your center before you set your heels and wrestle it back. The arc is huge and slow and stupid and you could not place it on a coin to save your life -- but you can feel, plainly, what it would do to anything standing in the wide circle of it. You stumble once, catch yourself, laugh short and breathless on the empty safe stone where a stumble costs nothing, and go again. A wild thing. A thing you would have to learn. But a real one.

And in the turning, with the core flung out and straining at the chain, you feel it -- the other thing in the iron, the one your fire never could read. Under the spin the stored force in the drum wakes against the motion, torquing and pulling in a way mere weight has no business doing, leaning on your turn with a will of its own that is not momentum and not heat and not anything your ember-sense can close a hand around. It is there. It is real, a deep wound-tight wanting sealed inside the metal -- and it is shut. Your ember slides off it the way water slides off oiled iron; reach for it as you might, there is no door. You turn it over in your mind as you turn the maul through the air, and the understanding settles cold and clear: the force is in there, and it will not answer you until you bind it -- truly bind it, the way you once made the ember yours -- and you have not done that, and until you do, it is only a very heavy stone on the end of a chain.
