You will not be back, and you cannot carry it all, so you stop being careful with what you can spend and start being curious instead. The cold bar first. You lift Cold Revenge dripping from the cistern, set both hands to it, and pull — not into an item this time but into yourself, drawing its cold charge in through your skin the way you drew the rivet's fire that day by the water.

It comes in WRONG. Where the ember came home smooth, a thing your body knew and wanted, this is the opposite of welcome — a hard, biting essence that does not belong in you, that fights up the channel and tries to bleed back out the moment it is in. A lesser grip would have let it shock through you and freeze something it should not. But you have the hands for it now: you hold the cold steady, thread it slow and deliberate through the meat of you, and make it show you exactly what it does.

What it does is nothing that lasts. For as long as you push it the flesh goes numb and stiff and strange, a brittle false hardness — and the instant you stop feeding it, it is gone, draining away like water through a fist, leaving not a grain set behind. Your body will not take the cold. The fire it took because the fire was already yours, the same single note your essence has always rung; the cold is the other pole, a thing you can hold in your hand and put into iron but never into the man. Ember already hardened in you everything essence can harden. There is nothing here the cold can add. You let the last of it bleed off, shake the chill out of your fingers, and set the drained bar down — answered, and none the worse for asking. Cold Revenge is an empty cold-husk now, to refill in the water if you want it; and you know one true thing more about what you are.
