The racer's start-line countdown goes off for nobody — three, two, one — and both front seats sit empty, the wheel jerking left on its own for a ghost that already took the first corner clean. Aura flipped it to attract mode an hour ago and hasn't looked back at it since. Awen has looked at it four times. There's a dead spot in the steering column, off-center, maybe four degrees left, and she keeps not mentioning it.

"You're doing the thing." Aura hasn't turned around. She's at the change counter with the takeout spread open, one carton already gutted, chopsticks going. "The face. At my machine."

"It has a dead spot."

"It's had a dead spot since before you."

"I know." Awen crosses to her. The whole room is dark except the cabinets, and the cabinets throw the only weather in the place — red off the racer, a cold blue wash from the rhythm cab in the corner, its marquee light still ticking at the ballast the way it has for months. "That's not a defense. That's a timeline."

The chopsticks come up over Aura's shoulder without her looking. Awen takes them blind; they've done this enough that the handoff just lands. She steals from the gutted carton on principle, which is the rule, the carton Aura opens first is the one they fight over.

"Off the cab, by the way," Aura says. "House rules. Dumplings stay off the Street Fighter cab."

"We're nowhere near the—"

"Pre-emptive."

Awen's bag is on the counter where she set it down, the twice-taped corner of the thing inside just showing past the zip. She turns it so the corner doesn't show. Not the point of putting it away; the point is she looked. The joint isn't true yet — the evening still has ornament on it, and she can't hand it over with ornament still on.

Aura came around the end of the counter a minute ago at half her usual speed. Half. Awen clocked it and said nothing, because you don't reach for a thing somebody's carrying carefully before they've set it down; you let them find the counter. Whatever it is, Aura will race straight past it about six more times before she lets it land. That's fine. Awen has all night and a dead carton to defend.

"Racer," Aura says. "Right now. You're down two in the room and I can feel you not caring, which is worse than losing."

"I'm eating."

"Bring it." Already moving — of course she's already moving, she's at the seats before the sentence lands, dropping into the left one, the good one, the one without the dead spot. "Loser gets the last dumpling taken from them at a time of my choosing."

Awen looks at the wheel in the right seat. Four degrees. She could fix it in ten minutes with the machine cold and would enjoy every one of them.

She sets the carton down and gets in anyway.
