It comes. In the last of the dark it gathers and lunges for the warm thing in the doorway — and you give it exactly what you have been holding back. You do not meet it in the open; you have stepped back into the throat of the entrance, where the stone closes in around you and there is nowhere for it to slide aside, nowhere to flank. You wait the half-breath until it commits its whole long body into the gap — and then you open the Furnace-heart's last fire through your own and throw it all forward at once: one broad wash of Ember poured into the doorway with nowhere to go but through it.

Let's see how the night likes meeting the sun.

The night does not like it at all. There is no dark here for it to be — the fire fills the choke wall to wall and the creature is caught full in the open flame with no slot to pour itself into, and the light does to it what light does to a thing built for the dark. It comes apart. The wave takes it head-on and flings the ruin of it back into the shaft, the pale shape blackening and breaking in the glare, and the long body that meant to kill you out of the dark falls away from your doorway dead before a single claw ever reaches you.

Then the fire gutters down and the dark slides back in — quieter, now, than it was. Whatever you feared up here is gone; you feel it in the air, the hunted prickle lifting off the back of your neck. By the last dim coal in your fist you can make out the wreck of it slumped against the broken stair: a long lean carcass, a tough pale hide stretched over too many ribs, and where its eyes should be, two clouded lenses that seem to drink the little light you have rather than throw it back. Strange salvage, if you want it.

The core in your other hand has gone dark and cold. You spent its last in the wave — an empty stone now, no glow left to give. You have your low-charged mace, your own fire, and the dark above you still to climb.

And the Tower marks the kill.
