Near the front, the two who had been gathering their coats reached the door at last and stepped out into the rain. The bell chimed its small bright note overhead — a brief draw of cold, wet air pulling in at the threshold — and then the door eased shut behind them and the warmth folded back over the gap they'd left. The room sat one table emptier now, its afternoon murmur dropped a shade lower, the slack hour widening quietly through the space. In the back corner nothing broke but that faint, far sound of the bell already fading: the warmth held, the joined hands stayed at rest in the space between the chairs, the rain kept its steady wash on the fogged glass, and the amber light leaned another faint degree toward evening. The room only thinned by the one departure, emptying on its own unhurried rhythm the way a café does as the afternoon goes long — and the shelter over the two of them drew, if anything, a little closer for it.
