You take the chain — the Warden's length, welded to the maul's butt since the grapple — and you work your ember into it the way you have worked it into every tool you have made, binding the iron to your own pole so that it stops being a thing strapped to the weapon and becomes part of it, and so the weapon's well to hold fire runs a little deeper. It is your home element going into plain iron, so it takes and it holds, no question of that. But you work it rough this time — the essence going in wasteful, the join honest but inelegant — and it costs you more than a clean bind would, drawing down your focus and pulling the maul's already-low core lower still to pay for itself. When it is done the chain is ember, of a piece with the rest, and the maul will hold a notch more fire than before. But a deeper well is an emptier one, and you have spent to dig it.

Then you sit. Down onto the threshold-stone, the way back at your spine, the haven's gold pressing its endless soft welcome against you — and you close your eyes and you do not take one thread of what the room is holding out. Not the laid table, not the warm-seeming hearth, not the patient open-handed host. You turn inward instead, to your own banked fire and your own set will, and you meditate the way you have on every floor below this one: drawing yourself back toward whole from your own center, on nothing but what is already yours.

Your body answers. The deep ache of the climb and the long grapple eases, your legs unknotting, the well of your focus filling back up from your own steadied core. But not all the way. Here, still and quiet and turned inward, the haven's pull is a headwind you feel on the last stretch of every breath — not hauling you over, you will not let it, but leaning on you, working hardest exactly when you are most at rest, so that the final measure of wholeness keeps sliding just out of your hands. And because your will is set so hard against it, you read it even as it presses: you feel the SHAPE of the thing's hunger as it leans in, patient and starving and wanting, very much, for you to stop refusing. You learn something true about what keeps this place by the exact weight of how it wants you.

Then you turn that recovered fire down into the maul — pouring focus into the deepened reservoir, reaching to fill the weapon and yourself both to the brim at once. And you meet the wall of it. Every measure of ember you pour into the maul is a measure out of your own marrow; the bigger well you just dug drinks more than you can spare; the two needles climb toward full and you cannot make them both crest. You bank the maul to a moderate, working charge — real fire held, enough to channel hard when you need it — and yourself to near-whole, but BOTH, brimming, is simply not a thing this threshold will give you. There is no fire here to draw on but your own. The haven's gold is all light and no heat, cold as the lie it is.

And as you reach, half-aware, for any warmth that is not yours to spend — you feel it answer, faint and real: the winding-drum core in your pack, the dense iron still holding the slow-cooling warmth of the deep places, the one true heat within your reach that would not be coming out of your own bones. You mark that it is there. Then you let the meditation settle where it has settled, and open your eyes on the threshold — near-whole, the maul banked and warm in your hands, its chain ember-bright and one with it now. The haven glows on, gold and heatless and patient, leaning in.
