You ask it without asking — the pad of your thumb moving gently over her hand, slow, deliberate, a question folded into a touch and left where she'll find it.

She finds it at once. Of course she does; she reads questions for a living.

Her thumb comes to rest over yours — a quiet stilling pressure, not a pulling-away. Your hand stays exactly where it was, held still and warm between both of hers. And then she turns from the fire and looks at you directly — after a whole night of sparing you her scrutiny, she gives you her face, because a question, it seems, deserves it.

"I felt that. The question folded into it — you needn't say it aloud, though you've a gift for saying things aloud, and I'd not have minded if you had." Warm, her eyes steady on yours, your hand kept. "So let me answer honest, since you've earned honest all night. It isn't no. Hear that plainly — it isn't no, and I'll not pretend I haven't felt the pull of you, sitting here. But it isn't tonight."

Gentle, and with no shame in it anywhere:

"You came out of a nightmare an hour ago and asked me to make the world feel real. I'll not let 'more' become one more thing you reach for in the dark to keep from falling back into it. When it's a yes, I'd have it be a clear one — asked out loud, by daylight, by a man sure it's me he wants and not just the nearest warm proof he's still here. You deserve to want it clear-eyed. So do I."

And then, a small, warm, rueful thing, her thumb resuming its slow pass over your knuckles as if nothing between you had moved — because nothing between you has:

"So ask me again when the sun's up and the fire's cold and it isn't a bad dream doing the asking. I'll still be here — that's rather my whole reputation."

The fire holds its gold. The frost keeps to its side of the glass. And her hands hold yours — still, warm, and precisely where they said they would be.
