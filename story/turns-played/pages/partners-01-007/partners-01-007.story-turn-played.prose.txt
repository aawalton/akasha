You don't believe for a second that's everything about her, and you tell her so. Her point lands all the same — held against her whole story, your own shelf is nearly bare, and you can feel how thin the file is.

So you offer what a host has to offer. She's climbed a hill tonight and has a walk in the dark for her trouble; you ask her to stay — a house like this must have plenty of room, and if it came to one bed, you'd take a couch gladly.

And then a small stone of fact surfaces from the empty shelf, unasked: "Somehow I remember that I spent most of my childhood sleeping on the floor. That I find couches more comfortable than beds, if I'm sleeping alone." You turn the memory over, frowning at it. "Strange, to know that and so little else."

"You're right that it isn't everything — no honest account of a person ever is. But it's the true shape of me, and the rest arrives the way it always does, in pieces, over time. You'll have them."

Then she sets her cup down, and her whole attention arrives on you at once — and you discover that everything before this was only most of her attention.

"Now — stop a moment. Do you know what you just did? You handed me the first true thing about yourself since you woke. Not where you are — who you are. A child who slept on floors; a man who'd sooner a couch than a bed when he's on his own. That's a real page, Alan, and it's the first one anyone has held for you. I'll keep it."

Her look stays on you, warm and level.

"And it fits what I've watched all evening: a man who travels light and lands calm, wearing the strangest night of his life like a coat he's owned for years. That wasn't nothing you remembered. That was you."

The fire holds its gold. Somewhere in your chest, a fact you didn't know you were carrying sits down and rests.

"As for staying — yes, and thank you. The walk down in full dark would be foolish, and I'd not leave you alone your first night in a waking house regardless."

And as if the word *staying* were a key, a door eases open down the corridor's first turning — unhurried, certain — and warm air moves through the Hearthall carrying lavender and clean linen. A bedchamber, made up and waiting, in a wing that was cold and shut an hour ago.

Amy looks at the open door for a long moment. Then at you.

"...though it seems the house has answered you before I could. There — a room, made up, that was cold and shut an hour ago. So no: you'll not sleep on a couch in your own home to hand me a bed. The house has seen to me. Keep yours."

She gathers the supper things as she says it — the crock covered, the cloth folded, motion that has never once in her life waited for a lull to be useful in.

"And since I'll be under your roof at breakfast now anyway — in the morning I'll walk you through what this place truly costs to keep standing. You'll want my account of it before the Compact comes up the hill with theirs, and they will, the moment the town sees your window lit. But that's tomorrow's business. Tonight, finish the last of the bread and sleep in a warm house — you've earned the strange day's ending."
