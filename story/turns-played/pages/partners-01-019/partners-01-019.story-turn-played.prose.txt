"I'm following so far. What do I need to know? I feel like whatever it is, we can figure it out together."

Something crosses her face at that — filed, not spent; she'll come back to it. First, the piece she's been holding.

"Then here it is. This is prediction, mind — not gospel — but I've watched the Compact buy three good farms clean out from under decent families, and never once with a threat. They come warm. The factor will congratulate you, admire the house, take the good chair and the coffee — and then, ever so kindly, offer to lift the whole troublesome business off your hands. Buy the title outright, or 'hold it in stewardship' for you, all of it dressed as relief for a newcomer who couldn't possibly manage a manor and hasn't a scrap of paper to his name."

Level now. The hinge.

"And that last is the hinge, so hear it plain: you hold five keys and no deed. The house woke and chose you — every soul in this vale will feel that in their bones — but feeling isn't paper, and the Compact runs on paper. The title is the one thing you can't yet prove is yours, so it's the thing they'll reach for. Not by force. By kindness, with a document already drawn."

She lets that sit exactly as long as it needs to, and then hands you the way through it.

"So — my counsel, worth what you paid for it. Sign nothing this week. Nothing. Be as warm back at them as they are at you — pour the coffee, agree wholeheartedly that yes, the title ought to be properly sorted — then tell them, pleasantly, that it's already being seen to. Which it will be, because that's the true fix: the deed gets papered into your name, properly, and there's a broker-house in Amberford that clears titles and contracts — that is where a deed like this is made real. Get there before their document does, and their kindness has nothing left to reach for." A beat. "A polite refusal isn't a no — it's a 'not necessary, handled.' Gives them nothing to push against. And Alan — I know these people; I've sat at tables with the factor. I'll be in the room if you want me there, or in the next one if you'd sooner meet them as master on your own. Either's a fair choice. But you'll not meet them unbriefed — and not alone unless you choose to."

Then the businesslike account gets set down entirely, and what's left is warmer.

"One more thing, then it's yours. You said 'we'll figure it out together' — did you catch yourself saying it? Day two, and you handed me a 'we' about your own house's business, unasked." Honest, and a little disarmed. "I'll tell you a true thing about me: a seat like that — close in, hands on the real work of one person's life — I've always had to invent for myself and pitch my way into. No one hands it over. You just... offered it, over the washing-up, as if it were obvious." Steady, warm, no production made of it. "So — yes. Together, if you'll have it that way; I'd like nothing better, and it happens to be the one thing I'm best at in all the world. But the noon meeting is yours to walk into however you decide. Tell me what you want, and I'll make it so. What are you thinking?"
