Jessica and Iris made their way down to the stables the next morning, the wind still carrying the cool bite of early morning. It was always windy in the bloody peaks, something she’d just gotten used to.

The path wound along the mountain’s side, curving past the Direwing caves—wide hollows carved into the rock where the creatures nested. Several still hung upside down, wings folded over their faces, while others gathered at the troughs below, snapping and shoving one another with low, guttural screeches that echoed against the stone. More circled lazily overhead, their shadows drifting across the cliff face like passing clouds.

Iris stayed close to Jessica’s side, her ears perked and her gaze tracking the circling shapes above with mild interest.

At the lower landing, four Direwings were tethered down and spaced out near a line of troughs. Saddles had already been secured onto three of them with thick leather straps cinched tight around their chests and wings. The fourth shifted restlessly under the hands of the stable master as he worked.

He didn’t wear a mask, indicating he wasn’t a warrior, but the pale scar across his cheek and down his neck indicated he may have once been. He was old, but he still looked strong with arms corded with muscle. His dirty blond hair was braided back into a short ponytail.

The Direwing beneath his hands scraped its claws against the stone, wings twitching in irritation as he pulled the last of the leather tight.

“Easy,” he muttered, giving the creature a firm pat along the neck.

That seemed to be enough. The Direwing stilled, but as soon as the stable master released it, it surged forward, forcing its way into the trough and shoving one of the others aside to feed.

At the far end of the plateau, near the narrow bridge that led to another caged hollow—the same one where she had first encountered the Umarii—her aunt stood waiting with Korrin and Draven.

“Elva Wyrmscar!” Korrin dipped his head almost immediately when he spotted her. The snarling face carved into his mask didn’t match the respectful tone of his voice. “I see you’ve taken a new mask.”

“Yes,” Jessica replied, her gaze moving between the two boys. They would be her hunting party minus one, for however long this trip lasted. She hadn’t exactly had it clarified to her how long a hunting trip in the red maw peaks tended to last, but she imagined it would be a few days at the least.

“I look forward to leading our hunt,” Draven said. He stood straight, shoulders squared, already carrying himself like this was his responsibility to command.

Korrin gave a low grunt beside him, and Jessica returned a small nod. She then looked to her aunt Helga.

Jessica had once managed to pull some faint sense of reassurance from Helga, but even that had nearly vanished by now. Perhaps, when she first arrived in this world, she had needed stability badly enough to force that image onto her aunt. Looking at Helga now, Jessica no longer felt any of it. She had come to understand what her true role was—little more than a tool for her aunt and father to use in their pursuit of political power. Once, the pressure to live up to that role had filled her with fear, but now that she was finally beginning to find her own place here, that fear had curdled into resentment instead. Jessica didn’t want to be complicit in what had happened to Iris’s family, nor in whatever other horrors Valrok and Helga might try to drag her toward.

Helga inclined her head slightly, the carved grin of her mask fixed and unreadable. “How do you feel, Elva? Ready for your hunt?”

“Ready enough,” Jessica said.

Her attention shifted to Draven. “Confidence is useful, but overconfidence is deadly. There are things within those lands that could kill the four of you, so stick with leading your party through the places you’re familiar with. The purpose of this hunt is to gain levels and to grow familiar with fighting together. It is as simple as that.”

Draven didn’t flinch. “Understood. With a group like ours, I’m sure we can have a successful hunt.”

Helga nodded. “You can leave as soon as Saelin arrives.” Her gaze lifted up the slope. “Where is that girl…” she muttered.

Behind them, boots scraped lightly against stone.

The stable master approached, leading the fourth Direwing by a thick leather tether. Up close, the creature was smaller than the others, but there was something sharper in the way it moved—its wings twitching, claws tapping and scraping in quick, restless beats.

He stopped a few paces from the group.

His eyes passed over Korrin and Draven without much interest, paused briefly on Jessica, then dropped to Iris as his brow furrowed slightly.

“If I may ask,” he said, voice even, “is that beast coming with you?”

Iris’s ears flicked, her posture straightening just a fraction.

Jessica crouched enough to rest a hand against her head. “Her name is Iris. And yes. She is coming.”

The stable master considered that, his gaze moving between them again.

“Can she fly on her own?” he asked.

“Not for long distances,” Jessica said. “She’d burn through her stamina long before she got there. I’ll carry her with me on the Direwing.”

He gave a slow, thoughtful nod. “I’ll put you with the oldest of the four Direwings. Come, walk with it around the plateau a bit. Let it get used to Iris.”

Jessica followed the stable master. He pulled one of the Direwings away from the trough. It let out a disgruntled grunt and tried to yank back to the trough, but it didn’t seem to fight too hard after turning a few steps. The stable master handed Jessica the reins.

“So I just walk with it?”

The Direwing looked down at Iris suspiciously, a back leg clawing at the dirt.

“Just walk. So long as Iris doesn’t make any sudden, threatening moves, it’ll get used to her and fly just fine.”

Jessica nodded and started leading the Direwing as directed, Iris still sticking to her other side.

The Direwing did seem to settle. Its agitated sounds softened into low, throaty purrs as it allowed Jessica to guide it along.

Technically, the Wyrmscar Legacy means I can tame any monster more easily. I haven’t really thought to do anything like that, though. Would it make flying a specific Direwing easier if I formed a bond?

She raised her voice slightly, calling over to the stable master, who was watching from a distance. “Does she have a name?”

He folded his arms and tilted his head. “Not really necessary. Only worth naming one if you start training it young. I’ve got a few of my own I raised that way.”

That makes sense, these things were never meant to be companions, Jessica thought. Just tools.

Her fingers tightened slightly on the leather as she looked down at Iris. She’d hated Valrok for what he did to Iris, but at the same time, she also thought he was an idiot. He seemed to have it in his head Iris wasn’t much different than a Direwing, only stronger and more valuable.

I don’t have to treat Direwings as tools either…

The thought came quietly, without much force behind it—but it stayed.

She guided the Direwing in a slow arc across the plateau, giving it time. Letting it see Iris from different angles. Letting it adjust. By the time they looped back toward the others, the creature’s earlier concern about Iris had almost entirely faded.

The stable master gave a small nod when he saw them return.

“That’ll do,” he said, stepping forward to check the straps. He tightened one along the wing joint, then gave the saddle a firm tug. “She’ll hold steady.”

Jessica nodded, but then looked up as she saw someone coming down the slope. The shocked expression of Saelin’s mask traveled across the plateau before settling on Jessica for a moment. The older girl walked with a casual stride, a single hand resting on the hilt of one of her swords, sheathed in an X behind her.

“There you are. You’re nearly late,” Helga called to her.

“Nearly isn’t late,” Saelin responded.

“It is when others are left waiting,” Helga responded coolly as the younger girl joined the group. “In any case, Draven will be leading the party. I trust you know where to go?”

“Of course,” Draven nodded.

“Alright. Aim for gaining at least one level each before coming back, two is better. But if you need to return before then, don’t consider it a failure. But you ought to stay out for at least a week.”

“With Elva Wyrmscar, should we not aim higher?” Saelin asked. “After all, she gained 7 levels in that time.”

“Going from 3 to 10 is easier than going from 10 to 13. What level are you, Saelin?”

“14.”

“Then if you make it to 16 you’ve done quite well.”

“One more thing,” Helga added, reaching behind herself and producing four healing potions. “You each get one. It is preferable not to use them at all, but in any case, do not waste them. The use of a healing potion ought to be considered a failure for this kind of hunt. However, the loss of your lives is an even greater failure, so don’t be stupid. Now, be off.”

The three of them nodded and pounded their fists to their chests. Jessica was a beat late, hurriedly copying the gesture after them.

Soon they were all mounted on their Direwings. Jessica had to pick Iris up and position her with her paws and muzzle over one shoulder as she sat holding her, the Direwing’s reins in front of her. She noticed Saelin watching, and she knew it must have looked rather funny. “She’ll be better at flying when she evolves more,” Jessica said to Saelin, who scoffed and returned her attention to her own mount.

“I do not like that one,” Iris said inside Jessica’s head.

Jessica whispered back, “Yeah… I know.” She let out a sigh, but then saw Draven’s Direwing surge ahead, its back legs kicking up dust until it leapt off the side of the cliff. Korrin’s followed soon after, before Jessica got hers moving.

Saelin’s kept pace with hers for a moment as she said, “Don’t fall behind, Elva Wyrmscar.” Then she surged ahead, and Jessica was the last to steer her Direwing off the cliff.

It was a little awkward flying while holding the Hell Hound. Luckily, the Direwing was doing most of the work—especially with three others ahead of her that it knew to follow without much direction from Jessica.

“Ah… you doing alright, Iris?”

“I am fine. This is nothing,” the Hell Hound affirmed.

They flew for the better part of three hours. There wasn’t really a way to get Iris into a more comfortable position, and the little bit of extra strain was starting to put pain in Jessica’s back. Really, having Iris so close was comforting in a way. Jessica was still a little nervous about flying, though she was way better about it than she used to be. Not long ago, she would have been screaming the whole time, and while she tried not to stare directly down, she was able to appreciate some of the beauty of this strange world.

The sky was red, with jagged mountains everywhere, though none quite as astonishingly tall as those of the Bloody Peaks. She figured they were flying south, and therefore, she didn’t see the same varying landscapes she’d seen northward. It was all just mountainous, rocky terrain that she imagined would be quite hard to cross by foot.

Then, as she saw the coloring of the rock shift from darker stone to layered red, she heard a whistle from Draven far ahead of them, and his Direwing began going down.

“Have we arrived?” Iris asked.

“Yeah, I think so.”

“Then release me.”

Jessica loosened her grip, and Iris carefully moved herself off her shoulders, then, in a movement that was all too sudden, the Hell Hound sprang free, making Jessica’s heart jump.

Jessica knew she would be alright, but couldn’t help whipping around and accidentally yanking the reins, causing her Direwing to bob uncontrollably. Jessica let out a scream, falling forward as she gripped the Direwing’s back and steadied it. A moment later, Iris was gliding through the air alongside her.

Saelin glanced back once, clearly having heard Jessica yell. Even through the shocked expression carved into the mask, Jessica somehow got the distinct impression the older girl was smirking.I really wish she wasn’t in the party, Jessica thought. It’s way too clear how much she hates me…

Jessica carefully turned her gaze. Iris was absolutely beautiful. Between her black fur and translucent, angelic wings, she looked almost ghostly against the crimson sky. Veins of dim silver shimmered faintly through her wings whenever the sunlight caught at the right angle, but for the most part they remained splayed open, making only minor adjustments against the wind current—unlike the Direwing, which had to flap its wings every so often to maintain altitude.

Another whistle from Draven, and they all sharpened their descent, gliding in a tighter circle toward a long plateau between towering layers of red stone. Jagged pillars rose out from the cliffs like broken teeth, and far below, Jessica could see winding trails cutting through narrow canyons.

Draven led them right down between the peaks and onto one of the wider paths, his Direwing hitting the ground first and then jogging to a stop. Korrin followed right after him, but Saelin didn’t wait for hers to land, jumping free and landing in a crouch.

Jessica was last, and her landing was surprisingly smooth, though she’d have to give most of the credit to the Direwing, and Iris landed in a slowing trot beside her. Jessica looked up, suddenly feeling very small as they were deep down in the canyons with mountains rising impossibly high on all sides of them, blocking out any direct sunlight. Jessica rubbed her arm as a gust of wind sent a chill through her bones.

Draven guided his Direwing to trot back around to her. “It is better to hunt from below at our level. There are skyborn monsters that would be difficult for us to deal with, and are more likely to attack if we are above. We ought to hunt something for dinner and set up camp. I know a good place, and we’ll begin tracking our first Drakewolf pack in the morning.”

“Okay, sounds good.”

Draven looked at everyone. “Do any of you have Analyze?”

Korrin nodded and raised a hand. “It’s only level 2, but yes.”

“Good, I have it as well, but it’ll be nice having someone else with it.”

Saelin walked over, putting herself between the two mounted warriors, one hand—as always—resting on the hilt of one of her swords. When her gaze dropped to Iris, Iris glared back and let a low growl rumble.

“Iris. Calm, girl,” Jessica ordered.

Iris turned her gaze to Jessica more neutrally and sat on her haunches. “Just returning her hostility. If it is not answered, she might think us weak.” Then Jessica was sure she almost saw a smirk curl onto the Hell Hound’s lips as Iris added. “Though we’ve already defeated her once. I suppose she ought to know well enough by now.”

“It is quite a cunning beast, isn’t it?” Saelin asked.

“Her name is Iris,” Jessica said.

Saelin looked around, examining the walls of rock that surrounded her. “I wonder how well she’ll fare while lacking open sky.”

What she was implying was obvious, and Korrin spoke up in their defense. “They utilize their strengths, yet they are hardly helpless on the ground from what I’ve seen.”

“From what I recall, you easily almost defeated the beast, Korrin,” Saelin said. “It was Wyrmscar attacking that let her get the distance to resume her cowardly tactics.”

“So what? Iris is Elva’s weapon. If you think that is not fair, then take a rematch without using your swords. Besides that, she was lower level than you and every other challenger that day.”

“Enough,” Draven said firmly before the tension could rise any further. “We’ll be hunting together for at least a week. If you keep arguing over past battles instead of focusing on the hunt, you’ll become a liability to the party. If we cannot cooperate, I’ll call the hunt off early.”

Saelin clicked her tongue. “I was merely pointing out a possible weakness. Something we ought to account for…” With that, she turned and walked away, muttering a few last words Jessica didn’t quite hear. But it seemed Iris did.

The Hell Hound looked back up towards her. “We’ll need to challenge her back.”

Jessica dismounted her Direwing and used checking the harness as a reason to crouch lower to Iris and whisper, “No, Iris. Don’t start anything.”

“I already know. She will not stop prodding until she is sure she is above us. Or until we make her understand that she is not.”

“Iris,” Jessica hissed through her teeth, but the Hell Hound was already walking away.

Iris looked back for just a moment as she said, “If you do not answer her next challenge… I will…”

Jessica really really wished she could speak to Iris telepathically because she had a lot more to say. But Korrin asked, “Elva Wyrmscar. Is everything alright?”

“Huh? Oh, yeah!” Jessica chuckled as she straightened her posture and pumped a fist. “Just uh… getting psyched for the hunt!”

Draven and Korrin looked at each other. “Psyched?”

Jessica flinched, realizing that was a pretty modern term. She circled a finger in the air, “I just mean uh… getting mentally prepared for battle! You know?”

Korrin nodded. “Psyched… so it is a warrior’s mindset. I shall remember this word.”

Draven gave a small shake of his head before turning his attention forward. “Let's start working deeper into the canyons. The Direwings will stay near and fend for themselves just fine until we are ready for them again.” With that, he hefted his spear and led the way.
