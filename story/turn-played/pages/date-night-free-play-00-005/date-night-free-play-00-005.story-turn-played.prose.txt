The wrapping's been off and on so many times the tape has stopped believing in itself; it gives without a sound. Then it's just the thing in her palm, in the blue light off the cabinet, and Awen makes herself hold it out flat instead of explaining it.

It's a key. Brass, because brass forgives being handled and only gets better for it. The blade she cut herself, by hand, on the mill in the back of the shop, to the pattern of one particular lock. The bow — the part you hold — she spent longer on than she'll say: an openwork wind rose, the eight points of the compass fanning from the center, and between them enamel the exact deep blue the cabinets throw in a dark room. It took her three tries to get the blue to lie down flat. She got it on the third.

She doesn't say what it opens. She wants to see if Aura gets there on her own.

Aura takes it, and doesn't say anything, which is the first strange thing — Aura fills a silence the way nature fills a vacuum, on principle, and she isn't filling this one. She turns the key over once. Her thumb finds the wind rose and stays on it a second, and Awen thinks, she likes the pretty part, everyone likes the pretty part —

and then Aura turns it back and looks at the blade. The working end. Runs the pad of her thumb along the cuts, the specific ones, the ones that fit exactly one lock in the world, and the fast goes out of her face.

"This is your shop." Not a question. Slow — one whole unhurried sentence, and Awen has never once heard her talk that slow. "You made me a key to your shop."
