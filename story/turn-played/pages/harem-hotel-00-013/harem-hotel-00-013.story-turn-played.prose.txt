The warm end of the hall gives way behind you as you go down toward the cold one — past your own door, past the room the wall grew Aria, to where the light thins and the runners end and the corridor narrows to the shut door it has always narrowed to. It has hung there dark since you woke. Aria comes with you, close at your shoulder, and neither of you pretends this is anything but what it is: the far end, and the thing the far end was built to make you earn.

The door is only a door. You put your hand to it and it swings, silent, as it was always going to, and the cold behind it breathes out over both of you. Whatever the Hotel means to test here, it was never the door.

The chamber beyond is bare and pale and colder than the hall, and it is not empty. The Doorward stands up out of the far wall as you cross the sill — the same dead-white plaster the whole place is grown from, pulling itself whole and upright, a heavy blank body a head taller than you and built wide — and it does not wait to be looked at. It turns toward the two of you with the slow certainty of a thing with one job and the mass to do it. And you came through the door to meet it as you are: one man, empty-handed, nothing on you but the clothes you woke in.

Aria steps up level with you — the Hotel that fits a room to its guest has fitted her to your measure tonight, and she carries it like she has decided not to mind. Her eyes go over the construct, fast and honest. "Slow off the wall," she says, low, "but it'll hit like the wall it came out of." A flick of her chin at the pale chest, where the plaster closed over itself and left a low seam. "And there — that's where it shut itself. Might be soft. I can't tell you for certain from here." Then, plainer, for you: "We're the same size as each other now, you and I. Stay off its inside, keep it between us, and we wear it down."

The Doorward takes its first heavy step, and the cold room narrows to the space between you and it.
