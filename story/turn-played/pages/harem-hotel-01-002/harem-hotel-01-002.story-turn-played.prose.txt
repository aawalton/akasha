"First off, you are stunningly beautiful," you say, up at her from the floor. "Second: do you know where we are? I can't seem to remember how I got here."

The words leave you and hang in the warm hall light. She does not reach to deflect them or perform modesty over them; she lets the compliment sit out in the open, received; a gift half-expected and welcome all the same.

"Stunning," she repeats, and keeps the word, turning it over once. "You've no idea where you are or how you came to be here — and you led with that." The beat she leaves is warm, and longer than it needs to be. "I think I'm going to enjoy you. That was fast work."

She comes off the doorframe, crosses to you, and folds down onto the carpet an arm's length away, silk pooling around her. She looks entirely at home on the floor of a hallway, a woman who has held conversations in worse spots than this.

"Where we are." She turns the question over the way she turned the compliment, and for once the answer doesn't come easy. "I don't have it. Truly. I came into this place much the way you seem to have — elsewhere one moment, here the next, no door behind me I can point to — and it hasn't seen fit to explain itself to me any more than it has to you." A glance up the long dim reach of the hall toward the far shut door, then back to you. "Whatever your pane told you when it measured you is very likely the sum of what either of us holds. Two people at the bottom of a thing neither can name past its name. I'd rather hand you that straight than play at being your map."

"And the how of it I have even less, if that's possible — only that my own blank matches yours. You woke with the front of your life torn off; I can't tell you what tore it, or whether this place does that to everyone it takes or did it to just the two of us." Her eyes come back to yours and hold. "But you are not doing this alone — that part I can make true just by staying. And I have always found the not-knowing to be the good part. So we'll call it that, and start there."

She puts out a hand — not to haul you up, only offered, palm open across the small distance of floor between you.
