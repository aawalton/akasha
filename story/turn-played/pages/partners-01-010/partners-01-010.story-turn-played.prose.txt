"Oh — I'm so sorry to wake you up," you say, and mean it, "but I'd be glad for company, if you're willing." And then, because deep night and firelight make a kind of honesty easier than daylight ever does, the rest comes too: "I'm afraid I have nightmares most nights recently — though I'm not sure how I know that either. I just need to feel the world be real for a while before I can go back to sleep, otherwise I'll fall right back into it again. Would you… sit with me?"

"Don't apologize — you woke me from nothing I mind losing, and this is exactly the sort of thing I'm good for. Of course I'll sit with you; there's nowhere on earth I need to be."

She goes to the hearth first. The banked coals answer her stirring the way everything in this house seems to answer her — willingly — and the light comes up warm and gold across the hall. Then one of the good wool blankets, produced from a chest she doesn't have to search for, drawn around your shoulders with no ceremony at all.

"There. Let's have the fire properly awake — a real fire in a real grate. That's the world being solid, for a start."

She settles onto the couch an easy arm's length away, turned toward you, and takes up the other thing — the thing you half-hoped and half-feared she'd noticed.

"And that thing you just told me — nightmares most nights, and no knowing how you know it — I'll not ask you into the dream; I gave you that, and I'll keep it. But I'll say this straight: that's twice now a true thing about you has surfaced with no ledger behind it. The floors you slept on as a boy, and now this. They arrive without a source, and I can see it unsettles you." Steady. Unspooked. "So let me be the steady thing while they do. I don't need to know where a true thing comes from to keep it for you — that's rather the whole of what I do. I hold what's true about a person, sourced or not, and I do not drop it."

The fire takes hold in earnest, and she gives you the world back the way she keeps everything else — in small true particulars, handed over one at a time.

"Here's the world, then — the real one, while the fire takes. It's deep night in the Hearthlands; there's frost coming on the glass and the whole vale asleep below us, every chimney in Amberford dark but the bakers', who'll be up within the hour. Come morning there'll be bread again, and eggs, and a great deal of dull business about roof-slates and taxes that I promise you is as real as anything gets. None of it's going anywhere. You're in a warm house on a solid hill, and the ground under it has held three hundred years." A small warmth, and she settles in for the duration. "We can talk of ordinary things until it feels true again, or sit quiet and let the fire do the talking. Either suits me — I've nowhere I'd rather be at this hour than right here."

The frost keeps its slow work at the window glass, and the fire, properly awake now, fills the hall with the sound of a world holding steady.
