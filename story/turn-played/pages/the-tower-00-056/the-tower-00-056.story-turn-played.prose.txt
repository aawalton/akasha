Then you climb — up the cleared floor, past the dead Sentinel's rubble, to the arch at the head of the stair the guardian died holding — and you go through it carefully, the Furnace-heart raised before you for its dim red light.

Beyond the arch, the floor falls away. There is no floor. You stand at the lip of a vast black shaft that climbs and drops past any light you carry — a lightless throat of the Tower with cold air sighing up out of the depths, dust and old grease on its breath — and far, far above, so high it might be a star, a single thin seam of grey: the way out. Stairs climb the wall in broken bolted flights, some fallen away into gaps; and past where the stairs give out there are SLABS — great flat stones rising and sinking through the dark on a slow, steady beat, passing each other on no machine you can see. You nudge a chip of rock off the lip. It falls, and falls, and you never hear it land. A fall here is not a hurt. It is the end.

And you are not alone in it. Somewhere up in the moving gaps between the slabs, something LIVES — you hear it before anything else: a dry shift of weight, a breath that is not the wind, circling in the black where your small light cannot reach. Then it goes silent, and strain as you might you cannot find it, cannot see it. It hunts the dark, and the dark is everything here, and your eyes were never your strength. But your mind is. Crouched at the lip with the core's glow in your fist, you read the whole shape of it cold: this floor belongs to the thing in the black, and the one thing that takes it back is LIGHT. And there — wedged in the wall at the first broken landing, within a careful reach — a guttered lantern-bracket and a sealed flask of oil, sitting just where a climber would want them. Light first. Then the moving stones and their patient beat, and the long fall under every leap.

You have not set a foot past the lip. You have only looked, and listened, and understood. The way up is a dark mouth with a hunter in it and a single lamp waiting to be lit — and you crouch at the threshold of it, a warm mace in one hand and a banked coal in the other, having learned the shape of the dark without yet stepping into it.
