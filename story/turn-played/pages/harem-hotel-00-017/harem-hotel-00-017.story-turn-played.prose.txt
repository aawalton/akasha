The thing is still fighting its own weight, heels scrabbling off the lost sill for a floor to set on. You do not let it find one. You are inside its guard before it can, everything you have thrown high — your shoulder into the cracked chest, driving up and back to take it down with you. And it goes: its footing tears away and you crash down together, your slight weight and its far greater one, in a tangle on the cold floor.

But a thing this size does not go down alone. As you drive it over its arms come around you — not fast, but vast, already closing before you can roll clear — and they clamp you to the pale mass as you land, pinning one arm to your side, crushing the air out of you. The pain is white and immediate. You are inside its reach now, with no way back out, and the grip is only tightening.

And Aria is there, reading the ruin of it in the half-second she has — you caught, the seam split wide and turned up to her by the fall. "Hold still," she says, and her hand goes past your cheek, so close it stirs your hair, into the open seam a hand's breadth from where you are pinned. You hold still, and trust the aim.

Her claws go into the crack to the knuckle and tear, and the Doorward answers it — a deep grinding shudder runs through it, the split running wider, something structural giving behind the plaster. The arms around you spasm. And the grip loosens: the crushing weight eases back a fraction, slack for the first time since it closed, as the thing convulses around its wound.
