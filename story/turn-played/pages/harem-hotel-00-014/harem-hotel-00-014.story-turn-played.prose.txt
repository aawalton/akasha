"I'll pull it off you," you tell her — "you've got the claws for it, aim for the seam." There is no time to hear whether the plan is good; it is the only one, and a body that size will not wait on your doubts. You break straight for the Doorward, loud and wide open, making yourself the one thing in the room worth turning toward.

It turns for you — slow off the wall, exactly as she said, but the arm that swings round with it is not slow, a whole pale slab of a limb thrown flat at where your head is. You are already not there. You drop back out of its reach on the balls of your feet, the blow shearing the air a hand from your face, close enough to feel the wind of it. That is the gamble: that you are quick where it is strong, that the trick is to be worth hitting and never be where the hit lands.

You give ground and keep giving it, circling, and the construct comes after the insult of you — dragging itself around after the target that keeps sliding off its inside, its back coming round, its blind flank opening to the room. Once its reach closes on the space where you just stood, and you feel exactly how that would have gone had it found an arm to close on. But it closes on nothing. You are three steps gone, and Aria has the opening you bought her.

She takes it without a word. She goes in low and fast at its chest, its attention snarled on you, and her claws find the low seam where it had sealed itself — and this time there is no might-be about it. It gives. Where the warded mass turned everything aside, the seam opens under her hand, and a long crack runs up from it with a sound like a flagstone splitting. The Doorward lurches.

But it is a big thing, and one crack will not bring it down. It wrenches itself off its back foot, the blank head swinging round to find the two of you again — slower now, favoring the cracked side, but up, and still square between you and the door it keeps.
