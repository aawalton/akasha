You raise the two clouded lenses to your eyes, and the world goes strange. The warm gold of the haven — the light that has been pressing at you this whole time, soft and insistent and kind — DIMS, drains, drinks away, because that is what these dead eyes do: they swallow light instead of catching it. And as the false gold bleeds out of the room, what was always behind it stands through, plain and cold.

The hearth is dead. Not banked, not low — dead: a heap of grey ash with no ember anywhere in it, throwing exactly the nothing your own skin told you it was throwing. The long laden table thins to bare boards set for no one, the steaming feast gone to the grey suggestion of nothing. The pale shapes drifted along the floor that were almost the legs of chairs are bones — stacked, and dressed over, a charnel pile the warm light had been draping in gold. And the host, the tall kind hooded host risen at the head of the table with its hands held open to you — it thins as you look at it through the dark, the welcoming shape going translucent and hollow, an empty projected image with nothing inside it, a picture of a host hung in the air where no host stands. Whatever truly keeps this room is not the smiling thing at the table. The smiling thing at the table is a painting of a lie.

And you notice, looking through these cold lenses, that it is not only the room's false light they drink. The maul rides glowing on your back — and through the lenses its honest ember-glow is gone too, flat and dark; the warmth of your own fire, the one sense that has been your truest compass on this whole floor, goes black behind a dead hunter's eyes. These eyes show you the lie by eating every light there is — the room's, and yours along with it.

For half a breath, looking at the hollow haven through a dead predator's eyes, you feel something you cannot place: a prickle that the room is looking back — that somewhere far past this cold hall something deep has felt a familiar kind of gaze open in the dark and turned, a little, toward it, the way one hunter feels the eyes of another. Then it is gone, if it was ever there. The hollow host stands at its bare table with its hands held open, patient, kind, waiting — cold and empty and laid about with bones. The threshold is under your boots. The way down behind you is still open.
