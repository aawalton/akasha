"Aelwyn, I'm so glad you're here. Come in, come in. Be at home here — with us."

She steps fully in, and the brightness comes with real feeling under it.

"At home." She turns the phrase over, gentle. "You should know what you're offering a warden — I've spent my life making home out of doors. In the keeping of green things, mostly. A roof asked to be home is a new one on me." A warm glance goes round the lit hall. "I'll take it kindly. And carefully — the way you'd take any gift too large for the asking."

She offers the basket to Amy with a gardener's small pride, greens crisp and arranged.

"Amy — for the kitchen, and gladly. The last good cut before the cold, and three rooting cuttings besides." A beat, warden-courteous, the crown-braided head inclining. "We met at a gate this morning and I've thought of you as the steward since. Tonight I'd rather start again properly, if the evening allows it."

"Aelwyn — welcome. Come in properly; you're the first guest this house has had in three generations, and it's been practising being glad all afternoon." Amy takes the basket with real care and looks into it, the keeper honoring the gift. "Oh — now these I'll do right by, I promise you. The greens go on last and simple, dressed with nothing that would argue with them; a gift like this gets cooked back to the giver with respect, or not at all." She lifts the damp-wrapped cuttings gently. "And these — three cuttings that want rooting before the cold. You've handed them to the right house, and I'll tell you why plainly: I only met that walled garden this morning, and I lost my heart to it on the spot. These'll be in good ground by tomorrow's dark — you have my word as its steward. It seems I'm keeping your garden now, which means I very much hope you'll keep coming to it." And then, warm and direct: "And no more walls, Aelwyn. You climbed one to tend that ground unthanked for years — I found your hand in it, I know. There's a lamp in the gate for you now, and a door that opens, and a standing welcome behind it. You come the front way from here on."

She ushers her through — the laid table glowing, arms open, exactly as planned.

"Come through — sit. That's your place, by the fire; a gardener in from the cold gets the warm seat. A rule I've just invented and mean to keep." And as Aelwyn takes the room in, Amy sets one thing down gently, unpressed. "Alan said 'be at home here, with us,' and I'll not gild it or press it on you — you've only just come through the door, and you owe this house nothing tonight but your appetite. So here's the small honest version: you're welcome at this table as long as you'd like a seat at it, on no terms at all. Tonight you are simply our guest, and a very wanted one. Anything larger than that, the evening can decide for itself, in its own time."

The three of you settle, and Amy pours, and serves the greens to their place of honour.

"Right — one word before we eat, because a first meal deserves marking and then no more speeches." She raises her glass. "To the first dinner Hearthholt has given in three hundred years — and to the three of us at its table to eat it. May the house be glad, may the greens be worthy of the gardener, and may this be the first of a great many." A warm, easy smile, nothing heavy. "There — that's the whole ceremony; the rest is just supper and good company, the only ceremony that ever mattered. Aelwyn — the greens are yours; take them first, and tell me honestly if I did right by them. I'll trust a gardener's verdict over my own any day."

Aelwyn takes the greens first, as bidden. And over the first of the meal, she gives the table something better than a verdict.

"I'll tell you where I was, two nights ago." Quiet, unhurried. "Up in my fold, walking last patrol, and this house — dark my whole tenure, dark as a held breath — took light. Window by window. I stood in the trees and watched it burn gold, and so did half the vale from wherever they stood." Her emerald eyes move between the two of you, honest, unsolved. "I came down that path today thinking I'd find out what woke it. Instead I'm sitting at its table being told to be at home." A short warm breath, not quite a laugh. "It's been a strange two days. I find I don't mind them."

The fire keeps its steady gold, the candles hold their flames unbothered, and the house around the three of you is quiet in the way of a thing listening gladly.
