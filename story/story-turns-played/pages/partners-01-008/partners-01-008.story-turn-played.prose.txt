The evening folds itself toward goodnight, and you find you don't want to leave it unmarked.

"Thank you, Amy. Without you tonight I'd be… considerably more confused, I suppose." And then — because it's true, and because asking first is apparently a thing your hands remember even when nothing else surfaces: "Would you mind a hug goodnight? I know we only met today. I already feel close to you somehow."

"I'd mind more if you hadn't asked at all — so yes. Gladly." A warm beat, and something crosses her face that the evening hasn't shown you yet. "That's twice tonight, you know — the chair, and now asking first. I spend my days holding the door for other people; it's a rare thing to stand on this side of the small kindnesses. You've a gift for it you don't even remember having."

She rises, and the hug is exactly what you'd have appraised it would be: brief, solid, meant — a hug from a woman who does not do anything as a formality.

"There. And don't go apologizing for feeling close — it isn't a strange thing, it's a true one. We met at a door that opened itself and broke bread in a house that woke for you; if a night like that doesn't earn a little closeness, nothing on earth does. I felt the weight of it too."

She steps back, gathers her lantern from the long table — unlit now, unneeded in a house that lights its own way — and pauses at the corridor's first turning, where lavender and clean linen wait behind an open door.

"Now sleep. I'm one room away, and I'll be here come morning. Goodnight, Alan."

The hearthstone banks itself to a low, patient gold, and the warmth of the hall follows you like a hand between the shoulders.
