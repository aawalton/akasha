The false floor goes to nothing under your heel.

You feel it drop — the painted-over ground you crossed a dozen times peeling away into open air behind you — and you do not think, you move, driving off your legs the way you drive off them on a bad scree slope: three hard lunging strides across a floor that is coming apart even as you cross it. The gold dies as you run. And the reason your eyes were shut dies with it — nothing left to lie to you now, nothing left to keep them closed against — and so they open, on their own, for the first time since the door of this place.

There is no haven. There was never a haven. What you run across is a bare stone den, low and wet and reeking, its floor a midden of everyone who ever sat down to rest here — packs and boots and long bones dressed in nothing now that the gold is gone. Ahead of you, where the warm onward door used to hang its promise, there is a plain stair cut up into the dark, cold air pouring down it. You reach it as the last of the lie lets go, and you get your boots onto stone that holds — stone that was under all of it the whole time — and the drop you never saw yawns shut behind you into black.

The thing you killed lies where it fell: lean and grey and unmoving on the wet rock. Near it, spilled from the wreck of the seat, two things that did not die with the haven — a clouded lens the size of your palm, and a heap of folded light gone slack and grey, the woven stuff of every warm lie this floor ever wore, lying there now like a shed skin.

You have cleared it, and the tower marks the crossing the way it always has. But you are shaking with spent adrenaline, your legs are water, and the fire in your maul is a dead black coal.

So you go down to the foot of the stair, out of the reek, onto the first bare step, and you sit, and you let yourself stop.

You let it all come back. Nothing here is pulling at you to sit and stay, nothing to refuse — only stone and dark and the draft off the climb above, and the rest is only rest. You breathe. The shaking settles out of your hands. The water goes out of your legs and the strength comes back into them; the deep well of your focus fills slow from its own bottom, up and up to the brim, until you are full of it again, clean and ready to spend. The ache the days put in you drains away, and when you finally lift your head, you are your own again, and full.

The maul is not. It lies across your knees dead, its heart a spent black coal that sitting will not relight — a made thing's price. The only flame you own to pay it hangs hooded and lit at your belt. A small cold weight to carry up out of a floor you walked out of alive.
