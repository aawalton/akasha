The bell sounded over the door as the rain followed them in — a gust of cold and wet at their backs, then the warmth closing over it. Alan shook the water from his coat; beside him Abby pushed the hood back from her hair, both of them blinking into the low gold light and the smell of espresso. The afternoon murmur barely lifted to note them. Across the room, past the half-full tables and the idle hiss of the machine, two worn armchairs waited in the back corner, angled toward each other as if the room had been holding them. Abby caught it first and tipped her head toward it. They crossed the floor and settled in, the rain a grey curtain on the windows beside them, the city blurred and far away — and for a moment neither of them said anything at all.
