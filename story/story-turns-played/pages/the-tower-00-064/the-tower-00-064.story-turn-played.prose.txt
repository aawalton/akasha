You have one thing left to spend and a use for it, so you spend it. Kneeling at the pyre with the last of your focus cool and low in you, you set to binding — pushing your own ember out of yourself and down into iron, the way you have learned to, only now you do three at once and do not stop to rest between them.

The hammer-head first, to finish what you started. The nascent ember you woke in it before is still there, half-set; you take it the rest of the way, drive the fire down into the grain and seat it, and feel the head go from iron-that-holds-some-warmth to a thing that is ember in its bones — done, bound, only wanting a haft. The brazier-stand next, the old iron basket that has held your every fire: it takes the bind easy and grateful, same as your own pole always does, and you feel it learn to keep a coal of its own and wake its own flame.

Then the cold bar — the one that was Cold Revenge, that you bit with the swarm's cold and have carried ever since. This one fights you. The old cold-bind is still in it, the other pole, and when your ember goes in the two grind against each other like wrong-cut gears. But the cold was only ever a crude grip, a thing that bled and needed water to hold at all, and your fire is HOME in iron — so you simply pour harder, and the ember does not negotiate with the cold so much as BURN IT OUT, scouring the old binding loose and taking its place clean. The bar goes from a thing that bites cold to a thing that holds your fire, and you understand a new edge of what you are: that you can walk into a crude opposite bind and evict it, make it yours. The cold is gone out of it. It is an ember rod now, like everything else in your hands.

That is the last of your focus, and you let it be. You heap what fuel you have left onto the brazier — the whole remainder, holding nothing back — and you set every ember thing you own into it: the mace, the rivet, the new-bound head and rod, the brazier-stand alight under all of it, and the Furnace-heart down in the heart of the coals. Then you sit, and you go still, and you meditate while the fire works.

Time runs out long and quiet by the water. The pyre eats its fuel and the metal drinks the fire, and one by one you feel them come up to full — a low chorus of charged ember all around you, every battery topped, the mace heavy with heat again, the Furnace-heart a small banked sun. And you fill too: the cool well of your focus rising back to its brim, the ache going out of your limbs, the last of the cold-burn from before closed over and gone. When you open your eyes the fuel is spent to ash and the fire is sinking, but you are whole and brimming and surrounded by charged iron, gathered and ready in the warm dark by the cistern — and the long black shaft of the next floor is still up there, waiting, whenever you choose to rise.
