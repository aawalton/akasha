Behind the counter, the espresso machine — idle now for a long stretch — bled its held pressure down. A low soft sigh slipped out of it, and after the sigh a faint settling tick of cooling metal, the small involuntary sound a room makes when nothing has called on it in a while. It carried no further than the front of the floor. It reached the back corner not at all. There the warmth held; the joined hands stayed at rest in the space between the chairs; the rain kept its steady wash on the fogged glass; and the deep amber light leaned another faint degree toward evening, the shop settling deeper into its own slack-hour stillness around the two of them.
