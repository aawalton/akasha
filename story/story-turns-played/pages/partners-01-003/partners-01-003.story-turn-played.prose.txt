You walk back up the rise toward the house.

The switchbacks are longer than they looked from the fork — hedged in close with that white-blooming something, the flowers pale as paper in the dark and the scent of them following you around every turn. By the third switchback the town lights are gone behind the hedge and there is only the climb, the two moons, and the one lit window somewhere above you, keeping its own counsel.

The iron gate at the top is locked. The newest key on the ring turns it smooth as if oiled last week — first of the five to find its door.

Beyond the gate the path runs up through a dark garden toward the manor's great door: dark timber under iron bands, older than the gate, older than most things you have appraised. The lit gable window burns high above it, patient. You are still a dozen paces from the door when — from down the path behind you, before your hand has finished deciding anything about the knife — a woman's voice, pitched to carry and to calm:

"Don't turn quick — I'd rather you heard me than found me. I'm coming up behind you, and my hands are full of supper, not trouble."

You turn slow. A lantern rounds the last switchback and climbs toward you, and the woman under it arrives a moment later: young, her breath quick from taking the hill fast, a covered basket riding one hip, the lantern held considerately away from your face. Put together in the way that never announces itself — good wool, good boots, nothing worn for show. She stops a polite few paces off, on the far side of the gate you just came through, and lets you look at her for exactly as long as you need to.

"Amy Laurens. Up from Amberford. I keep this house's affairs — the taxes, the gutters, the chimneys swept for nobody — and have done for years, though it's never once lit a window back at me until tonight."

Her eyes find the iron ring of keys in your hand and rest there a moment — something moving behind them that is not fear — and then come back up to your face. She waits, exactly as patient as the glass pane was.
