You are still inside her, still wrapped in the warmth and the after of it, when the two of you become — for the first time — two people reading the air at once.

She watches her letters; you watch yours. Hers hang where she can see them and you cannot; yours where you can and she cannot. For a moment it is almost absurd, the pair of you gone still and careful over each other's shoulders at nothing the other can find — and you feel the small tremor in her chest of a laugh she does not quite spend.

"Well," she says, low, her voice not yet all the way back to steady. "It seems we are both being asked to be generous." Her eyes track her pane, and her face keeps snagging on it — the wonder, and under the wonder that unnamed edge. "It paid me no mind before now. I have lived a long, long time, and I do not know the first thing about being wanted by it." Then the old warmth finds its way back into her mouth, even here. "You've had longer at this than I have. Go on — show me how it is done."

So you look at your own.

It is not reading you now. It is holding something open, the way a door holds open, and waiting on you to answer. You have had a single night of her, and already you could name a dozen things about her you would not give back — the way she reads a room down to its floor, the way she takes the tempo of a thing in her hand and slows it until it aches, the plain unarguable force of her that bends a night around itself without seeming to ask for it.

You turn your attention full to the pane, and see what it wants of you.
