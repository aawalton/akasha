"What do you suppose that shut door wants of me?" You say it half to her, half to the dark down the hall — thinking aloud more than asking.

She doesn't answer right away.

Your thumb moves over the back of her hand — the same slow stroke she gave you a moment ago, returned — and her hand turns a fraction in yours, pressing back into the hold: the ball come back over the net. Then the corner of her mouth deepens, and the look she gives you holds more frank interest than it did a breath ago.

"Now that," she says, "is exactly the right question — and I don't have the answer to it. That's the honest place to start." She holds a beat, unhurried. "And here's the truer thing: even if this place leaned down and whispered what waits behind that door, I don't think I'd pass it to you. Tell a man what's coming and he spends the whole walk bracing for the thing you named — and misses what's actually there. I wouldn't want to take that from you."

Then her gaze drops from yours for a moment and comes back, and she leans the smallest degree closer. "I'll give you one thing anyway, because I find that I want to — and hear me say it plain: I've no right to promise this. But whatever that door asks of you, I think you'll meet it. I've known you the length of one hallway, and I already believe it."
