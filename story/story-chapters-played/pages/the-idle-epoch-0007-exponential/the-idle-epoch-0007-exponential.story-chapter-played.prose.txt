Three vine constrictors. Same species, same level range, same attack pattern. She killed them one at a time, timing each engagement against the internal clock she had been running since she picked up a sword.

Same species. Same level. Same attack pattern.

Faster.

A lifetime of competitive fencing had trained her to feel timing differences measured in hundredths of a second. The aura's +15% idle efficiency was translating into something like +8% active combat performance -- not enough to matter in any single fight, but enough to add up over an eight-hour grinding day. Fewer fractions of seconds wasted between kills. More kills per hour. More XP. More of the one thing she needed and could not get enough of.

She stood in the clearing, sweat cooling on her neck, the canopy filtering alien light across her shoulders, and thought about what that meant.

Mira Vasquez did not idle. Idling was for people who could not fight. Idling was surrender dressed up as efficiency. She hated it a little, on principle.

She also could not deny what it meant: for the first time in three months, her XP curve had moved. Not by much. But it had *moved*. The wall she had been beating her fists against since Level 23 had developed a crack, and the crack had Callum Voss's name on it.

She sheathed her saber and walked back toward the warehouse, moving faster than she had on the way out.

"Chunk is going to need repairs after running the Shardfield twice daily," Jin said. "His structural integrity is not built for sustained Tier 2 farming. If he falls apart mid-session, we lose half our Residuum income."

"I will build him a repair script. The Enhancement Construct can optimize his self-repair cycle."

Jin blinked. "You can do that? Optimize a *summon* through your construct array?"

"Thread synergy. Your summons operate within my loop while they are in the Shardfield. The Enhancement Construct sees them as components of the system." Callum paused. "That is not an insult."

"I didn't think it was." Jin's grin came back, wobbly but genuine. "Okay. Twice-daily Shardfield runs. Full Residuum extraction. And I want to try something with Pixel's scouting pattern -- if I can extend the familiar's perception range by routing it through your Loop Sight --"

"Later. Planning tomorrow. Tonight we rest."

Jin gave him a look. "You never rest."

"Tonight I rest." Callum glanced at his status screen, at the Level 17 and the numbers that were still climbing in the background, the compound loop cycling even now, even during this conversation, generating Essence with every pulse of the Enhancement Construct. "The loop does not sleep. I can afford to."

---

The warehouse settled into quiet.

Jin fell asleep on the floor again, his beanie over his eyes, Pixel nestled in the crook of his arm. Chunk stood near the eastern wall like a faithful boulder, its stone body grinding softly as it self-repaired in the ambient Flux. Mira had retreated to her catwalk cot, but Callum could tell from the quality of her silence that she was not sleeping. She was thinking. He knew the difference now -- her breathing changed when she slept, deeper and slower, the controlled rhythm of an athlete who had trained herself to recover efficiently. When she was awake, her breathing was shallower, alert, the pattern of someone who still reached for her saber in the dark.

Callum sat at his workbench and opened his Loop Analysis to its new Level 5 capability. The world shifted.

Through the upgraded analysis, the compound loop was no longer an abstraction rendered in colored threads. It was a *system* -- visible, readable, its processes laid bare in a level of detail that made his previous Loop Sight look like looking at a machine through frosted glass. He could see the Enhancement Construct's optimization algorithm expressed as nested conditional logic. He could see the timing synchronization between threads, the packet structure of shared gains flowing through the synergy channels, the recursive improvement cycle expressed as a function that called itself with incrementally better parameters each time.

It was beautiful. The way a well-designed production line was beautiful -- not aesthetically, but structurally. Every component in the right place. Every process flowing at maximum efficiency. Every output feeding into the next input with minimal waste.

And it was visible.

That was the problem.

Callum focused his new Loop Analysis outward, past the warehouse walls, toward the Foundry District. He could see other players' automation setups now -- the new Level 5 capability. They appeared as faint threads in his augmented vision, dim compared to his own construct array, simple compared to his compound loop. A neighbor's basic gathering construct showed as a single green thread, looping between two waypoints with no branching logic. A combat-focused idle setup three blocks away appeared as a red thread with a two-skill rotation, efficient for its simplicity but fundamentally linear.

And between those dim threads, moving through the Foundry District in a pattern that was too regular to be random, he could see something else: two sets of paired threads, bright and purposeful, following patrol routes that converged on his warehouse from opposite directions.

Scout teams. Iron Vanguard.

They were not hiding. They did not need to. They were Level 25 at minimum, judging by the complexity of their movement scripts, and they carried the unconscious confidence of people who had never needed to be subtle because the weight of the largest guild in the Detroit Barrier Zone stood behind them.

Callum watched them circle his warehouse and felt the timeline contract.

Eight days. Maybe fewer.

He closed Loop Analysis and looked at the Enhancement Construct. It pulsed its steady rhythm -- cycle 51 now, each cycle faster than the last, each optimization sharper than the one before. The compound loop was a machine that made itself better at making itself better. It did not care about politics. It did not care about scout teams. It did not care about Drake Morrison or the Iron Vanguard or the fragile structures of power that governed who was allowed to accumulate and who was not.

It only cared about the numbers. And the numbers were climbing.

Callum opened his field notebook and wrote the day's final entry.

**Day 3. Level 17. Daily Essence: ~5,100 and accelerating. Thread synergy active. Loop Analysis 5 operational. Vanguard scouts confirmed.**

**The Broker says they are watching because they cannot explain me.**

**Let them watch.**

**We are not hiding. We are not slowing down.**

**The curve does not wait for permission to bend.**

He underlined the last line twice. Then he closed the notebook and lay back on his cot.

On his shoulder -- his collar, now -- Pip pulsed. Slow, warm, steady. Two beats. Three. A pattern that was almost a rhythm, almost a communication, almost the first syllable of a language that did not yet exist.

"Nine skill points," Callum murmured. "When the time is right, Pip. When the time is right."

The compound loop cycled. The Enhancement Construct pulsed. The thread synergy hummed. Somewhere in the Foundry District, two scout teams circled a warehouse they could not understand, reporting numbers they could not explain to a guild leader who did not believe in the mathematics of idle growth.

And in the center of it all, a Loopweaver with dark circles under his eyes and a familiar made of blinking light closed his eyes and let the exponential curve carry him into sleep.

The numbers climbed.

They did not stop.
