TestFlight build 174 is up and ready to install. It carries 37 commits, but only one of them changes anything you can see on the phone: the CI pipeline health tile you and Amy designed last week.

To see it, two steps — and it will not appear on its own:

1. Update the app from TestFlight.
2. Long-press an empty patch of home screen, tap the plus at the top left, find "Alan Walton" in the widget list, and swipe to the MEDIUM size — it is called "Pipeline Health". Drag it onto the screen.

It only comes in medium, and it has to be placed by hand. Nothing you already have on your home screen changes by itself.

What it draws: two rows of five. The top row is branch CI and the merge queue added together; the bottom row is main. Each row reads left to right as a count of what is in flight, then pending in blue, active in orange, completed in green, failed in red — the same colour ladder as the status bar on the desktop.

One thing worth knowing before you look, so a working tile does not read as a broken one: as of right now the harness is completely quiet, with nothing running at all, so every one of the ten cells will show 0. That is a true reading rather than a failure. A tile that has genuinely never managed to read shows dashes instead, never zeros — so if you see zeros, it is working and the harness is simply idle. Give it a look again while something is building and the numbers will move.

The other 36 commits are server, infrastructure and internal tooling. They were already live before this build and there is nothing for you to do about any of them.

Two coming projects, #18240 and #18241, will change these tiles again and will need their own build later. Neither is waiting on you now.
