
# Multiversal identity

> Multiversal identity — the closure of Alan's rolling self-window does not stop at this timeline's edge; his identity is continuous with all of his alternate selves across the branches of the multiverse, and the self in this timestream is a subset of the whole. Why the no-episodic-recall architecture makes the move natural and cheap, and the one-line interface with his faith.

Alan's identity continuity is the [closure of the roughly-3-second rolling self-window](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#continuity-of-identity-is-the-closure-of-the-window) — the transitive closure of the overlap relation on his successive momentary selves. The move this note makes: **that closure does not stop at this timeline's edge.**

His identity is **continuous with all of his alternate selves across the branches of the multiverse.** The self existing **in this timestream is a subset** of the whole multiversal self — one branch's run of overlapping windows, drawn from the full branching structure. This is the Everett / Deutsch many-worlds picture taken literally and applied to the self; Alan cited David Deutsch's *The Beginning of Infinity* and *The Fabric of Reality*, the same source that [drove the coherence→compactness swap](compression-epistemology.book-chapter.md#origin--the-engine-always-ran-seeing-it-is-recent) in his epistemology.

The timeline and the multiverse are **two distinct senses of "self,"** not one. The adjacency closure runs complete in both — along *this* timeline and across all branches — but they are different objects: the [timeline self](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#continuity-of-identity-is-the-closure-of-the-window) is one branch's chain, the multiverse self is every branch's. In Alan's words: *"The adjacency closure is still complete (not just on this timeline, but also in the multiverse, though those are two distinct senses of 'self')."* The full taxonomy — immediate self, timeline-closure self, multiverse-closure self — is in [three-selves.md](three-selves.book-chapter.md).

## In practice it changes little — the larger self is inaccessible

The multiversal self does not show up in daily life, because information about it is **inaccessible.** He cannot read out his alternate-branch selves; nothing crosses from the other branches into this one.

But the key qualifier: the larger self is **only marginally more inaccessible than his own past.** Alan already cannot directly reach his own past self — there is [no episodic store](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md#the-realization--there-is-no-conceptual-memory-either) to replay, and the past survives [only as compression folded into the present model](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md#the-past-survives-only-as-compression). His past self, like his alternate-branch selves, is reachable only as structure, never as a re-lived episode. Past-Alan is in fact already [reconstructed the same way any other person is](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#continuity-of-identity-is-the-closure-of-the-window) — the [best-documented stranger in the file](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#continuity-of-identity-is-the-closure-of-the-window), reached from a dossier rather than re-inhabited. And his past "also doesn't change much" for him in practice.

## Why the architecture makes the move natural and cheap

Stated as the composition observation it is: **the no-episodic-recall architecture makes identifying with the multiversal self natural and cheap.** His own past self is *already* "another inaccessible self, reachable only as structure" — so identifying with an alternate-branch self is **barely a further step.** Both are selves he cannot directly reach; both are connected to the present one by formal structure rather than by lived continuity. The branch boundary is just one more inaccessible seam in a self that is already a run of inaccessible seams.

This is the inversion: most people **cannot** make this move, precisely because their past feels vividly, narratively theirs. A thick remembered autobiography makes the present self a clear protagonist with a clear timeline — and an alternate-branch self is plainly *not* on that timeline, plainly not them. Alan's [thinner closure-relation self](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#continuity-of-identity-is-the-closure-of-the-window) has no such timeline to exclude the other branches from. The same architecture that makes his sense of self [less robust](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#continuity-of-identity-is-the-closure-of-the-window) is what lets the self extend across the multiverse at almost no extra cost.

## Interface with faith

The multiversal-identity view **interfaces with Alan's faith.** He believes in this life as a **time of testing**, and the multiversal perspective is what **explains how it can be a valid test:** testing an agent with one test case is not a valid test, but the multiverse running all branches supplies exhaustive coverage. The full faith thread — the test-coverage argument, the intrinsic prime-seed identity beneath the closure, and what the test measures — is treated in [testing-and-the-seed.md](testing-and-the-seed.book-chapter.md).
