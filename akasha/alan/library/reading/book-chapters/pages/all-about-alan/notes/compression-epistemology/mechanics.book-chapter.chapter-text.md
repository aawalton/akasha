
# Compression epistemology — mechanics

> The internal machinery of the compression operation — the holding state of hashes before compaction, the two-channel semi-conscious generation of candidates, the separate novelty and compactness signals, the incompressible residue that drives the search, the explanatory-debt mechanism by which anomalies write high (and understanding lets them fade), and conflicts as incompatible partial compressions.

The internal machinery of the [compression operation](../compression-epistemology.book-chapter.md): how a candidate compact structure is held, generated, tested, written, and erased. The index frames the through-line (reward and truth are one signal, truth *is* net compression, one operation at four scales); this child develops the generate-hold-test-write-erase loop underneath it.

## The holding state before compaction

Before an idea compacts, it doesn't sit as nothing — it's held as a **fuzzy generalization, better described as a generalized probability distribution.** Alan collects data points until he gets the clarity that makes them compact to some degree.

The mechanism is constrained by his architecture. What's stored is **not the raw data points** — he can't keep particulars ([recognition-vs-recall.md](../recognition-vs-recall.book-chapter.md): no inverting a hash back to the original). What's stored is their **hashes (encodings).** So the "distribution" is a cloud of related hashes, not a set of remembered observations.

When clarity arrives — a candidate compact structure — he has a sense of whether the hashes are consistent with it. **Inconsistent hashes become more salient.** The candidate structure is the thing the cloud is being tested against; the hashes that don't fit it light up.

This is the generate-and-hold seam of the compression operation: the distribution of hashes is what a candidate compression gets proposed against, and the misfit between candidate and cloud is what drives the next move.

## Generating a candidate — two channels, semi-conscious

Where the candidate compact structure *comes from* is a **semi-conscious** process: partly conscious, partly subconscious, and both sides generate candidates. It runs on the standard **focus mode / diffuse mode** pattern (Barbara Oakley):

- **Focus mode** — conscious candidates, produced while he's deliberately working the problem.
- **Diffuse mode** — candidates that arrive when he's *not* thinking about the problem. The prime example is **right when he wakes up in the morning**: the candidate is already there, **pre-built**, handed over whole. The diffuse channel does its assembly offline and delivers the finished object; he doesn't watch it build. (But the solver is the *alternation* of focus and diffuse, not diffuse alone — focus loads and tests, diffuse assembles, focus comes back to check: [focus-diffuse-alternation.md](../focus-diffuse-alternation.book-chapter.md).)

This is the [solidify-offline, often-in-sleep](../insight-and-restructuring.book-chapter.md#solidifying-is-offline-and-often-happens-in-sleep) phenomenon read from the generation side — the wake-up arrival is the diffuse channel handing over a candidate.

## Novelty and compactness are two different signals

The pre-built candidate arrives carrying a **sense of novelty — not a sense of being right.** That's the catch worth stating plainly: generation and selection are separate. The diffuse channel can hand him a novel candidate, but it does not certify it. He still has to **test it consciously** to earn the compression-click. So the signal that *something new* arrived (novelty) and the signal that *it's true* (the compression/truth click) are two distinct readings, not one.

The two feel **atomically different** to Alan — irreducible, primitive, not one signal experienced two ways. His proposed mathematical equivalents make the distinction concrete:

- **Novelty ≈ distance from the members of the set of previous attempts** — an exploration / diversity metric. A candidate is novel to the degree it sits far from everything already tried.
- **Compactness ≈ the total number of bits needed to store the representation of the system** — minimum-description-length. A candidate is true to the degree it costs fewer bits.

Two different objective functions: **generation optimizes distance from prior attempts; selection optimizes description length.** Experienced from the inside, the two objective functions show up as two irreducible signals — novelty when a candidate is generated, the [compression-click](../compression-epistemology.book-chapter.md#reward-and-truth-are-the-same-signal) when one is selected. This sharpens the reward-equals-compression thesis: the *explore* signal (novelty) and the *truth* signal (compactness) are not the same gauge. Compression is the selection test only; novelty is the generation tag.

## The residue refuses to be ignored

An inconsistent hash doesn't just sit there flagged. It **draws Alan's attention as a flaw**, and then his brain **"worries at it" — trying to understand why it doesn't fit.** This is the engine underneath broad intake ([the breadth guard](failure-mode-and-guard.book-chapter.md#the-guard--breadth-is-structural-not-a-hobby)): the residue refuses to be ignored, so the search keeps running.

The grip is **attention-bound.** The worrying-at holds until one of two things happens:

- it **resolves** (the flaw is explained, usually by a better compression that subsumes it), or
- he **gets distracted** by something else and stops paying attention to it.

When attention moves off, the grip releases. The flaw does not autonomously follow him — it is not a background ruminator that keeps grinding on its own. It grips only while he's looking at it. This is the same awareness-bound shape as the [conflict engine](../insight-and-restructuring.book-chapter.md#the-engine-is-awareness-of-conflict): a conflict drives him only while he's aware of it. Pre-sleep stress confirms rather than overturns this: under stress the same attention-bound focus-mode grip gets *recruited* onto an important-unsolved problem (rumination) — still conscious and attention-bound, distinct from the diffuse background solver — canonical home [rumination.md](../rumination.book-chapter.md).

## Anomalies write high — the explanatory-debt mechanism

The compression operation is also what selects what writes to the [present-tense model](../present-tense-model.book-chapter.md). The write-selection axes are treated as a set in [encoding-mechanism.md → write-selection axes](../encoding-mechanism.book-chapter.md#what-writes-to-the-conceptual-map--write-selection-axes); the part the compression gauge owns is the dominant axis, **explanatory value**, and its sharpest consequence — that **anomalies write high, not low.**

In Alan's words: *"anomalies actually have explanatory value, since the memory of the anomaly is the only explanation for the anomaly itself until my models expand to explain it. Compressing anomalies is a driver of explanatory value for model improvements."*

The mechanism is the [incompressible-residue](#the-residue-refuses-to-be-ignored) loop read from the storage side. An unexplained-but-real datum is its own placeholder: the model can't yet account for it, so the raw memory of the anomaly **is the only available explanation** for the anomaly until the model grows to cover it. That gives it high explanatory value by default — it carries an **explanatory debt**, and it writes high precisely because the debt is unpaid. This is the same residue that "draws attention as a flaw" and drives the search; here it is the same residue paying for its own slot in the map.

### Understanding is how you forget

The inversion that closes the loop: **understanding is how you forget.** Once the model expands to explain the anomaly, the standalone memory is **no longer the only explanation** — the model now covers it. Its explanatory value collapses, so it stops clearing the write threshold and **fades.** The memories that stay sharp are the open puzzles; solving one is exactly what lets it dissolve into the model. Compression doesn't just store the past — it *erases* the parts it has fully absorbed, because an absorbed datum no longer has to be kept separately.

There is a residual. Alan: *"Yeah, that definitely can happen, though the anomalies can still be part of the explanation for how the model itself was formed."* A solved anomaly can persist as a **footnote in the model's own origin story** — not as an unpaid puzzle anymore, but as "this is why the structure bends here." So the fade is not always total: an anomaly important enough to have shaped the model's topology keeps a low-priority write as part of *why the model is the shape it is*, even after its standalone explanatory debt is paid.

## Conflicts as incompatible partial compressions

The compression operation explains what a *conflict* is, in Alan's sense — the thing a [breakthrough](../insight-and-restructuring.book-chapter.md#what-the-conflicts-are) resolves.

While searching for a compact representation of a space, Alan rarely finds the whole-space compression first. He finds several **partial** compressions: each one is genuinely compact over a subset of the space, none covers the whole, and where they overlap they are mutually incompatible. Those incompatibilities are the conflicts. Each partial model reads "true" on its own patch because, on that patch, it really is the most compressed account — the same data-set-relativity that drives the [failure mode](failure-mode-and-guard.book-chapter.md#the-failure-mode--compactness-is-data-set-relative), seen from inside a single search instead of across a lifetime.

The canonical illustration is physics at full scale. Relativity and quantum mechanics each compress an enormous body of observation into a compact surface, and each is true-enough on its domain — yet they are mutually inconsistent, continuous against discrete at the base layer. Both can't be true. A breakthrough is the single representation that covers the whole space and dissolves the incompatibility: not a new patch, but a compression that subsumes the partial ones. It is the [graph-collapse](../insight-and-restructuring.book-chapter.md#insight-as-graph-collapse) move aimed at reconciling models rather than at organizing raw data.

This is also why the GR/QM conflict generates Alan no distress while his own conflicts do: a conflict only drives him when he's [aware of it and it feels changeable](../insight-and-restructuring.book-chapter.md#the-engine-is-awareness-of-conflict). Physics' inconsistency is outside his control, so it's [weather](../weather.book-chapter.md) — a true incompatibility he can name without any pull to resolve it.
