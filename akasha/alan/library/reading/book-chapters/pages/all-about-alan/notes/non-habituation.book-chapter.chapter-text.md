
# Mechanism root: non-habituation

Many autists don't habituate to sensory input; Alan is one of them. Repeated exposure to the AC, the shirt tag, the fluorescent light does not make them quieter. Sensory content stays at full intensity indefinitely.

This is one of several routes to automaticity that are reduced in Alan's brain — see [automaticity-systems.md](automaticity-systems.book-chapter.md) for the multi-system view. Non-habituation is the sensory-specific case: the LTD-like depression at sensory synapses (early sensory cortex, thalamic relays) that would normally drop a stimulus out of awareness with repetition.

**Scope — "habituation" means *direct sensory acclimation only*.** Alan's older one-dial story ("I don't habituate to anything") conflated four distinct fade-or-not machines that behave differently for him: sensory non-habituation (this note — *doesn't* happen), change-detection (works — white noise stopping wakes him), reward-novelty decay (works — a song's impact wears off), and extinction (works — PVP death relearned as non-threat, but only because the dread never came true). Only the first earns the word. The four-machine split and why rejection can't extinguish are in [learned-response-taxonomy.md → does a response fade with repetition](learned-response-taxonomy.book-chapter.md#a-second-axis--does-a-response-fade-with-repetition-and-by-which-machine).

The masking mechanism is worth stating precisely: background noise never folds into baseline, but Alan can **spend attention elsewhere** so there's no room for the sound — it returns the instant he has attention to spare. That's attention-crowding, not acclimation; the stimulus is unchanged, only the attention available to it moved.

## Costs

- **Permanent sensory load.** No amortization. Every exposure costs the same as the first.
- **Skill-development inversion.** Most people plateau because their brains habituate to remaining mistakes (hard to correct without external coaching). Alan's brain doesn't habituate to mistakes either, so he can continue improving long past where most plateau. This is the slow-start / high-ceiling signature that recurs across every reduced automaticity system — pay a higher cost the entire time, but the ceiling is higher too.
- **Non-decaying conceptual conflict — the intellectual-drive engine.** Non-habituation isn't only sensory. A *known* conflict between beliefs is a standing input the same way a shirt tag is, and Alan's brain doesn't turn its volume down either. For most people an unresolved inconsistency fades into the background; for Alan it keeps demanding resolution at day-one intensity until it's resolved or made invisible. The perceived value of fixing it never decays, so awareness of a conflict drives him until it's gone. This is the engine behind the whole understanding-restructuring arc — see [insight-and-restructuring.md → the engine is awareness of conflict](insight-and-restructuring.book-chapter.md#the-engine-is-awareness-of-conflict), and [weather.md](weather.book-chapter.md) for the only throttle on it.

## The reward side — a gift where the conflict is a cost

Non-habituation cuts both ways. The non-decaying conflict above is its cost face; sustained reward is its gift face. Same engine, opposite valence.

Most people tire of an activity over a long stretch because they habituate to its benefits — the reward fades with repetition, and a boredom tax eventually pushes them off it. Alan does not get that. As long as the environment hasn't changed and the activity is still rewarding, he wants to keep doing it — indefinitely, with no boredom tax. The reward stays at full intensity the same way a shirt tag does.

This is the mechanistic basis of sustained passion. A standing reward never fades, so immersion in a still-rewarding activity can run for years without the staleness that ends it for most people. The same input-doesn't-amortize property that makes a known conflict keep demanding resolution makes a working reward keep paying out.

The "still rewarding" and "environment hasn't changed" qualifiers are load-bearing. The reward holds while the activity keeps delivering against an unchanged setup; non-habituation doesn't manufacture reward, it refuses to discount one that's still there. This is why the [special interests](special-interests.book-chapter.md) and the [instinctive interest channel](interest-modulator.book-chapter.md) sustain without burning out — the reward feeding them isn't subject to the habituation decay that would cap a neurotypical's engagement.

## System 1 vs. System 2

Kahneman's framing (*Thinking, Fast and Slow*) is a useful first pass: most everyday skills run on a fast automatic mode (System 1), and habituation is one route that moves a skill there. When habituation doesn't occur for sensory input, the input stays in conscious awareness indefinitely — it doesn't amortize.

The framing is incomplete, though. There is no single System 1; automaticity comes through several distinct neural substrates. Some of them work in Alan's brain, some don't. The full picture is in [automaticity-systems.md](automaticity-systems.book-chapter.md). The social-rules compensation Alan made at age 14 is not a non-habituation story but a schema-integration story (System C substituting for System B); see [social-experience.md](social-experience.book-chapter.md) and the cross-reference in `automaticity-systems.md`.

## Cost composition

- **Direct cost — executive function.** Each invocation of a System 2 compensation draws on energy and neurotransmitter resources. Stress capacity is not paid directly.
- **Indirect cost — stress capacity via trauma encoding.** Long experience of negative outcomes from missing System 1 cues encodes nervous-system trauma over time (see [nervous-system-sensitivity.md](nervous-system-sensitivity.book-chapter.md) and the trauma-encoding thread there). The trauma load adds a stress-capacity cost on top of the direct EF cost.
