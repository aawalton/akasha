
# The fawn collapse — Upholder to Rebel at ~age 20

> Fawn collapse at ~age 20 — a near-fatal physiological breakdown that broke fawn as a viable survival strategy and flipped Alan from Upholder to Rebel, the origin of his refusal to assume common patterns transfer to him.

Around age 20 I hit a hard choice between two things that had, until then, both been survivable: keep applying social cues to avoid punishment, or protect raw physiological survival. In my words:

> At around age 20, I hit a hard choice between continuing to apply social cues to avoid punishment and raw physiological survival, my fawn response almost killed me medically. That broke fawn as a safe way to avoid punishment and rapidly flipped me from Upholder to Rebel.

[Fawn](stress-responses.book-chapter.md) — the mode that presents as people-pleasing rather than visible dysregulation — had been my working strategy for avoiding punishment. Running it hard enough, for long enough, to keep the punishment away brought me to a physiological breaking point. The body's demand for survival overrode the social strategy that was endangering it. Fawn didn't just stop working; it became **unsafe to continue running**, and the break was medical, not merely emotional.

## The flip — Upholder to Rebel

The collapse didn't just retire fawn. It flipped my entire orientation to rules and expectations. Before: an **Upholder** — someone who meets both outer expectations and inner ones, the type that keeps rules including its own, which is what let fawn run as a stable strategy for two decades. After: a **Rebel** — someone who resists rules and expectations as such, outer or inner. The flip was fast, not gradual, because it was forced by a medical emergency rather than talked through.

## The lasting stance — common patterns don't transfer

The collapse left a standing epistemic rule, not just a mood change: *"Just because something works for most people is not a good reason to believe that it would work for me."* Fawn is the concrete proof case — it is a strategy that works, on average, for most people navigating threat. It nearly killed me. So the general lesson generalizes past fawn specifically: **population-average validity is not evidence of validity for me**, because I already have one confirmed instance of a common strategy running me to a medical edge. This stance now governs how I weigh any borrowed pattern, not only social-threat responses.
