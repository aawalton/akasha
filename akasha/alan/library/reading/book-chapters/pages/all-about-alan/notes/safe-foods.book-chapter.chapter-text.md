
# Safe foods

> Low-resource-state-affordable feeding system, and the food-as-fuel frame and food map it sits within. The safe-food set is the foods Alan can handle when all four resource bars are low (low sensory cost, low EF cost, high nutritional return, low aversion risk). The wider frame: food enjoyment runs ~10% of others', so eating functionally rather than for pleasure costs him almost nothing; foods are selected on physical axes and lost to disqualification or forgetting, never boredom.

A defined set of foods Alan can fall back on when all four [resource bars](four-resource-model.book-chapter.md) are low. Promoted from a starter sub-system in [sub-systems.md](sub-systems.book-chapter.md) once probed in detail. "Safe foods" is the standard term for this category in the autism community.

## Function — maintain feeding when all four bars are low

The set keeps Alan eating at low resource state. Selection is for affordability across the four bars at low state, not for sensory preference. Whichever food is available and reachable in the moment gets eaten.

The starter framing in [sub-systems.md](sub-systems.book-chapter.md) called this "safe-food rotation as taste-management residue from childhood." Both halves were wrong. There is no actual rotation — selection is situational, not scheduled. The operational driver is not childhood taste-management; it is low-resource-state-affordability across the four-bar accounting.

## Food as fuel — the frame underneath

The safe-food set looks the way it does — bland, functional, repetitive — because of a wider fact about how food works for Alan: **food enjoyment runs at roughly 10% of other people's.** [No experiential memory](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md) means he can't reminisce on a past meal; [aphantasia](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md) means he can't anticipate a future one; in-the-moment enjoyment exists only when he is doing well, and even then it is muted. The consequence is structural: **the "food as pleasure / reward" driver — the engine of most overeating — is barely present in him.** A simple, repetitive, functional diet is not a sacrifice he is enduring; it is close to neutral.

So **"food is fuel" is his native, comfortable frame.** He seriously considered Soylent — all meals as a smoothie — and rejected it **only on health grounds** (Soylent's consistent use correlates with increased all-cause mortality), not on taste. Eating functionally costs him almost nothing.

Three properties follow from autism and shape the set:

- **No habituation, so no boredom.** [Non-habituation](non-habituation.book-chapter.md) means he doesn't get tired of foods. *"I'll eat a safe food until something changes and it is no longer safe."* A food stays in rotation indefinitely until a disqualifying event; foods are lost to **disqualification or forgetting, never boredom** (see [how foods enter and leave the set](#how-foods-enter-and-leave-the-set)).
- **Preference axes are physical, not flavor.** *"My favorite flavors are hot and cold."* **Temperature, texture, consistency, saltiness, and spiciness** matter far more to him than specific flavors — which is why a bland, physical-axis selection criterion costs him little. The hot/cold axis is one face of his [thermoregulation](thermoregulation.book-chapter.md#thermoregulation-as-a-cross-domain-axis).
- **Aversions are conceptual, not sensory — and one frame is dangerous.** His physical/sensory aversions are weak; his real aversions are conceptual. Two cases: thinking about *what meat is* triggers nausea; and **restriction spirals** — under an "eat healthier" frame, every food is "unhealthy" to someone, and the framing itself manufactures aversion. A naïve "eat healthy / pure foods" belief is therefore **actively dangerous** for him ([ARFID](autism-comorbidities.book-chapter.md) risk), not merely unhelpful. Any change to his eating has to be present-neutral-or-positive, never a restriction (per [Rule 3 of the volatility governor](volatility-governor.book-chapter.md#rule-3--im-not-allowed-to-make-choices-that-hurt-myself-not-even-if-they-benefit-others-not-even-if-the-others-are-my-future-selves), which forbids the sacrifice-present-for-future frame). The conceptual-aversion shape is **not specific to food** — a *non-food object* entangled with a dissonant association picks up the same initiation-blocking stress-cue; the worked case is a movement lever (his VR headset) in [health.md → the Beat Saber lapse-stack](health.book-chapter.md#beat-saber--the-worked-case-of-a-stacked-lapse).

## Selection criteria — four-attribute affordability checklist

Each food in the set has to clear four attributes. All four matter at low state; the four bars they each protect map onto the [four-resource model](four-resource-model.book-chapter.md).

- **Simple / bland — low sensory cost.** Minimal flavor, texture, or aroma load. Protects Stress Capacity from sensory drain ([four-resource-model.md → sensory drains](four-resource-model.book-chapter.md#cost-mechanics)).
- **Fast / easy preparation — low EF cost.** Minimal steps, minimal decisions. Protects Mana from preparation drain.
- **Dense macronutrition — high Health return per unit of effort.** Calories, protein, fat, carbs delivered per minute of EF spent. The set has to actually feed Alan, not just be tolerable.
- **Taste-aversion safety — guaranteed completion.** The food won't trigger a gag or refusal mid-meal. No Stress Capacity drain from a failed attempt. This is the criterion the "safe" label originally points at; in adult use it sits alongside the other three.

A food has to pass all four to enter the set. Missing any one — too sensory-loud, too prep-heavy, too low-density, too aversion-risky — and it doesn't qualify, regardless of how good it tastes when capacity is high.

## Selection mechanism — situational, not scheduled

No rotation calendar. No assigned day for each food. Selection at meal time is: pick whichever safe food is available and Alan can handle in the moment.

EF cost is pushed to set definition (upstream) rather than to per-meal decision-making (downstream). The decision "what should I eat" only ranges over the pre-vetted set; the cost of evaluating new candidates does not get paid in the moment. Same compensation shape as the [agent harness](agent-harness.book-chapter.md) and [urgency-removal.md](urgency-removal.book-chapter.md) — push impaired-EF work upstream into structure where the impairment doesn't apply. The set is the structure; real-time selection is the cue → action retrieval.

## Worked examples — current set

Three foods that pass all four attributes. Each ranges in EF cost so the right one fires for the right level of capacity.

- **Cheese bread.** Slice of bread, 1-2 slices of pre-sliced cheese on top, microwave 30 seconds, tablespoon of sugar on top to balance the macros. Under crisis-low capacity: paper plate and disposable fork, throw the dishware away instead of scrubbing off melted cheese.
- **Applesauce packets.** Pre-packaged, kept in the fridge. Grab one, screw the lid off, drink it, throw it away. No utensil, no plate, no preparation.
- **Fruit bowl with ice cream.** Bowl filled with whatever fruit is easily available and already cut and washed (bananas are always fast to prepare; otherwise strawberries, raspberries, etc.). Plain vanilla ice cream on top.

Applesauce packets sit at the EF floor (no prep, no cleanup, no decisions); fruit bowl is mid-range (a bowl, a spoon, dishware to clean); cheese bread is the highest-effort of the three but still passes the threshold for the bar it serves.

## Disposable-dishware sub-pattern

Under crisis-low capacity, dishware drops to disposable — paper plate, plastic fork — so cleanup EF cost drops to zero (throw away instead of scrub). The cheese-bread example is the worked case: melted cheese on a ceramic plate is the highest cleanup cost in the meal; replacing the plate with paper eliminates that cost entirely.

Capacity-conditional cleanup deferral. The set's prep stays the same; the dishware tier scales with capacity.

## Macro-balance thinking under low capacity

Macronutrient balance is explicit even at low state. Sugar gets added to the cheese-bread to balance the bread's carbohydrates against the cheese's protein and fat. The choice is not taste-driven — bland is the goal — it's nutritional-completion-driven.

The scaffolding persists where it pays. Macro-balance reasoning is cheap once internalized (the rule fires from "what does this meal currently have on it") and the payoff is real Health return per unit of effort.

## "Safe foods" as standard terminology

"Safe foods" is the conventional term in the autism community for this category — foods that consistently feel okay to eat across sensory and aversion variability. Alan's operational system maps onto the term, but extends the criteria beyond aversion-safety to the full four-attribute affordability checklist above. The starter line's "taste-management residue from childhood" framing was wrong; the system in adult use is structured around current resource state, not childhood preference history.

## The food map — what is where

What Alan eats is organized less by meal and more by **physical location** — which foods are reachable in which space, gated by the [environment lever](environment-lever.book-chapter.md) (out of sight mostly stops existing) and by his current capacity.

- **His room** — office, bedroom, bath, and home-gym in one; he could live here indefinitely given food. *Arms-reach healthy:* plant-protein bars and figs on the desk, reachable only when he is doing well (reaching costs EF). Figs are his one quality-fragile winner. *Sugar stash:* gifted or holiday sugar that is his — he can't reject or discard it without felt offense, so it goes in an arms-reach drawer and gets reached for when depleted. *The low-state floor:* at level ≤ 2 there is currently **nothing in the room he can get himself to eat.** The reach mechanism at low state is exact — **the desire for a food must itself supply the executive function to pay its own task-initiation cost.** Sugar qualifies because the craving funds the reach; a fig does not. He has no aligned food he craves at the lowest levels, which is the core low-state blocker.
- **The mini-kitchen** — shared with his son Joseph, just outside his door; Joseph's co-located food exerts zero pull and is cleanly ignored. His own items: fruit popsicles (reached for when overheated — [heat is a desire-trigger](thermoregulation.book-chapter.md#thermoregulation-as-a-cross-domain-axis)), unopened chocolate-covered strawberries (the sealed bag is an initiation wall), water bottles, applesauce pouches, and Orgain plant-protein smoothies — a long-running daily habit that **died when its cue, creatine, was dropped** (a "forgotten winner"). And **★ the muesli:** organic Winco muesli + Costco mixed nuts + orange juice instead of milk, eaten like cereal. He discovered it on his mission in Russia at age 20 and has eaten it consistently for ~20 years; **"works every time."** It is his **one reliable self-initiated full meal.** The only barrier is ~30 seconds of prep; he has tried pre-portioning and found it *more* prep — the recipe is at the end of 20 years of optimization.
- **Shared household food** — always changing, with one invariant: **sugar is always available.** Jen feels safe having options including sugar even though she rarely eats them and many are thrown away; the *availability itself* benefits her, so it is relationship-protected and cannot be removed. The behavioral cases here are food-stimming (ice cream, popcorn, fruit when doing well — his self-discovered trade-up is a layer of ice cream over a heaping bowl of pre-cut fruit, "even better than just ice cream"), desperation eating (the rule *"Alan must eat food every day"*; the best he manages is the least-missed sugar plus a soft cheese for protein and water), social eating, and delivery/take-out when everyone needs food but Jen lacks the capacity to cook (bandwidth eating, capacity-gated).
- **Away from home** — eating out, mostly with Jen, on a tradition of **splitting food** (usually one entrée, naturally portion-controlled, usually with a dessert or two because Jen loves treats); and Costco samples. **★ His soft rule: every store visit, buy something new to try** — a deliberate counterbalance against the long-term [ARFID](autism-comorbidities.book-chapter.md) contraction trend, and his standing discovery mechanism for new foods.

## How foods enter and leave the set

Foods enter by passing the [four-attribute checklist](#selection-criteria--four-attribute-affordability-checklist) above; they leave two ways, and both are losses he actively manages against.

- **Disqualification by quality-inconsistency.** A few bad units write a durable aversion. This killed his long-running pre-peeled boiled eggs (eggshells, plus the occasional rotten one) and his grapefruit cups (bitter, sometimes rancid). His current winners survive precisely *because* they are extremely consistent by design — consistency is a selection filter, not a bonus.
- **Forgetting it exists.** A perfectly good aligned food drops out of awareness ([EF and time-blindness](executive-function.book-chapter.md)) and quietly stops being eaten — the Orgain smoothie is the worked case. This is the live threat to his current survivors: not that they stop working, but that they stop being *seen*, which is why keeping a food [in the environment](environment-lever.book-chapter.md) is what keeps it in the diet.
