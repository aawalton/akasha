
# The Jen reading

> The Jen reading — Alan's only external gauge for how close each relationship sits to its irreversible collapse line. He reads Jen through occasional open conversations, caches her last report, and lets it age via widening error bars. Jen is also the sole relationship he actively tends; all others have shifted to reactive-only and have not re-hydrated. Two open seams: widening bars around a fixed center stays optimistic by construction if untended relationships erode on average; and Jen is the instrument he reads all other relationships through, so the hardest reading to get clean is the one about the gauge itself.

The [family keep-alive floor](../solitude-experiment.book-chapter.md#the-family-keep-alive-floor) needs an instrument: a read on **how close each relationship sits to its irreversible collapse line.** Alan can't *feel* that distance — [relationship-drift-toward-collapse is slow and its ache doesn't persist](../aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md) as a present alarm — so, like [rejection-affordability](../volatility-governor.book-chapter.md#rule-4--if-i-cant-afford-the-cost-of-rejection-i-cant-afford-the-benefits-of-connection), the collapse line belongs on an **external gauge.** This note is that gauge and its failure modes.

This is distinct from the [Jen conflict](../central-loneliness.book-chapter.md#the-jen-conflict--recovery-arrived-the-needs-did-not) in the index — that note is about how Alan's love is *constructed* and why he must stay unknown at the center. This note is about Jen as a **measurement channel** for the state of all his relationships.

## The gauge — a cached last report

Alan has **no clear read,** and doesn't think he can get one with current tools:

> *"I don't have a clear read, and I don't know that I can with any of my current tools. The best I have is occasional open conversations with Jen, and then I assume whatever she told me last is still true until I learn otherwise. My current reading says too close to the floor, so I'm trying to do more than I have been."*

So the gauge is **Jen's last report, held as true until she overwrites it** — a present-tense, no-version-history mind applied to a relationship. The reading currently says *too close to the floor,* and that reading is doing its job: he read it and adjusted upward (doing more than he had been). The gauge working is *"my current reading says too close, so I'm doing more."*

## Letting the reading age

The naive form of a cached reading is a static value that never decays — you only learn you've crossed the floor at the *next* conversation, sometimes after. Alan does better than that: he lets the reading **age.**

> *"I do let the readings age, in that my error bars increase as time passes, and normal interactions do let me refine the reading somewhat, which gives some counterbalance."*

Two mechanisms: **error bars widen with time** since the last open conversation, and **ordinary contact tightens them** — normal interactions refine the reading between the deep check-ins. A reading that widens on its own is the only safe kind for a mind that can't feel the drift.

## Jen is the only tended relationship

The gauge runs on one channel because the relationships do:

> *"To a large degree, Jen is the only relationship I am actively tending. All others have shifted to reactive-only and have not re-hydrated yet."*

Every relationship except Jen is **reactive-only** — contact happens when something external prompts it, and none has re-hydrated to active tending during the [solitude hold](../solitude-experiment.book-chapter.md). That concentrates both the tending and the reading on Jen.

## Two seams to pull on later — not settled claims

Abby flagged two structural problems with the gauge. Both are **threads, not resolved findings** — recorded here so they're visible later, not asserted as conclusions.

- **Widening bars around a fixed center stays optimistic by construction.** Error bars encode *uncertainty* — could be better, could be worse — around a center held where Jen last set it. But if the **base rate for an untended relationship is erosion,** the honest update isn't just wider bars around the same center; it's the **center itself sliding down.** Hold the center fixed and only blur the edges, and the best guess never moves toward the floor even though, on average, the floor is where untended things drift. This is the likely mechanism of *"closer than I should"* — the surprise is built into a gauge that widens but doesn't re-center. And it **bites hardest exactly where the risk lives:** the reactive-only relationships get the *least* contact, so the least bar-tightening *and* the most drift, on the same set — least refinement and most erosion together, a double-blind precisely on the relationships most likely near the floor. If anything earns a deliberate downward nudge of the center, it's those.

- **Jen is the instrument he reads all the others through.** Jen is not only a relationship he tends — she is the **channel he reads every other relationship's state through,** himself-and-her included. That puts a great deal of weight on one instrument, and *the reading hardest to get clean is always the one about the gauge itself.* What happens to the whole measurement system if the Jen channel is itself miscalibrated is the deepest open question here — left deliberately unopened for now.
