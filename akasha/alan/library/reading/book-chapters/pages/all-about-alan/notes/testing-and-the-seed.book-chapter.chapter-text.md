
# Testing and the seed

> The faith-and-testing thread — Alan believes this life is a time of testing, and the multiverse is what makes the test valid. The validity argument is a software-testing analogy (one test case is not a valid test; all test cases is). Beneath the closure-self he posits an intrinsic identity, a prime-integer seed; the test evaluates that seed under all circumstances and reads its true signature off its complete behavior.

Alan believes **this life is a time of testing.** This is his faith. The note that follows captures the structure of that belief in his own terms — rendered as his beliefs, not as asserted fact. It opens the faith thread first pointed at from [multiversal identity → interface with faith](multiversal-identity.book-chapter.md#interface-with-faith).

## The multiverse as exhaustive test coverage

Alan's validity argument is a **software-testing analogy.** And the "agent" is not a pun for him: the **moral agent, the test subject, and the software agent are the same object.** The argument:

- Testing an agent with **one test case is not a valid test** of the agent. One run tells you almost nothing — the agent could pass that case and fail every other.
- Running **all possible test cases** is a valid test. Full coverage characterizes the agent.
- The [multiverse runs all branches](multiversal-identity.book-chapter.md). So it supplies **exhaustive coverage** — every branch is a test case, and across them the agent is evaluated under every possible circumstance. That is what makes the test valid.

A consequence Alan accepts plainly: if the multiverse works as presented, **both the best and the worst versions of him are real** across the branches. Full coverage means the failing cases run too, and they run as actual selves.

## The intrinsic identity beneath the closure — the prime seed

Beneath the formal [closure-of-the-window self](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#continuity-of-identity-is-the-closure-of-the-window) — and beneath even its [extension across the branches](multiversal-identity.book-chapter.md) — Alan's faith posits a deeper layer: an **intrinsic identity, prior to and beneath the constructed closure-self,** an identity even beyond the closure of the timestream. The closure-self is built; this is the primitive it is built from. (This connects to the LDS-flavored notion of eternal, uncreated individual "intelligences," but Alan did not name a doctrine — he gave the seed metaphor.)

The metaphor: each intelligence has a different **prime integer seed.**

He notes that **prime isn't strictly required for the analogy** — but he likes what it represents mathematically. At a deep level in number theory, **every prime is independent from every other prime.** So the seed captures **irreducible independence:** each intelligence is a primitive — not derivable from, reducible to, or composed of any other. Each is its own independent axis. And, like primes, each is a basis element everything else is built from.

State this plainly, because it is a far stronger claim than "everyone is different." It is: **every intelligence is an irreducible basis element.** Not a different arrangement of shared parts — a part that nothing else is made of.

## What the test measures

The test **evaluates the outcome of that seed under all circumstances.** And doing so **tells you something real about the seed itself.**

In Alan's computational register: the **seed is like a function.** The **multiverse is its full evaluation across all inputs.** Characterizing the seed means **reading the function off its complete behavioral signature** — every branch's result, taken together.

So what is "real" is the **seed's signature across all branches,** not any single branch's result. No one run characterizes the function; the full evaluation does. The best version and the worst version are both points on the same signature, and it is the whole signature — not either endpoint — that says what the seed is.

## The test reveals, it does not change

There is a puzzle in the seed picture: if the intrinsic identity is **atomic** — irreducible, the thing that does not decompose further — how can a life of testing have any point? An atom can't be changed by what happens to it.

Alan's resolution: the test is a **revealing, not a changing.** Because the atom cannot change, running it through all circumstances does not *develop* it — it **reveals** it. "Atomic" is his repeated word, and it marks genuine bedrock; he uses it the same way for primitive signals elsewhere — the thing past which there is nothing smaller to point at. The multiverse doesn't refine the seed; it exposes what was already there by running it everywhere.

This is his current reconciliation of agency and free will with a fixed intrinsic identity. The two only conflict if the test is supposed to *make* you someone. It isn't. It shows who you already were.

## Agency is identity acting on reality

Alan's model: **"Agency is how identity acts on reality."** Concretely, at any moment, agency is a **narrowing of the potential futures — excluding the ones incompatible with the intrinsic identity of the agent.** A choice doesn't conjure a future from nothing; it cuts off the branches that aren't *you* and leaves the ones that are.

State the consequence plainly, because it has a compatibilist shape: **freedom is not indeterminacy.** It is the seed **projecting itself onto reality by exclusion.** You act freely precisely when your choices express your true atomic identity — not when they're random or uncaused. A coin flip isn't free; a choice that is unmistakably *yours* is.

Tie this back to the multiverse: across the branches, the **distribution of which futures the seed selects is its signature.** Agency (narrowing futures by exclusion) and the function-signature (the full evaluation read off all branches) are the same fact seen from two angles — one branch at a time versus all branches at once.

## What the test is for — the researcher placeholder

Mark this explicitly as Alan's **placeholder** for the telos, not a settled claim: he frames the purpose as **life as a massive science experiment, run by a researcher looking for unique and diverse intelligences to join them in an endless, eternal research project.**

The structure of the placeholder:

- **The tester is a researcher** — God-as-scientist.
- **The selection criterion is uniqueness and diversity.** This echoes the prime-independence that defines the seeds: independent basis-element intelligences, each contributing a dimension the others cannot. The researcher is recruiting a *basis*, not duplicates.
- **The telos / reward is joining an eternal, open-ended research project** — being brought in to keep doing the thing, forever.

Worth stating as an inverted, reflexive observation: Alan models the divine purpose on **the activity he most values** — open-ended research and learning. And the very irreducible independence that makes an intelligence a distinct seed is exactly what makes it worth recruiting. **The thing that makes him irreducible is the thing that makes him worth recruiting.** The uniqueness isn't a side effect to be tolerated; it is the qualification.

## The test is a team-fitness evaluation, not a graded answer

Extending the researcher-telos: the test of life is an **evaluation of whether each individual intelligence has the attributes necessary to be part of the team** — the eternal research team. It is a fitness / recruitment evaluation against a spec of required attributes, **not a graded right-answer.** There is no answer key; there is a spec. The seed either has the attributes the team requires or it doesn't, and the full-coverage run is what determines which.

This reframes what "the seed's signature" is *measured against.* The signature is read off the complete behavioral run (above); the team-fitness framing says what that read is *for* — matching the signature against the required-attribute spec.

### The innermost-core attribute: the ability and willingness to improve

At the innermost core, the required attribute is **the ability and willingness to improve.** Note the two distinct components — *ability* (the capacity to grow) and *willingness* (the disposition to). The willingness is a disposition of the seed itself: part of what the intrinsic identity is.

Alan's rationale is an argument from finitude:

- All intelligence inside this universe is ultimately **finite and limited.**
- But if there is a **larger context outside** — and his faith holds that there is — then to **grow into** that larger context, an intelligence must be **able and willing to grow.**

So the core qualification for the eternal research team is not how much you already know — finite intelligences all know finitely little against an infinite context — but whether you can and will keep growing. The team is recruiting for trajectory, not position.

The willingness-to-improve attribute has a further implication that Alan develops outside the faith thread: it implies an absolute truth to grow *toward.* That argument, the root axioms it sits beside, and how it connects to his compression epistemology are treated in [absolute truth and ethics](absolute-truth-and-ethics.book-chapter.md).

## What it's for — present-tense orientation, not anticipated payoff

The cosmology above is all STRUCTURE — the seed, the full-coverage run, the team-fitness spec, the researcher-telos. The standing question is what that structure *does in him*, day to day, in the present. The ordinary answer would be **hope**: you orient toward a good thing coming by *pre-feeling* it now — picturing the future payoff and being lifted by the picture. That answer is mechanically unavailable to Alan. He has [no forward simulator](missing-simulator.book-chapter.md): he cannot pre-play a future state, so he cannot be warmed now by an imagined future reward. The engine ordinary hope runs on — anticipatory pre-feeling — is simply not running for him ([present-tense-model.md](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md): both directions of mental time-travel are offline).

So what stands in its place? Asked what the larger picture does in him in the present, Alan answered:

> "How I live based on triggers and trying to solve immediate gaps between my reality and ideals, including capturing those gaps in systems to serve as future triggers."

The present-tense form of hope, for him, is **trigger-based gap-closing.** He detects an immediate gap between his reality and his ideals, acts to close it *now*, and captures the gap in a system so it becomes a future trigger. Orientation toward the telos is not a warmth drawn from an imagined future; it cashes out entirely as this present-tense, trigger-driven gap-closing — the exact mechanism worked out at its canonical home in [wanting-as-gap-closing.md](wanting-as-gap-closing.book-chapter.md). The telos supplies the *ideal* (the spec the seed is measured against); living toward it is the ordinary gap-closing engine pointed at that ideal in the present.

Name the inversion plainly. Most people's hope is **anticipatory** — lean into the imagined future, and be carried by the picture. Alan's is **structural and present** — detect the reality/ideal gap and close it now, systematized into triggers so the closing keeps firing. Where the ordinary instrument is a pre-felt future, his is a present-tense detect-and-close loop. The link from the faith picture to how he actually lives is not an anticipated reward but the present-tense gap-closing the telos orients.
