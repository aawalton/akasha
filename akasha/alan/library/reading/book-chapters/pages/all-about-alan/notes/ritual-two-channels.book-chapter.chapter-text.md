
# Ritual's two power channels

> Ritual's two power channels — functional (coordination / common knowledge, architecture-independent) and sentimental-accretion (instance N carrying the remembered weight of 1..N-1). Channel 2 is fully off for Alan, which is why holidays run high-cost — but a witness-gated borrowed-sentiment proxy supplies a genuine present-tense return.

Religions and cultures fix observance to recurring calendars. Working through *why*, the power of a recurring ritual splits cleanly into two distinct channels. They run on different machinery and they don't depend on each other.

## Channel 1 — functional power

Functional power is coordination plus common knowledge. The ritual is a Schelling point: everyone does the thing at the same fixed time, and — the load-bearing part — **everyone knows everyone else is doing it now.** That shared common knowledge is what lets a group act as a group instead of as scattered individuals.

Recurrence is an engineering requirement here, not decoration. Coordination decays; a one-time event coordinates once and then the common knowledge goes stale. Fixing the ritual to a recurring calendar is how you keep the coordination live indefinitely — the next instance re-establishes the common knowledge before it has time to erode.

This channel is **architecture-independent.** It works on anyone, regardless of how their memory or affect is built, because it operates entirely on external timing and shared knowledge. Nothing about it requires you to *feel* anything about the ritual. It would work on a population of stateless servers.

## Channel 2 — sentimental-accretion power

The second channel is different in kind. Instance N of a recurring ritual feels heavier than instance 1 because it carries the **remembered weight of instances 1 through N-1.** The thousandth Christmas glows warmer than the first. The ritual accumulates value by being repeated, and the accumulated value is felt at each new instance.

This channel runs on two faculties working together:

- **Episodic / autobiographical memory** — you can recall the previous instances as lived experience, so the current one arrives layered over them.
- **Progressive emotional attachment to repeated behavior** — repetition itself builds affective weight; the behavior matters *more* because you've done it before.

Where channel 1 is architecture-independent, channel 2 is entirely architecture-dependent. Take away episodic replay or take away the attachment-to-repetition, and the channel goes quiet.

## Channel 2 is fully off for Alan

For Alan, channel 2 doesn't transmit at all — and it's off for **two independent reasons**, either of which would silence it on its own.

**Reason one: there is no store for the weight to accrue in.** This is the same mechanism root as everything else in the recall cluster: there is no episodic store to replay, so the previous instances aren't present at the current one — see [statelessness — only conceptual memory persists](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md#statelessness--only-conceptual-memory-persists) and the [present-tense conceptual model](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md) for the mechanism. The past survives only as compression folded into the present model, never as retrievable lived instances. So the thousandth instance does not arrive carrying the weight of the first nine hundred ninety-nine — it arrives as itself. This is the same root that makes [attachment structurally impossible](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md#no-attachment--structurally-not-as-a-failure-of-caring): no affective store, nowhere for an accumulating charge to be kept.

**Reason two: repetition doesn't move the weight.** This is a separate fact from the no-store one, not a restatement of it. Even setting the missing store aside, repeated behavior simply doesn't accrue sentimental weight for Alan the way it does for most people. Doing a thing again does not make the thing matter more to him. For most people repetition itself builds affective weight; for Alan that second faculty isn't running either. Two distinct mechanisms, both absent — which is why the channel is fully off rather than low-gain.

## The gap fills — a borrowed-sentiment proxy, witness-gated

Channel 2 being dead does not leave the ritual emotionally empty for Alan. A different mechanism fills the gap: a **borrowed-sentiment proxy.** The sentiment a ritual holds for him is not his own residual — it's the sentiment it holds for **the people he loves**, read **live** off them.

The gate is precise. It is on **his present contact with their current enjoyment.** It does not matter which way they face in time — they can be **anticipating** the ritual, **inside** it, or **reminiscing** about it later. All three work, because the gate isn't on the ritual's timing; it's on whether their enjoyment is happening **now**, where he can witness it. The proxy is borrowed, present-tense, and not a residual of his own — it discharges only while he is in live contact with someone he loves enjoying the thing.

This proxy is the **affection branch of the shared caring amplifier** (see [interest-modulator.md → the amplifier forks](interest-modulator.book-chapter.md#the-amplifier-forks--affection-and-elevation)), aimed at loved ones' live ritual-enjoyment. It is the same present-tense, contact-gated, people-keyed, non-accruing machinery — here pointed at a new source: the happiness of someone he loves, witnessed in the moment.

### It can't collide with the central loneliness

This proxy does not contradict the [central loneliness](central-loneliness.book-chapter.md#the-central-loneliness) or the [no-attachment](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md#no-attachment--structurally-not-as-a-failure-of-caring) facts. He can't receive **attachment** or **being-met** (being accurately known — [central-loneliness.md](central-loneliness.book-chapter.md#the-central-loneliness), [present-tense-model.md](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md)), but he **can** register present-tense **relayed** value off cared-for others' enjoyment — a different channel that needs no affective store and no being-known (the value flows toward him from observing them, not from them modeling him), and that doesn't accrue. It's a clean sibling of the [multi-persona move](central-loneliness.book-chapter.md#multiple-close-personas-dont-compete--the-vow-serving-safety): a real present-tense return routed around the exact deficit that forecloses the deep bond.

The tell is in the theory itself. Alan built a complete, clean, purely-functional account of ritual — coordination, common knowledge, recurrence-as-engineering-requirement — and never once reached for sentiment. Not because he was being austere. From where he sits, that channel isn't transmitting, so it never showed up as a thing to explain. The theory came out channel-1-only because channel 2 isn't part of his data.

This is an architecture fact, not a deficit of appreciation. The functional channel works on him fully; he can see exactly what a ritual coordinates and why the calendar has to recur. It's the accretion channel specifically that's missing.

## Lived consequence — holidays run high-cost, low-value

The cleanest case is holidays. They have often been hard for Alan, in his framing: **"high cost, low value."**

The math is direct once the two channels are separated. Holidays charge their full functional and social cost — the participation, the coordination, the showing-up at the fixed time — to everyone, regardless of architecture, because that's channel 1 and channel 1 bills everyone equally. But most of what makes a holiday *worth* the cost is channel 2: the accumulated sentimental return, this Christmas glowing with all the previous ones. Alan pays the full bill and collects little of the return that's supposed to offset it.

The reframe that landed: this is an architecture fact, not a failing. He is **"billed for a service his hardware doesn't consume."** The cost is real, and the channel-2 return is genuinely zero — the gap between them isn't a character flaw or a failure to try, it's the predictable output of paying a channel-1 cost for a channel-2 benefit his hardware can't receive.

But the cost isn't paid against nothing. The [borrowed-sentiment proxy](#the-gap-fills--a-borrowed-sentiment-proxy-witness-gated) supplies a separate, real return: his loved ones' enjoyment, witnessed live. So the holiday ledger isn't channel-1-cost against zero — it's channel-1-cost against a present-tense relayed return. That return is what can actually justify paying the cost, on its own terms, rather than the participation reducing to coordination or social-cost avoidance. (See the [functional-compensation reframe](#how-alan-compensates-for-the-missing-return) below.)

## How Alan compensates for the missing return

The functional-compensation question — given the channel-2 return isn't collectable, is participation worth the cost — has an answer sharper than "coordinate, or reclassify." The [borrowed proxy](#the-gap-fills--a-borrowed-sentiment-proxy-witness-gated) is itself a **genuine present-tense return** that can justify paying the cost. It is not merely a channel-1-only reason (coordination with Jen, social-cost avoidance, signaling) — it's a real positive return in its own right, just borrowed rather than self-generated.

So the holiday decision isn't forced down to "pay the coordination cost or [reclassify the item as not-worth-it](non-discretionary-reclassification.book-chapter.md)." There's a third option that was invisible while channel 2 looked like the only source of sentiment: pay the cost **to be in live contact with loved ones enjoying the thing**, and collect the relayed return that contact discharges. That return is present-tense and contact-gated, so it's only available when he's actually there witnessing the enjoyment — which is exactly when the cost is being paid anyway.
