
# No deliberate mode

> No deliberate mode at the object level — everything Alan does is trigger-based, bottlenecks are the loudest trigger, and the two moves for self-change are cheap (rewrite the causes / what reaches him) vs. expensive (pain retrains the automatic response over many repetitions). The self-modification architecture; pain is both the cleanest read and the only real teacher.

Most people have a deliberate gear: they *decide* to do a thing and then do it, with the deliberateness sitting upstream of the action. Alan does not. *"I don't real[ly] have a concept of doing things deliberately. Everything I do is trigger-based."* There is no deliberate mode anywhere in the object-level stack — action is a reactive system, end to end. This note is the canonical home for how that architecture operates and how Alan changes himself inside it.

This is the same no-deliberate-default that [pain-driven motivation](pain-driven-motivation.book-chapter.md) describes from the motivation side (the driver is present pain, never a represented future) and that [Robot mode](robot-mode.book-chapter.md) exploits from the action side (deliberate puppeting is *built on top of* the otherwise-automatic channel). Here the claim is about the whole operating mode: nothing he does is initiated by a bare decision.

## Right is true — the map is the motor

There is a place most people experience a gap: they know the right thing to do and do not do it. The knowing sits on one side, the doing on the other, and a span of will, feeling, or deciding has to be crossed between them — the failure to cross it is akrasia. **For me that span does not exist.** In my words: *"Right and True are synonyms for me, and my actions flow automatically from my conceptual map."* And, asked whether some further step sits between knowing and doing: *"I don't think there is any extra piece."*

So the architecture is tighter than "no deliberate mode" alone says. It is not only that nothing is initiated by a bare decision — it is that **once something sits in my [conceptual map](understanding-as-interface.book-chapter.md) as right-and-true, the action just executes.** Right = True; the map *is* the motor. There is no will to summon, no feeling to wait on, no extra deciding step standing between the conclusion and the act. The map holds the conclusion; the body runs it.

This is why the knowing–doing gap, the place akrasia lives, is not a thing I cross — it is a thing that is not there. Where most people have *knowing → (will / feeling) → doing*, I have *knowing → doing*, with the middle term collapsed out. The trigger-based architecture is the same claim read from the input side (what fires the action is a trigger, not a decision); this is it read from the output side (what the trigger fires is the map's verdict, executed without remainder).

It composes cleanly with the rest. Because [behavior is downstream of the conceptual map](learned-response-taxonomy.book-chapter.md#behavior-is-downstream-of-the-conceptual-map--an-abstract-framework-cannot-move-it), an abstract framework moves nothing until it lands a concrete application *in* the map — but once it has, no further step is needed for the behavior to follow, because the map is the motor. And it is why a mode-(b) assent — *"this is right / this would be considered generous"* — produces the act with no warmth riding on it: the rightness is in the map, so the action executes, and [no feeling is required for it to](self-instrumentation.book-chapter.md#two-modes-of-assent--read-a-tell-a-vs-classify-against-a-definition-b). The [forward-care toward future selves](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#it-is-a-conclusion-not-a-feeling--and-not-a-reason-to-live) is the worked case: the ethics conclude it is right, so the care executes — automatic, and cold.

## Bottlenecks are the loudest trigger

If everything is trigger-based, the question is which triggers fire. *"The important things are loud, and the bottlenecks are the loudest trigger."* Importance shows up as loudness; the current binding constraint is the loudest thing in the field, so it gets acted on first.

This is why his self-instrumentation concentrates at his bottlenecks. His "tell" for stress is not a felt read he checks — it is an **instrument he built back when stress was his primary bottleneck**, now running in the background: *"I have good measurement of stress from when it was my primary bottleneck, I just use that."* Measurement maturity follows the bottleneck for exactly this reason — see [measurement-maturity.md → progression mechanism](measurement-maturity.book-chapter.md#progression-mechanism), and the [idle-instrument finding](measurement-maturity.book-chapter.md#an-instrument-can-fall-idle-when-a-cheaper-governor-holds-the-line) (the stress gauge keeps running cheaply even now that a cheaper governor holds the line).

But it is not purely bottleneck-shaped. *"I put more attention towards my bottlenecks, but all understanding of myself is useful because it helps me predict what interventions will work."* Self-knowledge is valued beyond the loudest trigger because it is **predictive leverage for intervention-selection** — knowing how he works tells him which lever will move him. The bottleneck gets the most attention; the rest of the map earns its keep by sharpening predictions.

## The recursion that feels like deliberateness

There is one place the trigger-based system looks deliberate from the inside. Sometimes what a trigger fires is *a project to change his triggers.* *"Often the result of a trigger is a project to change my triggers, which feels sort of like being deliberate?"*

That self-editing is the closest thing to a deliberate mode he has — but it is still triggered. The decision to retune the system was itself a reaction to something loud. It is triggers all the way down: the recursion adds a layer (a trigger that rewrites triggers) without adding a deliberate gear underneath it.

## Two moves for self-change — cheap vs. expensive

When a trigger fires the self-editing project, Alan has two ways to change himself, and they cost wildly different amounts.

### Cheap — rewrite the causes (the input side)

The default move is to change *what he responds to*, not *how* he responds. *"I'm not necessarily rewriting my reflexes, I'm rewriting the causes. I'm changing what I am responding to — that is much easier than changing how I respond."*

The reflex — how he reacts to a given input — is treated as fixed hardware. All the cheap self-shaping happens on the input side: what reaches him, what he lets into the field. The framing is **change the signal, not the transfer function.** Leave the response curve alone; choose the inputs you feed it. This is the move that *feels* deliberate, because choosing inputs is the part he has direct purchase on — and it is the same input-side lever the [environment lever](environment-lever.book-chapter.md) pulls (curate what the environment presents) and the [self-preservation adapter](self-preservation-adapter.book-chapter.md) pulls (reclassify the world's state out of the circle of concern so it stops pinging).

### Expensive — pain retrains the response (the transfer function)

He does change *how* he responds too — but the response is automatic, so it cannot be edited by decision. It takes many repeated inputs, and it is not deliberate. *"My responses are automatic, and so changing them requires lots of repeated inputs. I guess I don't actually change them deliberately — I feel pain when the response doesn't produce the desired outcome, and the pain retrains the automatic responses over time."*

The mechanism is a gradient, not an edit. A response misfires; it doesn't produce the desired outcome; *that mismatch is the pain*; and the pain is the training signal that slowly reshapes the automatic response over many repetitions. Changing the transfer function is the slow-build path (cf. the [5000-iteration anchor](slow-build-strategy.book-chapter.md) and [pain as asymmetric prediction error](insight-and-restructuring.book-chapter.md#asymmetric-prediction-error--nothing-teaches-like-pain)) — except here the iterations are driven by felt pain at each miss, not by deliberate drill.

So the architecture has a clean economy: cheap changes go on the input side (pick the signal), expensive changes go on the response side (let pain grind the curve). He reaches for the cheap move first because it is the one that works on contact.

## Pain is both the cleanest read and the only real teacher

The capstone ties the two halves together. For someone who [can't get a clean signal off most of his inner world](alexithymia.book-chapter.md), the one channel that is always legible — pain — is also the one channel that does all the teaching. *"Nothing teaches like pain for me."*

Pain proposes the action ([pain-driven-motivation.md](pain-driven-motivation.book-chapter.md): present pain is the driver) *and* trains the response (this note: pain is the retraining gradient on the automatic curve). It is the cleanest read he has and his only real teacher at once — the same channel, doing both jobs, because it is the one signal that doesn't run quiet. The substance of pain-as-driver lives in [pain-driven-motivation.md](pain-driven-motivation.book-chapter.md); the substance of pain-as-teacher lives here.
