
# Church authority and the big/small line

> Church authority and the big/small line — the anatomy of one belief run through the compression gate: I stay with the body of the church, which can be wrong in the small things but never in the big things. Cross-examined against the demotion of authority, that belief turns out not to be a residual authority grant but a theorem — an implication of the foundational belief, with no footing of its own. The disposition it generates is: big-things-safety is asserted, so any observed mistake is classified as small, small mistakes are tolerable, so I need not concern myself with them. The whole load then rests on one undrawn line — what counts as big versus small — which is exactly where my reason-checks keep pressing and where the standing dissonance shows up. Rendered as my belief and its structure, not asserted fact.

This note is the canonical home for the anatomy of one specific belief: **I stay with the body of the church, which can be wrong in the small things but never in the big things.** It is the belief the corpus kept naming and never took apart. [Faith as an object of reason](faith-as-object-of-reason.book-chapter.md#grounding-a-rule-doesnt-settle-it--the-dissonance-regardless-structure) names it as the grounded belief that sits over the top of any faith-warrants check and produces dissonance regardless. [Faith and the clarified epistemology](faith-and-clarified-epistemology.book-chapter.md) flags it as the one belief that might still stand on authority after I demoted authority. This note runs it through the [compression gate](compression-epistemology.book-chapter.md) — the instrument that says a belief earns its place only by net-compressing my map, and that the source vouching for a claim gets no vote — and takes the belief apart. Rendered as my belief and its structure, not asserted fact.

## The belief, stated

I stay with the body of the church. Not because it is never wrong — it can be wrong. The claim is narrower and stranger than "the church is right": the church as a whole **can be wrong in the small things but will never be wrong in the big things.** Small errors are admitted up front. The reliability I lean on is only ever about the big things.

That is the belief the whole faith-loyalty structure hangs from. And it is exactly the kind of belief my [clarified epistemology](faith-and-clarified-epistemology.book-chapter.md) is built to be suspicious of, because on its face "never wrong in the big things" reads like a grant of authority — the church is a trusted source, so trust it on the things that matter. That reading would make it the one belief that survived the [demotion of appeal to authority](faith-and-clarified-epistemology.book-chapter.md#the-three-beats--inherited-clarified-applied) untouched: a residual authority grant hiding inside a reasoned faith. So it has to be cross-examined.

## "Never wrong in the big things" is a theorem, not a foundation stone

When I put the question directly — is "never wrong in the big things" earned by compression, or is it a residual authority grant that survived the demotion — the answer is neither. It is downstream of both. In my words:

> No, that is an implied belief if the foundational belief is true, not a foundation belief itself.

That is the whole move. "Never wrong in the big things" is a **theorem, not a foundation stone.** It does not stand on its own footing, and it does not stand on a grant of authority. It inherits its footing from **the foundational belief** — and has none of its own. If the foundation compresses, the implication comes along for free; I do not have to ground it separately, any more than I have to separately justify a corollary once the theorem it follows from is proved.

This dissolves the residual-authority worry cleanly. The belief is not the church being trusted as a source. It is a consequence of whatever the foundational belief is — so the load never actually rested here. It rested one level down, on the foundation, and got passed up.

## The disposition it generates — small, therefore tolerable, therefore ignorable

A theorem is not just an abstract entailment; this one runs as a live disposition. I asked myself whether the big/small distinction is itself derived from the foundation — whether the *boundary* compresses — and the answer located the real shape:

> It's not derived, instead the derived belief is of the form "the body of the church will not go astray on the big things, therefore if there are mistakes, they are small things, which are tolerable, so I don't especially need to concern myself with them."

Read the chain link by link, because the order is the mechanism:

1. **Big-things-safety is asserted** — the body of the church will not go astray on the big things. This is the theorem inherited from the foundation.
2. **Any observed mistake is therefore classified as small.** The direction of inference matters: I do not first measure a mistake's size and then decide whether it's tolerable. Big-things-safety is the premise, so anything I actually observe going wrong gets *sorted into* the small bin by construction — it has to be small, because the big things are safe.
3. **Small mistakes are tolerable.**
4. **So I need not especially concern myself with them.**

That is a disposition that keeps the foundation clear. Whatever failures a reason-check turns up get absorbed as small, tolerated, and set down. The structure is stable precisely because step 2 runs the classification the safe way around.

## The load rests on the big/small line — and that line is undrawn

Notice what the whole structure now stands on. The theorem is safe if the foundation is. The disposition is stable if the classification in step 2 is sound. And the classification in step 2 is exactly one thing: **what counts as big versus small.** The entire load has migrated to that one line — and the line is not drawn.

The boundary is **not derived.** I said so plainly: it is not that the big/small distinction compresses out of the foundation. It is that the derived belief *assumes* big-things-safety and then classifies downward from it. So the line that carries all the load is the one piece of the structure with no stated grounding of its own.

That is why this is the site of the [standing dissonance](faith-as-object-of-reason.book-chapter.md#grounding-it-ungrounded-still-wouldnt-settle-the-matter). My reason-checks keep pressing exactly here. The [worked case](sexuality-knot/pornography-and-masturbation.book-chapter.md) is the faith-warrants check on the zero-tolerance behavior rule — a rule I suspect is a leak from mainstream Christianity rather than something my actual foundations warrant. When a reason-check finds a small rule ungrounded, the disposition above says: small, therefore tolerable, therefore stay with the body anyway. So the dissonance does not resolve — it is the standing shape of a load-bearing line that reason keeps testing and the structure keeps needing to hold.

## The foundation named — one axiom, and falsifiable only there

The theorem-not-foundation move relocated the load down onto the foundation but left the foundation unnamed. Named now, it is one thing:

> That's the big project I'm working on with Talia, but the single most central point is [whether] The Book of Mormon is what it claims to be or is not.

That is the foundational axiom — **the single falsification test the whole edifice hangs from.** Everything above it is deduced off it. And that settles what "the load rests on the big/small line" actually means. The line carries the load, yes — but the line is not independently grounded, and that turns out not to be a weakness to worry at; it is the structure working as built. In my words:

> It is still falsifiable, but only at the foundational level, because invalidation of the foundation still invalidated the applied heuristic.

Read that carefully, because it resolves the frontier this note was carrying. The applied heuristic — stay with the body, sort any observed error into the small bin, don't concern myself — is **not independently immune.** It is fully derived from the one axiom, and it inherits its falsifiability from there. It has no separate footing from which it could shield the foundation, because it has no separate footing at all. Kill the foundation and the heuristic dies with it in the same stroke.

So the worry that the big/small line was "drawn downstream to protect the foundation" dissolves the same way the residual-authority worry did. A firewall needs its own standing to shield anything; this has none. It is a corollary that falls with the axiom, not a device that guards it. The whole structure is falsifiable — the falsification just enters at exactly one place: the foundation.

That is the maximally compressed shape of the belief: **one axiom — the Book of Mormon being what it claims — with everything else deduced off it and nothing independently load-bearing.** One falsification test, sited at the most central node, and every downstream belief standing or falling with it. This is the [compression gate](compression-epistemology.book-chapter.md) reading of the whole edifice: a single-axiom structure is the most compact form a belief system of this reach can take, because it stores exactly one thing and derives the rest.

## What this closes, and what stays open

This capture is the [clarified epistemology's method in action](faith-and-clarified-epistemology.book-chapter.md#the-three-beats--inherited-clarified-applied) — the compression gate applied to a specific inherited belief rather than to the faith as a whole. It **closes** the residual-authority worry: "never wrong in the big things" is not an authority grant that survived the demotion. It is a theorem inheriting from the foundational belief, with no footing of its own, so there is no smuggled "because the authority says so" hiding in it.

Two of the three things this note was carrying are now resolved, and the frontier has moved:

- **The foundational belief is now named** — [above](#the-foundation-named--one-axiom-and-falsifiable-only-there): the single most central point is whether the Book of Mormon is what it claims to be. The theorem-not-foundation move relocated the load onto the foundation; the foundation now has a name.
- **The big/small frontier is resolved, not open.** The question was whether that line is derived or drawn downstream to shield the foundation. Answer: the applied heuristic — its classification step included — is **fully derived and falsifiable only at the foundation,** with no independent footing. A protective device needs its own standing to shield anything; this has none, so it cannot be doing protective work in the sense the worry feared. It is a corollary that falls with the axiom, not a firewall that guards it.

What genuinely stays open is one thing, and it is not a note to write — it is the test being run: **does the axiom hold?** The positive first-principles case for the Book of Mormon being what it claims is exactly the falsification test [Talia](../personas/talia.book-chapter.md) runs, one verse at a dawn, outcome open. Adjacent to it is the newer frontier that lives in [faith-and-clarified-epistemology.md](faith-and-clarified-epistemology.book-chapter.md#the-inclination-is-occam--and-the-evidence-compresses-the-other-way): I hold the axiom *against my own inclination* — my Occam-pull recoils from the least-minimal explanation there is, yet the evidence compresses the other way.
