
# Germany

Germany's StAG was modernized by the **Staatsangehörigkeitsmodernisierungsgesetz (StARModG)** effective **27 June 2024**: dual citizenship universally permitted, standard residency cut from 8 to 5 years, fast-track at 3 years for exceptional integration, and the old "Optionspflicht" (forced choice for jus-soli kids) eliminated. The **Merz CDU/CSU–SPD coalition** repealed the 3-year fast track on **30 October 2025**, but dual citizenship and the 5-year standard remain intact. Among descent-style routes, **§15 StAG and Art. 116(2) GG** (Nazi-era restitution) are by far the most-used heritage paths.

## Jus Soli — Birth on German Territory (§4(3) StAG)

- **Eligibility**: child born in Germany to foreign parents; at least one parent has had **5 years** of lawful residence (down from 8) plus an unlimited right of residence (settlement permit or EU equivalent) at birth.
- **Dual citizenship**: yes — Optionspflicht abolished June 2024; child keeps both passports for life.
- **Timeline**: automatic at birth.
- **Backlog**: none (operates by law).

## Jus Sanguinis — Descent (§3, §4(1) StAG)

- **Eligibility**: at least one parent was a German citizen at the time of birth. Citizenship passes indefinitely down the line **provided** no ancestor was born abroad after 31 Dec 1999 to a parent also born abroad without timely registration of the birth at a German mission (§4(4) StAG generation cut-off).
- **Pre-1975 mother gap (§5 StAG declaration)**: children born in wedlock 23 May 1949 – 31 Dec 1974 to a German mother and foreign father (and other gender-discrimination cases, illegitimate children pre-1993) can claim citizenship by declaration. Window open until **19 August 2031**.
- **Dual citizenship**: yes, automatic.
- **Timeline**: automatic if eligible; declaration cases ~6–18 months processing.
- **Backlog**: §5 declarations at BVA Cologne running **12–24 months** (May 2026).

## Restitution — Art. 116(2) GG (Nazi denaturalization)

- **Eligibility**: former Germans who lost or were deprived of citizenship 30 Jan 1933 – 8 May 1945 on political, racial, or religious grounds, **and all lineal descendants** (no generation limit; 2nd–5th gen routinely admitted). No language, residence, or income test.
- **Dual citizenship**: yes — never required renunciation; this is restoration, not naturalization.
- **Timeline**: 18–36 months from application to certificate.
- **Backlog**: BVA Cologne **12–24 months and lengthening** due to surge since 2021 broadening.

## Restitution — §15 StAG (broadened Nazi-era path)

- **Eligibility** (added by the 4th StAG amendment, in force 20 Aug 2021): covers persecution victims **not** covered by Art. 116(2) — those who surrendered citizenship before 26 Feb 1955 via marriage, foreign-citizenship acquisition, or release; were blocked from collective naturalizations; lost habitual residence due to persecution. Descendants included.
- **Dual citizenship**: yes.
- **Timeline**: 18–36 months.
- **Backlog**: BVA Cologne **12–24 months**; high demand, often combined with Art. 116(2) filings.

## Naturalization by Residency — §10 StAG (Standard)

- **Eligibility**:
  - **5 years** lawful ordinary residence (was 8 pre-reform).
  - Settlement permit or equivalent right of residence.
  - **B1 German** (Goethe/telc/DTZ) — speaking and writing.
  - Pass **Einbürgerungstest** (33 civics questions, ≥17 correct).
  - Self-supporting income — no Bürgergeld/SGB II reliance for self or dependents (exceptions: not-at-fault unemployment, parental leave, education).
  - Clean criminal record (minor fines OK; conviction > 90 day-fines disqualifies).
  - Commitment to the free democratic basic order; declaration includes **explicit acknowledgement of German responsibility for Nazi injustice and Israel's right to exist** (added June 2024 + tightened 2024).
  - Polygamous marriage or proven gender-inequality conduct disqualifies (2024 addition).
- **Dual citizenship**: yes universally since 27 June 2024 — no renunciation requirement.
- **Timeline**: 5 years residence + 12–28 months processing.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: 15–28 months typical; Berlin LEA has **40,000+** open files with the oldest from 2005, currently processing May 2022 submissions; Leipzig ~1,290 days; Bonn ~130 days; Munich/Cologne ~24 months; Bremen processing Q2-2023 submissions as of March 2026.

## Naturalization — Fast Track (REPEALED 30 Oct 2025)

- **Status**: the 3-year accelerated route (C1 German + exceptional integration: civic engagement, top academics, high earnings) introduced June 2024 was **repealed by the Merz government**, effective immediately on **30 October 2025**.
- **Applications already filed pre-repeal**: grandfathered; processed under old rules if intake was complete.
- **Practical impact**: only a few hundred of 2024's ~300,000 naturalizations used it; symbolic but closed.
- **Replacement**: none — the 5-year standard is now the floor for §10 StAG.

## Naturalization by Marriage — §9 StAG

- **Eligibility**: spouse/registered partner of a German citizen; **3 years** lawful ordinary residence in Germany; marriage existing **2 years**; B1 German; Einbürgerungstest; self-supporting; clean record; loyalty declaration.
- **Reduced residence**: can be cut further if marriage has lasted 3+ years and public interest applies.
- **Risk**: separation before approval defaults the application back to §10 (5-year track).
- **Dual citizenship**: yes.
- **Timeline**: 3 years residence + 12–24 months processing.
- **Backlog**: city-dependent; Bonn marriage track ~130 days, Berlin/Munich 15–24 months.

## Spätaussiedler — Ethnic German Late Resettler (BVFG §4 + StAG §7)

- **Eligibility**: person of German ethnic affiliation (German ancestry + cultural ties + German-language socialization in family) **born before 1 Jan 1993** in the former Soviet Union (or, for Poland/Romania pre-1993 emigration, eased rules); formal BVFG admission procedure from abroad; **B1 German** (or lower for some family members); take residence in Germany within 6 months of arrival.
- **Citizenship**: granted **by operation of law** under §7 StAG once Spätaussiedler certificate issues — no separate naturalization.
- **Dual citizenship**: yes since 2024 (previously had to renounce origin citizenship in many cases).
- **Timeline**: 1–3 years from BVFG application to certificate + citizenship.
- **Backlog**: BVA Friedland processing varies; lower volume than restitution.

## Discretionary — §14 StAG (Naturalization from Abroad)

- **Eligibility**: habitually resident **outside** Germany; close personal, cultural, economic, scientific ties to Germany; **public interest** in naturalization. Discretionary — no legal entitlement.
- **Common triggers**: prior study/schooling in Germany, German family abroad, distinguished scientific/cultural contribution, spouse posted abroad in public service.
- **Dual citizenship**: yes.
- **Timeline**: 2–4 years; outcome uncertain.
- **Backlog**: BVA Cologne; volume low, decision discretionary.

## Refugees and Stateless Persons

- **Recognized refugees / subsidiary protection / stateless**: eligible under **§10 StAG** on the standard 5-year track (no shortened refugee window in post-reform law — the pre-2024 "6-year for asylees" exception was folded into the general 5-year rule).
- **Dual citizenship**: yes; refugees previously already exempt from renunciation, now universal.
- **Restrictions**: same B1 + civics + self-support + clean-record tests; Bürgergeld reliance disqualifies absent fault-free exemption.
- **Timeline**: 5 years residence + 15–28 months processing.

## Investment / Citizenship-by-Investment

- **None**. Germany has no CBI program. For Germany's entrepreneur visa (§21 AufenthG) and other residency programs, see [../residency/germany.md](../residency/germany.book-chapter.md).

## Service-Based — Bundeswehr / Distinguished Contribution

- **Bundeswehr**: §37 Soldatengesetz requires German citizenship to serve; **no military-service path** to a passport. The 2025 conscription law debate floated this but no provision was enacted.
- **Distinguished contribution**: handled discretionarily under §8 (resident) or §14 (abroad) StAG with public-interest finding — used for elite athletes, scientists, cultural figures. Rare; case-by-case.

## Re-acquisition of Lost German Citizenship

- **§13 StAG**: former Germans (and minor children) who lost citizenship may reacquire by discretionary naturalization; no residence requirement; close ties suffice.
- **§5 StAG declaration**: covers the pre-1975 mother-line gap and other discrimination cases (deadline 19 Aug 2031).
- **Auto-loss reform**: since June 2024, **German emigrants no longer lose citizenship** when voluntarily acquiring a foreign one — Beibehaltungsgenehmigung abolished. Former Germans who lost citizenship 2000–2024 under the old rule can reacquire under §13.
- **Timeline**: 12–24 months at BVA Cologne.

## Comparison Summary

**Ranked by speed and likelihood (May 2026):**

1. **Jus sanguinis (descent)** — automatic at birth if eligible; instant.
2. **Spätaussiedler** — 1–3 years if ethnic-German + BVFG-eligible (narrow population, closing demographically).
3. **§5 StAG declaration** (pre-1975 mother-line) — 12–24 months, no residence; window closes 19 Aug 2031.
4. **Art. 116(2) GG / §15 StAG restitution** — 12–36 months, no residence/language/income; large eligible diaspora.
5. **§13 StAG reacquisition** — 12–24 months for former Germans.
6. **Marriage (§9)** — 3 years residence + ~1–2 years processing ≈ 4–5 years.
7. **Standard residency (§10)** — 5 years residence + 1–2.5 years processing ≈ 6–7.5 years.
8. **§14 from abroad** — discretionary, 2–4 years, low approval odds without strong public interest.

**Effectively closed**: the **3-year fast track** (repealed Oct 2025); **CBI** (never existed); **military-service-to-passport** (never enacted).

**Dual citizenship**: fully permitted since 27 June 2024 across **all** paths. Merz coalition has **not** rolled this back — Dobrindt (BMI) confirmed dual citizenship and 5-year standard remain. Watch for further tightening on civics, language, or income tests in 2026–27.

**Flag-worthy 2024–26 changes**:
- 27 Jun 2024: StARModG — 5-year standard, dual citizenship universal, Optionspflicht abolished, no-loss-on-foreign-acquisition for Germans abroad.
- 27 Jun 2024: Added explicit Nazi-injustice / Israel-existence loyalty declaration; polygamy and gender-inequality disqualifiers.
- 30 Oct 2025: Merz government repeals 3-year fast track.
- Backlog crisis: Berlin LEA ~40k pending, Leipzig ~3.5 years, surge driven by 2024 reform demand.
