
## Verdict for the target profile (US citizen adult, spouse + 3 kids, ≤€500k capital, portable income, no EU ancestry, no EU spouse, no renunciation)

**Multiple residency routes exist — DAFT is uniquely strong for US citizens — but every route lands at the same citizenship gate: standard Article 8 naturalization at year 5, which requires renouncing US citizenship by default.** The statutory renunciation exceptions (spouse of Dutch citizen at swearing-in, refugee, country-refuses-release, unreasonable-hardship, born-in-the-Kingdom, Article 6 option, Article 10 Royal Decree) do not cover a US-citizen DAFT-track adult — the US accepts renunciations on demand (so the "country-refuses" carve-out is unavailable), the applicant has no Dutch spouse, and the US passport has no formal "unreasonable hardship" status. Dutch PR (Type I) and EU long-term resident status (Type II) are reachable at year 5 but neither shortcuts to citizenship in another EU country (residence clocks don't transfer across member states under Directive 2003/109/EC — Article 4 only allows up to 2 years to count toward a second member state's 5-year clock). Full citizenship analysis and reform-cycle status: see [../citizenship/netherlands.md](../citizenship/netherlands.book-chapter.md).

**Recommendation:** drop the Netherlands from the citizenship-via-residency survivor list, but keep DAFT on file as the cleanest "live + work in the EU as a portable US entrepreneur" lifestyle/PR option, since DAFT → Dutch PR → EU long-term resident is achievable without naturalizing or renouncing.

## Active programs

### DAFT — Dutch American Friendship Treaty (1956)

The headline US-citizen-specific self-employment route, codified by the 27 March 1956 bilateral Treaty of Friendship, Commerce and Navigation. **US nationals only** (and Japanese nationals, via the analogous 1953 treaty).

- **Capital threshold**: **€4,500** deposited in a Dutch business bank account, available as working capital for the business. For an *eenmanszaak* (sole proprietorship) the €4,500 is the absolute floor; for a *BV* (private limited) or partnership the rule is **25% of paid-in capital, minimum €4,500**. The €4,500 must remain visible on the business account at renewal — drawing it down to zero risks non-renewal.
- **Liquidity rule**: the €4,500 must be **liquid** in a Dutch business bank account (not equipment, not goodwill, not inventory). Personal savings count only after transfer into the company account. The IND's 2024-2026 expedited pilot (see below) lets applicants file without the bank statement up front but reserves the right to audit; the deposit must exist before any audit.
- **Business structure**: *eenmanszaak* (sole prop), *VOF* (general partnership), *CV* (limited partnership), or *BV* (limited company). Sole prop is the standard DAFT shape — fastest KvK (Chamber of Commerce) setup, no notary needed.
- **National-interest test**: **explicitly bypassed under the treaty** — DAFT is the one self-employment route in Dutch law that skips the points-based assessment the Zelfstandige route imposes (see below). No business plan scoring, no "Dutch economic interest" review, no RVO involvement.
- **Permit validity**: **2 years initial**, then **5-year renewals** (extended from 2 to 5 years on 1 October 2020). Renewal requires the business to still exist, the €4,500 still on the books, and the holder still actively self-employed.
- **Family inclusion**: spouse / registered partner and minor children receive **derivative residence permits** with full work and study rights (spouse can take any job, not just self-employment). Family files concurrently or follows.
- **Income**: no statutory floor in the treaty, but renewal scrutiny expects the business to generate income that meets the Dutch minimum-wage threshold (~€1,995/month gross in 2026 for someone 21+); below that, the IND may question whether the activity is genuine self-employment.
- **Processing**: **2024-2026 expedited pilot** — IND decides without KvK registration, balance sheet, or business bank statement up front; documentation required within 6 months post-approval, subject to random audit. Practical timeline: ~2-4 weeks from MVV application at the consulate to permit issuance. The expedited procedure reverts to the pre-2024 document-up-front model at some point during 2026 — file early.
- **Citizenship clock**: DAFT years count fully toward Article 8 naturalization's 5-year residence requirement (and toward PR's 5-year clock).
- **Path to PR**: after 5 continuous years on DAFT, eligible for **Verblijfsvergunning Regulier Onbepaalde Tijd** (Dutch national PR, Type I) and/or **EU long-term resident permit** (Type II, Directive 2003/109/EC) — civic integration at A2, no public assistance, sufficient income. No renunciation required at PR. See PR section.
- **Fees (2026)**: €375 main applicant, €248 each family member.

### Highly Skilled Migrant — Kennismigrant (employer-sponsored, listed for completeness)

- **2026 monthly gross salary thresholds** (exclusive of 8% holiday allowance):
  - **≥30 years old**: €5,942
  - **<30 years old**: €4,357
  - **Reduced (Dutch-graduate / Zoekjaar transitions)**: €3,122
- **Sponsorship**: employer must be an IND-recognised sponsor (referentie). Falls under the project's employer-sponsorship filter — **excluded** for the target profile.
- Listed only so the table is complete and so DAFT can be contrasted: kennismigrant is the dominant route most US tech employees use; DAFT is the alternative for the self-employed.

### EU Blue Card — Netherlands implementation (employer-sponsored, listed for completeness)

- **2026 monthly gross salary threshold**: **€5,942 standard**; **€4,754 reduced** for recent graduates applying within 3 years of completing higher education (the employer must still be an IND recognised sponsor).
- Separate from kennismigrant but with similar sponsorship requirements. **Excluded** per employer-sponsorship filter.

### Self-employment — Zelfstandige (non-DAFT route, points-based)

- **Open to all non-EU/EEA/Swiss nationals**, but US (and Japanese) citizens almost always choose DAFT because it bypasses the points test.
- **Points-based assessment** by RVO (Netherlands Enterprise Agency) on behalf of IND: three categories of 100 points each — **personal experience**, **business plan**, **added value for the Netherlands** — requiring **≥30 points in each** category, plus serving an "essential Dutch interest."
- **Permit**: 2 years initial, renewable. Decision period 90 days, extendable up to 6 months while RVO scores the file.
- **Citizenship clock**: counts toward 5-year Article 8 — same downstream gate as DAFT.
- **Verdict**: dominated by DAFT for the target profile. Listed for completeness.

### Orientation Year — Zoekjaar (post-graduation 1-year job-search)

- **Eligibility**: recent graduates (within 3 years of completion) of a Dutch master's, PhD, or post-doc programme, or a master's from a [Times Higher Education / QS Top-200](https://ind.nl/en/residence-permits/work/residence-permit-for-orientation-year) ranked international university.
- **Permit**: **1 year, non-extendable**. Holder may work or run a business without restriction (no TWV needed); employers do not need to be IND-recognised sponsors during the year.
- **Transition**: typically pivots to kennismigrant (reduced €3,122/month threshold) or DAFT (US citizens only).
- **Fees (2026)**: €254.
- **Use case**: only relevant to a US-citizen applicant if they enroll in a Dutch master's first — long path, not the dominant route given DAFT's directness.

### Family reunification — Gezinshereniging

- **Eligibility**: spouse / registered partner / unmarried partner (≥18 months documented cohabitation) / minor child of a Dutch citizen or of a permit-holder with a non-temporary residence permit.
- **Sponsor income**: sponsor must show stable, sufficient income — **at least the statutory minimum wage** (~€2,193/month gross including holiday allowance, 2026 figures) for ≥12 months continuously.
- **Permit**: matches sponsor's permit duration; work permitted from day 1.
- **Citizenship clock**: counts fully toward Article 8 (5 years), or feeds the **Article 6 option for spouse of a Dutch citizen** (3-year marriage + 3-year residence → option declaration, **dual citizenship permitted**). The spouse option is the only Dutch citizenship route that lets a US citizen keep their US passport — but it requires a Dutch-citizen spouse, which the target profile does not have.
- **Verdict**: not applicable to the target profile (no Dutch spouse). Listed because family of a DAFT principal uses this paperwork mechanism for derivative permits.

### Long-term study — Studie

- **Eligibility**: admission to an accredited Dutch HEI (university, hogeschool); the institution must be a recognised IND sponsor for international students. Proof of funds ~**€14,400/year** (2026; ~€1,200/month) per IND tables.
- **Permit**: matches programme length, renewable. Work allowed up to 16 hrs/week during term or full-time in summer.
- **Citizenship clock**: study time counts fully toward the 5-year Article 8 clock (unlike Germany's half-credit rule).
- **Use case**: viable as a long-path entry for an applicant willing to enroll in a master's, then pivot to Zoekjaar → DAFT or kennismigrant. Dominated by direct DAFT for US citizens.

### Dutch Caribbean — Aruba / Curaçao / Sint Maarten / BES (Bonaire, Sint Eustatius, Saba)

- Aruba, Curaçao, and Sint Maarten are **constituent countries of the Kingdom** with their own immigration laws (DIMAS in Aruba/Curaçao; Sint Maarten Immigration & Border Protection Service). BES are Dutch public bodies under European-Netherlands-style IND rules with local variants.
- Local routes include **Aruba Investor Permit**, **Curaçao Investor Permit**, retirement / passive-income permits (specific to each island), and employer-sponsored work permits. Capital thresholds vary by island (Curaçao investor: ~ANG 500,000 ≈ €255,000; Aruba investor: ~AWG 100,000 ≈ €50,000 — verify at file time).
- **Kingdom-residency clock**: years on a Caribbean residence permit **count identically** toward the 5-year Article 8 naturalization clock and toward Dutch PR — the statutory phrase is "lived in the Kingdom of the Netherlands."
- **Tradeoffs**: Caribbean processing is generally slower (18-24 months for naturalization filings via the Cabinet of the Governor); civic-integration exams are bilingual (Dutch + Papiamentu in Aruba/Curaçao/Bonaire; Dutch + English in Sint Maarten/Saba/Statia).
- **No CBI** in any Kingdom territory.

## Permanent residence (year 5+)

Two parallel PR statuses at year 5; the IND evaluates both automatically when you apply.

- **Type I — Verblijfsvergunning Regulier Onbepaalde Tijd (Dutch national PR)**: 5 years uninterrupted lawful residence, civic integration at **A2** (confirmed Dec 2025 as still A2 — the parliamentary B1 motion has not been enacted into law for PR as of May 2026), no recent reliance on social assistance, sufficient stable income, no serious criminal record. Indefinite, but lapses on >6 months absence in a single stretch outside the EU (or >12 months for EU-Long-Term holders).
- **Type II — EU Long-Term Resident Permit (Directive 2003/109/EC)**: same 5-year residence + integration + income criteria, but adds **EU mobility rights** — easier (but not automatic) application for residence in another EU member state. Under Article 4 of the Directive, up to 2 years of long-term-resident status in NL can count toward a second member state's 5-year clock; the second clock does not transfer in full.
- **No renunciation at PR.** US passport intact. PR is the realistic ceiling for the target profile if avoiding renunciation is the binding constraint.

## Closed / historical

### Residence-by-Investment (abolished 17 April 2024)

- **Status**: abolished by the IND on 17 April 2024 — fewer than 10 permits granted in a decade. Existing holders may renew; no new applications.
- **Pre-abolition terms**: €1.25M qualifying investment (since 2013).
- **Implication**: the residency-by-investment route to Article 8 naturalization no longer exists for new arrivals.

## Why Netherlands dropped from the citizenship survivor list

DAFT delivers the cleanest US-citizen-friendly EU residency available — €4,500 capital, family included, 2-year permit, 5-year renewals, no national-interest hoops, fast IND processing. Combined with the Kingdom-residency clock (Caribbean territories count), it is an excellent **PR + lifestyle** package.

But the **citizenship gate at year 5 is Article 8 with renunciation by default**, and none of the statutory exceptions (spouse-of-Dutch, refugee, country-refuses-release, unreasonable-hardship, born-in-Kingdom, Article 6 option, Article 10 Royal Decree) cover a US-citizen DAFT-track adult. Dutch PR (Type I) and EU long-term resident status (Type II) at year 5 do not shortcut to citizenship in another member state — residence clocks do not transfer. The Schoof / Wilders cabinet's 2025 proposal to extend residence 5 → 10 years was dropped by the D66-VVD-CDA cabinet in January 2026, but the **renunciation-default rule remains untouched**, and the B1-language hike is on the legislative direction. Full reform-cycle status and the option (Article 6) carve-outs: [../citizenship/netherlands.md](../citizenship/netherlands.book-chapter.md).

For the target profile, the Netherlands is a "**live and work, don't naturalize**" jurisdiction.
