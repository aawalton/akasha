
# Luxembourgish Citizenship Paths (May 2026)

Luxembourg's regime is the **Nationality Law of 8 March 2017**, replacing the 2008 law that first reopened dual citizenship. The defining feature was **Article 89** — a generous reclamation path for any direct descendant of a Luxembourger alive on 1 January 1900 — which **expired 31 December 2025 and is fully closed in May 2026**. **Dual citizenship has been fully allowed since 1 January 2009.** The real bar across most paths is the **Sproochentest** (Luxembourgish: B1 listening, A2 speaking; ~70% pass rate) plus the *Vivre ensemble au Grand-Duché de Luxembourg* civics course/exam.

## Birth in Luxembourg (jus soli — double + conditional)

- **Category:** Birth on territory.
- **Double jus soli (automatic):** A child born in Luxembourg acquires nationality at birth if **at least one parent was also born in Luxembourg** (Art. 1, 2°). Unconditional — no residency or naturalization step.
- **First-generation (conditional, at age 18):** Child born in Luxembourg to foreign parents (neither parent born in Luxembourg) acquires nationality automatically **at age 18** if: (a) at least one parent had continuous legal residence in Luxembourg for the **12 months immediately preceding birth**, AND (b) the child has lived continuously in Luxembourg for the **5 years immediately preceding their 18th birthday**.
- **Stateless / unknown-parents:** Children born in Luxembourg who would otherwise be stateless, or whose parents are unknown, acquire nationality automatically.
- **Timeline:** Automatic at birth (double jus soli) or at age 18 (first generation).
- **Dual citizenship:** Allowed.

## Descent (jus sanguinis)

- **Category:** Descent — automatic at birth.
- **Requirements:** Automatic at birth if **at least one parent (or adoptive parent) is a Luxembourg citizen** at the time of birth, regardless of place of birth (Art. 1, 1°). No generational cap as long as each generation passes nationality.
- **Timeline:** Automatic; registered at the commune or via the nearest Luxembourg consulate.
- **Dual citizenship:** Allowed since 2009.

## Ancestry reclamation — Article 89 (CLOSED, May 2026)

- **Category:** Special — descendant of a pre-1900 Luxembourger.
- **Status:** **CLOSED.** Article 89 of the 2017 law allowed any direct descendant of an ancestor who held Luxembourgish nationality on **1 January 1900** to reclaim citizenship by declaration. Two staged deadlines: ancestor-certification request by **31 December 2018**, then signature of the recovery declaration before a Luxembourg civil registrar by **31 December 2025** (originally 2022, COVID-extended to 2023, then to 2025). **As of 1 January 2026 no new Article 89 applications are accepted.** Files already lodged before 31 Dec 2025 are still being processed under the 4-month rule (final 2025 tail clears by ~April 2026; statistics finalize ~Jan 2027).
- **2024–2025 throughput:** 163 adults naturalized via Art. 89 in 2024; 377 in 2025; ~6,668 adults held valid pre-2018 certifications going into 2025. Final cumulative count from the 2008–2025 window will be confirmed late January 2027 per Ministry of Justice.
- **Workaround for missed deadline:** A subset of would-be Art. 89 claimants may still qualify under **Article 23** (option by parent/grandparent) — see below.
- **Dual citizenship:** Was allowed.

## Option — Article 23, parent/grandparent who failed to pass nationality

- **Category:** Special — primary surviving ancestry path post-Art. 89.
- **Eligibility:** Adults whose **parent, adoptive parent, or grandparent** is or was Luxembourgish but whose nationality did not transmit (typically because pre-1986 Luxembourg law transmitted only through the father, blocking lines that ran through a Luxembourgish woman who married a foreigner). Also covers anyone whose parent/grandparent would have qualified under Art. 7/89 had they lived to claim.
- **Requirements:**
  - Documented unbroken lineage to the qualifying ancestor.
  - **No language test, no civics course, no residency requirement.** This is the headline workaround for Art. 89 missers.
  - Declaration signed in person before a Luxembourg civil registrar in the Grand Duchy.
  - No serious criminal record.
- **Timeline:** Nationality acquired **4 months** after the Ministry of Justice receives the option declaration (absent objection).
- **Backlog:** ~4-month statutory window; post-Art. 89 sunset volumes are dropping, so 2026 processing is at or near statutory.
- **Dual citizenship:** Allowed. **No deadline.**

## Naturalization by residency — Article 9

- **Category:** Standard naturalization.
- **Requirements:**
  - Age 18+.
  - **5 years of legal residence** in Luxembourg, with the **12 months immediately preceding the application uninterrupted**.
  - **Sproochentest** pass: Luxembourgish at **B1 listening / A2 speaking**. ~70% pass rate. Failing the speaking half can be compensated by the listening score.
  - **Civics:** *Vivre ensemble au Grand-Duché de Luxembourg* course attendance **or** equivalent exam pass.
  - No custodial sentence of 12+ months (or suspended sentence of 24+ months) anywhere in the world in the prior 15 years; no fraud in the application.
- **Timeline:** **5 years residency + ~8 months** statutory decision window from the Ministry of Justice = ~5 years 8 months total.
- **Backlog (May 2026):** Statutory 8 months; the 2022–2025 Art. 89 surge stretched typical processing to ~8–12 months. With Art. 89 closed, throughput is normalizing toward 6–8 months.
- **Dual citizenship:** Allowed.

## Option — Article 16, marriage to a Luxembourger

- **Category:** Naturalization by marriage.
- **Requirements:**
  - **3 years of marriage** to a Luxembourg citizen, **AND** either:
    - **3 years of legal residence in Luxembourg** at the time of declaration, **OR**
    - 3 years of marriage if the couple lives abroad (the 3-year marriage clock can include time before the Luxembourgish spouse became a citizen).
  - **Sproochentest** pass (B1 listening / A2 speaking) — same bar as standard naturalization. No exemption for spouses.
  - *Vivre ensemble* course/exam.
  - No marriage of convenience; effective cohabitation required.
  - No serious criminal record.
- **Timeline:** **3 years marriage** (often parallel to the 3-year residency) + ~4 months Ministry processing.
- **Dual citizenship:** Allowed; no renunciation required.

## Option — 20-year long residence (Art. 12)

- **Category:** Option — long-resident track for those who cannot pass the language exam.
- **Requirements:**
  - **20 years legal residence** in Luxembourg, with 12 months continuous immediately before application.
  - **24 hours of Luxembourgish language coursework** (attendance only — **no Sproochentest pass required**).
  - *Vivre ensemble* course/exam.
  - No serious criminal record.
- **Timeline:** 20 years + ~4 months Ministry processing. Designed for cross-border / long-resident workers who never mastered Luxembourgish.
- **Dual citizenship:** Allowed.

## Option — completed Welcome and Integration Contract (CAI)

- **Category:** Option — integration-program track.
- **Requirements:**
  - **5 years legal residence** (12 months continuous immediately before applying).
  - Completion of the Welcome and Integration Contract (CAI) **or** the introductory modules of the Pact for Intercultural Living.
  - **Sproochentest** pass (B1/A2).
  - *Vivre ensemble* course or exam.
- **Timeline:** 5 years residence + ~4 months processing.
- **Dual citizenship:** Allowed.

## Option — settled in Luxembourg before age 18

- **Category:** Option — youth-arrival track.
- **Requirements:**
  - Adult applicant who **established legal residence in Luxembourg before turning 18**.
  - **5 years total legal residence** (12 months continuous immediately before applying).
  - Sproochentest pass + *Vivre ensemble*.
- **Timeline:** 5 years + ~4 months processing.
- **Dual citizenship:** Allowed.

## Option — refugees and stateless persons

- **Category:** Special — humanitarian.
- **Requirements:**
  - Adult with **recognized refugee status, subsidiary protection, or statelessness**.
  - **5 years legal residence** (12 months continuous immediately before applying).
  - Sproochentest pass + *Vivre ensemble*.
  - No serious criminal record.
- **Timeline:** 5 years + ~4 months processing.
- **Dual citizenship:** Allowed.

## Option — adopted minors

- **Category:** Descent — option for adopted children.
- **Requirements:** Minor adopted by a Luxembourg citizen, or by foreign nationals legally and habitually resident in Luxembourg. Declaration filed by the adoptive parent(s).
- **Timeline:** ~4 months from declaration.
- **Dual citizenship:** Allowed.

## Option — volunteer military service

- **Category:** Service-based.
- **Requirements:** Foreigner who **completes at least one year of voluntary service in the Luxembourg Army** under the Army Law; option declaration on completion.
- **Timeline:** 1 year service + ~4 months processing.
- **Dual citizenship:** Allowed.

## Distinguished services (Art. 14)

- **Category:** Service-based — exceptional contribution.
- **Requirements:** The 2017 law provides for naturalization on the basis of **exceptional services rendered to the Grand Duchy** (scientific, cultural, sporting, economic, or humanitarian merit). Decided case-by-case by the Minister of Justice; standard residency and language requirements may be waived. Used very rarely — a handful per year, mostly elite athletes representing Luxembourg internationally.
- **Timeline:** Highly variable; ministerial discretion.
- **Dual citizenship:** Allowed.

## Reclamation (recovery) of lost nationality — beyond Art. 89

- **Category:** Re-acquisition.
- **Eligibility:** Persons who held Luxembourgish nationality and lost it (voluntary renunciation, prior loss under pre-2009 law that forbade dual citizenship, etc.) may reclaim by declaration. Not subject to a deadline. Some sub-cases require residency or language; the pre-2009-loss path is typically formality-only.
- **Timeline:** ~4 months from declaration.
- **Dual citizenship:** Allowed.

## Investment / CBI

- **Category:** None.
- **Status:** **Luxembourg has no Citizenship-by-Investment program.** An investor residence permit exists and counts toward the 5-year naturalization clock — but confers no citizenship shortcut. See [residency/luxembourg.md](../residency/luxembourg.book-chapter.md) for details. Plan on the full naturalization track.

## Comparison summary

Ranked by typical elapsed time and practical viability for an outsider with no Luxembourg ties:

1. **Article 23 (parent/grandparent option) — ~4 months.** Fastest path if you have documented Luxembourgish lineage that failed to transmit (commonly via a pre-1986 maternal line). No language, no residency. Primary surviving ancestry path now that Art. 89 is closed.
2. **Reclamation of lost nationality — ~4 months.** Only if you or your direct line previously held it.
3. **Distinguished services (Art. 14) — months but not generally accessible.** Discretionary.
4. **Marriage option — 3 years + ~4 months.** Real bar is the Sproochentest, not the timeline.
5. **Naturalization by residency — ~5 years 8 months.** The mainline path. Sproochentest is the gating constraint, not processing time.
6. **Refugee / CAI / youth-arrival options — 5 years + ~4 months.** Same residency as naturalization but with the option-procedure 4-month decision window.
7. **20-year long-residence option — 20 years + ~4 months.** Only viable if you've already lived in Luxembourg long enough and want to skip the Sproochentest pass requirement.
8. **First-generation jus soli (at 18) — 18 years.** Only if born in Luxembourg.
9. **Article 89 ancestry reclamation — CLOSED 31 Dec 2025.** Not available in May 2026; any pre-deadline files are still working through processing but no new claims accepted.

**Bottom line:** If you have a Luxembourgish parent or grandparent whose nationality didn't pass to you, **Article 23 is exceptional** — no language, no residency, ~4 months. Otherwise expect the 5-year-plus residency track with the Sproochentest as the real filter; the language bar is high enough that many long-term residents use the 20-year option instead.
