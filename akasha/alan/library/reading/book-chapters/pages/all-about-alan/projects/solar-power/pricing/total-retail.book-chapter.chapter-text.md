
# Total Retail $/W

The "installed price" residential customers actually pay, before any tax credit.

## National Average — Multiple Sources

| Source | Year | $/W (cash) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| [EnergySage Marketplace](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2025/04/29/u-s-residential-solar-falls-to-lowest-ever-2-50-per-watt-said-energysage/) | H2 2024 | $2.50 | Median of quoted prices through their bidding platform; lowest since tracking began in 2014. -6.4% vs H1 2024. |
| [EnergySage 2026](https://www.energysage.com/local-data/solar-panel-cost/) | Q1 2026 | $2.58 | Pre-incentive average. |
| [NREL Q1 2024 MMP](https://data.nrel.gov/submissions/307) | Q1 2024 | $3.15 | "Modeled market price" — 8 kW system, models the typical channel (in-home rep, dealer fee, etc.). |
| [NREL Q1 2024 MSP](https://data.nrel.gov/submissions/307) | Q1 2024 | $2.74 | "Minimum sustainable price" — same system without trade distortions or above-baseline margins. |
| [LBNL Tracking the Sun 2024](https://emp.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/2024-10/Tracking%20the%20Sun%202024_Report.pdf) | 2023 install year | Median fell ~$0.10/W vs 2022 (inflation-adjusted) | 3.2M-system national dataset. |

Why EnergySage is below NREL: the EnergySage marketplace pre-screens 500+ installers and forces competitive bidding. NREL's MMP models the broad market — including the door-to-door and in-home channels that carry $0.48–$0.69/W customer-acquisition cost ([NREL soft cost work](https://docs.nrel.gov/docs/fy25osti/92536.pdf)). The gap between $2.50 and $3.15 is mostly channel selection.

## Cash vs Financed

The same system priced through a loan runs materially higher than the cash sticker:

- **Dealer fee:** 15–30% of cash price ([CFPB Aug 2024 report](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_solar-financing-issue-spotlight_2024-08.pdf)). Average loan adds **22% / +$5,700+** on a typical system ([NuWatt 2026 lender comparison](https://nuwattenergy.com/en/solar-financing-2026/lender-comparison)).
- **Mechanism:** lender charges installer a fee for offering a below-market APR (0.99%, 1.49%, 2.99%). Installer rolls the fee into the financed principal, never discloses it as APR.
- **Worked example (CFPB):** $26,000 cash → $32,500 loan principal = $6,500 / 25% dealer fee.
- **Lease / PPA:** dealer-equivalent markup plus no ownership of ITC for the homeowner (the TPO owner takes it); typically the worst total-cost option.

**Practical rule:** if you can pay cash, the cash price is your real benchmark. If you must finance, expect the quoted "price" to be the loan-inflated number unless you specifically ask "what's the cash price?" — then watch the same installer drop the system price 20%+ to honor a cash deal.

## Bottom Line for Provo 2026

A reasonable cash target for a competitive Utah quote, no battery, single-plane composition-shingle roof, 200A panel already in place: **$2.40–$2.65/W**. Anything quoted above $3.00/W cash is either a bad channel (door-to-door) or hidden complexity (multi-plane roof, panel upgrade, premium hardware) — investigate before signing.
