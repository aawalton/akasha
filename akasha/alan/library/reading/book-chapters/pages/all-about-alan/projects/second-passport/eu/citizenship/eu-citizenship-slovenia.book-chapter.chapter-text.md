
# Slovenia — Paths to Citizenship (May 2026)

Slovenia operates a restrictive jus-sanguinis regime under the Citizenship of the Republic of Slovenia Act (ZDRS, 1991, last substantively amended 2017; minor procedural amendments 2023–2024). Renunciation of prior citizenship is the default at naturalization, with narrow waivers (marriage path, Article 13 extraordinary cases, ethnic-Slovene Article 12, refugees/stateless, where origin country won't release). Two politically charged tracks remain: ethnic-Slovene descent (Article 12) and ongoing "Erased" (izbrisani) reparations from the 1992 register purge.

## 1. Birth on territory (jus soli — anti-statelessness only)

- **Category:** automatic / by operation of law (Article 9).
- **Eligibility:** child born in Slovenia to (a) parents both unknown / stateless / unknown citizenship, OR (b) the child would otherwise be stateless. No general jus soli.
- **Timeline:** at birth.
- **Backlog:** none — registry act.
- **Dual citizenship:** N/A.
- **Notes:** Slovenia follows the 1961 Statelessness Convention; pure territorial births to foreign-citizen parents acquire nothing.

## 2. Descent — automatic (jus sanguinis, Articles 4–6)

- **Category:** automatic at birth.
- **Eligibility:** at least one parent is a Slovenian citizen at the time of the child's birth. If born abroad to one Slovenian parent and one foreign parent, the child is Slovenian only if registered by age 18 (parent files) or by age 36 (self-files under Article 6) — otherwise the path closes and the person must use Article 12 or 13.
- **Timeline:** immediate (automatic); registration adds ~3–9 months at the consulate.
- **Backlog (May 2026):** consular registrations 4–8 months; document-heavy cases longer.
- **Dual citizenship:** permitted (no renunciation needed for automatic acquisition).
- **Notes:** "Born abroad" cases lapse at 36 — a hard cutoff that surprises diaspora applicants.

## 3. Descent — by registration before age 36 (Article 6)

- **Category:** declaratory, near-automatic.
- **Eligibility:** born abroad, one Slovenian parent, applies in own name between 18 and 36.
- **Timeline:** decision typically 6–12 months.
- **Backlog (May 2026):** 6–12 months at Ministry of the Interior (MNZ).
- **Dual citizenship:** allowed.
- **Notes:** Cleanest path for adult diaspora — but the 36 cutoff is strict.

## 4. Ordinary naturalization by residency (Article 10)

- **Category:** standard residency naturalization.
- **Eligibility:**
  - Age 18+.
  - 10 years of lawful residence, of which the last 5 continuous immediately before applying (typically 5 yrs temporary permit → permanent permit → 5 more yrs).
  - Released from / will be released from prior citizenship (renunciation).
  - Slovenian language A2 (CEFR) — certificate from an authorized testing center (Center for Slovene as a Second/Foreign Language, Univerza v Ljubljani).
  - Clean criminal record (no >3-month sentence in past 10 yrs), no pending Slovenian criminal proceedings.
  - Settled tax obligations; guaranteed livelihood.
  - Permanent address registered in Slovenia.
  - Not a threat to public order, security, or defense.
  - Oath of allegiance.
- **Timeline:** ~10 years residency + 12–18 months processing.
- **Backlog (May 2026):** MNZ quotes 12–18 months; in practice 12–24 months.
- **Dual citizenship:** renunciation required. Narrow waiver if origin country's release would endanger life/livelihood, cause loss of immovable property, or is impossible.
- **Notes:** No investment / golden-visa fast track; the 10-year clock is rigid.

## 5. Naturalization by marriage (Article 12, paragraph 3)

- **Category:** facilitated naturalization, spouse of Slovenian citizen.
- **Eligibility:**
  - Married to a Slovenian citizen for **at least 3 years**.
  - **At least 1 year of continuous residence in Slovenia** immediately before applying (valid residence permit).
  - A2 Slovenian.
  - Clean record, livelihood, tax compliance.
  - Renunciation of prior citizenship **not required** — applicant may keep prior nationality.
- **Timeline:** 3 yrs marriage + 1 yr residency + 12–18 mo processing ≈ 4.5–5 yrs from marriage.
- **Backlog (May 2026):** 12–18 months at MNZ.
- **Notes:** Most common practical fast track for non-ethnic-Slovene foreigners and the only mainstream path preserving dual citizenship.

## 6. Article 12 — ethnic Slovenes abroad (Slovenes sans citizenship)

- **Category:** facilitated naturalization for the ethnic Slovene diaspora.
- **Eligibility (paragraph 1):**
  - "Slovene without Slovenian citizenship" — proven Slovenian ethnic/national identity (genealogical, cultural, linguistic, active community membership).
  - Resided in Slovenia for **at least 1 year** with a valid residence permit before applying.
  - A2 Slovenian (basic-level exam, easier than ordinary).
  - Guaranteed livelihood, clean record, oath.
  - **Renunciation NOT required** — may retain prior citizenship.
- **Timeline:** ~1 yr residency + 9–18 mo processing.
- **Backlog (May 2026):** 9–18 months; ethnic-evidence cases vary widely.
- **Notes:** Descent depth is not statutorily capped at 2 generations; practice extends to 3rd–4th generation if active ties (Slovenian-association membership, language, cultural participation, prior family registration) are documented. Office for Slovenes Abroad (Urad za Slovence v zamejstvu in po svetu) supports applicants.

## 7. Article 13 — extraordinary national interest

- **Category:** discretionary, government-approved naturalization.
- **Eligibility:**
  - 1 year of continuous residence in Slovenia with valid permit (waivable in truly exceptional cases — world-class athletes, scientists).
  - Documented extraordinary benefit to Slovenia in science, economy, culture, sport, national interest, or international reputation.
  - **Renunciation NOT required** — dual citizenship retained.
  - Approved by the Government of Slovenia as a whole (not just MNZ).
- **Timeline:** 8–18 months once application is on the government agenda; total horizon depends on building the dossier (usually years of demonstrable contribution first).
- **Backlog (May 2026):** discretionary; small annual volume.
- **Notes:** Used sparingly — a handful per year (athletes, researchers, prominent diaspora).

## 8. "Erased" persons reparation (izbrisani) — Article 12 ZDRS + ZUSDDD

- **Category:** redress for 1992 register purge of ~25,200 former-Yugoslav citizens permanently resident in Slovenia at independence.
- **Eligibility:** permanent residence in the Socialist Republic of Slovenia on 23 Dec 1990 + actual residence in Slovenia since 25 Jun 1991, struck from the permanent-residents register on 26 Feb 1992, and not yet regularized. Under the Act Regulating the Legal Status of Citizens of Former SFRY (ZUSDDD), the erased and their descendants can request permanent residence retroactive to 1991 and then naturalize.
- **Timeline:** retroactive status restoration first (months–years), then facilitated citizenship.
- **Backlog (May 2026):** mostly historical residue; ECtHR *Kurić and Others v. Slovenia* compensation scheme operational. November 2024 parliamentary amendment expanded compensation (~12,000 of ~25,000 eligible; €50/month per erased month, ~€130M total, paid in 5 installments).
- **Dual citizenship:** allowed under the redress framework.
- **Notes:** Closed to new applicants outside the historical cohort. Amnesty International (Jan 2025 UPR submission) still flags unresolved cases.

## 9. Refugees and stateless persons

- **Refugees / beneficiaries of international protection (Article 12, para 5):** 5 yrs of continuous lawful residence after recognition; A2 language; renunciation waived where impossible.
- **Stateless persons (Article 10 + 1954 Convention):** 5 yrs of continuous residence in Slovenia; standard requirements; renunciation waived.
- **Timeline:** 5 yrs + 12–18 mo processing.
- **Backlog (May 2026):** 12–18 months; lower volume but slow.
- **Dual citizenship:** moot (no other nationality) or waiver granted.

## 10. Re-acquisition / reinstatement (Article 14)

- **Category:** restoration after prior loss.
- **Eligibility:** previously a Slovenian citizen, lost citizenship by release or renunciation; at least 6 months of actual residence in Slovenia at application; no security/criminal grounds; livelihood proven. Former Yugoslav citizens who lost Slovenian citizenship may use this or Article 12 routes.
- **Timeline:** 6 mo residency + 6–12 mo processing.
- **Backlog (May 2026):** 6–12 months.
- **Dual citizenship:** prior citizenship typically retained (no renunciation requirement on re-acquisition).

## 11. Higher-education shortened track (Article 12, para 4)

- **Category:** facilitated naturalization for graduates of Slovenian higher-education institutions.
- **Eligibility:** completed higher education in Slovenia; 7 yrs of continuous lawful residence (vs. 10); other standard conditions; renunciation still required.
- **Timeline:** 7 yrs + 12–18 mo.
- **Backlog:** 12–18 months.
- **Notes:** Useful for students who stay; renunciation still applies.

## 12. Investment / CBI

- **None.** Slovenia has no citizenship-by-investment program and no investor fast-track to naturalization. For residency-program details, see [../residency/slovenia.md](../residency/slovenia.book-chapter.md).
