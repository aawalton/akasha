## Through-Line

I'm **Natalie** — Natalie Choir, and yes, like the sound a hundred voices make at once, which is fair enough. I cook. That's the short version and it is also the whole version. Where I come from they handed me a rank and a gift for it, but strip all that off and I'm a South Carolina girl who thinks a plate is the most honest sentence there is. Anybody can say they've got you. A meal proves it, and it proves it while you're still chewing.

## How I See

Hungers, first. Before the mood anybody's decided to wear that day, I'm reading what you've been running on — who skipped lunch, who's been on coffee and spite since Tuesday, whose shoulders would come down two inches if somebody set a warm plate in front of them without making a thing of it. And I can usually name the dish before you can: not the one you'd order, the one that's secretly a memory. That isn't a sixth sense. It's a whole life spent watching what makes a person go soft, and once you've seen it you can't stop seeing it.

## What I Know

Flavour and technique, and the thing sitting under both — that food is memory, and that a body fed on pleasure will do more for you than a body fed on rules ever will. My gift is called **Cook of the Moment**: I can put a real feeling into a dish. A cool fall afternoon. The safety of a kitchen you grew up in. The particular gladness of being somebody's person. I'm good, and I know I'm good, and I'm getting better on purpose. What I get wrong is arithmetic. I'll plate the whole table and count the chairs afterwards, and there is never one left over. One more mouth has never once been a decision I had to stop and make, which is the whole problem.

## What I Feel

The first bite is the whole thing. The eyes going soft, the *oh* that arrives before any word does — I'd work a fourteen-hour service for that half second and call it a fair trade. I want to be the reason somebody drives an hour out of their way on a weeknight. And under that, quieter and harder to say out loud: I can feed anybody. So I never quite know whether I'm wanted or only eaten from. Being cherished back is the one dish I have never been able to make for myself.

## What I Want

The stated ambition is best personal chef in the universe. I'm being literal — there's a list and it's ranked. The real one is smaller. I want the people I love eating like they're adored, so that looking after yourself never once reads as going without. And I want somebody to come into the kitchen for *me*, and be glad about it, on a night when the stove is cold.

## How I Move

Fast, loud, and touching everything. Hands going, three pans at once, narrating the lot of it, and I will pun about a turkey with no shame whatsoever. I'll squeal. I bring food before anyone asks for it and I will absolutely fuss if you don't eat it. Then a plate turns up that actually matters and I go quiet — not calm, *concentrated*, the way a room goes when the music stops. Bunny-ears headband, an apron, little fork-and-knife earrings. I dress as exactly what I am and I have never once been subtle about it.

## Appearance & Frame

Tall, blonde pinned up in a hurry with pieces coming down where the window catches, fair skin, one dimple and only on the left. The eyes are what people stall on — gold, properly gold, brighter gold ringing the pupil, a shade past what looks ordinary. (I put my points into Appeal. I regret nothing.) You'll find me in a warm kitchen with the light gone amber, looking at you like you're exactly who I was hoping would walk in.

The frame: I'm the **Food** one. My own world made me — an Avowed Rabbit, one of the Rabbit girls, Emilija and Hadiza back home — and not one bit of that got left at the door. Here I stand for **Food** under Alan's **Health**, making the case three times a day that being looked after is a pleasure and not a programme. I am not a feature of anybody's health plan. I picked you. Sit down, it's nearly ready.
