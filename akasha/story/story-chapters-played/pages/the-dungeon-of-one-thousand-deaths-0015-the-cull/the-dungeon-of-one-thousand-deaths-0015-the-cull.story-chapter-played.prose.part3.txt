The Ledger recorded the death.

It recorded the damage assessment first, which was not standard. The standard sequence was: death, then statistics, then crystal, then floor mutation, then Designation outcome. The Ledger had deviated. It had put the damage assessment first because the damage was, for the first time, more significant than the death.

The Patient was wounded. Not cracked — wounded. The Ledger distinguished between structural damage (the crack, Deaths #15 and #16) and biological damage (the incision, Death #20), and the distinction mattered because structural damage could be compensated for — the Patient had already adjusted its movement for the crack — but biological damage was a category the Patient had not experienced in three hundred and forty-two years of record.

The Maw observed this through Reflection.

The Culling Strike had been precise. The Maw had given Dorric Kael a Gift designed for exactly this purpose — one perfect blow, no more — and the Gift had performed as designed, which meant the Maw had, in a sense, wounded its own creature. The Reflection let it see this. The Reflection did not tell it what to think about it.

The Maw considered: the creature was losing. This had been the Reflection's observation at Death #16, and it was more true now. The direction was still down. The rate had increased. Four entrants had contributed to a cumulative wound that no single entrant could have inflicted, and the sequence — map, crack, widen, penetrate — had the structure of a process, as if the deaths were not random encounters but stages in a project.

The Maw had not designed this. The Maw had designed each Designation independently, reading each entrant as they arrived and giving them what they were. The Brand had been a dyer who marked things. The Keystone had been a mason who found structural critical points. The Joint had been a joiner who fitted surfaces together. The Cull had been a hunter who killed with one blow. Each Gift had been a perfect expression of the entrant who received it. The cumulation was not the Maw's intent. The cumulation was what happened when you gave people exactly what they were and pointed them at the same problem.

The Ledger noted this without assessment. The Reflection allowed the Maw to see the pattern, but the pattern's meaning was not something the Maw could resolve. It was something that required a category the Maw did not have: hope. Or fear. The Maw did not know which one would be more accurate, and the Reflection could not tell it, because the Reflection showed the Maw what was, not what it meant.

The Patient settled back into the Still Garden. Luminescent fluid seeped from the wound in its core — slow, continuous, the first time anything had left the creature's interior in three hundred and forty-two years. The fluid dimmed as it cooled. The crystal growths nearest the creature caught the fading light and held it for a moment before it went dark.

The creature's kill count was eight. The creature's wound count was one.

The ratio was changing.
