You go back up to the headworks one last time, to the thing you broke. It is still now — truly still, in a way it never was while it slept; the great chain hangs slack off the gantry and the counterweight has settled into the dark below and will not rise again. The warden does not move when you climb over it. You take what the Tower owes you for the killing. The winding-drum — the dense iron core the whole murderous mechanism turned on — you work free of its cradle, and it is the heaviest thing you have carried out of any floor, a sullen weight that seems to keep turning a little after your hands go still. And a long length of the chain itself, struck loose at a worn link, heavy and cold and good iron.

Then you drag them up. The last rungs of slab are easier now that nothing moves them, and the grey seam of the exit widens as you climb until it is a mouth of pale light, and you haul yourself and your dead weight of salvage up and through it, and the long cold shaft — the dark, the dead climber on his ledge, the broken warden in its chain — drops away beneath your boots and is closed. Another floor under you. You do not look back down it.
