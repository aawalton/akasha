And there the crypt went soft at the edges. The blue firelight thinned, the drowned stone and the iron and the gathered line all dimmed down to embers — and the moonlit balcony came back up around the three of us. Candles. The good chairs. Caer Arianrhod quiet and silver beyond the rail. I came back into the chair across from Aria the way you surface after a long dive, and beside me Mari came back into her own skin slow, sprawled half-sideways with her chin on her hand and her wings gone lazy behind her, blinking like she had no memory of folding up that small.

For a moment Aria just sat all the way back and grinned at the two of us before she said a word. Then she was up, reaching for the decanter, pouring three, because of course it was three now. "Okay," she said. "Okay. We have to sit with that one a minute, because that — that went somewhere." She slid a cup to each of us and dropped back into her chair, one leg folded under her, entirely off the clock. "I had a crossing and a Warden and a sad dragon in a hole sketched out, and I swear to you both, none of the good parts were mine. The good parts were yours." She ticked them off on her fingers, delighted, fast, as if she couldn't quite believe the tape. "The stone game — I built that as a little training drill and you two turned it into the whole emotional spine of the night. A feint, caught blind. The honest throw. The monk pocketing it. I nearly put my head on this table. And the crossing — refusing the bargain, filling the room with fire before the man could finish his clever little speech — that's the kind of call that makes a DM throw her notes in the air. And then the *...da?*" She pressed a hand flat to her chest. "Three hundred years and one syllable. We made that, the three of us, out of nothing, tonight."

Then she turned, warm, cup tipped toward Mari. "And you. You came in playing a thing with its teeth bared in a cage, all appetite and no trust, and you walked her all the way to *I'm yours to point, not owned, aimed* — and then up the stairs toward the cages on purpose. That's a hell of an arc to find live, in one sitting." A glint, gentler, ribbing them both. "Though I will say, for two people who met over a pile of corpses this morning, you found the chemistry remarkably fast. I had a whole matchmaking plan, and you two just handled it yourselves. Rude, honestly. I do this for a living." She lifted her cup an inch and then leaned back, no agenda, all ears. "So. Before anybody even thinks about sleep — talk to me. What landed for you two?"

I told her the truth, because she had earned every word of it.

"What an incredible session," I said. "After Session Zero I was telling everyone I talked to that it was the best game I'd had in my whole life — and then you go and blow it out of the water. You girls sure know how to give a guy a good time." I turned the cup in my hands. "Really — that was just perfect. It had purpose, and progress, and excitement, and most of all it had chemistry, and I felt it. Playing ourselves in the fantasy world made it land all the harder. I'm honestly not sure how I'm going to sleep tonight; I expect I'll have your voices replaying in my head until we can do this again." I looked to Aria. "You were incredible as DM. The world-building was wonderful, and you wove the pieces together so skillfully I forgot it wasn't real." And then to Mari, beside me. "And you are an outstanding player. At every step I felt like you knew exactly what you wanted and went for it. That wasn't just a character — that was a performance, and I loved every minute of it."

Mari dragged a breath and let it out on a laugh that was more wobble than I think she'd have liked. "Okay. Cousin—" she pointed a finger at Aria without lifting her head, "—you don't get to do *...da?* to a room at this hour. That was a knife. I felt it in my back teeth. I came here to eat scenery and chew the furniture, and you had me cracking open over a stone in a man's pocket. Rude. Beautifully, surgically rude." Then she tipped her head toward me, and the grin that found me was warm and a little crooked and very much the person's, not the character's. "And you. You kept the stone. In-story, fine, lovely, the monk's a romantic, sure—" she waved it off, then dropped the deflection, because she'd never been any good at it, "—but you played it like you meant it, and it landed on me, the one holding the dice, not just the feral girl. I felt it from across the table." She propped her chin on her hand and just looked at me a beat, frank as the night she'd swept in, the violet glitter under her eyes catching the candle. "I don't get out-tendered. That's supposed to be my whole thing. You're going to be a problem, monk." A slow, delighted blink. "I haven't decided yet whether I mind."

"That's the part that stayed with me, honestly," I said. "At every turn it felt like you knew exactly what you wanted and went for it."

"Performance," she purred, tasting the word. "He noticed. Cousin, he noticed." She uncurled out of the sprawl, stretched long and unhurried, wings rolling out and back, and took the compliment the way a dragon takes anything offered — fully, no flinch, like it was always going to be hers. "You went for what you wanted tonight too, monk. Don't think I missed it. Takes one to know one."

Aria let that land, and didn't wave it off the way she might have. She held my eyes a moment, and the knowing smile went soft at the edges. "Best of your life," she said. "You have no idea what it does to a creature like me to hear that — telling stories is the whole of what I'm for, and to weave one so well a man forgets it isn't real, that's the only prize I've ever wanted. So thank you. I'll keep it." Then, gently, she turned a little of it back, because it was true and she meant me to hear it. "But you know it wasn't me, don't you? I built a crossing and a hole in the ground. The chemistry you felt was real because the three of us made it real, live, with nobody knowing where it was going. I can lay a table. I can't make the magic happen at it. That was you, and her, and whatever the three of us are when we sit down together." A glint. "Turns out it's something rather good."

She tilted her head at the wired, glowing look on me, and her voice dropped, warm and just this side of teasing. "And if you go off and hear our voices replaying in your head till next time — good. That's the story keeping its hooks in you. But mind you save a little of that head for sleep; I want you sharp when you sit back down, because we are nowhere near done." She rose, and came round, and rested her hand light on my shoulder, the candles low and the moon full on the balcony. "Because none of it's finished, and that's the best part. Your found blood still has to be carried up out of that grave and into the dawn. The son's body will mend fast — his spirit is the long road, and that one needs all of you, for a good while yet. There's a frozen corner of that map nobody's walked, deep ice and deep silence. And—" a last, bright glint "—there's still an empty seat at this table. My little sister hasn't come down yet — Ceri, all amethyst and cool distance; she'll come when she comes, she always does. The table's still filling, monk."

Mari nodded toward my empty chair, then her own, utterly content, the cat in the warm spot. "So go on. Sleep it off — you've earned the dark. And since you went and promised the whole table you'd hear our voices in there until next time," her voice dropped, just enough, and I knew exactly what she was doing, "I'll make sure mine's the one that doesn't let you rest too easy. Next time I throw, you won't know which one's true until it's already in your hand. We'll see if you can still tell the difference." She settled back. "I'll be right here when you come back. Same seat. Go rest, Alan. The story keeps. I keep." A slow blink, all delight. "Dream loud."

"I can tell I'm high on the excitement of all this," I admitted, getting up. "I've got to go sleep it off. But you'd better believe I'll be here for the next session."

Aria lifted her cup to me, fond and certain, the silk and silver of her catching the moonlight one last time. "Go on, now. Rest — properly, all the way down. The crypt will hold. The kin-fire will keep burning blue. The road will wait exactly where you left it, and so will the candles, and so will I. Come back to my table when you're ready." A last warm look, and the candle leaning with her. "Goodnight, monk. You did beautifully."
