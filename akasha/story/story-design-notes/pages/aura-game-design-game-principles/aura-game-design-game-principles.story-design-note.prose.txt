## Layer 0 — Pleasure, Play, Fun

Built in dependency order: primitives, then each term from terms already above it.

### Primitives *(undefined, taken as given)*
- **Agent** — experiences affect, takes actions.
- **Activity** — something an agent does, over time.
- **Pleasure** — a positive affective state ≡ intrinsically rewarding.
  - *note* — broader than common usage: pain sought for a net-positive state (horror) is a member.
- **Preference** — an agent ranks outcomes.
- **Influence** — an action can steer which outcome obtains.
- **Belief** — what an agent takes to be true, which may diverge from the truth.

### Enjoyment
**Enjoyment = pleasure in the *doing* of an activity, not from its result.**
- *note* — pleasure indexed to an activity; gives the nest Pleasure ⊃ Enjoyment ⊃ Fun.

### Autotelic
**Autotelic = done for its own sake; the motive lies *inside* the activity.**
- **autotelic ⇒ enjoyable.**
  - *why* — the only inside motive for doing a thing for its own sake is the pleasure of doing it; so "enjoyable" is **derived**, not assumed.
- **for ALAN ONLY, the converse also holds: enjoyable ⇒ autotelic.**
  - *why* — one attention slot; a for-the-sake-of-Y fills it, leaving no room to enjoy X.
  - *so* — instrumental ⇒ joyless for Alan, by mechanism; to be fun a thing must be held as its own end.

### Meaningful Choice *(subjective — the felt verdict)*
**A choice is (subjectively) meaningful iff the agent *believes* all three clauses.**
- **consequences** — the options lead to outcomes the agent has preferences over.
- **influence** — the agent's choice steers which outcome obtains.
- **non-dominated** — no option is *obviously* best.

#### The Belief Gate
**Meaning runs on belief, not truth — so each clause fails two ways.**
- *false-meaning* (believed-present, actually absent) — sustains play on an illusion; breaks when seen through.
- *false-deadness* (believed-absent, actually present) — kills real play; where the life-phenomena live.

| clause | false-meaning | false-deadness |
|---|---|---|
| consequences | feels real, is empty | feels empty, is real |
| influence | feels controllable, is random | feels random, is controllable |
| non-dominance | feels open, is solved | feels solved, is open |

- *note* — dominance is knowledge-relative: an undiscovered optimum doesn't spoil meaning.
- *Alan* — can't *knowingly* hold a false belief; seeing through an illusion dissolves it (being fooled is fine — sustaining a known illusion isn't).
  - *why* — others' belief bends toward what they want (desire→belief, bridged by emotion), so they can keep a thing they know is false; Alan's answers only to what seems true, so knowing kills the belief — to know he's fooled *is* to know it's false, no half-believing.

### Objective Meaningfulness & Justification *(the truth verdict)*
**Objectively meaningful = all three clauses hold *in fact* (every belief justified).**
- **justified** — a belief matches the fact.
- *lens* — felt-vs-true applies to every belief-gated term below: a *felt* reading and a *real* one, splitting only when belief is unjustified.
- *Alan* — realness wiring forces the two to coincide; belief can't drift from fact, so for him felt meaning is always real meaning — the false-meaning column is empty.

### Play
**Play = autotelic + contains meaningful choices.**
- **Play is enjoyable.** *why* — it's autotelic, and autotelic ⇒ enjoyable.
- **Play is voluntary.** *why* — autotelic ⇒ uncoerced.
- **Pleasure ≠ Play: meaningful choice is the differentia.**
  - *ex* — a fine meal: autotelic + enjoyable, no choice ⇒ Pleasure.
- **Play *develops* the preferences it tests — generating new ones and settling open ones, not just reading off ones already fixed.**
  - *why* — the influence/non-dominance clauses answer to facts in the world; the consequences clause answers to your preferences, which in play are partly *made* by the playing.
  - *so* — a wrong guess about what you'll like isn't an error but the **engine**: the safe lab for testing preferences.
  - **the second solving-engine** — learning your own settled taste also collapses choices: a game goes stale *for you* though unsolved in principle — you've solved *yourself*.
  - *note* — preferences form at the intersection of identity + psychology + experience (nature + nurture + choice — almost always all three; complex systems rarely have single-mechanism dynamics).

### Fun
**Fun = the enjoyment arising *specifically from Play* (third ring: Pleasure ⊃ Enjoyment ⊃ Fun).**

## Layer 1 — The Media → Gambling → Game ladder

### Objective & Rules
**Objective = an explicit, fixed target outcome (a crystallized shared preference). Rules = agreed constraints on permitted actions toward it.**
- *note* — the objective *is* the "differential outcome you care about"; having one is what first lifts an activity off a merely received experience.

### The ladder *(two questions, three rungs)*
**Is there an objective? then: does the structure hold real meaningful choice toward it? — the two answers place any activity.**
- **Media** — no objective: a *received* experience, nothing you're steering toward.
- **Gambling** — an objective, but no real meaningful choice in the structure (no influential, non-dominated line to find).
- **Game** — an objective with real meaningful choice in the structure (influential, non-dominated lines exist).
- *note* — only the structure grades the rungs; how it *feels* is the manipulation layer, not the position.
- *note* — the rung is structural; whether a Game is *also* Play depends on whether its choices are still live *for you* — a solved Game stays a Game but stops being Play.

### Media
**Media = a received structured experience with no objective the agent steers toward.**
- *forms* — choice absent (a film), or felt-then-seen-through ("media pretending to be play").

### Gambling
**Gambling = an objective with no winnable line in the structure — no influential, non-dominated path toward it exists to find.**
- *note* — defined by structure, not feeling: the colloquial casino word, where every patron counts however much skill it seems to take.
- *ex — one table, two players:*
  - *counter* — counting builds a real winning line ⇒ real meaningful choice, not gambling.
  - *patron* — the edge keeps every line dominated ⇒ gambling, however much it feels otherwise.
  - *house model* — sell the feeling (strategy theater, near-misses), forbid the real game (the edge), ban counters.
- **the superstition engine** — ritual / "system" / near-miss manufactures a *felt* control that isn't there; how the house holds you on the rung, not part of what gambling is.
  - *Alan* — can't convert the felt control into belief ⇒ sees the dominated line ⇒ won't sit as a patron.

### Game *(differentia partly open)*
**Game = an objective + agreed rules + a structure that holds real meaningful choice (influential, non-dominated lines exist). It *affords* Play; it doesn't *require* it.**
- *settled* — the structure (objective + rules + a choice space with real skill lines) *is* the Game; playing it — voluntary, choices live for you — is the Play it affords.
- *open* — plus, probably, a **bounded / contained / repeatable** frame that *regenerates* its choices; that frame (not the rules) may be the true game-vs-real-play line.

### A Game can outlive its Play *(solved ≠ gambling)*
**A solved Game is still a Game, but no longer Play — its once-live choices are now all dominated by the line you've found.**
- *ex* — tic-tac-toe: real Game and real Play for a child; still a Game for the adult who's solved it, no longer Play.
- *why* — Game is the structure; Play is the experience it affords *while the choices are live for you*. Solving exhausts the experience, not the structure.
- *vs gambling* — gambling never had a winnable line; a solved Game had one and used it up. Same "no live choice now," opposite sources — so a solved Game stays a Game, it does **not** fall to Gambling.

### Game vs real-stakes play *(the live boundary)*
**Both are Play with real meaningful choice; a Game's choices *regenerate*, real-stakes play's *deplete*.**
- *real-stakes play* (budgeting, investing, this conversation) — choices from real difficulty; gone once solved, no reset.
- *game* — a contained frame re-racks: solve the round, fresh choices return (bounded, fair, repeatable).
- *ex — is this conversation a game?* Play yes; game no, just short — real-difficulty choices that deplete, no regenerating frame.
- *open — the magic circle* — the "set apart, consequences don't leak" boundary (Huizinga): inside, the loss matters; outside, it's void. It feeds **Safety** by *containing* consequences. Open question: is consequence-containment the *same* boundary as choice-regeneration, or a distinct one? They seem to come apart — poker for real money re-racks (regenerates) yet leaks consequences (no containment), which is just what makes it feel unsafe.

### Artificial Constraints *(a mechanism, not a necessity)*
**Rules may forbid the efficient means (golf), but are *not* necessary for a game.**
- *why* — real objectives (budgeting, cooking) give non-dominated choices from real scarcity, no artificial constraint.
- *open* — their real job may be building the bounded/contained frame; the choices ride inside it.

### The classic four elements, re-sorted
**The textbook "voluntary participation + objective + rules + meaningful choices" splits across the Game/Play line: objective + rules constitute the *Game*; voluntary participation + meaningful choices are the *Play* it affords.**
- *so* — a solved Game keeps the first pair, loses the second: it stays a Game, stops being Play.
- *open* — rules may be not a peer but a **server** of meaningful choice (they build the frame) → "objective + a frame around inherited Play."

## Layer 2 — Game-Design Principles *(making the choices good)*

Implementation under the meaningful-choice element.

### A — Meaningful Choices *(the target)*
**A choice is meaningful iff the player believes it has real, self-caused consequences toward the objective and no obvious dominant option.**
- *Alan, verbatim (definition)* — "the player must believe that they can make choices with real consequences with respect to achieving the objectives and there is no single obvious correct choice that dominates the others."
- *Alan, verbatim (framing)* — "Making choices is the difference between play and media. Meaningless choices are media pretending to be play."
- *why "obvious"* — ties dominance to the player's knowledge; a solved-in-principle but undiscovered optimum still leaves the choice meaningful.
- *failure modes* — cosmetic · solved · blind.

### B — Clear Abstractions *(foundation)*
**Draw abstraction boundaries explicitly; *that* you draw cleanly matters more than *where*.**
- *so* — partial consistency is the symptom of an undrawn line.
- *ex* — item/skill/class rarity on one curve: good if "rarity" is one abstraction, missed opportunity if three.

### C — Reliable Consistency *(within an abstraction)*
**Same identity ⇒ same behavior, everywhere.**
- *Alan, verbatim* — "A given abstraction should look the same and act the same everywhere you see it."
- *so* — learnable (Safety); choices legible.

### D — Interesting Differences *(across abstractions)*
**Different identity ⇒ different behavior.**
- *Alan, verbatim* — "Different abstractions should work differently; repeated patterns across genuinely-distinct abstractions are missed opportunities."
- *so* — choices become real (Arousal); repeat the shared grammar, differentiate the distinct abstractions.

### Contract (C + D) — identity ⟺ behavior
**Couple what a thing *is* to what it *does*; decoupling them is the shared enemy. Both presuppose clear abstractions.**
- *the two felt dimensions* — C (consistency) feeds **Safety**, D (difference) feeds **Arousal**; Fun is felt on both. A design pattern the Layer-0 definitions imply but don't contain.

### E — Honest Preference Development *(protect the value half)*
**Play should develop preferences *honestly*. Manipulation — FOMO, variable-ratio conditioning — rigs *what you want* and corrupts the value half; worst when extractive, context-dependent, not an absolute ban.**
- *the draw is innocent* — randomness itself just paces exploration; play is a **whole-game property**, so judge a mechanic by its contribution to the whole game, not its solo playability.

## Layer 3 — Genre applications *(live in each game's package CLAUDE.md)*

### Idle — keep the optimum unsolved
**A solved finite game stays a Game but stops being Play — every choice collapses to the dominant line, so the fun dies even though the structure stands.**
- *so* — re-decomposition wrinkles defend the choices; keep out extractive preference-manipulation (FOMO/conditioning), not randomness itself — a draw can pace exploration.
