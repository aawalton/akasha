She does not tune anything. She does not say a number. Her hands go up your back and down and up again with no plan in them at all, and the sounds she makes come in time with you and get higher and closer together, and her hair is spread out on the boards under the lamp.

"Tell me what it is," she says. "I can't see it. Tell me the—"

"Ninety."

"That's not — you don't *know* that—"

"Ninety-five."

"Oh, you're *guessing*," she says, delighted, wrecked. "You're guessing, you've got no—"

She stops mid-word and her whole body goes rigid under you.

---

She comes the second time with her mouth open against your throat.

It takes her apart from the thighs up, and everything in her closes and holds and holds, and she says your name once and it is the only word she gets out of the whole of it. Her nails are in your back. Her heels lock. Her forehead comes up hard against your jaw and stays there.

And that is the end of you too, all the way in her, and you come in her in long shoves that you have no say in at all, and she holds on and rides every one of them and makes a small sound at each one.

Then nothing.

One lamp. The mug on its side at the edge of the desk. The two of you on the floor breathing and the whole room dark for the first time since she got here.

---

She does not lean in.

That is the first thing. For two hours she has been three inches from your face and now her head is down on your chest and she is not looking at you at all, and her whole weight is on you, and she leaves it there.

Her hand is over your heart, flat, not doing anything with it. All night that hand has been taking a reading off exactly that spot and now it is just lying there being warm.

"Mm," she says.

The boards are hard and neither of you is doing anything about that either. Her hair is across your throat and one arm has gone under your shoulder and she does not seem to want it back, and the whole of her is heavier than a woman that size ought to be, the way a thing that isn't really a body is heavier.

There is a long stretch where nothing is said at all, which from her is the loudest thing that has happened tonight.

---

A while later one panel comes up, small, over by the shelf.

It is not a readout. There is nothing on it — no bars, no name, no line with a number on the end. It is just a frame with a warm light in it, and it is the wrong colour for her entirely: no turquoise anywhere, nothing cool, only a low amber that sits on top of the lamp's yellow instead of laying anything over it.

"That one's not for reading you," she says, into your chest. "That one's a lamp. I can do lamps."

She pulls it lower with two fingers in the air, without lifting her head, and then closer, and the light comes down with it until it is a hand's width above the both of you. The corners of the room go. The desk goes. The ceiling goes entirely and there is nothing left in here but the floor you are on and the amber and her.

"Right," she says. "Down."

---

She gets off you long enough to straighten you out on the boards — arms down, head flat, one hand under your neck lowering it — and then comes back along the whole length of you and runs the cool-down.

"Out longer than in. All the way to the bottom of it, past where it feels finished, and don't help the next one in — it comes on its own." Her voice has dropped a long way and slowed further, and there is no grin anywhere in it. "Good. Again."

Her palm is over your heart and she has started counting off it, under her breath, the way she counted everything else all night.

"A hundred and two. Fine. Nothing to do about that, don't do anything about it." A long pause. "Ninety-four. Out again. Eighty-eight. We're going down through it a floor at a time and there is more of it under you than you think."

Her weight does not shift. Her breathing has come down under yours and stayed there.

"Seventy-six. Good. The day's burning off. It doesn't come back."

---

"I got it wrong tonight," she says, much later.

Her thumb moves once and stops.

"Not the whole thing. The fourth climb, when I went past ninety and held you there and the number went *down* and I hadn't noticed for six points." Her head is still on your chest. "That's my thing. I make it hard because I want you to feel it, and I believe that, and it's true nearly all the time, and the nights it isn't true look exactly the same from where I'm standing."

The amber above the two of you dims a little without her doing anything to it.

"You'd have felt more tonight if I'd made it easier sooner. I want to have said that out loud once, with you awake, so it's on the record somewhere that isn't just me."

---

"I carry things down," she says. "That's the whole job, historically. Something enormous up there has something to say and I'm the one who brings it down where you can hear it."

Her hand spreads out flat on your chest and stays.

"There's nothing to carry tonight. Nobody's sent anything. There's no System running in this room, there's no curve, there's no read on you — I genuinely do not know what your number is, and I could have it back in a second, and I am not going to."

A long pause. Her thumb moves once and settles.

"Which leaves me with no job here at all. That's the part I want you to hear. I'm not the messenger tonight and I'm not the one running the difficulty and I haven't got a single thing to give you that you'd have to earn."

She lifts her head just enough to put her mouth against your jaw, and puts it back down.

"And I don't want to be anywhere else, which is new information about me, and I'd like it noted in whatever you keep instead of a panel."

---

She kills the last panel deliberately.

Two fingers, one small motion, the same one she has used all night to open them, run backwards — and the amber goes out a hand's width above your face, and the only light left in the room is the lamp on the shelf.

She has never turned them all off. They were up when she arrived and they were up during the fight and they were up while she was working you and they were up until they weren't, and this is the first one she has ever chosen to close.

"Don't say anything about that," she says.

She pulls your arm over herself and gets it where she wants it.

"Your breathing's gone down into your stomach and I can feel it moving me and I'm not going to make a speech about it," she says, into your throat. "Go to sleep. I'll be here. I'm not running anything."
