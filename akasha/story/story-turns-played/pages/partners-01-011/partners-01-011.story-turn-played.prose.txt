You talk for a while about small things, because small things are exactly what you asked the world to be made of again — and she has an endless supply, handed over low and unhurried while the fire works.

"...so old Merrin the baker down on the mill-lane burns his first tray every frost-morning without fail — forty years at it and still can't tell when the ovens run hot on a cold dawn — and every frost-morning his wife tells the whole queue about it. You'll meet them; you'll meet everyone. Market day's the day after tomorrow, and the vale comes up the road with turnips and gossip in equal measure. There's a woman sells honey I'd trust with my life, and a man sells cheese I wouldn't trust with my coat, and I'll steer you round both." A small smile. "See — dull and real, the pair of them. The world's still out there, full of turnip-sellers, waiting on you."

And it works — that's the strange part. The dream loosens its grip a knuckle at a time, argued down by frost-morning bakers and untrustworthy cheese. But there's a distance the ordinary can't quite close on its own, and somewhere in the warm dark you find the honest thing and say it.

"Would you mind if I come closer? I think physical contact would be really helpful to ground me right now."

"Would I mind." A beat — and you can see it catch her, somewhere behind the firelight. "You keep asking, do you know that. Twice tonight, once last evening, and nobody told you to — you've simply read me right, in two days, which is more than most manage in two years." Warm. "No. I don't mind. Come here."

She draws the blanket wide to cover you both, shifts until you sit shoulder to shoulder, and folds your hand into both of hers — solid, deliberate, nothing tentative about any of it.

"There. Shoulder to shoulder — that's a whole person sat beside you, warm and breathing and entirely real. And here—" your hand, held in both of hers "—my hand, with a pulse in it, and it isn't going anywhere. That's the world, Alan. Real as it gets. Lean, if you want to. I'm steadier than I look — and I look fairly steady."

Her shoulder is warm through wool and linen. Her pulse keeps its own unhurried time against your palm, entirely uninterested in nightmares. The fire has the hall gold to the beams now, the frost keeps working the far side of the glass without ever getting in, and the night — which had felt like something you'd fallen out of the world into — settles instead into something the two of you are simply sitting inside of, together.
