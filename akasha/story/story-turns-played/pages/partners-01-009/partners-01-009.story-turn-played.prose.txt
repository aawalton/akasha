Your room does its part. You undress down to your boxers, and — on a thought — check the closet: among a few older things smelling of cedar it holds pajamas, soft and warm and comfortable, and exactly your size. You decide to be glad of that instead of unsettled by it, and sleep takes you within minutes.

The dream, when it comes, has no shape you can keep. A road walked backward. A door that was never there, closing. A life's worth of names sliding off their faces one by one, and no hand of yours quick enough to catch any of them. You wake with the cry already out of you — sitting up, heart hammering, the dark of an unfamiliar room pressing close.

You don't try to go back to sleep. You find your way out through the corridor to the Hearthall, where the hearthstone keeps its low red-gold breathing, and there is a couch — deep, old, facing the warmth — that might as well have been put there for exactly this. You sit, and let the fire's slow rhythm argue your heart back down.

A light comes along the corridor — and a voice arrives before the shape does, pitched low so a rattled man isn't startled twice in one night.

"It's only me, Alan. It's Amy. I heard you — I'll not creep up on you."

She comes into the hearth's glow with a small lamp turned low, and keeps an easy distance. It's the first time you've seen her like this: a plain nightgown of fine soft linen under a wool dressing-robe in some quiet deep color, held loosely closed against the chill; soft slippers on the cold flagstones; and her hair down — a half-fallen sleep-braid, softer around her face than the pinned daytime version, the lamplight finding the gold in it. The polish is off-duty, plainly just-woken — and no less self-possessed for it.

"I'll come no nearer than you'd like. Are you hurt — or only shaken?"

And before you've finished answering the second half of that honestly, she has crossed just far enough to set a cup of water on the table within your reach — unfussed, as if it had always been going to be there.

"I'll not ask what you dreamt — it's yours, and naming it in the dark seldom helps in any case. But I'll ask you this: do you want company, or the room to yourself to let it clear? Both are decent answers, and I'll not be wounded by either." A small, steady warmth. "If it's company — there's a banked hearth wants stirring, I know where the good blankets are kept, and I'm wide awake now regardless. If it's solitude — I'll take myself back to bed and leave you the lamp. Your call entirely."

The hearth settles with a soft sound, somewhere deep in its banked gold. The lamp's flame stands steady between you.
