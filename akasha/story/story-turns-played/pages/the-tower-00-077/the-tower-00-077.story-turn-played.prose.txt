You give ground, fast.

Not in panic — on purpose. You back toward the way you came, toward the narrowing neck of stone that drops back to the cleared hall and the threshold beyond it, and you make the dark work for you: out in the open gallery the two of them could come at you from two sides at once, the second sliding wide to take you from where you weren't looking — but a thing that wants to catch a man who is leaving has to FOLLOW him, and there is only one mouth back. You feel them have to give up the flank for the chase. The wrongness that was opening apart around you closes back into a line: one of them pressing close behind, the other crowding the narrow space at its back, both funneled into the single throat of stone you are giving them, because that is the only way left to reach you.

You let the near one close. You wait, backing, until the skin-crawl of it is almost on you in the dark — and then you stop giving ground and you give them fire instead. You pour a wave of it down the throat of the corridor, the heaviest you can raise on what you've got, a flood of ember filling the one narrow lane they have to come through. It doesn't matter that you can't see them; they have to be HERE, in this lane, or they don't catch you — and so this is where you burn. And it CONNECTS. You feel it bite something real: the near wrongness flaring up hard and reeling back, the one crowded behind it catching the edge of the flood and recoiling too, real essence searing under real flame — not the empty hollow gutter the false heat would give, not light pretending to burn, but the genuine sick flare of living things that have just caught fire. You hurt them. Badly, maybe. You feel them reel.

You do not feel them fall.

And there is no time to learn which. You turn your back on the burning lane and you RUN — and giving your back, blind, to something that fast is the price of leaving, and you pay it: a blow rakes across you from behind, the trailing thing lunging through its own scorching to reach you, and it lands hard, a deep tearing line of pain that nearly takes your feet. You keep them. You run through it, down the neck, across the dead hall where the keeper's ash still lies, and you throw yourself across the threshold-stone — 

— and the wrongness ENDS. Sharp, like a line drawn: the heatless gold, the false welcome, the skin-crawl pressure, all of it cut off clean at the door, unable or unwilling to follow you out. Behind you the haven closes over itself, its hunger banked back down to patience; ahead the honest cold dark of the shaft you climbed waits, known and indifferent. You are out. You are bleeding, and your fire is burned down low, and you did not clear that room — but you are out, and you know more than you did, and you are alive to spend it. The threshold-stone is under you. The way down is open at your back, and the haven is shut at your front, waiting for you to try it again.
