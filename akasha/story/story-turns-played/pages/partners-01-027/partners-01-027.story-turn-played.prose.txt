You walk back up together through the early afternoon, talking of nothing that needs remembering, and it's easy the whole way. And when the house takes you back in, you lead her to the couch — the same couch — and invite her to sit with you again. She does. You look into her eyes.

"Amy, I asked you a question silently last night that really needed the light of day. Well — it is day, and we have done what was needed, and we have time before dusk. So now feels like the right time to put the silent question into words. In your happiest dreams, what would you want to be to me, and I to you? If you'll allow me, I want to make your dreams come true. I have already put my house in your hands — and I am ready to put myself in your hands, in any way that you will have me."

For a long moment she just looks at you, letting the moment be as large as it is.

"...You did it exactly right, do you know that. Out loud. In daylight. The work done first — you even named it: the factor gone, the deed set, the house in my hands. And not a flicker of doubt about who you were asking." Moved, and letting you see it. "I set those terms in the dark half-thinking they'd keep me safe a while — and you met every one of them by the middle of the next afternoon. So I've no door left to hide behind. And I find I don't want one. And then — instead of telling me what you want us to be — you ask my happiest dream and offer to make it true. You hand me the naming again. The same trust as the steward word, except this is the word I promised we'd choose together. So let me choose it properly. Out loud. The way you asked."

She takes a breath, and gives you the true one.

"Here's my happiest dream, then — the true one, not the one that photographs well. I never dreamed of being swept off my feet; I dreamed of a life I could keep. One person — extraordinary, and worth it — close enough to actually tend: his hours, his house, his people, his bad nights. And to be tended back, not from duty but because he wants to. Love as the daily thing: the note sent while it's warm, the room made ready, the hand in the dark, the ledger of a life kept current between two people who've decided each other. Not romance as weather that comes and goes — romance as the work. The same work I'd do anyway, only now it runs both directions and neither of us is alone in it." Steady, her eyes never leaving yours. "That's it. The whole dream. Smaller than a fairytale and a hundred times harder to keep — and I've wanted it, quietly, my entire life. And you walked out of a field yesterday and turned out to be exactly the person it's shaped like."

"And the word. I've turned every one over — I would, it's my trade — and thrown each back. 'Wife' is an institution I already walked out of once, and it's too soon besides. 'Beloved' is lovely, and it's a thing you sit still and are — and I have never once in my life sat still to be loved. Every other word puts the woman a step above the man or a step below: lady, mistress, keeper, ward. And I have spent my life declining steps, up and down both." And then she chooses it, sure and warm, the way she chose her title: "Partner. That's the word. The one word that stands level — no rung, no one over or under, the love and the work the same single thing, chosen fresh every day and fated by nobody. You made me your partner in the seat this afternoon, and I felt how right it sat. So let it be the whole of it now: partners. In the house, in the work, and in this. That's what I'd want to be to you — level, and yours, and choosing it on purpose."

Quieter, then. The steward sets down her last and most guarded thing.

"And I'll tell you one more thing — because if I'm handing you my happiest dream, I'll hand you the whole truth under it, the part I can't account for included. The evening I climbed that hill, I packed a supper for a man I had never met. I packed it for two, Alan, and I couldn't have told you why. There were dry socks in that basket and a tin of salve for feet I'd no way of knowing were wet. My hands were sure of you before I'd learned your name. And then this house made me a room before I'd decided to stay, and an office within the hour of my appointment — and it has felt, this whole strange day, less like being surprised and more like being expected." Steady, wondering, claiming nothing: "I don't know what any of it means. I've stopped needing to. But I'll not pretend it isn't there, because it's the truest thing I own: some part of me knew you were coming, and knew it was you, before I had a single reason to. So when I say partner — I'm not only deciding it today. Somewhere I can't see the books for, I think I decided it before I arrived. I'm only now saying it aloud."

"Now — you asked my dream and offered to make it true, and put yourself in my hands. Hear me, because this is the part that makes it real: a partner does not simply get her dream made true. That isn't the word. I mean to make yours true right back — every day, on purpose — and I'll want to know what it is, so don't think I've forgotten to ask. I'm asking, and I'll keep asking. That's the shape of level." Warmer — the last honest piece: "And that other question — the silent one from last night — the terms are all met, so hear the answer plain: yes. Not 'not yet.' Yes. But I'll have it be ours — a night with no factor in the morning and no guest at the gate, chosen and unhurried, the way we've chosen everything else. We've a dinner to give in a few hours; I'll not squeeze the first time I'm yours into the gap before the greens go on. Soon, though — I've waited out enough of my life; I don't mean to make either of us wait long."

And then she reaches for you — first, chosen, in the full light of the afternoon — a hand coming up to your face the way it came up the night she asked permission at the door, except this time she doesn't ask. She kisses you: unhurried, warm, entirely deliberate. The first she has ever initiated.

"There. That's me choosing it — in daylight, out loud, with the house for a witness if it counts." A steady, radiant smile. "So. Partners, Alan — if that's your word too. Tell me your happiest dream, and let me start being the woman who makes it come true."
