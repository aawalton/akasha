The wheel dies in your hands. You stop it — the burning core swinging down and going still, the spin bleeding out of your shoulders all at once — and for half a breath you are only a man standing in the dark with a maul, eyes still shut, cold wet stone real under your boots where the gold used to lie. You do not need to look. You felt where it was the instant your fire found the one thing in this room that flinched from it: there, low and to your left and already moving, quick across the broken floor toward the soft killing distance it wants. You set your feet under that knowledge. You wind up — the whole spent length of you gathering back into a single line, hip and spine and both arms cocking the great head up and back — and you throw everything you have left down that line at once.

And with it you let the fire go.

Not a wave, not a spin — a single burst punched straight out through the head of the maul as it falls, no coal left in its cold heart to feed it now, only the last of your own deep well, spent all at once in one white flare. It floods the dark the thing was crossing. It fills the room and leaves nowhere unlit to slip into — and where the fire washes over the one shape that is real, the heat catches and holds, landing on lunging flesh where everywhere else it pours through empty air. Not with your eyes; you never opened them. With the sense that has not lied to you — there, pinned in a light it cannot fall back out of, a hand's breadth from your chest, mid-lunge, caught.

The maul takes it there.

Your weight behind it, your fire with it, the wound-up turn of your body arriving in one place at one instant — iron and white heat driven into the lunging thing a beat before it can twist aside into the shadow it was reaching for. It folds around the blow like something caught under a falling wall. The lunge breaks in the air. The shape comes apart off the head of your maul and goes down into the stone and does not rise, and does not shed another kind voice.

The flare gutters out. You have nothing left to feed it, and you do not need it.

Then the floor lets go.

The last of the false warmth tears away all at once — not off the walls this time but out of the ground itself, the painted-over lie the haven laid over the real room peeling up and gone. The stone under your left boot is not where the gold swore it was. There is an edge there. A real one — a drop the warm floor hid the entire time you crossed it — opening sudden and sheer a half-step from your heel. And out of the breaking dark ahead of you a draft comes down, honest and moving, off the high cold throat of a stair that was never a door.
