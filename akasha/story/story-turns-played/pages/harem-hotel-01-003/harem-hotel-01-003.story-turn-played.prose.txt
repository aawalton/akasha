Your hand closes on hers — the grip of someone making sure the one solid thing in the room stays where it is — and you pull. She gives you her weight to brace against and rises as you rise, until you are both standing, close, closer than the leverage strictly needed, and you have not let go of her hand.

Her fingers settle in around yours rather than slipping loose, and they stay there — the hold yours to keep for as long as it does you any good.

"Up you come." She looks you over once you are standing. "On your feet, in your own hall. Better."

"Okay." You are on your feet now, her hand still in yours. "Where do we go from here?"

She turns her head to answer it with her eyes first; you follow the look. The hall runs its two directions off the small warm patch of floor the two of you make. Close on one side: the open door, the made bed, the low easy light. The far side darkens down its length to that single shut door at the end, patient, waiting on nobody.

"Two doors." A tilt of her head — first at the near one, then down the dark. "I don't know this place any better than you do, so take it as a reader's guess and not gospel: that near one — open, warm, already made up — has the look of somewhere to rest. And the far one, shut, all the way down there in the dark, has the look of somewhere that wants something from you before it opens." Her thumb moves once across the back of your hand, there and gone. "If I had to call it: the near way is the kinder one, and the far way is the adventure. But I'm guessing at a room I've not been inside. We could find out slowly — there's no clock on us that I can hear."
