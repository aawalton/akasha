You turn your back on the cold mouth of the gallery, and you go down.

It is not a retreat you are ashamed of. The skin-crawl thins behind you with every step you put between yourself and that re-cloaked dark -- the wrong oily prickle fading back to the edge of feeling and then below it -- and the further you descend the more your shoulders come down from around your ears. You know this stair; you came up it. The warm false gold lets you go without a fight, whatever the haven is it does not chase a man out its own front door, and the threshold line you crossed holds at your back the way it held before: a clean edge between the place that wants you and the place that merely is.

The warden's platform opens under you, and it is as you left it -- broad, bare, cold honest stone with no welcome woven into it and nothing alive on it but you. The thing that ruled this floor is gone by your hand, and the space it held is only a space now, wide and quiet and safe in the plain way the floor above can do nothing but counterfeit. You let the maul down off your shoulder. You let yourself down after it, your back to good solid rock, and for the first time in a long climb you stop holding yourself ready.

You are hurt more than you let yourself feel while it mattered. Now that it doesn't, the body sends the bill -- the deep ache of the blow you took on the way out, the scraped-empty floor of your focus, the heaviness sunk into your legs. You close your eyes, and this time it is only sleep. You let it take you.

And in sleep, your eyes work.

You are back in the welcome. The hall of soft gold opens its arms the way it did the first time, warm and bright and glad of you -- except the warmth is wrong now, wrong the way a thing is wrong in a dream: heat with no fire under it, gladness with nothing glad behind it. The host stands with its open hands. The light pools golden. It is all exactly as kind as it was, and in the dream you can see, where awake you would not let yourself look, that the kindness is a surface -- a skin of warm light laid over something the warmth is there to keep you from seeing.

There is a brightness off to one side, the way still water is bright, and your dreaming eye turns to it. Something looks back out of it. Not the kind host -- the thing the kind host is painted over, caught for half a sleeping breath in the bright cold surface, wrong and patient and nothing like the welcome it wears. You cannot hold it. The gold washes back in, the surface goes warm and smooth and lying, the host smiles its painted smile, and the dream forgets on purpose what it just let you glimpse.

But you do not, quite.

You wake whole.

It comes back slow and then all at once -- the cold stone beneath you, the bare platform, the lantern still burning steady where you set it down. You are mended. The ache has gone out of you, the deep tiredness has lifted, your focus has filled back to the brim -- a clean cold reservoir of it, ready to spend. The rest did everything rest can do. Your body is yours again, and full.

The maul is not. You take it up and feel it at once: the core rides low and nearly cold in the haft, a banked coal where there was a furnace, and sleep has not put one ember back into it. It is a made thing and not a living one -- it does not heal because you did. Its fire is gone where you poured it out a floor above, and it will not come back from nothing. It comes back from heat: fed in, held, given time. The lantern burns in its iron cage an arm's length away, small and steady and warm -- real warmth, honest fire, the one true heat-source you carry on a floor that only counterfeits it.

And you can feel, faint and far above you, that the floor you left has not stayed where you left it. Something in the warm dark up there has shifted while you slept -- resettled, re-warmed, smoothed itself back over -- the way a disturbed pool goes still again and gives no sign a stone was ever thrown. You cannot say what it means. You only know the haven above is not holding its breath where you wounded it; it has let that breath out, and drawn another.

You sit whole and rested on safe stone -- your focus full, your maul low, the lantern warm at your hand, and the cold climb waiting over your head.
