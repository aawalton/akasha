Aura takes the phone back and doesn't look at it, sets it face-down on the mattress like the evidence it is. She's up on one elbow now, and for once not doing anything with the rest of her — no bounce, no drift, no reaching for the next thing.

"Okay, so." She stops. Starts again, slower, visibly making herself do it. "I'm going to say a thing at normal-person speed, and you don't get to make fun of the speed."

"I would never."

"You would immediately." She does it anyway, slow, each word set down exactly where she wants it. "I've queued solo a long time. Longer than you'd believe if I told you the number, which I'm not going to. And I don't want a teammate for tonight." Her thumb finds the brass key on the carabiner at the edge of the bed, turns the wind rose once. "I want the standing slot. Player two, permanent, no expiry. You."

Awen discovers she's been holding her breath. She lets it out.

She reaches over and turns the phone back face-up.

"Add a line," she says. Quiet. Exact. "I saw the 942k months ago. Knew you'd left it up on purpose. Never said anything — because I wanted to keep it too."

Then she takes the brass key, and the whole loaded carabiner with it, and folds Aura's hand shut around the lot — her shop, Aura's keys, Aura's hand, all of it in the one fist — and holds it there.
