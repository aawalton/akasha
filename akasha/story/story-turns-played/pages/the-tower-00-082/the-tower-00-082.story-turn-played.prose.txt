You go back down to the safe stone and you let yourself stop. No tricks this time, no experiments -- only the old discipline, sitting still by the lantern with the cold honest rock at your back, drawing yourself toward whole from your own center. The platform asks nothing of you, and so for a long quiet while you take everything back: the burn goes out of your legs, the cool well of your focus climbs to its brim, the spin-dizziness and the ache of days of work drain away. When you open your eyes you are full, and rested, and done preparing.

You climb back up to the warm gold mouth of the haven -- and this time you have a plan that does not depend on the one thing this floor keeps turning against you. The last time you went in blind by warmth and the lack of it, the place learned to throw false heat and flood the very sense you walked by. So this time you will not reach for it at all. You will not feel for where they are. You will make a thing that does not need to know.

You set the maul down and run your Ember out along the welded chain -- home fire into bound iron, the way it has always gone easy -- and the whole length of it wakes orange, the chain and the heavy core at its end kindling into a live burning thing, fed not from your own well but from the banked store in the maul's heart. It costs the core dearly; you feel the Furnace-heart sink toward low as it pours its fire up the chain. But when you take Burning Anger up again in the tight close grip and roll the core-end out into its first slow circle, it goes around trailing flame -- a wheel of burning iron a body's-width wide, turning at the end of your arms. Let them come kind. Let them come from the side. There is no side to a circle, and the circle is on fire.

You start to spin, and you start to walk, and you do not stop doing either.

It is the crudest, surest thing you have ever done in this place. You cross the warm gold room as a moving wheel of fire, the burning core howling around you on its chain in a flat hard circle, your eyes half-shut and your senses deliberately shut tighter -- not looking for them, not feeling for them, refusing the room every hook it has. The false warmth pours over you and means nothing to a man who has stopped asking the warmth anything. The kind voices start up at the edges, close and warm and wanting you to stop, to turn, to let them near -- and you give them the back of your attention and the front of the fire, and you keep walking.

Most of the circle meets nothing -- the flail sweeps through warm shapes that aren't there, gold light parting around burning iron the way it parted around your tether once, no purchase, no meat. But twice, three times, something real lunges in for the opening a turning man must surely be giving them -- and finds the wheel instead. You feel it connect: a solid catch, a body taking the burning core full across as it closes, the sweep flinging it back singed and reeling out of your circle before it can land the intimate killing thing it came for. They are fast and they are patient and they time the gaps -- one of them rakes you across the ribs in the half-beat between passes, a hot line of pain, and another glances off your shoulder -- but a graze is all the circle gives them, and a graze is not how these things kill. They need you stopped and trusting and still. You are none of those. You are a burning wheel walking through their room, and you do not stop, and you do not finish them either -- you simply pass, sweeping them back bashed and smoking behind you, and leave them the empty gold to be patient in.

And then the warmth changes under you.

You feel the far side of the room come up -- the way out you were walking toward, the place the gold seemed to thin toward an onward dark -- and you carry your burning wheel through it and out the far side, and you let the spin wind down at last, the core swinging slower and slower until it hangs smoking and dim at the chain's end and the fire gutters low. You stand breathing hard in a new quiet, the kind voices shut off behind you, your ribs stinging where they caught you. You crossed it. Whatever that room was, you are through it.

But you are not out. You know it before you know how you know it -- the air here is wrong in a new way. The gold is thinner, grudging, stretched too thin over something it can barely cover; the warmth has gone threadbare and cold at the edges, and the welcome that pressed so hard a room behind you is fainter here, as if whatever weaves it has less to spare this deep. It is colder ahead. It is more total ahead -- a wrongness that does not bother as much with being kind. You came looking for the way up and you have found only another threshold, deeper in, opening onto a dark you have not yet read and have, for the first time on this floor, chosen not to. The burning core hangs dead-low in your hands. The maul's heart is spent down to a banked coal. And the cold thing ahead waits to be walked into, or not.
