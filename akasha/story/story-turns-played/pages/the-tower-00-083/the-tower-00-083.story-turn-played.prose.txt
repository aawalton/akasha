You do not wait for the cold to explain itself. You step down off the threshold into it, spinning, and go forward -- all in, the way you decided before your body could argue.

The gold goes threadbare around you as you descend. You cannot see it -- you have not looked at anything on this floor since you shut your eyes at its door -- but you feel it the way you feel a fire dying: the pressed warmth stretching thinner and thinner over something that was always underneath, cold coming up through the soles of your boots off stone gone wet and real. The slope pulls you forward and down. There are presences here too -- warm ones, glad ones, close enough to touch you -- and your burning wheel passes through them and finds nothing, that particular nothing you have learned to feel with fire: heat landing on no flesh, warmth spent into empty air. You give them nothing back. The pull against your chest -- the old sweet pressure, sit, rest, stay -- runs thicker in one direction than any other, and you have stopped thinking of that pressure as a temptation. It is a bearing. You follow the lie downhill to where it is being made.

The warmth gathers itself to meet you. Even through shut lids you feel it swell -- richer here than anywhere on this floor, gold pressed deep and generous, and out of the middle of it a voice, the gracious one, glad in a way that is almost grief: you came so far. Sit. You've earned it.

You answer with everything you have.

You pull the last banked coal out of the maul's heart and you reach down your own deep well after it, and you let the whole of it out in one turn of the wheel -- not the panic-burst of the drowning grapple, not the rough gout you drilled at the wall: a made thing, shaped all the way out, the hottest fire you have ever released and the most carefully. It leaves you as a single burning circle with you at the hub, and for one instant the room is honest.

Your fire finds nothing. And nothing. And nothing -- glad voice after glad voice standing in it unburnt, unmoved, unreal -- and then it finds flesh. One place in all that warmth where the heat lands and STAYS, where something recoils off it a beat too late and drags real dust under real weight -- and from that one point you feel every warm thread in the room pull taut at once, all of it, the table and the voices and the gold, running back like veins into the thing sitting at the head of the table. The one real thing in the haven. The thing that makes it.

You bring the wheel down.

The blow lands the way nothing you have ever struck has landed -- iron and fire into flesh that was never flesh, and the room breaks before the thing does. The gold tears off the walls like burnt skin. The table gutters out mid-steam; the voices die mid-welcome, all together, one breath cut everywhere at once; the warmth that has pressed on you since the threshold is simply gone, and what is left against your skin is cold wet stone and the smell of old bone -- a den, a larder, the true shape of every kind room it ever showed you.

And the cracked thing at the head of the table does not fall.

Something gets up out of it. You hear it shed the gracious voice like a coat hitting the floor, and what moves underneath is lean and quick and utterly without warmth -- quicker than the pair in the gallery, quicker than anything this floor has worn -- and it comes for you off the broken seat while your wheel is still swinging back around, while your fire sits low and guttering in you, and the dark it crosses to reach you is real.
