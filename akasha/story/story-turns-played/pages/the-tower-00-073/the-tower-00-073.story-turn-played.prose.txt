With the bones at your feet and the haven still glowing its patient gold at your back, you crouch and draw the winding-drum core out of your pack — the dense iron drum you tore from the dead Colossus two floors down, still faintly humming, still somehow not finished unwinding. A plan has been turning over in your mind, and it turns on this one question: whether the force wound up inside this thing is something you can fold into what you already carry — into your ember, your bound metal, the way you have folded heat into every tool you have made.

So you feel it out. The iron itself answers you easily — sound, dense, the best stock you have pulled from the Tower yet, and warm where your hand rests against it; that warmth you read the way you read all heat, plainly, a familiar note under your palm. You reach past the warmth, then, for the hum — for the wound force you can feel is in there, coiled and patient and real —

and your sense slides off it.

It is THERE. You have no doubt of that; the drum thrums with it, a stored tension like a held breath, like a bow that has been drawn for a very long time. But when you reach to read it the way you read ember — to feel its shape, its charge, how it might take a bind — there is nothing for your sense to close on. It is not heat. You test it against the cold you know too, the opposite pole you have wielded and been refused by, and it is not that either: it does not fight you, does not bleed, does not push back against your ember the way cold does. It simply is not on any scale your senses are built to weigh. A force with no temperature — and you are a creature who reads the world in temperatures.

You sit with it a moment, the cold iron heavy in your hands, and you let yourself understand the honest shape of the answer. The metal is yours to work, gladly — the finest core you have carried. But the force inside it is foreign, a kind you cannot yet grasp with what you have. Whatever your plan wanted from this, it is going to have to wait on a sense you do not yet own. Behind you the haven glows on, warm and golden and patient, offering everything and meaning none of it. The way down is still open.
