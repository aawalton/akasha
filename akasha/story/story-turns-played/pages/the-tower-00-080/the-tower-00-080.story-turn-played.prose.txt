You unlash the heavy core from the end of the chain and let the maul lie. The dervish-thing was never quite the point -- it was the door you were knocking on. The thing you want is inside the iron.

You take the winding-drum core up in both hands, hold it against your chest, and you begin to turn -- not the weapon this time, but yourself, your own body wheeling slowly on the cold stone, the dense weight cradled and spinning with you. It is not a thing you reasoned out so much as a thing you felt your way toward: the force in this iron is a turning force, a wound and waiting motion, and you cannot grip a turning thing by standing still. So you turn. You match it. You make of your whole spinning body a question put to the sealed tension in your arms, and you reach -- not with your hands, with that deeper sense, the one that has only ever known heat -- and you reach for something that is not heat at all.

And this time it is THERE. For the first time since you pulled this core from the wreck, the foreign force does not slide off you. You feel it -- truly feel it, catch hold of it with a part of you that did not know it could -- a vast wound stillness coiled in the metal, a held breath of pure stored motion, immense and patient and real. The thing that was a rumor under your hands at the flail's end is a presence now, gripped, known. You have found the door, and it is open, and the turning force pours up out of the iron toward you.

You pull it into yourself, the only way you have ever taken an essence in -- the way you drank the ember on the first floor, into the meat and marrow of you, to keep.

It does not keep.

The force surges up into you and finds nothing in you shaped to hold it. You are ember to the bone -- tempered and single-poled and made, all the way through, to bank fire; and this is not fire. It rushes in and sluices straight through you like a flood through an open sluice, touching everything, sticking to nothing, and pours out the far side of you as fast as it came. For one staggering instant you are full of a power you cannot feel the edges of -- and then it is simply gone, run out through your hands, back into the iron and the air, and you have kept not one drop of it.

The backlash takes you sideways. The unspent force, refused a home, shoves back the way force always does, and your own spin turns traitor -- the world wheels hard, your stomach with it, and you stumble three lurching steps across the platform with your head roaring before your heels finally bite and hold you up. You stand swaying on the safe stone, breathing hard, the core dead-quiet in your hands again, dizzy and emptied and strangely, fiercely glad. Because you understand now, with the certainty of a thing learned in the body: the force is real, and it is reachable, and you can touch it whenever you choose. You simply cannot keep it in yourself. You are the wrong vessel. To hold this, you would have to build it a home outside your own skin -- to bind it into something the way you once bound the ember into iron -- and that you have not yet done.
