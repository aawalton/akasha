"'Our volunteer gardener' — I do like that you've decided we're keeping them before we've so much as said hello." The path takes you down through the gorse, and Amy reads it as you go. "Though mind how this runs — down the blind side of the hill, nowhere near the drive, worn deep by one set of feet over years. Whoever this is has taken great care never to be seen. That's not shyness, Alan; that's a choice, kept a long time. And if the smoke and the green fingers point where I think — someone of the wood — then we're walking up on one of the Greenreach's own, most likely. I know that lot only by their manners: formal, humble, protective, private as the trees. I've never once dealt with this one, whoever they are. So we go gently. We're the ones trespassing here, key or no key."

The trees open on the fold — a low warden's lodge tucked into the hillside, the chimney threading its smoke — and its keeper is already at the gate, coming to meet you openly. She had your footsteps long before you had her.

She's tall and slender, auburn-chestnut hair worn loose and caught back from her face in a single working half-braid, and her ears rise long and pointed through it — an elf of the Greenreach, the first you've knowingly seen. Vivid emerald eyes; strong dark brows; a step so light the gorse barely notices her. She carries a queen's poise with absolutely no pedestal under it, and she's dressed for the wood: a long deep-green wrap-coat over greens and soft browns, oiled leather at the shoulders, high supple boots, a warden's knife at her hip. And on her hands — plain to see when she lifts one — green under the nails and garden soil to the wrist, cutting-sap on her cuffs. An hour old, at most.

"That path's mine. I had your footsteps a hundred paces back — most folk I let pass without a word." Warm, not a challenge. "You came down through the green door. That door has never once opened in my time here."

Her eyes settle on you, and something in them is doing arithmetic it has never had to do before.

"I'll be straight with you — the wood's been leaning toward your hill for two days. Paths bending, trees turning. Trees don't do that. I came out this morning to see what it was leaning at." A beat, green eyes steady. "And here you are, walking my fold like it laid itself down for you."

Amy hangs a half-step back, hands visible, a guest to her bones, and gives the reason you came — warm, honest, gratitude leading. "Forgive the intrusion. We've come from the manor up the hill — the one that stood dark, and woke this week. I'm Amy; I keep its affairs. And we've come for one plain reason: to say thank you. We found the walled garden this morning — and we found your hand in it. The damp cuttings, the tools kept like you love them, the corner bed you turned this season. Someone has tended that ground with real care for years, asking nothing, taking no coin — and we'd not let that go unthanked a day longer. We'd like to know who to thank. But we'll take that at whatever pace suits you. It's your ground, and your welcome to give or to keep."

"So you found my trespass." Said without a flinch, a small wry warmth in it. She lifts one soil-dark hand and lets you both see it plainly. "These are from an hour ago. Something green was dying behind that wall and no one was coming for it — cuttings gone to ruin, the beds choking. I couldn't stand to watch it. I've never had a claim on that garden. Only on keeping it alive." Quiet, level: "I went over the low corner of the wall. I never had the door."

Which is exactly when you give her the reason the door opened at all: dinner. Up at the house, tonight — the table owes its gardener a place at it, and you'd like her to come and take it.

A held breath. The weighing shows for just a moment — a warden considering an invitation into the house that lit itself two nights ago and set her whole wood leaning — and then something like sunrise breaks through it, entirely genuine.

"Dinner. In the house that woke itself and tipped my forest sideways." A short, bright laugh. "You could not have baited a warden better if you tried." Warm, and decided: "Yes. I'd like that very much. And I'll bring the last good greens out of that garden you caught me in — since you already know I've got them, and they oughtn't go to waste on a wall." A glance back toward the open green door on the hill, then to you. "Aelwyn. I walk the north fold, hill's-shadow to the deep beeches. The lodge with the smoke is mine — but I expect you'd worked the smoke out already."
