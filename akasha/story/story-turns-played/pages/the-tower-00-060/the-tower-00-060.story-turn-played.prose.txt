There is no preparing anything over a dead hearth, so you go and find fire's food. Down the cleared flights to the first floor — the bottom of all this, where you started — and you work it over slow and thorough, turning up what a hundred climbers and your own earlier passes have left: a broken stool gone to splinters, the dry guts of something wooden long collapsed, a drift of brittle debris in the corners that takes a flame. It is not a woodpile, but it is real fuel, more than you hoped to find in a place so picked-over, and you bundle the best of it up and haul it back up to the water and the cold brazier — an armful for the fire, and a little over to keep.

You build it up properly this time: the foraged wood stacked in the basket and the coil of rotted rope laid through it for tinder, and then you call a seed of your own Ember down into the heart of it the way you can now without a flint or a spark to beg from. It catches. The rope crackles and the old wood takes and the brazier wakes into real flame, throwing live orange light across the water and the stone — the first proper fire you have had since the night you forged your flesh.

Into it you set your three fire-things. The Furnace-heart, dark and spent, you bed down in the coals with a seed of essence in it; the little rivet beside it, the same. And this time the mace goes in too — for it is bound again, not the dead iron it was that cold night, and when you give it its seed the head drinks the heat and holds it, its low charge beginning to climb. All three sit in the fire now, drinking, their charges rising as the pyre burns down through your fuel. The forge is alive again, the leftover wood stacked dry at your side, and the work you came down here to do is waiting on whatever you decide it is.
