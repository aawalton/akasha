You wake to bacon.

No grey edges, no braced feeling, no residue of the road or the dream — just morning light doing generous things through the window and a smell from below making its case. You come out as you are, pajamas and all, and follow your nose down the corridor.

She's at the range, and daylight gives you a version of her you haven't met yet: a well-cut wool day-dress in a soft moss color, sleeves pushed up, a clean linen apron over it; her hair pinned back up, simply and deliberately — last night's loose sleep-braid gone, the daytime composure restored, though the cooking heat is already working a few strands free again. Good boots. The plain old gold ring on her right hand catching the pan-light. Entirely herself, put back together — and somehow warmer for your knowing, now, what the put-together version is holding.

She glances up from the bacon, and there is no awkwardness in her anywhere. Only a real, pleased once-over.

"Good morning. Well — look at you. You slept. Properly, by the look of it: no grey under the eyes, none of that braced look you came down the hall with last night. The dream stayed gone." Back to the pan. "Good. I hoped it would."

You ask how you can help.

"And there it is — 'how can I help.' Do you know, I've been keeping a small tally: the chair you held for me, the asking before you did anything, the whole of last night, and now this before you've had so much as coffee. You are, it turns out, constitutionally unable to be in a room with a working person and not pitch in." Warm, and the task is in your hands before the sentence ends. "So — yes. There's a pot of coffee wants bringing to the boil, and plates in the dresser behind you. Lay two, and mind the coffee doesn't climb out of the pot. That's help, and it's real."

And then, while you're laying the plates, her voice changes — not darker, exactly; leveler. The keeper's register.

"Now — while you lay those plates, here's a thing I can't make sit right, and I'd sooner say it than sit on it. This bacon. The eggs, the flour, the butter, the milk still cool. I keep this house's books, Alan — I know to the penny what's come up this hill in ten years, and it's nothing. No cart, no delivery. And I walked into that larder at first light and found it full — stocked fresh, in a house that's not held so much as a sack of flour in three generations."

A level look over the pan — unspooked, and not pretending either.

"I've no account for it, and I don't care for not having an account — it's near my whole trade. So: something is provisioning this house, and it isn't me and it isn't any road. The bacon's real regardless; we'll eat it. But your walls are keeping you, and I mean to learn how." A beat, and it lightens. "That's one of two things we need to talk through over breakfast. The other wears a Compact seal and will be up your hill by noon. Eat first, though."

The coffee climbs toward the boil, right on schedule.
