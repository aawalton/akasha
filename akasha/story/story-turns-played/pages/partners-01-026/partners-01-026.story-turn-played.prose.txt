"A pleasure to meet you, Aelwyn! No need to worry about trespass — you are welcome at Hearthholt, house or grounds, any time you desire, day or night. If there's one thing I've learned, it's that the house has excellent judgement — and it seems to like you, so that's good enough for me."

She's quiet a moment. You get the sense the brightness is being held back on purpose, while the thing you've just said arrives at its full weight.

"You've just done a strange thing, and I don't think you know its size." Level, warm, no gush at all. "For years that garden was the one place I let myself go where I wasn't allowed. Over the low wall, in the grey light, so no one would know. And you've handed me the gate." A breath. "I'll not pretend that's small. It isn't. Thank you."

Then she gives you something back — a thing worth knowing about the woman your house likes.

"Here's a thing you should know about me. I read the wood's mind for my living — which tree's sickening, which path wants walking, when the Greenreach has taken against something. Living places have opinions. I've spent my life learning to hear them. So when you tell me your house likes me — I don't take it lightly. Your wood leans at you. A door that never opened for me turned today, of all days. And your house likes me." A beat, the green eyes even. "That's a great deal of agreement, from things that don't usually bother to say so."

She names it. She doesn't try to explain it. Somehow that makes it land harder.

"Then that's settled — and let me make it real, since an invitation's only as good as its details." Amy, warm and concrete, the steward making the thing true. "Come at dusk. The greens you're bringing, and everything else is on us; it'll be the first proper dinner this house has laid for a guest in longer than either of us has been alive, so forgive it if it's a touch grand about the occasion — the house has waited three generations for someone to feed. There'll be a lamp in the gate for you and a warm kitchen at the end of it. And you'll not need to knock — you've a standing welcome now, and I'll see the whole house knows your face."

The sunrise arrives properly then, bright and unheld.

"Go on up — the walk's yours, and I've work before dark. Greens to cut, my fold to walk, and apparently a first supper inside the very house that's spent two days leaning my whole forest sideways to get a look at me." A short warm laugh. "You could not have made a warden more curious if you'd planned it." She steps back toward her gate — then turns, something quieter under the brightness. "I'll come to the front door tonight. First time I'll have entered anything here the proper way — feels only right. Till supper, then."

The path carries you both back up through the gorse, the green door standing open ahead, and Amy is fond and lightly wry at your shoulder.

"You do realise, Master Alan, that you've just handed the run of your house to a woman you met an hour ago. Day two, no less." Then, warmer — the recognition plain: "...Not that I've the faintest standing to object. That's the exact open-handedness that got me a room and a name off you this very morning, and I'd not change a hair of it. I'll only be keeping the list of who this house adores — that's a steward's job. It's becoming rather a lovely list."

Behind you, thin and steady, the warden's chimney keeps its fire. Ahead, up the hill, the house waits for dusk and its first guest in three generations.
