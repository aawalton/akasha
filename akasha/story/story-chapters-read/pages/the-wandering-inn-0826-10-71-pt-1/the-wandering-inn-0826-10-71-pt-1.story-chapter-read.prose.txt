It was time for a barbecue. A cookout. A get-together.
A throwdown.
Possibly. So, put on your Zenze best. And be ready.
Everyone was coming to the party.

Wall Lord Ilvriss was putting on a suit as the sounds of early-morning work surrounded him. He felt slightly sleep-deprived; he wasn’t in his usual bed.
And ‘usual bed’ meant the one at his dig site. This bed was another temporary bed in a tent just outside the Cowpat Plains. Somewhere close enough to the plains to be right next to the magic door, but also far enough away to stop smelling the poo.
An ideal spot, in short, for a neutral gathering. No man’s land—except for the woman who owned the lands and who was already compensated for the usage thereof. And Salazsar had been there all of yesterday and this morning, setting up.
When a…nervous…[Princess] showed up with her daughters, she found the gathering was not the disaster she envisioned, or chaotic. Ilvriss had arranged for an early afternoon meetup, and things were proceeding on pace for that.
Not ‘we will make it if we rush and nothing goes wrong’, no. It was…Lyonette stopped as a team of Salazsarian Drakes and Gnolls finished setting up canopies, and she saw no less than six places being prepped for smoking or roasting meat. Some of said vittles were already done.
Calescent, Imani, and a group of [Chefs] were lugging out pre-prepared tubs of roasts they’d slaved over last night, and a Centaur was drawing a temporary Rune of Preservation around the entire area. And another rune to keep the insects away.
“Palt! Hello.”
He jumped and sidled around when Lyonette called out. His face grew hostile, and the [Princess] spoke.
“We don’t want to get in your way. We’re just here for the festivities. Are you and Imani staying?”
The Centaur hesitated, then took the black cigar out of his mouth. While he was standoffish, he nodded to Nanette, Mrsha, Dalimont, and Ushar.
“Hello, Lyonette. No, we’re not sticking around. That is—she wants to meet Ulva Terland, but this sounds like a Solstice event. So we’ve agreed on a compromise.”
“Well, we appreciate the help in helping set this up. And that is, ah, sensible. Ulva Terland isn’t coming with the best of intentions. Actually, no one is.”
Palt nodded as he shuffled his hooves.
“Terlands don’t play around. Ulva was always the meaner of the two Terland Twins. And her Golems can see through any illusions. Even the Elusive Lot don’t play games in her lands. So…good luck.”
It was one of the most civil conversations he’d had with anyone from the inn since the Winter Solstice. Lyonette thanked him as Mrsha sniffed the air.
Mother, I believe I shall attend this event.
“Oh, you will, Mrsha? How gracious of you to condescend to that after we’ve been toiling away in the Cowpat Plains.”
Nanette teased Mrsha, and the girl solemnly patted her stomach.
I crave the meats. With that said, we probably should prepare for trouble.
“I’m calling for Elia to stand watch. She and Todi will help with guard duty. Not that it’ll matter if the Golems attack…”
Palt shifted, clearly uneasy with how likely it was for Terlands to attack. But Lyonette just turned.
“But Wall Lord Ilvriss really has done well. Is…he even has serving staff?”
He’d hired from Invrisil, Liscor, and Pallass. Highly attractive [Servers] who were standing and being briefed on the order of events by Welsca. Mrsha even spotted a pair from Wishdrinks that Liska had recommended, who were very excited for an inn-related job.
No matter where Lyonette looked, things were…ready. For instance, she had the obvious thought that some music and entertainment would be needed—only to see a full band setting up from Pallass. That, she realized, was the power of Ilvriss, which was familiar to a Calanferian [Princess].
The nigh-unlimited power of someone with a checkbook and connections. And a willingness to use them.
No, it was the familiarity…Ilvriss’ staff and Nerul were all experts in hosting social events, and no matter what Lyonette tried, she couldn’t see anything that needed improvements. In fact, he had more advantages than even Lyonette would have thought of, because of some of his guests and workers. There would be people from his expedition too, of course. Including, unfortunately, Wall Lord Dramm. But the young Drake seemed somewhat subdued. He watched as a cursing team of workers, including Miner Georgie, hauled benches and tables into place. And he said not a word.
Possibly because Lady Damia Reinhart was here herself, setting things up, and she had a flair for extravagance.
“No, no. Put it here, and are those cooling spells active? What do you mean, ‘no’? [Mage]! Maaaaaaaaaaage!”
She began screaming, and Palt trotted over. Lyonette turned and saw a huge, covered…statue? It could be nothing less than that. Six feet tall, covered by a cloth, but it was being lowered down by a group of very nervous [Bakers] and [Patissiers] from Invrisil. The cloth shifted and revealed—Mrsha’s jaw dropped.
Mother, pinch me. Or do I not espy a six-foot tall statue of a Golem made out of chocolate?
It moved past Wall Lord Ilvriss, who was drinking a cup of coffee. He stared at the statue much like everyone else, but with the colorful expression of a man who knew he had just paid for that. Lyonette waved at him.
“Er, Ilvriss, good morning! Everything seems to be in hand. What’s that?”
He coughed as Damia waved her hands and Palt produced spells to preserve the monstrosity.
“That…would be Lady Damia’s contribution, Lyonette. I gave her a budget to impress the Terlands.”
“And her idea was a giant statue? That’s so…okay, it might work.”
Terlands loved that kind of thing, Lyonette just bet. To both’s relief, it wasn’t pure chocolate. It was, in fact, a cake. With frosting. Damia looked inordinately pleased with herself.
“Wall Lord Ilvriss, Princess Lyonette, I got this fairly cheap!”
“How…?”
“I just mentioned who it was for! I would have made it into a real Golem, but that would have been too hard. How’d I do?”
She wore the sparkle-eyed expression of a puppy wanting to be told she’d done well. Lyonette’s mouth moved, and Ilvriss managed a smile and thumbs up.
“Excellently, Damia. This is the kind of chaos we need. In moderation. Er, any more surprises?”
“Well, your uncle sent a bunch of Salazsarian pork…”
“Dead gods, the good stuff? I miss that. Mrsha, want to try real pork?”
She jogged over as Ilvriss rubbed his claws together. Lyonette had heard of Salazsarian pork, and she was dubious—until she got a sampler. Dead gods, it was good! She was chewing on some as Ilvriss counted.
“Steaks, sausages, burgers, hot dogs—what’s the difference between—oh, right, one’s from Erin’s world, so it’s heinous—all made with Lischelle meat. Calescent’s been setting up all morning, and I had to kick some idiots who didn’t know the score on Goblins. We should be prepared by lunch and ready in advance for several hours.”
“Splendid, Ilvriss. So…are you ready?”
The Terlands were coming. The [Ranchers] were coming. Neither group liked Drakes, and at least one had a serious axe to grind. Actually, the Lischelles had just been cattle-raided, so if this cookout went as badly as the last one…
We could be seeing Golems and [Ranchers] coming at us, possibly teamed up. Lyonette swallowed, but she had no great plans, just a desire to diplomacy harder than ever before. She really, really hoped Nerul would make it.
Lyonette already knew who wouldn’t make it.
“I thought we’d be able to get a few other notables to act as shields—er, delightful distractions. Unfortunately, Liscor’s Council is down to the last few days of elections. And we don’t want the Assembly of Crafts.”
Ilvriss shuddered.
“That lot? Absolutely not. They’d grate. But what about the rest of the inn’s guests? Archmage Valeterisa’s a wildcard…but an impressive one.”
Lyonette grimaced.
“She flew away the moment I mentioned Ulva Terland. Which suggests she doesn’t want to be embroiled in politics, or she’s gotten in trouble. She, Grimalkin, Salamani—all the [Mages] refused me to my face! They said they might show up, but only for a moment, then they’d go back to building their damn academy in the High Passes.”
Damia had been heading off to check on more work, but she tripped and whirled, eyes round with excitement. Ilvriss just scratched at a neck spine. His were long and slightly drooping, and Lyonette eyed them.
“The mage academy, right. How is that going?”
“Well, they’ve found a spot, but no one’s telling me. It’s like children and their forts, I swear. Wall Lord—your neck spines aren’t trimmed!”
“I know, I’ve been busy, and it’s not like we have a Drake [Barber] out here—”
“Ugh, it’s not appropriate! Ushar, does someone in our staff know how to trim neck spines? Get someone from Liscor, if not!”
“I’m not trimming—don’t you dare wave a knife around my neck spines, Lyonette. They’re not like your hair! I don’t tell you when you’ve got split ends! Also, are you wearing that to the cookout?”
He had on a suit, dark blue, with a gemstone pinned to the breast, a Salazsarian classic with the stitching of the City of Gems embroidered onto the cuffs and long pants of the same color with even a tail-flap, though he thought that was stupid and antiquated.
Lyonette? More like a riding dress, a dawn yellow and cornflower blue. She did a twirl, and he noticed the familiar, white boots on her feet.
“We might do some dancing! I need to be mobile. What, isn’t it attractive?”
“I’m not dancing or negotiating for the future of my expedition with an overgrown flower. Nanette’s doing better than you are.”
The young witch had, in her usual style, refused to dress at all conventionally. But in her way, she’d just taken another convention—she had on a red plaid shirt and blue jeans, in classic [Woodcutter] fit…according to Rose. It actually looked decent, if a bit weird. Mrsha had on a kilt.
Lyonette’s glower made Ilvriss edge out of kick-range.
“I worked hard on this, Ilvriss! You look like you’re about to give a presentation on a merger in Salazsar!”
“I am what I am, said the stuffy Drake to the dancing daffodil. The daffodil is you.”
“How dare—I consulted about this! I tried on two dozen dresses, and it wasn’t easy because I don’t have much of a wardrobe anymore! But this is the only thing Nanette didn’t approve of!”
Ilvriss was dodging backwards, grinning as she went to stomp on his feet. He frowned.
“Why would that m—oh. And Nanette told you to pick that?”
“No, she just approved everything else, but—”
Lyonette slowed. She and Ilvriss turned, and Nanette Weishart slowly rotated with the most sinister smile on her face. Lyonette realized she’d been sabotaged at the same time as Ilvriss. He started laughing until she landed a full-body stomp on his foot. Then she was rushing off.
“Ushar! To the dressmakers! Oh dead gods, we don’t have time—”
The insidious nature of Nanette’s fashion was like a plague. It wore you down, shaped you even when you resisted it. Until you forgot what fashion was and realized it was all just a word. Ilvriss went over to shake Nanette’s hand.
Mrsha surreptitiously pulled out a bowtie and stuck it to her fur.

They were coming. Xitegen fussed over his clothing that morning in Celum.
“Cookouts. We’re going to meet some of the locals, apparently. Not nobility, and the inn and Wall Lord Ilvriss. A suit? Suits are too damned hot. Primera, Seconda, what does one wear at a cookout?”
There was a momentary flash from the two Golems, whose hearts were still beating. Slowly…but they made Xitegen uncomfortable. He recalled what Primera had said to him. Neither one had addressed it, but the Golem’s reply was swift, as ever. Just a bit…colored. With thought.
“This knowledge is not in our collective understanding, Wall Lord. House Terland does not participate in commoner events. The temperature will be quite hot until evening. [Ranchers] will likely wear clothing suited to their work, if more formal.”
“So, what? Overalls and boots? Bleh.”
In the end, Xitegen went with his own style. Which meant some of the new track pants he’d bought from Invrisil, a very light, breathable material in dark grey. Grey being a Terland color.
They just had to make grey their family color, didn’t they? Veltras were green, El were yellow, Wellfar blue, and Reinharts loved their pinks, purples, and reds since they hogged everything. But Terlands had to be grey.
He threw on a similar, light t-shirt and then added a blazer after a moment, which was colorful at least. Xitegen bounded out of his mansion onto Celum’s streets—and then nearly ran into his great aunt.
“Aunt Ulva, you’re—”
“Inspecting Celum. Clearly, you haven’t done enough with the city.”
“I’ve only been here a few—”
“Yes, yes. I see the defenses, and it’s been rebuilt decently from the Bloodfeast Raiders attack, but you didn’t go the entire way. Young people never do. Dig up all those streets and have them properly re-paved. Every house here isn’t up to our standards. Make them stone and rebuild.”
She was giving orders. To his people. Golems were moving down the street, ripping up paving stones—Xitegen protested.
“Great Aunt, that street’s from Celum before the raiders! And the homes belong to citizens—”
“We’ll just rebuild their homes better.”
“But they’re their homes! I don’t wish to cause ill will—”
“Good, I’m doing it for you. What are you wearing?”
Ulva Terland had on a semi-formal riding dress in shades of grey that made her look like a living statue herself. She had only offset it with some jewelry in the hair, like the Seal of House Terland, their official stamp—it was acting as a hairclip, glittering with magic, unto a Relic.
You had to admit that was stylish, in a way. Xitegen protectively shrugged off his blazer.
“It’s only a bit of color—”
“It’s red. Get something else. And what are those pants?”
“Running pants.”
“Find something else or I will have you attend in your underpants.”
“If you insist, Aunt.”
He made a show of untying his pant’s laces. Ulva saw Xitegen’s scowl, and the two stared at each other. Auras clashed in the streets. Xitegen would suffer his Great Aunt’s authority, her snark, even being thrown out a carriage at speed.
But he drew the line at personal attire.

The rest of the Terlands were more upset than the two leaders. They were, in fact, very worried about the possibility of trouble, especially now that they knew it might mean Golems would be destroyed. People? All fine and well. Golems, like the ones Nerul was holding hostage?
We must halt this!
