This time the silence stretched longer. Adhira seemed to struggle with his words, though Harker couldn’t imagine why. People lost family all the time. His story was nothing special.

“What…” She hesitated, as if picking her words carefully. “What did you do before this?”

“I was a healer.”

“Really? I haven’t known many Talented who have taken up that profession.” She tilted her head up, thinking. “I suppose alchemy is closely related, considering the medicines they produce. Did you not wish to train in it? Or even smithing?”

“I didn’t have the option.”

“Oh. It’s just, healing is an Untalented profession. Herbalism—”

“Is a large part of alchemy. You said so yourself.”

Adhira shrugged. “Sure, but alchemy is so much more. Sea-touched herbals can only take you so far. The real power in alchemy is infusing Talents, and that can’t be done with a bunch of weeds. Aanything that doesn’t aid in our Descent is a distraction.”

Harker’s lips thinned and that uncomfortable quiet returned. She seemed to realize it too—Adhira opened her mouth twice in an attempt to break it, but the words died on her lips.

Herbalism is useless, she says.

While he admitted infusing Talents was incredible—and something he planned on learning as soon as possible—he burned at the idea that his training had been pointless. He could produce all sorts of useful—

Well, now isn’t that interesting. Harker spotted something in the distance, clinging between wet ferns. He had nothing to prove, but he wasn’t going to take insults to his mother’s profession lying down. He stepped off the path.

“Where are you going?”

Harker held up a finger but didn’t answer as he disappeared into the underbrush.
