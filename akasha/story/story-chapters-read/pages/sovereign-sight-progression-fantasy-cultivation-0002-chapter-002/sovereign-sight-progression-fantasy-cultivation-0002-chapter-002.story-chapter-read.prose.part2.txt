Rope, fire-starter, skinning knife, and some dried rations. Harker lifted those to his nose. Still good. No mold either. They all went back into his satchel. It was quick work, mostly due to practice. Planning for emergencies was about half of Harker’s daily activities.

Law 3a — Have An Escape Route most often walked hand-in-hand with Law 3d — Follow The Plan. Observation and preparation had saved his hide a great many times in his eighteen years in the Vale.

With the bag he could disappear into the wilds for a few months. Long enough for things to die down. He’d done it before—it was one of his first moves when events began to outpace his ability to survive them. The lock on his door may keep out wild beasts, but the Vinells were a different story—not even the ruins would stop them for long. The people of the Vale might be superstitious fools, but anger was an excellent goad.

Damn boots. Damn Vinells. He finished counting his emergency coin. It was too little, but perhaps enough for a new pair. Had his job gone as planned, none of this would be an issue. Harker took a slow, measured breath. He ignored the hard twinge in his chest and the headache that tried to swallow his thoughts.

Law 1b — Want Nothing. What could not be had was worthless, and chasing after it was a sign of a fool.

“A quick trip into town,” he muttered aloud. The pack went onto his shoulders, over his woolen cloak and thick sweater. “Then I’m gone.”
