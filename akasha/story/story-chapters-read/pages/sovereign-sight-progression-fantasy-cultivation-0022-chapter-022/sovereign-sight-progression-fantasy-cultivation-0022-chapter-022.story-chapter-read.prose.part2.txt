Neither logic nor his Laws put his unease to rest, however. Instead, there was only the dire certainty that he was avoiding thinking about since he’d walked into the store. Is this how normal folks are treated?

He’d always known people in Vale were overcharging him and his mother, if they even sold to him at all. Garon had done it too, though he’d at last had the grace to be honest about it. Supplies were too expensive in the Vale, his mother always said. They had instead survived on what they could hunt and forage and farm.

In reality, the Vale was only expensive to him.

A spark of rage kindled behind the sourness, but it had nowhere to go. The Vale was behind him now, and whether or not the Vestige killed him, he wouldn’t be a Knack ever again.

“Finally!” Stillwater said, standing up from where he was leaning against the building. He sniffed. “Mm, smells like some good eatin’ in there.”

“Not for you,” Harker snapped, pulling his pack out of the creature’s reach. “You—”

His vision vibrated. A scent drifted across his nose and it spiked through his head like a bolt of lightning. Harker bent over, dizzy, heart-pounding in his ears, as Stillwater shouted something he could barely make out.

“—hey! What’s wrong with you? Human!”

The scent flooded his senses, and he gagged as certainty filled him.

He’d been poisoned.
