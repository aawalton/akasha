She must have a cold. I’ll brew her some tea when we make camp.

Harker ran his fingers over its sides, which were covered in a slimy residue and no small amount of salt and silt. Impurities from the Sea had clearly built up at the bottom of the tidepool, but the majority of its potency had focused entirely on the plant that grew on the boulder’s top.

“Common muskgrass?” he muttered to himself. “No, it’s usually green and far less radiant.” This was violet and glowed enough to light up their patch of the dim cavern. Muskgrass was known for many things, chiefly for being a nutrient-dense source of food for fish and other creatures.

The coralsnout must have been snacking on this for years to grow so large.

“That’s quite rare but I was hoping for something more interesting,” Adhira said, and for some reason her voice was a touch annoyed again. “A sunken sword or a tidal gem, at least.”

Harker turned toward her. “You know what it is?”

She had already put her clothes back on and was busy arranging her armor, but seemed surprised at his interest. “Of course. Violet muskgrass is given to children along with their mother’s milk. It heightens your inner sensitivity.”

“For what purpose?”

“For what—? Ah.” She nodded to herself. “I forget you’re from the sticks. It’s for finding your Locks so you can build tributaries to each one and open them up. Useless to us, of course, but it sells well at just about any market.”

“I see.” Harker kept his face still, hiding the elation that spiraled through him. Without Locks or other tributaries, this would be invaluable. Carefully, pulsing his Talent the whole time, he harvested as much as he could before bundling it into his supplies.

Soon, he promised it, before sheathing his new dagger in his belt. Very soon.
