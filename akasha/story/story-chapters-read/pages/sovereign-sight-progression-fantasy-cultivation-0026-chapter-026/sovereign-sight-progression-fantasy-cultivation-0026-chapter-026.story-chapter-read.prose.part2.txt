She gripped her axe with both hands, her stance shifting into something low and aggressive. “Where?”

“I’m not—”

Two huge, yellow shapes ripped through the underbrush on their left and right, their massive heads leading fang-first toward the girl.

Harker darted back, easily keeping distance as he drew his knives.

Adhira didn’t bother.

“Yes!” She caught the first on the flat of her axe. Its two-foot wide snout rebounded off the metal, and she kicked the second serpent in the jaw, snapping it shut and sending its slithering length sailing over her head.

The first twisted, opening its maw and revealing slick fangs. They plunged toward her, but she stomped her foot and Water gushed beneath her. “Bulwark!”

An earthen wall heaved upward, plowing into the serpent’s pink mouth. It choked, jaws forced wide, before it attempted to pull back. With a casual spin, Adhira lopped the snake’s head clean off.

“Impressive,” Stillwater said. “How come you’re not that good?”

“Harker! Look out! The serpent is—” Adhira trailed off. “Oh.”

“Huh.” Stillwater said, hands on his hips.

Harker pulled his knife free, having thrown it directly into its fist-sized eye. He dropped his Talent, enduring the haze of disorientation as he cleaned the blade as casually as he could manage.

“Behind you,” he said.

Adhira spun, just in time for third, blood-red serpent to rise from the trees. It was at least twice the size of the others, and its wedge-shaped head was the size of a wagon-bed.

She laughed. “Oh now it’s getting fun!”
