“If he had, you would not have gotten so close."

The three of them exchanged a look. Two had enough sense to be afraid, but their leader, a short man with narrow shoulders, hid behind bravado. "Try me, veilguard. I never had my hand held at your fancy school. I earned my Depth in the real world."

Sejal only smiled.

“Hold,” the Acid Talent said, putting her empty hands up. “We seek no quarrel with you or the veilguard.”

“Speak for yourself—”

“She speaks for all of us.” The other woman—the Air Talent—spoke over the man. “She is our first in this mission.”

Sejal watched all three of them. Their unity was flawed. She could use that. “What is your mission?”

“To rescue those that had been taken from the local villages and kill the Stitcher.”

Something exploded in the distance. “How has that gone?”

“Better if everyone we tried to save didn’t try to kill us,” the man said, halfway between a growl and whine.

That confirmed things. The people she’d remembered in the tent… Had the Stitcher worked his darkness on them? Had he tried it on her? Sejal frowned. “The Stitcher did not affect my mind or Talent. I am untouched by his corruption.”

“That’s what the last one said.”

“Then allow me to prove it to you,” she said, reaching for her hip. “Jump.”

Illwrought dropped from the camouflaged netting without a sound—until they landed.

The ground heaved upward, and all three mercenaries were sent somersaulting backward. Sejal rode the wave, weight shifted to the side as three abominations screeched through unnaturally fanged beaks.

She unsheathed the chakrams from her hip. “Now fight, mercenaries!”
