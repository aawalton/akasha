“I’m fine,” I said, and pushed past him. My shoulder brushed his and I floated to the next step down. “It was a bad night, okay?”

He didn’t follow. He just stood there on the landing with the wind pulling at his robes and watched me float down the stairs. I could feel, as much as see, his eyes on me the whole way.

I wanted to tell him. The weight of it was right there, pressing against the back of my teeth. It would be easy. Four words would do it: my uncle killed someone. But telling Finn meant making Finn carry it.

Finn would carry it because that’s who Finn was. He wouldn’t tell my secrets, and it would eat away at him the same way it was eating away at me. I couldn’t do that to him.

Sara was waiting at the bottom of the stairs. She didn’t say a thing. She gave me a grim smile, looked up at Finn, and then turned and walked toward the Green, and I floated after her.
