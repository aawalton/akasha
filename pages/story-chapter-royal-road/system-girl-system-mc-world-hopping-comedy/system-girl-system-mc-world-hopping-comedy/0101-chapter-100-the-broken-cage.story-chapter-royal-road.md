Larena could tell instinctively that something was terribly wrong.

Her older sister, Laurana, was always gloomy and sad. That was the baseline reality of living in the Greenwood estate. It was a suffocating, heavy sadness that seemed to seep from the floorboards. But this time was different. When the haggard butler had grabbed Laurana's arm and pulled her away, the look in her sister's eyes had made Larena’s gut wrench violently.

It wasn't just sadness. It was absolute, hollow terror.

Larena stood alone in the grand, immaculate courtyard, clutching the hem of her uniform. She had heard the whispers from the other staff while she worked. They spoke in hushed, terrified tones in the laundry rooms and the dark corners of the kitchens.

“Whoever goes to the Lord’s study never returns.”

The pit in her chest kept growing, a cold, heavy stone of panic. Why did she just stand there watching? She should have insisted on being the one to go. The reality of the situation was starting to settle in, creeping up her spine like ice water. The stories weren't just rumors. Laurana wasn't coming back.

She looked around frantically, her breath hitching in her small chest. She needed to find someone. Anyone to help her.

She spotted two human guards stationed near the heavy oak doors of the main entrance to the mansion. They were the ones who had witnessed the entire exchange with the butler. They had seen Laurana weeping on the ground.

Larena ran toward them. As she approached, the guards met her frantic gaze for a split second, then immediately turned away, staring pointedly at the empty sky above the estate walls.

She knew they wouldn't do anything against their master. They were paid to be blind and deaf to the atrocities of House Greenwood. But desperation overrode logic.

She grabbed the nearest guard's armored gauntlet with both of her small, trembling hands.

"Mister," Larena begged, tears spilling over her lashes. "You are strong. Please. Help my sister."

The guard yanked his hand away with brutal force, sending Larena stumbling backward onto the cobblestones. He didn't look at her. The other guard also kept his face turned resolutely away, his jaw clenched tight.

Larena scrambled to her feet. She began frantically looking around the courtyard for someone, anyone, to help her sister. There must be something someone can do.

Is this reality? Is this the reality of their lives? To be snatched away from their families in the elven villages, forced to work tirelessly for no pay and meager rations just barely enough to keep them alive? Just waiting for the moment their lives end in a dark room at the whim of a monster?

How is this any different from livestock?

The bitter irony was starting to dig deep into her young mind. Even working animals—the plow horses and the hounds—were given some degree of compensation for their hard work. They were fed well, brushed, and spared from the chopping block because they held value.

It looked like the existence of the elven staff was completely below even them. They were entirely expendable.

She soon came to the cold, horrifying realization that no one inside the mansion would help her. The staff was too terrified. The guards were complicit.

She stumbled towards the grand, heavy iron gates of the manor, intending to do something that was strictly forbidden.

None of the elven staff were allowed to talk to anyone outside the mansion walls. Strict, brutal punishment awaited her, and her immediate superior, if she were to try and do so.

But with her sister in imminent danger, reason was no longer an enticing option. Furthermore, her immediate superior was her sister. Due to Laurana being called to the study, there was no one assigned to keep an eye on Larena.

This created a unique situation. A fragile, terrifying window of opportunity.

Even the guards stationed beside the main iron gates ignored the little girl. They were completely unaware that her immediate supervisor had just been dragged into the Lord’s study. They simply thought she was just a little girl curious about the outside world, perhaps trying to catch a glimpse of a passing carriage.

They, too, knew the cruel fate that awaited her eventually, so they didn't react when Larena approached the grand gates. She grabbed onto the cold iron bars frantically, pressing her face between them, searching the crowded street for someone. Anyone.

A sizable group of soldiers on horseback was currently gathering outside the mansion.

Larena didn't know why they were there. She just thought of the guards from the inside, who also wore similar armor, just with a different symbol on their chest. This was a massive gamble. She only had one shot at this. She knew the exact moment she opened her mouth to scream, the guards manning the gates would rush to stop her and silence her permanently.

She scanned the mounted soldiers. Her eyes locked onto a woman sitting atop a massive, armored warhorse.

She was a beautiful looking lady, her crimson hair tied back in a severe, practical braid. The woman looked furious, her eyes burning with a scary, intense light.

Their eyes met through the iron bars.

Larena’s little heart couldn't hold on anymore. With tears dripping down her face, staining her collar, she reached her small arm out from inside the gate's bars, crying out with all the strength in her lungs.

"Please! Help my sister!"

As she thought, the moment the words left her mouth, the two guards stationed on the gate immediately moved to stop her, reaching for their truncheons.

But it was too late.

Parisa, sitting on the other side of the gate, had felt a terrible gut feeling during the entire, furious ride here from the northern encampment. Her initial, explosive anger had subsided into a slow, deadly simmer. She had been waiting in front of the estate, discreetly gathering her troops, preparing to charge in and interrogate everyone about the happenings inside the walls based on the letter she received.

When this little girl reached out toward her, screaming for help, Parisa could see a little bit of herself in the child. Trapped in her father's estate, tormented, helpless. She knew the look of absolute, unadulterated terror on the girl's face was genuine.

Also, she trusted her little brother. Ren’s information about the misgivings happening inside the mansion must be entirely accurate.

The other reason she was standing in front of the mansion with a cavalry unit was that she was trying to find a plausible reason to enter without shifting the political focus onto her younger brother's espionage. The letter mentioned the information was obtained from a reliable source, but it was hard to prove in a royal court without exposing Ren's methods. She had been trying to find any other legal justification to breach the gates.

This little girl asking for help was justification enough. As the Royal Inspector, it was her sworn duty to help any citizen who brought forth evidence of misgivings.

Parisa shot forth from her saddle, shocking her attendants. They thought they had finally gotten a moment to breathe and organize the troops.

Her body was engulfed in roaring, crimson fire.

She didn't wait for the gate to open. She melted the heavy iron bars on her way through, the metal slagging and dripping onto the cobblestones like hot wax.

She landed gracefully on the other side and immediately grabbed the nearest guard by his helmet, pinning him effortlessly against the stone pillar of the gatehouse.

The other guard frantically drew his sword, shouting, "This is the manor of the Noble Lord Greenwood! How dare you enter the premises and attack one of his retainers!"

Parisa’s assistant, a seasoned battlemage, quickly rushed forward through the melted gap in the gate. "Sheathe your blade, fool!" the assistant barked. "This is the Noble Lady Parisa, most decorated battlemage of the Royal Army. She has been bestowed the honor of Royal Inspector from the King himself. She has the authority to investigate anyone in the kingdom, even nobles. Her role is to uphold the law."

Upon realizing who the mad lady pinning their comrade actually was, the guards began visibly sweating. Everyone in the capital knew of the mad battlemage who slaughtered thousands when she entered the battlefield on the northern front. The Greenwood estate barely had a hundred personal guards.

Plus, she was acting on royal authority. The guards couldn't stop her legally or physically.

So, they resorted to trying to convince her to leave. But deep in their hearts, hidden beneath the fear of their master, all they secretly wanted was for the lady to barge in and find out what the Lord was doing behind closed doors.

"My Lady," the second guard stammered, lowering his sword slightly. "Don't listen to this child. She is... she is making things up. A wild imagination."

Parisa gave the man a death glare that made the air around him physically heat up. "Are you going to tell me what I can and cannot investigate? Is she not a citizen of our country?"

The guard stammered, caught in a lie. Technically speaking, Larena was an elf, but she was never reported in the official census. She didn't legally exist. But admitting that was a crime in itself.

The guard lied. "Yes. She is."

Parisa smirked, a dangerous, sharp expression. "Then it is my duty to investigate her situation."

She let go of the guard pinned to the wall. He slumped to the ground, gasping for air.

Larena immediately staggered forward, her knees shaking, and grabbed onto the strong looking lady's heavy military coat.

"Please help my sister," Larena begged, sobbing uncontrollably. "She was called to the Lord's study… my sister... my sister was saying something about serving the Master…. No one comes back after serving the Master. Please save her…"

The girl fell to her knees, clutching the hem of Parisa's uniform. "Please. I'll do anything… you can have anything. Please don't let my sister become one of the lost… we just reunited two days ago. Please…"

Parisa first looked at the terrified guard. "The Lord forcing an attendant to service them. That is against the Kingdom's laws regarding contracted labor."

Parisa’s rage reached a crescendo. The ambient mana in the courtyard began to visibly warp and distort the air, shimmering with violent heat. She gently set aside the little girl's hands, stepping past her.

"Don't worry," Parisa said, her voice dropping to a terrifying, deadly calm. "I swear I shall bring her back to you."

She leaned low, getting into a running stance. Complex, glowing red magical circles flared to life all around her, snapping into existence at her feet and wrapping around her calves. She was stacking buffs on buffs. Agility. Strength. Dexterity boost.

She sprung forward like a slingshot breaking at high speed, her boots shattering the cobblestones beneath her as she launched herself toward the main doors.

Noble mansions all followed the same general, gothic design in Dolaria. The Lord's study would always be above the main stairs on the second floor, straight ahead, meant to entertain guests and project authority.

Parisa shot up the grand marble stairs, a blur of crimson and gold. She didn't even give a thought to the heavy, reinforced oak door of the study. She planted a booted foot squarely in the center of the wood, blowing it apart with raw, magically-enhanced brute strength.

Splinters of oak and twisted iron hinges flew into the room like shrapnel.

The sight that greeted her sickened her to her core.

The pompous, fat man was standing over the helpless elven maid. She assumed instantly this was the little girl's sister.

The maid was in a pitiful state. Her simple dress was torn to rags. Deep, bloody gash marks covered her arms and back, clearly from a heavy leather whip. Massive, dark bruises were blooming all over her face and body.

Parisa saw the girl lying in a spreading pool of her own blood, being brutally beaten by the Lord. It was true. Her brother's letter was completely accurate. This Lord was truly, irredeemably vile.

Lord Greenwood, on the other hand, was quite displeased at his fun being interrupted by an exploding door. He looked up, his chest heaving, his knuckles bloody. He saw Parisa's face standing in the ruined doorway and completely misread the situation.

"I was just about to ask for a second," Greenwood sneered, wiping sweat from his brow. "For the insolence of interrupting me, you shall be second. Let me finish playing with this one first."

Parisa saw the Lord pull his arm back, preparing to pummel the girl's face again, a look of sadistic glee twisting his features.

The world slowed down.

The girl on the floor was taking shallow, wet breaths, clearly hanging onto life by a very thin thread.

Parisa dashed across the room with all the might she could pull into a single, focused strike. She didn't want to kill the man. She needed him alive for the trial.

She landed a devastating, spinning kick directly onto Greenwood's torso.

The impact sounded like a cannon firing. The fat man’s body deformed around her boot. He was launched backward, tearing through the heavy mahogany desk as if it were made of paper. He crashed heavily onto the far stone wall, creating deep, spiderweb-like cracks from the sheer force of the impact. He slumped to the floor, groaning, completely incapacitated.

Parisa's Royal Guards came rushing into the ruined study a moment later, their swords drawn.

Parisa didn't look at the broken Lord. She looked at her lead assistant.

"Heal this girl at any cost," Parisa commanded, her voice ringing with absolute authority. "Even if it costs you your life. She is the prime witness to these atrocities."

The assistant rushed forward immediately, dropping to his knees in the blood. He began barking orders to the other several mages in the unit, who gathered together and started casting high-tier, glowing white healing magic on the beaten and battered maid.

Parisa turned away from the gruesome scene. She reached into her coat and took out her Royal Inspector seal. It was a heavy medallion of platinum and sapphire.

This item had a very specific, highly classified special functionality. It allowed her to temporarily seize control of all contracted individuals within a certain radius.

In truth, the State kept a magical back door to all contracted individuals for situations exactly like this, acting as a fail-safe against rogue nobility. With this item, Parisa could temporarily transfer the magical contracts from Greenwood to herself, granting her the authority to order them to speak the absolute truth without fear of the Mana Poisoning triggering.

She held the seal high, funneling her immense mana into the artifact. It pulsed with a blinding, blue light.

"Bring all the servants to me," Parisa ordered her guards, her voice echoing out into the hallway, promising a reckoning that would shake the capital to its foundations. "I shall personally question all of them."
