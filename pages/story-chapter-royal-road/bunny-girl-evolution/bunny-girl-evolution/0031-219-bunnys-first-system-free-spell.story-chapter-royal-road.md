“He got arrested?!” exclaimed Elise. “Why?!”

“Well, when they were going around asking everyone about you, he said he knew that you were a changeling, but didn’t say anything. The rest of us were pretending to be tricked, so he got in a bit of trouble.”

“What happened to him?”

“Oh, they let him go. Theresa told them that he only found out very recently, which was true, and that he informed Emmett immediately, and they believed her. So he’s back on campus this semester. He’s asked me about you, like, three times now. I bet he’d be really happy if you dreamwalked to him.”

“No,” said Elise. “And don’t you dare tell him I said anything about him.”

“But he would be so happy.”

“Sophie, I’m serious. I have no interest, and it’s cruel to play with his feelings like that.”

Sophie’s smile faded. “Yeah, I guess you’re right.”

“But you know whose feelings it might not be cruel to play with?”

“Who?”

“I seem to recall a boy in your class who–”

“Stop!”

Elise spend the next few minutes coaxing Sophie into admitting that despite her maybe potential interest, she was pretty sure that she was never going to get a boyfriend, because now everyone knew she was a Dragon’s Goddaughter and it was messing up everything. A lot of guys had actually asked her out, but none of them had been interested in her before, and the one that she was interested in was now too scared to do anything. Elise told him that she should just make the first move in that case, but Sophie vehemently rejected any such proposals, and Elise eventually had to drop the subject.

“What about Nick and Bianca?”

“They’re fine, I guess,” said Sophie with a shrug. “Nick is a little annoyed because he lost all the privileges and respect he got for all the Blood Syndicate stuff. He’s not officially in trouble or anything, but since everyone knows that Ostra set up the Blood Syndicate, it’s kind of a weird subject. And now everyone’s treating him weird because he was involved, like he didn’t get captured and forced to fight for entertainment against his will or something.”

“Yeah, that sucks.”

“Actually, Bianca’s got something going on now, maybe,” said Sophie. “That professor who found her wanted to talk to her about maybe doing some more research on the Carsas plants and stuff. I dunno how that’s going, though. I think they’re talking about a research grant or something, but Bianca isn’t sure since a lot of the research will be taking place on-site.”

“Are they setting up a teleportation pad on Carsas?”

“Yeah, but it won’t be ready for a few months, so she’s got time to think.”

“I see.”

“Oh yeah, speaking of professors…”

From there, Sophie entered full chatter mode, talking for literal hours about the more minor things that had happened since Elise left. Her professors were all experts in their fields, but half of them were “extremely weird.” Elise’s first thought when she heard that was of Patricia Dellinger, and only a few minutes later, Sophie confirmed that Dellinger was exactly who she was talking about.

Elise was shocked to learn that Dellinger was actually a 9th Tier mage. She had assumed that the crazy old woman was impressive to be able to keep her position in spite of her personality, but she hadn’t realized how impressive she was. She was apparently one of only seven publicly known 9th tiers in all of Jelor, and was among the oldest and presumably strongest of them. And she was not any better in a one-on-one setting than she was in the classroom.

“She started telling me which banks would be able to detect my invisibility spell!” Sophie exclaimed. “Why does she even know that?!”

Her other teachers were also eccentric, though none quite as much as Dellinger. Her combat teacher smelled like alcohol and had her just endlessly drill with simple spells every lesson. Her aether teacher was Emmett’s sister, who was not as impressive or as patient as her brother, and was understandably not in the best headspace. Her “adventuring” teacher was her favorite, since every lesson with her involved a field trip across the country through a teleportation scroll to a new and interesting environment where Sophie learned about survival tactics and things like that in the middle of dangerous spawn zones. Elise thought that sounded extremely exorbitant, especially knowing how much teleportation scrolls cost, but she was glad that Jelor was actually putting everything into her education. And a little worried, since the fact they were dedicating so many resources toward her meant they expected to need her…

The conversation eventually turned back to more serious topics, and Elise learned that, to her relief, things had largely calmed down since the initial revelation of Ostra’s involvement with the Blood Syndicate. They had been so caught off-guard by Jelor finding out that most of the high-ranking members were in prison cells, and the ones who got away were lying low. Freddy was supposedly dead, Firona had disappeared after her battle with Emmett, and Rayna’s location had never been known in the first place. Just Rayna and Firona alone were enough to wreak havoc if they chose to, but thus far, they had not.

Interestingly, nothing had actually changed in Corunia, in spite of everything else. Elise and Sophie spent a little while theorizing about why, and they came up with two viable options. Either Oberon was already known by most governments in the world, and was discrete enough from Ostra not to provoke the wrath of the world, or he had infiltrated every government so thoroughly that they were terrified to go after him out of fear of what he could do. Sophie thought it was the latter, while Elise hoped it was the former, but was likely a combination of the two. Even if he was a known quantity, she couldn’t imagine being comfortable in a leadership role while also knowing what Oberon was capable of.

Elise was actually a little bit sad to hear that nothing had changed in Corunia. That meant that Oberon had done nothing in response to Titania’s requests for him to ascend, and that meant that if anything had happened to Titania…

Their conversation continued for a full two hours until Sophie started yawning too much to talk for very long. Elise was doing the same, so fifteen minutes later, they wrapped up their conversation, and Elise left after promising to visit again three times a week.

The next day, Elise felt extra motivated, and dove straight into working on her aether pulses. Seeing Sophie not only doing alright, but having kingdom-level resources poured into her education had inspired her to work harder too. She didn’t expect to catch up to Sophie in magical ability, but she at least wanted to get to the point where she could actually use magic without the System.

Two weeks passed quickly as she worked her way through each and every single aether pulse variation until she had them all down. Many times, she had been tempted to skip steps and try to jump straight to manipulating mana, but the crystal warned against that, and she reluctantly took the warning to heart. This was possibly the only resource on mastering mana without the System in the entire world, and she wasn’t about to go disregarding what it said just because she was impatient.

At the end of the two weeks, though, she finally got the last one down. Each one was more abstract and strange than the last, but the more she did, the more she could almost feel her brain being rewired. It was a completely alien way of thinking about aether, at least to her, but the more she did it, the more it started to intuitively make sense.

Her biggest breakthrough had been when she realized that the translation of the instructions wasn’t quite perfect. She had been connecting to the System to use {Tongues} to understand what it was saying, but there were some words in the crystal that she realized didn’t translate to English very well. For example, when it told her to “rotate” her aether, it wasn’t actually saying to rotate it. It was a completely different verb with no English translation, so {Tongues} was approximating. And there were similar mistranslations with words like “oscillate,” “push,” “pull,” and a few others.

When she realized that, it completely changed the way she read some of the instructions. I took some trial and error to figure out what the words were supposed to translate to, and even then, she didn’t have any proper English for them, but she understood what they meant. It also made her realize that she was doing some of the older drills wrong. She had to relearn a few of the beginner drills, but once she had those down, it made the later ones significantly easier, now that she knew what she was actually supposed to be doing.

When she finally got the last variation, she shouted loudly, startling the Abyssal Rat who had the misfortune of becoming her practice partner. It started squirming against its restraints, but Elise ignored it as she summoned the crystal, and reread through the instructions for doing the first mana drills. She had already read through so many times she had it memorized, but she didn’t want to screw anything up.

When she finished, she turned back to the rat, who was still trying and failing to break free. She used her homebrewed version of {Suggest} to soothe it, calming it down so it held still again, then held out a paw near its face. She didn’t really know why she was doing it this way, but she felt that a moment like this needed a witness. A witness who wasn’t terrified and angry and trying its best not to look at her.

The final step of jumping from aether pulses to actual magic was simple on paper. All she needed to do was connect to mana with her aether. It was completely different from anything she had done before, but the crystal also said it was supposed to be quite simple for anyone who had finished all the aether pulse variations, which she had. It would be easy to connect to her own mana, at least. The crystal said that connecting to atmospheric mana was possible, but very difficult, and came with risks she wasn’t ready for, so personal mana was all she was to use for now.

She closed her eyes, focusing on her soul, and reaching out to her mana cores. She had nearly forgotten about her mana cores after unlocking them so long ago, but the crystal actually discussed them extensively. Under the System, the mana cores were really only useful for accelerating the body’s natural purification process, but outside the System, they did much more. They served as anchors for aether threads, and they could be controlled much more precisely than many people believed they could for use in various types of spells.

It took Elise two minutes or so to first establish the connection. And then began going through the steps outlined in the crystal. It was immediately apparent why she had been instructed to master the aether pulses before attempting this. Those aether pulse variants were exactly what she needed to use to control the mana in her cores.

It was also obvious why she had been told to practice on a partner, rather than on her own cores. Her cores were very particular about how they accepted aether. Connecting was relatively simple, and basic aether pulses were smooth. They did very little except send tiny ripples through her internal mana, but they were simple and easy. Doing anything more complicated that was likely to break the connection, though. If she “rotated” her aether too fast, the connection broke. If she “oscillated” too rapidly, it broke. She accidentally snapped the connection a dozen times in her first quarter hour, just from imperfect aether pulses.

I did not have these mastered as well as I thought I did, she realized.

But still, she was determined to get something working, so over the next two hours, she gradually worked on the series of aetheric gestures that the crystal outlined, patiently restarting whenever she messed up. It was tedious and frustrating, but also satisfying. The initial aether pulse only rippled the mana, but as she got further and further down the chain, more specific things happened. The mana flowed, first slowly, then more quickly. It moved in her entire body, but it also gradually began to concentrate in her foreleg, traveling toward her paw.

She hit a wall once she got the mana to her paw. Going from inside her body to outside was a huge step, and the crystal said it would be difficult to get on the first day, but she kept pushing anyway. It took another hour for her to finally get the smallest puff of mana to exit out the front of her paw. Baring her teeth in what was likely a terrifying smile, she redoubled her efforts working through the gesture chain again, each time letting out more and more mana until finally, she had a steady stream flowing out of her.

Now the final step! she thought excitedly.

She re-opened her eyes, staring at her paw, as she sent the final aetheric pulse toward her mana core, changing the nature of the mana in her paw from neutral to light. The invisible mana stream suddenly became a visible one of pale light, startling the rat and making it screech in anger. Its reaction shocked Elise so much she accidentally let the spell drop, but then she burst out into laughter.

“I did it!” she shouted, falling backward in exhaustion. “Finally!”

She held out her paw again, running through the aetheric gestures until the light appeared again. It was the simplest spell in the crystal, it took her three hours to figure it out, a full two and a half minutes to cast, and it was completely useless—unless her goal was to anger Abyssal Rats, apparently—but it was magic, and she had done it entirely on her own, without relying on the System. She had never felt so accomplished.

I still have a long way to go, she thought. But I can’t wait until I get there.
