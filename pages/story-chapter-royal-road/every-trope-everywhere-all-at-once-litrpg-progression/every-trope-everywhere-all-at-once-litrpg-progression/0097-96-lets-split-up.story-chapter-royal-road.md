The same long red carpet stretched from the front door down through the entrance hall. The same wooden furniture was still covered in dust, and the chandelier above was still as dim as before, swaying despite the lack of wind inside the building. Two stairs stood on the opposite end of the room, leading up to the second floor.

But there was one thing that wasn’t the same as the last time I had come here.

Camille.

She wasn’t anywhere in sight. She wasn’t there to greet us or welcome us into the manor.

“Camille?” Reina called, cupping her hands around her mouth. “Camille, are you here?”

Her voice bounced through the walls, echoing back to us. Nothing.

“Maybe she’s asleep?” Kaelor tried. “Actually—do ghosts even sleep?”

“Or maybe she’s invisible,” Talon said. “She’s a ghost, after all.”

I shook my head. “She’s a ghost that we can see. Though the strange thing is that if your hands go through her body, she’ll pass out.”

Meriel raised an eyebrow. “A ghost that passes out when a hand goes through her?”

“Yup.”

“Interesting…”

Kaelor shrugged. “Let’s split up and take a look around. We’ll cover more ground that way.”

Ah yes. The famous “let’s split up in the creepy haunted mansion” strategy. That always ends well.

[TROPE INITIATED: LET’S SPLIT UP]

Description: Investigating a haunted location becomes more efficient once everyone separates. Surely nothing bad will happen. Either that, or everyone ends up getting their own ghost encounter.

Completion criteria: Split up and find Camille.

Note: A proven method in parties with a brown dog with black spots.

Huh. Since that initiated a trope, I guess we really should split up after all.

“Talon and Reina—come with me,” Kaelor said. “We’ll cover the ground floor. Meriel and Allene can handle the second floor.”

“Sir, yes sir!” Talon exclaimed, sounding awfully excited despite the situation.

“Sure thing!” Reina said.

Before Meriel and I could say anything about it, the other three headed off toward a hallway to the side, leaving us in the entrance room.

“They sure do make decisions quickly,” Meriel said. “How carefree. I actually like that about them.”

I facepalmed. “The more time I spend with them, the more I realize we rarely stop to think first before acting.”

She laughed. “It’s been working quite well so far, hasn’t it? It gives our party some personality.”

I smiled. “Yeah… they do make things pretty fun.”

We began to head toward the stairs.

“Did a trope initiate just now for you?” Meriel asked.

“Yup—the one where a party splits up in a creepy place. You’re getting really good at reading me now, aren’t you?”

“Well, it’s obvious from my perspective because I’m actively looking for it. Seeing your eyes go from side to side could only mean one thing. Considering how the others don’t notice it—and how I also never noticed it prior to my awakening—it’s a lot less obvious to other people than it seems.”

“Actively looking for it…? So you like to stare at me? I didn’t take you for a creep.”

She pinched my cheek.

“Ow!” I cried.

“That’s what you deserve for being a rascal.”

“Oh, yeah?”

I pointed at her. In that instant, she bowed as low as she could, keeping her head below mine.

“You—!” she said, flustered. “Not this again!”

“BAHAHAHA!” I let out, putting my hands on my hips and puffing my chest out. “This skill’s a lot more useful than I thought!”

[SKILL: THE FORCEFUL BOW]

Type: Active

Effects:

- Force the target to bow below your head level

- Only works on Meriel

“Stop it, this instant!” she cried.

“I don’t know how to!”

“Quit messing around!”

At that moment, we heard a cry. We froze.

“What was that…?” I whispered. “It came from upstairs, didn’t it?”

We stayed quiet for a moment, waiting to see if we would be able to hear it again. It never came.

I walked up the stairs. Meriel followed behind.

“I’m seriously creeped out,” I said, hugging myself. “If I hear that again, I might just run out…”

“That’s fantastic and all, but could you release the spell already?”

I turned back, watching her walk up the stairs while her head was still down. I snickered at the sight. If I squinted my eyes enough, it just looked like some old lady with bad posture going up the stairs.

“Sorry, sorry,” I said, releasing the skill.

She stood upright, stretching her neck side to side. “I hate that skill of yours. I really do.”

“Well, maybe don’t be violent to me next time,” I said.

“Oh, come on. You call pinching your cheek violent?”

Then, the same cry came again—even louder than last time. We exchanged glances.

“Get ready,” Meriel said. “I wouldn’t be surprised if something jumps out at us from the shadows.”

I nodded, pulling Dawn Sunderer out of my [INVENTORY].

Once we reached the top of the stairs, we looked around for any sign of where the scream had come from. The manor up here looked as still and dead as it did on the first floor. The decor was consistent throughout the manor, from the paintings to the lighting to the furniture.

“You go ahead,” Meriel said. “I’ll be right behind you.”

“Huh…? Why should I go first? We should be heading through together.”

“You’re the one with a sword, not me.”

I rolled my eyes. “Just be honest for once. You’re scared, aren’t you?”

She looked away.

“So you are scared!” I laughed.

“Just shut up and keep walking. And besides, this is a better formation. I’ll be able to support you from the back with my spells if anything happens.”

I shrugged, walking ahead. “Alrighty, if you say so. I guess awakening didn’t give you more bravery. Just let the great Allene take care of things from here on—”

The cry came again—so loud that it almost sounded as if it were just in the room right beside us.

…

Wait a minute. It was coming from the room beside us. There were dozens of doors through the hallway, but coincidentally, the one closest to us was the one the cry had come from. I don’t know whether to be terrified or glad that our search ended early.

I gulped, gripping Dawn Sunderer tightly. I walked up to the door and looked back. Meriel nodded.

“Get ready,” I began, grabbing the door handle. “Three… two… o—”

The door opened before I could do so myself, causing me to fall through. I faceplanted onto the floor and nearly lost my grip on Dawn Sunderer, but I managed to keep it in my hand despite the fall.

“What the—?!” I cried. “We’re under attack! Meriel, help me now!”

But she didn’t say anything.

When I looked up to see who had opened the door, my eyes widened.

It was Camille.

“Allene?” she said, looking at my rather… distasteful position on the floor. “What are you doing here?”

I quickly picked myself back up, wiping away the dust from my clothes. How embarrassing.

“Um…” I began. “I just thought I’d come back here to visit you. Actually, I wanted to talk to you about something.”

“Talk to me…?” she said.

Meriel stepped into the room. “It’s about Aurelius. But first things first—was that you who was crying just now?”

“Ah,” she said, turning away to hide her face. “Did you hear that?”

“You were crying quite loudly, so yes,” Meriel said. “We heard it while we were on the first floor.”

“Did something happen?” I asked.

Instead of an explanation, she just gave an apologetic bow.

“I’m sorry. It’s nothing. I should’ve been down there to greet you all. I’ve failed my duty as a maid.”

I shook my head. “Camille, please don’t worry about that. Could we just talk to you for a quick second?”

She looked at me, then at Meriel.

“Ah,” Meriel said. “Sorry, I forgot to introduce myself. My name is Meriel—one of Allene’s friends. We have a few other friends down on the first floor looking for you as well. I hope you don’t mind our sudden entry.”

Camille shook her head. “Oh, not at all. The more guests, the merrier.”

“I’ll go back downstairs and grab the others,” Meriel said. “You stay here with her, Allene.”

I nodded. She headed out the door.

A second after, something in my pocket began to vibrate.

“Hm?”

I reached in and pulled it out.

It was the Soul Anchor ring. I forgot I had left it in there after I had grabbed it from my [INVENTORY] to show it to the others.

But why was it shaking?

The ring began to pulse with a faint purple light.

Once.

Twice.

Three times.

Each pulse was just a little brighter than the last, and Camille noticed it as well.

“That ring…” she whispered. “That’s Sir Aurelius’s!”

When she stepped closer, the ring’s vibration grew more rapid. I knew right away what that meant.

The ring was reacting to her.
