“What…?” I muttered. “Why’s the Soul Anchor ring reacting to you?”

“I… I don’t know,” Camille said.

What was even stranger was that it only reacted to her now instead of back then with Aurelius. This wasn’t the first time she had come close to the ring.

“But wait… why do you have Aurelius’s ring with you?” she asked.

Before I could say anything, Meriel and the others appeared by the door, stepping into the room.

“Oh?” Kaelor began. “Is that Camille? Nice to meet you. I have to say—I’ve never seen a ghost in person before.”

“Hello!” Talon waved, bowing.

Camille raised her brows the moment her eyes met with Reina’s.

“Reina…? You’re here as well?”

Reina nodded. “It’s so nice to see you again! I hope it wasn’t so painful after um… you were killed in front of our eyes.”

Camille shook her head. “Oh, not at all. It just felt like a pinch.”

That was funny, considering I had told Reina the exact same thing back then. Speaking of which, I was reminded of the moment I intentionally decapitated Reina so that we could respawn at the blue lantern checkpoint to escape Aurelius.

Ah, good times—

—is what I would say if I was insane.

In typical maid fashion, Camille stepped aside and opened her palm toward the room, welcoming everyone in. Once they came inside, she shut the door behind her.

The room was furnished with a red sofa, a small table, a bookshelf, and a fireplace. A small bed was tucked away at the corner, large enough for one person and one person only. If I tried sleeping there with Meriel, Reina, and Aeris like our situation back at home, someone would end up suffocating to death. And for some reason, I’m about one hundred percent confident that “someone” would be me.

The ring in my hand continued to vibrate without end. It was, undoubtedly, reacting to Camille. Each time I brought the ring closer to her, the shaking intensified.

By now, it was shaking so immensely in my hand that the friction caused my skin to burn. I yelped, dropping the ring. It clattered on the ground, landing just beside Camille’s foot.

“Careful!” Kaelor warned. “Don’t drop something so precious!”

Before anyone could question what had happened, the ring let out a bright purple glow, covering the entire room in its color.

“AGH! My eyes!” Kaelor cried, shielding his face.

“What’s going on?!” Reina yelled, turning away from the light.

Once the light settled, we turned back to the ring—or at least, where it should’ve been.

My eyes scanned the entire floor. Did somebody accidentally kick it away while we weren’t looking?

“Allene,” Meriel muttered. “Don’t make any sudden move.”

“What? Why—”

Someone’s breathing ran along my neck, causing shivers to travel down my spine.

Someone… something… was standing just behind me. But it wasn’t the same kind of breathing you would hear from a normal person. Not Kaelor, not Reina, not Talon.

Every breath sounded like a gasp. It wheezed between each one, letting out a deep rumble every third breath. It sounded like the kind of breathing you would hear from a monster whose lungs have been damaged by ashes.

I kept my eyes ahead. Meriel and Camille were standing in front, staring at what was behind me with widened eyes.

The others didn’t say anything. Was it out of fear for what was right behind me? What could possibly be so threatening that even Kaelor didn’t dare say a single word?

“Fascinating…” a deep, raspy voice said. “A soul that has unraveled, and a soul that cannot be recognized, both in one room.”

I gulped. Something about the voice made me too afraid to talk.

“Face me,” he said. “I’ve not appeared as an enemy.”

I slowly faced him.

He had hair as black as the void, long enough to reach his chest. His eyes were entirely black, and his pupils were just tiny slits burning red. His upper body wasn’t covered by any fabric, exposing all the scars from battle, but everything below his waist was draped in cloth. His ears were as long as Meriel’s, and his horns were as large as Aeris’s. His wings resembled those of a bat, but they were tattered and torn, seemingly unsuitable for flight.

“A demon,” Kaelor said. He had a dagger in his hand, which he had drawn while I wasn’t looking.

“Put your weapon away,” the man said. “Bloodshed doesn’t suit me.”

“I don’t see any other reason why you would appear before us,” Kaelor said.

The demon shook his head. “Don’t lump me in with my foul brethren. Violence is all they know. But I know better than them. Let us talk. If we fail to come to an agreement, I will return to where I came from, and we can pretend we’ve never crossed paths.”

“Agreement…?” I said. “For what? And who even are you?”

The man raised his right hand. We gasped. The Soul Anchor ring was on one of his fingers.

“My name is Gal’Meth, the soul residing within this very ring. The body you’re seeing now is nothing but a projection. Any harm done to this body means nothing, which is why I would suggest you lay down your weapons and spare your energy.”

So the soul that was said to be inside the ring when I had cast [SMITH’S EYE] was him all along? A demon?

[ITEM: SOUL ANCHOR RING]

Quality: Masterwork

Durability: Perfect

Craftsmanship: Impossible. Crafted using techniques unknown to modern blacksmithing.

Hidden Observations:

- This artifact houses a dormant soul

- The soul remains sealed

- Activation requirements are currently unknown

The fact that he had appeared before us now meant that the activation requirement had been met, right?

“Why have you decided to show yourself now?” I asked.

Gal’Meth pointed across the room. “Because her soul will soon perish.”

It wasn’t at Meriel or Reina.

It was at Camille.

“Perish…?” Reina said. “What do you mean?”

The demon stared at Camille. “It would be better if you heard it from her instead of me.”

We all turned to her. She looked away, keeping her head low.

“Camille…” I said. “What does he mean?”

She didn’t respond.

“Camille,” I tried again. “Please.”

I walked in front of her, making her look at me. Her eyes were beginning to grow teary, but she managed to hold herself together.

She took a deep breath before sitting down on the chair beside her.

“They’re gone,” she began. “Rin… Velena… Zed. Even the others. They’re all gone.”

None of those were names we recognized.

“They were supposed to be in their rooms,” she continued. “But when I went to check, they were all gone. I don’t understand why!”

I exchanged glances with the others.

“Those must be the other people Aurelius was trying to save,” Meriel whispered.

“Other people?” I whispered back.

She nodded. “Remember what his notebook said? He wasn’t just trying to save Camille. There are other souls in this manor as well, trapped in this dungeon like she is.”

Gal’Meth stepped toward Camille, standing beside her. “And you’re next,” he said. “Your soul will be the next to perish. This dungeon is damaged. The others are already gone because of it, and you’ll be the next to disappear.”

“Dungeon?” she asked.

That’s right. Camille wasn’t supposed to know she was in a dungeon. The rules prevented sentient creatures inside dungeons from even understanding the concept, just like Aeris before she escaped with us.

“Dungeon…” Camille said again. “So I’m trapped in a dungeon…?”

“You know about it?” I asked.

She nodded. Could it be because the dungeon’s broken now?

“The only reason her soul—and the other souls—were able to exist so long without a proper vessel was because the dungeon allowed them to,” the demon said. “But now that this prison is on the verge of collapse, their souls can no longer be sustained.”

Hearing that, Camille slumped into her chair, looking down at her lap. “That can’t be…”

“Is there anything we can do about that?” I asked.

Gal’Meth smiled. “Of course there is. Which is why I’ve appeared before you.”

Hearing that, Kaelor shot forward, pointing the dagger at the demon’s face. Though it was just an inch away from his eye, Gal’Meth didn’t react. He just smiled.

“You act rather rashly for a Firstborn.”

“Don’t listen to him,” Kaelor said. “The demon’s here to make a contract with us. Don’t do it. Not unless you want to end up like Aurelius.”

My eyes widened as the pieces started to fall into place. “Contract…? Wait, so the demon who gave Aurelius the power to manipulate souls—”

“Is this one,” Meriel said.

I gulped, turning to him. His smile persisted despite the blade in front of him.

“You make the contract sound scarier than it should be,” the demon said. “A contract only exists if both parties agree. Nobody loses in any of this.”

“How could you say that, knowing what happened to Aurelius?” Kaelor asked.

Gal’Meth shook his head. “Did I cause his insanity? Did I ever tell him to kill? To conduct experiments with souls and bodies? No. I never did any of that. I simply provided him with the magic he wanted. In return, he gave me a significant portion of his lifespan, allowing my existence to continue. Even if you hadn’t killed him during the battle back then, he would’ve died regardless in the years to come.”

“Why would he sacrifice such a thing?” I asked.

“Because he was willing to sacrifice everything—even himself—to save Camille and all the others who died in his manor on that tragic day. The Soul Anchor ring had been his family’s heirloom, passed down through generations. When he lost everything, his soul began to fade. That’s what happens to a soul when all hope is lost. It fades. But that was when I appeared before him, offering him the means to bring them all back. And yet…”

The demon chuckled. That was the most human emotion we’d seen from him yet. “The archmage imprisoned Aurelius and ruined his plans. Not that it concerns me. I already got what I needed from him.”

“So you appear whenever a soul begins to fade?” I asked.

He nodded. That fully confirmed the Soul Anchor ring’s hidden activation requirement. He appeared before us now because of Camille’s fading soul.

I turned to her, realizing how quiet she was. Her expression was completely hidden by her lowered head.

Out of nowhere, Camille shot up from her chair, her eyes wide open as tears streaked down her cheeks.

“Aurelius… What do you mean he’s dead?!”
