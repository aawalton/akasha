Zora had been depicted as a gigantic moth in the information packet Nick received after stepping through the rift, but that was no longer the visage the monarch wore.

While the dreadbeast still had compound eyes, segmented antennae, and gossamer wings, its form was far more human after devouring Fenrir and Ban-Li. Wreathed in a mantle of sinister shadows, the Death of Dreams ran its gaze across Nick, showing no sign of duress as he took its measure in turn.

Zora’s slender frame was ten feet tall. Its wings were nowhere to be found, although he had a hunch the creature could manifest them at will. The monarch walked on two legs and used the other four limbs as arms. Each extremity was covered in razored protrusions, which meant it didn’t need weapons to deal a mortal blow.

The dreadbeast’s Concept was wrapped around its torso like a robe, reinforcing the impression that Nick was looking at a person rather than a monster or beast. Its gaze was alert, and it was considerably more intelligent than Vashir or Xuthu—a creature capable of utilizing complex strategies, multileveled tactics, and deception.

Eager to analyze his opponent before their battle began, Nick absorbed the information his various perceptual powers provided between the drawing of one breath and the next, examining Zora with Size Up, mind sense, mage sight, and something new, a gut feeling that was coming from his Concept itself.

His danger-sensing skill recognized the mothman as a dire threat, but it was having a hard time providing details. He couldn’t get a clear sense of its attributes, let alone any weaknesses. Size Up isn’t advanced enough to read a creature this powerful, the dreadbeast is countering it somehow, or both, he decided. It’s a good thing Yarren already told me about its abilities.

With a surge of will, Nick hyper-focused on Size Up’s feedback, squeezing a few extra drops of insight from the skill. The dreadbeast had him beat in raw levels by a considerable margin. It specialized in a combination of magic and psychic attacks; plus its minions and the abilities it had stolen from the other monarchs.

His mind sense encountered a wall—he simply couldn’t read the creature’s intentions or emotions. When he tried, his psyche slid past like oil over water. This was his first time encountering an opponent with advanced psychic powers, but he could tell right away that was the problem.

Nick had a vague impression that his will had more weight and his reserves of Psi were deeper, but he lacked a technique that could pierce the mothman’s mental shield. Predatory Domain might do the trick, but it will only work once, and only if I strike when Zora’s guard is down.

His mage sight wasn’t impeded. The monarch either didn’t know how to use mana stealth or saw no point in doing so. The mana roiling in its core felt… turbulent. Fire and darkness waged war on one another, lacking the synergistic harmony of Bandit’s multi-aspected energy. There were two other frequences in the dreadbeast’s core, and they didn’t play nice with each other.

He had perceived traces of them before, but this was his first time perceiving these variants in quantity. In that moment, he was certain that he was looking at death and dream mana, which were circling Zora’s energetic network like two predators claiming the same territory. Nick’s mana pool was considerably bigger, but that was probably because the dreadbeast had conjured so many constructs while pouring energy into the ritual for days.

The final partition of his attention was focused on the information he was receiving through the All-Devouring Blade. This sense was new to him, but their Concepts were resonating with one another. In part because they shared elements in common, but mostly due to their inherent animosity and close proximity.

Zora’s Concept was overbearing—even with so much of its magic dispersed across the city to maintain its Nightmare army. It was thicker than Nick’s, if that was the right word. The dreadbeast was deep into Tier 2, while he had only ascended a short while ago.

However, its energy didn’t feel unified the same way Devour did. The Concept’s framework was pure Nightmare, but there were burning patches and swaths that felt like skittering shadows suspended within the ravenous maleficence that wanted to terrorize him to the breaking point and feast on his fear.

Whatever process it used to absorb its kin left portions of their Concepts intact instead of subsuming them completely like the All-Devouring Blade. That must be why Zora can use fire and shadow magic, while I didn’t gain any new spells from consuming Xuthu and Vashir.

His multileveled analysis was completed in record time. Zora had studied him too, but Nick had the impression the monarch hadn’t gleaned as much. Yarren mentioned the creature was cautious by nature and preferred not to engage an unknown quantity head-on. That information was proven accurate when instead of attacking, the monarch spoke, every sonorous syllable oozing with a strangely compelling intensity.

“What are you? I sense Xuthu and Vashir’s power but not a speck of their essence remains. You have devoured their minds, memories, and magic in the truest sense of the word, leaving nothing of their nature behind. Your Concept is clearly vampiric, but it doesn’t drink from the bountiful energy this world has to offer.

“Stranger, you are no friend to my kind, but we walk kindred paths. The multiverse is vast, and there are countless planets to consume. Why risk our limitless futures by battling to the death before reaching for the stars? Did you come here for the library? I would be willing to share a portion of its riches as a gesture of goodwill. I’ll even let you keep what you stole from the… lesser members of my kind.”

The monarch’s tone was reasonable and its words were persuasive. In spite of himself, Nick found himself considering the merits of the proposal. This wasn’t his planet and Zora was immensely powerful. Why not call off this foolish quest, negotiate for Bandit and Kenji’s release, and leave this world to its fate with some bloodline gems for his trouble? Nick couldn’t protect his people if he was dead, and the dreadbeast was too strong—too brilliant, cunning, and superior in every…

Something is wrong. Its words are honey, but Zora is radiating killing intent. His eyes narrowed when he spotted a delicate weave of dream mana floating around his head, blended with an energy he instinctively recognized as the creature’s Psi. In that moment, he knew the Death of Dreams was guiding his thoughts and tinkering with his emotions.

Nick reflexively free cast pure mana, pushing it out of his channels to encase his body. At the same time, he gathered his will and flexed, shoving the monarch’s psyche away from his own. When their Psi touched, Zora’s shield faltered and he received a glimpse into the dreadbeast’s mind.

The creature was lying through its teeth. It hated him with an intensity that took his breath away. He had stolen a prize from under the monarch’s nose, and it intended to make him suffer until his body was broken and his mind a shattered mess. It was playing games to buy time, waiting for its army to arrive before making its move. Nick had taken out a fair chunk of the constructs on this side of the city, but thousands were headed his way.

Lurk was still battling Zora’s elite guard, but the dino was hard-pressed. More worrisome still, Bandit and Kenji were facing off against an endless swarm, making their last stand before they were overwhelmed. The dreadbeast delighted in the plight of his friends. It was eager to fill them with fear, drain their energy to the dregs, then torture them to death for daring to challenge its rule.

The images were graphic, gruesome, and hit him all at once. Combined with the monarch’s abhorrent attempt to control his mind and their Concepts’ compulsion to brawl, something inside Nick snapped.

Rage roiled in his gut as killing intent erupted from his position like a star going nova. Consummate anger evaporated Arctic Clarity, fueling the force of his psychic onslaught when Predatory Domain broke over the dreadbeast like a leaden tsunami.

Later, he would realize that in addition to threatening his friends, the creature had tried to dominate his mind, an act that was anathema to the All-Devouring Blade. Nick’s Concept refused to suffer such indignity, resonating with a fundamental aspect of his being that had awakened during his war with the Crimson Blight. He would die before he succumbed to the will of another, and anything that attempted to consume him would be Devoured in turn.

In that moment of absolute fury, his body moved of its own accord. Instead of casting spells or swinging his sword, he leapt while drawing back his fist. He closed the distance in the blink of an eye—poised to clock the arrogant creature square in the jaw.

What happened next surprised them both.

The dreadbeast turned its head to bite, intending to shear his hand off at the wrist. However, man and monarch alike had dramatically underestimated the speed of the strike. Zora had no way of knowing Nick’s attributes had skyrocketed when Blightborn evolved, and Nick was still adjusting to the extraordinary upgrades that had enhanced every fiber of his being.

He hadn’t internalized the extent to which tripling his Magic improved Force Acceleration on top of his explosive gains in Dexterity and Strength. As a result, he had been unconsciously holding back after leaving the Blade World, relying on self-preservation instincts that didn’t apply to his present form.

Unburdened by his prior conceptions of the possible, his untainted anger dredged up every scrap of the power at his command—might beyond what any human had known before. His fist issued a thunderous crack as it shattered the sound barrier, knuckles flying faster than his eyes could track.

While Nick’s acceleration was astonishing, it was only half the story. The force behind the blow was more impressive still.

His haymaker landed like a wrecking ball fired from a rail gun, flattening the monarch’s face as it went rocketing back. Zora’s shadow-wreathed body bounced like a stone skipping across still water, smashing a series of benches before striking the library’s wall with an impact that resounded across the deserted metropolis.

As his Concept roared its defiance, Nick followed suit, trailing in the monarch’s wake in a blinding burst of speed. When the battle was over, he would recognize the absurdity of attacking the dreadbeast barehanded, but in his altered state, he didn’t give it a second thought, sheathing Blightbane so he could whale on the mothman with both hands at once.

He wasn’t a trained pugilist, but his opponent wasn’t either. His fury-fueled assault broke through its defenses, leaving Zora unable to call upon its magic as his fists and feet landed in a flurry, overwhelmed by the lightning-fast onslaught.

He snapped a roundhouse kick that sent it spinning, swept its legs out from under it, then landed an uppercut that cracked the creature’s carapace and launched it into the air. Nick sprang from the ground, overtaking the monarch in a flash. He conjured a platform to stabilize his position before bringing his fists together and driving the dreadbeast down hard enough to split the paving stones.

He kicked off the rim of the barrier and sank his heels into the mothman’s body, splattering ichor across the stonework. He grabbed Zora’s feet and whirled like a dervish, demolishing lesser structures before the Death of Dreams collided with the library hard enough to vibrate his bones.

By now, Arctic Clarity had come back online, cooling Nick’s Concept-fueled fury and letting his rational mind govern once more.
