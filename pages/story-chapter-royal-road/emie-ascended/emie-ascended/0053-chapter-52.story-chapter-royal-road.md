I ignored the chime indicating I’d received a message as I finished the latest layer of enchantment on the pendant. This was my third attempt at a fairly basic reactive barrier artifact – well, fairly basic compared to the types of items I used to be able to produce.

It should have been relatively easy to create such an enchantment considering I had a skill that did something quite similar, but reality didn’t always work that way.

The failure point in both of my previous attempts had been with the ‘reactive’ part of the enchantment. Though I had a solid understanding of what I wanted, I couldn’t get the enchantment to finalize, even though the item should be high enough quality to sustain such an enchantment.

This time, I was breaking down the concept, basically identifying every potential trigger that should cause the artifact to activate. It was time-consuming and slow since each potential trigger required a separate layer of enchantment.

The current set of instructions successfully fused to the pendant, causing me to relax slightly. The artifact still wasn’t complete, but I was at a point where I could take a break.

Setting my enchanting focus aside, I grabbed my communicator and pulled up the message.

[Porter Headquarters: Are you available to open a portal between Bloom and Sorenguard in two hours? Sorry for the late notice. The assigned Porter got caught up in a demon attack and won’t be able to make it for the transfer.]

I glanced at the clock. It was already 8 p.m.

Since neither Heaven nor her partner Norm had access to the Portal skill, the scheduled Porter must be someone that I hadn’t met. Not that it mattered.

Though I was only scheduled to manage portals one day per month, I’d agreed to make myself available for emergencies like this, so I wasn’t all that surprised by the last-minute request.

It wasn’t even all that last-minute, considering I had nearly two whole hours until the transfer was scheduled.

I sent a message back in agreement. The money for portal transfers between major cities was not something that I could easily dismiss. And given that both locations had Ascender Dungeons, it was entirely possible that one or more of the travelers had reached the fourth evolution.

That meant the fee associated with managing their travel would be significant. Like, more than I’d likely make the entire time I was bronze rank if I weren’t a Porter. I’d be crazy to pass up the offer.

It was also why I’d been so willing to agree to adding more portal transfers when Sasha had asked.

Setting the communicator aside, I looked at the pendant on my workbench. I wanted to finish it, but my mental state had already shifted away from what was necessary to complete such a delicate enchantment.

I made a few notes to document where I was on the project before sending it into storage and removing one of the simple rings I’d purchased from a local smith.

It was made from a regional alloy that wasn’t too expensive and took well to enchantments. Visually, it looked like polished silver but without the tendency to tarnish.

Since I had a limited amount of time, I decided to spend it crafting a few storage artifacts. Though they might seem more difficult and complex than a simple reactive barrier, the truth was quite the opposite.

At least, that was the case for me.

It was easy for me to create a dimensional space where time did not pass. It was also a simple matter to anchor said space to an item and incorporate a function that allowed the user to store and retrieve items.

And that was really all there was to it unless I wanted to link some kind of essence or genetic lock for added security.

Though each instruction was an individual ‘layer’ in the enchantment, I was familiar enough with each step that the entire process took less time than trying to embed a single layer of instruction for the barrier pendant.

I finished five rings before calling it a night and putting everything away. That left thirty minutes of playtime with Aerie and Stephen before I’d need to change into something more appropriate for that kind of work.

Appearances were pretty important for Porters.

“Did you get it to work?” Stephen asked when I appeared in the living room of our shared apartment.

I did a double-take when I saw my normally stoic and somewhat reserved boyfriend sitting on the couch reading an actual book with a small, white and gray kit perched on top of his head. Aerie’s tiny, prehensile paws clutched tufts of the archer’s hair, giving him a wild, unkempt look, which contrasted sharply with his unbothered expression.

Suppressing a chuckle, I said, “No. I got distracted by a transfer request and set it aside so I didn’t mess up my latest attempt.”

“Oh. Did something happen?”

“The Porter assigned got caught up in a demon attack.”

Stephen frowned. “I hope they’re okay.”

“I’m sure they are, but you know the rules. If a demon attack happens while they’re there, they have to stay until the end.”

“Well, at least the compensation will be significant. I’m assuming it’s a portal request, not a teleportation request,” he asked, raising his brow.

“You would be correct.”

I reached over and extracted Aerie from Stephen’s hair. It took a few seconds to untangle her claws from how she’d nestled in.

The small kit grasped my fingers tightly and wrapped her long tail around my wrist. Though she had grown in the weeks since she came home with me, she was still tiny. The weight of her in my palm was barely worth mentioning.

Stephen combed through his hair with his fingers once she was removed, saying, “I should probably get a haircut soon. It’s so much easier to deal with when it’s short.”

When we’d met, Stephen’s hair had been long. I hadn’t thought much of it since I was pretty used to seeing men with long hair by that point, but he’d decided to start cutting it shortly after he’d joined our party.

I’d made a random comment about how men in my old world usually wore their hair short, and he’d interpreted the comment to mean that I preferred men with short hair. We hadn’t even started seeing each other at that point, but his action had made it abundantly clear that he wanted to impress me.

It was sweet, and I was touched that he’d changed his look just because he thought I might be more attracted to him if he did. I made sure to tell him afterward that I’d gotten used to seeing men with long hair and had nothing against it, but he’d insisted that he actually preferred it short.

Since I really didn’t care either way, I didn’t argue. But I made it a point not to make such comments anymore. I didn’t want Stephen to feel like he needed to change anything about himself to suit my preferences.

That said, his actions were enough to make it clear that he cared about how I viewed him in that way, and it wasn’t much later that we officially became a couple.

Though an entire year had passed, sometimes it felt like we’d only just met. At the same time, it seemed like we’d known each other for far longer.

“Do you want to go to the park for a few minutes?” I asked Aerie.

She couldn’t truly understand me yet, but our bond made it so she understood the intent behind my words. The small kit perked up immediately, excited by the thought of going outside to play.

It didn’t matter much that it was dark out already. Asolades had excellent eyesight, and the park was still fairly well-lit at this time of the evening.

“Are you going to take her with you for the transfer?” Stephen asked.

“Probably not. She’s small enough that nobody would likely care, but I don’t think it’s a great idea to take her along just yet. Maybe when she gets a bit older and we can communicate better.”

Stephen wasn’t showing any signs of standing, so I asked, “Do you want to come along, or are you going to stay here?”

“I’ll stay. I’m kind of into the story I’m reading right now. I hope you don’t mind.”

“Not at all. We won’t be long. I’ll bring Aerie back before heading to the Dungeon Complex to handle the transfer.”

Aerie, as usual, was ecstatic to go to the park. Not just because she got to run around and play, but because other pet owners and those with socialized beast companions often brought their pets and companions to the park to play as well.

The fact that it was late didn’t matter in the slightest since plenty of beasts were nocturnal or semi-nocturnal, and a lot of delvers basically ignored normal day and night schedules.

“Emie!” a familiar voice called out when I appeared near the area of the park where those with small pets tended to congregate.

“Illia! How are you?”

“I’m doing well. How are you and Aerie?” the Tamer asked, immediately focusing in on the small asolade in my hand.

“We’re good,” I said as Aerie slipped out of my grasp and launched herself at the three asolades playing nearby. One was only a bit larger than her, likely the sole remaining sibling from her litter. The others had likely already been sold.

“Good!” Illia replied before smiling down at the excited asolades. After a moment, her smile dampened, and a line appeared between her brows. “Aerie looks a little smaller than she should at this age. Is she not eating well?”

I ducked my head slightly and said, “She eats fine. She’s probably smaller because I keep her in stasis when I can’t be with her. Between work and our time in the dungeon, she might be a little behind her littermates.”

“Ah, yes. That would do it at this age,” the Tamer agreed. “It won’t matter much once they’re older, but with how young she is now…”

“Yeah, it shows. It’s not optimal, but I’d rather her stay a bit younger instead of spending time alone, and I certainly don’t feel comfortable having her join us in the dungeon until she starts gaining levels.”

“Oh no. I totally agree. A lot of beast tamers start far too early with that, in my opinion. It’s honestly best if she doesn’t even start leveling until she’s closer to her adult size.”

We’d had this discussion before Illia had agreed to sell me Aerie, but I understood her desire to bring it up again.

“I have no plans to feed her any cores until she’s at least the equivalent of six months old,” I assured her.

“Good.”

“Did you decide to keep one from her litter?” I asked, motioning toward the smallest of the three asolades in Illia’s group.

Illia shook her head. “No. I thought I’d found her a good home, but things didn’t work out.”

“I’m sorry to hear that.”

Illia shrugged and said, “It’s okay. I’m just glad they brought her back instead of abandoning her somewhere.”

“Did they give a reason?”

“They said she was too destructive,” the Tamer said with a frown. “When I asked for details, it turned out they were leaving her home alone for most of the day without providing anything to entertain her.

“Obviously, she was going to get bored and get into stuff. I made sure everyone who took one of my babies home knew that. They basically locked her in the daughter’s room, then got upset when she knocked over a bunch of fragile collector’s toys the girl had on a shelf.”

Illia shook her head. “She’s an asolade. She climbs and can get onto shelves. I didn’t think that was something that needed to be expressly stated.”

“Aww. The poor thing. She must have been so confused,” I said as I watched the slightly larger kit chase Aerie up a tree before running away when Aerie turned the tables by launching herself toward her like a bullet.

“Probably, but thankfully, they didn’t bond her. She was only meant to be a pet, not a beast companion.” Illia’s eyes lit up slightly, and she tilted her head as she watched the younger kits playing together. “You know, if you’re worried about leaving Aerie alone, it’s actually better for asolades to be in pairs.”

Looking at me with a sly smile, Illia said, “I know you mentioned only wanting one kit, but I’d be willing to sell you Princess for a discount if you’re interested. I only refunded half the money they paid for her.”

I really didn’t want a second asolade since two asolades seemed to get into a lot more trouble than one. But watching the pair play, I couldn’t deny that Aerie seemed happy.

The name would have to go, though.

Shaking my head, I said, “I can’t make that decision right now. I need to talk to my boyfriend about it first since we live together.” Glancing at the time, I added, “I also need to get going. I have a job at 10:00, and it’s 9:50.”

“Oh. I understand. Well, if you change your mind, let me know.”

I nodded and sent a pulse of intent to Aerie before saying, “I’ll talk to Stephen about it when I get home. The job won’t take long. I’ll let you know one way or another tomorrow.”

“I appreciate that. I’m really not trying to pressure you, but seeing how happy Aerie is doing and knowing that you’d keep her in stasis instead of letting her be lonely… well, that’s exactly the kind of companion I’d want for all of my babies.”

“I get it,” I said, sending a stronger message through the bond to the reticent asolade when I got a feeling of rejection back. She did not want to leave.

With an exasperated sigh, I teleported over to the kit and snatched her from the air. “Come on. I need to get you back to Stephen so I can go to work. We’ll play again later.”

Looking at Illia, I said, “I’ll message you. Either way, we should get together soon so Aerie can play. It’s clear she misses her family.”
