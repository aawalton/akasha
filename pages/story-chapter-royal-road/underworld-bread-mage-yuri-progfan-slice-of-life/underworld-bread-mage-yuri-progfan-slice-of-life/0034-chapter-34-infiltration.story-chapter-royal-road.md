The caverns around this depth were illuminated by buds of crystals now decorated upon the walls and ceilings. It was still dark, but bright enough for Marina to see the uneven ground beneath her foot. Reaching for the System device, the blue screen popped into reality. 4:05 pm; it’s been a couple of hours since they arrived. She looked around; no sight of any insectoid creatures, only rocks and stones, though the ever-growing sound of chirps was attempting to drive her to the edge of her sanity.

“Are we close? I feel like we are close.” Marina looked to her right.

“Their outpost is built at the end of this tunnel,” Hecate said. “You will see once we take the final turn.”

“Exciting…” She murmured.

Regardless of her worries, Marina kept to herself as the two of them approached the end. Sparkling reflections of torch fire danced amongst the shadows belonging to that of a humanoid figure. Hecate leaned forward alongside the wall, taking a peek at what was ahead of them.

“They seem to be going about their duties, as they had been before,” Hecate whispered. “You want to take a peek, my dear?”

“Might as well…” Taking a deep breath, Marina took a small step towards the light. In front of her were dozens of bipedal insectoids. They shared many similar traits with the Roach brothers they had encountered before, though their build was much smaller, standing about five feet tall. Some of them were lighting up campfires, and some were cleaning their weapons, but many just sat around idly, taking naps or having a side chat.

Marina found this scene to be rather… unexpected, and almost adorable to a certain extent, especially the ants who were trying to carry boxes wider than the length of their arms.

“You sure these are the same ants you described this morning?” Marina turned around.

“Indeed,” Hecate said. “Do you feel better about yourself now?”

“A little bit…” Marina sighed. “But there are still two thousand of them in there. How do I know where to look?”

“I suppose that is a point I should’ve thought out more thoroughly.” Hecate pondered. “Change of plans…”

Please don’t let me go in alone, please don’t let me go in alone! Marina prayed. “Okay, new plan, let’s hear it.”

“I shall join you in the quest to search for the two bees.” Hecate proposed.

“That’s great, sticking together is always good.” Marina was beyond relieved. Yes! I have never wanted to be useless as much as right now!

“However,” Hecate added. “When we do locate them, you must stay with them until I distract the entire colony and get them to safety. Can you do that, my dear?”

No, I can’t do it, and I don’t want to do it on my own. Marina froze as blood drained from her face. “I-I can certainly try…”

“Good girl, now stay close and don’t wander off.” Walking into the open, Hecate grabbed Marina by the wrist.

And just like Marina, Hecate’s body is now also in a transparent state, unseen by the dozens of critters who stand guard at the upcoming cavern entrance. Though the two were unable to be observed through their cloaks of invisibility, they were, however, still bound to the material world. Any sound or physical contact could very much reveal their existence and ruin their newly designed plan entirely.

Hugging the side walls, Marina held her breath as they approached the first ant soldier. She tiptoed behind Hecate while trying her best to keep up. Surprisingly, these critters were even more adorable up close than when she observed them from a distance; their big, round eyes were quite the opposite of intimidating, which begged Marina to ask: if not a friend, then why friend-shaped?

She was curious, but did not want to stick around and find out.

Looking ahead, Hecate pointed deeper into the caves at a structure dug from the stones and reinforced with crude metallic bars. But there were a couple of big problems standing in their way. The open area ahead was filled with hundreds of ant soldiers with spears in hand, thrusting and swinging at their assigned wooden targets. This was a training field. Past it was another heavily guarded structure, one that seemed to be filled with chests and crates, piled with weapons and armor.

“Hecate, how are we supposed to get through?” Marina whispered directly into Hecate’s ear. “They literally filled this place to the brim; there is no way we don’t bump into at least one.”

“The solution is right above us.” Hecate raised a finger.

“Huh?” Marina tilted her head upwards. “I don’t see anything useful–oh, Hecate… don’t tell me we’re climbing the ceiling.”

“It is a perfectly safe path for us to get from where we are to where we need to be,” Hecate explained as she dashed out of an ant’s way, pulling Marina backwards alongside her. “Don’t worry, my dear, I will make it fast and painless.”

“Flying is one thing, but hanging upside down is another.” Marina was flustered, her whispers much louder than before. “Hecate, I might pass out or throw up or scream. I don’t know if I can make it.”

“Close your eyes, my dear.”

Without wasting another moment on banter, Hecate swiftly wrapped her arms around Marina’s waist. Twisting the tip of her foot, she launched herself onto a nearby cliff, trailed by a wave of dust. Repeating the same sequence of actions, she aimed for the ceiling of the cavern. A soft shockwave of energy burst from her heel, redirecting the two of them upside down as Hecate sank herself into the rocks.

Marina could feel her feet growing colder and her face growing warmer. It was odd and disorienting. The series of maneuvers performed by Hecate caused more vertigo than anything she had experienced in her life, even more than her first time riding a crowded school bus.

“Am I… still… alive?” Marina said, her eyes still closed.

“No, dear, you have already died. That is why you’re here in the first place. How could you forget?” Hecate joked.

“Just hurry up and get to the other side, please, I beg of you,” Marina said. She could feel her stomach churning.

“As you wish, my dear.”

Under the effect of her Technique, Hecate's body became magically adhesive to all surfaces, but with Marina in her arms, she could only tread much more slowly due to the inability to use all her limbs. Any impatient missteps could result in a disaster. But taking a glance at Marina’s painful expression, she decided she needed to hurry. Compared to herself, Marina is only a mere mortal who was born to obey the laws of this world, not to defy them.

“Almost there, my dear.” Hecate comforted to the best of her ability, balancing the cadence of her footsteps.

Marina offered no reply, other than clutching herself closer to Hecate.

Halfway through, Hecate picked up her pace even more, and each of her steps brought her closer to the finish line. At last, she twisted her body before jumping atop what appeared to be the armory.

“My dear, are you alright?” Hecate asked, laying Marina down on the rooftop.

“Yeah… I just… need a minute…” Marina said. “We there?”

“Yes, my dear, we are currently overlooking what I believe to be the dungeon,” Hecate replied.

“Alright,” Marina rubbed her face as she proceeded to sit up. “I think my body is still functioning. Do we just sneak in or what?”

“Well, actually, this is where we part ways, my dear.” Looking down, Hecate pointed at one of the guards. “I will cause a distraction, you must grab the key off that ant.”

“How?” Marina stared. “I have exactly zero martial prowess.”

“Here.” Hecate reached into her satchel. “This will come in handy, I’m sure of it.” She pulled out a wooden club. “I placed my utmost trust in you, my future consort. Don’t let me down now.”

“There is no way you only give me this, Hecate. I—” Marina turned around to find exactly no one. “Godammit, Hecate.”

“...”
