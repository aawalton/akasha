Fear the virtuous monsters. Fear those cruel and brutal, who act in service of evil, yet temper their behaviors with discipline and virtue. Corrupt monsters driven by lust, wrath, and thirst, by the baser instincts that reign over a being, are easily betrayed, lured out of position, and slain at the behest of a wiser foe.

But a virtuous monster will not be met with the same fate. For they hold their own leash, they govern themselves, and for all the darkness spread by their actions, there is a light within themselves, a light contrasted against the dark. For their minds are high, their hearts are true, and though they might enact things beyond all considerations of ethics, something deeper yet guides them. A code, perhaps not to the decency of the world or those around them, but a code toward mastery and horizons higher.

For even the most vicious of demons can take pride in a well-constructed hell shaped by their own hands…

—Legend-Philosopher Melaia Kelhaus

Virtuous Monsters

Their Necromancy spell was pre-shaped to be a concentrated blast. The outer layer of corpses marked the boundary of the explosion, preventing it from expanding wider. This aspect of the spell wasn’t added to contain the collateral damage, but rather to condense the effects on the specified targets.

There was little more to be said about the Deathless' resilience. A targeted Necromancy detonation, even this extreme, would leave his soul scalded, but do little more than that. Such was the assessment provided by Maria’s elite Necromancers, and the mercenaries she'd hired from the Necrotechs concurred. With how Gate Piety used the Deathless as a bomb themselves, striking him with their own Necromantic spells to devastate Compact in their preemptive assault, it was indication enough of what to expect.

As such, the true target was the auxiliaries, and the user of the Dreaming Storm—Legend Uva Mettabon, the other half of the equation guarding Gate Piety. If they could remove her, or even incapacitate her for a time, the offense could push all the way to the Gate itself, and the battles would become pitched rather than an even struggle in the field. The logistical and shaping capabilities of Gate Piety could not be underestimated, and it was more than the Deathless that tilted things in their favor.

Furthermore, there was the Demigod himself, Paragon Adam Arrow—the object of Lord Scorn's ire. If he could be resolved, then the invaders might be able to regain their Awareness and intelligence advantage. So long as his glaring star loomed overhead, everything and everyone below was at risk of being branded by his treacherous senses.

Maria watched through the projection of the Diviner’s table in front of her as the corrosive blast climbed high, consuming Gate Piety’s defenders in a screen of sickly green mana. The same glow settled over her marble-pale skin, her white wisps of hair flowing through the air like a gossamer shawl. A pregnant tension built within the combat intelligence center of Vulketh as Pathbearers commanding the invasion force waited to witness their triumph.

A low laugh slipped out from Belalu as he looked on with his gleaming bright eyes. His body was a mess of intermingling glass shards, the geometries that made up his person fracturing and parting with every passing intersection. He was more jigsaw puzzle than person, but in this moment he maintained a consistent form, anchoring himself in this dimension to savor the moment he thought his fifth son avenged.

Maria wasn’t quite so optimistic.

“I have to admit, Legend Corcusa, I wondered to myself what would make a Bloodline Progenitor of the First Blood embrace and christen some Umbral slave as her desired disciple. A few other Lords of Law judge your ascent as a mistake of lust, but I know Grandmother Ophereus, and she has no taste for girls, not physically. But your mind... You have all my compliments today, dear. Your mind is a devious fucking little thing.” Lord Belalu’s voice was like a thousand nails sliding down the face of a chalkboard, projected into your brainstem. Anyone beneath Master-Tier in Toughness in his presence would go deaf in an instant, and he enjoyed inflicting such harm.

Maria didn't respond, didn't even make eye contact. She saw no point in feeding this glutton of depravity. As far as she was concerned, Lord Belalu of Compact was a non-factor, because his armies were not here. His elite Pathbearers fled and broke at the first sign of trouble, choosing to let others die in their stead. In the end, he was a banker playing at being a warrior and strategist, and was simply present to avenge a personal slight.

A live projection of the battle was cast over dimensions to them from the perspective of a Scout, and Belalu, Scorn, Maria, and a number of Vultegs studied the unfolding scenes from their densely veiled bunker deep under the mana-infused sea of liquid metal making up Vulketh’s oceans. Shadows and Dimensionality shrouded Maria's person, but she wouldn't go undetected for long. Sooner or later, the Gate Lord would spread his Awareness wide, and everything would be revealed. No secrets would hide beneath the light, but by that point she would either be engaging once more or evacuated, depending on the outcome.

“Fourteen fucking million of my Fingerlings dead,” Lord Scorn spat with derision. “Let's hope your little sacrifice play worked, bloodsucker, because fourteen million dead fucking Vultegs is a pretty hefty fee for one cocksucking Demigod.” His visage wasn't manifested, but the waves of his mind and divine aura seeped into the dark ambiance of the intelligence center via his Avatar. Maria didn't bother learning the name of this Vulteg. She was a tall specimen, her head tentacles ridged with Chronomancy-infused jewelry. Likely a time mage of some kind, but soon to be spent and wasted, just like all the others.

Scorn was similarly insufferable compared to Compact's Lords of Law when it came to strategy or personality, but his manpower was ample, and the magic he wielded was immense. His understanding of Biomancy, Pyromancy, Geomancy, and Dimensionality was practically unparalleled. The resources he provided toward this conflict left Maria with no complaints. For all the bio-forged horrors the First Blood could craft, there were thrice their number in Vultegs who yearned to die for their god.

But above even all this, Scorn's true worth resided within his Divinity and his Domain of Isolation.

With it, no one could breach his personal dimension unless he willed it, and for this conflict he specially did so, seeking to return the wounds Adam Arrow and the thieves who stole Gate Theborn had dealt to his ego ten times over. It was thanks to this Domain that the invasion could continue on at all. For every High-Tier Pathbearer who survived from the first two offensives was deemed unusable, their souls burned and turned traitor by Adam’s immolating Awareness.

But every boon he brought came with a drawback in the form of Lord Scorn himself. The god wasn't just miserable to be around—he was an active detriment to any situation that didn't relate to magic, wards, or hiding away and soaking in the dark. His only advice on the battlefield, and the main reason why the Vultegs lost the second offensive altogether, was that he liked to perform a strategy called "charge, charge, and charge some more!" His idea of tactics and planning was simply to amass more people and throw them at the enemy.

It wasn't that Maria didn't understand why he thought like this. How could he not develop such bad habits after creating a world like Vulketh, a world that now hid the High-Tier, commanding Pathbearers leading the offensive? The Vultegs were expendable, down to the last one. There were untold billions of them, constantly hatching from the rivers of burning magic circulating across their planet. They fought and butchered one another for the favor of their patron god. Despite their full sapience, they were more resource than people, as they were volunteered to outside forces like Compact, the First Blood, and Outer Dimensions Beyond in exchange for favors and protections, with everything gained flowing upward toward Lord Scorn himself.

Those same flaws existed within the culture and society he created. The Vultegs refused to respect or heed anyone who wasn't Lord Scorn or a direct commanding officer of his legions. Every last one of the tentacle-faced creatures needed to be bullied into compliance and refused to listen to reason. Worse yet, the peer commanders Maria had to work alongside barely regarded her as sapient in return. Most of them jockeyed for position with each other, abandoning their own forces on the battlefield if only to disgrace a hated rival, even at the cost of ruining an entire operation.

The First Blood had its problems. Maria knew them better than anyone else on Integrated Earth, but even considering them, your standard vampire was practically a generous, communal, and selfless saint compared to the average Vulteg soldier.

Maria kept to her silence and observed the fading Necromancy spell. She didn't speak when there was nothing of substance to provide. Silence was also her weapon against the two fools she was subservient to, and it was subservience that defined her position. Though she was named Field Marshal to the alliance, that was subject to change at any given time depending on the whims and fancies of Scorn and Belalu.

Furthermore, there were two reasons why the First Blood volunteered her to begin with: the stated reason was her intellect and strategic acumen. The unstated part related to her expendability and alienation. Maria Corcusa should never have been embraced. It was done on a whim because of the appreciation of a madwoman. That madwoman was considerably powerful and stood as the single greatest Social expert in all the Abyss, but she was a single woman nonetheless; one Legend against many was an insurmountable problem of arithmetic when it came to politics.

All that placed Maria in a precarious position, but that was the tale of her life. She shouldn't be here. She shouldn't have survived. She shouldn't have been embraced. She shouldn't have made it to Legend. Yet still, she persevered. And she would continue to persevere against the Deathless, the Paragon, the Seeker, and the rest of Gate Piety. She would seize and claim that vaunted, Unique mana core for the glory of her people. With that, and a few favors from Scorn and Belalu, she might just be capable of challenging one of the First-Generation Elders and finally assuming and securing an unassailable place amongst the Eldest.

And after that happened, she would see certain reforms put into action, and certain Bloodlines severed at their source.

“The fuck’s this?” Scorn snarled, his voice shaking the room.

Belalu leaned in, his previous prideful expression lost, and the cohesive nature of his body thrown into disarray. He was a mess of sliding shrapnel once more, a mess of shards piercing and passing through one another. Maria remained stone in expression, in emotion, in focus.

The same quality did not exist within Lord Scorn, who seemed a centimeter away from the mother of all mental breakdowns. “FUCKING CUNT FUCKER BITCH FUCK CUNT BITCHFUCKCUNTCUNTCUNTCUNT FUCK BITCH! WHAT WAS THE POINT OF ALL THAT IF IT DIDN’T DO ANYTHING!?”

“But… how?” Belalu asked, more flabbergasted than enraged. “What is that?”

He was talking about the misty hand that had manifested to guard the Dreaming Storm and the Deathless—the often-rumored manifestation of the Gate Lord’s Domain.

“Another uncovered problem we need to solve,” Maria answered. “I suspect we’re facing a Domain of Protection. And it works on Necromancy as well.” The information was useful, but the issues it presented were troublesome. Each of the Favored Three was monstrous individually, but together, as a single force? “We don’t have the numbers nor force required to take this gate right now.”

Her acknowledgement was so casual Belalu nodded reactively—before he froze and turned his face of trembling glass shards toward her. “I must have misheard, Legend-Assassin.”

She ignored him and sent a psionic command to the commanders of the third offensive. “This is Field Marshall Maria Corcusa. All forces: Retreat and hold action at field-base three. Skirmishers and saboteurs as well. Execute.”

“YOU’RE FUCKING SURRENDERING? TO—TO THAT FUCK! THAT BOW-FUCKING CUNT!” Scorn’s shout hit Maria so hard her nose broke and her left eye burst. Still, she refused to betray herself—not to fear, not to pain.

“No,” she replied, her injuries regenerating in moments—bones clicking back into shape piece by piece and a new eyeball sprouting like a budding flower. “Not surrendering, but we require a recalibration of strategy. Direct assaults are not enough; we haven’t even breached the gateway yet. The mana core itself will be another problem once we get inside. But they have weaknesses—they lack the manpower to fully guard against us, and the conditions within the Gate itself are as volatile as any battlefield according to our sources. We surprised them here today—we need to surprise, escalate, and collapse them internally before the next offensive.”

Maria took a gamble and turned away from Scorn’s Avatar and Belalu. She didn’t mean any insult, but not feeding and comforting the egos of powerful Pathbearers was often misunderstood as an offense all the same. But if she engaged with them and allowed them to push blame and stew in their own misery, her fate would be sealed regardless. “Lord Scorn—I beg your allowance to depart from your dimension. It is time I seek out a potential solution for our problem and create better conditions for our next attempt.”

“Potentially—don’t fucking show me your back, you corpse-sucking whore! Stop and explain yourself! What fucking solution? What next attempt? What are you trying to pull here?”

The air around her went black. A fist formed of mystical gravity clenched her tight and drove the breath out of her body. She didn’t gasp or tense; predators and abusers fed off feedback. She was a stone. Just a stone. Until the moment it was time to see things done. As she was made to face the higher powers in the room, she regarded Belalu and Scorn with a brief nod. “Apologies. I forgot to explain myself in my haste. The orcs, my Lords. The orcs are the solution. The Challenger is the wedge I drive into the Gate Lord’s defense.”

Belalu’s shattered form shivered for a moment before he burst out laughing, the noise wretched enough to almost break Maria's facade. “You—no, this is… The orcs aren’t any kind of solution, Field Marshall. They’re just bad business. I don’t know what new madness was born in the back of your mind, but mixing grayskins in with our troops—who need to be reconstituted again—is just going to end with a massacre. The orcs can’t be trusted, especially with so many of them in league with Adam Arrow. I don’t know what kind of Holy War the Challenger is running, but if one of mine is spotted on the side of the enemy—”

“You are right. The orcs are not to be trusted, but they can be used. And I don’t intend to mix them in with our forces. Instead, I wish to share what we learned about Gate Piety’s behavior in the field and potential weaknesses with the Challenger to see if they can help us solve our problem without any more lives lost on our part.”

Both Scorn and Belalu went silent—and the silence was a sign she had them.

“We can muster even more resources,” she began. “More lives. More High-Tier Pathbearers. Drown them with our own Legends. Scour their lands with Pyromancy and plagues. Eventually, we will be able to overwhelm them. But that does not solve our problem. The Favored Three will gain levels from those battles—the Deathless himself almost certainly will not be defeated permanently, and if we seek to continue this grinding struggle of attrition, they will collapse the mana core and flee into the embrace of the Yellowstone Empire. And we have already spent far too much on this endeavor for the returns to be worthwhile. Even if we take the Quest rewards into account.”

“ADAM FUCKING ARROW IS NOT BETTER THAN ME!” Scorn roared, and his Chronomancer Avatar burst apart into a splatter of gore—even her Legendary-Tier body unable to sustain his wrath.

A furious flame was kindled in the pit of Maria’s stomach. Waste. So much fucking waste. Another Legend lost—thrown away with nothing gained. How did you ever become a god, Scorn? How could the System ever think you worthy of such a privilege?

A smear of shapeless flesh remained where the Vulteg once stood, but more offensive was how Scorn’s presence never faded. He didn’t even need to waste her at all; he was fully capable of manifesting as himself in his own realm. But no. He hid in his tower; he refused to face the light or even his own people in person.

He wasn’t even a proper tyrant in the end, just a coward with a crown.

“He is not,” Maria lied through her teeth. “And he is not worth any more of your anger and effort. I will see this done. We have done enough here today. There is another side to this equation, and I will see them applied. Personally. As an act of redemption for failing to bring us victory. I will seek out the Challenger alone. I will see the orcs unleashed between the cracks and gaps we discovered. And I will see the scales balanced in the end.”

And both of you cut out of the glory as I turn the orcs on you in turn, she added in the confines of her mind.

Belalu and Scorn hesitated once more—but the atmosphere was broken as the former began to laugh. “Why not? Why bloody not? Let the girl try. If she dies, well… The First Blood can’t possibly shame itself in this affair anymore than it already has, yes? But if she succeeds—”

“I will,” Maria said through her teeth.

Belalu began to come into shape for the first time, and she saw him for the man he was rather than the enchanted mess of glass that hid his appearance. Like a pane of glass shattering in reverse, the gleaming pieces came together and promptly transmutated into flesh, revealing a towering elf with a sunlit mane of hair and an alloyed griffon fused into his flesh as a set of living armor. “If she succeeds, then we’ll have our rewards regardless. Let her take another swing, Scorn. What’s the loss? Not ours, right?”

“Yeah,” Scorn grumbled after a second. “Not fucking ours.”

Maria was stone—but it took so much effort not to smirk back at them. Little do you know. Little do you fools know.

But what Lords Scorn and Belalu missed, Valor saw. He saw Legend-Assassin Maria Corcusa for what she was. He saw, and knew what needed to be done.

Just a few meters away, hidden in the darkness, in the depths of Vulketh without the knowledge of its Dimension Lord or highest Legends, He Who Stills Eternity stalked the enemy leadership and stole knowledge where Adam’s gaze couldn’t reliably reach—at least not until the bombs were primed and he was prepared to leave.

But before he made to depart, he added a final assignment for himself: kill Maria Corcusa, for the sentiment she held toward his disciples was a sentiment shared. She needed to be resolved as soon as possible so that Scorn and Belalu could go back to wasting lives and killing their own empires, and to deprive the Challenger of any and all capable allies.

The worst part was, Valor could taste the potential in her: a Vaketh-Insul. Not quite like Shiv, but she shared an emptiness with the Deathless—a will to claw at power in spite of their environment or adversity.

And one didn’t let a monster like that live. Because sooner or later, they would go from being a monster to something far, far greater—and far, far worse.
