Chapter 29: Death's Bride

The street stretched out long and straight in front of us. The same road we'd been dragged down in the cage, running dead center through town, buildings pressed close on either side all the way to the distant gate we'd first entered through.

Genrik and the boss were still frozen down at that far end with a few remaining raiders, maybe three. Between them and us, scattered across the road at odd intervals, lay the raiders Death had already put down. Behind us were the two women raiders who'd taken up positions flanking our group.

Gwyn's hand moved from my back, trailing past my cheek, reaching toward the bundle of red flowers Death still held out.

I stepped backward instead, pulling us both away from him.

"Why are you moving?" Gwyn leaned closer to my ear. Her voice had an edge of being annoyed.

"I don't like you accepting those flowers," I explained based on reasons I myself don't know.

She sighed. "Are you stupid? No, you really are. He's literally your own skill. Which means, technically, I'm accepting these flowers from you."

I turned my head to look at her.

My head was pushed away, but still managed to get a glimpse. Her face had gone a shade redder than usual. Probably the effect of blood loss catching up to her.

I nodded, and moved us both back toward Death.

"Hey! What are you doing?!" One of the raiders swung her weapon toward us. "Don't move!"

Gwyn's hand stopped mid-reach. "Say, would I still be alive if I actually took those flowers?"

"Of course," Death answered, without hesitation.

"I mean, she'll obviously shoot me the second I do." Gwyn tilted her head toward the woman's raised weapon.

Death didn't answer imidiately this time.

I looked at the weapon. Then at Gwyn. Then at the woman again, her aim steady, her finger resting against whatever mechanism made the thunder happen.

I understood the problem and turned to put myself between Gwyn and the weapon instead.

"I told you not to move!" the woman raider shouted.

The crack of thunder hit me square in the chest. It did nothing at all, beyond opening one more hole in a robe that had already given up most of its structural integrity days ago.

"You too?!" The woman's eyes went wide, weapon still smoking faintly at the end.

Gwyn used the opening to snatch the flowers straight out of Death's hands.

The moment her fingers closed around the stems, he came apart. He folded back down into shadow, the whole shape of him dissolving and reforming. And instead of drifting away it moved toward her, coiling up around her arms, her shoulders, climbing until it had settled into a long black cloak that swayed gently despite the still night air. The flower petals scattered mid-transformation and reassembled themselves into something new: a crescent moon, deep red, mounted on a slim handle, resting in her open palm.

"Are you okay?" I asked.

"Yeah." She flexed her wounded shoulder experimentally, testing it. "Doesn't hurt anymore." She glanced down at the bandage. "The wound's still there. I can feel it bleeding when I move. I just can't feel the pain of it anymore." She pause. "Oh. New skill." She squinted at whatever notification only she could see. "Death's Bride?"

The second armed woman fired without warning.

"Gwyn!" I turned to put myself in front again, but I was too late. The end of the weapon was already smoking.

Then Gwyn moved behind me. I was glad she was still alive.

She leapt down from my back before I could react, closing the distance to the woman raider in three quick strides, and swung the red crescent moon in a single arc.

The woman went down.

Gwyn stood still. Her eyes and her weapon glowing the same deep red as the blood of the woman on the ground.

The last woman raider remaining at the other side summoned a wild cluster of thunder all at once. "Damn it! I swear I'm quitting this job after this transaction!"

Gwyn's cloak surged forward. A lot of something small and metallic dropped from the cloak.

"I see. So that's the secret behind these weapons of yours." Gwyn raised one hand.

The thunder-summoning device exploded in the woman's hands a heartbeat later.

"Shit, why now?!" She flung the ruined remains of it sideways.

Gwyn was already in front of her. "Since I now know it's metal, I simply made it detonate before it could be used." She drew the crescent blade downward and the last of our woman captors dropped where she stood.

I looked at Gwyn. Somehow, in the space of a few minutes, she'd become something like Death.

Down at the far end of the street, near the gate, our remaining opponents were still occupied with Genrik and their boss.

Gwyn started walking toward them.

I watched her go as something in my chest finally loosened. The closest thing to relief I'd felt since the cage door first closed on us… until the wall to our left exploded inward.

Stone and timber sprayed across the road, and through the new gap came something that hadn't existed a moment ago. A creature made entirely of darkness with a head with only one feature: a month with too many sharp teeth. The same creature I'd seen before except a hundred times the size, taller than the wall it had just come through, taller than the buildings on either side of us.

A creature of the abyss.

Gwyn stopped walking. She turned to face it. "Raffea, do you think your light could beat that?"

My hand found my scarf. I still remembered Dad's instructions. Cover it. Always.

But I needed to break that promise now.

I pulled it free in one motion. The area illuminated.

The giant creature screeched and staggered backward several steps.

"Don't run, you idiot!" someone shouted from above it.

I looked up. There was a man riding the creature's head. His robes tattered, posture too relaxed for someone perched on something that size. He somehow looked familiar…

"Hey!" He squinted down at me. "Your neck — don't tell me. You're that giraffe?"

I didn't answer.

"Let's find out who you really are, then." He looked down at the creature beneath him. "Flatten her."

One enormous hand rose and came down toward me like it was swatting a fly.

I raised both arms to cover my head.

I anticipated an impact. The impact didn't land.

I lowered my arms. Gwyn stood in front of me and the creature had one hand fewer.

Something massive crashed down behind me. I turned. The severed hand had hit the ground and come apart into dozens of smaller creatures. They scattered, tearing through doors and sometimes walls. They dragged screaming humans out of their homes. Several of them peeled off, angling toward us.

Gwyn's blade moved in a wide arc.

Three of the smaller creatures met it head-on, and they split down the middle. But both halves reformed instantly, smaller now, but whole again, and lunged at her from six directions at once.

She spun, blade trailing red light behind it, catching all in the same motion. They split again. Twelve creatures, each smaller than the last, each still coming.

"Oh, come on." Gwyn's voice had lost its earlier calm.

She cut again. And again, faster now. The crescent blade was a continuous arc of motion, each strike answered by another division, another handful of smaller shapes peeling away from whatever remained.

I watched the numbers climb before they finally began to fall as black stones clattered on the ground.

By the time the last of them evaporated, Gwyn was breathing hard, shoulders rising and falling. "This is exhausting."

In front of us near the gate, the remaining raiders summoned thunder on the creatures. Thunder cracked uselessly against them. Temporary holes appeared and sealing shut instantly. "What the fuck are these things?!"

Genrik released the boss from his grip and blended back into the street.

The man above the giant laughed. "You mortals really are entertaining. Good way to burn off some steam before I get my revenge on the other gods."

A crack of thunder answered him. The boss, finally free, aimed everything he had at the man riding the creature's head.

"Shit. Why is today filled with monsters?"

The man glanced down at him. "Interesting little toy you've got there, mortal. Shame I happen to be a god."

He pointed one bored finger, and a cluster of smaller creatures peeled away from the giant's body. They jumped on the boss from every direction.

"Fuck!" He kept summoning, thunder cracking again and again with no effect at all. "Pusher you bastard! I swear I'll raise the price when I survive this!"

His three remaining men bolted, abandoning him where he stood.

Beside me, Gwyn's cloak dispersed, the crescent blade dissolving back into scattered petals, and she herself dropped. She clutched her wounded shoulder, face twisting with pain.

"Gwyn!" I ran to her.

She grabbed a fistful of my robe and pulled me down toward her. Her breath came fast and shallow. "Spirit energy… Give me spirit energy."

I extended my hand, drawing the smallest amount I could manage from my neck.

"More," she screamed, "I need more than that."

"But—"

Before I could finish, she bit down on my neck.
