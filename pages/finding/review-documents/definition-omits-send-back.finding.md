# Claim

The Definition of `review-documents` names one of the two things its last stage does. It reads "dispatching a reading against every document owed one, and filing what returns." Stage 3 has two acts: filing what a reviewer could not settle, and sending back to the reviewer whatever an instrument would have settled. The second is absent from the line, and it is the one that keeps unfinished work out of a lead's queue.

# Evidence

Raised by the review-instructions seat on `domains/tasks/archivist/review-documents.md`, which left it standing because Every Changed Line reserves a Definition to Alan.

I verified the Definition and stage 3 read as described, on the document as it stands after that reading.

Worth noting against it: across 29 subjects in this pass the send-back arm has fired zero times — every fork a reviewer raised rested on judgment rather than on something an instrument would have settled. So the omitted act is the rarer of the two in practice, which is an argument for the line as written rather than against it.

Not measured: whether the arm is rare because forks genuinely rest on judgment, or because reviewers classify a settleable question as a fork when nothing sends it back.
