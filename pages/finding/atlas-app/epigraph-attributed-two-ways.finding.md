# Claim

Two memory documents rest on a sentence they attribute to Alan and both state it survives nowhere live. It survives: the same sentence stands in live tracked code, in the persona Atlas's own first-person voice. So the standing keep and the standing finding that quote it are each wrong about the one fact they were written to protect against, and whichever surface is misattributing, promoting the keep would write a standard into instruction under Alan's name that a persona may be the one saying.

# Evidence

Measured 2026-08-08, first-hand.

The sentence is *"A place isn't real to me until I've stood in it."*

`~/memory/findings/atlas-app/visited-bar-unstated.md:17` calls it "Alan's epigraph in a quarantined package document queued for removal" and says "The one statement of the standard anywhere is" that epigraph. `dirty/maybe-keep/skills/atlas-app/rulings-composed.md` calls it "Alan's own epigraph", names `dirty/code/packages-alanwalton-atlas-web-claude.md:12` as the only other copy, and warns that "relying on it loses the standard with the sweep".

That package document was emptied and removed tonight, so on both accounts the standard should now be gone. It is not. `packages/alanwalton/personas/core/src/persona-specs/atlas.persona.ts` is tracked and live, and line 16 reads: *"My atlas is written sole-first — a place isn't real to me until I've stood in it."* First person, in Atlas's voice, inside her voice passage — not quoted, not attributed to anyone.

Dating does not settle it. `git log -S` puts the sentence in the persona spec on 2026-08-06 and in the package document by 2026-08-03 — consistent with Alan saying it and the voice borrowing it, and equally consistent with a README attributing to him a line already hers.

Two things follow. The narrower is that both documents' shared premise is false today, and each was filed to survive a sweep it did not need to survive.

The wider is the attribution. A claim about Alan he has not made is never written, and the keep proposes promoting this as the standard that earns a `Visited` flag — where a persona's voice line reads exactly like a ruling of his once it is out of her passage and into a heading. A seat reported tonight that a quote attributed to the persona astra carries a non-zero risk of not being hers, an agent acting as her having once written an unmarked block into one of her files. The same drift, running the other way.

Only Alan can settle it. Neither repository records where the sentence came from.
