# Claim

The reasoning that decides a Departure has nowhere to stand once its entry reaches Design. Two entries landed today whose reason Alan gave while wording them and which no section will hold: write-time refusal of a bad comment on `domains/code-comment.md`, and a formatting check that refuses rather than rewrites on `domains/formatting.md`. Each is a Departure a reader could undo while reading the entry correctly, because what makes it stick is the reason rather than the statement.

# Evidence

Both entries stand in Intent today, and both reasons reached me in the message that announced them rather than from either document. On formatting: the check must refuse so that its firing means something bypassed the gate, and a check that silently fixes what it finds can never carry that signal. On code-comment: the point of refusal is the write itself rather than the merge queue, which is what rewording the entry to "written" rather than "landed" was for.

`domains/domain-intent.md` holds that an entry names a state and never a reason for it. `domains/domain-design.md` holds that a Design entry carries no reason for itself. An entry rewritten from the first section into the second therefore passes through no section that takes the reason, and the rewrite is the moment the reason is last in anyone's hands.

`pages/finding/domain-design/design-entries-carrying-reasons.finding.md` records 11 Design entries elsewhere carrying a because-clause against that bar. Whether those breaches are this same pressure surfacing was not judged here.

Not measured: whether a principle or rule on the same domain could carry the reasoning instead, and whether any reader has in fact undone a departure for want of it. Both cases here are same-day and neither has reached Design yet, so nothing has been lost so far — the claim is about where the reason goes when they do.
