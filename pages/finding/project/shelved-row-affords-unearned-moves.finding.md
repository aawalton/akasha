# Claim

Absorbing a project row, closing it into another, and dispatching it are each one command from a row that has been through no stage at all. The move lands, the row afterwards looks worked, and what is missing is the evidence the skipped stage would have produced — which is the one thing nothing downstream can see. Nothing in the corpus says an affordance on a row is not a permission.

# Evidence

The judgment, in the words of the lead who wrote it. `dirty/skills/narrative-abstraction/rulings.md`, emptied 2026-08-07: "None has been through exploration, so none may be absorbed, closed into another project, or dispatched on the strength of its own evidence. All three moves are available from the row alone, and none of them is a substitute for the exploration that has not run."

A live document pulls the other way, which is why this is not obvious. `domains/tasks/lead/define-project.md:38`: "The shape of the project set is yours to change for as long as it is undispatched. Combine, split, sequence and close as freely as the work needs. Two decisions stay Alan's: closing a substantive project he asked for … and parking one at `someday_maybe`, which is his to ask for." That gates what goes ON the shelf and leaves what comes OFF it to the same free hand. A shelved capture is the least-examined row in the set, so the freest move in the estate runs on the thinnest evidence.
