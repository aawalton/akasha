# Claim

"Both read as ordinary projects from inside either one" stands verbatim on both parent tasks and one careful reader could not settle what it means. A second reader kept the bullet on grounds that never reach the sentence.

# Evidence

Measured 2026-08-06.

`domains/tasks/projects/build-parent-commit.md:19` and `domains/tasks/projects/build-parent-deploy.md:19` carry the same bullet verbatim: "**Name** what two children would settle differently, and any gap your lead did not already name — onto a project where it is some child's, onto the tree's `obligations` where it is nobody's. Both read as ordinary projects from inside either one."

The reading of `build-parent-commit.md` reported it could not settle the last sentence, and declined to rewrite it: "rewriting a sentence I have not understood lands a claim nobody made." Its best attempt was that a gap recorded as a project and one recorded as an obligation both reach a child as ordinary project work, so the choice costs the child nothing — offered without confidence.

The reading of `build-parent-deploy.md`, run separately and later, returned a KEEP on the same bullet. Its stated grounds, from its per-slice report: "`obligations` is real: `ops project move-to --help` documents an obligation gate firing at `awaiting_manager_claim`, at `awaiting_lead_verification` and at the terminal close, over a row property whose holders are `manager` or `#<seq>`. The slice's routing — onto a project where it is some child's, onto `obligations` where it is nobody's — matches what that gate can hold."

That justifies the ROUTING and says nothing about the trailing sentence. So the second reading is silence on the disputed clause rather than an explanation of it.

What "both" refers to is the open question: the two destinations, or the two kinds of gap. "From inside either one" admits the same ambiguity.

Filed rather than repaired: nothing establishes the intended sense, and a repair would land a claim neither reader could source. It is one horizontal change either way, both sites moving together.
