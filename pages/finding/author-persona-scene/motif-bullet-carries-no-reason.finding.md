# Claim

One child bullet — "Plant three or four recurrences of each motif and reverse the last" — carries no reason clause where the task schema's own child comment expects one, and the reading reports it is the only such bullet of twenty-five.

# Evidence

Measured 2026-08-06 on `domains/tasks/scenewright/author-persona-scene.md`, slice 3.10.

The bullet states the act — plant three or four recurrences of each motif, reverse the last — and stops. Every other child bullet in the document pairs its act with why the act is what it is, which is the shape `tools/document/schemas/task.ts` describes for a child in its own comment.

The count of twenty-five children with this being the only bare one is the reading's measurement. I did not recount it; the shape of the bullet itself I did verify.

Why it was not repaired, in the reading's words: writing the missing clause would be inventing the rationale. The reason a motif wants three or four recurrences rather than two or five, and why the last should reverse, is craft knowledge the document does not carry anywhere else, so a seat supplying it would be authoring a claim and presenting it as a repair.

What a bare bullet costs, and why this is worth a look rather than a shrug: an instruction with no reason is obeyed where it is recognised and not transferred anywhere else. A writer meeting a case the rule half fits has nothing to reason from.

Weighing the other way: `domains/agent-harness.md` carries Cut The Obvious, and where a reason would be one the reader supplies unaided, the bare form is correct and the schema's expectation is the thing that is too strong.
