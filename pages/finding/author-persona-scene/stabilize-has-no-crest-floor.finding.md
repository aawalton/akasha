# Claim

`stabilize` constrains the crest with the word "modest" and no number, while `pleasure` gives it 15 per cent. A writer under `stabilize` can satisfy that and still land the two-movement crest the next bullet calls skipped.

# Evidence

Measured 2026-08-06 on `domains/tasks/scenewright/author-persona-scene.md`.

Line 23 — "`stabilize` runs the descent no shorter than the climb and keeps the crest modest; `pleasure` runs roughly climb 55 · crest 15 · descent 30, its descent still real rather than an epilogue."

Line 24 — "**Slow** the crest more than the climb. The pull is to accelerate into it and out the other side because the fast gear is sitting right there, and a crest compressed into two movements reads as skipped however hard it hits."

So one of the two weightings is quantified and the other is not. Under `pleasure`, 15 per cent of roughly 25 movements is about four, which clears the two-movement failure by construction. Under `stabilize`, "modest" is bounded from above and not from below, so a writer can obey line 23 and violate line 24 without the two lines ever appearing to disagree.

Both readings of "modest" are available in the text and nothing picks between them: modest relative to `pleasure`'s 15, or modest in absolute movements.

Filed rather than repaired: the floor is a number, and choosing it is a judgment about how the scene should feel under that weighting rather than anything an instrument settles.

Related: `pages/finding/author-persona-scene/stabilize-and-pleasure-undeclared.finding.md` — the same two words are also used as terms of art the corpus never declares, so what `stabilize` even IS remains open, and a floor cannot be set before that is.
