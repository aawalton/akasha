# Claim

The instruction corpus has no settled spelling register: British forms dominate, but `organized` and `uncolored` stand on perimeter surfaces, so nothing can be appealed to when a persona's register is in question.

# Evidence

Two American spellings stand on the perimeter, verified at their lines:

`domains/code-quality.md` line 11 — "how an estate of code is organized and structured".

`folders/design-system.md` line 29 — "Prefer the uncolored expression of a value unless the color names a semantic category."

The surrounding corpus is otherwise British throughout, and Alan writes British.

This surfaced from a persona review rather than as a spelling sweep. `personas/lali.md` says "the math" in her Conduct while her Definition and her domain slug both say "mathematics", and the reviewer went looking for an estate convention to settle whether that was register or drift. It found none to appeal to, and these two lines are why.

The persona case may well be deliberate — a spoken "the math" against a written "mathematics" is a register difference rather than an inconsistency, and that is the lead's call on her voice. The corpus case is separate and is what this records.

`global` binds Ubiquitous Naming: the same name for a concept in code, data, interface and prose alike, and a second spelling reads as a second thing. That rule is about names rather than orthography, so it does not decide these two lines — but it is the nearest thing on the perimeter, and it points the same way.

I have not swept the corpus for further instances. Two are enough to establish that a reviewer looking for a convention will not find one, which is the claim.
