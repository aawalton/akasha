# Claim

`review-initiative` tells a seat to offer the whole of its report so it can be ruled on in one reply, while One At A Time and `define-initiative` both require Alan to be given one item at a time.

# Evidence

Raised by a reader on `domains/tasks/lead/define-initiative.md` during the plain-language sweep, while checking that document against its pair.

`domains/tasks/lead/review-initiative.md:29` — "**Offer** what you would do, so the whole of it can be ruled on in one reply."

Against it:

- `domains/persona.md`, One At A Time — "Give Alan one item at a time, in a chunk he can finish." Its warrant says a long block is left partway, and several items in one pass are answered as one.
- `domains/tasks/lead/define-initiative.md:25` — "**Take** Alan's ruling on each objective before drafting the next." Its warrant names bundling as the failure: the objective he would have cut is approved on the strength of the others.

The two documents are a pair, swept together, and a seat holding both feels the pull. They may be reconcilable — review-initiative is about a set of proposed interventions, define-initiative about a set of objectives being drafted, and the units are not the same. But One At A Time does not turn on the unit. It says several items in one pass are answered as one, which is exactly what "ruled on in one reply" asks for.

The reader made no change, because a plainer wording does not settle which of the two binds. What is owed is a ruling on whether a review report is an exception to One At A Time, and if it is, saying so where the exception lives rather than leaving two documents to disagree.
