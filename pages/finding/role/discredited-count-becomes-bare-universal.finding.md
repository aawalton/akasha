# Claim

When a count in a claim is challenged, deleting the number and falling back on a bare universal — "all of them", "consistently", "every case" — reads as a correction and is the one repair that cannot be checked. It upgrades a falsifiable claim into an unfalsifiable one while presenting as rigor, so nothing downstream can re-count it and nothing reports that the claim got weaker. The repair that keeps the claim checkable is to narrow the quantifier's scope until the number is true of it.

# Evidence

Ruled 2026-07-28. It stands only in `dirty/skills/agent-harness/rulings/measurement.md`, which is quarantined and queued for its own removal, so this is filed to outlive that sweep.

The ruling's own words: "A wrong count is falsifiable and gets re-counted; a bare universal reads like a correction and is unfalsifiable. Deleting the number to escape a challenge upgrades a checkable claim into an uncheckable one and presents as rigor."

Its own author had committed the error being ruled on. The same source records, under a separate ruling, that they had written "~169 checks" into the problem statement of a project about uncounted numbers, that the figure was unbounded and resolved to three populations none of which was 169, and that the three measured populations were on record and should be used instead.

Live state checked 2026-08-07, and nothing carries the act. `domains/instrument.md#Population` — "State the population size where an instrument reports, and fail where it could not look at one" — binds what an INSTRUMENT reports, not what a seat writes in prose under challenge. `domains/role.md` carries Verification and Estimate, neither of which is about repairing a discredited number.

The direction matters and is why this is worth keeping: softening a challenged quantitative claim into a vague one is a move that costs nothing to make, satisfies the challenger, and leaves no trace that the claim stopped being checkable.
