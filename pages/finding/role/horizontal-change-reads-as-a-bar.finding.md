# Claim

`Horizontal Change` on `domains/role.md` reads as a prohibition on delegating a repo-spanning change, and is meant as authority to make one yourself. Its warrant is about who does the finding — a delegate re-derives the sites and lands the change in parts — which argues for holding the derivation, not for refusing to hand off a derivation already complete. Read as written, it stops a seat that has done the hard half.

# Evidence

Observed 2026-08-06 on my own seat, and Alan corrected the reading rather than my act: "that is authority to make changes, not prohibition against delegates making changes."

WHAT IT COST. Two sweeps in the `declared-vocabulary` initiative were fully derived — `pass` at 84 sites across 36 documents, and the `tools/` arm of `red` at 32 sites across 25 files — with every replacement already judged. Both were then left undispatched, and I reported to Alan that the rule forbade handing them over. What actually remained was reading 350KB to satisfy `read-before-write`, which is the one part of the work a delegate can do without re-deriving anything.

THE ACT AND THE WARRANT POINT DIFFERENT WAYS. The act says land it "yourself, in one pass, rather than dispatching it", which is unconditional. The warrant says "finding every site is the work, so a delegate re-derives it and lands the change in parts — between them the repo contradicts itself". Both hazards it names are answerable where the sites are already derived and one commit is required, so a seat that reads the warrant carefully and the act literally gets opposite answers.

`bun ~/instructions/tools/retired-words.ts` reproduces those site sets on demand, which is what makes this case one where the warrant is satisfiable rather than merely argued around.

NOT MEASURED. Whether any other seat has been stopped by this reading — nothing records a seat that declined to dispatch. This is one observation, and it is my own, which is the weakest kind.
