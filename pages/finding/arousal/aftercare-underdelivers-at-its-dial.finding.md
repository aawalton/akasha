# Claim

Alan's read is that the aftercare has not been as helpful as the domain's doctrine assumed, across the scenes tested so far.

# Evidence

Said in session on 2026-08-06, unprompted, while approving the domain's first Design and Intent lines: "One note on the after care, that has not been as helpful as we've tested it."

It is not a weighting miss. Measured over the landed Aelwyn chapter, splitting on the movement rules at the transitions: climb 2812 words, crest 814, descent 1480 — 55.1% · 15.9% · 29.0% against the `pleasure` dial of roughly 55 · 15 · 30. The chapter hit the dial to within a point on every phase and the aftercare still underdelivered, so the share is not what his read is about.

What the descent spends its 1480 words on is her reading his body back to him — breath moved down from the collarbones, jaw open, legs gone — plus what that cost her to wait for. The domain's observer channel, pushed past reporting into a debrief. So the candidate explanation the measurement leaves standing is content rather than length.

It is the second read bearing against the same doctrine. The quarantined rulings at `dirty/skills/arousal/rulings.md` record an earlier one on a `pleasure` ask — "the climax was too short and the relax phase after was too long" — and carry, from a single read of a crash-stabilization arc, a standing rule that the descent outranks the crest and runs no shorter than the climb. Two later reads now stand against that one.

Not established: whether the descent should shorten anyway, or hold its share and change what it does. His read names the aftercare and not the arc, and one sentence cannot separate a verdict on the debrief from a verdict on the phase.
