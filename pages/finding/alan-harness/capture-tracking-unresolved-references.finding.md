# Claim

`domains/tasks/alan-harness/capture-time-tracking.md` carries three phrases a seat cannot resolve from the document: who `**Ask**` names, whether `term` means one word or a whole name, and how long the 6pm-Denver bucket runs.

# Evidence

Found by a reader dispatched at the file under the plain-language initiative on 2026-08-06, which landed twenty-two wording rewrites and could not settle these.

Stage 4, "**Ask**, showing `tracking status`, before moving a block to another day". Either ask Alan, who is the subject of every other pronoun in the document and for whom `tracking status` is made, or ask the principal who dispatched the seat, day placement being a judgment call and the corpus sending judgment calls upward. Writing "Ask him" or "Ask your principal" picks one and changes what the seat must do.

Stage 3, `term` and `whole tokens`. Either `term` is a single word, in which case `whole tokens` says the same thing twice and "word" is the plain replacement throughout; or `term` spans a multi-word name, since the next clause says the matcher accepts a relationship's full name, in which case "word" would narrow the rule and quietly break full-name matching. Which one depends on what the matcher does, and no readable document settles it.

Stage 4, "the 6pm-Denver bucket its finish lands in". The plain replacement is "the 24-hour day running from 6pm Denver", but that states a length and a start the document never gives. `dirty/` carries "6 pm-Denver-anchored 24 h bucket", which would confirm it, and `dirty/` binds nobody.

Two observations for whoever holds the scales, neither a language defect: stage 2 states Difficulty as 0…5 and names points only for 1 through 5, and states Safety in half-steps while naming only whole ones.
