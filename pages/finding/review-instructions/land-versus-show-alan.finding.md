# Claim

The task tells a dispatched reviewer to land what Every Changed Line tells it to show Alan first. `review-instructions` names Cut, Repair, Trim, Rewrite and Land as its acts, and singles out only Add as the one whose judgment-resting part "goes back to the principal rather than into the document". Every Changed Line on `domains/domain.md` covers a domain's Definition, Design, Intent, Principles and Rules with no review exception. A reviewer on a domain document is told both things at once.

# Evidence

Raised by a review-instructions seat on `domains/file-kinds/tests.md`, the fourth reviewer this pass to reach the same pair of rules unprompted. It resolved the conflict conservatively — it landed its one repair in the Tasks section, outside the five that Every Changed Line names, and landed no wording change inside Principles or Rules. It reported that this cost nothing on its own subject but will cost the next run.

I verified both halves firsthand. `domains/tasks/archivist/review-instructions.md` stage 2 lists Cut, Repair, Add, Trim, Rewrite, Write and Land; only the Add step carries "anything resting on judgment goes back to the principal rather than into the document".

This is a different claim from the three already filed under `domain` this pass, which concern the two rules disagreeing with each other. This one is the task disagreeing with the rule.

Not measured: whether any landed review has actually changed a Definition or Rule that Alan did not see. I did not audit the commit history for that.
