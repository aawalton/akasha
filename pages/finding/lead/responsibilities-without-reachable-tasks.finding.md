# Claim

Three of `lead`'s four responsibilities send a lead to work whose tasks this surface does not index, and a lead pinned to an ordinary domain never boots the surface that does.

# Evidence

Responsibilities 1 to 3 on `domains/roles/lead.md` send a lead back to a domain's definitions, its design and intent, and its principles and rules.

The tasks for exactly that work are `domains/tasks/lead/define-definition.md`, `domains/tasks/lead/decide-principle-or-rule.md` and `domains/tasks/lead/define-principle-or-rule.md`. All three carry `domain-owner: domain` and are indexed from `domains/domain.md`'s Tasks section. None is indexed from `lead`.

The evidence that they are out of reach rather than merely elsewhere is a seat's own boot. A seat pinned `--domain lead` embeds the `domain-parents:` chain and nothing else — it omits both `domains/folders/instructions-repo.md` and `domains/domain.md`, which `ops instructions governs` names for that path. So the boot walk is the parent chain rather than the governs set, and a lead pinned to any domain other than `domain` itself never meets those three tasks.

Confirmed independently by the archivist filing this, against its own boot: pinned `--domain domain`, it received the parent chains of its four attributes and not `domains/folders/instructions-repo.md`, which governs the same path.

Not closed by the reading. Tasks on `lead` stands at 8 rungs against a cap of 10, so indexing all three needs a displacement, and what `lead` should index is a decision about what the role is rather than a correction an instrument settles.

Raised by the `review-instructions` reading of `domains/roles/lead.md` on 2026-08-05.
