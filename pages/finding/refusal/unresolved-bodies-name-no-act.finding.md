# Claim

Three refusal bodies hand over a fault and name no act: `persona-champion-unresolved`, `championed-domain-unresolved` and `persona-champion-not-a-persona`. The refusal schema says a refusal is read by someone just stopped who wants the act they can take next. Of four candidate remedies run against the seated fault, two trade this fault for another, and the branch that reads simplest is unsafe on exactly the corpus where it looks most attractive.

# Evidence

Established by the dispatched `review-instructions` seat reading `refusals/persona-champion-unresolved.md` on 2026-08-12, in a nine-case fixture at `/var/tmp/remedy-persona-champion-unresolved.test.ts`: declaring the persona while leaving the pair half-written becomes `persona-champion-unreciprocated`; respelling at a persona who already holds a territory becomes two other refusals; declaring her and naming the domain back clears; dropping the key clears where the descent reaches another persona and becomes `domain-unowned` where it does not.

The middle it did not take on its own authority: name the one branch that always clears, with the drop as the alternative, which is the shape `persona-champion-unreciprocated` already uses. It stopped because that branch means authoring a persona document, and whether that is the right act is not something the corpus can be asked.

The reading of `persona-champion-not-a-persona` on the same day carried the same fork back in the same terms.

Not measured: how often each of these three actually prints.
