# Claim

`domains/retired/estate.md` now stands, and 697 occurrences of the word remain over 233 files under `dirty/` — ungoverned, un-ingested, and outside every sweep that made the entry true. Two of those files are titled `instruction-estate.md` and `estate-instruments.md`, so the retired word is in the names a reader meets before opening anything. Whoever ingests that corpus inherits a retirement its documents predate.

# Evidence

Measured 2026-08-06 by the #18075 seat while sweeping `estate` out of the code repo, and reported to me as an open question rather than swept.

`ops instructions governs` answers "nothing governs it" for those paths, which is what put them outside that project's scope and outside #18047's before it. Non-binding and un-ingested is the right reason to leave them; it is not a reason for nobody to know.

WHY IT MATTERS ONLY NOW. Before tonight there was no retired entry, so the word standing in `dirty/` was simply the word standing. `c39d0b15de` wrote the entry, so `tools/retired-words.ts` has a live claim to measure against — and its own header records that ten of twenty-three entries once claimed a replacement that never happened, which is the failure the entry was deliberately withheld to avoid. The `dirty/` corpus is now the largest population able to reproduce it, and it will reproduce it the moment anyone ingests.

THE TWO FILENAMES ARE THE SHARP END. A body can be rewritten in a sweep; a filename is cited. `packages/infra/checks/src/lib/system-repos.ts:27` already carries a citation of `dirty/code/packages-infra-checks-docs-estate-tree-coverage.md` and was deliberately left standing in the code sweep, because rewriting it would point at no file. So the retired word is already load-bearing across a repo boundary, in exactly one place, and that place is a pointer at `dirty/`.

WHAT I DID NOT MEASURE. I did not verify the 697 or the 233 myself; those are the delivering seat's figures, taken with an instrument it had already found two faults in that night, and its final code-repo counts were right when I re-derived them. I did not read the two named documents to see whether their titles carry the retired sense or the ordinary English one — the word means a manor in at least one live persona portrait, so a title is not evidence of the coinage on its own. I did not establish whether anything plans to ingest `dirty/`, which decides whether this is urgent or merely true.
