# Claim

A Design line on `domains/jargon.md` states an act where Design bars acts. Line 13 reads "A word whose plain sense competes with the one being declared is written plainly instead." `domains/domain-design.md` says a Design section states what the domain is, never what a seat should do, and this line's two neighbours state facts. The passive addresses nobody, so it is borderline rather than plainly outside. "…is not declared" would state the same decision without the act.

# Evidence

Raised by a review-instructions seat on `domains/jargon.md`, which identified the defect and could not land it: every line of that document except its frontmatter sits in a Definition, Design or Intent section, `domains/domain.md` § Every Changed Line covers all three, and nothing in its dispatch recorded a release.

I verified line 13 reads as quoted, and that lines 15 and 21 around it state facts rather than acts.

The reviewer called this the smallest of the three defects it found. No criterion settles it — it reported that the document holds no command, path or count for an instrument to check. Not measured: whether any reader has acted on the line as an instruction.

SECOND READING, 2026-08-07, ON SCALE RATHER THAN WORDING. A later dispatched review reached the same verdict and added what makes it more than cosmetic: this Design section is on every agent's boot. `domains/global.md` names `jargon` under `glossary:`, and `tools/lib/drawn-in.ts` hands a drawn domain's `# Definition`, `# Design` and `# List` verbatim — never its `# Intent`. Since global is the root every seat reads, an act-shaped line reaches every boot, from a Design section on a document that otherwise binds nobody.

I confirmed both halves: `global.md` line 14 lists `jargon`, and `drawn-in.ts` line 78 loops over exactly Definition, Design and List. The second reviewer proposed "A word whose plain sense competes with the one being declared cannot be declared", close to the recast already in the Claim above, and noted the original's second half — write the plain phrase — is already Plain Or Declared's first clause, so nothing is lost.

Still not measured: whether any reader has acted on it.
