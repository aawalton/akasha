# Claim

Three instrument docblocks give Single Authority a warrant close to the inverse of the rule's own. They say a copy is forbidden because divergence is silent; the rule says a copy is the survivable case, because it has an original and a reader meeting both can tell which drifted.

# Evidence

`domains/agent-harness.md` states: "A copy has an original, so a reader meeting both knows which drifted; two binding surfaces have none. Their disagreement is a contradiction nothing adjudicates, not a stale copy."

The three paraphrases:

- `tools/document/schemas/domain.ts:100` — "one because divergence is silent. Her database row carried it a third time until the drift that cost moved"
- `tools/document/schemas/persona.ts:54` — "forbidden because divergence is silent; what admits this one is that refusal, so removing it"
- `tools/checks/domain-edges.ts:38` — "she holds are one statement made twice. A copy is forbidden because divergence is silent, so what"

Read against the rule, "a copy is forbidden because divergence is silent" states the opposite ground: the rule's objection to two binding surfaces is precisely that neither is a copy.

The reviewer did not treat this as a repair. `persona.ts` says in the same breath that its pair is "one statement made twice rather than an original and a copy", so what is loose may be the vocabulary rather than the claim, and `tools/checks/findings-sorted.ts` states it correctly. Whether the rule's warrant sentence should be reworded so readers stop inverting it is the open question.
