# Claim

A delegated survey scoped to a subtree reports no contradictions where contradictions stand, because a contradiction between a subtree and the document above it is visible from neither alone. The clean report is the same shape as a real absence, and the scope that produced it is stated in the brief rather than in the answer, so the reader who holds the brief is the only one who could tell the two apart.

# Evidence

Observed on 2026-08-16 while surveying 137 files under `books:all-about-alan/projects/`, across two threads, `second-passport` (96 files) and `solar-power` (41).

Two Explore delegates each read one subtree, `eu/` and `other/`, and each reported no contradictions found. Contradictions stood in both: three countries are carried live by the synthesis files above those subtrees and struck by the files within them. Neither delegate read the synthesis files, because neither subtree contained one.

The same survey found contradictions firsthand where one reader held both layers. In `solar-power`, `customer-generation.md` records that Provo City Code 12.03.080(5) caps standard residential net metering at 25 kW AC and names the constraint as one to bring back to scope, while `sizing/pv.md`, `sizing/recommendation.md` and `sizing/topology.md` each plan 40 to 41 kW AC. I confirmed those four files myself rather than taking the delegate's word.

Not measured: how many of the 88 country files the two delegates read, whether either would have found the contradiction had its scope included the layer above, and whether any instruction given to them named the layer boundary. Nothing was read of delegate transcripts; the scope claim comes from the brief each was given and from what each reported back.
