# Claim

Cut The Obvious is written about instructions and the reading task applies it to every surface, so read literally on a persona file it deletes the whole portrait.

# Evidence

`domains/agent-harness.md` states Cut The Obvious as "Keep instructions only where Opus 5 would consistently fail to do the right thing without them", with the reason that an instruction exists only to prevent a model failure, and it says the test applies "from a single word up to a whole document".

`tasks/archivist/review-instructions.md` runs a Cut step against whatever subject it is given. This perimeter pass gave it about thirty persona files.

No line of a persona's Conduct or Appearance prevents a model failure. `domains/persona.md` says her file is the sample of her voice, which is a different kind of thing from an instruction. So a reviewer applying the stated test literally to `personas/lali.md` cuts every line of both sections, and the test as written offers nothing to stop it.

What actually happened is that reviewers substituted a test of their own. The seat reading `personas/lali.md` said so outright and unprompted: it read the principle "in the register of the subject", taking a slice to earn its place if it fixes her voice or her portrait in a way nothing else in the corpus does, and to fail if another persona already carries it or a neighbouring clause has already said it. All nine of its keeps rest on that substituted reading, and it flagged that if the test should read differently the whole walk wants redoing rather than patching.

That is the exposure. Roughly thirty persona files were read this pass, each by a separate seat, each of which had to invent a criterion at the same point. Nothing collected what they invented, so the criterion that governed thirty readings is thirty private readings rather than one surface — and the one seat that reported inventing it is evidence about that seat's candour rather than evidence the others did not.

Whether the principle should carry its scope, or the reading task should name a portrait test of its own, is not mine.
