# Claim

A seat distilling a surface commits once per wording change, and every seat bound to that surface is charged a full re-read per commit — so a single editing pass costs its readers as many refusals as it lands edits.

# Evidence

On 2026-08-06 between 09:47 and 09:51 a lead seat pinned to `domains/tasks/lead/change-instructions.md` was refused at least eight times in succession, its shell blocked each time until it re-read all 20 lines. The count is a floor rather than a total: it was still rising while this was being written, and two of the refusals blocked the filing and then the correcting of this finding.

The changes were of this size: a stage heading shortened from "What stands in for verification" to "Verification"; a trailing clause removed from that stage; "which is the door onto this repo" reduced to "the door onto this repo"; two words cut from the definition; the first bullet's sentence reordered twice; and `reviewed-at` moved to the day of the reading. None altered what the task asks a seat to do.

The obligation is per commit rather than per settled state, and nothing in the notice says how much moved. A reversal and a trimmed article arrive in identical words — "a surface your attributes name has just been rewritten, and you had read the body that went" — so the reader cannot price the re-read before paying it.

Whether this is a defect is the open part. Nothing can tell a distillation from a reversal at the moment of the write, and the guarantee the refusal protects is that no seat composes against text that is gone. What is measured here is only the cost.
