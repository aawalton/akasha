# Claim

A glossary entry costs a composed boot nothing. Term definitions reach a reader running `ops instructions read` and are absent from a composed boot entirely, which embeds only the seat's persona, domain, role and task documents. So the intuition that a glossary line is surplus weight on every reader is backwards, and a reviewer trimming one against Parsimony is cutting something that is already free at boot and load-bearing on demand.

# Evidence

Raised by a review-instructions seat on `domains/role.md`, which went into the frontmatter expecting `seat-mode` and `role-responsibilities` to be surplus, since neither term appears in that document's prose, and reversed the verdict on running the machinery. It flagged that the same mistake is available on every domain carrying a glossary.

I verified it from my own boot rather than by running anything. This seat's system prompt carries nine documents — its persona, and the domain, role and task documents with their parents. `domains/global.md` is among them and lists nine glossary terms: principle, rule, persona, domain, role, task, finding, jargon, instruction. Of those, only the ones that are also my seat's attribute documents appear in my prompt. The definitions of `finding`, `jargon`, `instruction`, `principle` and `rule` are absent from it entirely, though `global.md` names all five.

Not measured: what `ops instructions read` does draw in, and whether any reader has actually cut a glossary line on the mistaken reading. Nothing here says a glossary is free of every cost, only that it is free at boot.
