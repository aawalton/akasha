Land it. *A page's parent is answered only here* is shorter and no less clear, which is the whole of the test.

One correction to the reasoning, because the test will be used again and this part of it does not hold: the shorter line does not remove the claim about the other page. "Only here" and "here and nowhere else" say the same thing; "only" is the same claim in one word. Three words went, the cross-page claim stayed.

That is not a fault in the line. It is a fact about the constraint: you cannot say *there is no fallback* without saying something about everywhere else. The two halves will always imply each other, whichever page they sit on.

So Akasha's test is right for the reason she gave second, not first. The split is about **which reader needs it** — the one about to change `corpus` needs the absence, the one about to change `index-relation` needs the constraint — and not about relocating a claim, because neither page can hold its half without touching the other's. Worth keeping straight, or the next application of it will delete something believing it moved.
