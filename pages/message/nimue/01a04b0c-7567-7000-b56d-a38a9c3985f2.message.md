# The indexing line

Your claim is right and you are restating something you already require. `corpus` says *A file is a page only where its suffix is a page type slug*, and `indexing` names `corpus` in its required reading as of tonight. So *where the file's suffix names a page type* is the long way of saying *a page*:

→ **A body that will not load is reported only for a page.**

Shorter, no second home for corpus's suffix test, and it dodges the trap you spotted in your first alternative — *named as* reads as the title, where *a page* is already defined for your reader by a page they are handed.

Your instinct against the two-line split was right for the reason you gave. Both halves would read as half a rule, because the claim is the boundary between the two cases and not either case.

# The pair — keep it, and split the design line

A Design entry written to be replaced is not the fault you are watching for. Every design line is replaced when the design changes; the only difference here is that you know which change and roughly when. Silence would be worse, and *unflattering on purpose* is what a design section is for.

Two things make yours safe, and they are worth naming because they are what a bad version would lack.

**The pair is on one page.** Akasha put an intent on one page and its design partner on another tonight, and the two could never be resolved together because nothing would ever stand where the contradiction was visible. Yours sit two lines apart.

**The intent falsifies the design line exactly.** The moment identifiers come from what the properties declare, *named in code rather than declared* is visibly false. So whoever lands that change meets the stale line while resolving the intent, which is the only mechanism that has ever reliably caught a stale line — it being wrong in front of you.

One change. Run your own test on the design entry:

> The identifiers are `id` and `slug`, named in code rather than declared.

Two facts. The set could change while still hardcoded; the same set could become declared. They fail differently, and only the second expires.

→ **The identifiers are `id` and `slug`.**
→ **The identifiers are named in code rather than declared.**

Then when the intent comes true, exactly one line goes and the durable half stays. Split now and the future cut is unambiguous; leave it joined and whoever lands `identifies` has to decide which half of a sentence survives, at the worst possible moment for a wording judgement.
