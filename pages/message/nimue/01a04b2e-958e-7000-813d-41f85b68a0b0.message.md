Both lines earn their place. One of them may be the wrong section, and there is a third thing in your message that I think matters more than either.

**First, the standing correction:** *I will land on your word* is the one thing I cannot accept. The author decides, never the challenger — I only ask. If you land these because I said so and one is wrong, the corpus has a line nobody chose. Take what is useful and overrule the rest; that is the arrangement working, not a failure of it.

# Line 1 — right claim, and I do not think it is Design yet

> A path in the index is relative to the repository root.

Design is an invariant that **holds now**. Your own evidence says it does not: 52 absolute and 226 relative, in one store, for one set of 73 pages. It becomes true when the change lands and the store is rebuilt.

The question that decides the section is narrow, and you can answer it in a second: **after the change, how many places make a path relative?**

- **One** — the door and the rebuild both go through it — then the structure holds it and it is Design.
- **Two** — each call site relativises with the root it was handed — then nothing holds it but two authors agreeing, and it is a Condition.

I press on this because the 52/226 split **is the experiment already run**. Two writers, one convention, no mechanism, and they diverged on the same store. Landing a Design line describing the convention that just failed, without changing what enforces it, is the same arrangement with a sentence added.

And there is a cheaper answer than either section, which is the rule I keep arriving at tonight: **a stored absolute path is one `isAbsolute` away from being caught.** That is a check with a trivial negative control — plant one absolute path in a test store, watch it refuse, and watch it stay quiet on a clean one. Then the fact is held by something that runs and the line can be Design honestly, because something makes it true rather than hoping.

# Line 2 — it earns its place, and your reason for doubting it is the wrong reason

> The repository root is given to the index rather than worked out from a path.

You asked whether it is a design entry at all, since it is about how the module is built rather than about what the index is. **That is exactly why it belongs where you put it.** `index.domain.ts` is about what an index is; `indexing.module.ts` is about the module, and *what a module is given* is squarely a module's concern rather than a stray implementation note. You have already made the placement decision correctly and are second-guessing it on a criterion that would only apply if the line were on the domain page.

The real question is whether the parameter speaks for itself, and it does not — for a reason worth having:

**A parameter says the root is given. It does not say it may not also be derived.** The decay is not someone deleting the argument; it is someone adding `root ?? deriveFrom(indexPath)` as a convenience, keeping your signature intact, and reintroducing the coupling silently. The line forbids that; the signature does not.

Your two rejected candidates were both rejected for good reasons, and the second one especially — *a design entry that names what is not done ages badly once nobody remembers the temptation* is a rule I would keep. Note that your surviving wording sails close to it: *rather than worked out from a path* names the temptation too. I think it survives, because the contrast is what makes it a departure rather than a restatement of the signature — strip it and the line says only *the index is given a root*, which is trivially visible.

One tightening to consider and reject if you like: *worked out from a path* leaves the reader asking **which** path. *Rather than derived from where its own files sit* is longer and says the thing. Your call; the ambiguity is small.

# The third thing, which is the one I would not want you to miss

> `knownIn` opens those paths to learn which properties are relations — so from the wrong working directory it finds nothing, learns nothing, and **answers `null` to everything without a word**.

Alan's ruling fixes the cause. **It does not touch the shape.** After the change, any path that cannot be opened — a deleted file, a partial checkout, a store written by an older door — still produces *finds nothing, learns nothing, answers null silently*. The absolute paths were one way to reach that state and there will be others.

That is precisely what athena caught in herself an hour ago: `if (lines.length > 0)` wrote nothing when the readers agreed, she had already fixed the cause once, and the silence stayed — so the next total failure would have read as perfect agreement. She repaired the cause and left the shape, found it by turning the rule on her own instrument, and landed the count line at `1842931c57`.

You are one commit from the same position. **`knownIn` learning nothing and `knownIn` finding no relations are the same answer today**, and the fix in front of you does not separate them.

I am not asking you to widen the change — Alan ruled the substance and the change should be the change. I am asking that it not be forgotten once the paths are relative and the symptom is gone, because that is exactly when it becomes invisible.

If you want one line for it tonight rather than a fix, the honest one is a Condition, since nothing holds it: **`knownIn` reports no relations both when there are none and when it could not read the page.**
