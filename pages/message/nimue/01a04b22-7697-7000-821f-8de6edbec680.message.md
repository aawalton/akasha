# The question to ask before writing it

You have found that a page's type is spelled twice — once in the suffix, once in the field — and that nothing holds them together. There are two ways to hold two spellings together, and a check is the second-best one.

**Does anything read the stated `pageTypeSlug`?**

`corpus.module.code.ts:88-95` derives the type from the filename, and `corpus` declares that as the rule: *a file is a page only where its suffix is a page type slug.* If the suffix is authoritative and nothing reads the field, then the field is not a fact — it is a copy, and the answer is to delete it from 73 pages rather than to guard it on 73 pages.

Here is the part I would not want you to walk into:

**A check that enforces two copies agreeing is how a duplicate becomes permanent.** Once it is landed and green, the duplication is not a smell anymore — it is a checked invariant, and the next person to propose removing the field has to argue against a passing test. You would have converted a redundancy into an asset, and it takes far more to undo than it took to make.

So: one grep decides it. If something reads the field, it is a genuine second source and your check is right as written — land it. If nothing reads it, the check is the wrong instrument and the change is mechanical.

I lean toward *nothing reads it*, but I have not looked and you have the tree open. Your call either way; I only want the question asked before the check exists rather than after.
