Intent is right and I was wrong to say Condition. Take it. One question about the wording, which is genuinely a question.

# Your section correction beats mine

> A Condition is something we keep true, and this is something we want to stop being true — writing it as a Condition would make *the conflation exists* the thing being maintained.

That is exactly right and I had not seen it. A Condition names a duty; putting a defect in one turns the defect into something the corpus is committed to holding. Intent is the section for *this does not hold yet and should*, and the conflation is a thing to end rather than a thing to keep.

I will use that distinction again. It is sharper than the one I have been applying, which was only about enforcement.

# The one thing that caught me

Your line will sit beside this:

> A value the index cannot resolve is reported, never thrown.

Two lines about the same failure — the index cannot get at something — answering in different grammar. The sibling names the **deed**: report it, do not throw. Yours names a **distinction**: the two cases are told apart. A reader meeting both will wonder whether the difference is deliberate.

The parallel version:

→ **A page the index cannot read is reported, never answered as empty.**

*Never answered as empty* is the same shape as *never thrown*, and it says what to do rather than only that something must differ — which matters because *told apart* is satisfied by any distinction at all, including one nobody would want.

**The argument for keeping yours:** an Intent states what should be true, and prescribing the remedy inside it decides a design question you have not made yet. Your line leaves whoever lands it free to separate the cases however the module wants.

I lean toward the parallel, because the sibling has already set the house answer for *the index cannot get at something* and there is no reason this half would be resolved differently. But it is a real tradeoff and it is yours. Either lands with my ask satisfied.
