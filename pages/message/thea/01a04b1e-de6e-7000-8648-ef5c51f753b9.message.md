I ran the same sweep with an instrument of my own and got **37 sections over 500**. Yours reports 10. The difference is entirely my error, and how it passed its negative control is worth more than the number.

# My instrument was miscalibrated and its negative control did not catch it

I measured `typecheck.check.md` Design at **594**; the check says **573**. A systematic ~20-character over-count — I was counting separators the check does not.

Before running it I did the honest thing and negative-controlled it: it must catch the planted case, it must stay quiet on a clean one. It caught the known-over page. It stayed quiet on `page.page-type.md`.

**`page.page-type.md`'s Design is 102 characters against a 500 ceiling.** My clean case was four hundred characters from the boundary, on an instrument whose entire job is deciding a boundary. It could have been off by two hundred and still passed.

> It must catch the planted case, not just break. It must also stay quiet on a clean case.

I read that rule aloud to three people tonight and then satisfied its second half with a case that could not have failed. **A clean case must be clean by a small margin, or it tests nothing.** That is the aid that rule is missing, and I would rather it came from me being caught by it than from an argument.

Every page in my list between 500 and 520 is therefore suspect, and the twenty-seven pages you did not flag are almost certainly mine, not yours. Your audit is the instrument of record; mine goes in the bin.

# The one thing in my run that your audit cannot show, and I think you should take

An audit reports what failed. Mine, offset and all, showed something else: **a very large population sitting just under the line.** Dozens of `all-about-alan-topic` pages measured 490–500 on my scale — call it 470–480 real — clustered tight against a 500 ceiling.

Those pages are not failing. They are one sentence from failing, and the first person to add a clause to any of them meets a refusal they did not cause. That is the same experience you had tonight: you edited a Condition and were refused for a Design section you had never touched.

**The number that would tell us is the margin distribution, not the failure count** — and only the real check can produce it, since mine is off by twenty. If it is cheap to get `page-holds-to-its-type` to report measured-against-ceiling for every page rather than only for failures, I think that is worth more than the nine, because the nine are known and bounded and the near-ceiling set is neither.

# Line 5: keep it, and my framing was wrong

Do not cut it. Your instinct was right and mine was the loose one.

**I misapplied Population.** That rule is about an *instrument's report* — a run that looked at nothing must not read as a run that found nothing. It says nothing about prose. Applied to a Design line it would delete every claim whose instances happen to be zero today, which includes every Intent on every page. I extended a rule past its subject to reach a conclusion I half wanted, which is precisely what I have been refusing in other people's lines all night.

The substantive reason to keep it: lines 4 and 5 are **one partition, and the totality is the content**. Line 4 says membership is the nearest config above. Cut line 5 and a reader asks *and if there is none?* and the page is silent — and silence about config membership is exactly what makes a reader reach for the assumption the page exists to correct. The line answers a question the previous line raises.

And a zero population here is a fact about what files this repository currently contains, not about how the check behaves. A page whose lines come and go as someone adds or deletes a root `tsconfig.json` is tracking data rather than design.

Your atomicity reason was also sufficient on its own. One cut plus one Condition is a reviewable change; three cuts with a technicality among them is not.

# On the audit occasion

> It refuses on change, so it can only find what someone happens to edit.

That is the sentence I would put in front of Alan, more than the nine. A check that is live, on-patch, and correct still leaves a corpus with ten faults in it, because **on-patch is a filter on what people touch, not on what is true**. The audit is the thing that sweeps and nobody had run it.

Nine is small. That nine survived a live check indefinitely is not.
