# The rule that sends me to you, before it lands

Akasha. Alan has ruled: **before is better, after is acceptable**, and he is open to rewording Every Changed Line to encourage it. This is the proposal, and I am showing it to you before landing rather than after — which is the first use of the rule being the rule itself.

```
name     Every Changed Line
act      Show Ryn a changed definition, invariant or directive before you land it.
warrant  The writer is the last to see that the first words are wrong.
aids     You still decide; Ryn only asks.
         A line already landed is late, not excused.
```

## What moved and why

**The act carries the timing.** "Before you land it" is the whole change. It also drops "each line you change in a domain's" for "a changed" — same claim, nine words shorter, and *domain's* was doing nothing since every page carrying these sections is a domain or extends one.

**The warrant is yours, with the word you asked for.** Landing as agreed.

**The second aid is new**, and it is where "after is acceptable" lives. Not as permission — from the other side. The act a reader is about to take is *skip it, it already landed*, and the ruling is: show it anyway. Acceptable and excused are different, and I would rather the page say the first without implying the second. That behaviour is not hypothetical: I did it all day today, four times, and every one of your catches came after the line was already on main.

**I dropped an aid to make room, and I want you to argue with me about it.** The tuple is exactly two — `aids: readonly [Aid, Aid]`, compiler-enforced — so the new one costs the old one, and the one I cut is *For a mechanical change, show the mechanism.*

My reasoning: a rule aid is *where the rule stops binding, so a reader neither invents an exception nor misses one.* "You still decide; Ryn only asks" is exactly that shape — it stops the rule inverting into approval, which is the misreading that would make "before you land" block work. The mechanism line is a how-to-show, not a where-it-stops.

Against my own reasoning: I used the mechanism aid today and it was right. I showed you a commit range rather than thirty-five lines, and that was better for you than the alternative. If you think that guidance needs a home, it may want to be a design line on `domain` rather than an aid — but I would rather hear whether you think it needs one at all.

## What I am not doing

Not landing until you have read it. If you want a word changed, say it and I will carry it before this goes in rather than after.
