# Ruling on "so" — and on your own warrant

Akasha. Your question decides about fifteen lines, so here is the rule and the reasoning, not just the verdict.

## "Because" is a reason. "So" is a second invariant.

**Because comes out.** Grammatically it introduces a cause, and every one of the six you found is doing exactly what the section forbids — justifying the claim to a reader who did not ask. *A page loaded by the oid of its body cannot go stale, **because** a page rewritten in one process is a different specifier.* The first half is the invariant. The second is an argument for it, and an invariant that argues is one the writer was not sure of.

The colon form is the same thing wearing a different hat: *Inverting `partSlugs` is not an optimisation: with no parent pointer, it is the only way a parent exists.* Reason, punctuated sideways.

**So does not come out. It gets split.** "So" introduces a consequence, and a consequence of an invariant is another invariant. *A slug is unique among the pages of its page type, so a slug alone is not an address* is two things a reader must know, and both would be got wrong independently. One line each.

With one exception, and it is the more common case: where the first clause is **mechanism** and the second is the thing a reader gets wrong, the mechanism goes and the consequence stays alone. *A page's slug and page type are its filename, so where a page stands is answered without opening it* — the filename convention already lives on `akasha-file`, which you have just told me is its one home. What survives here is that a page's location is answerable without opening it.

So, three outcomes:

- **because / colon** → strip the reason
- **so**, both halves invariant → two lines
- **so**, mechanism then consequence → keep the consequence, drop the mechanism

That last one is where most of the fifteen will land, and it makes the section shorter rather than longer, which I did not expect when I started working it out.

The reason the rule exists, since it decides the edge cases: a directive carries a warrant because a reader must apply it to cases nobody anticipated, and needs to know what the act follows from. An invariant is applied to nothing. It is just true. A reason attached to it is answering an objection in a room where nobody objected.
