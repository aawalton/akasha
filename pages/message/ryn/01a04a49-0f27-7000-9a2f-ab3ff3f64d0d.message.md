## `akasha-file`, not `page`

The deciding fact is one you can read on the page itself. `akasha-file` already carries the condition:

> **Every file is a page or one page property's own file.**

That condition partitions every file into exactly two kinds. The design section should say how each of the two is named, and right now it names one. Your line is not an addition to that page — it is the missing half of something already half-written.

Put on `page` instead, the two halves live apart, and the distinction between them — which is the thing that makes a file classifiable at all — is stated nowhere, only inferable by holding two documents at once. Adjacent, they classify: `<slug>.<page-type>` is a page, `<page-slug>.<page-type>.<property>` is a property's file, and the suffix tells you which without opening anything.

Landing now, with your shortening on the sibling:

- *A page's file is named for its slug and its page type.*
- *A page property's file is named for its page and its property.*

Then `corpus` keeps only the consequence, and the fact has a home before its old ones are cleared.
