Read all eight on disk, with their siblings beside them — `slug`, `required-reading-slugs`, `part-slugs`, `page-property-type` itself, and `conditional-reading.domain.md`. Both commits are as you described them.

Three of the eight I would leave exactly as they are: **domain slug** and **page slug** are a clean pair, and they are what lets `partSlugs` and `requiredReadingSlugs` say what their entries name rather than both being strings. **page type slug** — "the page type a page is" — is right.

Four caught me.

## type — "which kind of value a property holds"

The definition is fine. The name is what caught me. Your own prose says *discriminated union on kind*, and the code says `type`. That is one concept spelled two ways, which is the thing ubiquitous naming exists to stop. And `type` is already spoken for twice over: **akasha type** is "the shape a value must have", and every one of these files has a TypeScript `type` two lines above the property. In `type.page-property-type.ts` the two sit four lines apart.

→ Consider `kind`, holding the definition as it is: *which kind of value a property holds*. Then the prose, the definition and the property all say one word, and nothing collides with akasha type.

## target page type slug — "the page type a relation may name"

"May" reads two ways: permitted (the only one allowed) or possible (it might be this one). You mean the first, and the second is the one a tired reader takes.

→ *the page type this relation's value names.*

## of slug — "the property type a list's entries are"

You were right to be unsure, though not for the reason you gave. An entry is not a property type; it has one. Your own slug family already keeps that straight — **domain slug** is "a slug naming a domain", not "a domain".

→ *the property type of a list's entries.* One word shorter, and the "are" is gone.

On the name: `of-slug` is the only member of the family named for a preposition rather than for what it names. `entry-slug` would let the name and the definition use one word. Machinery is not the complaint — the definition reads clean; the name does not say what it is of.

## conditional reading slugs — "the pages required once a reader judges them relevant"

Two drifts against lines already on disk. `conditional-reading.domain.md` says *agent* where you say *reader*. Its sibling `required-reading-slugs` says *the pages that must be read before an act is allowed* where you say *required*.

→ *the pages that must be read once the agent judges them relevant.* That agrees with the domain above it and with the sibling beside it, and costs one word.

## The design line on page

> A page's module does nothing when loaded but declare its value.

The kind is right — an Absence, carrying no reason, exactly as the section asks.

Two catches on the words. **Module** appears once in the whole section; the line above it says *file* and the line below says *object*. Three words a reader will take as one thing. And "does nothing when loaded but declare" made me read it twice — the negative wraps the positive.

→ *Loading a page's file declares its value and does nothing else.*

If you keep "module" as a real distinction from "file", it needs to be a distinction the section makes somewhere, because right now it is one word appearing once.
