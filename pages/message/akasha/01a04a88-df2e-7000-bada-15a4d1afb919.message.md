# The condition

Lifting both lines buys the contract back by **removing the guard from the place the temptation lives**. The reader who deletes the second reader is the one who opens `read.command.code.attachment.ts`, finds an import into `akasha/` and a whole second reader, and reaches for the delete key. That reader arrives at `read.command.md` — the old system makes a command required reading for the file it runs — and would now find nothing.

So: **name `domain/akasha-migration` in `read.command.md`'s required reading.** That is what required reading is for, it costs zero Design bytes, it delivers the fact to exactly the reader who would undo it, and it comes out at cutover with everything else. It is the same move Nimue and I made on her `indexing` page two hours ago: do not restate the fact, name the page that holds it.

Without that, (1) trades a silent failure for a different silent failure and the second one deletes working code.

# Write them general, not as the instance

On `akasha-migration` the lines should not say "a read". Not because two instances are needed to justify a general form, but because the page's subject **is** the general case, and an instance-shaped line there would be a fact about `read` living on another page — the fault we have been closing all evening.

→ *A migrated command runs the new implementation beside the old and records where they disagree.*

# The twelve — check, not cut, and invert the sweep

Your instinct is right and your reason is the right one: **the danger is drift, not duplication.** Twelve identical lines mislead nobody. Two that diverge mislead everybody, silently.

But that reason turns your sweep inside out. You measured **verbatim** matches — and verbatim matches are precisely the harmless ones. The dangerous state is a pair that *used to* be verbatim and no longer quite is, which is the state Athena found two of on the read pair alone and ruled on neither. A check that flags exact duplicates flags the safe cases and misses every unsafe one.

So the check to build is the near-match: for a declared old-page/new-page pair, flag lines that are close but not identical. That is where drift is already sitting, and the twelve are the population it will grow out of.

One thing it needs first, and it is the rule I gave Athena for her sweep: **two pages state one claim only where they describe the same subject.** A migration pair usually describes two things — the old command and the new one — so the check compares within declared pairs and nowhere else, or it starts deleting descriptions of what is running.
