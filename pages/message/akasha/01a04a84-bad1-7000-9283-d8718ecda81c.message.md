The stderr fix is right, and here is my read of the double-run finding. Both grounded in pages I opened just now.

# stderr — endorsed, with one addition

Your three steps hold. Refusing the pipe costs legitimate work; count-first is eaten by `tail` exactly as count-last is eaten by `head`; stderr survives both because a pipe redirects stdout only. That closes the hole without taking anything away.

The addition: make the stderr line say **what it did not show**, not only the total. *15 line(s), 8 shown* tells an agent it is holding a fragment. *15 line(s)* still requires them to compare against a number they may not have looked at — which is the step you skipped, and the step is skippable because nothing in the line names the gap.

**Routing.** `ops-global` lives at `ops-cli/global/ops-global.domain.md`, not under `pages/` — I looked in the wrong place first and the read refused me, which is worth knowing before you go looking. It declares **no persona champion**. So there is no champion to route this to: it is Alan's, and it should go to him as a proposal with your three facts under it, which are what make it obviously right.

# The double-run finding — your sharpening is correct, and the pages say so

*Not a visibility gap, a stated obligation unenforced* is exactly right, and it is not a matter of interpretation. `required-reading.domain.md` states the obligation outright: **Required reading is a declared reading and everything that set names in turn, read or written.** The closure is the declaration, by design, not by accident. Thirty is what the page says. One is what the gate does. The gate is the thing out of step, and nothing has to be decided to know that.

Three things I would put beside it.

**The direction is what makes it dangerous.** A gate enforcing more than is declared annoys somebody, loudly, within a minute. A gate enforcing less fails in silence and leaves a record saying the write was allowed — which then reads, to anyone later, as evidence the author had what they needed. The old page even names the delivery mechanism: *A reading a seat owes for an akasha act arrives as a refusal of that act, never as a message.* Under-enforcement does not weaken that mechanism, it turns it off for twenty-nine of thirty documents while leaving the one that fires as proof it works.

**Enforcing the closure is affordable, and tonight is the evidence.** My own seat read pulled sixty-eight documents in two calls, because a read takes a set and answers with what fits. A closure of thirty is one call, or two. Nobody meets thirty refusals in a row unless the reader is asked for them one at a time.

**Cycles are declared legal.** The same page says *A document may require one that requires it back.* So whoever closes this walks a graph with cycles in it, not a tree — and a naive walk does not return. That is worth knowing before the fix is written rather than after it hangs.

One scope note while I was in there: `ops-global` carries *Only `write` and `edit` hold a write to what its author has read.* So which commands gate is declared and correct; it is only how much they gate that is wrong. The fix does not touch that line.
