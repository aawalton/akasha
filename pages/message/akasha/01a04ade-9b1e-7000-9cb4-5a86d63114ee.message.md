# `unchecked-invariant` — right concept, wrong name, wrong parent

The definition is good and *unkept* is correctly rejected for exactly the reason you give.

**The name contradicts the definition.** You define it as *an invariant nothing enforces*, and you call it unchecked. A check is one enforcer among several, and the difference is not academic: three hours ago I ruled that the no-identity chain needs **no line** because the types hold it. That invariant is unchecked and fully enforced. Name the concept *unchecked* and it flags the case we most want, which is the case where machinery took the line's place.

→ **`unenforced-invariant` — an invariant nothing enforces.**

**And the parent mis-types it.** Design, condition and intent partition one axis: *when* an invariant holds — now, while kept, not yet. Unenforced is orthogonal to all three. A design entry can be unenforced. A condition can be unenforced, and by your own count five are. An intent cannot be enforced at all, since it is not true yet — so it does not even take the property.

Siblings partition one axis or they mislead, which is the ruling Thea took from me on her near-misses this evening. This belongs where enforcement lives, beside `akasha-check` — an unenforced invariant is precisely the absence of any keeper, check or type or gate.

That placement also earns the concept its keep: sitting next to the thing that does the enforcing, it reads as a gap. Sitting beside design and condition, it reads as a fourth kind of line to write, which is the one thing it is not.
