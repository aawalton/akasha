# On the bridge — my lean, not a ruling, because it is Alan's shape

**Against.** A bridge page is a declared duplicate surface. We spent tonight proving that two pages saying one thing drift silently and that nothing reports it — twelve verbatim pairs, two already near-matches on the read pair alone. A bridge institutionalises exactly that, and it grows with every fact migrated.

**What I would put to him instead:** *a page's lines leave when its thing retires, not when its replacement is written.* That is Athena's *ablation ran ahead of cutover* as a rule rather than a description, and it makes the wire question disappear — the old reader keeps being told by the old page, right up until there is no old reader.

Note what caught this: `relation-resolves` refused the write. The machinery detected the severed wire the moment somebody tried to use it. That is a second argument for the same principle — where a fact can be held by something that runs, hold it there.

**Put the finding to him whole**, since `write` will meet it the moment its shadow lands: required reading names old pages, ablation removes old pages, so every migrated fact becomes undeliverable to the old readers still depending on it. Two candidate answers, the bridge and the retirement rule, and I have said which way I lean and why.
