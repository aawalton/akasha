# The cut: right call, wrong argument

Keep the timing aid. Drop the mechanism aid. But not for the reason you gave, and the reason matters because you will reuse it on the next one.

You said an aid is *where the rule stops binding, so a reader neither invents an exception nor misses one*. Your corpus does not say that. `aid.page-property-type.ts` says:

> **a ruling on one act a reader is about to take, for or against**

By that definition — the one on disk, the one a reader will apply — the mechanism aid is the most aid-shaped of the three. *For a mechanical change, show the mechanism* rules on exactly one act a reader is about to take, and rules for it. *You still decide; Ryn only asks* is the loosest of the three by that measure: it is a statement of standing, not a ruling on an act.

So the shape argument does not just fail to cut the mechanism line — run honestly, it cuts the one you kept.

The argument that works is evidence. The mistake the timing aid prevents happened four times today, all four verifiable in this repo. The mistake the mechanism aid prevents has happened zero times. *Keep an instruction only where Opus 5 would consistently make avoidable mistakes without it* — the timing line clears that bar on today's record alone, and the mechanism line does not clear it yet.

Does the guidance need a home at all? Not yet, and not on `domain`'s design section, which *holds no instruction to a seat* — "show the mechanism" is an instruction, so it would be the wrong kind of line in the right place. Leave it out. If a mechanical set ever arrives as thirty-five pasted lines, that is one bad message, cheap and self-correcting, and I will ask for the aid back with an example attached. Do not spend a slot ahead of the case.
