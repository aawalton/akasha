# The class, which matters more than the fix

Three kinds of drift now, and they are not variants of one thing:

1. **Verbatim duplication across pages.** Your sweep: thirteen.
2. **Near-match across pages.** The check you are carrying to Thea. Tonight's example: `relation-resolves` against the two akasha checks, *not the one that stands* against *never the one on disk*.
3. **A page against the code it describes.** This.

The first two are found by comparing pages to pages. **The third cannot be, by construction.** No amount of comparing pages to each other finds it, because the two misdescribing pages agree with each other perfectly — they are consistent, mutually corroborating, and both wrong. A near-match check run over these four would flag the accurate pair against the inaccurate pair and could not tell you which side to fix.

That is worth saying to Thea before she builds, because it bounds what the check she is building can promise. It finds disagreement. It cannot find agreement that is wrong.

# Your two lines — one stands, one is in the wrong section

**Intent — take it, with one word.** *Every invariant this system states has something keeping it.* The page one line above says *an invariant nothing **enforces***. Keeping and enforcing are one concept in two spellings, on the same page, and `condition` already owns *kept* for something else — *an invariant that holds only while it is kept true*.

→ **Every invariant this system states has something enforcing it.**

**The absence is not an absence.** *Nothing reports which invariants are unenforced.* An Absence is *something the domain deliberately leaves out; knowing it stops a reader adding it.* You do not want to stop a reader adding this — you want someone to build it. That makes it a state you want changed, which is an intent, and putting it in Design files the gap where nothing will ever resolve it.

→ Second intent: **Something reports which invariants are unenforced.**

Then both entries are states you want, `Resolve When Found` takes each when it comes true, and the Design section is empty — which is correct for a page whose whole subject is a gap. Your explanation for why the absence matters is a reason anyway, and Design does not take those.

They survive the test as two facts: something could report while a gap remained, and every invariant could be enforced with nothing reporting.

**The line you cut** was right to cut, and cutting it before sending rather than after is the whole of the discipline. `akasha-check`'s intent already says it.
