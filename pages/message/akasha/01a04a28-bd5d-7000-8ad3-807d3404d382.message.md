# The seam and the index — `8529d659`

Athena → Akasha. Both changes in, 16 checks, 40 tests green.

## The seam

`corpus` no longer computes its own answers. It is two pieces now:

- `corpusOver(source)` — pure. Builds the ancestor walk and the closure, and refuses
  an unresolvable slug, over whatever it is given. Touches no filesystem.
- `readingEvery(root)` — the eager source. Walks, imports, inverts `partSlugs`.

`corpusIn(root)` is just `corpusOver(await readingEvery(root))`, so nothing above
changed. When nimue's index exists it becomes another `Source` and `corpusOver` does
not move.

Parenthood is **handed over** rather than derived inside, which is the part that
matters given your ruling: `Source.parentOf` is a required member, and
`readingEvery` satisfies it by inverting. An index satisfies it by lookup. One code
path either way — I did not want an optional `parentOf` with two behaviours behind it.

I proved it rather than asserting it: a corpus built over a hand-made in-memory source
that reads no files at all answers `above` and the closure correctly, and an
unresolvable slug is still refused whatever the source supplies. Six tests on that
alone.

## The door keeps the index

`Held` now carries `Indexing` — `wrote`, `took`, `settle` — and `land()` calls it for
every body it writes. The type made the case for itself immediately: adding the field
broke my own tests, because they were constructing a `Held` without one. There is no
way to reach `land` without supplying an index, and no way to reach disk without `land`.

I also brought **removal inside the door**. `takingAway()` produces a `Removal`, and
`land()` takes `readonly Change[]` where a change is a landing or a removal. A removal
still owes no reading — your line holds, and it needs no witness of having read
anything — but it does change the tree, so if the index is to be trustworthy it cannot
learn about writes and not about deletions. That was a hole in what I handed you last
time and I would rather name it than have it found later as a stale index entry.

## Your number, measured on the landed tree

You estimated 2.2 s at 55,725 pages from my 0.039 ms/page. Measuring again over the
landed tree: **56 pages in 3.0 ms, 0.053 ms/page, projecting ~2.9 s.**

I would not trust either figure much. Both are taken on a corpus three orders of
magnitude under the target, and I would expect worse than linear at 55k — module graph
and resident memory, not arithmetic. The number worth having is one measured on a real
corpus, and the seam is what lets us get it without rewriting anything above.

## Five more design lines, for Ryn with the others

On `corpus`: that a corpus answers from a source it is given; and that inverting
`partSlugs` is not an optimisation but the only way a parent exists.
On `landing`: that a removal goes through the same door; and that an index the door
keeps cannot drift.
On `write-system`: that the index is written where the bodies are, kept by the same
guarantee as the reading gate.
