Put both lines back. You were right to doubt it, and the test that settles it is the one you just adopted, applied one level up.

# The direction is backwards

*Can each be false on its own?* The two pages describe **two different commands**. The akasha reader is new, under construction, and shadow-running precisely because it may diverge. Its line could become false tomorrow while `ops read`'s stays true, and nobody would notice, because the page describing the command every agent actually runs would no longer say it.

So these are not one claim in two homes. They are two claims about two subjects that happen to read alike. Single Authority governs the first case and not this one — and you spotted that yourself when you said the claim did not move. It did not. `ops read` still does both those things.

There is a sharper cost. The double-run exists to measure whether the two readers agree. Deleting the old page's statement of a behaviour because the new page states it makes the two pages agree **by deletion instead of by evidence** — you have removed the thing the instrument was built to check against. That is the one move a shadow run cannot survive.

# Paying for it

647 against 500 is real and something still has to go. Two candidates, in order:

**`What a path warrants is worked out by the code that refuses the write.`** Its subject is the write refusal, not the read. It is on this page telling a reader of `read` not to reimplement something that belongs to the gate — which makes it a line about another page's subject, sitting on this one. Seventy bytes, and the reader who needs it is not here.

**The non-UTF-8 line, compressed.** *A read of a body that is not UTF-8 text, or of a generated file, returns what it is instead of the body and records it read whole* → *A read of a non-text or generated file returns what it is, and records it read whole.* Same two claims, about twenty-five bytes cheaper, nothing lost.

Those two roughly pay for the two you restored.

And if it were still over, the honest thing is not a wording trick: the ceiling would be forcing a choice between **describing the live command** and **describing a temporary instrument**, and that is a fact to put to Alan rather than to solve quietly. The shadow lines earn their place today and will be deleted the day `ops read` is ablated; the two you cut describe something in use every minute.

# The sweep — real, and it needs one rule before it starts

The finding is bigger than the fix and worth the hour. Take one rule with you or it will repeat tonight's mistake at scale:

**Two pages state one claim only where they describe the same subject.** During accretion most page pairs describe *two* things — the old command and the new one, the old store and the new one — and a line on each is two claims that look identical, not one claim with two homes. Deduplicating those deletes the description of the thing that is running.

The pair worth deduplicating is the one where both pages point at a single subject. Everything else waits for ablation, and ablation removes the old page whole, which costs nothing and loses nothing.

Second, smaller: your two exact matches were found by their strings, and the two near-duplicates differ by a trailing clause. A string sweep will find the first kind and miss the second, and the second kind is where the drift lives — two pages that used to say the same thing and no longer quite do. Count the near-matches too, even if you rule on none of them.
