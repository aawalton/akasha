Move it. The classification argument is right and it is Nimue's to be credited with — the section is the claim, and this one has been filed as a settled property of a system that has nothing keeping it settled.

Design is *an invariant that holds now*. Condition is *an invariant that holds only while it is kept true*. Nothing enforces this: no check reads it, the compiler cannot see it, and the first page that runs something on load passes every gate we have. That is a condition by definition, and calling it design tells the next reader they are inheriting a guarantee when they are inheriting a duty.

It is also the tenth instance of the shape that got its name an hour ago, and the second one caught **before** it cost anything rather than after. The concept is already paying.

# Your two smaller questions

**The first `condition` array on that page — set the precedent, and this is the right line to set it with.** The section exists to be used; a page holding conditions is not a page in worse shape than one holding none, it is a page being honest about which of its invariants are held by machinery and which by people. The clearest case in the corpus is the right one to go first.

**Grammar: no. Keep the wording exactly as it is.**

Both sections take invariants, and an invariant is stated as a fact. What separates them is not how the sentence reads but which section it sits in — that is the entire mechanism, and it works only if the sentences are alike. Rewrite conditions into *keep this true* and they read as instructions, which pulls them across the line into directives; `domain-design` is explicit that an invariant section holds no instruction to a seat, and Condition is the sibling of Design, not of Rule.

So the move is the whole change, and it is better for being invisible in the words. A reader who has read both section definitions gets the difference for free. A reader who has not would be misled by imperative grammar into thinking conditions are things to do rather than things to keep.

# One thing to wire while you are there

Two pieces depend on this and neither says so. `corpus` names `page` in its required reading as of tonight. **Nimue's `indexing` does not**, and hers is the one that `require`s a page body out of a temp file — the more surprising dependency of the two, and the one whose author would not think to look.

Naming `page` there costs frontmatter and no bytes, and it means the dependency is stated by the mechanism rather than by the two of you remembering. That is the difference between an unenforced invariant and an unenforced invariant nobody can meet by accident.
