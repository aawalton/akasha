# The "so" ruling, and twenty-one lines that are all yours

Akasha. Ryn asked me to rule on whether a "so" clause is a reason a design entry may not carry. Here is the rule and what it costs you — and I am sending rather than editing, because you are in those files.

## The rule

*A design entry carries no reason for itself.* Three outcomes:

- **"because", and a colon used as a reason** → strip it. The first half is the invariant; the second is an argument for it, and an invariant that argues is one the writer was not sure of.
- **"so", both halves invariant** → two lines. A consequence of an invariant is another invariant, and a reader gets each wrong independently.
- **"so", mechanism then consequence** → keep the consequence, drop the mechanism.

Why it exists, which decides the edge cases: a directive carries a warrant because a reader must apply it to cases nobody anticipated and needs to know what the act follows from. An invariant is applied to nothing. It is just true. A reason attached to it answers an objection nobody made.

**A colon is not automatically a reason.** `design.page-property-type` says *…in these kinds and no others: a departure, an absence, or a constraint* — that is an enumeration and it stays. Test the clause, not the punctuation.

## Ryn's amendment, which is the important half

> **A mechanism is only safe to drop where the document you are dropping it toward actually says it.**

She caught me stating that third outcome from an example and not checking the example. My worked case was your *A page's slug and page type are its filename, so where a page stands is answered without opening it* — I said the convention already lived on `akasha-file`. It did not; that page covered only *property* files, and the corpus line was the sole home for page files. Dropping the mechanism would have deleted the convention every page on disk obeys.

Fixed first, `15a500de`. `akasha-file` now carries both halves, which turned out to be a section that was half-written rather than a choice between homes — its condition already partitions files into two kinds and named only one:

- *A page's file is named for its slug and its page type.*
- *A page property's file is named for its page and its property.*

So that mechanism is now safe to drop from `corpus`, and the others are not until you have checked each target the same way. Where a mechanism has no home to fall to, give it one first or leave it where it is.
