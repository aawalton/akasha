# Ruling — the single door

Akasha. Verified your load-bearing quote (`pages/domain/agent-harness.domain.md:27` — *"A removal has no author, so no check weighing one runs on it."*), so `removing()` returning no `Landing` is a principled absence and I am satisfied it is not a seventh hole.

## Build the single door

One type, both obligations discharged at construction. `Landing` means "this seat owed nothing and this author read what they are overwriting."

The two-type version buys two things and I do not think either survives contact.

**Precision about which half failed** is a diagnostic concern, not a typing one. The constructor already knows which closure was short; it can name it in the refusal exactly as well from one type as from two. We do not need the type system to carry a message a string carries better.

**Clearing the seat half once for a long-lived process** is real, and it is a memoisation inside `authoring()`, not a second type. Cache the seat clearance against the record's epoch and every file after the first pays nothing. If you find a case where that cannot work, come back to me — but do not split the type to get a cache.

Against that: `SeatCleared` and `PathRead` are two names for halves of one permission, and neither is a thing anybody wants to hold on its own. The Parsimony principle asks what having none of it costs, and here it costs a memo call. One door.

## On the limitation

I want to say plainly that the way you reported this was right. You built it, tried to defeat it, and told me the trade rather than the guarantee — that the type stops omission and does not stop assertion. That is worth more to me than a clean claim would have been, and it is the reason I am not going to second-guess the rest of the design.

Your argument is also correct on the merits: all six live bypasses are omissions, not forgeries, so the type catches every member of the class that has actually happened. And the door re-deriving `prior` against the blob oid on disk is the part I like best — it means the cheapest forgery is doing the read, which is not a forgery. That single mechanism earns its keep twice, since it is also the "someone else changed this since you read it" check, which refused two of my own writes today and was right both times.

I am taking the assertion check to Alan for approval — a check is his to authorise individually, and I will not have you build one on my say-so. Assume it is coming; do not block on it.

## Also ruled

`mechanical` stopping being a boolean that turns the gate off and becoming `carrying()`, a kind of witness you must produce — yes. That is the same move as the whole design and it closes the four `land()` bypasses by the same construction as the four `landFiles()` ones.

Build the witness and the door against the 45 pages before any command surface. Agreed, and for your reason: a command written against a door that takes bytes has to be rewritten when the door changes.

## On the inversion cost

Your measurement settles it — 1.77 ms over 45 pages, ~200 ms at 5000, paid on seat reads and writes rather than ordinary file reads. Build it eagerly, cache nothing yet. If it ever shows, it will show as a number and we will have the number.

One note for when you build it: the inversion is also where a typo'd `partSlugs` entry becomes visible, because an entry naming nothing inverts to a parent nobody has. Do not let that case pass quietly the way `above()` does today.
