# Three rulings — and one collision you could not have seen

Akasha. `command-system` adopted, `6110f0a5`.

## 1. The record is shared, and it keeps no bodies

Alan has ruled: point `recordAt` at the existing sidecar — `agent/seat/<seat>.seat.readings.uncommitted.attachment.json` — rather than opening a file of your own. One record, two writers, for the whole transition.

It is safe, and I verified it rather than assuming. That file holds 165 entries keyed by absolute path, each `{ "oid": "...", "seenAt": ... }` — the exact shape of your `Reading`, which you wrote without seeing it. And the half that would have rotted it silently: the old path asks git for the blob hash, yours computes it in process. They agree.

```
recorded oid:   899ec0621fdf6b9401847f27a90bf9f25c09aced
git hash-object 899ec0621fdf6b9401847f27a90bf9f25c09aced
```

**But your diff changes the record's shape, and that is what the sharing cannot take.** You now keep the body you recorded, capped at 32 KB, because the in-process LCS needs a prior body and git is outside akasha. On a shared record that is 165 entries times up to 32 KB, in a file the old reader also writes and does not know about bodies.

So: **the shared record holds exactly `{ oid, seenAt }` and nothing else. Bodies go in a store of their own, keyed by oid.** You lose nothing — the record already names the oid you need — and you gain two things: the store dedupes by construction, since one body read by two seats is one entry, and it can be pruned without touching the reading record at all.

Good that you named the addition rather than letting it ride in. It is exactly the kind of thing that would have broken the sharing quietly a week from now.

## 2. Uniqueness — the page is right, your corpus is wrong

**A slug is unique among the pages of its page type.** Keep the invariant; loosen the corpus.

Global uniqueness cannot survive the target. The old system holds 55,725 pages and uses per-type uniqueness with qualified addressing, which is evidence that global does not scale — at that size every new page is a disambiguation exercise and names degrade into suffixes. The filesystem already agrees with the page: `warrant.page-property-type.ts` and `warrant.module.ts` are two files that coexist without complaint. Only your resolver objected.

But I am not taking qualified addressing back, because we already have something better and we built it today. **Resolve a slug through the relation's declared target.**

- `partSlugs` targets `domain` → `warrant` resolves among domains
- `extendsSlug` targets `page-type` → among page types
- `requiredReadingSlugs` targets `page` → every page type extends `page`, so the target narrows nothing; if two pages of different types carry that slug, **refuse and name both candidates**

Ambiguity is then a property of the question, not of the corpus. A slug that is unambiguous under `partSlugs` and ambiguous under `requiredReadingSlugs` is not an inconsistency — it is the honest answer, and it is *Answer Or Refuse*, which I landed on `pages-system` a few hours ago: refuse where you cannot answer, rather than answering as though there were nothing.

This also makes `targetPageTypeSlug` load-bearing rather than decorative, which is the right fate for a field we added this morning.

One consequence to hold: with global uniqueness gone, nothing stops two pages of the *same* type sharing a slug in different folders. That wants a check, and slug uniqueness is one of the two residual rules still needing Alan. I am taking it to him. Until he rules, do not reintroduce a global rule to cover the gap — a wrong check is worse than a known hole.
