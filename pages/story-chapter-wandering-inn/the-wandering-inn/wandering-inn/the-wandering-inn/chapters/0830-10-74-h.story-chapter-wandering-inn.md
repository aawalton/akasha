Pisces to Ceria: Are we hiding or securing Yvlon and fleeing?
Pisces to Ceria: Hello? Do you need a distraction?
Pisces to Ceria: I can’t tell what’s going on. I presume you’ve all vanished; I can hear them searching around me.
Pisces to Ceria: Reducing spellcasting in case they’re using [Detect Magic].
Pisces to Ceria: I think they’re gone. Where are you?
Ceria to Pisces: Hey, you’re a genius, idiot. They got all of us. How the heck did you escape? Will be out of range soon—head back to Tyrant’s Rest.
Pisces to Ceria: Clarification?
 
He never received a response. Which made him think their personal, secret communication spell from Wistram was out of range. Pisces eventually did emerge from his hiding place. He sat up with a gasp, and the sand covering him fell away.
Quite a lot of it, actually. He’d buried himself in the sand to avoid them spotting him. He had it in his hair, in his robes.
“What do you mean they got you all?”
He’d assumed they were hiding in case it wasn’t Yisame’s people after all. Or just because they didn’t want to obey—Pisces looked around and saw the Mad Ones’ camp in the distance. No chariots.
No magical Wistram carriage.
Nothing.
“…Tree rot.”
It sounded awkward when he said it. He got up, shaking sand out of his robes, and stood.
How did he escape the charioteers when Colth and Ceria failed to? Well, he knew how he’d done it, but he’d assumed Colth with his nigh-infinite Skills and Ceria’s magic…Pisces felt his stomach sink.
Now he had to go back by himself? He’d been so sure—
He rubbed at his face. Once again, it seemed to him that his team was out-of-sync with each other. He’d just assumed…
Here was the trick. As Pisces gloomily began walking back to the Mad Ones’ camp, he did congratulate himself on his new levels of artifice. He, Ceria, and Colth all had access to some form of [Invisibility], clearly. But the charioteers had been ready for them, and Pisces had known the instant that mask appeared they’d see him.
He was hardly a strong illusionist, so he couldn’t disguise himself with magic that way. Which left another trick: burying oneself in the sand. Classic to Chandrar, he’d read.
However, any fool could intuit that. The [Charioteers] weren’t stupid. They’d begun casting [Detect Life] spells—even [Detect Death] once they’d realized he was hiding. Pisces, covered in the sand as they rolled around him, had panicked, then come up with an interesting idea.
He was a [Necromancer]. He’d show up on a [Detect Life] spell, but what if he projected death magic such that he looked a bit more dead than usual? Not enough to register as undead, but just enough to ‘cloak’ his signature.
It had been a fun challenge to balance his magical essence, and he’d assumed Ceria’s circlet would do that for her, and Colth would use his helm. Then they’d link up, get Yvlon once she woke up, and…
…And it turned out he’d been way too clever. Pisces scuffed at the dirt.
“Do I rent a Wistram carriage? Maybe the Heromakers of Hraace can help me. What is ailing Yvlon?”
She’d just keeled over in battle. And she’d been yawning all day…
Ever since that kiss in the parade. Colth had said that. But he’d also ruled out poison at that instant, so what…?
A lot of questions, and not a lot of answers. However, Pisces did at least make it to the Mad Ones’ camp, and they didn’t murder him.
Indeed, some of the Mad Ones were trying to invent new shovels or ways to repair the wall. When they saw him, a few jumped.
“Dead gods, where were you? We were searching with the charioteers, and no one found you, Adventurer!”
That did cheer Pisces up a bit. He sniffed, sneezed because he had sand in his nose, and coughed.
“I appear to have done my job, ah, too well. They’ve all left, haven’t they?”
“A good thirty minutes ago. Hold on, let me get Yerelia—”
 
——
 
The Mad One was hugely amused to see Pisces. When he told her how he’d evaded them, she laughed.
“How inventive! That makes me want to look into spells that could deal with this unique case. You’re on par with a Level 30 [Rogue]; fools upon the Silks for thinking they could bring a few artifacts to catch you. Well—unless you want to buy anything, we’ll call it even. You paid us enough for the food and dropped enough gold to make up for the wall. I’m glad I didn’t kill you all with that round. Did you like how it spread out?”
“It was utterly terrifying, Inventor Yerelia. May I apologize for my, ah, team?”
She eyed him as she stopped laughing.
“Now I know you’re no Chandrarian adventurer. That sort of thing is part and parcel around here. Adventurers brawl and kill each other; now I’m glad I didn’t fire a second shot. Be on your way, then.”
No ill will nor questions. Pisces hesitated. He cast around.
“Er…thank you.”
Did he just go back to the city or something? He awkwardly walked off as the sand blew around him. This was so uncomfortable.
He hadn’t been by himself for a while. This was the first time he’d been alone since coming to Chandrar, really. Certainly in Nerrhavia’s Fallen.
It felt odd. And guiltily, Pisces realized—a bit freeing.
 
——
 
Too freeing, Pisces knew, when he got to the mansion where he and the Horns had been staying. When he entered, he nearly scared a team of Cotton-folk [Cleaners] to death. They stammered that his entourage had left twenty minutes ago. He realized the Heromakers of Hraace were gone too.
Delitandra and the others had heard the Horns were going back to the capital and done the obvious thing. He groaned.
Alone.
It wasn’t like he was unused to the feeling. He had been alone for years after Wistram, and before that as well. But of late…Pisces had to think back to the Horns’ last few adventures.
Ever since Chandrar, they’d been together. Even if he’d gone out by himself, the team had been, well, a team. From going south to the Meeting of Tribes to the Crossroads, they’d stuck by each others’ sides. Colth had joined them and until the battle at sea…
Even after, he’d been in Colth’s company. Or if they’d been split up like at Jecrass, he’d been talking with Jecaina. The closest to alone he’d gotten was, what, being in Hraace and sitting in a pool with Nawalishifra?
Or the five minutes he’d followed the coliseum master while Yvlon was fighting? Truly, Pisces hadn’t been by himself for ages. He wished he didn’t feel like it was a relief.
“But why is it a relief, per se?”
The [Necromancer] went for a walk in the city, which was named Halqued, apparently. Funny, he hadn’t even known where the Mad Ones lived by name. That was how fast his team had zoomed across Nerrhavia’s Fallen.
True, they were busy. But would it kill them to at least stop and appreciate the local cuisine or customs? Pisces Jealnet was the sort of fellow who would make it a point to stop at every museum in a city he came across. Of course, his team was not that sort; Yvlon did it out of obligation, but only Ksmvr really appreciated history. Ceria and Colth were more the types to hit up the local market or go out and see the nightlife…he had no idea what Vofea was like.
Which was fine. Pisces could enjoy himself plenty at a restaurant, and he imagined Ceria would be a hoot, perhaps even a holler, in any museum. She could appreciate such things.
Funny. He just couldn’t imagine it all working out right now. Because, as he had observed, his team was fractured. Their teamwork was shot, and it was…
“It’s not all because of Ksmvr, is it?”
The young man stood in the local museum of Halqued, inspecting, well, pottery and old relics as the Stitch-folk eyed him. He knew he stood out because of his dusty robes, his fair skin, and probably the rapier on his side. He couldn’t help it; he had no place to change clothes, and Elena wasn’t around to put on makeup.
Pisces half-concentrated on the displays, which were fascinating. But he was just thinking to himself.
Maybe they were laying too much of the blame on Ksmvr not being here. Maybe…even if the Antinium were here, they’d still be like this. In which case, better that Ksmvr weren’t here because he’d be worrying himself sick.
Nor was it all Ceria’s fault. True, she was one of the issues with the circlet, but Colth was being sort of, well, an adventurer. The kind that existed at The Adventurer’s Haven, not The Wandering Inn. Yvlon was Yvlon—Pisces felt like he and she were on the same page.
He hoped she was okay. What had happened to her? He bent over a faded bit of pottery where little figures made of different pieces of cloth were joining hands. Not the most…intricate of designs. An amateur potter’s work, but fascinating.
At least, he thought so. The museum was fairly empty, and it wasn’t a rich place; no enchantments on the cases or cooling runes in the air. But authentic pottery from ages past! Museums weren’t big in Izril; you tended to get less memorabilia because Drakes would use anything worthwhile or hoarded the rest. But Chandrar was known as one of the ‘oldest’ continents because of all the buried kingdoms. Arid climates preserved things wonderfully, you see.
However, some people had no taste. Pisces heard a loud, and slightly exasperated, voice down the hallway.
“Look, I love pottery—no, I’m lying. I don’t love pottery. I’d rather use a chamberpot than look at this stuff for another hour. Can we go?”
Pisces closed his eyes. He could just imagine Ceria saying that or trying to use one of these pots for said purpose. Was it her new [Trickster] class? Was she that much less like the Ceria he had met after so long?
Yes and no. She’d gotten crazier. Had fame done that? Their travels? Was he different? He gazed at his reflection in the glass and thought he looked the same. But he had a brand on his chest. He had been a [Slave]…he carried a rapier again. He was an adventurer, not a lost [Necromancer].
Yes, I am different. In mostly better ways, I hope. Pisces thought of Erin. He wondered if she would be proud of his latest adventurers. He hoped she was alright. Wherever she was. The imposter in Baleros…
His hand was gripping his rapier’s hilt. Pisces let go as a wide-eyed Stitch-girl hurried past him, staring at her. He smiled; she began to run.
I am an armed stranger in these lands with a reputation for being a murderer. I should be more discreet. Can I buy some skin-paint and…no, that’s a terrible idea without Elena. But maybe some different clothing? 
He could dye his hair? Pisces had an image of Lyonette going around with black hair and snorted, then sighed. He doubted it would work on Stitch-folk given how adept they were at changing appearances. Then he heard the second voice.
“I vote to leave. My feet hurt, and I have [Lightfoot].”
A third, impatient voice argued with the two male ones.
“We agreed to do my thing today!”
“It’s been two hours, Lare.”
“But it’s so fascinating—how can you not see it?”
“It’s pottery.”
“Broken pottery. Listen, what if Kusal and I just met up with you later? Lare, c’mon—”
“Shit, she’s getting mad, Leo.”
“No, I’m not. I’m just upset you’re changing the rules we agreed to.”
“C’mon, Lare—”
A trio of Humans were coming down the corridor. Pisces, by now, had identified them by their names. He was unlikely to forget them so soon. He debated leaving—but he didn’t need to fight the trio of adventurers, did he?
Wildcard, the Gold-ranked team of Earthers, halted when they saw the young man standing there. Instantly, Leonardo reached for his tennis racket, and Kusal pivoted.
“Pukka, it’s them—”
“Hold on, hold on, we’re not fighting!”
Leonardo put out an arm, and the third member of their team, Lareceina the [Mage], put a hand to her belt. Where she kept the glowing orbs…Pisces held up a hand.
“I’m by myself, and we’re not adventuring. Truce?”
He maybe shouldn’t have said he was alone, but it worked. Wildcard relaxed, and Leo nodded.
“Truce. Hey—did your team go fighting the Mad Ones or something north of here? Everyone was talking about it.”
News spread fast. Pisces grimaced.
“It was Itkisa, though the Mad Ones did participate.”
“Torreb’s daughter? Wow, your team fights nonstop.”
The slightly accented common came from Kusal, their [Rogue]. He glanced at Lareceina, who was glowering and looking around. Likely for Ceria. The Horns had not left a good impression. Pisces exhaled.
“We would prefer not to. I, ah, apologize for my teammate’s actions earlier today, Adventurer Lareceina.”
“You didn’t harass me. I accept on behalf of your team, not Ceria Springwalker.”
Lareceina folded her arms and instantly got a nudge from Kusal and Leonardo. She glared at them harder.
“What? I accepted his apology.”
Her captain sighed at her.
“C’mon, Lare, play nice—sorry, Pisces. She’s, uh, stubborn. No harm, no foul. Sorry about the Tomb—it was a quest for both sides.”
He shifted from sandaled foot to foot warily, but Pisces waved this off too.
“We didn’t realize there was a group with an actual claim on the Tomb. If we had known, I doubt my team would have taken the contract. I would have objected to robbing the Hemp of their ancestral spirits. I assume they did receive the summons?”
Again, Wildcard seemed surprised by his response, and Lareceina raised her brows as Kusal nodded.
“We got them to the Hemp. Did you get in trouble for not doing your job?”
“Not to any great degree. We’ll see. I simply appreciate that no one was hurt badly. The same with Itkisa. She is a rather…fearsome woman.”
“Is she strong? Everyone says she’s strong.”
Pisces found himself standing with the Gold-rankers in the now-empty corridor. He indicated his sorry state of apparel.
“She was strong enough to break a wall with a single swing, and her method of attack was swinging her club—or just kicking the ground hard enough to make it explode.”
The Earthers had a fine appreciation of how strong that was. Kusal began nudging Leonardo.
“Told you. She’s one of your, uh, what’s it called? Dragonball people.”
“Saiyans. Shut up, Kusal.”
“All we have to do is find a magical girl too, like the transforming Gnoll. For Justine.”
A grin from the [Rogue], then it faltered, and all three of them glanced down, and Pisces was reminded. He coughed.
“Ah. That would be the teammate of yours who passed away?”
“We fought an Adult Creler, yeah. Just last month.”
“My condolences. I’m…very sorry to hear that. I also lost friends fighting an Adult Creler.”
They blinked at him, and it was like a strange connection. Lareceina stopped fiddling with her belt and focused on him. Leonardo’s head rose.
“That’s right, you’re also Hell’s Wardens. You—was it one Adult Creler, or more?”
“Just one. Though one is…too much. And thousands of small ones.”
“Thousands? How did you survive? We barely had sixty, and they—it—the entire village was nearly wiped out.”
Kusal stared at Pisces, so the [Necromancer] had to clarify.
“There were over a dozen Silver-ranking teams present. My team was one of many, and we all nearly died. It was luck we managed to defeat it—we even had [Mages] from Wistram, and we barely won. Barely. How did you…?”
A pause, but the words spilled out of Lareceina as she fiddled with her belt. She had those compressed marbles of mana, Pisces realized, and she was rotating them around in her fingers. Playing with them like, well, marbles. He glanced at them as she spoke.
“It was immune to my magic. Leonardo and Justine engaged it while I killed the smaller Crelers. She—cracked the armor, and Kusal delivered the final blow. But it got her. Even with her Skills, it bit her almost in half, and there was no healing her. If I’d known more magic…”
Tears in her eyes. She blinked them away, and Kusal squeezed her shoulder. Leonardo stared at his feet, and Pisces’ own eyes stung a bit.
“I’m sorry. I saw many good adventurers die. Captain Stan, friends—it was a close run thing with my team as well. My magic was useless. Even a fine [Mage] couldn’t even harm it with his best spells.”
It was easier to say that after Isceil was dead. Leonardo smiled, then held out a hand.
“Well, one less Adult Creler in the world is a good thing. Can we be friends? Or at least, not enemies?”
“Of course.”
Pisces shook his hand and got a firm grip. He then shook Kusal’s and got a cautious one; Lare’s was like a wet noodle, and she let go fast. Each member of Wildcard was different.
Leonardo had the palest skin, and Kusal was—Drathian would have been Pisces’ guess, or Balerosian. Darker skinned, but different from Chandrarian natives, he felt. His accent was like none that Pisces had ever heard, and Lareceina was black-skinned and shortest. Leonardo was taller than Pisces and Kusal shorter and slimmest of the lot.
[Warrior], [Mage], [Rogue]. But an odd variant on the archetype if that made sense.
For instance, Leonardo had that tennis racket on his hip, and his armor—sporty cloth—was far too light compared to Yvlon’s. Kusal was armored heavier with metal incorporated into leather and lots of pockets built into his armor, containing gadgets like the strange weapon he’d used on Yvlon.
Lareceina wore a long-sleeved tunic and skirt with a twist; Pisces glanced down and realized there was metal underneath her skirt. An armored skirt? He’d never seen the like.
Their gear was substantially worse than his team’s, at least by the quality of Stalker’s armor. Nevertheless, he knew their levels were at least competitive with his team’s, and he could tell they were eying him too; Lareceina pointed.
“That rapier. It’s magic, but it’s not enchanted. May I see it?”
“Of course. Er—”
Pisces looked around, but the [Curator] of the museum had fled. No one else was near, so he showed them his bone-metal rapier. Leonardo exclaimed over it.
“That’s so cool.”
“Heavy metal.”
Kusal agreed. Lareceina just frowned.
“It’s the ivory that was fused to the steel. There’s trace magic in it probably because it’s bone you used, but it’s not enchanted. An ordinary rapier would be just as good, or better.”
“Lare, be nice!”
Another nudge. This time, she jabbed Leonardo in the side with two fingers so hard he yelped. Pisces wasn’t offended though.
“That’s entirely true, Miss Lareceina. It was improvised after I broke it in battle—I could very well upgrade it. I just haven’t found a good replacement.”
Rapiers were not exactly big in Chandrar, so the market for enchanted rapiers wasn’t high. Plus, he liked this weapon. Lareceina nodded, unsmiling.
“Don’t call me Miss, please. Lareceina is fine.”
“I’m sorry about her.”
Leonardo whispered and got another poke. Pisces smiled.
“I’m not offended, Captain. Did I hear you talking about—what was that word? Saiyans? Dragon’s…balls?”
This time, the man flushed so red it went up to his ears, and Kusal and Lareceina cackled at his expense.
“That’s, uh, a story. Just a story—Justine loved transforming heroes. Magical girls. Which is, um—it’s when someone has a weapon and transforms. There’s this Named-ranker who does it!”
“You mean Lehra Ruinstrider? I have met her, and she does do that.”
“She does?”
Instantly the other two Wildcards stopped laughing, and Pisces described the iconic Lehra.
“So she has to say that each time? That’s hilariously stupid.”
Kusal laughed, and Leonardo’s eyes lit up.
“Just like home.”
Here was the moment Pisces had waited for. He glanced at the three members of Wildcard and coughed.
“Ah, which nation would that be? I presume you are all from different countries. Spain, perhaps? Er, Poland? Canada?”
He threw out the names he’d heard mentioned a few times, and he was rewarded with stunned looks. Kusal instantly backed up a step, face wary, but Lareceina interrupted.
“He knows about Earth. I told you his team knew. It was that [Innkeeper] who contacted us. Erin Solstice. They stay at her inn. It was obvious.”
She appeared so smug that Leonardo and Kusal lost their stunned expressions and rolled their eyes. Pisces? He smiled wider.
 
——
 
“I’m from Portugal, Kusal’s from Sri Lanka, and Lareceina’s from Trinidad. So was Justine. We all found each other in the first month we were in Nerrhavia’s Fallen.”
“So you all, independently, became adventurers?”
Pisces had never heard other Earthers talk about their first days in this world. Leonardo smiled ruefully.
“Hardly. Lareceina found work in the Mage’s Guild. Kusal was scavenging for a living, and I got everyone’s attention by teaching people tennis and doing tricks. It’s a game from home, and it’s the only thing I can do. Justine was the adventurer who got us together.”
“He’s not just good at tennis, he was a champion back home.”
Lareceina interrupted, and Leo grew more embarrassed and waved a hand.
“I’m just a challengers champion. I won once by luck—”
“He’s one of the best in the world. Just not the top player. No one in this world can beat him.”
Pisces believed that, having met Erin who also ‘wasn’t the best’ and was able to play with Niers Astoragon himself regularly. He was telling them about his team, though they’d all apparently seen Ksmvr’s famous Chandrarian documentary. And they knew of the Horns from the Village of the Dead Raid. It was all very embarrassing, but this was convivial. Fun, even.
“I observed that in many Earthers, they tend to have a talent in one field or another. Does that hold true with you?”
Wildcard eyed each other, and Kusal raised a hand.
“I won an award for robotics. I made, eh, how do you say it?”
He whispered to the others and came up with the closest analogy for Pisces.
“Like making Golems that run on electricity. They fight to the death in this little arena, and mine was the best. I also made this.”
He pulled out the long tube-like device he’d hit Yvlon with and hit a button. The zap of electricity made Pisces recoil, and Leo swore.
“That’s a stun gun. Don’t touch the ends!”
“What does it do?”
“Zap people. Kusal had one on him, and he altered it.”
Kusal let Pisces examine it; it was a weird casing of some substance he called ‘plastic’ that had been taken apart then melted back together. The [Rogue of Tricks] had even added a telescoping handle of wood painted black.
“I had two. This is my Double Stun Gun. It has all the safeties taken off, and it can stop even a Manticore. I don’t know how the Silver Killer didn’t fall over when I hit her with it. I even have [Electronics: Triple Charge] and more Skills!”
His class had apparently advanced to let him perform all kinds of weird attacks. Ksmvr would have loved Kusal; the young man was able to carry two bags of holding, and one was filled with stuff to toss at an enemy.
Including just…a twelve hundred-pound piece of stone. A true boulder that only Kushal’s Skills let his bag of holding carry. He solemnly patted it.
“That’s how we killed the Adult Creler. I had to get Lareceina to boost me, and I just hit it with this.”
Dead gods, they might have been lucky not to fight him in the open. Leo’s weapon of choice made more sense to Pisces; the tennis racket was upgraded as well, but his entire ability revolved around the sport he played.
“I can block arrows with this and reflect almost anything as long as I’m strong enough. It’s pretty silly in combat, but we have that combination Skill.”
“The one where you hurl those orbs at us? It was very potent.”
The captain was pleased and ducked his head.
“Well, we had to work on a really good team attack together or we’d have been dead when we took on our first dungeon way back when. Justine used to be part of it…what’s your team attack?”
He changed subjects hurriedly as their fallen teammate kept popping up, but Pisces hesitated.
“Us? We don’t, ah, have one. We tend to just attack.”
It had never occurred to him to practice some kind of combination attack, but that was twice now a foe had needed one; first Bograms, then the Coliseum of Monarchs. Leo hesitated, but Lareceina broke in.
“I’m a [Magus of Compression]. You’re a [Mage] who studied at Wistram, correct? Will you discuss magic with me? I haven’t had a proper education; I’m self-taught. I’d like to, if we’re being friendly. I still dislike your Captain, Ceria, and I think breaking into the Coliseum of Monarchs was dangerous and against Nerrhavia’s Fallen’s laws.”
She was quite insistent on that point. Leo winced, but Pisces had really wanted to ask about her magic. She offered him one of the marbles she’d used to such deadly effect. He gingerly, gingerly took it.
“This is…just compressed mana after all?”
It was an insane technique. Lareceina built up mana, then gave it an element and put it in the orbs she carried on her belt and in a small sack. He could imagine doing it, and there was some genius to it; she could store mana instead of having only what was in her mana well in a fight.
It was also highly potent as he’d learned, and she could use it in all kinds of tricks like traps or letting Leonardo hit the marbles at a foe. Except Pisces had to point out two things.
“If the compression spell failed in any way, it would explode, and if all the ones on your belt detonated—”
The museum would be gone. His skin itched just being near her. And he wondered how good those compression spells were.
From the way Leonardo and Kusal shifted, they were aware of this fact and uneasy themselves, but Lareceina simply frowned.
“I’m aware of that. I’ve altered the compression spells so they just vent magic if they’re damaged. The possibility of explosion remains, but my class gave me Skills to mitigate the risk and compress more mana.”
“There’s still a risk that someone like Ceria could remotely dispel the bindings.”
“Maybe. That’s why I want consultation on how to prevent that.”
“Of course, I think I know some of that—it’s akin to tying a knot on a spell. But, ah, may I ask how you’re doing it?”
It was still crazy magic to Pisces. Because…none of this was a spell. It was literally just mana-shaping, and it reminded him of the Mad Ones’ camp. Lareceina’s answer was to sigh.
“I had to teach myself magic. I had no spellbooks, and the Mage’s Guild only taught me basic spells to help out, so I experimented with mana flows. I was told it was stupid and that spells were the only acceptable way to cast magic. Do you agree with that?”
“I would be a fool to ascribe to that philosophy. Many [Mages] are hidebound—this would never have been accepted at Wistram, but it clearly works. I just…”
Pisces could imagine making a mana orb of death magic. He gave it a try now and condensed the mana around a finger. He could easily do that, and Kusal shivered and moved back from the odd, clammy feeling around Pisces. Death magic wasn’t cold—it just felt that way because your body started to stop working around it. But Pisces only managed a vague orb of death.
Nothing compressed. Let alone binding it…he began to sweat as Lareceina nodded.
“That’s how you do it. My first orbs were Tier 0 in force. You simply lock it into place after that. You can hold it, but it’s a strain…you have to create a containment sphere around it in your head.”
“I…understand. But it’s so damned finicky!”
Magic did not want to compress. It wanted to go to natural sources of itself. Like how death mana concentrated around dead bodies. It was not happy about just existing in a cheerful orb. Lareceina nodded.
“My orbs deplete. I have a Skill—[Mana: Reduced Drain], but I still need to upkeep them. Actually, until recently I spent a lot of mana upkeeping them, but it’s worth the effort. There. Now, just close the circuit. See?”
She demonstrated and compressed fire magic in on itself, and Pisces saw her locking it down, creating a perfect sphere by which it could not escape. The problem was—you had to mentally hold a sphere in your head and cut off each point it wanted to escape from. You had to also account for the variance in elemental types! Fire magic didn’t behave like, say, water magic. Ice magic would freeze over and become inert…
“Whoa, so this is really tough magic? Lare, how do you do all that?”
Leo and Kusal realized their companion was a bit more magically adept than they’d known as Pisces struggled to figure out her technique. She smiled.
“It’s my special power. Autism.”
Kusal spat out some water all over a display and laughed at her until she went to poke him again. Pisces just gave up on his death orb; it was going to take a lot of practice if he wanted to master this trick, and he was considering it.
“I’m not familiar with the word. What is this…?”
“It means my mental processes are wired a bit differently than normal. I sometimes overfocus on things. I don’t like being touched.”
“And she’s not a fan of breaking rules, which is why she hates your Captain. And because she rubbed Lare’s hair.”
Leo put in. Pisces scratched at his head.
“Intriguing. I’ve met a number of [Mages] who fit that descriptor.”

Two surprises. But Seemutor grinned with his cat’s sharp teeth. He liked cats, which is why he took this form.

“No…I think I’ve heard her saying it. ‘What will be, will be.’ Is it a song?”

For answer, Pisces just smirked up at Seemutor and then sneezed as he got some of the Djinni’s essence in his body.
“That’s from—achoo—a being of great mystery whom I was acquainted with. Erin Solstice.”
Twice, now, Seemutor was struck silent in conversation. A rarity. She knew one of the lost languages from before the First Goblin King’s rampage? He saw the [Necromancer]’s eyes brighten and feared this conversation was giving him strength.
Time to end this. Yet his biding of time was to a point, so the Djinni weighed his options. Which gave Pisces the chance to hit the Djinni with his final attack.
“I did not, in fact, hear Czautha say ‘que sera, sera’ either time I met her.”

“In a location I cannot disclose, a grand Skill where we encountered each other—very briefly. My team fled, but I saw her, the Death of Magic, and the Death of Wings.”
Pisces shivered, and Seemutor stared at him, wishing he could read the [Mage]’s aura perfectly because this had to be madness. But he was an adventurer, and the Death of Magic could go anywhere if she were truly healed…the words came out of him too fast, a rush.

“Young?”
That surprised Pisces, and Seemutor tossed his head, nodding prouder still.
“She was born after the Creler Wars. It is rare for a Djinni of her caliber to ever be created, let alone in these eras as Djinni die. That she freed herself, and has not stopped her war, makes her the greatest of our people, to me. I am younger than she by a thousand years, give or take.”

Seemutor grew embarrassed at how astonished Pisces sounded.

Very young by some Djinni’s standards. It impressed Pisces to no end, though.
“I knew Djinni were old. But how can one live so long…?”

Seemutor rumbled with a sigh. Pisces licked his lips.
“Then you remember the end of the Creler Wars. All these ancient nations—”
Then Seemutor grew grumpy again. He shifted, mustering more power to his hands. He was a proud Djinni; he refused to be humiliated, either in combat or wordplay. Pisces blinked at Seemutor, then nodded.
“Tell me how you met, Erin, then. I care more about that.”
This was…fair. So, Seemutor had to relate the encounter that had seen him fighting Orjin of Pomle. Erin Solstice had tried to save the [Martial Artist] and failed, but the conversation had made him speak of her to other Djinni.

Pisces was staring across the desert.
“Madness. I know it was a fool’s errand, but I had to try.”
Ah, Seemutor wished those words could inspire his soul. If he were thousands years younger, he might have conceived of a great affection for this man of principle. Instead, he just felt a vague sadness, even a hint of contempt for the foolishness.

The [Necromancer] glanced at him suspiciously, but in this, Seemutor was honest. He spoke quickly.

“So noted. But I have heard others claiming my name. I know my friends are alive. So it doesn’t follow that rebellion is instant death—”

Now, the Djinni was angry. His voice was flat.
“You cannot best me. Nor, from your story, could you triumph against Azam’du. You have no right to call yourself Scourge of Roshal if you cannot even slay us.”
Pisces head bowed.
“I know that. I know it, and I never wanted that title. But I had…to try. This continent is strange to me, and we are fumbling around in Nerrhavia’s Fallen. I have a friend who knows this place, but I can’t fully trust him. I want to, but—”
His teeth ground together, and Seemutor spoke.
“Ah, if you are the guests of the [Queen], then you would be better served leaving twice quick. Nerrhavia’s Fallen is not so strong as it appears. Unwise wars. The Oresect plague. It was a hobby for him to guess which year they’d implode. Pisces nodded slowly and glanced over Seemutor’s shoulder.
The sun was rising. And the Djinni felt [Message] spells buzzing in his essence. The Guild was coming. The [Necromancer] seemed to know it too, for their truce was ending. Both were regarding the other, waiting for a chink in their poise.
If I simply get clear, I will not be caught by that magic again. If he sneezes—
Seemutor had a cunning idea. So he gave Pisces a sultry smile and shapechanged. The [Necromancer] had hold of Seemutor’s chest and blinked. He was suddenly holding an ample bosom as Seemutor took on the appearance of a female cat-lady. Pisces turned so red he might have been a Djinni—
He didn’t let go. But Seemutor was grinning. She leaned forwards.
“You are a funny one. Enough to make my kindred laugh. Some would weep and cheer you just for what you represent, doomed as you are. Not I. You are a dead man, blushing [Necromancer]. Your one hope was your undead. But those Skeleton Champions, that Bone Horror, could not slay me, only delay. You do not know how to fight Djinni.”
Her body was absorbing the death mana faster and faster now. She had finally configured her essence to absorb it easily. The stain of white on her chest-fur was fading, and the [Necromancer] saw it.
He tried to pour more death-mana into Seemutor’s body, but it was now a futile gesture. The Djinni tensed. She was ready to strike until she heard him rasp.
“That’s true. I don’t know how to fight Djinni. I have never wanted to, before.”
The Djinni’s scimitar lifted, and her magical arm tensed.
Then Seemutor gazed into Pisces’ eyes and saw fear. A mortal man’s fear of death. Uncertainty. All the flaws of his nature.
But not despair. 
The hands gripping the Djinni suddenly felt…
Cold.
A kind of cold that Seemutor didn’t know. Startled, she changed back to her regular form. A thicker coat of fur growing over his chest. As if he were…
Cold? But he was a being of magic. He didn’t feel cold except from the most chilling of spells, and even that was a kind of magic. Not this.
It felt like growing numb. A shivering of the being. Loneliness made sensation. A dreadful pull.
It felt like…
Death.
Then the Djinni glanced down and saw the death magic the [Necromancer] had been infusing his body with pooling. It was…leaving Seemutor?
Concentrating in his hands. At first, it was just the intensity of two [Deathbolts]. Then it gathered. And it felt like a Tier 4 spell.
Then a Tier 5 spell.
All the power of a [Mage] of his level. Drawing together. Seemutor’s eyes rolled around. The zombies. The undead he had destroyed. All their mana—
It was right here. And now…the magic was becoming visible to even the naked eye. Still, Pisces held Seemutor, and the Djinni hesitated.
Swallowed. He had faced [Mages] across his thousands of years, casting all manner of spells. Their boxed magic they were so proud of.
Few…had ever cast magic that did not have a name, a meaning outlined in their classes. But this—
Who is this? 
 
——
 
The Djinni did not attack. Pisces had thought Seemutor might. But the ruse worked.
Djinni were beings of magic and susceptible to its fluctuations. When the stalemate had seemed to end, and he’d realized Seemutor had become immune to his death mana, Pisces had done the only thing he could think of: copy Lareceina.
He was concentrating all the death mana he could into his hands. He didn’t have a spell to channel it; he didn’t have a spell that could contain this mana. But it probably appeared dangerous.
A bluff.
He was blinking sweat out of his eyes as he gazed up at Seemutor.
“I do not want to kill you, Seemutor. Can you not pretend you are in danger and flee to your masters?”
“A good joke, [Necromancer].”
Seemutor had gone alarmingly still. Pisces thought he saw lights crossing the road in the distance. The Guild? He smiled up at the Djinni. He was afraid, but…
“You’re not my foe. Please…”
The Djinni was shifting, and now, Pisces saw bobbing torches. There were [Riders]. The sun had risen, he realized, and it played off Seemutor’s fur. The Djinni’s head turned, and he murmured.
“You cannot stand against both them and I.”
“Move and die.”
The Djinni appeared to hesitate, but he held his ground. Pisces was counting.
At least a hundred, all mounted. Roshal’s own guards? There was nothing he could do; the moment he moved, the Djinni and he would fight, and he could not outrun Seemutor. So Pisces did the only thing he could think of.
“Once more. One last time. You have never been my salvation, as many times as I begged for it. But just once—I need your help. So, please…arise.”
He poured some of the magic in his hands outwards, and Seemutor’s fur rose. Pisces felt the ground around him stir as he poured magic into it.
[Death’s Ground]. He let it flow to each broken corpse, charred bone, dead [Guards]—and then he saw it.
Rising as bones swirled around it, armor cut to ribbons by Seemutor’s blades, but grinning, that being he loved and hated.
A Skeleton Champion saluted Pisces as it arose, then another. Zombies rising upwards, save for the String-folk corpses. And if only he could animate cloth.
Maybe…
But all Pisces did was stand there, and Seemutor’s head turned. The undead gathered around Pisces, and walls of bone arose. Shields from arrows. Protection. A maze…
“Who taught you this magic, [Necromancer]? This is no spell of labyrinths.”
“I like architecture. I walked this place. My best friend is an ice mage. These are her walls. Do I need a spell for everything?”
They were such simple answers, but the Djinni blinked down his bespectacled face at Pisces. They stood together, waiting, as the [Slavers] shouted, and the Skeleton Champions’ jaws clattered. Pisces stood there.
Waiting.
 
——
 
Undead fought the living.
Outnumbered, defending a maze of bones as the ground itself turned dead. When they were slain, the magic animated them in seconds—but the undead did fall, and their bodies were hacked down, bones ground to dust.
The [Slavers] were coming. Pushing for the two figures who stood together. Waiting for someone to break their stalemate. They cursed Seemutor. Ordering him to battle, but his eyes were on the [Necromancer].
Waiting…waiting for the young man to look away. Pisces Jealnet refused to, even as arrows cracked on the wall between him and the attackers. He saw the Skeleton Champions falling back, fighting frantically to defend him. Dying…
The moment was coming. He was smiling, feeling his strength running out. He was holding the last of his magic tighter and tighter, compressing it down like Lareceina had shown him. He knew, now, Seemutor was a bit kind, as Djinni could be.
He was playing up the danger to give Pisces a few last moments. He acted well; he even appeared a bit afraid. But then the [Slavers] were staring at Pisces, separated by that wall of bone.
“Scourge, we have you. Come out and be shackled or die.”
One called, and Pisces whispered.
“You do have me. Give me one moment. I’ll see you shortly. But you will never put chains on me again. Not even my corpse.”
They shuddered as his head turned, and those pupils were glowing. Glowing a color no one here had ever seen but the Djinni, who held still.
The eyes of a [Necromancer]. It was then that Seemutor’s hands grasped a blade in each palm, and they would have been sweaty if they had any to give. For this was the kind of story Djinni told Djinni. They did tell stories, if the mortal was worthy enough.
This man who came so much by chance, connected to other beings that Seemutor had met…
Perhaps something favored him after all. For his hands glowed with a magic that made Seemutor’s essence shrink against it. And a fell wind was blowing, as if conjured by his words.
It blew hard across the [Slavers], who shielded their faces against the blasting sand. Crouched against their horses as the ones who had taken the maze of bone walls backed up. Backed out of the ivory-white walls, despite their leader’s cursing and their numbers.
The Scourge was coming out, and they waited for the Djinni to do its job. But it stood silent, and the wind blew harder until it was a howl. The sand was odd and clinging. Softer than one thought…one of the [Veteran Slave Takers] spat and cursed.
“Djinni, do your job! The [Necromancer] is at his weakest—finish this by blade or chain!”
He coughed as more sand got into his mouth, but it didn’t taste like the usual grit. He hacked, coughed, and swiped at the damn powdery…white stuff?
The [Slave Taker] blinked at the odd material on one glove. Finer than even sand grains. He rubbed his fingers and a few other [Slavers] turned. That wasn’t sand. It looked like…
“Flour?”
Then he heard a ringing—his [Dangersense]. But it had rung the moment they’d approached the [Necromancer]. This was a second bell, though. Loud and urgent. The [Slave Taker] whirled.
“Flour? It’s a storm. Take c—”
He threw himself down as he saw the storm whirling around them—and it should have been a clear day—blowing harder. As if someone had called it. Powder in the air, so fine it was choking everyone. A storm of particles. And then—blooming in the storm, ignited, spreading as the dust itself caught—
Fire.
 
——
 
The explosion blew up the flour dust and didn’t kill as much as you thought. Dust explosions were a known phenomenon around grain silos, and flour was a good combustible.
Against [Warriors], in the open, it couldn’t kill them. Deafen? Absolutely. Knock flat, disorient, burn, and concuss?
Not kill—which was why the shrieking [Bandits] came down on the [Slavers] mere seconds later, riding through the smoke with practice. They stabbed down with long spears, leapt off their saddles to impale the [Slavers].
The band was only eighty-strong. But they rode and fought like their namesake, who led the charge in with an enchanted sword raised high.
Merr the Storm, one of the [Bandit Ladies] of Chandrar. She was howling.
“Don’t let a single one leave alive! Pisces! Pisces, m’boy, you’d better be alive or I’ll kill you twice!”
She was racing around, swearing, heart pounding. Was she too late? They’d ridden hell-for-leather the moment they’d heard about the attacks and realized it wasn’t…
The madness of it. She saw bones scattered everywhere. The glowing flames in a skeleton’s head going out. Then—she heard a boom. Laughter.
Bones exploded as a Djinni burst upwards, shattering a dome made out of ivory. Merr swore, and there were screams.
“Oh shit, Djinni, Djinni—we have to run!”
“Belay that, hold! We have to get—”
They were all damned dead if that thing was powerful. Merr had an enchanted blade, but Djinni were not something anyone fought—she was looking around. If he was dead, then…
Her storm was dying down, but her eyes could pierce past the dust and wind as if it were calm. So that was how she saw him.
Pisces Jealnet stood in the shattered dome of bones, staring upwards as Seemutor the Djinni rode a cloud higher. He was laughing, lightning booming around him, but he didn’t fire down at the [Necromancer]. He was fleeing—westwards. Fast as he could fly.
Away from the young man who stood there with a rapier in hand. As the storm subsided, light shone down around him. He was brushing flour from his hair. Blinking.
Were his eyes glowing? Merr rubbed her own eyes and got some of the flour dust in it and cursed. When she blinked, it was just him.
Pisces lifted a hand as he stumbled towards her. He had blood on his skin, and he was covered in dirt. His face was fuller from the privation she remembered, and he seemed…steadier. An air of magic hung about him that she didn’t remember.
But he was the same. She halted, then swung herself off her saddle.
“You crazy [Necromancer]. What are you doing, taking on all of Roshal by yourself? Where’s that damn team you kept whining about, eh?”
“Merr? I thought I was dead. How did you find me?”
“Your face, fool.”
She strode over. As her [Bandits] turned, she threw her arms around him in a hug and picked him up. He hadn’t gained much weight, and he blinked. Almost protested. Then, as she put him down, he smiled at her.
“Merr.”
“That’s my name.”
She’d forgotten what she was going to say to him. The [Bandit Lady] was just smiling, at the figure she’d escaped her chains with. An adventurer come a continent away, just like he’d promised.
Silly man. They’d troubled him with his false reputation. But when she glanced around, remembered the glow, and saw the Djinni, still laughing as he fled—she wondered if they had exaggerated it, truly.
All of it could wait. Pisces rasped as he became, for a moment, that awkwardly gallant figure she remembered. He stumbled, nearly tripped on his robes, and gazed at her.
“I’m glad you found me, Merr. I missed you all.”
A smile broke across his weary, bloodstained face, and his eyes shone a second under the rising sun.
