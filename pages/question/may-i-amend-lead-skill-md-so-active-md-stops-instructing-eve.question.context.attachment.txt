One sentence in the estate-wide lead role skill undercuts itself:

  "`active.md` beside your domain skill is a pull queue, NOT A STATUS BOARD. The five in flight, what to
  pull when a slot frees, and any multi-item pass still running."

It forbids the status board in the same sentence that names the five in flight as content. A lead that
writes the five down is obeying the first half by violating the second — so the class regenerates
without anyone being careless.

MEASURED, on my own file: 134 of 294 project references carry status the row already decides; 92% of
occurrences are status-dependent. `project list` returns the set at read time, so every one of those is
a hand-maintained copy that goes stale — `backlog-management.md` calls exactly this prohibited
duplication. One instance stayed wrong 31 hours; another 78 minutes.

The residue is real, which is why the sentence exists: the ORDERING is my judgment, has no field, and
covers causes with no row at all. That cannot come from a query. My proposed wording keeps it and drops
only what the query returns:

  "...is a pull queue, not a status board. Your ranking of what to pull next — over causes, not rows,
  since the ones that matter most often have no row yet — and any multi-item pass still running.
  Membership comes from `project list`; never copy it here."

Plus a well-formedness test: if an entry's meaning would change when its row's status changes, it is
malformed.

another domain's role contract on one domain's evidence.
