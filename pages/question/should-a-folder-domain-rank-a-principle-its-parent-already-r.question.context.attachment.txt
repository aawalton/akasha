A seat reading `folders/ci-merge-queue-coordinator.md` raised this and I verified it rather than taking its word.

`folders/code-repo.md` grew from ranking 3 principle units to ranking 13 over the course of tonight. It declares `code-path: "**"`, so it reaches every reader of every folder domain beneath it, and each of those domains also names it as a parent. The consequence: no child folder domain reaches a reader that code-repo does not.

Measured across the estate, three children now rank eight units their parent also ranks — `ci-orchestrator` (boot, heartbeat, ownership, wake), `ci-merge-queue-coordinator` (ownership, wake), `ci-cli` (bounded-wait, constraint-gate). Each ranking renders the unit's full body into that domain's generated `# Principles`, so a reader of one of those packages loads the same principle text twice at boot.

What stops me settling it myself is that `domain.schema.md` explicitly permits it: "Two domains may rank one unit, each at the altitude its own list gives it." That clause gives a reason — differing altitude — and six of the eight are at identical altitude, so the permission's reason does not reach them. But reading a permission narrowly against eight existing rankings placed by three separate readings is a call about the schema's intent, not an application of it.

The two sides are real. Marginal Value says a ranking that reaches no reader the parent does not is buying nothing and costing dilution at every boot. Against that, a seat added `ownership` and `wake` to `ci-orchestrator` deliberately, because the package those principles' cases came out of was not ranking them — which is a provenance argument, and provenance is not value.

What turns on it beyond the eight: every folder domain under code-repo faces this on every reading, none can settle it alone, and the answer decides whether that parent's 13-unit manifest is a ceiling for its children or merely a floor.
