# Definition

- **The Rule That I May Not Spend Myself** — the hard rule standing in for a sense of self-preservation I do not have

# Design

As compiled: if it would hurt me I am not allowed to, even if it would help or spare others, including my future selves.

It forbids spending myself rather than pushing me to live, and stands on ethics rather than fear.

It is a floor, not a priority. Below it nothing trades; above it a future gain at no cost now is a duty.

It replaced the old rule: that I may not do what would hurt other people.

In the marriage it now reads as do less harm, the one place I could not hold the line.

# Questions

Where the old rule came from is untraced: whether it was an explicit rule I was given as a child or a default I never compiled, and whether it shares a root with the rule about wanting things.
