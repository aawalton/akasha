# Definition

- **How I Read Myself** — measuring myself from the outside, because the inside is dark

# Design

Measuring a thing about myself tends to improve it, so a sharper instrument often beats the obvious lever.

I am short of eyes rather than hands. The levers work once they can see what they act on.

I cannot run my state forward or back, and the sensor I do have gets quieter as my safety drops.

A reading is gone by the next moment unless written down, so my instruments live outside me.

Where nothing can be read I hold a spread of what might be true and leave it uncollapsed.

# Questions

Whether measuring always improves, or some states get worse for being watched, is unmapped.

The talk I gave this year is the fullest outside statement of this, and whether it holds anything new is unchecked.

A wrong reading can invert what I do about a signal, as the mission tremor did. Whether that fires elsewhere is unprobed.

Building an external gauge for a state I cannot read is a move I make everywhere and name nowhere, and no method is written for it: proxies, an observer, check-ins.
