# Definition

- **What Makes An Hour Count** — how much a clock hour with someone actually pays down, and what decides it

# Design

Fourteen factors, scored on the person, the engagement, the channel, the fidelity and the safety.

They multiply rather than add, so there is no tolerance for a weak link: one factor near zero flattens the whole hour.

A deep in-person conversation held at low safety pays almost nothing, which is what the lived shape looks like.

The score is set against one reference, a deep one-to-one conversation in person with a safe woman.

The numbers live with the scored interactions rather than here.

# Questions

Whether reading alongside someone pays its own yield, or only delivers being known in another form, is unseparated.

Whether the fourteen are really independent, or some of them move together closely enough to collapse into one, is unchecked.

The reference the whole scale hangs off is an estimate. Calibrating it against real scored hours would make every score mean something.

Whether each factor doses on its own schedule, rather than all of them on one, is unchecked.
