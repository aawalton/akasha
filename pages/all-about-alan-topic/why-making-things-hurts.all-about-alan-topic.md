# Definition

- **Why Making Things Hurts** — an alarm that fires when I make something, and where it came from.

# Design

The wound is on making, not on playing. A bounded game with rules and a way to win does not fire it.

The alarm is two layers: a floor that is pure structure, and a charge above it that is a belief and can be dissolved.

The alarm concentrates on setting up, before anything begins, and peaks as the setup finishes.

Every criticism of my work lands as a criticism of me.

A judge I can neither escape nor deny installs and grades me from inside forever. One I can walk away from is gone.


# Questions

Whether building something physical sits in the inside-judge core or the outside-judge edge is the remaining probe.

Why the charge keys to finishing the setup rather than spreading across the making is unaccounted for, when aphantasia should make the pre-act moment cheap.

The graduated-dosing apparatus is scoped to connection and has never been brought here. No dose, no ceiling, no ladder.

Whether making things alongside the girls raises safety enough to quiet the alarm is untested.
