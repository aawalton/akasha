# Safety — intervention pathways and how it climbs

> Safety intervention pathways and how it climbs — the indirect integral pathway vs. the direct fast vagal pathway, the three climbing levers running at three clock speeds (capacity surplus, Jen relationship-repair, daily Health shock), a live reading of the model in operation, and the no-high-return-exemption rule.

*Part of [Safety](../safety.book-chapter.md) — the pathways Safety responds to and the levers that raise it.*

## Direct and indirect intervention pathways

Safety responds to two classes of interventions, distinguished by mechanism and time constant:

- **Indirect — the integral pathway.** Stress Capacity recovery feeds Safety through the time-integral. The current recovery stack ([meditative-breathing.md](../meditative-breathing.book-chapter.md), [sleep.md](../sleep.book-chapter.md), [hot-bath.md](../hot-bath.book-chapter.md)) is anchored to stress-capacity hours, not Safety hours. These pay Safety back slowly because the integral averages Stress Capacity over time; even at high recovery rates, the integral moves only so fast.
- **Direct — the fast pathway.** Vagal-stimulation interventions act on parasympathetic state on a much faster time scale than the integral. The recovery stack's modalities partially overlap with this class — the long-exhale phase of meditative breathing engages parasympathetic activation directly, and hot baths produce sympathetic withdrawal followed by parasympathetic rebound (see [hot-bath.md → mechanism](../hot-bath.book-chapter.md#mechanism-autonomic-shift-not-sweat-mediated-cortisol-excretion)). The decomposition of which fraction of the stack's effect runs through each pathway is open (see [how safety climbs](../../../../all-about-alan-topic/how-safety-climbs.all-about-alan-topic.md)).

The asymmetry between [aftershock dynamics](aftershock-dynamics.book-chapter.md#aftershock-dynamics-after-exposure-damage) and the integral-only pathway explains why the integral alone is insufficient under exposure conditions: an exposure event drops Safety in minutes; the integral restores it over days-to-weeks. A fast direct lever applied during or immediately after exposure could truncate the alarm signal before it fully encodes, shortening the aftershock tail.

Direct-lever candidates not yet in the recovery stack are tracked separately and not promoted to system files until tested (system inclusion criterion: "Alan has actually used it").

## How Safety climbs — three levers at three clock speeds

The [threshold gate](../safety.book-chapter.md#safety-is-a-threshold-gate-not-a-metered-budget) is protective: stay at or under the current level and the drain stops. But the gate doesn't *raise* the line. Holding the protective rule prevents loss; it doesn't by itself produce gain. Raising the Safety level comes from three layered inputs running at different speeds. In Alan's words: *"I have some models around this. Running a consistent capacity surplus over time seems to increase my normal safety range, so that's the best long-term lever. Improving my relationship health level with Jen makes a big impact as well on shorter time horizons. On the level of a single day, overspending will drop my health level."*

- **Slow — sustained capacity surplus (best long-term lever).** Running a consistent surplus of capacity over time raises his *normal Safety range*. This is the same integral pathway as above, read at the baseline-range scale; it is the strongest lever but the slowest.
- **Faster — relationship health with Jen.** Improving his relationship-health level with Jen makes a big impact on Safety, on shorter horizons than the surplus lever. This is the highest-fit connection channel; the relationship analysis lives at [central-loneliness.md](../central-loneliness.book-chapter.md) (the Jen conflict and the being-known center) and [connection.md](../connection.book-chapter.md). Here it registers only as a Safety lever.
- **Daily — overspending drops Health, not Safety.** On the scale of a single day, overspending drops his *Health* level. The daily clock lands on Health; the Safety range holds. Safety is not a single slow integral that everything feeds — the fast daily shock is absorbed by Health, while the Safety range moves on the slower lever timescales.

So Safety is layered inputs at different speeds, not one slow integral. The clock-speed *durations* of each lever — how long the surplus lever takes vs. the Jen channel — are still open (see [how safety climbs](../../../../all-about-alan-topic/how-safety-climbs.all-about-alan-topic.md)). But *which* lever is live right now is answered below.

### The model in operation — a live reading

A current-state datum that confirms the three-speed model running. In Alan's words: *"Yeah, I'm coming back up from a dip. I think I'm in the 2.5-3.5 range today. Mostly repairing relationship to move the needle at the moment."*

- **Where he is.** Safety today is in the **2.5–3.5 range** — about a level below the [~4.5 baseline, band [4.0, 5.0]](progression.book-chapter.md#progression-history), climbing back from a dip.
- **Which lever he's pulling.** In a dip he reaches for the **relationship-repair (Jen) lever** — the faster, hours-to-short-horizon channel — not the months-long capacity-surplus one. You reach for the lever that works fastest on the timescale of the problem, and a dip is a short-timescale problem. This is the layered model picking the speed that matches the need.

### The affordability rule has no high-return exemption

The repair lever is the highest-payback channel — but it does **not** get to spend at or above the threshold. In Alan's words: *"I try to keep the repair work affordable as well."*

Relationship repair is itself an activity with a difficulty level. From a 2.5–3.5, *real* repair can sit at or above his current Safety line — which by the strict [threshold gate](../safety.book-chapter.md#safety-is-a-threshold-gate-not-a-metered-budget) is exactly what he avoids. He does not deliberately spend at or above the threshold even for his most valuable lever; he keeps the repair work affordable too. So the affordability rule governs **everything**, the best channel included. There is no high-return exemption — the line holds for the lever that would pay back most, not just the marginal ones. The Jen channel's relationship analysis stays at [central-loneliness.md](../central-loneliness.book-chapter.md) and [connection.md](../connection.book-chapter.md); here it registers only as the live Safety lever and the proof that the rule has no exceptions.
