# Relational history

> Relational history — the chaste relational chronology and the involuntary attraction pattern under it: not naturally monogamous (no attachment glue), a constant involuntary auto-focus on the most feminine attractive person in view, simultaneous crushes, femininity as safety + physiology; the numbers (100+ first dates, ~6 seconds, 2 kisses); Kerry Shelton, Jenny Williams, and Jen (6 weeks meeting-to-married); the marriage decision as a worked example of the decision rubric; and the strict touch standards.

The chaste relational chronology, and the attraction shape under it. This is the lead-up that [sex-with-jen.md](sex-with-jen.book-chapter.md) follows from.

## The attraction shape

I am **not naturally monogamous,** which makes sense, since I [can't form emotional attachments](../love-decomposition.book-chapter.md#sorting-the-components--the-walk). There is no attachment glue binding me to one person, so monogamy is a chosen structure, not a felt default. (Attraction itself runs on [intact instinct](../love-decomposition.book-chapter.md#sorting-the-components--the-walk); it is the *attachment* that's missing, not the draw.)

The draw is loud and involuntary. **At all times, always, a significant fraction of my attention auto-focuses on the most feminine attractive person in my field of view.** This can be really inconvenient, but it doesn't seem to be voluntary. And it isn't singular: I get "crushes" on almost every girl I talk to who is at all feminine and attractive to me, all at the same time. What pulls is **femininity, because it feels safe and also aligns with my physiology** — the same cue set the [femininity-as-safety-signal](../femininity-as-safety-signal.book-chapter.md) and [two-channel attraction](../attraction-two-channels.book-chapter.md) notes model from the outside.

## The numbers

A few anchors carry the whole shape of my dating history:

- I went on first dates with **over 100 girls.**
- I went on second dates with about **six.**
- I've **kissed only two girls** in my life.
- I had **one girlfriend,** at 19.

## Kerry Shelton — the magical chaste first love

My very best friend from ages **6–8** was Kerry Shelton. I played with her almost every day, had sleepovers at her house — we were so close. Then she moved away to Oregon, and I assumed I'd never see her again, since that's what happened when my friends moved away.

Ten years later she came back, on her own, without her family. We were both 18, and we fell in love, and it was magical. We spent a ton of time talking, a good amount kissing, some cuddling, but stayed very chaste — the least chaste we ever were was a finger brush on the side of her breast once while cuddling, the night before I left for a semester-abroad program in Nauvoo, Illinois. We'd had a "Define The Relationship" conversation where we were clear it couldn't be permanent yet — I couldn't make any final commitments until after I served a [mission](../life-chronology.book-chapter.md#fire--working-adult-2040).

In Nauvoo, after some time away from the intensity of it, I read an apostle's article about how missionaries should leave with "no entanglements," girlfriends included. That night I called Kerry from the phone in the hall and broke up with her. It was one of the most painful conversations of my life, and she didn't understand at all. **Neither of us has communicated with the other since that night** — though I've thought about reaching out a few times more recently, as I've felt the desire for connection more in my life.

(The same night, a rowdy after-curfew party got the faculty cracking down, and I was swept up in it. Everyone up that night was asked to come in for consequences; I was the only one who came, and the only one who suffered — community service in the cafeteria for weeks. My grandfather directed the program, so they couldn't afford to show me any favoritism. The service repaired the program relationships; it never repaired the one with Kerry. Recorded brief — it's adjacent to the relationship, not its center.)

## Jenny Williams — fell hard, fully chaste

After the study abroad I went back to Nauvoo for nine months to work for my grandfather — he invited me on a whim with three hours' notice, and 30 minutes later I was gone. There I fell hard for a girl named Jenny Williams. We both held the same standard of no relationships before a mission, and I was about to leave, so we never went further than a few double dates and lots of conversations — **never held hands, never kissed.** But she told me the school she was planning to attend, and I made plans to join her there afterward.

I left on a mission at **20.** While I was gone, my companions all knew I was planning to go to school with Jenny Williams. The week before I went home, I got a letter from her: she'd changed her mind and wasn't going to be there. I didn't have any other plans, so I went anyway.

## Jen — six weeks from meeting to married

I got home from Russia just after midnight after 36+ hours of travel, was released as a missionary at noon, and my parents drove me to the new school (George Wythe College, Cedar City, UT) that day. After an evening seminar, my childhood best friend David Eggertsen — who'd found me an apartment — was introducing me around. The first person to come up was a cute girl who said, **"Hi, I'm supposed to meet you."** That was Jennifer Goff.

Four weeks later we went on our first date; a week after that we were committed; two days later engaged; five days later married. **Six weeks from the day we met to the day we were married, engaged for five days.** (My mission companions all assumed she was the same Jenny I'd planned to attend school with — which was funny.) My parents had dated a few months but were engaged only a week; her parents dated a week but were engaged a few months — we just took the short ends of both.

As fast as it was, and as much as others have questioned it, I still believe it was right and what both of us needed. She fit me and I fit her better than any of the 100 girls before — only Kerry and Jenny Williams came close. A big part of that was both of us being **AuDHD and not knowing it,** which is also why we came together so fast: we had a clarity most people don't get.

## The marriage decision — a worked example of the rubric

Each of us decided we were ready to commit in a non-standard way (not surprising, given the neurodiversity). For Jen, it was realizing that how concerned she was about hurting my feelings with her decision was *itself* evidence of how much she cared.

For me, I evaluated the relationship against a **[rubric](../decision-rubric.book-chapter.md), the way I do all important decisions.** It scored high on every dimension except physical attractiveness, which was medium. A line kept running through my head — *"If you want to be happy for the rest of your life, never make a pretty woman your wife. Learn from my personal point of view, choose another woman to marry you."* I don't know where it's from or whether I'm quoting it right, but it stuck. I decided I did **not** want to be the kind of person who lets physical attractiveness have a **veto** when choosing an eternal companion. External beauty is temporary and the least important thing about a person, so I moved forward. Looking back, I can tell I *was* feeling the infatuation crush — and that datum matters: **the crush / infatuation system does work normally for me,** a contrast to the flat affect elsewhere — but the decision was still made on reason, not on the crush.

## The touch standards, and the omens

Our standards for physical touch were very strict. We held hands early, near when we first started dating. We didn't kiss until we were engaged, and even that was a chaste kiss. We didn't go further than cuddling until after we were married. (The wedding night onward is in [sex-with-jen.md](sex-with-jen.book-chapter.md).)

So many miracles happened on the way, and [omens — the right thing at the right time with a precision that doesn't match probability](../decision-rubric.book-chapter.md) — are one of the ways I get revelation. Despite the five-day engagement, we had a ring (gifted by my paternal grandfather), a temple wedding with my maternal grandfather getting permission from the church's First Presidency to officiate, and — after four fruitless hours of apartment-hunting — a man in a blood-donation line overheard Jen's roommates, turned around, and offered us his mother's basement at a price that was almost nothing. Every one of those five days brought new miracles. Recorded compact: the faith reading of the omens is its own [thread](../sexuality-knot.book-chapter.md).
