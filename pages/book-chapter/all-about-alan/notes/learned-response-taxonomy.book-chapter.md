# Learned-response taxonomy

> Two kinds of learned response — belief-mediated responses that can be rewritten top-down by changing the founding belief (behavior changes immediately), and pre-cognitive somatic conditioned responses where the body fires before cognition and insight cannot reach, which require systematic exposure rather than belief change. The taxonomy explains why understanding a mechanism fixes some of Alan's patterns and not others, and states the general behavior-change mechanism — behavior is downstream of the conceptual map, so an abstract framework moves nothing until it yields a concrete application integrated into the map.

Alan's conditioned responses split into **two classes**, and the split decides which remedy works. Getting the class wrong wastes effort: insight applied to a response that runs below cognition does nothing, and exposure applied to a response that a belief drives is the slow way around a fast door.

## Class 1 — belief-mediated responses

A belief-mediated response runs **through** a belief. There is a behavior, the behavior traces to a founding experience and the belief that experience installed, and the belief is what produces the behavior. The causal chain is `experience → belief → want → behavior`.

Because the chain runs through the belief, it is reachable from the top. Trace the behavior back to the founding experiences and the underlying belief, **rewrite the belief**, and the behavior changes **immediately** — top-down. This is exactly the move of [Emotional Archaeology](emotional-archaeology.book-chapter.md): excavate backward from the disapproved behavior to the origin experience, replace the belief with one that explains the same evidence more compactly, and observe the new behavior follow. The [giving pattern](emotional-archaeology.book-chapter.md#the-worked-example--the-childhood-needs-belief) is the worked case — about a **90% success rate** when the response is genuinely belief-mediated.

The reason it can be fast: nothing fires before the belief gets consulted. The belief is upstream of the response, so changing the belief changes the response on the next occasion.

### Behavior is downstream of the conceptual map — an abstract framework cannot move it

The general statement of the Class-1 chain, in Alan's words: *"in general, I need concrete applications in my conceptual map to change my behavior."* An abstract framework, however correct, does **not** change his behavior on its own. Behavior changes only once a **concrete application is integrated into the conceptual map** — the same map-compression event that produces meaning and holds the life-guardrail ([meaning-from-clarity.md](meaning-from-clarity.book-chapter.md); [discrete-self.md → it is a conclusion, not a feeling](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#it-is-a-conclusion-not-a-feeling--and-not-a-reason-to-live), where the integrated conclusion is what stands rather than a thought re-run each time). The lever is the map, not exhortation or abstract assent: until a framework yields a concrete application that lands in the map, there is no `belief → want → behavior` chain for it to run through, so it changes nothing.

This is exactly why his [Axiomatic Ethics framework](axiomatic-ethics.book-chapter.md) has not yet changed how he lives — and why its [bridge out to a bounded mind without the oracles](axiomatic-ethics/dilemmas-and-open-questions.book-chapter.md) is the live work. The framework is oracle-assuming and abstract; it has not yet produced a concrete application that integrates into the map, so it sits as a correct-but-inert framework rather than a behavior-driver. The behavior-change mechanism names the bottleneck: the framework cannot move his behavior until it yields a concrete, integrated application.

Not every belief-mediated case is fast to *fix*, though, because the belief can be **true.** The [only-perfection-is-safe](perfection-is-safe.book-chapter.md) dig is the canonical Class-1 case where the founding belief is an accurate compression of the childhood Alan had — he really was punished despite good intent. The chain still runs `experience → belief → want → behavior`, so it is Class 1 and the response is in principle belief-reachable; but step 4 (replace the belief) is hard precisely because there is no falsehood to correct, only a true-but-costly belief to re-compress. Class 1 names *which lever fits* (belief, not exposure); it does not promise the lever is easy to pull when the belief is earned.

## Class 2 — pre-cognitive somatic conditioned responses

A pre-cognitive response does **not** run through a belief. The body fires before cognition runs at all. There is no upstream belief to rewrite, because the response completes before the conceptual layer that would hold a belief even loads.

The anchor case is the [criticism alarm](criticism-and-ostracism.book-chapter.md): criticism (and the whole compressed bucket of pushback, argument, complaint) trips a survival alarm below conscious thought. Knowing the truth — *she's only wrestling* — arrives too late, because the alarm has already fired by the time the knowing is available. Insight **cannot reach it.** Understanding the mechanism does not disarm a response that completes before understanding can run.

What reaches a Class-2 response is **systematic exposure therapy** — repeated, regulated re-encounters with the feared stimulus under conditions safe enough that the nervous system re-learns the cue as non-threatening. The belief lever does nothing here; the exposure lever is the one that fits. The forward design for Alan's criticism alarm specifically is [regulated-anchor exposure](regulated-anchor-exposure.book-chapter.md).

But classic exposure is not the only Class-2 route, and for one cluster Alan **rejects** it. The [five still-active survival rules](perfection-is-safe/origin.book-chapter.md#renegotiation-status--five-still-active-two-broken) are Class-2 (belief won, behaviors still fire below cognition), yet standard somatic disconfirmation — *"you asked for something and were fine"* — doesn't take, for the same reason [random social punishment can't be extinguished](#a-second-axis--does-a-response-fade-with-repetition-and-by-which-machine): a handful of safe re-encounters can't disconfirm a schedule that punished at random. The route he uses instead is **surplus-gated affordability-re-testing** — expand only when he has a surplus of social capacity, read whether the expansion is actually affordable, and re-ask "is it safe now?", holding *dangerous* as **provisionally true** rather than asserting it was never dangerous. It is not belief-change and not extinction; it is re-running the standing experiment under survivable conditions as capacity grows. Full mechanism: [surplus-gated graduated testing](recovery-ladder.book-chapter.md#surplus-gated-graduated-testing--how-a-social-behavior-actually-shifts). The quantitative account of *why* re-testing under surplus can thread a random schedule at all — raise the Safety center so the schedule's breaches fall below the line, turning would-be reinforcement into disconfirmation — is the [extinction model](safety/extinction-model.book-chapter.md).

The [five still-active childhood survival rules](perfection-is-safe/origin.book-chapter.md#renegotiation-status--five-still-active-two-broken) — *be nice, be quiet, don't take up space, don't ask for anything, take care of yourself* — are a second Class-2 instance, and the cleanest demonstration of why the class matters. Their founding verdict (*my needs aren't important*) has already been [worked and replaced](emotional-archaeology.book-chapter.md#the-worked-example--the-childhood-needs-belief): the belief-argument is **won.** Yet the five rules keep firing — because they run from the same pre-cognitive layer the criticism alarm does, below where the corrected belief sits. Winning the belief did not switch them off, which is the Class-2 signature exactly: a response that does not run through the belief is not reached by changing the belief.

### A conditioned reflex, not a prediction

The Class-2 signature is easy to mis-state as *anticipation* — the body bracing because it forecasts judgment. It is not. A Class-2 response is a **post-traumatic conditioned reflex**: the nervous system fires the old pattern because past judgment wired it to, with **no cognitive prediction** running first — no thought *"someone will judge me"* required. It is a reflex installed by the past, not a forecast of the future, which is exactly why it can fire below cognition (there is no prediction step to run) and why insight at the surface can't reach it.

The [creativity stress response](creativity-scars.book-chapter.md#the-step-1-behaviors--the-behaviors-not-the-belief) is the worked confirmation, settled by both Alan and Abby. Alan's correction, which walked back an earlier "anticipatory" framing: *"I don't get a stress response because I am thinking or feeling that someone will judge me. I get a stress response because that's how my nervous system responds, and it responds that way because of judgement in the past."* Abby endorsed and adopted the name: *"Post-traumatic stress response is exactly the right name, precisely because it's a conditioned reflex, not a prediction: no thought 'someone will judge me' required — the nervous system just fires the old pattern."*

This is the [guard-moved-inside](perfection-is-safe/origin.book-chapter.md#the-pain-correction--the-guard-moved-inside) reflex read from the response side: once the punisher is internal, the response needs neither a present judge nor an anticipated one — it fires from the installed pattern alone. So the diagnostic for a conditioned reflex is not "does he expect to be judged" but "does the body fire on the bare cue, unseen, with no judge forecast at all" — yes for the scarred creative act.

## A second axis — does a response fade with repetition, and by which machine

The belief-vs-exposure fork above decides *which lever fixes* a response. A separate question is *whether a response fades on its own with repeated exposure* — and here Alan's long-held one-dial story ("I don't habituate to anything") broke under his own counterexamples. The fade-or-not behavior is not one dial. It is **at least four distinct machines**, and only one of them earns the word "habituation."

1. **Sensory non-habituation** — reserve "habituation" for *direct sensory acclimation only*. Most brains fold a constant background into baseline and stop attending to it; Alan's does not. Background noise never fades. He can mask it by [spending attention elsewhere](non-habituation.book-chapter.md) — when there's no room left, the sound has nowhere to land — but it returns the moment he has attention to spare. This is the machine the word names, and it's the one Alan lacks. Full mechanism: [non-habituation.md](non-habituation.book-chapter.md).
2. **Change-detection** — a separate alarm entirely, and it works fine. At sleep he runs white noise; if it *stops*, the change alone wakes him. Responding to a change in the field is not sensory acclimation — a system can detect change while never habituating to the steady state.
3. **Reward-novelty decay** — different machine again, and it also works. A song's impact wears off with repeated listens; it stops surprising him. The reward gets marked down with familiarity. (This is the same decay [non-habituation.md → the reward side](non-habituation.book-chapter.md#the-reward-side--a-gift-where-the-conflict-is-a-cost) says *doesn't* happen for a still-rewarding **activity** — the distinction is novelty-of-a-fixed-stimulus, which decays, vs. ongoing reward from an unchanged activity, which doesn't.)
4. **Extinction — not habituation.** The first ESO PVP death flooded him: rage-quit a week, then a few days, then a day, then fine. His nervous system **relearned that PVP death isn't a real threat.** This is not the stimulus fading; it is the *meaning* of the event being disconfirmed. And it has a hard precondition — extinction works **only when the dreaded thing doesn't actually happen.** He "died" in PVP repeatedly and nothing real was ever lost, so the alarm got disconfirmed and stood down.

The payoff of separating the machines: **"put yourself out there and you'll toughen up" is not just useless for rejection, it's backwards.** Extinction needs the feared thing to *not* arrive. On Alan's mission, every rejection *was* the dreaded thing actually happening — ostracism in the flesh, [~10,000 times in six months](criticism-and-ostracism.book-chapter.md#rejection-cant-extinguish--each-instance-is-reinforcement), the threat coming true at every door. Living the catastrophe on repeat is **reinforcement, not extinction**, so the last rejection hurt exactly as much as the first. That is the mechanism behind the non-habituation of rejection pain specifically — not a missing dial, but the one alarm exposure can only ever prove right. Full treatment in [criticism-and-ostracism.md](criticism-and-ostracism.book-chapter.md#rejection-cant-extinguish--each-instance-is-reinforcement).

## Why the taxonomy matters

It explains a pattern Alan had no account for: **understanding the mechanism mitigates some of his patterns and not others.** Mechanism-knowledge reduces the alarm of a [flop response](stress-responses.book-chapter.md#flop-in-detail) and dissolves a belief-mediated giving compulsion — but it does nothing for the criticism alarm. The taxonomy says why. Mechanism-knowledge works where the response runs through cognition (Class 1, and the [alarm-reducer effect](stress-responses.book-chapter.md#flop-in-detail) where knowing recontextualizes a sensation). It fails where the response completes before cognition (Class 2). Same intervention, opposite results, because the two classes have different wiring.

The two classes are not a strict partition of every response — some sit in between, and the [mechanism-knowledge-as-alarm-reducer](stress-responses.book-chapter.md) effect is a third thing again (it changes how the cognitive layer *interprets* a sensation rather than rewriting a belief or re-conditioning a reflex). But for choosing a remedy, the belief-vs-exposure fork is the load-bearing distinction.
