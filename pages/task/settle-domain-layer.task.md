# Definition

- **Settle domain layer** — one domain's children: what they are, what each is called, and the order they stand in.

# Loop

1. **The domain in front of you, read from the tree he reads.**
   - **Take** which domain that is from the Intent entry on your own persona's document. Several walks run at once, one to a persona, and the same entry on a domain would be read at every boot by every agent beneath it rather than by the one walking.
   - **Ask** `ops domain champions --tree --json` for its children, and work from those alone. It stands each domain in the one place its parent puts it, and that is the view Alan reads, so it is the tree the layer is being settled in.
   - **Pull** the one node out of the object rather than printing the tree, which draws every domain there is and buries a six-line layer inside the whole tree.
   - **Read** each child whole before proposing anything about it. One read as an orphan wanting a home until its own line said it was a kind of domain.

2. **The layer, settled with him before anything is created.**
   - **Take** his list and his glosses as they stand, and land nothing he has not seen.
   - **Count** what his list leaves out, against the children the tree reports. Five buckets for one domain missed the central concept, six other children, thirty-eight properties, and a domain he had created himself an hour earlier.
   - **Say** which leftover stops you. A layer proposed without the thing every one of its members is about is not the layer below it.

3. **The lines, drafted as a family in one frame.**
   - **Write** every sibling in the frame the parent sets, so the narrowing each makes is the only thing that differs between them.
   - **Report** where the frame breaks rather than forcing it. One of seven asked a question rather than keeping track of anything, and breaking the pattern was the right line.
   - **Test** each pair for containment. Two glosses where one is a special case of the other are a parent and a child about to be landed as peers.

4. **The move, priced against the chain of required reading before it lands.**
   - **Ask** `ops domain champions --path <file>` what is required for a document before moving it, and read that against what the move would leave. A move that inserts a parent above the old one is additive; one that replaces it drops every document that stood above.
   - **Refuse** a subtractive move until you have named what leaves. Dropping one edge took a person's whole document out of every agent's boot, and the only thing that reported it was a test asserting the chain byte for byte.
   - **Carry** what leaves to somewhere its readers still reach, rather than taking the loss quietly.

5. **The landing, one call per kind.**
   - **Create** every new parent before anything names it. A reparent naming a slug that stands nowhere yet is refused, so two commits are the order rather than a failure.
   - **Use** `ops write` for new domains, `edit --mechanical` for reparents and sequences, `mv` for a slug rename, `rm` for a removal. An array is one act in each of them.
   - **Rename** the term in a Definition together with the slug. `mv` moves the slug and refuses to guess at the sentence, so a name moved alone lands a line that is false rather than stale.

6. **The definitions, read back as a layer once it stands.**
   - **Read** every child's Definition off the layer as it now stands, rather than off the draft you proposed. Children arrive from different parents, and a line that narrowed the old one narrows nothing under this one.
   - **Show** him the whole set at once rather than a line at a time, so the layer is judged as a family. A line reads as fine on its own and as a duplicate beside the sibling it overlaps.
   - **Test** each line against the parent's. A child that does not narrow its parent is either naming the parent again or standing in the wrong layer.

7. **The required reading, rebuilt against what the walk cannot deliver.**
   - **Name** in `required-reading-slugs:` only the terms a reader beneath would misread the domain without. Each entry arrives whole and counts as read, so every reader beneath pays for it at every boot.
   - **Cut** any term the reader already meets walking up, which `ops domain champions --path` will tell you. The chain delivers a reader's own ancestors, so an entry naming one buys nothing and dilutes what stands beside it.
   - **Keep** the term standing in one branch and wanted in the others. Reaching sideways is the whole of what required reading does, the walk having only the one direction.
   - **Stop** at what a reader gets wrong rather than at what belongs to the subject. A hundred and eighty-three entries were stripped here for enumerating a domain's vocabulary instead.

8. **The cursor, advanced before you stop.**
   - **Rewrite** that entry on your persona to name the next domain whose children are not settled yet, the moment the one it names is settled, and delete the entry once nothing beneath that domain is left to expand. One name stands there at a time, so an entry outliving its layer sends the next run at work already done, per [Resolve When Found](../page-body-section/domain-intent.page-body-section.md#resolve-when-found).
   - **Take** that next name from the `sequence-slugs:` of the domain above, and drop to the first of the layer below only once every domain in this one is settled. The walk never climbs above the domain your persona champions, that domain being the whole of what this walk covers.
   - **Say** where the walk stands in your last words to him, so the next run starts from a cursor rather than from a re-read.

# Invariants

- **The shape is his and the words are yours.** He holds the model the tree is being shaped from, so every count, every name and every edge is his to rule on. The drafting, the pricing and the landing are yours.

- **A move that shrinks a chain of required reading is a regression until you have said what left.** Nothing reports a rule that stopped applying, and the document reads correctly from both ends afterwards.
