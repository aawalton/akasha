# Definition

- **Run naming exercise** — putting ten fresh names for one idea in front of whoever chooses, beside the name it carries now.

# Sequence

1. **The idea, said without its name.**
   - **Write** one sentence for what the thing is, using none of the words in the name it carries now. The ten you draft next are only as wide as this sentence, and one written with the old word inside it returns ten spellings of that word.

2. **Ten names.**
   - **Give** ten, rather than the three or four that arrive before the drafting gets hard. A field of three hands the standing name the win by default, and denying it that is the whole of the exercise.
   - **Draft** the ten without the standing name in front of you, and without any near-variant of it. A name read a hundred times reads as correct whatever it says, so drafting beside it returns ten spellings of it.
   - **Write** each name in the plainest word that carries it, one the reader already uses in the sense you mean. A word that has to be explained was chosen for its sound, and it sounds best to whoever just drafted it. Say it out loud in a sentence about the thing: a word you would not speak is not the plainest one.

3. **The list, handed over.**
   - **Put** the standing name back in beside the ten, as an eleventh that can win. Standing earned it nothing, but a name that is already right loses nothing by being drafted against, and barring it makes every exercise a rename.
   - **Say** in one line what each name claims the thing is, so the choice runs between claims rather than between sounds.
   - **Give** your own pick after the eleven rather than instead of them. The choice belongs to the reader, and holding a view back spends their turn on work you could have done.
