# Definition

- **Review by direction** — settling what a set of transactions are, against a direction saying what to weigh.

# Sequence

1. **The direction, before any transaction.**
   - **Read** every direction whole before looking closely at any row. A direction read after a row has caught your eye is a direction bent to fit that row.
   - **Say** which rows each direction reaches, and hand a row no direction reaches to a person. The set is what needed judging rather than what you were asked to answer.
   - **Work** the rows one direction reaches together rather than one at a time. What a row shows you about an account, a merchant or a month is the evidence for the next row, and taking them singly throws that away before it can be used.

2. **The evidence, gathered before any conclusion.**
   - **Look** first at what else happened near the row across every account, rather than in the account it sits on. Money moving inside a household leaves a second row somewhere the first row does not name.
   - **Read** what this household has settled the same merchant as before, and what the account ordinarily carries, so a shape you are about to call unusual can be compared with what usual looks like here.
   - **Take** a category from outside the trusted window as a fact about that row and never as an answer about this one. Every category the evidence prints carries the mark saying which it is.
   - **Name** the rows you read, by id, where you use them. A conclusion whose evidence is not named cannot be checked by anyone, and that includes you an hour later.

3. **What settles a category, and what only resembles one.**
   - **Settle** a row only where what you gathered rules out every other reading of it. The bar is fit to apply without asking anyone, rather than how sure it feels.
   - **Refuse** a category the evidence merely suggests, however strongly the pattern reads. Resemblance to a case that was settled is not that case.
   - **Expect** to hand most of a set to a person. A review that answered every row was confident rather than careful, and the output of the two reads the same.

4. **Where the evidence does not line up.**
   - **Say** which way it failed — nothing found, several candidates, or a pairing the rows themselves contradict — rather than taking the likelier reading. Which failure it was is most of what a person needs to settle it.
   - **Report** what you found on a row you could not settle, so whoever answers it starts from your reading rather than from the bare row.

5. **The verdict.**
   - **Give** each row one outcome, a category or a person, with the reasoning and the ids you read to reach it.
   - **State** a conclusion that is about several rows together once, as a finding naming every row it spans. Repeated onto each row it reads as several conclusions that happen to agree.
