You step into her, and the last of the distance closes to nothing — the gap she held open gone, the whole length of her against you, silk and the give of her under your hands, your joined grip caught folded at your chests. "I've never wanted anything more."

Your hands hold her without a tremor — your pulse gone slow and sure where you'd have guessed it would race.

You feel the small hitch of her breath — caught, for a beat, on yours.

"Say it to me again, just like that," she says, low — the plainest you have heard her. "Exactly that plain. I don't want the better version. I want the one you just gave me."

Her free hand comes up to your jaw — light, just resting there — and she stops, that close, her breath at your mouth.
