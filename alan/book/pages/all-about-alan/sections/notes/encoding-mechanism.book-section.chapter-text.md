
# Encoding mechanism

> Unified NS-familiarization encoding mechanism underlying Investment (positive-valence) and trauma-conditioning (negative-valence). Same physiological substrate; output sign determined by Safety state at encoding time.

Two phenomena that look unrelated — repeated practice making an activity cheaper to do (Investment), and repeated exposure making a stimulus a trauma cue — share one substrate. The mechanism is **NS-level familiarization through repeated pairing**. The sign of the encoded association is determined by the Safety state at encoding time, not by the mechanism itself.

## Mechanism

Repeated pairing of a stimulus with a state encodes a physiological association at the nervous-system level. The pairing accumulates over events; once the encoding lands, the stimulus retrieves the associated state on subsequent presentation, independent of the original context. This is the same substrate Alan's trigger-based memory architecture uses for cue → concept retrieval — see [trigger-based-memory.md](trigger-based-memory.book-chapter.md). The cue here is the paired stimulus; the retrieval is the physiological state.

## Valence is determined by Safety state at encoding

The encoding mechanism is sign-neutral. What sign the encoded association carries depends on the Safety state of the nervous system at the time of the pairing.

- **High-Safety / low-cost pairing → cost-reducing familiarization.** Repeated exposure under regulated conditions encodes the stimulus as low-cost. Cost-per-event drops on subsequent presentations; the same activity becomes cheaper to do. This is **Investment** — the cost-multiplier modulator named in [interest-modulator.md → Passion split](interest-modulator.book-chapter.md#passion-split-in-alans-architecture).
- **Low-Safety / high-cost pairing → aversive conditioning.** Repeated exposure during a destabilized nervous-system state encodes the stimulus as a threat cue. Subsequent presentations retrieve the threat response on partial cue match. This is **trauma**. Full mechanism root in [trauma-encoding.md](trauma-encoding.book-chapter.md).

The two pathways are the same physiological process running on opposite signs. The conventional framing treats trauma and skill-acquisition as separate topics; the substrate says they aren't.

## Intensity-threshold asymmetry

Negative stimuli more easily pass the intensity threshold required to trigger encoding. Positive encoding can happen but typically requires higher cumulative intensity or volume to clear the same threshold. The asymmetry is a survival-biased nervous-system feature — a single dangerous event needs to encode immediately; a single safe event does not need to encode at all. It is not Alan-specific. It lands particularly heavily for Alan because of compounding mechanisms below.

## Operational consequence — the 5000-iteration anchor decomposes into two drivers

Alan's slow-build break-even against neurotypicals lands at roughly 5000 iterations ([slow-build-strategy.md → the 5000-iteration anchor](slow-build-strategy.book-chapter.md#the-5000-iteration-anchor)). With the encoding mechanism named, the long horizon decomposes into two compounding drivers:

- **Driver 1 (primary): aphantasia-driven lack of statistical learning capacity.** The implicit accelerator other people get from System B isn't available ([automaticity-systems.md → System B](automaticity-systems.book-chapter.md#b-implicit-social-statistical-learning--reduced)). Acquisition runs on the deliberate-effort pathway. This affects learning rate even when exposure intensity is high.
- **Driver 2 (secondary): positive-encoding threshold asymmetry.** Long cumulative exposure is required to clear the threshold for positive-valence encoding. Even when the deliberate pathway is producing per-iteration value, the underlying familiarization needs time to accumulate.

Driver 1 dominates the slope; Driver 2 widens the horizon. The slow-build strategy works on both — it accumulates iterations past the deliberate-pathway crossover and past the positive-encoding threshold at the same time.

## Offline rehearsal counts as exposure

Iterations don't have to come from live experience. Offline conceptual processing of a stimulus or skill — mental rehearsal, physical drill out-of-context, written rehearsal — counts as repeated pairing for the familiarization substrate. The encoding substrate doesn't distinguish "rehearsed pairing" from "lived pairing" sharply; both feed the same familiarization counter.

Operational consequence on the Investment side: deliberately rehearsing a positive pattern offline contributes to clearing the positive-encoding threshold. "Think about it a lot in a safe state → the substrate familiarizes → the in-situation cost drops on next live exposure" runs on the same encoding mechanism as live high-Safety pairing.

This is the substrate-level statement of the operating-mode architecture's offline-rehearsal pathways. The substrate is sign-neutral and source-neutral; the runtime architecture sits on top. See [operating-mode-architecture.md → offline rehearsal pathways](operating-mode-architecture.book-chapter.md#offline-rehearsal-pathways--how-the-weight-layer-trains).

## What writes to the conceptual map — write-selection axes

Everything above is the **affective** write path: NS-familiarization writes a physiological association by repeated pairing. There is a second, separate write path — the one that updates the [present-tense conceptual model](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md). The conceptual model has no episodic store; what reaches it is selected, and the selection is governed by **several axes of write probability**:

- **Repeatability** — how often the same pattern recurs.
- **Interconnection** — how many other things in the model it links to.
- **Explanatory payoff** — how much it accounts for.
- **Emotion** — the affective charge at encoding.

The axes are not equal. In Alan's words: *"Emotion is mostly quiet, strong emotions are rare, but they do have a bigger impact when they happen. However, the explanatory value is likely the strongest of the dimensions."* So **emotion is the weakest contributor in practice** — it spikes the write probability when a strong emotion fires, but strong emotions are rare, so across the run it accounts for little. **Explanatory value is the strongest.** This is the conceptual-channel parallel to the affective channel's [intensity-threshold asymmetry](#intensity-threshold-asymmetry): there, a negative stimulus's intensity is what clears the write threshold; here, an experience's explanatory payoff is what clears it.

### Biography writes low

Life-events and autobiographical episodes score on the **lower side** of the write axes — Alan: *"I don't know that I would say least-privileged, but its definitely on the lower side."* The mechanism: a one-off episode loses on three of the four axes at once — it doesn't repeat (low repeatability), it links to little (low interconnection), and it accounts for little beyond itself (low explanatory payoff). So it writes low **even when emotion is high**, because emotion is the weakest axis and can't carry the write alone.

The consequence is structural: **"Alan" is built mostly out of his best explanations.** An experience earns its slot in the model by how much it accounts for, not by how much it happened to him. Stated as the inversion it is — he is, in effect, *a theory of the world that happens to include itself.* The autobiography is a thin layer; the bulk of the self is the compressed structure of what he has judged true.

The keystone framing, confirmed by Alan ("Yeah, that sounds right"): the inner write-system isn't broken — it is **lossy by design, tuned for explanatory compression, not for keeping his life.** Biography and the derivations behind beliefs both get compressed away because the system optimizes for the most compact account of the world, not for preserving the record of how that account was built. The downstream consequence — beliefs going authorless and staying trustworthy anyway because the audit is outsourced to a wide input stream — lives in [broad-learning.md → outsourced audit](broad-learning.book-chapter.md#outsourced-audit--trusting-the-collision-rate).

### Anomalies write high — the explanatory-debt mechanism

The key inversion is that **anomalies write high, not low** — the opposite of what "one-off, doesn't repeat" would predict. Alan: *"No, anomalies actually have explanatory value, since the memory of the anomaly is the only explanation for the anomaly itself until my models expand to explain it. Compressing anomalies is a driver of explanatory value for model improvements."*

The mechanism is precise. An unexplained-but-real datum **is its own placeholder.** The model can't yet account for it, so the raw memory of the anomaly is the *only available explanation* for the anomaly — it stands in for the structure that doesn't exist yet. That gives it high explanatory value by default: it carries an **explanatory debt**, and it writes high precisely because the debt is unpaid. An anomaly is a write the model is forced to keep because it can't yet absorb it.

This is the [compression](compression-epistemology.book-chapter.md) operation seen from the write side: the incompressible residue that drives a model toward a better fit is exactly the high-priority write. The compression-side treatment — including the consequence that **understanding is how you forget** — lives in [compression-epistemology.md → anomalies write high](compression-epistemology/mechanics.book-chapter.md#anomalies-write-high--the-explanatory-debt-mechanism).
