
# Autism comorbidities

- Mechanism hypothesis: autism is primarily changes in the protein pathways controlling the regulatory nervous system, so people with autism have an elevated risk of a broad swath of nervous-system disorders.
- As much as autism itself profoundly influences the course of someone's life, the comorbidities often have an even more pronounced impact — and they are the one tier Alan would *fix*: the **physical tier** in [models-of-disability.md](models-of-disability.book-chapter.md), consequences whose repair lives in the body and does not touch the person, as against the neurotype itself, which he refuses to change.

## Alan's profile — triaged comorbidities

Triage of common autism-comorbidity candidates against Alan's experience. Each "apply" entry is captured here at first-pass depth; deeper probes deferred to follow-up chunks.

### Apply

- **ANS disorders, not POTS.** Various autonomic-nervous-system disorders apply, but not POTS specifically. Body-temperature dysregulation is one specific entry now captured in [thermoregulation.md](thermoregulation.book-chapter.md); other ANS specifics deferred.
- **Sleep — partial.** Sleep is hard sometimes. Not a constant or severe disorder; the onset / duration / fragmentation pattern is now captured in [sleep.md](sleep.book-chapter.md).
- **Anxiety / depression — secondary.** Mostly stress-induced rather than primary mood disorders. Mechanism: stress capacity overload → mood symptoms, not primary mood disorder → stress. Implication: the intervention surface is stress capacity (see [nervous-system-sensitivity.md](nervous-system-sensitivity.book-chapter.md), [autism-burnout.md](autism-burnout.book-chapter.md), and the four-resource framework in [four-resource-model.md](four-resource-model.book-chapter.md)) rather than mood-targeted treatment as primary.
- **OCD — mild.** Specifics deferred.
- **Eating issues — mild ARFID + ADHD-med appetite interaction.** Some ARFID-leaning patterns. ADHD medication suppresses appetite, so there are periods of just not eating; not a classical body-image or restrictive-eating disorder. The operational complement — the small set of foods that holds when everything is low — is [safe-foods.md](safe-foods.book-chapter.md).
- **Immune dysfunction — possibly, via allostatic load.** Working hypothesis: immune-system activation contributes to allostatic load, and Alan's symptoms come from the allostatic load rather than from direct autoimmune attack on tissue. This is mechanistically distinct from a classical autoimmune comorbidity (where the immune system attacks the self directly) — the immune activation is real, but the symptoms route through the regulatory nervous system rather than through direct tissue damage.
- **Alexithymia — double-dose.** Comorbidity of *both* autism (via non-habituation: emotional patterns don't get implicitly habituated) *and* aphantasia (via lack of emotional memory and imagination: no internal emotional reference points to label). Canonical home: [alexithymia.md](alexithymia.book-chapter.md).
- **Trauma encoding / CPTSD — multiple dimensions.** Applies across multiple dimensions. Mechanism treated at the sensitivity × aphantasia root in [trauma-encoding.md](trauma-encoding.book-chapter.md); personal trauma dimensions stay personal and are not enumerated there.
- **ADHD.** Co-occurs at high rates with autism. In this book ADHD is treated as a separate neurotype ([adhd-as-neurotype.md](adhd-as-neurotype.book-chapter.md)) and at the AuDHD intersection rather than as a downstream comorbidity, but it appears on every clinical comorbidity list and the high co-occurrence is itself mechanism-suggestive.

### Don't apply

- GI disorders.
- Chronic pain syndromes (fibromyalgia, ME/CFS).
- Migraines.
- Tinnitus / hyperacusis / misophonia.
- Tics / Tourette's.

### Family history, not personal

- **EDS / hypermobility.** Alan has not noticed it personally. His mother is diagnosed with EDS. Worth flagging given the autism × EDS literature.

### Framing uncertain

- **Sensory processing disorder (SPD).** Alan is unsure what SPD means as a category beyond "sensory issues in general", which all autistics have to some degree. The diagnostic boundary between SPD and the sensory dimension of autism itself is not clear here. Sensory experience is canonically captured in [sensory-experience.md](sensory-experience.book-chapter.md); whether the SPD label adds anything beyond that is an open framing question, not an apply/don't-apply question.
