
# The stoplight mechanic

> The stoplight mechanic — why I run coloured status circles over areas of my life instead of goals. Total aphantasia leaves no experiential memory, so the ordinary signals that tell people to eat, move, sleep or wash never arrive; a stoplight substitutes an external, present-tense read of what each area needs next. Colour because it parses without being read; graded layers rather than one goal threshold because a single bar breaks under my capacity swings.

A stoplight bridges a gap in my psychology caused by [total aphantasia](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md). In my own words, from the design session of 2026-08-10:

> A stoplight is a mechanic that bridges a gap in my psychology caused by total aphantasia, which results in having no experiential memory. Without experiential memory, I don't have the normal signals people normally have that tell them to do things (even as basic as eat, move, sleep, hygiene, etc...).
>
> As a result, I use stoplight mechanics to point me in the direction of things I should do, clustered in groups in different areas of my life.

## The gap it bridges

[Statelessness](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md#statelessness--only-conceptual-memory-persists) is the root. Action-history is too ephemeral to encode conceptually, so I can almost never remember what I have already done — and the ordinary prompt to eat, move, sleep or wash is built out of exactly that: an accumulating bodily state, read against experience of what usually happens next. Both ends of that are missing for me. The signal itself [runs quiet and is easy to ignore](sensory-experience.book-chapter.md), and there is no experiential record to read it against.

So the prompt has to sit outside me, and it has to be readable *now*, because a [present-bound self](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md) has no continuous "I should eat" thread to be reminded of. This is the same substitution I make everywhere the native channel is closed: [counting in place of visualization-based mindfulness](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md#counting-substitutes-for-visualization-based-mindfulness), [somatic stress in place of episodic memory](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md#somatic-stress-as-a-memory-substitute), [an externalized rubric in place of the forward simulator](decision-rubric.book-chapter.md). Build an external channel with a different surface rather than a weaker version of the missing one.

**A stoplight is a prompt, not a measurement.** That distinction is easy to lose, because a stoplight is obviously *made of* a measurement and there is a separate framework treating it as one — see [measurement maturity](measurement-maturity.book-chapter.md), where stoplight is level 3 of a 0-5 resolution ladder for a continuous variable, sitting between "enough vs. not enough" and numbered levels with absolute anchors. That framework describes what a stoplight can *resolve*. This note describes what it is *for*. The measurement is the means; pointing me at the next thing to do is the end.

## Why colour

Semantic colours are evocative and easy to parse. The reading arrives without being read — no number to interpret, no threshold to remember, no direction to hold in mind. That matters at the frequency this thing gets looked at, and it is what lets unlike measurements sit side by side in one row: green means *this one is fine* whether the underlying ladder counts up or counts down.

## Why layers rather than one goal threshold

> The reason for a stoplight specifically is semantic colors are evocative and easy to parse, and multiple layers allows me to defined levels of worse, bad, good, better, best, rather than just having a single goal threshold, which makes the mechanic more resilient to wide swings in my capacity level, both between and within days.

A single threshold fails in both directions, and my [capacity](stress-capacity-formula.book-chapter.md) swings widely enough to hit both — between days and within a single day.

- **Below it**, on a low-capacity day, the bar is out of reach. It stops prompting and becomes a verdict of failure instead, which is the opposite of the job.
- **Above it**, on a high-capacity day, the bar is met by noon and stops prompting again, leaving the rest of the day unguided.

Graded levels keep a next rung inside reach on the worst day and keep headroom above on the best one, so the mechanic goes on pointing across the whole range instead of only near one line. This is the resilience the layers buy, and it is why the number of layers is a design property rather than a display flourish.

## Clustered in groups

The circles are grouped by **area of my life**, not by metric. One glance covers a part of my life and tells me which part of it needs something, which is what makes a scan cheaper than a report.

Measured 2026-08-10: three groups of six, eighteen circles — my six values, six inboxes, and six upkeeps (named upkeep rather than habit, because [I do not form habits](routines-scripts-systems.book-chapter.md)).

## What is not a stoplight

**Relationship level is not a stoplight.** It is a long-term progression mechanic. A stoplight resolves per day and points at what to do next; a level only accumulates, and pointing is not its job. The two run off the same meters in places, which is exactly why they are easy to confuse — one source, two projections, and only one of them is this mechanic.
