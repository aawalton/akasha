
# Perceived certainty gap

Alan is routinely perceived as more certain about his claims than he actually is. The surface form of his assertions ("X is Y", "sound is roughly 5x louder for me") reads as ~95% confidence to listeners, while his actual per-claim confidence is closer to 70-80% before a further global "half my beliefs are wrong" discount.

## Mechanism

Several factors compose:

- **Mathematical-proof speech shape.** Alan was a math major. He thinks and speaks like a mathematical proof: minimal words, structure carries the precision, qualifiers do not add content. In a proof, "P implies Q" does not get hedged with "I think P implies Q" — the assertion is the conclusion of explicit reasoning and stands or falls on the reasoning. Alan applies the same shape in conversation.
- **Autism-driven directness.** Hedges that do not add information feel like noise. Where a non-autistic speaker uses meta-qualifiers ("I think", "in my experience") for social register rather than information content, Alan tends to omit them — the precision is already in the structure of the claim.
- **Aphantasia-driven crispness.** Claims output from a System 2 conceptual model rather than from felt-sense. The model's output is crisp by construction (numbers, mechanisms, causal chains); there is no felt-fuzz to soften it in phrasing. A non-aphantasic speaker drawing on impression has natural fuzz baked into their language; Alan does not. (See [aphantasia-mechanism.md](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md).)
- **Global "half my beliefs are wrong" stance.** Alan applies a baseline assumption that at least 50% of what he believes is false. This is *not* a per-claim probability discount — he evaluates per-claim confidence based on current knowledge, then applies the global stance as a separate factor to prevent overcommitment and stay open to change. The stance affects how readily he updates a belief, not the phrasing of any individual claim.

## Two-layer structure

The gap has two layers, only one of which is visible to listeners:

- **Listener-visible** — the per-claim phrasing. Flat assertion form, hedges (if any) baked into the numbers, no outer meta-qualifier. This is what creates the projected ~95% confidence.
- **Listener-invisible** — the global "half are wrong" stance. Because it does not change phrasing — it is a posture toward openness, not a probability adjustment — listeners cannot read it from his speech.

The listener gets a ~95% reading; Alan's effective confidence is closer to 35-40% (per-claim 70-80% × global 50% openness factor). The gap is roughly 50-60 percentage points, not the 15-25 the per-claim phrasing alone would explain.

## Scope

The gap appears across the board, not by domain. Mitigation, however, is health-mediated: when overall stress capacity and metacognitive bandwidth are higher, Alan inserts hedging more reliably and the gap narrows. When health is lower, the math-proof default reasserts itself.

## Cost

The cost is conflict-shaped. Listeners with a competing position read the flat-assertion as a confident challenge and respond with defensiveness, which escalates into conflict. Conflict is cost base tier 4 in [stress-capacity-formula.md](stress-capacity-formula.book-chapter.md) — far above Alan's typical safety level, so a small phrasing trigger compounds into a very high stress-capacity cost. The composition:

- Flat assertion → projected ~95% confidence.
- Defensiveness response from listener.
- Conflict, paid at base tier 4.
- Nervous-system-sensitivity amplification, plus traumatic encoding of the conflict episode itself (see [trauma-encoding.md](trauma-encoding.book-chapter.md)).

Charm contexts are the inverse — confidence is a feature there, not a detriment, and the gap is net-positive. The earlier framing of "tact early" as the developmental adaptation to high conflict cost (in [social-experience.md](social-experience.book-chapter.md)) is closer to charm than tact: charm uses confidence; tact would require hedging. The gap costs in conflict-prone contexts where charm does not carry the social register.

## Mitigation

The direct mitigation is an explicit prophylactic preamble that overrides the math-proof default — leading a claim with "I'm not sure about this, but…", "my best guess is…", or "I might be wrong, but…". The preamble works when remembered. **Remembering is the bottleneck**, gated by metacognition and stress capacity.

Conscious in-conversation enforcement is historically less successful for Alan than slow internalization. The general pattern: rather than trying to enforce the change in real time, Alan keeps internalizing the concept until the new behavior fires automatically. This is schema integration — System C in [automaticity-systems.md](automaticity-systems.book-chapter.md) — a genuine route to automaticity that runs on different machinery than habituation.

Trajectory: probably improving slowly, but the data is sparse. Alan has been in medical social isolation for ~18 months, so the gap comes up much less than it used to and the trajectory is hard to measure.
