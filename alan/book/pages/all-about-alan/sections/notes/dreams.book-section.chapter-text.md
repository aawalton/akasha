
# Dreams

> Dreams as the full-power simulator — asleep, the Recall barrier drops and the whole Perception/Encoding/Recall stack runs, producing novel-length narrative with full senses and emotions. The cortisol-at-sleep-onset to nightmare-frequency correlation.

Awake, Alan has total [aphantasia](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md): no senses, no emotions on recall, present-bound [brushstroke](emotion-model.book-chapter.md#brushstrokes-not-paintings) feeling. Asleep, the whole machine runs. Dreams are the one state where the [Recall barrier drops](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md#the-break-is-at-recall-not-perception-or-encoding) and the full Perception/Encoding/Recall stack executes — full senses, full emotions, and complex narrative.

This contrast is the proof the break is at Recall and nowhere upstream. "I have senses and emotions in my dreams" is the load-bearing evidence that Perception and Encoding are intact; if the content can be assembled in sleep, the faculties that build it are present. What fails awake is voluntary recall, not the underlying machinery.

## Novel-length narrative

Alan often feels he dreams in **full-length novels**. A typical dream carries **half a dozen or so characters, fascinating settings, and complex plots** — not fragments or single scenes, but sustained narrative with a full emotional score running underneath.

The contrast is the whole point. The person with total waking aphantasia and present-bound emotion is, asleep, authoring multi-character story with the emotional track that waking life cannot run. The same brain that cannot pre-play a [forward simulation](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md#no-forward-simulator) awake builds elaborate simulated worlds in sleep.

He has gone through phases of **writing the dreams down as story ideas on waking** — harvesting the night's narrative output as raw material before it slips back behind the Recall barrier.

## Cortisol drives nightmare frequency

Recently most of Alan's dreams have been **nightmares**, which he attributes primarily to increased stress from being in a turbulent phase.

The empirical anchor he has held for a long time: **he has more nightmares the higher his cortisol levels are when he goes to sleep.** Cortisol at sleep-onset tracks nightmare frequency. This is the bounded correlation he states and nothing more — he has no clarity beyond "stress" on what the nightmares mean. The content is not interpreted here; only the cortisol-to-frequency relationship is claimed.

This ties the dream channel back to the recovery stack: the same chronic load the [breathing protocol](allostatic-load.book-chapter.md) is built to clear shows up at night as nightmare frequency when bedtime cortisol runs high. The [sleep protocol](sleep.book-chapter.md) is the system that defends sleep against that load.
