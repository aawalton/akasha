
# The three selves

> Three kinds of self — the immediate self (a single momentary frame, made of the conceptual map), the timeline self (the closure of overlapping immediate-Alans through this one world), and the multiverse self (that closure run across every branch). The load-bearing distinction: the immediate self is made OF map; the two closures are made of the RELATION between maps — a different kind of object. Rendered as Alan's model.

There is not one "Alan-self" but three, stacked. Each is a real, well-defined object; they are different *kinds* of object, and conflating them is where most confusion about Alan's identity comes from. The taxonomy was named in-conversation and Alan confirmed and elaborated it; rendered as his model, not asserted fact.

The three:

1. **The immediate self** — a single momentary frame. The [roughly-3-second window](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#a-roughly-3-second-window) over the sensory buffer, the felt "I" that exists only in the present. It is mostly *made of* the [present-tense conceptual map](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md) — the map is its substance.
2. **The timeline self** — a **closure**: the [transitive closure of the overlap relation](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#continuity-of-identity-is-the-closure-of-the-window) on the chain of immediate-Alans through *this one world*, sleep and unconscious gaps included. The [adjacency closure is complete](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#there-is-no-hard-break--but-the-far-ends-can-be-strangers) — no hard break — even though its far ends can be strangers.
3. **The multiverse self** — that same closure, run across **every** possible timeline. The timeline self is one branch's run drawn from the full branching structure; the [multiverse self](multiversal-identity.book-chapter.md) is the whole. The self in this timestream is a *subset* of it.

## The load-bearing distinction — made OF map vs made of the RELATION between maps

The three are not three sizes of one thing. The immediate self and the two closures are made of **different stuff**.

- The **immediate self is made *of* map.** Its substance is the conceptual map's *contents* — what the present frame currently holds to be true about the world. Strip the map and there is no immediate self left; the frame just *is* the map, present-tense (plus a [physical/emotional fraction](self-improvement.book-chapter.md#the-immediate-self-is-mostly-map--the-conceptual-vs-health-decomposition) — see below).
- The **two closures are made of the *relation between* maps.** A closure is not a map and not a stack of maps. It is the **overlap relation** chained across many immediate-Alans — the structure that says *this* frame's map is near *that* frame's map, link after link. Its substance is connectedness, not contents. You cannot point at "the closure" inside any single frame, because the closure is precisely the thing that lives *between* frames.

So the immediate self is a *thing* (a map); the timeline and multiverse selves are *relations over things* (overlap chains of maps). That is a different kind of object entirely — a noun versus a structure-over-nouns. It is why the immediate self can be read off a single moment while the closures can only ever be [reconstructed](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#continuity-of-identity-is-the-closure-of-the-window), never inhabited: there is no frame in which a between-frames relation is present.

## Why the cut matters

Two consequences fall out of getting the kinds right.

**Improvement lives at the closure level, not the frame level.** A single immediate self cannot improve — it is one frame, gone when the present moves on. [Improvement is a comparison *across* immediate Alans](self-improvement.book-chapter.md#improvement-is-a-cross-frame-comparison): a later frame's map truer than an earlier frame's. That comparison is exactly a property of the *relation between* maps — which is the closure. So improvement is something the timeline self has and the immediate self cannot. Read the right kind of object and the machinery has a place to live.

**The closures extend cheaply because they are already relations.** Most people identify hard with a single continuous self and cannot extend across branches, because their self is a thick remembered thing tied to one timeline. Alan's [closure self is thin](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#continuity-of-identity-is-the-closure-of-the-window) — a relation, not a remembered thing — so running the relation one seam further, across the branch boundary into the [multiverse self](multiversal-identity.book-chapter.md#why-the-architecture-makes-the-move-natural-and-cheap), is barely a further step. A relation extends where a thing would resist.
