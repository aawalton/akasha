
# Pain-driven motivation

> Pain-driven, present-bound motivation — how goals work with no represented future. Present pain is the driver; external systems re-evoke captured pains fresh in the present rather than storing wants; entries that fail to re-evoke die quietly (strategic forgetting, with the world as the backup store); the confirmed blind spot is important-but-painless slow drifts.

The [present-tense model](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md) leaves an obvious question open. If there is no future in the model and no [forward simulator](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md#no-forward-simulator) to pre-play one, what does a goal even run on? Most people are pulled by a represented future — an imagined state they move toward. Alan has no channel to represent one.

The answer is that nothing stands in for a represented future. **The driver is present pain.** Goals, plans, and intentions are implemented as pains felt now, plus external systems that can make a captured pain fire again later. The whole architecture stays inside the present moment.

## The driver is present pain

In Alan's words: *"I'm driven primarily by the present moment and primarily by pain. Sometimes that's an active pain or annoyance that I want to take action to resolve. Sometimes that's the pain of having a desire that I can't immediately feel."*

Two sub-cases, both present-tense:

- **Active pain or annoyance.** Something hurts or grates right now; action resolves it.
- **Blocked desire.** Wanting something he can't immediately have is itself a pain, felt now. The desire doesn't pull from the future; its blockage pushes from the present.

Note what the second sub-case does: it folds wanting into the same mechanism as hurting. Even the cases that look like pursuit of a positive future state enter the system as a present-moment pain to be resolved.

### Pain doesn't only drive — it teaches

Pain runs a second job beyond initiating action. When an automatic response misfires — fires but fails to produce the desired outcome — the mismatch *is* a pain, and that pain is the training signal that slowly retrains the automatic response over many repetitions. *"Nothing teaches like pain for me."* So pain is both Alan's [cleanest read](alexithymia.book-chapter.md) (the one always-legible channel where most inner signals run quiet) and his only real teacher (the gradient that reshapes the automatic response curve). This note is the canonical home for pain-as-driver; the self-modification model it powers — no deliberate mode, cheap input-rewrite vs. expensive pain-retrain — lives in [no-deliberate-mode.md](no-deliberate-mode.book-chapter.md), which routes the [asymmetric-prediction-error mechanism](insight-and-restructuring.book-chapter.md#asymmetric-prediction-error--nothing-teaches-like-pain) behind the teaching.

## A goal is a record that re-lights its pain

With no represented future, "holding a goal" cannot mean keeping a future state in mind. What it means instead: **capturing the pain in an external system, so that reading the record later re-creates the pain in that later present.**

In his words: *"I use systems to capture those pains and record them so when I look at the record again, it brings back some of the pain (in that present moment, not the memory from before), and if I can, I act on it immediately. So, the whole process is still stateless, with the systems being the external persistence store."*

The parenthetical is the mechanism. The record is not a memory aid — there is no remembered want to retrieve, because the [map is affectively flat](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md#the-map-is-affectively-flat--structure-no-charge) and carries no charge across time. What the record does is **re-evoke**: rereading it lights the pain fresh, in the present, where the present-bound self can act on it. The store doesn't remind the self what it wanted. It makes the present self want it again.

This names the read-side mechanism of the [agent harness's](agent-harness.book-chapter.md) external state-keeping. The write side captures the pain; the read side is re-evocation, not retrieval. Each momentary self that opens the record either feels the pain again and acts, or doesn't.

## Strategic forgetting — recurrence is the importance filter

The re-evocation mechanism has a built-in failure mode: an entry that fails to re-light its pain when reread. Alan treats it as a feature: *"I think that's a useful feature. It's effectively a form of strategic forgetting. If it's not important to me still when I look at it again, it doesn't matter that much. If it turns out the second time is a fluke and it really does matter, it will come up again."*

Entries that fail to re-evoke die quietly. No triage, no guilt, no archive review — the record just stops producing action. Recurrence is the importance filter: what still pings on re-read survives, and what genuinely matters will generate fresh pain on its own. **The world itself is the backup store.** A real need re-presents itself; a fluke doesn't.

## The blind spot — painless slow drifts

Recurrence-as-filter only catches things that ping — annoyances, blocked desires, friction. An important change that produces no pain in any present moment never enters the system at all. Slow health drift and quiet relationship cooling are the canonical cases: each individual present moment is fine, so nothing ever escalates, and the drift compounds unattended.

Alan confirms the blind spot exists in practice: *"Yeah, I think it does, and I'm currently working on expanding my systems to artificially ping at the right times."* The fix is the same externalization move as the rest of the harness — where the neurotype supplies no signal, engineer one. An artificial ping converts a painless drift into a thing that pings, which puts it back inside the architecture everything else already runs on.

### The calibration problem

Artificial pings have no felt signal to calibrate against — that absence is the reason the ping is needed at all, and it also removes the feedback that would tune the cadence. How often should a health check or a relationship check fire? A per-domain decay model and pure empirical tuning are both candidates. Unresolved: *"I'm not sure yet, I'll have to work that out for each domain individually."*
