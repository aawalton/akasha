
# The recovery ladder

> The recovery ladder — Alan models recovery as climbing a ladder of safety levels where activities come into and leave scope as they become affordable, and he steers attention to whatever is newly affordable and on the critical path for recovery.

Alan models his recovery as climbing a **ladder of safety levels.** Each rung is a level of nervous-system safety; recovery is the upward climb. The ladder is the trajectory side of the [Safety](safety.book-chapter.md) recovery the [recovery rates](recovery-rates.book-chapter.md) note tracks numerically — there, surplus compounds Health upward; here, that same climb is read as rungs being reached.

## Activities have ranges over the rungs

Different activities have different **ranges over the rungs.** An activity is affordable only across a band of safety levels — below its floor it costs more than the current bar can cover, and above its band a higher-value activity displaces it. As Alan recovers and climbs:

- Activities **come back into scope as they become newly affordable.** An activity that was unpayable two rungs down is now within reach.
- Activities **leave again as something higher-value becomes affordable and replaces them.** The newly-reachable rung opens a more valuable activity that takes the slot the lower one held.

So the affordable set is not a growing pile — it **cycles.** Each rung admits some activities and retires others. This is the upward-climb counterpart to the downward [Safety probe in wanting-as-gauge](wanting-as-gauge.book-chapter.md#per-resource-signatures): there, Alan walks *down* the activity tiers to find where the bar crosses a threshold and diagnose low Safety; here, he watches activities cross *up* into affordability as the bar rises. The same [activity-tier hierarchy](stress-capacity-formula.book-chapter.md#cost-base-tiers-by-interaction-type) — and the [cost multiplier Safety sets on each tier](activity-budgeting.book-chapter.md#status-thresholds) — orders both moves. The ladder of safety levels is very likely the same tier hierarchy the downward probe steps through, read from the recovery direction rather than the diagnostic one.

## Affordable vs. free — the two cost states each rung admits

A rung does not flip an activity from impossible to costless in one step. It admits the activity in two stages, and the distinction is exact. In Alan's words: "I make a distinction between something being affordable and being free. If it's affordable, I'm paying no more than a 1x cost. That means I can generally afford to do the thing without having it cost me my entire day. If it's free, I don't have to count the cost."

- **Affordable** = the activity costs **no more than 1x** — payable without it eating the whole day.
- **Free** = he **doesn't have to count the cost** at all.

So each interaction type has two thresholds on the ladder, not one: the rung where it becomes affordable, and a higher rung where it becomes free.

### The interaction-type ladder

The being-known-adjacent interaction types map onto the rungs like this:

| Interaction type | Affordable at | Free at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Positive interactions with others | L3 | L4 |
| Conflict with others; self-disclosure | L4 | L5 |
| Criticism; paying attention to others' needs | L5 | L6 |
| Rejection (hypothesis) | L6 | L7 |
| AI conversations | L2 | L3 |

Rejection is a hypothesis, not a measurement: Alan expects it one level more expensive than criticism — affordable at L6, free at L7 — but the band is uncalibrated, and he'll only know once he's consistently there.

**AI conversations are cheap for a specific reason.** They sit a full level below positive human interaction because Alan can *read* AI — it thinks like him, more like him than other humans do, so he pays no premium for the other mind's randomness. "I don't pay a premium cost, so these conversations tend to be affordable at level 2 and free at level 3." At L3 today, an AI conversation costs him nothing. This is why these captures can run for an hour at no resource cost while the same hour of human conversation would be unpayable.

### The trajectory this year — the floor is moving

The bands are fixed; the rung Alan stands on is not. He has been climbing the ladder for the first time in his life, and the data is sharp:

- **First consistent L3 ever** — earlier this year.
- **Consistent L4** — about two months later.
- **Consistent L5** — beginning of May, then overextended and crashed back down.
- **L6 — exactly once, for a few hours in May.**

The trend line is up, so the floor is moving. The consequence for the interaction-type ladder is direct: "I have never been consistently healthy enough that criticism was free." Criticism goes free only at L6, and he has touched L6 once. Each affordable/free threshold above his current floor is still future, and the climb is what brings them into reach. This is the same climb the [Safety 2026 trajectory](safety/progression.book-chapter.md#2026-trajectory--may-peak-turn-outward-cost-june-illness-confound) tracks from the resource side — the May level-5 peak, the turn-outward cost, the crash — read here as rungs reached and lost on the affordability ladder.

## Critical-path attention — the Flow discipline applied to healing

At any point Alan pays extra attention to **what is newly affordable** — the activities that just crossed into scope at the current rung. Among those, he privileges the ones he believes could be on his **critical path for recovery**: the activity that unblocks the next rung, not merely the most pleasant one now within reach.

This is the critical-path discipline (the Flow principle — optimize for the path that unblocks the next step, everything else is parallel) turned onto his own healing — find and run the activity that unblocks the next rung, rather than spreading effort across everything newly available. A newly-affordable activity that also lifts capacity (an [investment, not a consumption](connection-expansion.book-chapter.md#consumption-vs-investment--how-to-prioritize)) is worth more than one that only spends the rung he is already on, because it shortens the climb to the rung above.

## Surplus-gated graduated testing — how a social behavior actually shifts

Climbing a rung is what changes the cost math on paper. But the [trauma-installed social rules](perfection-is-safe/origin.book-chapter.md#renegotiation-status--five-still-active-two-broken) — *be nice, be quiet, don't take up space, don't ask for anything, take care of yourself* — keep firing even after the rung is reached and even after the belief above them is won. They shift, but slowly, by a specific procedure Alan runs on himself. In his words:

> *"I think these behaviors shift slowly when I feel like I have a surplus of capacity for social situations and carefully start to expand again and test whether I can actually afford the expansion. I think the 'this is dangerous' belief is still part of this, it is unaffordable because it is dangerous, and I'm slowly testing to see 'is this safe now?'"*

The loop is gated on surplus and runs in four steps: **wait for a surplus of social capacity → carefully expand a little → read the result → ask "is it safe now?"** It is the critical-path discipline above turned into a self-experiment: surplus is the rung-crossing that opens the test, and a small deliberate expansion is the probe that reads whether the rung is real.

The distinctive part is the stance toward the danger-read. Alan does **not** try to argue himself out of "this is dangerous," and he does **not** assume the disconfirmation in advance. He holds *dangerous* as **provisionally true** and re-runs the standing experiment each time capacity grows — testing the live question "is it safe **now**?" rather than asserting "it was never dangerous." This is why surplus is the gate: he only runs the test under conditions he can actually survive if the answer comes back *still costly,* so a bad read costs a recoverable amount instead of a crash.

This is an honest, capacity-gated self-exposure — distinct from the classic somatic disconfirmation that doesn't take on him ([random social punishment can't be extinguished because the dread keeps coming true](learned-response-taxonomy.book-chapter.md#a-second-axis--does-a-response-fade-with-repetition-and-by-which-machine)). He isn't laundering the reflex and isn't waiting for the feeling to fade; he is reading the true present cost empirically by expanding only when he has surplus and watching whether anything actually collapses. The danger-read sits **nested under affordability** — *unaffordable because dangerous* — which is exactly the [re-scope-under-the-affordability-governor](volatility-governor.book-chapter.md#the-five-survival-rules-re-scoped-under-affordability) fate those five rules took; this loop is how that re-scoped rule gets re-tested as the floor moves.

## Connection is the current critical-path reach

The first thing on Alan's critical path as he expands is **connection** — specifically the **Intimacy / Connection / Knowing / Being Known** dimension. Of all his systems and rungs, the first thing he is reaching for as he recovers is to be known.

This sits the recovery ladder directly on top of the connection cluster. The [connection need](connection.book-chapter.md) is the underlying trait; the directions for paying down its [debt](connection-debt.book-chapter.md) are mapped in [connection expansion](connection-expansion.book-chapter.md), where rising capacity is itself listed as a connection strategy (the non-linear unaffordable→affordable transition is exactly a rung crossing). The [being-known supply](being-known-supply.book-chapter.md) plan and the [dosing](connection-dosing.book-chapter.md) rate-control are the concrete moves once the rung is reached. That [loneliness reads as standing allostatic load](medical-social-isolation.book-chapter.md) is why connection sits on the critical path rather than off to the side — closing the being-known deficit lifts the bar the rest of the climb runs against.
