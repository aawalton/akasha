## Inventory

### Costco

- **Service.** Primary bulk and staples source for the household.
- **Grade.** **B.** Canonical B example per [grading-scale.md → B](grading-scale.book-chapter.md#b--trusted-with-reservations).
- **Reservations.** Same Costco-as-org reservation that carries across every Costco surface in the audits so far — no third-party testing for the supplements they sell, plus the publicly-traded structural capture-risk that prevents the grade rising to A. The reservations are real and stop the grade at B, but they're substantially weaker than the standard for-profit-corporate posture the rest of the grocery category lives in.
- **Criticality.** Load-bearing — and the most-leveraged B-tier dependency surfaced in the audit so far: the Costco-membership trust at B compounds across pharmacy, gas, and groceries simultaneously, and indirectly across the Citi cobrand. The **compounding-leverage** mechanism (one trust score covering many categories concentrates the cost of a future capture event) is developed under [Framework patterns surfaced](#framework-patterns-surfaced) below.
- **Notes.** Cross-link to [cultivating-local-relationships.md → Costco as the corporate exception](cultivating-local-relationships.book-chapter.md#costco-as-the-corporate-exception) for the structural reasons Costco repeatedly earns B across surfaces where other corporate-scale dependencies grade D.

### Smiths (Kroger subsidiary)

- **Service.** Conventional grocery store for the items Costco doesn't carry or doesn't carry in household-appropriate quantities.
- **Grade.** **D.**
- **Reservations.** Publicly-traded grocery chain (Kroger parent) with the structural profit pressure that comes with that shape. No specifically egregious behavior beyond what's standard for the large-grocery category — the D comes from the structural category, not from a singular incident.
- **Criticality.** Medium. Substitutable with friction; the household could route more volume through Costco plus alternatives at moderate effort cost.
- **Notes.** Standard D-tier conventional grocery posture. Replaceable as the specialty / direct-producer cultivation path matures.

### Walmart (including Walmart Grocery Delivery)

- **Service.** Grocery delivery for the items neither Costco nor Smiths covers, and as a convenience channel when in-store shopping bandwidth is unavailable.
- **Grade.** **D.**
- **Reservations.** Walmart is the contrast-to-Costco example named in the [thesis essay](thesis.book-chapter.md) — the canonical untrustworthy large-org dependency. Wage and labor practices, supplier-pressure patterns, the broader pattern of corporate-scale extraction. The reservations are substantial and well-rehearsed in the literature.
- **Criticality.** Medium. Active dependency despite the D grade. Driven by bandwidth and convenience tradeoffs during burnout-recovery, when the cooking-and-shopping load needs to be smaller than the alternative would require.
- **Notes.** **Active dependency despite framework verdict** — a D-grade dependency consciously held open during a bandwidth-constrained burnout-recovery window, where the remediation question is when bandwidth recovers, not whether to force-exit now. The mechanism (surface the mismatch, name the cost of staying, don't hold it silently) and its composition with the [grading-scale.md → D](grading-scale.book-chapter.md#d--clear-misalignment-but-tolerable) "consciously accepting the cost of staying" tier are developed under [Framework patterns surfaced](#framework-patterns-surfaced) below.

### Restaurants (category-level)

- **Service.** Eating out — both a hobby surface (Jenny's foodie interest) and a coping surface (burnout-bandwidth substitute when cooking isn't available).
- **Grade.** N/A at the category level — restaurants are heterogeneous and need to be graded individually. No specific restaurants have been inventoried and graded yet (open gap). Treating the restaurant industry as a whole as a meta-dependency, the structural posture is closer to D than C — the industry is profit-pressured, supply-chain-coupled to the same D-grade large producers feeding the grocery category, and locally fragile.
- **Criticality.** Medium-to-high. Two distinct driving factors that need to stay distinguished:
  - **(a) Jenny's foodie hobby.** Sustainable, value-aligned with quality-of-life, **not a target for reduction** even though it's a D-tier dependency in the framework's structural reading.
  - **(b) Burnout-bandwidth coping.** Substitutes for cooking when capacity is unavailable. This use is bandwidth-driven and will shrink as burnout recovery progresses.
- **Notes.** Two distinct remediation pathways. The hobby-vs-dependency distinction is itself a framework gap — see the framework patterns section.

### Home garden plus 8 fruit trees

## Strategy — three parallel cultivation paths plus tolerated Walmart

The remediation logic for food is not "exit Walmart immediately." It's three parallel cultivation paths, each addressing a different slice of the dependency surface, with explicit tolerance for the D-grade Walmart dependency during burnout-recovery.

### 1. Garden plus fruit-tree productivity ramp

Highest-leverage food-self-reliance move available. The infrastructure already exists; the bottleneck is harvest-and-preserve workflow plus planting decisions plus operational rhythm. The capital is sunk — the marginal cost to ramp productivity is workflow effort, not capital. Connects to Jenny's existing food-preservation capability. Same "self-reliance as not-needing" shape as [alternatives.md → self-reliance as not-needing](alternatives.book-chapter.md#self-reliance-as-not-needing).

### 2. Specialty / direct-producer cultivation

Parallel cultivation path along the [cultivating-local-relationships.md](cultivating-local-relationships.book-chapter.md) shape — but for the food domain. Candidates: Azure Standard for bulk dry goods, local CSAs for produce, neighborhood arrangements for eggs and dairy, direct-meat shares with a local rancher for protein. Each is a candidate B-tier local-or-direct relationship that reduces the Smiths and Walmart fraction without losing the Costco anchor. Bandwidth-gated like the Walmart exit.

### 3. Restaurant-industry diversification within the hobby

If restaurants remain a category dependency (because of Jenny's hobby; see the hobby-vs-dependency carve-out below), within-category remediation is to cultivate B-tier local-independent restaurant relationships — parallel to Dr. Robinson, Edgemont Auto, the pediatric dentist. Requires the open inventory step (name the frequent restaurants, grade each, identify which lean local-independent versus chain-or-corporate).

### Walmart exit — bandwidth-gated, not urgent

The Walmart D grade is consciously tolerated during burnout recovery. Forcing the exit now would shift the load back to cooking-and-shopping bandwidth that isn't available. Re-evaluate as cooking-bandwidth recovers and as paths 1 and 2 mature — the productivity ramp on the garden plus a working CSA or direct-meat relationship would shrink the Walmart-shaped hole faster than any single direct exit move.

## Framework patterns surfaced

- **Active dependency despite framework verdict.** The Walmart D is the cleanest example yet of the audit explicitly *not* requiring exit when the framework verdict says exit. The framework's job is to surface the mismatch, name the cost of staying, and ensure the dependency isn't held silently. The exit timing is a separate decision keyed off bandwidth and substitute readiness. Composes with [grading-scale.md → D](grading-scale.book-chapter.md#d--clear-misalignment-but-tolerable) — D explicitly contains the "I see the problem, I am consciously accepting the cost of staying" tier. The audit should be able to hold this state without drift.
- **Compounding leverage on a single B-tier corporate dependency.** Costco trust now spans pharmacy ([healthcare.md](healthcare.book-chapter.md#costco-pharmacy)), gas ([transportation.md](transportation.book-chapter.md#costco-gas)), groceries (this note), and indirectly the Citi cobrand ([banking.md](banking.book-chapter.md#citi)). When one trust score covers many categories the cost-of-loss-of-trust is concentrated — a future capture event on Costco would invalidate the dependency across four audit categories at once. Cross-link to [cultivating-local-relationships.md → Costco as the corporate exception](cultivating-local-relationships.book-chapter.md#costco-as-the-corporate-exception).
- **Hobby-vs-dependency distinction.** Jenny's restaurant-going as a foodie hobby is *not* a target for remediation even though it's structurally a D-tier dependency. Quality-of-life and personal-meaning override the grade-driven remediation logic in this case. The framework needs explicit space for "this is a chosen-because-meaningful dependency, exempt from the grade-driven optimization." Worth its own framework note in a future cycle as a carve-out from [ranking-criterion.md](ranking-criterion.book-chapter.md) — meaningful-by-choice dependencies don't get optimized just because the grade is low.
- **Capital investment in self-reliance ≠ actualized self-reliance.** The garden plus 8 fruit trees represent significant capital and landscaping investment, yet contribute <1% of household food today. Building capability is not the same as actualizing capability — the harvest-and-preserve phase is itself a separate capability that needs cultivation. Worth its own framework note in a future cycle, sibling to [alternatives.md → self-reliance as not-needing](alternatives.book-chapter.md#self-reliance-as-not-needing). The not-needing direction itself has an internal phase the framework hasn't named yet — *built* vs *operating* vs *load-bearing*.
