
# Sensory-regulation tools

> Four-tier preference hierarchy for sensory-regulation tools — automatic > flexible > on/off > nothing. Each tier reduces the EF cost of regulating a continuous sensory load. Generalizes across modalities (visual, auditory, thermal).

Sensory regulation has continuous costs (see [four-resource-model.md → Stress Capacity](four-resource-model.book-chapter.md)), so tools that match the dose to the load beat tools that force binary choices. There is a four-tier preference hierarchy across every sensory modality.

## The hierarchy

From best to worst:

1. **Automatic.** Senses ambient input and adjusts continuously without conscious input. Photochromic sunglasses. Climate auto-adjust. Adaptive screen brightness when it tracks the actual load.
2. **Flexible.** Manual continuous adjustment. Dimmer slider. Volume knob. Thermostat with a continuous dial.
3. **On/off.** Binary toggle. Light switch. Mute button. Window open or closed.
4. **Nothing.** No tool at all. Body has to absorb the full load.

## Mechanism: each tier reduces the EF cost of regulation

The cost being regulated is real and continuous; the question is who pays it and how:

- **Automatic externalizes the regulation entirely.** The system senses and adjusts; no conscious input needed. EF cost approaches zero. The dose tracks the load automatically.
- **Flexible matches the dose to the load with manual input.** Low-but-present EF cost — Alan has to notice the load and turn the knob — but the match can be precise. Inverted observation against standard EF-budget framing: the EF cost on the regulator is small, and it pays back many times over by capping the sensory drain on Stress Capacity.
- **On/off forces a binary choice that's almost always wrong.** Either over-correct (light off when a little light would do) or under-correct (light on when a little less would do). The dose can't match the load. Forces a constant micro-decision: is this room over-lit enough to warrant flipping the switch?
- **Nothing forces full body adaptation.** The body absorbs the entire load with no regulation surface. Highest cost. The visible behavior (squinting, flinching, withdrawal) is the body's response; the felt cost is the Stress Capacity drain underneath.

The pattern is the inverse of what shallow EF-economy framing would predict. A "flexible" tool requires more conscious moves than an "on/off" tool, so it looks more EF-expensive — but the *total* cost (EF on the regulator + Stress Capacity drained by the unmanaged load) is lower with flexible tools, because matching the dose to the load is what keeps Stress Capacity from leaking.

## Generalizes across modalities

Concrete instances Alan operates with daily:

- **Visual brightness.** Captured in [visual-sensitivity-bundle.md](visual-sensitivity-bundle.book-chapter.md). Outdoor sunglasses are flexible (put on, take off). Indoor lighting is on/off. Screen brightness modes are flexible (brightness slider) but mode-switching by time of day is on/off-shaped. Aspirations: photochromic sunglasses (automatic), continuous indoor dimmers (flexible).
- **Volume / sound.** Captured in [auditory-sensitivity-bundle.md](auditory-sensitivity-bundle.book-chapter.md). AirPods Pro are nominally automatic-or-flexible (transparency dial, ANC level), but in practice Alan defaults to **full-on ANC** and overrides downward by physically pulling an AirPod slightly out for transparency. The reason is asymmetric cost — under-dampening costs much more than over-dampening — so the safer default is the protective extreme rather than the matched dose. **The asymmetric cost shape modifies which tier is optimal in a given modality.** When the load is high and the cost shape is asymmetric, default-max plus manual relief beats automatic-adaptive. Dark noise stacks on top as a masking layer when residual remains. The whole stack runs on top of a quiet basement office (spatial layer).
- **Touch / tactile.** Captured in [tight-clothing.md](tight-clothing.book-chapter.md). Tight clothing across the full kit (undergarments, shirts, pants, socks) is **default-tight 24/7** with partial loose-break under low Mana. Same shape as the auditory bundle: default-max protection, manual relief downward, asymmetric cost (under-protection costs more than over-protection). Second confirmed modality for the asymmetric-cost flip. The [weighted blanket](weighted-blanket.book-chapter.md) is a third tactile tool, but it runs the opposite direction — default-off, strategic deployment, coin-flip hit rate. Same modality, different mechanism class (movement restriction + heat sink rather than grounding + saturation), different cost shape (chest weight blocks the breathing protocol; over-weighting often exceeds the value). The weighted blanket falsifies a universal-generalization reading of the cycle-10 finding — the asymmetric-cost flip applies *when under-protection costs much more than over-protection*, not to every sensory tool. **Tactile baseline management** is a distinct cost-shape pattern in the same modality: [lotion regimen](lotion-regimen.book-chapter.md) and [skin-on-skin prevention](skin-on-skin-prevention.book-chapter.md) both run a default-maintained-baseline-with-restore-after-disruption shape. No intentional downward relief — disruption is externally caused (water from handwashing; unavoidable body contact), not chosen. Different cost shape than the asymmetric-cost cases; flagged as a candidate cost-shape refinement.
- **Thermal.** Layers are flexible (add or remove one at a time). The hot bath is a separate stress-tier escalation (see [hot-bath.md](hot-bath.book-chapter.md)). Closing the shower door turns the bathroom into a sauna — that's an on/off lever, used rarely. The weighted blanket joins the thermal toolkit as a heat sink for overheating-direction stretches (see [weighted-blanket.md](weighted-blanket.book-chapter.md) and [thermoregulation.md](thermoregulation.book-chapter.md)). Thermoregulation in general is a domain where automatic tools (thermostats, climate control) clearly beat flexible ones.

## Two cross-tool generalizations

Cycle 11's weighted-blanket capture sharpened the cross-tool picture into two distinct generalizations, only one of which holds across the full kit.

- **Asymmetric-cost adaptation: holds when under-protection costs much more than over-protection.** Auditory bundle and tight clothing both fit — high load, asymmetric cost, default-max plus manual relief beats automatic-adaptive. The weighted blanket falsifies the universal reading: its cost shape is symmetric or inverted (over-weighting often exceeds the value, chest weight blocks breathing), so default-off is correct. The flip generalizes under specific cost-shape conditions, not universally.
- **Runtime adjustability: holds across the kit.** All three tools — AirPod manual partial-removal, tight-clothing partial loose-break, weighted-blanket mid-sleep legs-only / full-body swap — adjust their configuration based on real-time signal. The runtime-adjustability shape doesn't depend on cost-shape symmetry. Distinct generalization, separate from the asymmetric-cost question.
