## The wedding night

We did the wedding first, then the honeymoon, then the reception — which is how I think it should always be done, much better than the normal pattern. The wedding night was the **first time having sex for both of us.** It was slow and awkward for both, as I expect most first times are, but we did it and enjoyed it.

We kept the lights off, and that has been the standard all the way through. It helps both of us with **sensory management** — the same sensory-cost logic that runs everywhere else in how we live.

## The mechanics — and the weight/confinement inversion

Almost all of our sexual interaction has been standard intercourse: roughly **80% with her on top, 20% with me on top.** The split is driven by a valence inversion between us. Jen has sensory triggers around feeling weight on her or being confined — both are aversive for her. **For me, weight and confinement are a positive.** So the position that costs her is the one that pays me, and vice versa; the 80/20 is the equilibrium that mostly spends from my surplus rather than her deficit. Note the inversion plainly: the same physical input reads opposite on the two of us.

## Birth control and pacing

- **Birth control.** For the first ~four years, when we weren't ready for kids, we did outercourse instead. After that we adopted condoms, and that has been our only birth-control method ever since. We had unprotected sex only when we wanted to have kids. We've had **three kids.**
- **Pacing.** At the very beginning, about once per week. After kids were born it dropped to about once a month, and monthly has been the most common pacing throughout — though irregular, as everything is for both of us. Jen has trouble if she doesn't get intimacy around once a month, so that is her natural pacing, usually **aligned with her cycle.** (I have no comparable clock — see [horniness-as-hunger](regulation-not-desire.book-chapter.md#horniness-is-like-hunger).)

## The initiation contract

I always had a hard time navigating initiating sex, with some painful experiences early on. So we developed an **implicit contract: she initiates when she is ready, and I almost always respond** — yes about 99% of the time. This routes around the part that was costly for me (initiation) and keys the pacing to her cycle-aligned need, which is the reliable signal between us.

## Jen's growth

In the last few years Jen has grown a lot in coming to accept sex as more natural and positive than her upbringing instilled. For our generation, the #1 message taught to teenagers was **"DON'T HAVE SEX,"** so unlearning that took a long time. (She first let her mother talk to her about the details of sex only the night before our wedding — she'd decided young to do that just-in-time, not before.)

She has had prolapse issues from the three pregnancies, and found a **vaginal-muscle training tool** that helped her learn a great deal about her own body and arousal mechanisms — she's even masturbated a few times now, for the sake of learning.

In the past year we've tried **manual stimulation** about a half-dozen times, for each of us. We've found it incredibly effective for me, but not as effective for her — manual stimulation is still extremely uncomfortable for her in both directions, so we haven't done much experimentation there.

## The orgasm asymmetry

Orgasm for Jen has been much harder than for me. I climax about **98% of the time;** she has a full climax **less than 5%** of the time. So I focus primarily on her needs during sex, knowing I'll get there one way or another.

For a long time, when she didn't reach climax, I internalized it **as my fault** and tried to do better. Eventually we learned that climax for her is just **not as intense most of the time,** and the full-intensity version is vanishingly rare — it was never a thing I was failing to produce. We think part of that is tied to her weight being higher than she'd like (about 200 lbs). I try hard never to mention her weight — she's sensitive about it, and it's socially sensitive in general — but sometimes I've been too open, and I'm sure that has caused damage for her.

## The after-sex drops

For probably 80% of our marriage, the two of us crashed in opposite registers after sex: **Jen had a strong negative emotional drop; I had a strong physiological drop** that made me very sleepy. Two different mechanisms, same moment.

The remediation came later and is simple on both sides. I've learned to **linger and cuddle** until she's ready to get up, rather than going straight to sleep. She's learned to **eat some chocolate or another treat** afterward to get a positive boost. Both have helped.
