
# Health

> Health — stress capacity bar. Roughly the time-integral of Stress Level, with adjustments for direct interventions. Two distinct quantities (Stress Level and Stress Capacity) have been conflated under "Health"; full disentanglement is in progress.

Health is the stress capacity bar — the buffer I can sustainably draw against. Operationally: integrated stress accumulation over time, with adjustments for direct interventions (sleep, rest, recovery).

## Two distinct quantities historically conflated

"Health" has been used to refer to two distinct quantities:

- **Stress Level** (instantaneous, 0-100). The current load. 20-year measurement; predates the autism diagnosis. Anchors: 0 and 100 are both fatal (different reasons); 5 = complete relaxation; 15 = light activity; 25 = athletic exertion; 35 = indirect threat to survival (being yelled at by a boss); 45 = direct threat to survival (being literally chased by a bear).
- **Stress Capacity** (the bar). Roughly the time-integral of Stress Level, with adjustments for direct interventions. Currently expressed as nervous-system-health percentage 0-100%. Capacity reaches ~1% at the burnout floor (catastrophic ADL failure) and is at ~5% as of 2026-04.

These two operate at different time scales and respond to different inputs. Their relationship is roughly: Stress Capacity = ∫(Stress Level cost over time, with intervention adjustments). The disentanglement isn't complete in the existing notes; see [health bar](../../../all-about-alan-topic/health-bar.all-about-alan-topic.md).

## Health-level scale (numbered, 4 capacity hours per level)

Alongside the 0-100% Stress Capacity readout, Health carries a numbered level scale parallel to the [Safety scale](safety.book-chapter.md). Each level spans 4 capacity hours; the scale extends symmetrically into surplus and deficit territory.

| Level | Capacity range |
| --- | --- |
| Level 3 | (8, 12] hr surplus |
| Level 2 | (4, 8] hr surplus |
| Level 1 | (0, 4] hr surplus |
| Level 0 | exactly 0 — no deficit, no surplus |
| Level -1 | [-4, 0) — 0-4hr deficit |
| Level -2 | [-8, -4) — 4-8hr deficit (the self-defeating window) |
| Level -3 | [-12, -8) — 8-12hr deficit |

Above Level 3 (>12hr surplus) is required for the optimal level of nervous-system recovery. The scale doesn't cap at 3 — it continues upward through Level 4, 5, etc. — but Level 3 is the threshold the recovery stack is built around.

**Operating target: stay above Level 3.** Below the threshold the recovery rate is positive but sub-optimal. Above it, the math is in the regime that produces the compounding-upward trajectory in [recovery-rates.md → surplus / deficit balance](recovery-rates.book-chapter.md#surplus--deficit-balance).

### Daily morning math

The morning cycle illustrates how the scale composes with the recovery stack:

- **Sleep deposits ~6-10 hours of stress capacity** at the [1-hour-per-hour-slept rate](recovery-rates.book-chapter.md). 6 hours slept → +6 capacity hours; 10 hours slept → +10 capacity hours.
- **Morning starts at Level 2-3.** 6 hours slept lands inside Level 2 (4-8hr surplus); 10 hours slept lands at the top of Level 3 (8-12hr surplus).
- **Morning meditative breathing climbs above Level 3.** Continuous [4-in / 12-out breathing](meditative-breathing.book-chapter.md) through the morning takes the surplus past 12 capacity hours, into the >Level-3 regime where the recovery-rate math runs at its target.

## Self-defeating window at Level -2

A confirmed cross-resource finding: at Level -2 (4-8hr deficit), Health can be too low to recover Health.

The mechanism runs through [four-resource-model.md → cross-resource dynamics](four-resource-model.book-chapter.md#cross-resource-dynamics): low Health directly reduces Mana and Stamina. The recovery interventions that pay Health back — the [hot bath](hot-bath.book-chapter.md), exercise — both cost Mana and Stamina. At Level -2 the bath becomes unaffordable exactly when most needed.

"One of the most difficult times for me." The window tends to land at Level -2 specifically; less severe deficits (Level -1) leave enough Mana headroom to fire a bath, and deeper deficits (Level -3 and below) don't show up empirically in the current operating regime — Level -2 is where the self-defeating dynamic sits.

This is a structural property of the four-resource model, not a property of any one intervention: any recovery move that draws on resources Health gates can hit the same wall. Recovery moves that don't draw on Mana or Stamina (sleep when it can be initiated; passive lying-down) are the escape paths from the window.

## Capacity → Safety → cost-multiplier → rate

Stress Capacity (the Health bar) and the instantaneous Stress Level reading are not independent dimensions. The coupling runs through [Safety](safety.book-chapter.md):

- Lower Stress Capacity → lower Safety level (Safety is roughly the time-integral of Capacity).
- Lower Safety → higher [cost multiplier](stress-capacity-formula.book-chapter.md) on the same input (each Safety level ≈ 2x cost change at high tiers).
- Higher cost multiplier × the same stressor input → higher `dState/dt` — Stress Level rises faster.

**Rate amplification, not level amplification.** A given external stressor does *not* produce a higher Stress Level reading instantaneously when Capacity is lower. State rises faster from the same input, and reaches higher levels sooner — but the same input does not read higher in the same moment. The distinction is load-bearing: rate-amplification is what compounds the burnout decline (~20% per year), and what reverses sign during recovery as Safety climbs and the multiplier drops.

This closes the Capacity-vs-State compositionality question. They are not independent and they are not directly compositional; Capacity affects State via rate-amplification through Safety.

## Movement — the expenditure and recovery arm

"Move" is the expenditure-and-recovery limb of the health-first refocus, the motion counterpart to the [eating arm](points-as-hunger-substitute.book-chapter.md). Like eating, it does not run on a spontaneous urge — movement options move through the same **aligned-options lifecycle** (discovery → qualification → retention → loss) as foods, and the same gating decides which are reachable at his current state.

The current movement repertoire:

- **Walking — the lifelong baseline.** Already his primary [stim](stimming.book-chapter.md); a typical day is ~10,000 steps and a high day has reached ~45,000. It is the cheapest, most reliable option because it is also regulation, not just expenditure.
- **Indoor pacing.** The low-floor form of the same thing — available when leaving the room is not.
- **Strength work — a coached single-set protocol.** A minimal, high-intensity single-set-to-failure split, deliberately structured so the per-session initiation and time cost stay low. Tied to the [environment lever](environment-lever.book-chapter.md#intervention-2--make-things-visible-to-direct-attention): bringing the weight set *into* the office produced his all-time record (40 workouts in 2 months).
- **Hiking** — Rock Canyon and Khyv Peak, the higher-effort outdoor form, affordable in better states.
- **Beat Saber — a "forgotten winner."** He once reached the top ~7th percentile; it is high-quality cardio (aerobic plus arm) that stopped being done, not one that stopped working. Forgetting was *one* cause among several — its lapse is the [worked case of a stacked lapse](#beat-saber--the-worked-case-of-a-stacked-lapse) below, and the loss modes it surfaces generalize past it.
- **The Provo Rec Center gym** — the most capacity-expensive venue, gated to higher [Safety](safety.book-chapter.md) levels the way the [collaborator bring-in](central-loneliness/eating-as-care.book-chapter.md) and other surplus-only moves are.

Movement is governed by the same resource accounting as everything else: exercise costs Mana and Stamina ([four-resource cross-resource dynamics](four-resource-model.book-chapter.md#cross-resource-dynamics)), so at the [Level -2 self-defeating window](#self-defeating-window-at-level--2) the recovery-bearing forms become unaffordable exactly when most needed — which is why the cheap, regulation-doubling baseline (walking, pacing) carries the floor and the expensive forms wait for surplus.

### Beat Saber — the worked case of a stacked lapse

A "forgotten winner" rarely dies of one thing. Beat Saber — high-quality cardio he was once globally good at — fell out of the repertoire from a **stack** of independent causes, no single one of which would have been decisive. Naming the stack matters more than naming any one cause, because most of the layers are **generalizable Alan-patterns**, not facts about this one game:

1. **The May tech-purge.** He dropped nearly all tech to cope with a severe depression over the state of the world, and the headset went into storage with everything else. A whole-environment removal, since [out of sight mostly stops existing](environment-lever.book-chapter.md). Now resolved.
2. **AirPods Bluetooth contention.** A single pair of AirPods fighting to pair across desktop, laptop, phone, and headset added a small, repeated friction at the start of every session. Mechanically trivial (a per-device USB dongle fixes it), but a [task-initiation cost](environment-lever.book-chapter.md) sitting right at the threshold is exactly where activities die.
3. **Disappointment-shock defensive quit.** *(Generalizable.)* Fast early leaderboard progress, then a plateau, then a defensive temporary quit — a recurring shape Alan describes as being a "serial quitter." The quit is a protective reflex at the moment progress stalls, not a verdict on the activity's value; the tell is that he **returns to things that are genuinely valuable** once the shock fades. So a disappointment-quit is a *new loss mode* on top of the [disqualification and forgetting](safe-foods.book-chapter.md#how-foods-enter-and-leave-the-set) that drop aligned foods: an option can leave the retained set by a plateau-triggered flinch and still be worth re-entering.
4. **Injury-triggered pause that never self-cancelled.** *(Generalizable.)* Shoulder and elbow pain after overplaying produced a sensible defensive pause — but the pause had **no built-in return cue**, so it never expired, and a valuable activity quietly died of a *temporary* injury. This is the sharp lesson: a defensive pause without an external return cue is indistinguishable, weeks later, from a permanent stop. It argues directly for **external return-cues** on any pause taken for a transient reason — the [environment lever](environment-lever.book-chapter.md) pointed at restarting rather than starting.
5. **Re-establishment is gated at a higher Safety level than maintenance.** *(Generalizable.)* Restarting a lapsed habit costs more than sustaining an active one — it carries a higher [difficulty level](safety.book-chapter.md#re-establishment-is-gated-higher-than-maintenance), so it needs an **expansive (high) Safety level** to clear the gate. Through ~6 weeks below that threshold a restart was simply not an option; as Safety stabilizes at 4-5 the re-entry comes back within reach. Maintenance would have been affordable the whole time — it is the *re-establishment* that was gated out.
6. **A stress association on the headset itself.** *(Generalizable — object-level.)* The headset carries a moderate-but-real stress association that creates [task-initiation friction](safe-foods.book-chapter.md#selection-criteria--four-attribute-affordability-checklist) before the cardio ever begins — the **same shape as Alan's [conceptual food aversions](safe-foods.book-chapter.md#selection-criteria--four-attribute-affordability-checklist)**, applied to an *object* rather than a food: a thing entangled with a dissonant association becomes a stress-cue, and the friction lands at the start. The health-coaching rule that follows is narrow and firm: **don't build movement on a lever that requires pushing through dissonance to start** — that breaks Alan's own [affordability rules](safety.book-chapter.md#safety-as-the-current-binding-constraint) and is the wrong foundation for a habit. The source of that association is sensitive, sits outside the movement model, and is treated strictly as lever data in [central-loneliness.md → the unspoken domain](central-loneliness.book-chapter.md#an-unspoken-domain-compounds-the-loneliness--sexuality-and-attraction); the deeper work there is not Health's lane.

The composite lesson: when a valuable activity lapses, the recovery move is to **disassemble the stack** rather than re-summon willpower — clear the friction (the dongle), set an external return-cue (defeat the never-cancelling pause), wait for the Safety level a re-establishment needs, and refuse to rebuild on a dissonance-gated lever. Several of these layers are the same loss/re-entry modes that govern [aligned foods](safe-foods.book-chapter.md#how-foods-enter-and-leave-the-set) and the broader [aligned-options lifecycle](points-as-hunger-substitute.book-chapter.md#how-this-composes-with-the-rest-of-the-model); Beat Saber is just where the whole stack happened to land on one activity at once.
