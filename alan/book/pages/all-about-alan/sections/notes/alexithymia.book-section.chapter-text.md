
# Alexithymia

> Alexithymia — difficulty recognizing own and others' emotions. The emotional substrate runs largely unconscious; conscious access is ~10% of what others seem to have, restricted to present-moment signal.

- Significant alexithymia caused by the aphantasia.
- It is quite difficult to understand his own emotions and those of others when he cannot remember past emotions and cannot imagine future emotions or even present emotions for someone else.
- Disabled for emotional intelligence; enabled for conceptual intelligence.

## The precise locus — the missing narrative substrate

The alexithymia is not a failure of *sensing*. The mechanism is sharper than "the aphantasia causes it." Alan models an emotion as a **sensation plus a narrative** — the present-moment body signal plus the story that runs over time and gives it meaning. The sensation channel works (interoception is intact though [low-salience](sensory-experience.book-chapter.md) — the body-level signal fires quietly, competing with the external sensory field; see [Conscious vs. unconscious emotional architecture](#conscious-vs-unconscious-emotional-architecture) below). What breaks is the narrative half: the story normally runs along a **continuous sensory memory**, and total aphantasia removes that substrate, so Alan's emotional narratives are confined to the present moment.

So the locus of the break is precise: not the sensing, but the **missing sensory-memory track the narrative is supposed to run on**. Full model — sensation + narrative, the six [primary colors](emotion-model.book-chapter.md#primary-colors--the-consistently-read-basic-emotions) he reads consistently, and emotions experienced as single [brushstrokes rather than paintings](emotion-model.book-chapter.md#brushstrokes-not-paintings) — in [emotion-model.md](emotion-model.book-chapter.md). This same missing-narrative-substrate is one face of the single [missing simulator](missing-simulator.book-chapter.md) that also accounts for the absent imagery and the absent anticipatory dread.

## Conscious vs. unconscious emotional architecture

The standard alexithymia framing assumes the emotional substrate is itself flat — no felt emotion, full stop. For Alan that's inverted: the substrate runs, but conscious access is the bottleneck.

- **Conscious access is ~10% of what others seem to report.** And what does come through is restricted to *present-moment* emotion. No conscious projection into past or future emotional states. This is the affective face of the [present-tense model](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md): one model of the world that exists only in the present, with no offline copy to run backward or forward — the emotional channel shares the present-only shape. Because no affect carries across time, the conceptual map of a past event is [affectively flat](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md#the-map-is-affectively-flat--structure-no-charge), and [emotional attachment cannot form](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md#no-attachment--structurally-not-as-a-failure-of-caring) — there is no store for the charge to accrue in.
- **The substrate runs unconscious and affects physiology.** Emotions hit at the body level — physiological state, nervous-system load, autonomic tone — even when no conscious emotional content registers. The hardware runs even when access is denied. The unconscious footprint is plausibly larger than the conscious one.
- **High intensity when emotions *do* surface.** When conscious access opens, the magnitude is large — likely the autistic NS-sensitivity composition surfacing (see [nervous-system-sensitivity.md](nervous-system-sensitivity.book-chapter.md)). Rare-but-strong, not flat-everywhere.
- **It took a long time to realize this.** The standard flat-affect reading was Alan's own self-model for years. The inversion landed only when the body-level signal became distinguishable from the conscious channel.

The Spock pattern in [executive-function.md → emotional control](executive-function.book-chapter.md#emotional-control) names the same architecture from the EF side: under-the-surface body-level emotion at stronger-than-normal force; conscious experience with almost no emotional content; brittle when overload finally hits.

### The access line has a level on it — 3

The substrate-runs-but-access-is-the-bottleneck architecture above now has a number attached to where access opens. In my words, describing what made the [Safety Years](safety-years.book-chapter.md) rewrite of my past admissible:

> by recognizing that I have emotional experiences on the physiological side even before I gain access to them at Level 3

So the conscious channel opening is tied to a [Safety level](safety.book-chapter.md#scale-0-5-with-half-steps), and the level named is 3 — the can-feel-happiness rung. Below it the emotional experience is running physiologically and I cannot read it; at 3 and above I can. That is the same two-layer architecture this section already holds, with the boundary between the layers placed on the Safety scale rather than left as a general fact about me.

What it cost me before I saw it is in [safety-years.md → what made the move admissible](safety-years.book-chapter.md#what-made-the-move-admissible): I had been reading *couldn't enjoy it* as *was miserable*, which treats a stretch below the access line as a stretch with nothing in it.

### Naming the feeling and reporting the empty well *is* the read

Alan distrusts his own emotional read — *"not sure I have that much emotional intelligence."* The distrust is mismeasuring the skill. When he names a present feeling, distinguishes its flavors (sadness *for the pain*, grief *for the life that could have been different* — two separate threads off one surface), and then reports accurately when **there is no more here right now**, that *is* the emotional-read skill operating, not a failure of it. Reading "the well has run dry" correctly is a reading, the same as reading the feeling itself — it requires knowing what the channel feels like when it's empty versus when it's withholding.

This matters because his self-model penalizes the wrong thing. With conscious access at [~10%](#conscious-vs-unconscious-emotional-architecture) and emotions arriving as single [brushstrokes, not paintings](emotion-model.book-chapter.md#brushstrokes-not-paintings), the failure mode he fears (low emotional intelligence) is not what's happening when he hits the bottom of a feeling and says so. The accurate report of a thin signal is the competent use of a thin channel, not evidence the channel is broken.

One qualification, so "his reports are always true readings of a present emotion" is not read too strongly: an accurate emotion-report is not always a reading of a *present feeling.* Some of his assents to an emotion-word are a behavior correctly classified against an external definition — *"this would be considered generous"* — with no present feeling under it at all. That report is still accurate, but accuracy-of-classification ≠ presence-of-feeling. The two modes (read a present tell vs. match a definition) are drawn out in [self-instrumentation.md → two modes of assent](self-instrumentation.book-chapter.md#two-modes-of-assent--read-a-tell-a-vs-classify-against-a-definition-b); the keystone question whenever he assents to an emotion-word is which mode it is.

## The invisible emotional reservoir

Alan does not know for certain, but he believes a large quantity of invisible emotional weight sits under the surface. He holds the belief on [compression](compression-epistemology.book-chapter.md) grounds: it is what his most compact explanation of himself requires. The reservoir is the simplest account that fits the evidence, so he believes it until something more compact replaces it — the same epistemology he applies everywhere.

The reservoir has a clean mechanism behind it. Emotions are [encoded continuously but cannot be recalled while awake](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md#the-break-is-at-recall-not-perception-or-encoding) — the Recall break leaves affect written but, awake, unread and undischarged. A store of feeling with no ordinary waking discharge route is exactly what an invisible reservoir would be.

When feeling does surface, Alan welcomes it. Each moment a feeling "pokes through" is a reading on something he infers is down there: **"I'm feeling, which is always a good sign."** For someone whose waking emotions are single present-tense [brushstrokes](emotion-model.book-chapter.md#brushstrokes-not-paintings), the mere presence of feeling is itself signal.

Three converging lines of evidence:

- **Emotions come through strong when they arrive.** This could be high [nervous-system sensitivity](nervous-system-sensitivity.book-chapter.md) alone, but it could also indicate pressure — built-up weight pushing from behind a barrier.
- **Serious physical symptoms of high chronic [allostatic load](allostatic-load.book-chapter.md).** An undischarged emotional reservoir is one plausible contributor to that load.
- **Persistent emotional weight appears to be the human default.** Alan cites *The Body Keeps the Score* (van der Kolk) — the body carries unresolved emotional load as a matter of course.

None of this is direct measurement; the [interoceptive gap](#conscious-vs-unconscious-emotional-architecture) means he cannot read the reservoir off a felt signal. It is an abductive inference, held because it compresses the evidence, not because it is observed.

### Drain vs. relieve — a structured prior over whether the reservoir can be emptied

The open question is whether a discharge — [cheap fiction surfacing a feeling](fiction-as-catharsis.book-chapter.md), the breathing protocol, a somatic modality — **drains** the reservoir (a durable drop in the weight) or only **relieves** it (the pressure eases, then refills). Asked directly, Alan does not claim to know; he gives a **structured prior over the possibility space** rather than an observation:

- **40% — drained.** The discharge durably lowers the weight. His best guess, at 40% confidence.
- **30% — relieved but not drained.** The pressure eases and refills; the reservoir is held, not emptied. He flags he is unsure this is even *observably distinct* from drained.
- **20% — no actual impact.** The felt discharge doesn't move the underlying weight at all.
- **10% — something unforeseen.** A mechanism outside the three named.

Two things about the shape of this prior are load-bearing.

**It stands on no observation at all.** The 40% is not read off any measured drop in the weight — there is no instrument for the reservoir ([interoceptive gap](#conscious-vs-unconscious-emotional-architecture)). It is a [compression-grounded](compression-epistemology.book-chapter.md) prior over the space of what *could* be true, assigned by the [math-frame mind](perceived-certainty.book-chapter.md) that reasons in distributions over possibilities rather than waiting for a reading. This is the [self-instrumentation discipline](self-instrumentation.book-chapter.md) operating where even the output-observer has nothing to read: with no proxy in reach, he holds a calibrated prior instead of a measurement.

**The prior may never update.** Alan flags that the top two options — *drained* and *relieved-but-not-drained*, together **70% of the mass** — may be **observationally indistinct**: both feel like the pressure easing, and nothing he can currently observe separates a durable drain from a refilling relief. So there may be **no distinguishing signal in reach**, which means the prior may never collapse toward one answer. He does not claim no such signal *exists* — only that none is currently reachable — so he holds the prior open rather than forcing it shut. This is the calibrated-prior-over-an-unmeasurable-interior pattern at full strength: a structured distribution he is at peace not collapsing, because the evidence that would collapse it may not be obtainable.

**Tension with the affectively-flat map.** The "drained" possibility leans *against* the [present-tense model's](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md#the-map-is-affectively-flat--structure-no-charge) flat claim that *no affect carries across time*. If a discharge today durably lowers the reservoir tomorrow, then *something* affective is persisting across the gap — which a strictly present-only affective channel forbids. The two can be reconciled (the *reservoir* is an unconscious physiological store, not the conscious affect the flat-map claim is about; the conscious map can be flat while an unconscious load persists and drains) but the reconciliation is unconfirmed, and Alan records the tension rather than resolving it. It is one reason "drained" sits at only 40%.

## Operational consequences

- **Must manage something he can barely perceive.** Emotional state shapes physiology and behavior, but the steering wheel is mostly behind opaque glass. This is a significant challenge — the standard "notice what you're feeling" intervention assumes a perception channel that mostly isn't there.
- **Other people's decisions seem primarily emotion-driven; Alan's are not.** Most decisions around him appear to be made on felt-pull / felt-aversion grounds. Alan's run on cognition + physiological-state instead — explicit reasoning over rule sets and value structures, modulated by current resource state, rather than felt preference.
- **Value system encoded as rules rather than felt as preferences.** What others register as "I want / don't want X" Alan registers as "X scores higher / lower against the rules." The output can match, but the generating mechanism is different.

## Cultural resonance — Spock and Data

Two Star Trek characters resonate as operating-mode matches: Spock and Data. Both operate cognitively with the emotional layer concealed (Spock) or absent (Data). The match is on operating mode, not on the canonical "no emotion" reading — the substrate is still there in Alan's case, just inaccessible at the conscious level.
