
# The environment lever

> The environment lever — the second of the two ways Alan changes what he wants. Because his identity is present-bound with no spontaneous recall, his environment functions as his externalized memory, attention, and agenda; he authors it so the only wants that re-fire are the ones he endorses. Two interventions (remove-to-reduce-load and make-visible-to-direct-attention), trigger curation, and the build-it-or-it-disappears failure mode.

A want has two upstream causes — belief and environment ([emotional-archaeology.md → changing the want, not the action](emotional-archaeology.book-chapter.md#changing-the-want-not-the-action)). [Emotional Archaeology](emotional-archaeology.book-chapter.md) is the method for the belief lever. This note is the second lever: change the environment, and the want changes. Why authoring the environment *is* authoring the wants is [wanting-as-gap-closing.md](wanting-as-gap-closing.book-chapter.md): a want is the desire to close a perceived gap, the want fires automatically from the perception, and the perception is the one point Alan can move — so controlling the triggers is the only upstream lever there is.

The environment lever is far more load-bearing for Alan than it is for most people, and the reason is mechanical. His identity is a present-bound, roughly-3-second [discrete self](discrete-self.book-chapter.md) running over one [present-tense model](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md) with no spontaneous recall — [recognition works, recall is broken](recognition-vs-recall.book-chapter.md). Nothing surfaces on its own. A want is not held as a continuous intention that persists across time; it has to be **regenerated in the present** by something he can see. His cognition is **trigger-driven, not recall-driven**: a cue fires, and the cue retrieves the next thing ([trigger-based-memory.md](trigger-based-memory.book-chapter.md)).

So the environment is not the backdrop his agenda runs against. The environment **is** his memory, his attention, and his agenda — externalized. What is present in it is what can come to mind; what is absent from it mostly cannot. He authors the complete list of what is even allowed to cross his mind by authoring what surrounds him.

## Out of sight mostly stops existing

Without memory, it is much less frequent for something to come to mind. Alan needs a **direct trigger of some sort**. Put plainly: **out of sight mostly stops existing.** For most people a want that drops out of view persists quietly as a remembered intention and resurfaces later. For Alan there is no quiet backlog to resurface from — the remembered-intention store is the thing that does not exist. If the cue is gone, the want does not fire, and a want that does not fire is, functionally, not there.

This cuts both ways, which is what makes the environment a lever rather than only a constraint. The same fact that lets a wanted thing vanish lets an unwanted want be removed by removing its cue. The two interventions below are the two directions of that one fact.

## Intervention 1 — remove things to reduce load

Take a cue out of the environment, and the want it fired stops firing. Two worked examples:

- **No visible clocks.** Alan updated his desktop environment to have no visible clocks. The time-pressure stimulus is what generates the want to hurry; removing the stimulus changes the want, toward less hurry. This is in the service of **"The Ruthless Elimination of Hurry"** (John Mark Comer) — the principle that hurry is a thing to be removed at the source rather than managed in the moment. (The same no-clock move appears as one urgency input among many in [urgency-removal.md](urgency-removal.book-chapter.md); here it is an instance of the general load-reduction rule rather than of urgency specifically.)
- **A minimalist aesthetic.** Alan keeps a very minimalist aesthetic in his own spaces. Less in the environment is less to load — fewer cues firing fewer wants, so the present-moment trigger field stays quiet enough that the wants he endorses are not drowned out.

## Intervention 2 — make things visible to direct attention

The opposite direction. Put a cue into the environment where he will see it, and the want fires from the sight of it. The worked example:

- **The weight set.** Alan moved his weight set from right outside his office to inside his office. Then he **worked out 40 days over the course of 2 months — an all-time record for him.**

Why this works for him specifically is the recall fact again. There is no continuous "I should exercise" thread persisting across time to act on. With the weights outside the office, the intention had to be recalled spontaneously — and spontaneous recall is the broken step, so it mostly never fired. With the weights inside the office, the *sight* of them regenerates the intention each present moment. Visibility supplies the trigger the missing recall cannot; the want re-fires; the behavior follows. The 40-in-2-months record is what happens when a want that kept failing to surface is given a standing cue to surface from.

## Trigger curation — removing high-entropy triggers

If the environment is the complete list of what can cross his mind, then what is *in* it is a choice, and a wrong choice is expensive. The danger is **high-entropy triggers** — cues that fire his attention in many unchosen directions at once. The example: **social media.** A high-entropy trigger does not regenerate one endorsed want; it scatters attention across a wide spread of wants he did not pick.

So Alan has **removed most of his high-entropy triggers.** Curating them out keeps his trigger-environment firing only the wants he endorses. This is the same architecture as [urgency-removal.md](urgency-removal.book-chapter.md) — push the work upstream into the structure of the environment rather than fighting each unwanted pull in the moment, because in-the-moment gating depends on EF he can't rely on — applied to the full attention field rather than to urgency alone. The point of authoring the environment is not only to *add* the cues he wants; it is to *deny entry* to the cues that would author his attention for him.

## The failure mode — build it or it disappears

The cost of a trigger-mind, stated at full strength in his words: **"I either build them into my environment or they disappear."**

There is no middle state. A thing that is not built into the environment is not held in reserve — it falls out of the environment and falls out of existence. This is the [out-of-sight rule](#out-of-sight-mostly-stops-existing) lived as a life-arc rather than a daily inconvenience. Alan has **had a lot of things disappear, especially through years when he couldn't afford them.** Scarcity narrowed what he could build into his environment, and everything that didn't make the cut was gone — not deferred, gone.

This is the same shape as the contraction in another domain. The connection account ran the same way: an environment too narrow to carry the wanted thing forces the thing out, and recovery is a slow re-expansion as capacity returns ([connection-expansion.md](connection-expansion.book-chapter.md), [conceptual-reallocation.md](conceptual-reallocation.book-chapter.md) on the capacity that gets reallocated). Now, **as he is recovering, he is starting to expand again after contracting so far.** The environment that shrank to almost nothing under scarcity is widening again, and with it the set of wants that have a standing cue to fire from.

## The third lever — physiology and the safety-gating

Belief and environment are the two levers on *what he wants*. There is a third lever, and it sits one step downstream: **physiology — his current [Safety](safety.book-chapter.md) / resource state — gates which wanted behaviors he can actually afford to act on.** The want can fire correctly and the cue can be present, and the behavior still won't run if the state isn't there to pay for it. This is not a third upstream cause of the want (the two-cause model above holds); it is the affordability gate between a fired want and an executed action.

The gate is what makes the same option reachable at one state and unreachable at another. It is the mechanism behind the [aligned-options lifecycle](points-as-hunger-substitute.book-chapter.md#how-this-composes-with-the-rest-of-the-model): an option can be discovered, qualified, and retained, and still sit unfired because the present Safety level won't clear its cost — which is why low-state floors (no affordable food in the room, the expensive gym out of reach) appear even when the belief and environment levers are set correctly. The three levers compose: belief and environment shape the want, physiology gates the act. Applied to eating, the [points trigger](points-as-hunger-substitute.book-chapter.md) is this full lever set pointed at a single domain — a manufactured present-moment cue (environment), built on a gamified self-model (belief), fired only when the state affords the reach (physiology).
