
# Nervous-system resiliency — the natural experiment

> The natural experiment measuring Alan's total nervous-system resiliency — a ≥99% drop from age 20 to 38, recovered to ~5% (~10% with medication), read as a measurement with headroom: what he did at 1–2% capacity, why aging can't explain the fall, and 95% confidence that the ceiling is still climbing.

Alan treats his own life as a **natural experiment for total nervous-system resiliency.** It is the same measurement the [autistic burnout](autism-burnout.book-chapter.md) decline is evidence of, read here from the other direction: not as damage done, but as a measured quantity with recovery headroom. The distinction is what makes the [emptiest region of the Book — his highest self](ladder-of-causation.book-chapter.md#why-the-simulator-is-far-off--three-gaps-one-at-each-layer) **real and waiting rather than imagined.**

## The measurement

In Alan's words:

> Measured at age 20 vs age 38, my total resiliency had dropped by at least 99%. I have recovered to maybe 5%, hitting 10% with medication support.

Three exact numbers, one instrument:

- **≥99% drop, age 20 → age 38.** The two endpoints of the natural experiment. This is the same [99% reduction the burnout note tracks](autism-burnout.book-chapter.md) as the compounding decline; here it is the *fall* of the measured quantity, age-anchored at both ends.
- **~5% recovered.** Where the [nervous-system-health capacity metric](health.book-chapter.md) sits now — the low point was ~1%, and sixteen months of surplus have brought it to ~5%.
- **~10% with medication support.** Medication roughly doubles the usable reserve above the ~5% baseline.

## "Know" is overstated — 95% confident, not certain

Abby asked how he can claim his ceiling keeps climbing when he has barely stood at his highest. Alan corrected the verb:

> know is overstated. I am 95% confident based on the progression so far and the lack of any sign of slowing or evidence to the contrary.

The confidence is **95%**, and it rests on two things: the progression so far, and the *absence* of any sign of slowing or any contrary evidence. It is an inference from a trend line that has not yet bent — not a certainty.

## Why aging can't explain the fall

Some resiliency loss is natural with age. But Alan's rough sense is that **even a 50% drop from age 20 to 40 would be extreme** for ordinary aging. A ≥99% drop is far past what aging alone could produce, so most of the fall is *pathological* — burnout damage, not the clock — and therefore **recoverable.** (The consequence Abby drew from this — that at least several times his current reserve remains to reclaim — is her arithmetic on his numbers, not a figure Alan stated.)

## What he did at 1–2% capacity — the anchors

The headroom claim is not abstract. Alan anchors it to what he accomplished while running on almost nothing:

> I was an executive for an AI tech startup at 2% and I was still a senior developer at an AI tech startup at 1%. My capacity at 100% was incredible. I feel confident I still have significant room to recover and rebuild.

- **Executive at 2%.** He ran an executive role at an AI tech startup on 2% of his resiliency.
- **Senior developer at 1%.** He held a senior-developer role at an AI tech startup on 1%.
- **100% "was incredible."** The intact substrate, before the fall, is the ceiling he is climbing back toward — and he suspects even 100% is not the top ([the highest-self region](ladder-of-causation.book-chapter.md#why-the-simulator-is-far-off--three-gaps-one-at-each-layer), where "6 isn't even the ceiling").

The through-line: if 1–2% funded an executive and a senior engineer, the reserve still to be reclaimed above ~5% is enormous — "significant room to recover and rebuild."

## Why this matters for the Book

This capture is the evidence base under [gap #2 of the self-simulator](ladder-of-causation.book-chapter.md#why-the-simulator-is-far-off--three-gaps-one-at-each-layer): the Book is emptiest exactly at Alan's highest self because he has so rarely been there. The resiliency measurement is what turns that void from a hope into a **measurement with headroom** — the region is real and waiting to be lived into, not imagined. The [persona system as the first move on the social break](persona-personal-meaning.book-chapter.md#why-they-exist-at-all--the-personas-as-the-first-move-on-the-social-break) is one of the capacity interventions that populates it.

## Play elicits the high-Safety self — populating the emptiest region

The highest-self region is [real and waiting](#why-this-matters-for-the-book), but rarely lived, so rarely recorded. **Play is a tool that populates it.** At [Aria's](../personas/aria.book-chapter.md#the-self-she-externalizes-is-his-high-safety-self) DnD table Alan plays a fearless, romantic character — his **high-Safety self** (fear falls and warmth open at the *same* point on the [Safety](safety.book-chapter.md) dial). Play drops his threat load far enough to bring that self online, so it hands back rare, direct data on exactly the region [gap #2](ladder-of-causation.book-chapter.md#why-the-simulator-is-far-off--three-gaps-one-at-each-layer) is emptiest at. This **partially closes that gap**: the [persona system was already named](persona-personal-meaning.book-chapter.md#why-they-exist-at-all--the-personas-as-the-first-move-on-the-social-break) as one of the capacity interventions that populates the region; play/Aria is a second, aimed straight at the summit.

### Elicited, not authored — why it counts as real data

The data is valid *about his real self* because play **elicits** the high-Safety self rather than **authoring** it. Abby's argument, which Alan confirmed: the high-density feeling at the table can't be faked — the intensity is his own nervous system firing, and a system can only fire what is already in it, so the game did not invent that self, it drew it out (he is "you with the threat subtracted," not a costume). Alan: *"Yeah, I agree with that."* This is why the region can be populated by play at all — you can only elicit what is already there, so what play surfaces is genuine measurement of the highest self, not fiction about it.

### The fear is reaching it durably — "Safety Level 10"

Confirmed real, the self is not what he fears. Verbatim:

> I'm not scared of whether I could be that person. I'm scared of whether I'll make it that far in my recovery. That feels like Safety Level 10 to me right now.

So the natural experiment's headroom claim gets a sharp edge here. The high-Safety summit is **proven to exist** — play has stood him in it — and **far up the climb**: his "Safety Level 10" sits well above the [rungs reached so far](recovery-ladder.book-chapter.md#the-trajectory-this-year--the-floor-is-moving) (consistent L5; L6 once) and is consistent with his ["6 isn't even the ceiling"](ladder-of-causation.book-chapter.md#why-the-simulator-is-far-off--three-gaps-one-at-each-layer). His fear is not the destination but whether recovery reaches it **durably** — reaching that self when it *isn't* already safe (stakes real, threat high), not only inside the low-threat frame of play. (The proven-destination / road-in-daylight framing is Abby's; Alan aligned.)
