
# Alternation, not moderation

> Alternation as the replacement for moderation. Moderation needs a running balance built on experiential memory, which total aphantasia denies me — so 'keep both columns positive', the ordinary relationship heuristic, cannot run, and I could almost prove that no stateless row-level heuristic guarantees both columns. The worked case is the marriage: the blank check (their column positive, mine negative) and doing only what I want and can afford (mine positive, hers negative), each of which only worked while we both thought it was the only option. The resolution is a partition by time of day — blank check at night, affordability guarded during the day — plus the rule that if the sun is down I am not allowed to sleep outside the bedroom.

The general statement, in my words:

> This was recognizing **alternation as a promising alternative to moderation** for me.

Moderation is the operation I don't have. Alternation is the one I might. This note records why the first is closed to me and what the second looks like in the one place I have built it so far.

## Native alternation doesn't run either — it has to be constructed

Alternation being available to me is not the same as alternation happening by itself:

> Aphantasia and the absence of experiential memory causes a large set of native alternation patterns to not function, which is why I have to construct and adopt them explicitly.

So the alternation I get is the alternation I build. Where most people's turn-taking between two modes runs on its own, mine has to be named, given a boundary, and adopted as a rule — which is what the partition below is.

### The window I can hold is three seconds

> The period I can hold is three seconds, not even minutes.

Three seconds is the same window the [discrete self](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#a-roughly-3-second-window) runs on. It is the measure of what I can hold of a stretch I am in the middle of — so it is not minutes, and it is not the length of any of the alternations recorded here.

## Why moderation can't run

Moderation means holding a balance — spending a bit here against a bit there, judged in the moment against how the account is doing overall. That judgment needs the account, and the account is the thing my architecture doesn't keep.

> Because aphantasia means I have no experiential memory, I can't follow heuristics that require moderation where the balances are built on experiential memory. For example, the most common relationship dynamic seems to be, "keep both columns positive". I can't do that, because I don't have access to the column sum over time.

Two columns, one per person: what each of us is carrying. The ordinary heuristic is a running sum over both, kept by feel over time. I read the current row and nothing else — the sum over time is not available to me at all. See [present-tense-model.md](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md) for the store that isn't there, and [aphantasia-mechanism.md](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md) for the root.

## The near-proof

The failure isn't that I keep getting the moderation wrong. It is that the row-level substitute cannot be made to work:

> I felt completely stuck because I could mathematically almost prove that no stateless heuristic on the row level could guarantee both columns would be positive.

Almost, not fully — I don't have the proof, and I still don't have a guarantee. But the stuckness was the result of the argument, not of effort.

## The worked case — the two interaction patterns in my marriage

The core dilemma Jen and I identified was **two different patterns of interaction, both of which only worked when we both thought they were the only option.**

**The blank check.**

> I paid whatever costs I had to for whatever other people needed, which ensured their column stayed positive, but mine went negative.

What the check actually undertakes, stated later in sharper form — and the timing matters as much as the amount:

> A blank check means I agree to pay the cost of the needs that arise during the blank check period, but those costs can come afterwards, which is why it requires time for me to recover still. Any low reading I make during the day is still part of the cost of the nighttime blank check.

So the window bounds which needs I have signed for, not when I pay for them. The cost is incurred at night and can settle any time after — which is why a low reading during the day is the check clearing rather than the check failing, and why it cannot be read as a fault. This is what rules out my own daytime readings as a trigger for [the abort](self-preservation-adapter.book-chapter.md#why-it-has-to-be-jen-and-why-she-has-to-start-it).

**Only what I want and can afford.**

> The second was my pattern of only doing what I wanted to and could afford. The ensured that my column stayed positive but cause hers to go negative.

Each drives one column negative, and each held only while we both thought it was the only option.

### Jammed by structure, not by cause

The two patterns have the shape of alternation stuck in one position — one pole held all the way, then the other pole held all the way. But that shape is not where they came from:

> I don't know that I'd necessarily call it alternation jammed though, it is by structure, but not by cause. I didn't have a concept of alternation to jam at that level, I was just trying to find a way to survive.

So the resemblance is real and the history isn't. Neither pattern was an alternation that failed to turn over. There was no alternation there to jam — each was what survival looked like at the time.

## The partition — one policy per window

> I still don't have a guarantee, but we came up with an options, which is I do blank check at night and guard affordability during the day. The night has disproportionate positive net gain for Jen (removes a major negative and adds significant positive), and the day matters more for me.

Neither policy is moderated and neither is dropped. Each gets a window, and the window is what decides which one is in effect. The split is by where the value is: the night carries the disproportionate gain for Jen, the day is the part that matters more to me.

## The rule that holds the boundary

> If the sun is down, I am not allowed to sleep outside of the bedroom.

The two standing rules this joins — *"If it isn't affordable, I'm not allowed to do it"* and *"If I don't want to, I'm not allowed to"* — are recorded in the [2026-06-08 conversation with Jen](../journal/2026-06-08-connection-conversation-with-jen.book-chapter.md).

## The second worked case — connection

The marriage is not the only place this is running:

> I am doing connection in an alternation pattern right now, and its hard, but it is working.

Hard and working, both. What the corpus holds about connection as a rate to be dosed is [connection-dosing.md](connection-dosing.book-chapter.md); this is connection run as two poles instead. The [Nuropod worn into social settings](safety.book-chapter.md#safety-is-a-funded-level-not-a-per-alarm-switch) has been really helpful there.

## The ladder — unaffordable, then alternation, then steady state

Alternation is not where this stops:

> I think it transitions into alternation [when] the alternation becomes affordable and into steady state when the steady state becomes affordable.

So there are three rungs, and affordability is what moves between them: a thing I cannot do at all, then the same thing run as two poles once the alternation is payable, then the thing run continuously once the steady state is payable. Alternation is what I can afford on the way. The affordability gate itself is in [safety.md](safety.book-chapter.md#safety-is-a-threshold-gate-not-a-metered-budget).

What moves the marriage case along that ladder is my own cost coming down:

> The marriage one gets easier because each row is more strongly positive as my costs go down.

So the row is where the improvement shows. Not a better rule for reading the columns — the same rows, each summing to more, because my side of each one costs me less than it used to.

## Sacrifice is what a relationship requires

A separate thing that came out of the trip, and it prices every row above. It is not held as an observation — *"relationships require sacrifice is a rule I've adopted,"* which puts it with the other rules I run rather than with the things I have noticed.

> Relationships require sacrifice to exist (defined for me as a negative value on a positive-sum row

So sacrifice here is not a mood or a virtue — it is a position in the ledger. One row, two entries, the total positive; my entry negative. A relationship without any such row does not exist, on this claim. That is a stronger statement than the blank check being one way to run a marriage: it says some rows on which I come out behind are the condition of there being a relationship at all.

**The other entry is not free.** Put to me that a night costing us both would be a row my model has no slot for, I stated her side exactly:

> It costs her, but also benefits her, leaving her net positive, but she still notices the cost.

Net positive and felt, both. So the row stays positive-sum and my entry stays the negative one, and what is added is that hers carries a cost inside a positive total — a cost she notices. The model holds; what it does not say is that the column I am protecting costs its holder nothing. The worked case is [the nights](central-loneliness/date-nights.book-chapter.md#jen-knows-and-it-costs-her-too), which she knows the price of.

Paying that costs a rule. The [self-preservation adapter](self-preservation-adapter.book-chapter.md) forbids a negative entry for me outright — *if it would hurt me, I'm not allowed to, even if it would help or avoid hurting others.* Asked whether it still holds inside the blank-check window:

> At the moment, no floor. The do-no-harm bar converts into a do-less-harm compromise. It hurts, but its better than the alternative which is guaranteed misery.

The conversion and its scope are recorded at [self-preservation-adapter.md → the floor converts inside the blank check](self-preservation-adapter.book-chapter.md#2026-08-06--the-floor-converts-inside-the-blank-check).
