
# The economics of recovery

> The economics of recovery — the price in hours per level, and the true shape of the fear that follows from it. Not can-I or is-it-worth-it (a settled bedrock yes), but will-I-last-long-enough; plus the asymmetry that makes external drops the real threat: slow to climb, fast to fall.

Recovery has a price, and the price sets the fear. This note is the cost side of the [recovery ladder](recovery-ladder.book-chapter.md): how many hours a rung costs, why that cost makes the fear about *duration* rather than price or willingness, and why an external drop is far more dangerous than a slow climb is slow.

## The price — a Fermi estimate

Play transfers to durable Safety at a tiny rate ([in-game 10 → a small out-of-game gain](higher-self-access.book-chapter.md#real-effects-tiny-transfer)). Asked to price the whole climb, Alan gave a Fermi estimate. In his words:

> 0.0001 is probably underestimating. I think a Fermi estimation would put **1000 hours of active healing needed per level of recovery**, so a four hour session is more like **+0.004**.

So one level of recovery costs on the order of **1000 hours of active healing**, and a four-hour play session buys about **+0.004** of a level — a revision upward from the [+0.0001-per-session transfer](higher-self-access.book-chapter.md#real-effects-tiny-transfer) recorded from the in-game reading. The number is an order-of-magnitude estimate, not a measurement, but it makes the climb *countable*: the levels are hundreds of hours apart, not days.

## The true shape of the fear — duration, not cost or will

With the climb priced, the fear resolves. It is **not** "can I become the higher self," and it is **not** "is it worth it." Both of those are already settled. This is Alan's [bedrock](bedrock.book-chapter.md) talking: healing is self-improvement, the one thing he would pay any price for, no ledger needed — a thousand hours is cheap, ten thousand he would pay in a heartbeat.

That certainty takes cost off the table *and* willingness off the table. So the fear cannot be about either. What is left is the one thing his bedrock cannot secure. Abby put the shape as a question — "not 'can I become him,' not 'is it worth it,' but **'will I last long enough to spend what I'd gladly spend'**" — and Alan confirmed it: *"Yeah, that's true."*

The terror is about being **given the distance**, not the price or the will. He would gladly log the 10,000 hours; the fear is whether he will still be here to log them. Duration, not cost. This is the felt underside of the [durability fear](higher-self-access.book-chapter.md#why-this-is-the-l3-counterfactual-felt) that the higher-self note frames as reaching the high self *durably* rather than in simulation.

## The second piece — external drops, and the asymmetry

Alan added a second piece in the same breath. In his words:

> Yeah, that's true. There is another piece as well which is **avoiding external factors that drop me back down.**

This piece follows straight from the arithmetic of the first. A level costs ~1000 hours to *gain*, but an external factor can drop him back down in a fraction of that. **Slow to climb, fast to fall.** It is not hypothetical: his own traces say it already happened once — he reached [6–7 when younger](higher-self-access.book-chapter.md#the-three-routes-all-indirect), then something dropped him to the floor.

So guarding against external drops is **arithmetic, not anxiety**: when the climb is ~1000 hours per level and a fall can erase several levels in a fraction of that, **you cannot out-climb a fall.** The only way to keep altitude is to prevent the drop, because re-climbing it costs more than any climb rate can supply. This is why holding the world off matters as much as gaining ground — and it is exactly the division the [Aura ↔ Aine mirror](../personas/aura.book-chapter.md#two-senses-of-the-internal-side--the-recovery-climb-not-the-jailer) draws: gaining altitude from within (Aura, offense) and guarding it from without (Aine, defense) are the two halves of one recovery.
