
# Automaticity systems

The neurotypical brain has an automatic compression-and-amortization layer that runs across many domains: sensory habituation, implicit social statistical learning, action-sequence chunking. In Alan, this layer is reduced or absent across multiple subsystems. The deliberate-effort pathway remains intact, and in some places exceeds typical performance.

A consistent signature shows up across every reduced subsystem: **slower start, higher ceiling**. Without the automatic accelerator that compresses experience into pattern, acquisition is slower. Without the automatic plateau caused by habituating to remaining mistakes, the ceiling is higher.

There is no single "System 1". Automaticity comes through several distinct neural substrates. Some of them work in Alan's brain; some don't. The picture is multi-system, not binary.

## The five systems

### A. Sensory habituation — REDUCED

- **Substrate:** LTD-like depression at sensory synapses (early sensory cortex, thalamic relays).
- **Typical:** stimulus intensity drops with repeated exposure — the AC, the shirt tag, and the fluorescent light fade out of awareness.
- **Alan:** sensory content does not become quieter with exposure.
- **Cost:** permanent sensory load. Canonical treatment in [non-habituation.md](non-habituation.book-chapter.md).

### B. Implicit social statistical learning — REDUCED

- **Substrate:** right posterior superior temporal sulcus (pSTS), the social statistical learner; dovetails with the broader two-systems mentalizing account (Apperly & Butterfill 2009).
- **Typical:** passive observation of others encodes social rules — when to make eye contact, conversational rhythm, status signals — without conscious effort. Same shape as language acquisition.
- **Alan:** at age 14, ran a deliberate yearlong conceptual investigation of social interaction rules and integrated them through System C instead.
- **Cost:** System B's job has to be done by System C, which is slower and more expensive. Canonical treatment in [social-experience.md](social-experience.book-chapter.md).

### B'. Externally-cued S-R habit learning — INTACT

- **Substrate:** dorsolateral striatum (DLS) sensorimotor loop. Dissociates from System D via Tanji & Shima 1998.
- **Typical:** a cue in the environment automatically triggers a learned response. Brake light → foot moves.
- **Alan:** intact, but with the slow-start / high-ceiling signature. Acquisition feels slower than typical because there's no implicit-learning accelerator. Plateau is higher because there's no habituation to remaining mistakes.
- **Concrete:** Alan can drive and "forget he is driving" — responses fire automatically on cues.
- **Note:** the slow-start / higher-ceiling signature ([below](#quantitative-shape--slow-start-higher-ceiling)) is most directly visible here — the deliberate pathway picks up work the implicit accelerator would otherwise do.

### C. Schema integration — INTACT

- **Substrate:** medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC) + anterior temporal lobe (ATL). Slow consolidation over days and weeks.
- **Typical:** a novel concept gets integrated into existing schemas. Once integrated, retrieval no longer requires effortful search — the concept surfaces when relevant.
- **Alan:** intact. This is what was originally called "internalization-until-automatic" in [perceived-certainty.md](perceived-certainty.book-chapter.md). It is a genuine route to automaticity, just via different machinery than habituation.
- **Concrete:** Alan's age-14 social-rules study integrated through this system. Concepts that get internalized work this way.

### D. Internally-generated action-sequence chunking — REDUCED

- **Substrate:** pre-SMA/SMA + DLS task-bracketing. Graybiel's start/stop bursts mark the beginning and end of a learned chunk (Graybiel & Grafton 2015).
- **Typical:** a sequence of steps performed many times gets compressed into a single chunk that runs without conscious step-by-step monitoring. Brushing teeth, the morning routine.
- **Alan:** even after thousands of repetitions, brushing teeth still runs from a familiar mental list. He's still consulting the list. The list never disappears.
- **Cost:** internally-generated sequences stay explicit indefinitely.
- **Note:** the slow-start / higher-ceiling signature applies here too, but with a harder ceiling — there's no clean substitute pathway for motor sequences (unlike the B'-substitutes-for-D trick that works for symbolic-token sequences).

## Discriminators between B' and D

Both involve sequences of action; only B' automates. Two clean discriminators:

1. **Effort and multitasking ceiling.** Following a sequence always feels effortful. Alan generally cannot perform two sequenced tasks simultaneously and correctly, but he can perform two responsive tasks simultaneously.
2. **Redirection opportunity.** When following a sequence, there is always an opportunity to redirect and do something else instead. Alan tries variations on sequences much more than he has observed others do — an advantage in skill refinement, offset by the increased cost.

Driving illustrates the split. System B' handles the responsive parts (cue → response), so Alan can drive on autopilot. System D would handle the navigation sequence (turn here, exit there) — but that does not automate. So when Alan drives on autopilot, he often does not arrive at the intended destination.

## Integration with the three-mode operating architecture

The five-system framework names *which* automaticity routes are available. The [three-mode operating architecture](operating-mode-architecture.book-chapter.md) names *how* the routes execute at runtime. The two compose, they don't replace each other.

- **Weight layer ≈ System D substrate.** The physical / weight layer where trained motor and action patterns run is the substrate System D would normally chunk into. In NTs, System D writes chunks here through implicit sequence statistical-learning. In Alan, System D's writer is reduced; the same substrate is still writable, but via deliberate-pathway training that clears the [5000-iteration crossover](slow-build-strategy.book-chapter.md#the-5000-iteration-anchor) on each sub-pattern.
- **External conceptual scaffolding (the agent harness) ≈ System D externalization for cognitive operations.** What System D can't chunk internally gets externalized into [the harness](agent-harness.book-chapter.md). The harness is the runtime delivery channel for the externalized step list; the externalized list is the System-D-substitute content.
- **Internal conceptual processing ≈ upstream training mechanism for both.** Offline rehearsal trains the weight-layer substrate and refines the conceptual scaffolding. The same "think about it a lot" activity feeds both routes — deliberate practice that lands on the weight layer, and conceptual elaboration that updates the externalized harness rules.

Frame the operating-mode architecture as integration, not replacement. The five-system framework is the substrate map (which channels exist); the three-mode architecture is the runtime map (how the channels execute and train).

## Compensation strategies

- **B' substitutes for D in symbolic-token sequences.** Alan creates conditional triggers — each word triggers the next word — using System B'. He has memorized entire books this way, faster than most. Does not transfer cleanly to motor sequences, where the cues are not as discrete or as reliably distinct. The same architecture extends well beyond text memorization — see [trigger-based-memory.md](trigger-based-memory.book-chapter.md) for the unification across three domains (text memorization, talks/presentations, the agent harness).
- **C substitutes for B in social rules.** Deliberate conceptual investigation plus schema integration replaces implicit social statistical learning. Slower (the age-14 study took a year). Higher ceiling (the integrated rules are explicit and falsifiable).
- **No D substitute exists for action-sequence chunking.** Mental lists for internally-generated sequences remain explicit indefinitely. Coping likely involves externalization rather than automation.

## On the System 1 / System 2 vocabulary

Kahneman's framing is a useful first pass — a fast automatic mode and a slow deliberate one. But "System 1" is not a single thing. Recent dual-process work (Evans & Stanovich 2013; Melnikoff & Bargh 2018) shows automaticity comes through several distinct substrates, the five above among them.

What Alan lacks is *some* of those routes (A, B, D). Others (B', C) work. "Autistic compensations stay System 2 indefinitely" overgeneralizes — it's accurate for the routes that don't work, wrong for the ones that do.

## Quantitative shape — slow start, higher ceiling

The signature has a measurable shape. A two-pathway exponential-approach model (implicit pathway with a residual-error floor; deliberate pathway with no floor; engagement gating so the implicit asymptote suppresses further deliberate practice) reproduces three numbers that match Alan's lived experience:

- **Acquisition is ~5–15x slower.** Central anchor: roughly 10x. Where peers reach a working level after a few hundred trials of casual exposure, the deliberate pathway needs a few thousand to reach the same level.
- **The long-run ceiling is ~10x lower in error rate.** Peers plateau around 10–15% residual error. The deliberate pathway drives that down by roughly an order of magnitude over the long tail — often to ~1% or below. At 10x past the crossover, the gap is wider still: the implicit-floor learner stuck near 15% error; the deliberate learner near 0.5%. ~30x error-rate gap.
- **Crossover lands around 5,000 trials of practice** — about 10x the typical-practitioner stopping point. Where peers stop earlier (say ~2,000 trials), the crossover never visibly arrives. From the outside, peers look permanently better. The strategic response — pick games whose horizon stretches past the crossover, cede games whose horizon doesn't — is captured in [slow-build-strategy.md](slow-build-strategy.book-chapter.md).

### Frame the gap as an error rate, not as a raw skill delta

A raw-skill delta of "+1.7%" sounds trivial. Reframed as "10x lower error rate" it reads correctly. The ceiling difference is the *floor* of mistakes — how many slips per hundred trials remain — not the height of skill. Always state these differences in error-rate terms.

### The mechanism: arrested development

Why does the implicit pathway have a *lower* ceiling than the deliberate one? Because hitting the implicit asymptote turns off further deliberate practice. The skill feels done. Ericsson called this arrested development; modern motor-learning work calls it premature automation. The implicit pathway saturates near good-enough, and that "good enough" signal suppresses further engagement of the slower deliberate pathway. The plateau isn't a hard limit on skill — it's the implicit floor turning off the deliberate-improvement engine.

In Alan, the implicit accelerator is reduced (System B; System D), so the saturation signal doesn't fire. The deliberate engine keeps running. That's the mechanism behind slow-start, higher-ceiling.

### What 10x slower looks like in practice

A skill peers pick up casually over a year of incidental exposure takes ~10 years of deliberate practice to match. For most of those 10 years, Alan looks worse at the skill than peers. Past the crossover, the gap inverts — Alan ends up significantly better than peers who plateaued, because the deliberate pathway never tapered off.

### Where the signature applies — and where it doesn't

At the group level, statistical learning is mostly *intact* in autism on standard SL paradigms (Foti et al. 2015 meta-analysis; Obeid et al. 2016 meta-analysis). The deficits cluster in two places:

- **Socially-cued SL** (System B in this framework — right pSTS).
- **Internally-generated sequence chunking** (System D — pre-SMA/SMA + DLS task-bracketing).

So the slow-start / higher-ceiling signature applies to skills that lean on those systems — most things social, action sequences that need to chunk. It does not apply broadly to all skill learning. Don't oversell the signature outside its domain.

### Where the numbers come from

The model is in [`automaticity-systems.sl-simulation.py`](../../../../all-about-alan/chapters/notes/automaticity-systems.sl-simulation.py). It runs two exponential-approach learning curves (implicit-pathway with a residual floor; deliberate-pathway without one) with engagement gating that suppresses the deliberate pathway as the implicit one saturates. Run with `python3 sl-simulation.py` to print acquisition rates, ceiling deltas, crossover trial, and a parameter sweep. The functional forms come from Heathcote, Brown & Mewhort 2000 (exponential learning curves at the individual level); the residual-floor anchor comes from Tsay et al. 2021/2022 (implicit motor adaptation); the slow-acquisition / comparable-or-better long-run shape matches Solomon et al. on probabilistic RL in autism.
