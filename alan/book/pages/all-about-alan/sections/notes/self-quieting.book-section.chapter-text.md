
# Self-quieting

> Self-quieting — keeping external expression quiet to keep the environment regulated. Loud expression amplifies others' responses, which amplify the environment back at Alan; quiet expression keeps the loop quiet.

Alan doesn't express emotions loudly. The reduced volume is deliberate, not affect-flatness — a self-regulation strategy with a specific feedback-loop mechanism.

## Mechanism

The environment responds to expressed signal. Loud expression amplifies others' responses, which amplify the environment back at Alan. Quiet expression keeps the loop quiet.

The strategy operates one step upstream of the standard sensory-regulation tools. Earplugs, sunglasses, and tight clothing cap input *after* the environment generates it. Self-quieting reduces the environment's generation of input in the first place.

## Why it matters specifically for Alan

Three factors compound to make the strategy load-bearing:

- **NS sensitivity.** Sensory and social inputs cost more per unit than they would for a typical nervous system ([nervous-system-sensitivity.md](book-section/all-about-alan/nervous-system-sensitivity)). The marginal cost of an amplified environment is high.
- **Non-habituation.** Repeated amplified responses don't fade with exposure ([non-habituation.md](book-section/all-about-alan/non-habituation)). Each loop iteration costs again.
- **Aphantasia + ~10% conscious emotional access.** Alan can't always tell from the inside when the loop is about to spin up. Keeping the input small by default is cheaper than detecting the spin-up after it starts.
