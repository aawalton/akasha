
# Visual-sensitivity bundle

> Visual-sensitivity bundle as a system — sunglasses outdoors, lights-off indoors, screen-brightness modes by time of day. Felt cost is Stress Capacity drain, not pain. Cohabitation accommodation, magnitude gap, and aspirations.

A coherent system across every screen and lighting environment: keep luminance and contrast as low as the task allows. Promoted from a starter sub-system in [sub-systems.md](book-section/all-about-alan/sub-systems) once enough captures named the bundle as one mechanism applied across modes.

## Protocol summary

Three modes, one underlying rule (low luminance, low contrast):

- **Outdoors — sunglasses on sunny days only.** Not overcast, not "generally outdoors" — only when ambient light is high enough to trigger squinting. Indoor sunglasses are considered but not currently used.
- **Indoors — lights off in rooms Alan is in.** Binary off / on lever; no continuous dimming on the current setup.
- **Screens — backlit, brightness mode tracks time of day.** Dark mode at night, sepia / light mode during the day. Same rule applies in bed (see [sleep.md → in-bed wind-down stack](book-section/all-about-alan/sleep#in-bed-wind-down-stack)) and in the bath (see [hot-bath.md → stack interactions](book-section/all-about-alan/hot-bath#stack-interactions)).

## Felt cost: Stress Capacity drain, not pain

High ambient light triggers visible squinting. The *felt* cost, though, is regulatory load on the four-resource bar — specifically a drain on Stress Capacity (see [four-resource-model.md](book-section/all-about-alan/four-resource-model)) — not nociception.

**Inverted observation.** Standard light-sensitivity framing uses pain language: bright light is "harsh", "stabbing", "painful". For Alan it is not pain. It is a tax on the same Stress Capacity bucket that social load, cognitive load, and unmanaged sensory input all draw from. The squinting is the body's response; the cost is upstream of the squint.

This places visual sensitivity on the same axis as other sensory drains (sound managed by the [auditory-sensitivity bundle](book-section/all-about-alan/auditory-sensitivity-bundle), touch managed by [the lotion regimen](book-section/all-about-alan/lotion-regimen), thermal managed by layers and the bath). Different modality, same resource.

## Magnitude gap

The cost is real but uninstrumented. No rate anchor analogous to:

- Sleep at 1 stress-capacity hour per hour slept (see [recovery-rates.md](book-section/all-about-alan/recovery-rates)).
- Hot bath at 3 stress-capacity hours per hour bathed (see [hot-bath.md](book-section/all-about-alan/hot-bath)).
- The auditory bundle dropping from a ~50% pre-AirPods baseline to a rare residual today (see [auditory-sensitivity-bundle.md → magnitude](book-section/all-about-alan/auditory-sensitivity-bundle#magnitude)).

A controlled comparison — sunglasses on vs. off across same-load conditions — would anchor a rate in the same shape. Flagged as a follow-up.

## Cohabitation accommodation

Same input, opposite valence. Alan drains on ambient light; his wife stimulates on it (see [sensory-experience.md](book-section/all-about-alan/sensory-experience) — her inverse profile is captured there: light = positive stim + executive-function support).

Resolution shape:

- **Per-room negotiation, default-defer.** Alan defers to his wife's preference by default unless he is "really not doing well" — at which point a low-capacity threshold triggers override.
- **The accommodation is an ongoing cost on Alan's stack.** Same shape as social-cost-via-accommodation patterns in [social-experience.md](book-section/all-about-alan/social-experience): the cost is not paid once when the rule is set, it is paid continuously while the accommodation runs.

This is one concrete instance of a more general pattern — same input, opposite valence within a household, resolved by default-defer-with-override. Worth probing whether other household accommodations (sound, temperature, scheduling) follow the same structure.

## Aspirations

Two specific upgrades, both moving up the [sensory-regulation-tools.md](book-section/all-about-alan/sensory-regulation-tools) hierarchy:

- **Adaptive (photochromic) sunglasses.** Continuous adjustment to ambient brightness — moves from "flexible" (manual on/off) to "automatic" (no conscious input). Externalizes the regulation entirely.
- **Continuous indoor light dimmers.** Moves from "on/off" (the current binary) to "flexible" (manual continuous adjustment). Lets the dose match the load instead of forcing over- or under-correction.

Both are concrete instances of the four-tier preference hierarchy: better tools beat worse tools because each tier reduces the EF cost of regulation.
