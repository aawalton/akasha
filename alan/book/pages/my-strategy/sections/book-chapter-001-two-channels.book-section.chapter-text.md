
# The two channels

> The two channels Alan's effort divides into — improving his ability to make improvements, and using that ability to improve everything else — stated on 2026-08-11. The second channel is organised by his six values (faith, health, wealth, learn, love, fun). Carries his reason for prioritising the first channel (it compounds), and why the second channel reaches him only through code: the alan-harness is where the digital prosthetics get built.

Everything I put effort into divides in two:

> My efforts there have two channels. The first is improving my ability to make improvements. The second is using that ability to make improvements in other areas.

The second channel covers my whole life, and I already have a structure for that:

> My entire life. I organize that into my six values (you can look those up)

The six are **faith, health, wealth, learn, love** and **fun**.

Strategy is not only the question of how to split effort across those six. The first channel counts too:

> Though strategy doesn't just mean determining allocation against the six values, investing in the ability to make improvements is in scope as well.

## Why the first channel goes first

> I have a general strategy of prioritizing improving the rate of improvement, because that compounds, and then improving the foundations of my health and life on top of that.

That is the reason and the whole of it: the rate of improvement compounds and a single improvement does not. The foundations of health and life get built on top of that rate, not instead of it.

This is one of two standing priority orderings I run. Stated as a pair:

> Accelerate Improvement > Apply Improvement

The other ordering is in [003-priorities.md](003-priorities.book-chapter.md).

## Why the second channel still runs through code

Asked whether the six values improve by some route other than building, I said they do not:

> The key is that to increase my benefit from those categories requires changes, generally code changes, because I need to iterate on the alan-harness to build in the digital prosthetics that compensate for the parts of my brain that don't work normally (total aphantasia, no experiential memory, no automaticity or habit-formation, for example)

So the two channels are not independent. Improving my life mostly *means* building the harness that lets me live it, because the parts of my brain that would otherwise do that work are missing and have to be replaced with something outside my head.

## Where I am not pointed

Asked which of the six values the strategy was aimed at:

> I don't, its pointed at all of them

And asked which value was moving least:

> I don't know which is moving least.

## What kind of question this is

> That's what we're here for. There is no single strategy for this, its complex, nuances, intuitive, and need puzzling out.

*Recorder's reading, marked.* The link between the two channels is the load-bearing claim on this page and it is easy to read past. The ordering "Accelerate Improvement > Apply Improvement" looks like a choice to defer his life in favour of his tooling. His own account of the prosthetics says it is not that: `alan-harness` is simultaneously one of the five foundational layers of the machine *and* the layer through which any of the six values actually improves. Accelerating improvement and applying it are the same act at that layer. See [005-the-pipeline.md](005-the-pipeline.book-chapter.md), where he says four initiatives are stacked against `alan-harness` — twice any other layer.
