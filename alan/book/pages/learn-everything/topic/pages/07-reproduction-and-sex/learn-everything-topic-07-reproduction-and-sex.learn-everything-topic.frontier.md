- The sexual side of the two-mutation comparison is unbuilt. He has the asexual side whole — the second mutation must arise again inside the first one's lineage, at the product of the two rates — but he has not said what a sexual population does with the same pair, which is to bring the two carriers together and make the double mutant in one mating at the sum of the rates rather than the product. Nor has he reached what becomes of A and B meanwhile in the asexual case, where the two lineages compete and the losing one is carried off with its mutation.
