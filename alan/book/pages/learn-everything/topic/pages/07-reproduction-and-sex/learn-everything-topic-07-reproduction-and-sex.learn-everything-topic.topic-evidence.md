- **Cleared:** named parasites and disease as the selective agent unprompted, which is the leading account of why sex persists. Reached monoculture vulnerability as the mechanism of exposure, and produced the Cavendish banana and Panama disease as a worked case without being asked for one.
- **Did not clear:** argued the benefit at the level of the species — that varied genomes improve the chances the species survives — where the twofold cost falls on one female in one generation. Did not part recombination from mutation as the source of the variation, and did not reach frequency-dependent selection, so nothing in his account pays the cost in the generation the cost is charged.
- **Cleared further:** moved the payoff into the right timeframe once the individual-level cost was pressed, and did it unprompted — reached that parasites turn over on a far shorter generation than their hosts, so a clone's reckoning arrives within a few host generations rather than over deep time. That is the coevolutionary core of the account that holds, and he built it himself from the cost argument rather than recalling it.
- **Cleared further:** answered that a common sexual genotype would be caught too, which is negative frequency-dependent selection reached unaided — he put the liability in commonness rather than in the reproductive mode, and a clone falls out as the limiting case. Went past the question twice over, unprompted: held that the offspring of even two shared parents carry more variation than a clone, which is segregation and recombination as novelty manufactured rather than uniformity merely avoided; and raised the boundary case of an artificially cloned genotype that is sexual in origin but clonal in distribution, to show the mechanism depends on how genotypes are spread rather than on what the mode is called. He built the novel case himself and then handled it.
- **Cleared:** parted recombination from mutation unaided, the plank the account had been missing. Put mutation as the source of genuinely novel alleles, valid or not, and recombination as a remix of material already filtered — every allele in the pool having been good enough to carry a parent to reproduction. Held recombination sufficient for variation in the short run and mutation necessary over the long run, on the ground that shuffling a fixed pool runs the pool down rather than replenishing it.
- **Did not clear:** the question was what a sexual population can do with one new mutation that a clonal one cannot, and how fast. He answered the distinction rather than the question, and did not reach that recombination brings two beneficial mutations arisen in different individuals into one genome, where a clonal lineage must wait for the second to arise inside the first.
- **Cleared:** asked for the fate of two beneficial mutations arising in two individuals of a large asexual population, he answered at once that the only route to a descendant carrying both is for the second to arise again inside the lineage already carrying the first, and then put a rate on it unasked — the two mutation probabilities multiply. That is the closure of asexual lineages named exactly, and the multiplicative cost of a double mutant is the quantity the whole Fisher–Muller argument depends on. He reached both without a prompt toward either.
