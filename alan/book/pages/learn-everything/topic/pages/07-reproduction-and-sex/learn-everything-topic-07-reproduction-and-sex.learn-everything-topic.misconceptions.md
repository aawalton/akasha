- Took the benefit of sex to accrue to the species over deep time. Selection keeps no account at the species level: an asexual mutant out-breeds her sexual neighbours every generation and owns the population long before any epidemic arrives, so a payoff that lands on the species cannot fund a premium charged on an individual. **Corrected in the same session** — pressed on who pays and who collects, he moved the payoff to the individual mother's own daughters in the next generation and rebuilt the argument from there without further prompting. The error was in where he located the accounting, not in the biology, and it did not survive being shown the ledger.
- Took recombination itself to spend variation — that shuffling a fixed pool of alleles tends toward homogenization over the long run, so mutation is needed to replenish what the shuffling used up. A shuffle changes which individuals carry which alleles and leaves the allele frequencies in the population where they were, so it spends nothing. The erosion he was pointing at is real and mutation is indeed the replenishment, but the eroding agent is drift and fixation rather than recombination. **Surfaced, not yet worked** — the correction was named to him and parked rather than pressed, and he has not been asked to rebuild the claim.
- Inverted which mode of reproduction lets selection read an allele's own fitness. Held that in the asexual case the only thing deciding an allele's survival is its direct fitness, and that recombination adds a combinatorial fitness on top of that. It is the other way round, and the inversion is the whole prize: with no recombination selection can weigh nothing smaller than the genome an allele sits in, which is exactly why a +3% mutation was annihilated by a +5% one sitting in a different genome. Recombination is what breaks an allele out of its background so that the allele's own effect is what selection reads. **Closed by being shown rather than by him** — pressed, he defended the inversion rather than yielding it, and the defence was half right: he answered that fitness is always relative, which was granted. But the baseline he reached for to price B was A's genome, and A and B are at different loci with no locus at which they are alternatives, so the comparison he made was between whole organisms and could not have been between alleles. Reaching for a rival genome was the structure of the problem showing through rather than a slip. He was then handed the reading rather than reaching it.
