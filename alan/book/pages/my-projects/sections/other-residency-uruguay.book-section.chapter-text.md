
Uruguay offers an unusually straightforward residency surface for a stable LATAM jurisdiction: a single "Residencia Permanente" program that grants permanent residency on day one (not after a temporary stage), modest income thresholds (~USD 1,500/month single), and a 3-year (family) / 5-year (single) path to naturalization that runs concurrently. Rule of law is strong, banking is functional, and the country has historically attracted retirees and remote workers from Argentina, Brazil, the US, and Europe. Two parallel regimes coexist: **immigration residency** (Dirección Nacional de Migración, DNM) and **tax residency** (Dirección General Impositiva, DGI) — they share evidence but are legally distinct.

## Residencia Permanente — The Main Route

Uruguay's flagship is **Residencia Permanente direct** — unlike most countries, there's no mandatory temporary-residency stage. Apply once, get permanent residency directly (subject to substance review).

### Income / proof
- Single applicant: ~USD 1,500/month stable passive or productive income.
- Married couple: ~USD 2,500/month combined.
- No statutory floor; DNM and notaries apply these as working norms.
- Acceptable: pensions (government, employer, private annuity), dividends, rental income, royalties, salary, freelance / consulting (if documented).
- Documents apostilled and translated to Spanish by an official translator; a Uruguayan *escribano* (notary) prepares the income certification.

### Physical presence floor
DNM requires "ánimo de permanencia" — intent to reside. Practitioners cite 6 months in the first year and ~183 days/year thereafter as the working norm; long absences (>6 consecutive months) put the application or status at risk. The Corte Electoral applies its own stricter view for downstream citizenship.

### Processing
6–12 months currently (reported improvements bringing it to ~8 months). Applicant can live in Uruguay during processing on the *constancia de inicio de trámite* (filing receipt), which functions as proof of legal presence.

### Citizenship pathway
The 3 / 5-year constitutional clock starts on **filing date**, not card-issuance date — so residence accrual runs in parallel with processing. See [citizenship/uruguay.md](../citizenship/uruguay.book-chapter.md).

## Tax Residency (Residencia Fiscal) — Separate Regime

Tax residency is determined by DGI under Decree 148/007 and is **legally independent** from immigration residency. You can hold one without the other (and many investors do). Five qualifying tests, any one suffices:

### Day count
**More than 183 days in a calendar year** in Uruguay. Sporadic absences of up to 30 days are excluded from the count, so effective threshold is ~140–150 days of actual presence. DGI applies substance-over-form — purely paper presence has been rejected.

### Real estate investment + 60 days
Investment of **3.5 million Indexed Units (UI)**, approximately **USD 545,000–560,000** as of 2025, in real estate (residential, commercial, industrial, or rural; multiple properties aggregable; mortgage permitted), made on or after July 1, 2020, **plus** minimum 60 days of physical presence per year. The flagship "tax holiday" pathway — pair with the 10-year IRNR exemption.

### Business investment
Direct or indirect ownership in a Uruguayan company valued **over USD 2,202,640** (15 million UI), made on or after July 1, 2020, **plus** at least 15 new full-time direct jobs created in the calendar year. Day count not required if investment qualifies.

### Innovation fund alternative
**USD 100,000/year for 11 consecutive years** contributed to a government-backed National Innovation Fund qualifies for tax residency without the property or business thresholds.

### Center of vital interests
If your spouse and dependent children live in Uruguay (e.g., children attending Uruguayan schools), DGI may grant tax residency without the day count — but the agency is the arbiter.

### Tax holiday
Tax residents who qualify from Jan 1, 2026 onward may elect the **IRNR (Non-Resident Income Tax) regime** for up to 10 years (11 including the grant year), exempting foreign-source capital income, or alternatively a permanent 7% flat rate.

## Mercosur Residency Agreement
Free regional residency for nationals of Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, Bolivia, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Venezuela, Guyana, Suriname. Requires birth in or 5+ years of naturalization in a Mercosur member/associate state. **US citizens are not eligible** — the US is neither a member nor associate. Citizens of eligible countries get temporary residency for up to 2 years on production of passport, birth certificate, and police clearance; convertible to permanent.

## Investor / Business Residency
Two thresholds visible in current practice:

- **USD 100,000 in productive investment** (real estate, business, productive activity) commonly cited as the working floor for an investment-justified Residencia Permanente — used to satisfy DNM's "medios de vida" rather than monthly income.
- **15 new full-time jobs + USD 2.2M+ business** for the elevated tax-residency variant.

Temporary investor residence (up to 2 years) is available during the larger business build-out. Path to citizenship: same 3/5-year clock.

## Retiree / Pensionado (Independent Means / "Rentista")
Not a separate visa name in Uruguay — retirees apply for **Residencia Permanente** under the income test (passive pension income). USD 1,500/month threshold is the de facto retiree threshold. Pension letters from government, employer, or private annuity provider apostilled and Spanish-translated. Most popular pathway; Uruguay is well-positioned for retirees due to political stability, climate, healthcare quality (mutualistas), and predictable tax treatment.

## Independent / Professional Residence
Self-employed professionals (consultants, freelancers, remote workers with established business) qualify for Residencia Permanente under the income test if income is documentable and substantially passive or contract-based. Active employment income from foreign sources also accepted in practice, though passive income simplifies the file. For genuinely active remote work, the Digital Nomad permit is the streamlined entry; conversion to Residencia Permanente follows.

## Family Reunification
Spouses, civil-union partners (concubinato), parents, and unmarried minor children (or adult children with disabilities) of a Uruguayan citizen or legal resident qualify under Law 18,250 Art. 10. The sponsor must demonstrate legally accredited income and give express consent. No separate financial floor for the dependent beyond the sponsor's own income coverage. Citizenship clock for the dependent starts on their own filing.

A **2024 executive decree** created a special residency program for ~20,000 foreigners (mostly Cubans) in migratory limbo on family or educational grounds — targeted, not a general expansion.

## Student / Academic Residence
**Temporary residency** for stays > 180 days but < 2 years for educational purposes. Documents: enrollment letter or scholarship proof on institutional letterhead, proof of means, police clearance, health insurance. Convertible to Residencia Permanente upon meeting income test or completing studies + employment.

## Work Residence
Temporary residency tied to a Uruguayan employer / contract. Employer issues a work contract, applicant files with proof of employment. Convertible to permanent residency after the temporary period. Less common than the direct Residencia Permanente route for foreigners with sufficient passive income.

## Digital Nomad Visa
Launched **May 2023** as one of Latin America's newest remote-worker permits.

- **Duration**: 180 days initial, extendable once for another 180 (12 months total).
- **Eligibility**: remote workers, freelancers, self-employed professionals working for clients / employers **outside Uruguay**.
- **Income**: no statutory minimum; sworn declaration of "sufficient financial means."
- **Documents**: valid passport, sworn declaration, online application. **No criminal record** required for initial 180 days; required for the extension.
- **Fee**: no standard visa fee; small administrative fee (~USD 10–15) at later stages.
- **Processing**: ~10 days post-application.
- **Conversion**: time spent on the Digital Nomad permit can be credited toward Residencia Permanente if the applicant transitions before the 12 months expire and meets the income standard — and toward the 3/5-year constitutional citizenship clock from filing date.

## Recent Changes (2024–2026)
- **May 2023**: Digital Nomad permit launched.
- **Aug 2024**: Executive decree special residency program (Cuban / asylum-seeker limbo population).
- **Mar 1, 2025**: Yamandú Orsi (Frente Amplio) inaugurated. No restructuring of the immigration residency framework announced; managing 2025 migrant surge with humanitarian-pathway widening rather than restriction.
- **Jan 1, 2026**: Tax holiday rules continue (10-year IRNR exemption or 7% flat rate election) for new tax residents from this date forward.
- Permanent residency processing times reported improving from 12 to 8 months.
