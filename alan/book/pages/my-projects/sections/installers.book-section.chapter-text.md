
# Rooftop Solar Installers Serving Provo, UT

Research compiled May 2026 covering local Utah, regional Intermountain West, and national installers confirmed serving Provo / Utah County (84604). Sources: SolarReviews, EnergySage, BBB, ConsumerAffairs, BestCompany, Utah DOPL, and company sites. The list is split by scale for scannability:

- [Local Utah installers](book-section/installers/local) — HQ in Utah, primary footprint along the Wasatch Front
- [Regional installers](book-section/installers/regional) — Utah-HQ or neighboring state, covers multiple Intermountain West states
- [National installers](book-section/installers/national) — multi-state operators with Utah presence (often through subcontracted or rebranded crews)
- [Defunct / red-flag operators](book-section/installers/defunct) — companies recently exited, revoked, bankrupt, or under enforcement
- [Summary observations](book-section/installers/observations) — patterns, standouts, and risks

Quick orientation:

- Provo / Orem is the gravitational center of Utah's residential solar industry. ION Solar (Provo HQ) and Blue Raven Solar (Orem HQ) are the two largest national-scale installers based locally.
- Utah requires solar work to be performed under an E200 (General Electrical Contractor) or E201 (Residential Electrical Contractor) license issued by the Utah Division of Professional and Occupational Licensing (DOPL); verify any installer at [dopl.utah.gov](https://dopl.utah.gov/) before contracting.
- The 2024-2025 wave of bankruptcies (SunPower Corp., Lumio, Sunnova, Freedom Forever, ADT Solar) means warranty risk is real for any installer not already 10+ years old and demonstrably profitable. Prefer cash purchase from a long-established local installer with workmanship coverage backed by a manufacturer (e.g., Solar Insure, IronRidge, Enphase Upgrade Program) rather than the installer alone.
- Lease/PPA operators (Sunrun, Sunnova) are the highest-risk financing path in Utah because Utah's net metering successor program (Customer Generation Program) and Rocky Mountain Power's low export credit make ownership economics far better than third-party-owned systems.
