
# Polish Citizenship Paths (May 2026)

Poland operates under the 2009 Act on Polish Citizenship (Ustawa o obywatelstwie polskim, in force 15 Aug 2012). Regime is primarily jus sanguinis with limited anti-statelessness jus soli. Two distinctive paths anchor diaspora demand: **confirmation of Polish citizenship** (the workhorse for descendants of pre-1939 Polish citizens — no residency, no language, just documentary proof of an unbroken chain) and **Karta Polaka** (ethnic-Polish heritage card opening a fast-track to citizenship). Dual citizenship is tolerated. Naturalization currently requires B1 Polish from a state-recognized body. A government bill (Sept 2025) to extend naturalization to 8 years and a Nawrocki bill (Aug 2025) to push it to 10 years are both pending; the Sejm rejected Nawrocki's bill on 9 Jan 2026 but PiS resubmitted a similar draft on 17 Apr 2026.

## 1. Confirmation of Polish citizenship (jus sanguinis, diaspora workhorse)

- **Category**: Declaratory — confirms citizenship already held by operation of law via uninterrupted chain from a Polish-citizen ancestor.
- **Eligibility**: Direct-line ancestor was a Polish citizen (typically post-1920, when the 1920 Citizenship Act took effect); chain of citizenship never broken (no naturalization abroad before the child was born under pre-1951 rules; no foreign-army service pre-1951; no renunciation; no loss under the 1920/1951/1962 Acts). Mother-line chains valid from 19 Jan 1951 onward; father-line valid throughout. No residency, no language, no income, no age cap.
- **Documents**: Ancestor's Polish documents (passport, military, civil registry, naturalization-abroad records with dates), unbroken birth/marriage chain to applicant, apostilled and sworn-translated.
- **Timeline**: Decision typically ~12 months; complex archival cases 24–36 months. No residency clock.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: Masovian Voivode (handles applicants with no Polish residence — i.e., almost all diaspora) reports ~12 months standard; other voivodeships similar. After confirmation: register foreign birth/marriage certificates in Polish civil registry (1–3 months), then PESEL + passport.
- **Notable**: Voivodeship offices have tightened documentary standards 2023–2025 — more rejections for thin paper trails; pre-application archival research is now near-mandatory. Fee raised 1 Aug 2025 to PLN 277.
- **Dual citizenship**: Fully compatible — applicant was already a Polish citizen.

## 2. Citizenship by descent at birth (jus sanguinis, automatic)

- **Category**: Automatic by law for children born to at least one Polish-citizen parent, regardless of birthplace.
- **Eligibility**: One parent holds Polish citizenship at the child's birth.
- **Timeline**: Immediate; document via transcription of foreign birth certificate into Polish civil registry.
- **Notable**: For adults born abroad who never registered, the operative procedure is confirmation of Polish citizenship (path 1).

## 3. Jus soli — anti-statelessness only

- **Category**: Residual safeguard, not a real path.
- **Eligibility**: Child born or found in Poland to unknown parents or to parents lacking any citizenship.
- **Notable**: Poland has not ratified the 1961 Statelessness Convention or the European Convention on Nationality; the safeguard does not cover children whose parents legally cannot transmit citizenship. The European Network on Statelessness flags this as a continuing gap.

## 4. Citizenship by adoption

- **Category**: Automatic, retroactive.
- **Eligibility**: Full adoption by a Polish citizen completed before the child turns 16; child is then treated as Polish from birth.

## 5. Karta Polaka → fast-track citizenship (ethnic Poles)

- **Category**: Two-stage ethnic path: Karta Polaka first, then recognition as a Polish citizen.
- **Karta Polaka eligibility**: At least one parent or grandparent, or two great-grandparents, of Polish nationality (ethnicity, not citizenship — documented via civil/baptismal/school records or Polish-organization activity records); basic Polish language; cultivation of Polish customs; written declaration of belonging to the Polish nation before a consul or voivode; applicant does not currently hold Polish citizenship or a Polish residence permit. Historically targeted at the post-Soviet diaspora; geographic eligibility was broadened in 2019 to all nationalities of Polish descent worldwide.
- **Citizenship step**: After receiving Karta Polaka and a permanent-residence permit issued on its basis, 1 year of uninterrupted legal residence in Poland → apply for **recognition as a Polish citizen** at the voivode.
- **Timeline**: Karta Polaka issuance ~3–6 months at consulate; settle in Poland with the dedicated permanent-residence permit; 1 year residence; recognition decision ~6–12 months. Total ~2–2.5 years from Karta in hand.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: Stable. Consular Karta Polaka workflows are well-resourced; voivode recognition decisions for Karta holders are among the most reliable approvals.
- **Notable**: Robust and politically protected path; not affected by the Nawrocki/government naturalization-tightening bills.
- **Dual citizenship**: Tolerated; Karta Polaka itself requires the applicant not currently hold Polish citizenship, but acquiring foreign citizenship after Polish naturalization is fine.

## 6. Repatriation (ethnic Poles from Asian former-USSR republics)

- **Category**: Sui generis — repatriation visa confers citizenship on arrival.
- **Eligibility**: Ethnic Pole (or descendant) currently residing in Armenia, Azerbaijan, Georgia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, or Uzbekistan; no current Polish citizenship; settlement plan (housing for ≥12 months + employment commitment) provided by a Polish gmina or adaptation centre.
- **Timeline**: Citizenship acquired the moment the holder crosses the border on the repatriation visa.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: ~8,000 applications pending, mostly from Kazakhstan. 2025 budget doubled to PLN 80.8M to fund ~900–1,000 repatriates/year; at that rate clearing the queue takes 8+ years.

## 7. Naturalization (recognition) — standard residence path

- **Category**: Discretionary by voivode under Art. 30 of the 2009 Act ("recognition as a Polish citizen").
- **Eligibility (standard, May 2026)**: 3 years uninterrupted legal residence in Poland on a **permanent residence permit** or long-term EU resident permit, with stable income and legal title to housing; **B1 Polish certificate** from a state-recognized body (postsecondary-school certificates no longer accepted after a transition window ending 30 Jun 2026 — only PCJK state exam, recognized higher-ed Polish-language diploma, or equivalent qualify); clean criminal record (Poland + home country); no threat to defence/security; integration into Polish society. Permanent residence itself generally requires 5 years prior temporary residence → **~8 years total** for the standard track.
- **EU Blue Card holders**: 3 years residence after permanent-residence permit (2024 EU harmonization shortened the underlying PR clock too).
- **Timeline**: Voivode decision 6–12 months once filed.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: 6–12 months typical at most voivodeships.
- **Notable**: Government draft (Sept/Oct 2025) would extend the PR-held requirement from 3 → 5 years (total ~8 years explicit), add a naturalization test, tax-residency requirement, and loyalty declaration. Nawrocki draft (Aug 2025) would extend to 10 years on PR; rejected by Sejm 9 Jan 2026 but PiS resubmitted 17 Apr 2026. Fee raised 1 Aug 2025 to PLN 1,000.
- **Dual citizenship**: No renunciation of prior citizenship required.

## 8. Naturalization — reduced residence categories

- **Spouse of Polish citizen**: 2 years uninterrupted residence on a permanent-residence permit **plus** 3 years of marriage at time of application. Total ~5 years from wedding (3 years marriage + the 2 years PR-residence usually overlap with the 3+ years needed to obtain PR via marriage). B1 Polish, clean record, income, housing all required. Marriage-based cases average 12–18 months processing. Same-sex marriages not recognized.
- **Refugees**: 2 years residence on a permanent-residence permit (after refugee-status grant unlocks PR).
- **Stateless persons**: 2 years residence on a permanent-residence permit.
- **Minor children of applicants**: Acquire by extension if both parents consent (child ≥16 must also consent).
- **Karta Polaka holders**: 1 year (see path 5).

## 9. Investment / CBI

- **Category**: None. Poland has no citizenship-by-investment programme. Investor/business visas grant residence only — clock then runs under path 7.

## 10. Service-based / exceptional merit — Presidential grant

- **Category**: Discretionary, unconditional grant by the President of the Republic under Art. 18 of the 2009 Act.
- **Eligibility**: None specified in statute — sovereign decision. In practice used for exceptional merit, athletic representation, humanitarian cases, ancestral chains too thin for confirmation, and any case the voivode-recognition route cannot accommodate. No residency, language, or income test.
- **Timeline**: Highly variable — months to years; no statutory deadline. Applications submitted via voivode or consul; Chancellery of the President prepares; President signs.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: Opaque. Fee raised 1 Aug 2025 to PLN 1,669.
- **Notable**: Ministry of Interior is floating extending integration/loyalty criteria into the presidential procedure too — would constrain what has historically been an absolute discretion.

## 11. Restoration of citizenship (lost between 1962 and 1998)

- **Category**: Limited reacquisition under Art. 38–39 of the 2009 Act.
- **Eligibility**: Held Polish citizenship and lost it **before 1 Jan 1999** (typically under the 1962 Act — most common cause: emigrants who obtained Polish-consular permission to change citizenship, or who acquired foreign citizenship under the 1962 regime). Disqualifications: Axis military service 1939–1945, acts against Polish independence, human-rights violations.
- **Timeline**: Apply via Polish consulate → Minister of Interior decides. Several months.
- **Notable**: Affects only the named individual, not descendants. People who lost citizenship **after** 1 Jan 1999 do not qualify (and rarely lost it — modern Polish law makes loss difficult and renunciation requires presidential consent). For pre-1962 losses, the relevant route is usually confirmation of Polish citizenship (path 1) because the loss often didn't legally occur.

## Dual citizenship policy (May 2026)

- Poland tolerates dual citizenship: a Polish citizen may simultaneously hold another nationality; Polish law does not strip Polish citizenship for acquiring foreign citizenship.
- **On Polish territory, a Polish citizen is treated as solely Polish** (Art. 3 of the 2009 Act): must enter/exit on Polish passport, identifies to Polish authorities with Polish documents, all obligations (military, tax for residents) are owed to Poland without reduction. Penalties exist for *exercising* foreign citizenship inside Poland (e.g., presenting a foreign ID to authorities) — not for *holding* it.
- Voluntary renunciation requires consent of the President of the Republic; rarely granted.

## Comparison summary

Ranked by speed of obtaining the passport, for a likely applicant:

1. **Confirmation of Polish citizenship** — ~12–18 months if the documentary chain is clean; no relocation, no language, no age. Highest leverage if even one direct-line ancestor was a Polish citizen post-1920 and emigrated without formally losing it. Fastest practical path for diaspora.
2. **Karta Polaka → recognition** — ~2.5 years from Karta in hand (Karta ~3–6 months + 1 year residence + ~6–12 months recognition). Requires actually moving to Poland and basic Polish, but documentary bar for descent is looser than for confirmation.
3. **Birth to a Polish parent / adoption by a Polish parent before 16** — automatic; only transcription remains.
4. **Repatriation visa** — citizenship on entry, but ~8-year application queue from the Asian former-USSR republics.
5. **Marriage naturalization** — ~5 years from wedding (3 years marriage + 2 years on PR).
6. **Standard naturalization** — ~8 years (5 years temporary + 3 years PR) with B1 Polish and tightening regime.
7. **Restoration** — fast but narrow (pre-1999 losses, individual only).
8. **Presidential grant** — unpredictable; not a planning path.

Effectively closed: jus soli (anti-statelessness only); CBI (does not exist); restoration for post-1998 losses.

Watch in 2026: the government's 8-year draft and the PiS 10-year draft. Either, if passed, would not affect confirmation, Karta Polaka, repatriation, marriage, or refugee/stateless tracks — only standard naturalization.
