
# Importance over urgency

> Importance over urgency — the general law under Alan's psychology. He weights what matters far above what is loud (equivalently, magnitude over noticeability). Two floors resolve the apparent contradiction with moment-to-moment interest-chasing; the Not Doing quadrant lets importance kill urgency outright.

There is a common through-line under Alan's whole psychology: **he weights importance much more than urgency.** In his words: *"That is related to a common through-line for my psychology. I tend to weight importance much more than urgency."*

This is the same rule the [magnitude-not-noticeability discipline](safety.book-chapter.md#the-deepest-mover-is-the-slow-one--magnitude-not-noticeability) names in different clothes. He weights what *matters* over what is *loud*. The two phrasings point at one law: importance = magnitude (how much a thing actually moves his life), urgency = noticeability (how loudly it pulls at the present moment). The law says steer by the first, not the second.

## The fast signals are exactly the ones not to steer by

The signals that are loud are reliably the ones that matter least, and the signals that matter most are reliably quiet. So the law is not a preference — it is a correction against a systematic illusion, where salience runs opposite to magnitude.

The concrete anchors, all from the [Safety / bottleneck history](safety.book-chapter.md):

- **Safety vs. stress.** [Safety is the biggest lever in the whole system](safety.book-chapter.md#safety-is-the-hub-the-whole-system-feeds) and barely budges day to day; raw stress is what he *feels* moment to moment. Steer by Safety, the quiet large one — not stress, the loud small one.
- **Nervous-system recovery vs. Vyvanse.** [Nervous-system recovery is the slowest of the three EF-bottleneck retirers and the one he expects to matter most](four-resource-model.book-chapter.md#mana-was-a-real-former-bottleneck--the-migration-ran-ef--safety); Vyvanse is the most noticeable because it is fast and sharp, and *not* the largest.
- **The second integral vs. raw stress.** [Safety is the second integral of Stress](safety.book-chapter.md#safety--the-second-integral-of-stress) — two integrations of inertia downstream — and it governs more than the instantaneous reading he actually feels.

In each, the loud signal (stress, Vyvanse's sharpness, the moment-to-moment pull of boredom or interest) is the one he must *not* steer by; the slow, quiet, high-magnitude variable (Safety, nervous-system recovery, the second integral) is the one that actually moves his life.

## Two floors — why interest-chasing is not a contradiction

There is an apparent contradiction. Moment to moment, Alan runs on what looks like the *opposite* of importance-over-urgency: he chases interest. Boredom yanks him to ESO; an idle slot spins up a fresh team. That is the [boredom thermostat](harness-attention.book-chapter.md#boredom-is-the-needle--what-paces-the-switching) — the loud little pull deciding what he touches next. If importance-over-urgency were the whole story, he would never let boredom steer at all.

The resolution: **importance and urgency operate on two different floors.**

- **Importance decides what is even on the board.** Which twelve streams get to exist at all is an importance decision — nothing reaches the board without passing the importance test first.
- **Interest then freely picks which one he touches next.** Because everything on the board *already passed* the importance gate, it does not matter which of them interest grabs — they are all worth doing. Interest is free to range over the board precisely because the board is pre-filtered.

So interest-driven moment-to-moment behavior is not a violation of importance-over-urgency. Importance already did its work **upstream, at board-admission time.** Once the gating is done, letting interest pick among the survivors is not a failure to weight importance — it is what you are *allowed* to do once importance has guaranteed every option is worth it. The [boredom/interest pacing](harness-attention.book-chapter.md) ranges only over an importance-gated board.

## The Not Doing quadrant — importance gets to kill urgency

Importance does not merely outrank urgency for Alan. It gets to **kill** it. In his words: *"If it is urgent but not important, I move that to my Not Doing quadrant."*

The Not Doing quadrant is a named place where urgent-but-unimportant work goes to be **deliberately left undone** — not "later," not "delegate," not "when I get to it." Undone, on purpose, permanently. Most people's urgent-but-unimportant items keep nagging and claw their way back onto the pile; Alan's go genuinely quiet the moment they land there.

The felt side, in his words: *"deciding not to do something is equivalent to Done, it drops off my list completely."* The urgent thing *lets go* the instant he judges it unimportant. It does not keep tugging.

Alan did not derive this principle from his own wiring — he **internalized it from [The Done Manifesto](done-manifesto.book-chapter.md)** (Bre Pettis & Kio Stark) after a single read. Read as a spec for a mind, the manifesto is a spec for his, and "Not Doing = Done" is its plainest line for him. The source, the full thirteen-point text, and the line-by-line architecture fit live in [done-manifesto.md](done-manifesto.book-chapter.md).

### Why deciding-not-to-do equals Done — the stateless mind

For most people a not-doing decision doesn't actually close. The item is an open loop, and the open loop persists and re-tugs — the decision to skip it doesn't discharge it. For Alan it does. Deciding-not-to-do is **equivalent to Done** because the decision discharges the item from his [present-tense state](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md) entirely.

The mechanism is the [stateless / present-tense mind](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md): he keeps one model of the world that exists only in the present, with no offline store holding open loops off to the side. There is no archive for an undone item to sit in and re-tug from — once a decision removes it from the present model, it is simply gone, the same way a [completed action leaves no residue](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md#the-map-is-affectively-flat--structure-no-charge). For someone with a persistent open-loop store, "not doing" and "done" are different states (one nags, one is closed); for a [stateless self](discrete-self.book-chapter.md) whose only state is the current model, they are the **same** state — both are *absent from the present model*, and absence is absence. This is why importance can kill urgency cleanly for him where it can only defer it for most people: the kill is a deletion from present-tense state, and present-tense state is all there is.
