
# Cross-gender interaction map

> Cross-gender interaction map — conservative, pre-decided bright-line rules governing cross-gender interaction, built as an accommodation for two missing faculties (aphantasia and autism). Bright lines replace the real-time judgment others run in the moment.

A set of conservative, discrete, pre-decided rules governing how I interact across gender. It's a fidelity system — bright lines I hold to rather than judgment calls I make in the moment.

The map exists because two faculties most people use to calibrate cross-gender interaction are missing for me:

- **Aphantasia.** Many married men run an imagined check — "would my wife be comfortable seeing this?" — picturing the scene and reading the anticipatory feeling. I can't run that simulation. No imagined scene, no anticipatory feeling (see [aphantasia-mechanism.md](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md)).
- **Autism.** I don't absorb the implicit social cues others use to calibrate in real time. Reduced implicit social statistical learning means the in-the-moment signal that tells most people where the line is doesn't reach me (see [social-experience.md](social-experience.book-chapter.md)).

With both real-time channels dark, I substitute bright lines: discrete, observable, checkable rules with deliberately conservative safety margins. The same substitution autism forces everywhere — explicit schema in place of absent implicit calibration.

## Why conservative

The margins are wide on purpose. The [connection debt](connection-debt.book-chapter.md) model carries a ~10:1 negative-to-positive asymmetry: one bad hour costs about ten good hours. Under that loss function, defense beats offense by an order of magnitude, so avoiding the negative tail is worth far more than chasing reward. A bright-line map with conservative margins is the optimal policy for a cost dominated by its downside.

## The gendered core

Two rules apply to other women and not to other men. They are narrow and physical:

- **Proximity.** Maintain physical distance when 1-on-1, even in private. (This was a stricter never-1-on-1 rule when I was younger; it has relaxed to distance-in-private.)
- **Contact.** Avoid physical contact.

The taxonomy behind the map is flat — spouse, kids, other women, other men, and not much finer. Autistic context-independence: without an internalized model or implicit cue-reading, I tend to treat everyone in a category the same.

## The gender-independent rules

These apply to everyone outside immediate family:

- **Physical-contact caution.** Default to avoiding all contact outside the immediate-family circle, with narrow exceptions for socially-required contact — handshakes, extended-family hugs.
- **Emotional-disclosure guard.** Push intimacy toward the intellectual rather than the personal. I substitute intellectual intimacy for emotional intimacy, which keeps disclosure in a register that reads as more socially acceptable.

## The monitoring substitute

Most men in my context use the imagined "would my wife approve?" gut-check as an internal monitor. I can't run it, so I replace it with external structure:

- **Never initiate.** An implied rule against initiating contact with women. (No bright line against initiating with men — just low interest there.)
- **Transparency with spouse.** An external monitor in place of the absent internal one — making the interaction visible to my wife substitutes for the felt check I can't perform.

## The known blind spot

The bright lines are physical and behavioral, and they catch physical and behavioral risk well. The failure mode they don't catch is **unguarded emotional / intellectual intimacy** — precisely the channel that would most need a monitor, because it's the one I can't feel myself entering. The emotional-disclosure guard above is partial: I guard toward the intellectual, but intellectual intimacy itself runs unguarded because it reads as safe. Adding a monitor for this channel is the open work on the map.
