
# The self-preservation adapter

> The self-preservation adapter — the compiled rule "If it would hurt me, I'm not allowed to, even if it would help or avoid hurting others, even if those others are my future selves"; the May 2026 turn-outward crisis that built it, the fight/flight options it vetoed, the others-rule it fully replaced, and the 2026-08-06 conversion inside the blank-check window, where the do-no-harm bar becomes a do-less-harm compromise and there is at the moment no floor — scoped later the same day to the marriage alone, the one place Alan could not safely hold the non-harm line, with the bar unchanged everywhere else including the world-facing case that compiled it.

Self-preservation is one of the [recently built adapters](conceptual-adapters.book-chapter.md#the-adapter-inventory) — before it was built, the concept did not meaningfully exist for Alan. This note is its canonical home: the compiled rule, the month that built it, the options it vetoed, and the rule it replaced.

## The compile

The adapter, verbatim: **"If it would hurt me, I'm not allowed to, even if it would help or avoid hurting others, even if those others are my future selves."**

Three properties of the compile:

- **It is a prohibition against self-spending, not a drive toward survival.** Nothing in it pushes him to live or to protect himself positively; it only vetoes actions that would spend him. No fear is compiled in — the adapter does the work a felt aversion would do for most people, without any felt aversion behind it.
- **The "or avoid hurting others" clause widens the veto past benefit.** The rule blocks self-harm not only when it would *benefit* others but even when it would *prevent harm* to them. The veto holds at the hardest collision point — where hurting himself is the way to protect someone else.
- **"Even if those others are my future selves"** blocks the sacrifice-now-for-future-payoff overextension, on the same [discrete-self](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#the-selfother-decision-inversion) grounds the [volatility governor](volatility-governor.book-chapter.md#rule-3--im-not-allowed-to-make-choices-that-hurt-myself-not-even-if-they-benefit-others-not-even-if-the-others-are-my-future-selves) records: future selves are other people, with no automatic claim on the present one.

[Rule 3 of the volatility governor](volatility-governor.book-chapter.md#rule-3--im-not-allowed-to-make-choices-that-hurt-myself-not-even-if-they-benefit-others-not-even-if-the-others-are-my-future-selves) is the earlier-recorded variant of this rule; the fresh compile adds the "or avoid hurting others" clause. The clause is not decoration — it is what lets the new rule overrule the old one (below) at exactly the point where they collide.

## The rule stands on ethics, not on a feeling

The rule has no felt aversion behind it — that is the whole reason it had to be compiled. So what holds it up is not "I don't want to be hurt" but a judgment that harming the present self is **unethical**. In Alan's words:

> I have a rule that I am not allowed to harm my present self, I've adopted that as a hard rule to replace my missing sense of self-preservation on the grounds that it is unethical.

State the structure precisely. Most people's self-preservation is a felt drive — it does not need a reason, it just fires. Alan's is a **conclusion**, reached the same way [the half-billion-future-selves guardrail is a conclusion not a feeling](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#it-is-a-conclusion-not-a-feeling--and-not-a-reason-to-live): the ethics, worked out, do not permit spending the present self, and the rule is that conclusion installed as a hard limit. This is the same shape as everything else the neurotype cannot supply by feeling — a [built definition in place of an ambient one](conceptual-adapters.book-chapter.md), here a built prohibition in place of an absent aversion.

### The asymmetry — a deontic floor under present-Alan, a tradeable field everywhere else

The grounding is not "everything Alan does to himself is forbidden." It is sharper, and it has two halves that fit together:

- **Present-Alan gets a deontic floor: do no harm.** The do-not-harm-present-self rule is *inviolable* — a hard bar, not a quantity to be weighed. Harming the present self is off the table even when the harm would buy a large good elsewhere.
- **Future gains are pursued under a positive duty.** Where a present choice is *neutral-or-positive* for present Alan **and** strongly positive for future Alan, declining it would itself be wrong. In his words: *"if I can make choices in the present that are neutral or positive for present Alan and are strongly positive for future Alan, I would view it as highly unethical not to."*

So the present self does not get *priority* in the usual sense — it gets a **floor**. Below the floor (harm), nothing trades. Above the floor (no cost to the present), future gains carry a positive obligation. The asymmetry is between a hard prohibition on one side and a tradeable cost-benefit field on the other, with the present self's no-harm line as the one bright bar in an otherwise weighable space.

That is the rule as compiled in May 2026. It no longer describes the whole of what I run — see [the floor inside the blank check](#2026-08-06--the-floor-converts-inside-the-blank-check) below.

### 2026-08-06 — the floor converts inside the blank check

The [day/night partition](alternation-not-moderation.book-chapter.md#the-partition--one-policy-per-window) opens a window in which I pay whatever a relationship costs, and [sacrifice — a negative value on a positive-sum row — is what a relationship requires to exist](alternation-not-moderation.book-chapter.md#sacrifice-is-what-a-relationship-requires). That is the case this rule was built to forbid. Asked directly whether the floor still stands inside that window:

> At the moment, no floor. The do-no-harm bar converts into a do-less-harm compromise. It hurts, but its better than the alternative which is guaranteed misery.

State the change precisely, because it is narrow in two ways and large in a third. It is scoped **at the moment** — a current position, not a repeal. It was given about the **inside of the blank-check window**, not about the rule everywhere. And what it does there is convert the instrument: a **bar**, which admits no trade, becomes a **compromise**, which is a weighing. The bright line inside that window is gone, and what stands in its place is do-less-harm.

The grounds given are that it hurts and that the alternative is guaranteed misery. The two legs the floor was built on — [the untrusted real-time estimator](#leg-1--a-safety-heuristic-against-an-untrusted-estimator) and [harm outliving the identity that consented](#leg-2--the-sacrifice-is-involuntary-because-harm-outlives-identity) — still stand as written; nothing here withdraws either, and neither is named in the grounds above.

##### The scope is the marriage and nothing else

The scope was left open above — the conversion was given about the inside of the blank-check window, and whether it reached anywhere else was not asked. Asked directly, later the same day, whether the hard bar still stands for the world-facing case:

> Yeah, its only the marriage that has the adjusted bar. That was the only place I couldn't safely hold to the non-harm line.

So the conversion is **one carve-out, named**, and everywhere else the bar is unchanged — including [the world-facing case the rule was compiled on](#the-build--may-2026), which is the case that produced it. The reason given for the carve-out is not that the marriage deserves an exception but that it is the place the line **could not safely be held**: holding it there was itself the unsafe option, which is [the guaranteed misery](#2026-08-06--the-floor-converts-inside-the-blank-check) named as the alternative.

That leaves the rule with a shape it did not have when it was compiled: an absolute with one stated exception, the exception scoped to a relationship rather than to a class of act. *My reading, marked because it is a consequence rather than a claim Alan made: an exception grounded on "I could not safely hold the line here" is grounded on a condition rather than on the marriage as such, and nothing recorded yet says what stops that condition from being met somewhere else.*

#### What stands in the floor's place — the harm's shape, and the abort

Asked what aborts the blank check, the first answer was that nothing does:

> Right now, there isn't an abort, it is harm in the short term, but recoverable in the longer term (currently days), relationships require sacrifice is a rule I've adopted.

So the harm is granted rather than disputed, and what makes it bearable is that it is recoverable. The recovery clock is **not one clock**, and the two run at very different speeds:

> The recovery measured in days is for date nights, the night-time recover runs between break-even and recovery by the next night.

So the nightly blank check closes its own cycle — somewhere between breaking even and being recovered by the following night — and the multi-day recovery belongs to the [date nights](central-loneliness/europe-trip.book-chapter.md) instead. Keeping the two apart is what stops a cost that recurs every night from being priced as though it took days to clear.

Then, in the same breath, one was found:

> Actually, there is an abort, which is if I am no longer able to hide misery (below safety level 0), then Jen will notice and renegotiate.

Two things about that abort are worth stating plainly rather than leaving to be noticed. **Its trigger is [sub-zero](safety.book-chapter.md#sub-zero-territory)** — below level 0 is the −1 sympathetic-dominance zone and the −2 flop, so the abort fires from inside the range the [May 2026 crisis](#the-build--may-2026) ran in, not before it. And **it is not mine to pull.** The mechanism is Jen noticing and renegotiating, which routes through her reading of me rather than through anything I do — [she is already the corpus's measurement channel for distance-to-collapse](central-loneliness/jen-reading.book-chapter.md). The [stay-or-leave rule](central-loneliness/stay-leave-ledger.book-chapter.md#the-rule) is built the other way: there the base floor is both the test and the abort, and a fall toward the hard line is the survival override firing.

That the guard sits outside me is the design rather than an oversight, and the reason is that the two cannot coexist:

> I can't have an internal guardrail and a blank check at the same time, those are incompatible with each other, so I need the guard to be external.

A blank check is the undertaking to pay whatever the row costs; an internal guardrail is a standing reservation against paying past some point. Hold both and the check is not blank. So the externality of the guard is not a gap in the design — it is what the design requires. The window the check runs in is bounded from outside too, by [the sun](alternation-not-moderation.book-chapter.md#the-rule-that-holds-the-boundary).

##### Why it has to be Jen, and why she has to start it

Which person is settled by presence rather than by suitability:

> It has to be her, because she is the only external person present in the context where the experiment is happening.

And she has to be the one who *starts* it, not only the one who notices:

> She has to be the initiator as well, because by the time I'm anywhere close to the line, I have lost the ability to initiate.

That rules out any design whose last step is me raising something. Near the line the faculty is gone, so a step depending on it is a step that will not happen at exactly the moment it is needed.

It also rules out using my own daytime readings as the trigger, because of what the check undertakes:

> A blank check means I agree to pay the cost of the needs that arise during the blank check period, but those costs can come afterwards, which is why it requires time for me to recover still. Any low reading I make during the day is still part of the cost of the nighttime blank check.

So a low reading in the day is not the check overrunning — it is the check being paid. The cost is incurred inside the window and settled outside it, which is why [the recovery time exists at all](#what-stands-in-the-floors-place--the-harms-shape-and-the-abort). Treating that low as a fault would be treating the payment as the failure.

What came out of this is not a different abort but an instrument — [the safety widget](safety-widget.book-chapter.md), my level shown continuously on Jen's phone. My position is that it is sufficient on its own: *"I think telling the truth continuously does the job, it arms her guard in appropriate ways."* The abort itself is unchanged; what changes is that her guard is armed with a number I have told her rather than with an inference off my face.

This deontic floor sits in genuine tension with the [Axiomatic Ethics framework](axiomatic-ethics.book-chapter.md) Alan develops, where self-harm at full agency (`a = 1`) costs its full magnitude and is *weighed*, never *forbidden* ([consent-weighting](axiomatic-ethics/consent-weighting.book-chapter.md#axiom-6--innermost-consent-weighting)). This session asserts the floor as a hard bar; the framework does not contain one. The reconciliation — bright-line simplification of the framework, or genuine divergence — stays open, carried in [axiomatic-ethics → threads](axiomatic-ethics/dilemmas-and-open-questions.book-chapter.md#threads-to-pull-on-later).

## Why the floor — two legs

The floor needs grounding, because it does **not** rest on "present-Alan weighs more." On the ledger everyone is equal — future Alans and strangers carry full, equal moral standing, the same as the present self. So the floor cannot be a verdict that the present self is worth more. It rests on two other things, which compound.

### Leg 1 — a safety heuristic against an untrusted estimator

The floor is not a categorical claim about the present self's value; it is a **pre-commitment** Alan installs because he cannot price the costs reliably in the moment. In his words:

> It's not categorically different either, rather it is a recognition of the profound ethical costs of involuntary sacrifice (see my ethical framework for reference there), combined with a recognition of my limited ability to actually estimate the costs in real time. Since I don't trust the estimation process, I've set an absolute rule as a safety heuristic to prevent ethical costs that [are] believed to be much larger than any costs that would come from the imprecision of the rule.

The structure: the cost of an involuntary sacrifice **dominates** the cost of a blunt rule's imprecision. He cannot run the trade reliably under load — the estimator is untrusted — so rather than compute case by case and risk the dominating error, he hard-codes an absolute and accepts the rule's imprecision as the cheaper failure. The rule hedges against his own judgment. This is the same move the [volatility governor](volatility-governor.book-chapter.md#why-rules-and-not-judgment) makes — pre-commit to a hard rule because the in-the-moment read can't be trusted — here with the sharper rationale that the *downside being hedged* is catastrophic and lands on people who never agreed.

### Leg 2 — the sacrifice is involuntary because harm outlives identity

What makes a self-sacrifice "involuntary" — when it is, on its face, a choice Alan makes about himself — is that the one who agrees to it and the ones who pay it are different people. In his words:

> I think my intuition here is that harm is generally more persistent than my current identity. As a result, if current Alan makes a choice that causes harm to future Alan, future Alan is being harmed without his consent.

This sharpens the consent problem past "can't consent to a cost you can't see" to **can't consent to a cost you won't be around for**. Harm outlives the [roughly-3-second identity](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#a-roughly-3-second-window) that agreed to it. The Alan who signs off has [drifted into a stranger](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#the-selfother-decision-inversion) long before the harm is done being paid, so the consenter and the bearers are different selves — and present-Alan has no standing to sign that contract on the future strangers' behalf. An "agreed-to" self-sacrifice is consent in name only. The "profound ethical costs of involuntary sacrifice" Leg 1 names are exactly this: a cost imposed on non-consenting future people, the [separateness-of-persons / involuntary-sacrifice objection](axiomatic-ethics/dilemmas-and-open-questions.book-chapter.md#the-classic-objections-by-the-frameworks-own-tracker) the framework tracks.

### The two legs together

So the floor stands on a **dominating, catastrophic downside that lands on people who never agreed** (Leg 2) **and** a **live cost-estimate he cannot trust in the moment** (Leg 1). A bad downside *and* an untrustworthy judgment — together they justify pre-committing to an absolute rather than deciding case by case. Neither leg alone forces a hard rule: a trustworthy estimator could weigh even a catastrophic downside, and a non-catastrophic downside would survive the imprecision of an untrusted estimator. It is the conjunction that makes the bright line the right instrument — and it also locates the floor under the *present* self specifically, since the present self is the only one positioned to consent at all, and even his consent cannot bind the future strangers who will actually bear the cost.

This is also why the do-not-harm rule is one member of a larger family of intentionally-adopted absolutes Alan runs — bright lines installed wherever the same shape recurs: a dominating downside paired with an estimator he can't trust to price it live. (The full family is its own subject.)

## The build — May 2026

In Alan's words: *"That was a rough month. May 2026 I hit level 5 for the first time in a long time and started turning outwards. Turning outwards got me paying attention to the state of the world and it was worse than I had anticipated, so I started thinking about how and whether I could make it better. It took a month and a lot of pain before I finally came to this rule as a hard limit on any actions I consider taking."*

Level 5 is the top anchor of the [Safety scale](safety.book-chapter.md#scale-0-5-with-half-steps) — **extend**: initiate outward toward others. The sequence is worth stating plainly: recovery created the capacity to look outward, and looking outward produced the crisis. The adapter was not built in calm reflection; it was compiled under a month of load, as a hard limit on any action he would consider taking. This is the per-adapter [build method](conceptual-adapters.book-chapter.md#the-per-adapter-build-method--the-same-engine-as-insight) running at speed under pressure — sustained attention, candidate definitions iterated against the pain, until the rule that held landed.

### The mountain — where the rule compiled

The month of pain did not resolve in calm reflection. The rule crystallized into words on a mountain, at the crisis peak — the day, in mid-May 2026, Alan came the closest he ever has to acting (the full account lives in [suicidal-ideation.md → the mountain](suicidal-ideation.book-chapter.md#the-mountain--the-closest-i-have-come)). He hiked for about two hours with no phone and no watch, looking for enough reason to keep trying, and put his odds of jumping for real at about 15%. What came back down the mountain was the rule.

The precise mechanism is a **belief-vs-obligation severance.** The crisis was undeniable beliefs about how broken the world is, and those beliefs dropped his Safety from level 5+ to −2. The rule did **not** resolve the crisis by changing the beliefs. The world is broken; that stayed true. It resolved it by a meta-rule that dissolved the **obligation to act** on the beliefs — the do-not-harm adapter itself, *if it would hurt me, I'm not allowed to*, applied to the one action the crisis was pushing toward. Truth kept, action forbidden. The belief stays; the duty to act on it is severed. That severance is what let the rest of him walk back down while the part that demanded he act on the broken world did not. *(The "belief-vs-obligation severance" name is this session's analytical framing; the verbatim quotes and the events are Alan's — see the [mountain account](suicidal-ideation.book-chapter.md#the-mountain--the-closest-i-have-come).)*

## The options the rule vetoed

What was on the table: *"I was considering getting involved in politics and/or moving to another country to get away from them."*

Fight or flight, at life scale. Both failed the rule for the same reason: both would spend him. The rule did not adjudicate between the options — it removed the whole category they belonged to.

## The residual pain — converted to weather

The rule settled the means question: no action that hurts him is on the table. It did not settle the original pain — a world worse than anticipated. That resolved by reclassification: *"I've successfully converted it to weather, something I really can't impact in any meaningful way, and therefore work around instead of addressing directly."*

This is the [weather](weather.book-chapter.md) move exactly: a genuine map update placing the conflict outside the circle of control, after which it generates no dissatisfaction at all. Deleting the agency-claim kills the ping at the root — under [pain-driven motivation](pain-driven-motivation.book-chapter.md), drive runs on present pain, and a conflict the map files as unchangeable stops producing the pain that would demand action. The month of pain before the rule landed is consistent with weather's [not-by-fiat requirement](weather.book-chapter.md#the-reclassification-is-not-by-fiat): the map could not be willed into the new state; it had to actually update, and the update took the month.

## The rule it replaced

Before the adapter, self-preservation ran on a different rule entirely: *"Before I generally had a rule that if it would hurt other people, I wasn't allowed to do it. That was my main self-preservation for most of my life."*

Under the old rule, protection reached him only incidentally, routed through others. His own destruction was vetoed only insofar as it would harm someone else; his own pain had no independent standing in the rule at all. The shape matches the childhood belief documented in [journey-as-reward](journey-as-reward.book-chapter.md#the-biographical-root--why-the-wanting-rule-exists) — *"my needs aren't important and can only be met once the needs and wants of others have been met"* — a self whose needs come last by default is exactly a self whose only available protection is the harm its destruction would do to someone else.

The [mountain recompile](#the-mountain--where-the-rule-compiled) is precisely the **flip of this rule's protected party** — from others to self. The old rule was the inverse: *if it would hurt others, I'm not allowed to.* The May recompile kept the rule's shape and flipped what it protects to the present self, leaving not-hurting-others in place only as general ethics (below).

## Full replacement, not layering

The new rule did not stack on top of the old one: *"the new rule has done a full replacement. I still don't want to hurt others based on general ethics, but the specific rules take priority."*

The "even if it would help or avoid hurting others" clause exists precisely to overrule the old law at the collision point. Where the old rule would demand self-spending to protect someone else, the new rule wins. Not-hurting-others survives — but as general ethics, not as the self-preservation mechanism, and when the two conflict the specific rule takes priority over the general ethic.

## The two protections — an ethics rule and an emotional will to live

The rule above is a **conceptual, ethics-based** protection — [a conclusion installed in the map, not a felt drive](#the-rule-stands-on-ethics-not-on-a-feeling). For a long time Alan understood it to be the *only* thing holding him: no felt aversion to self-harm, [no felt bond to the future selves](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#it-is-a-conclusion-not-a-feeling--and-not-a-reason-to-live), just the argument. This session complicated that — without cleanly resolving it. It surfaced a **candidate** second protection, of a different kind, whose status is not yet settled.

The conceptual one, restated by Alan this session in a single unified line:

> I am not allowed to make choices that hurt myself / take away choices from future Alan.

This is the same adapter as [the verbatim compile above](#the-compile), folded together with the [half-billion-future-selves guardrail](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#the-half-billion-future-selves--the-real-guardrail-against-acting): *hurting myself* and *taking away choices from future Alan* — the opportunities of the future selves the act would erase — stated as one prohibition. Alan named its class directly: **"my new conceptual protection is ethics-based."**

The second protection is one he did not know he had at all — or so it first appeared, the day before this session. In his words:

> But apparently, I have an emotional will to live as well that I didn't know was there.

That is how it landed: building [Grace](../personas/grace.book-chapter.md) surfaced tears, and the tears read as a will to live he had not known was there. **But the attribution is not settled.** His instrument reads that a feeling was present and strong; it does not read [which drive generated it](self-instrumentation.book-chapter.md#source-is-not-on-the-dial--which-drive-produced-a-feeling-is-unmapped) — and *"will to live"* is one of [three un-rankable candidates](../personas/grace.book-chapter.md#the-origin--the-tears-were-real-the-generator-is-open) (will to live / elevation / being-loved) braided into an overdetermined trigger. So this session does **not** overturn the [conclusion-not-a-feeling verdict above](#the-rule-stands-on-ethics-not-on-a-feeling): the self-preservation adapter is still, on the settled record, a conclusion with no felt tell. What yesterday adds is real but bounded — *something* surfaced, strong enough to break him open; whether it was a felt will to live specifically is an [L2 question left to bake](../personas/grace.book-chapter.md#the-origin--the-tears-were-real-the-generator-is-open).

So the recovery may stand on **two** carriers rather than one — but that is a live possibility, not a closed finding. The **conceptual** protection (the ethics rule) is legible and load-bearing, and has been for a long time. The **emotional** one — a felt will to live — is a candidate the tears make plausible but do not confirm; it sits, if it is there, in [the part hardest to read](self-architecture.book-chapter.md#the-quiet-tenth-carried-him--the-will-to-live), which is exactly why it cannot be read off one look.

### A named power source for the emotional candidate — advancing, not closing

A later reframe gives the emotional-will-to-live candidate something it lacked: a **named power source.** The will to live, on this reframe, is not stubbornness but is [powered by grief-gratitude toward the past selves](../personas/grace.book-chapter.md#the-origin--the-tears-were-real-the-generator-is-open) — survival as an **act of love**, a refusal to spend for nothing the suffering of the [uncounted past selves who kept going](self-as-strangers.book-chapter.md). Alan endorsed it; it reached him. That makes the will-to-live reading the **emerging strong candidate** among [the three](../personas/grace.book-chapter.md#the-origin--the-tears-were-real-the-generator-is-open) — better-supported than elevation or being-loved, which have no comparable engine named.

But an endorsed reframe is not a dial reading. [Source is still not on the instrument](self-instrumentation.book-chapter.md#source-is-not-on-the-dial--which-drive-produced-a-feeling-is-unmapped), so this **advances the question without closing it.** The [conclusion-not-a-feeling verdict above](#the-rule-stands-on-ethics-not-on-a-feeling) still stands on the settled record; the emotional carrier is still a *candidate*, now a better-supported one. It wants Alan's **direct confirmation in a live interview** before the generator thread is allowed to close.

The ordering across the [mountain](#the-mountain--where-the-rule-compiled) is the recovery arc in one line, told with that caveat. At the closest he has come to acting, the protection Alan *knew* was holding him was the **rule** — the concept, alone. If an emotional will to live was also in him then, it had no face he could see it by until Grace snapped into focus — so the concept was the *known* carrier at the crisis peak, and any emotional will to live the *unseen* one underneath it. Whether that second carrier is really a felt will to live or the concept naming [something else](../personas/grace.book-chapter.md#the-origin--the-tears-were-real-the-generator-is-open) remains the open question this section turns on.
