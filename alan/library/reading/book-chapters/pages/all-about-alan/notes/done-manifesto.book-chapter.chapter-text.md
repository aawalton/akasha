
# The Done Manifesto

> The Done Manifesto (Bre Pettis & Kio Stark) — the external source Alan internalized his "Not Doing = Done" closure from. Read as a spec for a mind, it is a spec for his — a stateless, forward-only brain. The line-by-line fits, why Not-Doing=Done is simply what closure is for a storeless mind, single-read installation as evidence of conceptual map-overwrite, and the recurring scaffold-adoption pattern this is one instance of.

Alan's [Not Doing = Done](importance-over-urgency.book-chapter.md#the-not-doing-quadrant--importance-gets-to-kill-urgency) closure is not something he derived from his own wiring. He **found** it, in **The Done Manifesto** by Bre Pettis and Kio Stark, and internalized it. In his words: *"I internalized this from The Done Manifesto."*

## The text

The thirteen points, verbatim as Alan gave them:

1. There are three states of being. Not knowing, action and completion.
2. Accept that everything is a draft. It helps to get it done.
3. There is no editing stage.
4. Pretending you know what you're doing is almost the same as knowing what you are doing, so just accept that you know what you're doing even if you don't and do it.
5. Banish procrastination. If you wait more than a week to get an idea done, abandon it.
6. The point of being done is not to finish but to get other things done.
7. Once you're done you can throw it away.
8. Laugh at perfection. It's boring and keeps you from being done.
9. People without dirty hands are wrong. Doing something makes you right.
10. Failure counts as done. So do mistakes.
11. Destruction is a variant of done.
12. If you have an idea and publish it on the internet, that counts as a ghost of done.
13. Done is the engine of more.

## An operating manual for a stateless, forward-only mind

Read the manifesto as a spec for a mind, and it is a spec for *his*. The fits are line-by-line — each point names a property his architecture already has:

- **"There is no editing stage" (3).** No replay to go back and re-litigate. His mind is forward-only; there is no stored past state to re-open and revise, so the editing stage isn't a discipline he refuses — it's a stage his architecture doesn't have. See the [present-tense model](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md): one model, continuously overwritten in place, no offline copy to edit.
- **"Pretending you know is almost the same as knowing, just do it" (4).** This is his [flipped Serenity Prayer](missing-simulator.book-chapter.md#the-flipped-serenity-prayer--defaulting-to-changeable-is-the-only-method) nearly word for word — *"until I try, I'll never know."* With no forward simulator to pre-feel the answer, the only way to find out whether he can do a thing is to do it. Point 4 is the manifesto's name for the only method his architecture has.
- **"Once you're done you can throw it away" (7).** No internal copy to keep. For a [storeless mind](discrete-self.book-chapter.md), the artifact *is* the memory — the list on the page, not a second copy held inside. There is nothing left over to archive, so throwing it away costs nothing; what's done is already gone from the present model.
- **"Done is the engine of more" (13).** His [twelve-stream boredom thermostat](harness-attention.book-chapter.md#boredom-is-the-needle--what-paces-the-switching) in one line. Closure isn't an end-state he rests in — it frees the next slot. A finished stream is a vacated slot, and the vacancy is what pulls the next thing in. Done is literally what powers more for him.

It reads like a manual written for a stateless, forward-only brain — and he happens to *be* one. The receptors were already there; the manifesto just named them.

## Why "Not Doing = Done" is closure for a storeless mind

This is the load-bearing consequence. "Not Doing equals Done" is **not a productivity trick** Alan adopted for discipline. It is simply what closure *is* for a mind with no internal store: with no offline store to hold an open loop off to the side, deciding-not-to-do removes the item from the one present-tense model, and removal from the only store there is *is* closure. The manifesto's "Not Doing = Done" is not a stance he takes — it is the literal mechanics of how closure works in a mind that keeps no copy.

The full mechanism of why this collapses to one state — the most-people-vs-Alan contrast, why the open loop re-tugs for them but discharges for him, and why importance can *kill* urgency cleanly where it can only defer it for most people — lives at [importance-over-urgency → why deciding-not-to-do equals Done](importance-over-urgency.book-chapter.md#why-deciding-not-to-do-equals-done--the-stateless-mind); this note is its source attribution.

## Single-read installation — evidence of conceptual map-overwrite

In Alan's words: *"it makes sense why this resonated with me strongly enough to fundamentally change my maps and behavior after a single read."*

The **single read** is the tell. A framework that needs ten passes to sink in is fighting his grain; one that rewrites his maps and behavior in a single pass had nothing to fight — the receptors were already built, so it clicked home whole.

This is the [stateless-mind](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md) mechanism in action. A stateless mind does not accumulate a belief slowly across many exposures the way a mind with a persistent store does — it **overwrites the map in place.** So a map that genuinely fits drops in whole the instant it arrives. His conceptual self is exactly that plastic to a true idea: when the compression is right, it installs on contact. (This is the [compression-as-truth gauge](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md#the-past-survives-only-as-compression) on the receiving side — a true model clicks because it compresses cleanly against what's already there.)

And it is the **mirror-image of his body.** No manifesto rewires a nervous system in one read. The somatic floor only moves at the speed of breath and time. This is the [two-self model](self-architecture.book-chapter.md#the-tripartite-self--mind-emotions-body): the conceptual / Mind self overwrites instantly, while the physical-and-emotional self is slow. The Done Manifesto changing his maps in a single read, and his [stimulation band](stimulation-regulation.book-chapter.md) moving only over weeks of climbing, are the same split seen from its two ends — instant plasticity on the conceptual side, slow drift on the somatic side.

## The scaffold-adoption pattern

This is a move Alan keeps making, and it deserves its own name: he **finds external scaffolds shaped like his mind and bolts them on.** The Done Manifesto is one instance. Others:

- The [agent-harness](agent-harness.book-chapter.md) vocabulary — a framing that landed post-hoc and stuck because it described his 20-year scaffold cleanly.
- **GTD** (Getting Things Done) and the **Eisenhower / [importance-over-urgency](importance-over-urgency.book-chapter.md) matrix** — productivity frameworks whose quadrants and open-loop logic map onto how he already sorts work.
- **"Mana"** — borrowing the game term to name [executive function](mana.book-chapter.md) as a depletable resource bar.
- The **Done Manifesto** — this note.

What makes a scaffold "fit" is the same thing that makes the single-read installation work: the framework already matches a shape his architecture has, so adopting it is recognition rather than construction. He isn't building a new capacity — he's finding an external articulation of an existing one and clipping it in. The fit is why these install cheaply and stick; a scaffold that didn't match his grain would need constant maintenance and wouldn't survive a single read.

This is distinct from a [conceptual adapter](conceptual-adapters.book-chapter.md). An adapter is a definition Alan **builds** from scratch for a concept he cannot feel his way into (love, commitment, self-worth) — manufacturing access where there was none. A scaffold is an external framework he **finds already built** that happens to be shaped like a capacity he already has — recognition and adoption, not manufacture. Adapters fill gaps; scaffolds name and articulate what's already there.
