
Norway is **not in the EU** but is bound by **EEA + EFTA + Schengen + Nordic Passport Union**. This creates a hard two-track system: **EEA/EFTA/Nordic citizens** (and Swiss) register and need no residence or work permit; **third-country nationals (TCNs)** including US citizens must hold a residence permit issued by **UDI** (Utlendingsdirektoratet) before any stay beyond 90 Schengen days. Norway has **no golden visa / residency-by-investment** program. **Svalbard** is a separate jurisdiction with unique visa-free residence under the 1920 Treaty.

## EEA / EFTA / Nordic / Swiss track (no permit)

### Registration certificate (EU/EEA/EFTA/Swiss)
Anchored in Immigration Act Chapter 13, which transposes EU Directive 2004/38/EC. EU/EEA/EFTA and Swiss citizens may live, work, and study in Norway without a permit. Stays > 3 months require registering at a police station (deadline 3 months from entry) on one of these grounds: employee, self-employed, service provider, student, or self-sufficient (own funds + health insurance). The certificate confirms a pre-existing right — UDI cannot refuse registration if conditions are met, and absence of registration does not affect the underlying right of residence. **Permanent residence right** after **5 years** of continuous lawful residence; this is the EEA equivalent of TCN permanent residence and feeds the citizenship clock.

### Nordic citizens
Danish, Swedish, Finnish, and Icelandic citizens may move and reside freely under the Nordic Passport Union — no registration, no permit. Population-registry move is sufficient. Shortened citizenship clock (2 years) — see `citizenship/norway.md`.

## Third-country residence permits (UDI)

First-issue processing typically **4–8 weeks** for skilled-worker tracks but **6–25 months** in 2025–2026 for family-reunification and student tracks under backlog. Apply before entering Norway or before the existing permit expires.

### Skilled worker / specialist
Norway's main work track for university-graduates and vocationally-trained TCNs (medical doctors, IT specialists, engineers, academic researchers).
- **Education:** completed higher education or ≥ 3-year upper-secondary vocational training.
- **Job offer:** concrete, full-time, with pay and working conditions not below Norwegian norms. Minimum salary ~NOK 312,000/year baseline; collective-agreement levels typically higher.
- **Employer pre-registration:** from **February 2026**, employers must submit a digital confirmation of the job offer through UDI's system before the applicant can file.
- **Duration:** up to 3 years, tied to contract. Renewable.
- **Path to PR / citizenship:** yes, full credit.

### Job-seeker permit
For skilled workers seeking employment in Norway.
- **Eligibility:** same vocational/degree requirements as the skilled-worker track.
- **Financial means:** ≥ NOK 27,116/month (~NOK 325,400/year).
- **Duration:** up to 1 year (UDI's published max); some implementations cap at 6 months.
- **Work allowed** part- or full-time, including in unskilled roles, while searching.
- **Path to PR / citizenship:** does not itself count for PR — convert to skilled-worker permit before it expires.

### Self-employed / entrepreneur permit
- **Skilled-worker qualifications required** (same education bar).
- **Sole proprietorship only** — limited companies (AS) do not qualify on this track.
- Credible business plan; business registered in the Norwegian Register of Business Enterprises; revenue must support the applicant.
- Appropriate housing documented.
- Initially 1 year, renewable while business operates.
- **Path to PR / citizenship:** yes.

### Student permit
- Full-time admission to a Norwegian university / university college.
- Financial means: ~NOK 15,488/month (~NOK 170,368/academic year).
- Tuition (where applicable for non-EEA students) must be funded or already paid.
- **Work:** 20 hours/week during studies; full-time during holidays.
- **Path to PR / citizenship:** student years are counted only at reduced weighting for PR; full credit for the citizenship clock once the applicant moves to a qualifying permit.

### Au pair (effectively closed)
The au pair scheme was **discontinued from 15 March 2024**. New TCN applications are no longer accepted. Only applicants who registered before the cut-off may still receive a first permit; current holders may renew within the 2-year cap. EU/EFTA citizens may still work as au pairs under free movement. Au-pair time has never counted for PR or citizenship and that does not change.

### Family immigration
- **Eligible relations:** spouse, registered cohabitant (≥ 2 years prior cohabitation), child < 18, fiancé(e), full sibling, parent of a child in Norway.
- **Income requirement (sponsor):** NOK 416,512/year pre-tax (adjusted each May), maintained in the prior year, current year, and across the decision window.
- **Apply within 6 months** of the sponsor's permit being granted.
- **Processing:** routinely 4–6 months, but 20–25 months under 2025–2026 backlog.
- **2026 proposals:** removing parent/sibling reunification rights where the over-18 reference person has their own family in Norway; adding Norwegian-language and self-support gates on spouses.
- **Path to PR / citizenship:** yes.

### Posted worker / service provider
- **EU/EEA-company posting:** posted workers from EEA companies may stay > 3 months and apply for a residence card. Under the Posted Workers Directive they receive Norway's standard labour protections (equal pay, working time, leave, H&S).
- **TCN employees of EEA-based companies** establishing a business in Norway may stay up to 3 months without a residence card under EEA right-of-establishment rules; beyond that they need a permit.
- **Path to PR / citizenship:** posting time is not full residence-clock credit and is typically structured for short engagements.

### Cultural workers / researchers / specific categories
UDI issues permits for: established artists / culture-disseminators producing or promoting Norwegian culture, religious leaders, humanitarian / NGO staff, working-holiday participants, working guests in agriculture, athletes, and trainees. Researchers with their own funds (self-funded researchers) have a dedicated sub-track; researchers / lecturers staying < 3 months with a bachelor's+ do not need a permit. These tracks generally count toward PR and citizenship if continuously renewed.

## Svalbard — visa-free residence (Treaty of 1920)

Svalbard sits **outside Schengen and outside the Norwegian immigration regime**. Under the Svalbard Treaty (signed 9 February 1920, in force since 1925), citizens of every nation — not just signatories — may live and work in Svalbard indefinitely **without a visa, residence permit, or time limit**. Norway exercises sovereignty but has chosen to extend treaty rights universally.

Hard practical limits:
- The visa-free right covers **Svalbard only**. Travelers reaching Svalbard via mainland Norway need a Schengen visa (double-entry if planning to return through mainland).
- Residents must be self-supporting; there is no Norwegian welfare access in Svalbard, and the Sysselmesteren can require departure of anyone unable to support themselves or housing themselves.
- **Time in Svalbard does NOT count toward mainland Norwegian permanent residence or citizenship.** It is a parallel residency, not a path to a passport.

Useful as a low-friction year-or-more living option for any-nationality remote workers, retirees, or entrepreneurs willing to tolerate Longyearbyen's extreme climate. Not a Norwegian residency hack.

## Investor / golden visa — does not exist

Norway has **no golden visa, residency-by-investment, or citizenship-by-investment program** as of May 2026. This is a deliberate policy stance, not a gap: Norway prioritizes active economic contribution and EEA labour-market integration over passive capital flows. There are no government-bond, real-estate, or donation pathways. The only investor-adjacent route is the self-employed permit, which requires active operation of a sole proprietorship in Norway. Third-party agencies marketing a "Norway investor visa" are repackaging the self-employed track plus discretionary specialist categories.

## Digital nomad — mainland has none

Mainland Norway has **no formal digital-nomad visa**. Remote workers must qualify under an existing UDI track (skilled-worker, self-employed, family, student). Schengen short-stay (90/180) is the only option for casual remote work from Norway. **Svalbard** is the unique workaround — visa-free indefinite residence regardless of citizenship — though Svalbard time confers no mainland rights.

## Permanent residence permit (TCN)

- **Residence:** 3 years' continuous residence on a qualifying permit (5 years on some categories). Au-pair, student-during-studies, job-seeker, and short stay/temporary permits do not qualify.
- **Absences:** ≤ 7 months total over the 3-year qualifying window.
- **Language:** complete the required Norwegian courses **plus** pass an oral Norwegian test (current threshold A2 for PR; B1 for citizenship).
- **Civics:** pass samfunnskunnskapsprøven (social-studies test) in Norwegian, or its accepted alternative.
- **Self-support:** sufficient income during the prior 12 months (no major reliance on social assistance).
- **Conduct:** clean criminal record in Norway and prior countries.
- **Gap rule:** must not have been without a valid permit for more than 3 cumulative months.
- After PR: indefinite right to live and work; counts to citizenship at full rate.

## Recent changes (2024–2026)

- **Au pair scheme discontinued 15 March 2024** for non-EEA applicants; only EU/EFTA citizens may now au pair.
- **B1 Norwegian for citizenship (Oct 2022, still in force).** PR remains at A2.
- **Skilled-worker digital employer pre-registration mandatory from February 2026** before applicants may file.
- **Family-immigration tightening proposed 2026** — narrowed parent/sibling rights, added language/self-support gates for spouses.
- **UDI processing-time blowout 2024–2026.** Family-immigration and citizenship wait times reached 25–30 months by Q1 2026; UDI labels its published times "guides, not promises."
- **2025 G-adjustment:** the basic amount G — which feeds salary minimums and the 3G citizenship-shortcut threshold — rose to NOK 128,116 (3G = NOK 384,348 for the 2025 tax year).
