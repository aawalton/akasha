
# Maltese Citizenship Paths (May 2026)

Malta is a small EU island state (since 2004) whose citizenship regime is dominated by three facts as of May 2026: the European Court of Justice killed Malta's investor-citizenship scheme (MEIN) in Case C-181/23 on 29 April 2025 — ending the last EU CBI program — and Malta formally repealed it via Act XXI of 2025 (in force 24 July 2025), replacing it with a discretionary, non-transactional "Citizenship by Merit" route; ordinary naturalization is among the longest in the EU (a discretionary 18-year practical floor under the Minister's full discretion, even though the statutory minimum reads as 5 years); and Maltese descent (jus sanguinis with no generation cap if the chain was preserved before the 1 Aug 2007 / 2028 deadline) is the cheap and reliable path. Dual citizenship is unconditionally permitted (Citizenship Act §7, since the 10 Feb 2000 amendments).

## Birth on Territory (jus soli) — None

- **Basis**: Maltese Citizenship Act (Cap. 188) §5. Since the 1 Aug 1989 amendments, citizenship at birth in Malta requires at least one Maltese-citizen parent — pure jus sanguinis with a birthplace tie-in.
- **Stateless-child protection**: A child born in Malta who would otherwise be stateless is granted Maltese citizenship at birth.
- **Foundlings**: A newborn found in Malta and whose parents are unknown is presumed Maltese.
- **Practical timeline**: N/A — birthplace alone confers nothing unless the stateless or foundling rule triggers.

## Descent — Maltese Parent (jus sanguinis)

- **Basis**: Citizenship Act §5. A person born outside Malta after 1 Aug 1989 to at least one Maltese-citizen parent is Maltese by descent.
- **Pre-1989 cohort**: Born **21 Sep 1964 – 31 Jul 1989** abroad — only paternal transmission applied. Maternal-line claims for this cohort go via §5(2)(b) registration (since 2000 amendments) — non-automatic but as-of-right.
- **Other requirements**: parent's Maltese citizenship documented; no residency, no language test, no clean-record gate (citizenship is automatic at birth).
- **Timeline / processing**: register the birth at the Maltese consulate or Public Registry; typically **3–6 months** for the birth certificate and passport.
- **Fees**: nominal (€15–€50 in consular fees).

## Descent — Maltese Origin / Registration (Article 5(2)(b) / Article 3(5))

- **Basis**: Citizenship Act §3(5) and §5(2)(b) — registration as a citizen of Malta for adults born abroad to non-citizen parents who can prove unbroken direct-line descent from an ancestor born in Malta whose own parent was also born in Malta (so practically: great-grandparent → grandparent → parent → applicant, all in the direct line).
- **Generation cap**: **None** in the statute — chains of four or more generations are accepted if documented.
- **The 1 Aug 2007 / 2028 tripwire**: Under §3(5) as inserted by the 2007 amendments, any direct-line ancestor who was **alive on 1 Aug 2007** must themselves have **registered or applied for Maltese citizenship** to keep the chain alive. Their descendants must then register **by 1 Aug 2028** (a 21-year window from the 2007 amendments) or the right lapses. After 1 Aug 2028 the route narrows sharply — only descendants of ancestors who pre-deceased the 2007 cutoff (or who themselves registered in time) remain eligible. This is the binding deadline for diaspora claimants and is the single most consequential post-MEIN fact for descent applicants.
- **Other requirements**: documented descent (birth/marriage certificates for every link); oath of allegiance; **no residency requirement**, **no language test**, dual citizenship permitted, no investment.
- **Timeline / processing**: **6–12 months** is the standard processing window via Aġenzija Komunità Malta once the dossier is complete; document-gathering (church records, civil registries in Canada/UK/Australia/US — the four largest diaspora destinations) commonly takes longer than the bureaucratic decision.
- **Fees**: ~€450 (registration fee + certificate).
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: ~9–14 months from filing to certificate; spiking as the 2028 deadline approaches.

## Naturalization by Residency (Article 10)

- **Basis**: Citizenship Act §10. The statutory minimum reads as **5 years**: an applicant must have resided in Malta for an aggregate of at least **4 years out of the 6 years** immediately preceding the **12-month** continuous-residence period that ends on the application date — i.e., **5 years total**.
- **Practical reality**: granting is **entirely discretionary** under §10(1) ("the Minister may"). Routine rejections without reasons, and a long-standing administrative practice of declining applicants who lack Maltese origin until they have lived in Malta for far longer, mean that in practice **non-origin applicants commonly wait ~18 years** of continuous residence before approval. This is among the longest **effective** thresholds in the EU even though the statutory minimum is one of the shorter ones.
- **Other requirements**: ≥18 years old; good character (police certificates from every country of residence in the past decade); adequate knowledge of **Maltese or English** (Malta accepts English — no exam, assessed informally on documentation); adequate means of support; oath of allegiance.
- **Timeline / processing**: 12–24 months for the decision after filing, but the practical 18-year residence floor swamps the decision lead time.
- **Fees**: €450 application + €50 certificate.
- **No appeal**: refusals are not subject to judicial review on the merits.

## Naturalization by Marriage (Article 6(1))

- **Basis**: Citizenship Act §6(1). A foreign spouse of a Maltese citizen may register as a citizen of Malta after **5 years of marriage** and **continuous cohabitation** at the date of application (and at the date of registration).
- **No residency-in-Malta requirement**: the couple need only have lived together (anywhere) — both spouses sign a joint affidavit attesting cohabitation for the full 5 years.
- **Other requirements**: marriage still subsisting (or the marriage lasted ≥5 years if the spouse has died or the couple has separated by death/divorce after 5 years — see §6(2)); good character; oath of allegiance. No language test, no investment.
- **Timeline / processing**: ~12 months from filing to certificate per the Aġenzija Komunità Malta service standard.
- **Fees**: €150 application + €50 certificate.
- **Civil unions**: treated equivalently to marriage under the 2014 Civil Unions Act.

## Citizenship by Merit (Article 10(9), Act XXI of 2025) — Replaced MEIN

- **Basis**: Citizenship Act §10(9) as inserted by **Act XXI of 2025**, in force **24 July 2025**: "The Minister may grant a certificate of naturalisation as a citizen of Malta to any person who renders or has rendered exceptional service to the Republic of Malta or to humanity, or whose naturalisation is considered to be of exceptional interest to the Republic of Malta."
- **Eligibility**: discretionary; criteria are achievements in science, entrepreneurship, sports, arts, philanthropy, or other manifestly superior contribution. **Not transactional** — there is no published price, no real-estate or bond requirement, no donation tier. Drafted explicitly to comply with CJEU C-181/23.
- **Genuine-link requirement**: applicants must demonstrate a substantive connection to Malta beyond payment — residence, ongoing presence, or service to the Republic.
- **Volume cap**: not specified in statute but expected to be very low (single digits to low tens per year).
- **Timeline / processing**: not yet stabilized — first cohort under the new framework still pending as of May 2026.
- **Dual citizenship**: permitted.

## Investment / MEIN (formerly IIP) — TERMINATED

- **Status**: Terminated by **CJEU Case C-181/23 (29 April 2025)** and repealed by Act XXI of 2025 (24 July 2025). The non-transactional **Citizenship by Merit** route above replaced it. Pre-ruling transitional cases remain in legal limbo. See [residency/malta.md](../residency/malta.book-chapter.md) for full history and transitional context.

## Reacquisition / Resumption of Lost Citizenship (Article 27)

- **Basis**: Citizenship Act §27 and related provisions of the 2000 amendments. Any person who **lost Maltese citizenship** by voluntary acquisition of a foreign citizenship **before 10 February 2000** (the date dual citizenship became unconditional) is entitled to resume Maltese citizenship.
- **Eligibility**: prior holding of Maltese citizenship; loss by foreign-citizenship acquisition pre-2000; application for resumption.
- **No residency or language requirement**.
- **Timeline / processing**: **3–6 months** typically.
- **Fees**: nominal.
- **Significance**: covers the post-WWII emigration cohorts to Australia, Canada, the UK, and the US who lost Maltese citizenship on naturalizing abroad — they (and notably their children who would otherwise have inherited at birth) can come back into the system.

## Refugees and Stateless Persons

- **Basis**: Malta has acceded to the 1951 Refugee Convention and the 1954 Convention on the Status of Stateless Persons but is **not** a party to the 1961 Convention on the Reduction of Statelessness.
- **Refugee path**: recognized refugees follow the standard §10 naturalization route — no statutory reduction in the residence threshold and no waiver of the Minister's discretion. In practice, the same long effective wait applies.
- **Stateless persons**: the only meaningful protection is the at-birth jus-soli rule for children born stateless in Malta (covered above). Adult stateless persons in Malta have no fast track to citizenship — they fall back into §10.
- **Practical note**: Malta has historically been one of the **less** generous EU states to refugees on the naturalization axis; the 18-year practical floor applies.

## Distinguished / Exceptional Services (pre-2020 Article 10(9))

- **Historical basis**: §10(9) of the Citizenship Act previously allowed the Minister to grant citizenship for "exceptional services to the Republic of Malta." This was the legal hook MEIN was bolted onto in 2014.
- **Current state (May 2026)**: rewritten by Act XXI of 2025 into the Citizenship by Merit route described above. Pure-merit grants (no investment) for distinguished individuals — Olympic athletes who represent Malta, eminent scientists who relocate, etc. — are still possible under the same §10(9), now stripped of any transactional character.

## Comparison Summary

Ranked by total elapsed time from a cold start to passport in hand, fastest to slowest:

1. **Descent — Maltese parent (jus sanguinis)** — **3–6 months**. Automatic at birth; just register at the consulate. Fastest path if eligible.
2. **Reacquisition / resumption (§27)** — **3–6 months**. For anyone who lost Malta on pre-2000 foreign naturalization.
3. **Descent — registration via grandparent/great-grandparent chain (§3(5) / §5(2)(b))** — **9–14 months** processing; document-gathering may add years. **Hard deadline: 1 Aug 2028** if the chain depends on a post-2007-alive ancestor.
4. **Marriage (§6(1))** — **5 years marriage + ~12 months processing ≈ 6 years**. No Malta residency required, only cohabitation.
5. **Citizenship by Merit (§10(9), new)** — **discretionary, very low volume**. Unpredictable; not a planning option.
6. **Naturalization by residency (§10)** — **5 years statutory minimum but ~18 years effective floor** for non-origin applicants. The slowest practical path in the EU.
7. **Investment (MEIN)** — **CLOSED 29 Apr 2025 / 24 Jul 2025**. No new applications. Pre-ruling pipeline cases unresolved.

**Highest likelihood of success for an outside applicant with no Maltese ancestry**: marriage. Without a Maltese spouse, the realistic options collapse to (a) the new Merit route if you have a world-class profile, or (b) the 18-year residency haul.

**Single best path if any Maltese ancestor exists in the direct line**: registration under §3(5) — **act before 1 Aug 2028** or risk losing the chain.
