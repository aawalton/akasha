
# Denmark

Denmark runs one of the strictest naturalization regimes in the EU: 9-year continuous residence, Prøve i Dansk 3 (~B2), the indfødsretsprøven civics test (36/45 plus 4/5 on "Danish values"), full self-support, and a quarantine for any criminal record. Citizenship is granted by parliamentary act — the Folketing passes a naturalization bill twice yearly (April / October) listing names, which adds structural delay on top of the 19–30-month ministry review. Dual citizenship has been allowed since 1 September 2015. No jus soli, no CBI, no marriage discount.

## 1. Descent (jus sanguinis) — automatic at birth

- **Category**: birthright by descent.
- **Eligibility**: at least one parent (mother, father, or co-mother) is a Danish citizen at the time of the child's birth. Applies regardless of where the child is born (since the 2014 reform — pre-2014 children born abroad to unmarried Danish fathers had restrictions).
- **Timeline**: automatic at birth; passport issuable immediately.
- **Backlog**: none for citizenship itself; passport processing weeks.
- **Notable**: children born abroad must take active steps to retain Danish citizenship before their 22nd birthday (residence in Denmark or sufficient ties), else lose it under § 8 of the Indfødsretsloven.

## 2. "Lost generation" — restoration to descendants of Danish women born 1898–1953

- **Category**: descent / restoration (transitional).
- **Eligibility**: children born abroad to a Danish-citizen mother between 1 January 1961 and 31 December 1978 (who could not pass citizenship under the pre-1979 rule), plus former Danes who lost citizenship by acquiring another before 1 September 2015. File a declaration with Udlændinge- og Integrationsministeriet.
- **Timeline**: months after declaration (no parliamentary act required).
- **Sunset**: this transitional declaration window was extended once and **expires 1 July 2026**. After that, jus sanguinis only flows forward through the post-1979 rule.
- **Backlog**: declarations are processed administratively; rush to file before July 2026.

## 3. Naturalization — standard residency (9 years)

- **Category**: ordinary naturalization by parliamentary act.
- **Residency**: 9 years uninterrupted lawful residence, holding a permanent-residence permit at the time the bill passes.
- **Language**: Prøve i Dansk 3 (~B2). PD2 only accepted if no public benefits in past 2 years and ≤3 months in past 9.
- **Civics**: indfødsretsprøven — 45 multiple-choice questions, must score ≥36 and ≥4/5 on the "Danish values" sub-block. Held twice yearly (typically late May / late November), fee ~905 DKK.
- **Self-support**: no public benefits in last 2 years; ≤4 months total in last 5 years (cash benefits, integration benefit; SU loans excepted).
- **Employment**: 3 years 6 months of full-time work or self-employment within the last 4 years (the "employment requirement" added in the 2021 agreement).
- **Criminal record**: any unconditional prison sentence is a permanent bar. Suspended sentences trigger 6–7½-year quarantines; unsuspended sentences trigger up to 30-year quarantines from release. Fines >3,000 DKK for social-control / immigration / social-fraud offenses trigger a 6-year quarantine. Undeclared traffic fines can disqualify.
- **Other**: no overdue public debt; signed declaration of allegiance to Denmark and the constitution; in-person citizenship ceremony with handshake (mandatory since 2019).
- **Fee**: 6,270 DKK (2026).
- **Timeline**: ~9 years residence + 19–30 months ministry vetting + wait for next parliamentary bill + ceremony = **realistically 11–13 years end-to-end**.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: ministry processing 19–30 months. Second 2025 bill was cancelled (Danish EU presidency H2-2025); one bill tabled January 2026, effective 1 April 2026. Realistic queue-to-citizen wait at front of queue stretches 2.5–3.5 years from filing.

## 4. Naturalization — Nordic citizens (Sweden, Norway, Finland, Iceland)

- **Category**: simplified naturalization by declaration.
- **Residency**: 2 years continuous residence (per Indfødsretsloven § 3 declaration route) — **fastest practical adult path**.
- **Language / civics / employment**: waived under the declaration route.
- **Criminal record**: same quarantine rules as standard.
- **Mechanism**: submit declaration to Udlændinge- og Integrationsministeriet — no parliamentary bill needed.
- **Dual citizenship**: yes (since 2015).
- **Timeline**: 2 years residence + months of administrative processing. **Total ~2.5–3 years**.

## 5. Naturalization — refugees and stateless persons (Convention-recognized)

- **Category**: reduced-residency naturalization.
- **Residency**: 8 years (refugees recognized under § 7(1) and stateless persons under the 1954 Convention). Permanent residence required ≥1 year (vs. 2 for general applicants) at bill-passing.
- **Language / civics / self-support / criminal-record**: standard rules apply.
- **Timeline**: 8 years residence + ministry review + parliamentary bill ≈ 10–12 years.

## 6. Stateless children born in Denmark (1961 Convention)

- **Category**: 1961 Statelessness Convention obligation.
- **Eligibility**: child born in Denmark who is and remains stateless, age 18–21, lawful residence in Denmark for 3 of the last 5 years, no serious criminal record. Acquired by declaration.
- **Timeline**: declaration-based; weeks-to-months processing once filed in eligibility window.
- **Notable**: Denmark was the subject of a 2011 scandal for failing to honor this obligation; rules were corrected and remain in force.

## 7. Danish-identifying residents of South Schleswig (Bonn–Copenhagen Declarations)

- **Category**: special minority path.
- **Eligibility**: members of the Danish minority in Southern Schleswig (Schleswig-Holstein, Germany) who relocate to Denmark. Per the 24 January 2020 agreement, leniently included in naturalization bills after **2 years continuous residence** in Denmark.
- **Language / civics**: still required but applied with reduced strictness.
- **Timeline**: 2 years residence + ministry + parliamentary bill ≈ 3–4 years.

## 8. Distinguished services / extraordinary contribution

- **Category**: discretionary parliamentary inclusion.
- **Eligibility**: no codified residency / language / civics threshold; the Folketing Naturalization Committee may include individuals of "særlig interesse" (special interest) — world-class athletes, scientists, cultural figures, persons of strategic value to Denmark.
- **Timeline**: months from political sponsorship to inclusion in next bill.
- **Notable**: extremely rare; politically sponsored; no application form. Effectively closed for self-selection.

## 9. Marriage to a Dane

- **Category**: no separate path.
- **Eligibility**: marriage gives **no reduction** to the 9-year residence requirement, no language waiver, no fee reduction. Spouses naturalize on the standard track. Marriage may shorten the family-reunification residence-permit timeline upstream, but the citizenship clock is unaffected.

## 10. Birth on territory (jus soli)

- **Closed.** Denmark has no jus soli. Birth in Denmark to non-Danish parents confers no citizenship rights beyond the stateless-child provision in path 6.

## 11. Citizenship by investment (CBI) / golden passport

- **Closed.** Denmark has no investor-citizenship program and no fast-tracked passport-for-investment route. Investment can support a residence permit, which still runs the standard 9-year clock.

## 12. Re-acquisition by former Danes (pre-2015 dual-citizenship victims)

- **Category**: restoration.
- **Eligibility**: former Danish citizens who lost citizenship by acquiring another nationality before 1 September 2015 may re-acquire by declaration. Conditioned on no serious criminal record. The window for declaration-based re-acquisition **closes 1 July 2026**; after that, former Danes must apply through ordinary naturalization.
- **Timeline**: months after filing the declaration.

## 13. Re-acquisition by former Danes via residency (§ 4)

- **Category**: restoration via residency.
- **Eligibility**: former Danes who resumed residence in Denmark may regain citizenship by declaration after 2 years continuous residence (provided loss of citizenship was not due to fraud).
- **Timeline**: 2 years residence + administrative processing.

## Comparison summary

Ranked by realistic time to passport (May 2026):

1. **Descent at birth (path 1)** — instant. The only "fast" Danish citizenship for non-Danes is via a Danish parent.
2. **Lost-generation / pre-2015 former-Dane declaration (paths 2 and 12)** — months, but window closes **1 July 2026**. Urgent.
3. **Re-acquisition via 2-year residence (path 13)** — ~2.5 years; former Danes only.
4. **Nordic citizens (path 4)** — ~2.5–3 years from arrival. The fastest adult naturalization path for non-Danes.
5. **South Schleswig Danish minority (path 7)** — ~3–4 years.
6. **Stateless children born in DK (path 6)** — bound by birth and age window.
7. **Refugees / stateless (path 5)** — ~10–12 years.
8. **Standard naturalization (path 3)** — ~11–13 years given current backlogs.
9. **Distinguished services (path 8)** — not self-selectable.
10. **Marriage, jus soli, CBI (paths 9, 10, 11)** — no Danish path exists.

**Effectively closed for an outside applicant without Danish ancestry, refugee status, or Nordic citizenship**: only path 3 (standard 9-year) is open, and it is unusually slow even by EU standards.

**Dual citizenship**: permitted since 1 September 2015, both for Danes acquiring another nationality and for foreigners naturalizing in Denmark. No renunciation required.

**Recent changes (flag-worthy)**:
- The **2021 political agreement** (in force 1 July 2021, extended through 30 June 2026) tightened: added 3½/4-year employment requirement; raised criminal-record quarantines (up to 30 years); added handshake at ceremony; introduced 5-question "Danish values" sub-block on civics test requiring 4/5 correct.
- **2025 disruption**: second 2025 parliamentary bill cancelled due to Denmark's EU presidency H2-2025; only one bill in 2025–26 session (tabled January 2026, effective 1 April 2026). Thousands of applications frozen pre-election. Realistic queue wait now 2.5–3.5 years on top of residence.
- **November 2025**: government appointed a commission to design screening for "antidemocratic beliefs" among citizenship applicants — expected to produce further tightening 2026–2027.
- **2026 fee**: 6,270 DKK (up from prior years).
- **Sunset 1 July 2026**: transitional declaration windows for pre-2015 former-Dane re-acquisition and the 1961–1978 "lost generation" close. Acting before this date is irrevocably faster than after.
