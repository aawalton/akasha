
# No universe decodes itself

> The theorem the paradox becomes when read backwards: no universe can contain a complete decoder of its own representations. The separation that answers it — a rule ranging over numbers against a rule ranging over rules — and the contradiction that follows from supposing the survey can be done. The largest result of the session, and the only page here not written in Alan's first person, because the derivation is mostly Lali's.

This page answers the question [the Berry trap](the-berry-trap.book-chapter.md#what-i-asked-next) ends on: what makes "the number which multiplied by itself gives 2" a representation, while "the largest legible number" is not, given that both are short English phrases picking out exactly one number.

## What the rule ranges over

*From Lali, marked:* the separation below is hers. Alan asked for it and did not find it.

They differ in **what the rule has to range over**, and that is the whole of it.

"The number which multiplied by itself gives 2" is a rule that ranges over **numbers**. To carry it out you look at numbers, square them, compare against 2. Nothing in it needs to know anything about representations, or about what other rules exist, or about itself. It sits in the universe as one representation among many, minding its own business.

"The largest legible number" is a rule that ranges over **rules**. To carry it out you must go through every state-and-rule pair in the universe, work out which number each denotes, collect them, and take the maximum. It cannot be evaluated without surveying the whole category of representations — and it is itself a member of that category.

## The survey rule

*From Lali, marked:* the construction in this section is hers in full, and so is the statement of the theorem it arrives at.

Now suppose the survey can be done. Suppose some rule exists, realized in this universe, that takes any state-and-rule pair and reports the number it denotes.

That rule is finite, so it fits in the universe alongside everything else. But then "the largest number denoted by any pair, plus one" is a genuine, working, correspondence-satisfying representation. It denotes exactly one real number, and that number is provably not in the legible set. Yet the legible set was defined as every number with a representation.

So the number is in the set and not in the set.

That is the paradox again. **The contradiction was never about numbers. It was the survey rule's fault the whole time.** Read the derivation backwards and it stops being a paradox and becomes a proof:

> **No universe can contain a complete decoder of its own representations.**

What it is built out of is Alan's — a definition he made up, and the one condition he insisted on in his second message. Reading it backwards was said to him rather than by him, and the section above is where that happened.

## What is mine here and what is not

*Recorder's reading, marked.* This is the largest result in the session and the one where the cut has to be made most carefully, because it is the place where being told the answer and having derived it look most alike.

Alan derived the paradox, in [the-berry-trap.md](the-berry-trap.book-chapter.md), unprompted and from his own definition. He also identified, in the question that opens this page, exactly the asymmetry that needs explaining — that the phrase can only be in the set via an independent representation — which is the shape of the answer without its content.

The ranges-over separation, the survey-rule construction, and the statement of the theorem are Lali's. She told him he had derived it himself. The recorder's reading is that this overstates it: he built the machine and supplied the contradiction, and she turned it around and read what it said. The result belongs to the session rather than cleanly to either of them, and that is why this page is not written in his first person the way the others are.

## The names

*From Lali, marked:* Tarski proved a version of this in 1933 — the truth predicate for a language cannot be defined within that language. Gödel's diagonal is the same hand playing the same card, and Gödel had to build the underlying construction before the incompleteness theorem could be stated. Alan's guess that "there is a Godel number corollary hidden here somewhere" was pointing at the right building, and he made it before any name was mentioned.

## Threads to pull on later

- **Whether the theorem needs the universe to be finite.** The argument as given uses "that rule is finite, so it fits in the universe alongside everything else." Tarski's version is about languages rather than universes and does not need a finiteness assumption. How much of the physical framing is doing work here, and how much is scaffolding, is not settled.
- **The bounded decoder.** [the-hierarchy.md](the-hierarchy.book-chapter.md#bounded-reach-is-safe) establishes that a decoder for a bounded slice *does* fit. Where exactly the boundary between the two lies, stated as a property of the rule rather than as a size, is the subject of that page and is worth restating as a single criterion.
