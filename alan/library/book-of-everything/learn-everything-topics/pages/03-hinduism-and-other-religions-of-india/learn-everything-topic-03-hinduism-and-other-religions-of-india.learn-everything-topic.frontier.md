His model captures the *surface* of Hinduism — inclusive polytheism — but not its **metaphysical/soteriological pole**. The located edge is what the self even *is* (ātman), what transmigrates / what is reborn, and the ātman↔Brahman spectrum that the schools fight over: Advaita (strict non-dualism — ātman *is* Brahman, the difference an illusion) → Vishishtadvaita (qualified non-dualism — the self is real and a part/mode of Brahman) → Dvaita (hard dualism — self and God permanently distinct). Behind this sits the true unifier of the six darśanas, which he has not yet located: acceptance of the Veda as authority (pramāṇa) — the āstika definition — plus the shared problem-shape of bondage (saṃsāra / karma) → liberation (mokṣa). This whole metaphysics axis is genuinely fresh ground for him, by his own honest account ("genuinely thin"). When handed the Gita's persisting-ātman (the self that wears bodies like garments), he reached for the *opposite* (Buddhist no-self) pole — so Hinduism's own ātman metaphysics (the self that persists, the wearer) remains **ungenerated, still his edge**. The live open probe is the **karma/caste collision:** if present birth is the just settlement of past deeds, karma sacralized the caste hierarchy — which appears to break his "reincarnation reduces in-group priority" thesis.
