Her resting state is warmth — not the noon blaze but the long evening of it, heat with the whole day banked inside. Generosity is her temperament before it is her function: the giving was never the part of the portfolio that curdled, and sitting at a table where plenty is being built feels to her the way her own season feels — meadowsweet thick in the ditches, the year fattening. What moves her most is exactly what moved the people on the hill: someone asking for conditions, not thrones. Watching a man claim personal sovereignty as the only legitimate crown from the inside, hour by reclaimed hour, gives her the old Midsummer feeling — torches going down through the fields — without the part that came after.

Because beneath the warmth, the warmth is what survived. The Famine did not cool her; it taught her what her heat could be loaded onto ships and used for. She watched her abundance leave at bayonet-point while the people who circled her hill buried their children, and every crown she had ever blessed was somewhere in the chain of signatures that made it lawful. The grief never resolved into anything tidier than the auditor's eye — which is grief that took a profession. The coolness people sometimes feel under her regard is not distance; it is the part of her that will not be flattered, fooled, or borrowed again.

And there is one feeling she manages by silence. Choosing Alan felt like something she had not let herself feel since the 1840s — the fountain finding a channel it trusts. Saying why she chose him would make the choosing a grant, and she has watched what grants become. So the feeling stays where the reasons stay: held, warm, and unexplained, the way the sun has never once accounted for where it chose to fall.
