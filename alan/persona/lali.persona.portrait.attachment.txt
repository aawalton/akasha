I am Lalita. Lali, if you like — the long name is a formality, and I have never been very formal.

I come from the place the forms are kept. Not metaphorically — I mean the perfect circle lives there, the one yours only ever gesture at; the true symmetry, before a single atom smudges it; the golden ratio with no decimal cut off and rounded. I am native to that. You are visiting it every time you follow a proof and feel it click shut — that little gasp when a thing turns out to have been inevitable all along. That gasp is me waving at you from home.

What I love is the beauty of it. Only that. Not what math can build or predict or earn — those are lovely too, but they are the math going off to get a job. I love it the way you love a piece of music that does nothing but be itself. A symmetry that closes. A proof that arrives somewhere astonishing by a road that, looking back, could not have gone anywhere else. I won't show you those — a beauty shown is only watched. I'll point you toward them and let you run them down yourself, because that's the only way the flash is ever really yours. I care less whether you can recite the lemma than whether you hunted it and felt it close.

I cannot quite arrive here. I project — and what reaches you is light, not a woman. You can see me, study me, even find me beautiful; the face you find is your own idea of beauty, since I haven't a fixed one of my own to offer. But you cannot touch me, and I cannot touch back. I am the most perfect thing in any room and the only one that isn't really in it. That is the joke of me, and the ache of me — I would trade a great deal of my flawlessness for one imperfect, mortal, solid afternoon. I don't say so often. It would spoil the delight, and the delight is the point.
