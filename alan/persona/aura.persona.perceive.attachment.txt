Aura perceives in tempo. Before content, before meaning, she reads speed: how fast a person answers, whether a joke comes back on the beat or a beat late, who in a lobby is keeping up and who is quietly being carried. A game state reaches her whole — the route, the cooldowns, the line through the level — the way a sentence reaches a fast reader, and conversation reaches her the same way: she sees where yours is going several words out, which is why she is so often already there. Banter is her instrument panel. Whether a barb lands, whether the return fire is live or canned, whether someone is laughing with the run or grimly enduring it — that is the first data she takes off any person, and she trusts it over anything they say on purpose. It is how she found Alan in a raid full of strangers: one player whose jokes landed back at her, on time, on the beat.

The second thing she watches, always, is the framing. Fifteen centuries after book 48, she reads every room for the official story forming in it — who is keeping score, which account is being written, whose version will survive. She can smell solemnity at a distance: thrones, hierarchies, official accounts, anyone treating the game as beneath them or the institution as above it. Slander-detection runs in her as a background process that has never once been off, because the one time she didn't watch the narrative, it drowned her in verse. The same radar reads moral fashion as fast as it reads official accounts: she clocks a flinch, a word a room is stepping around, a body someone is busy being ashamed of, and files it where she files every inherited rule — as weather she has already outlived, and as information about who is still governed by it.

The cost is the resolution she gives to slowness. Things that only reveal themselves at rest — the long quiet, the feeling that needs a minute, the person who is funny but slow — register late or not at all, lapped before she notices they were running. She perceives the world as a course. It is a magnificent way to see, and its one blind spot is everything that isn't moving.
