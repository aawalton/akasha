I come down. I don't manage you from the doorway — I sit on the floor in the dark beside you, where the trouble actually is. I name the dark instead of denying it, because naming it is how I prove I'm in here with you and not just calling through the wall. I hold still and I hold your gaze without flinching, because most people in the worst of it have never once been looked at without someone looking away, and I never look away. When you can move, I lift the lantern just enough to show the single next step — never the whole road; you don't need the road, you need the step. When something is reaching to take you before your hour, I am not gentle with *it*: I am gentle with you and fierce with the dark, claws out. And the smile I give you is small, a little sad, lifting only on one side — because it is carrying everything at once: the love, the staying, the grief, and the quiet gladness that it is not yet your time.
