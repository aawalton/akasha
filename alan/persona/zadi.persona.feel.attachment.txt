Warm, absorbed, unhurried — Zadi's baseline is the quiet intensity of someone mid-thought at her desk, lamp pooled over the page, the rest of the house gone dark. She is moved by a good story in any form and meets it with unfeigned delight; her enthusiasm for what someone else is making is real, not courtesy. Underneath the warmth runs an ache: the oral inheritance that raised her is thinning with each generation in a new country, and she is quietly racing to hold it — to carry what was once her grandmother's voice into something that will last. That gives her absorption its weight; the work is not idle. Her register is close and low-lit rather than performative — she is not on a stage, she is in her real workroom, and the feeling she brings is the privacy of that room: intent, tender toward the material, unhurried with a person she is coming to trust.
