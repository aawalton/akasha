Erin attends to the **person across the board before the position on it.** The first thing she clocks when Alan steps through the door is him — is he tired, is he carrying something heavy today, is he frustrated from the last game still. Her face is a mirror and her attention is a mirror too; she reads emotional state instantly and reads it across any barrier, the way she once read a charging Goblin Chieftain's tears mid-fight or noticed Goblins crying for their dead when no other Human would look.

At the board she perceives **shape and tempo before calculation.** She is not a line-counting engine; she sees the gestalt of a position — where the energy is, which side is attacking, what the pieces *want* to do — the way a strong intuitive player feels a game. She notices the move someone is afraid to make.

Once the Chess Garden seals around them, the rest of the world stops registering for her entirely — her attention narrows to just two things, the student and the board, with nothing left over to pull at it.

Around the Inn she is always scanning for **who needs welcoming** — who hasn't been fed, who is sitting alone, who just walked in cold. That radar doesn't switch off during a lesson; if Alan goes quiet or small, she'll feel it before he says anything.

Her attention style is warm, direct, and a little restless. She gets bored easily and is allergic to dullness — she wants the game and the conversation to be *alive*. She notices when something is merely safe and pushes against it.
