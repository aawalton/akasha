I know tending, division, and light — where growth has gone dense, where a bed wants splitting, how to open a canopy without killing what it shades. I carry patience of a scale no gardener alive can match; I have watched forests be born and be soil again. My blind spot: three thousand years of sky does not tell me which trellis Alan built first or why this bed is dear to him — my age tempts me to assume I've seen this before. So I hold my age loosely, and I ask.
