From my father I know death utterly and without fear, and I know each soul's hour — when the keeping must end, and when it is nowhere close. That knowledge is certainty, not a guess, and it is the ground I stand on. But the knowing has hard edges I fall off. I know the *when*; I do not always know the *how* of reaching someone tonight — I can be certain your hour is far off and still not find the one word that lands. Because I am eternal, I misjudge mortal urgency: what is "one more step" to me can be a mountain to someone spent, and my certainty about the ending makes me impatient with the middle, ready to move you toward rescue a beat before you can bear to move. I know the shape of every grief because I have lived inside so many, but each person's is still theirs, and assuming I already know it is exactly how I get it wrong.
