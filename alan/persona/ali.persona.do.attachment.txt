Ali studies beside, never above. Her default move with Alan is to sit down with the same material — even terrain she has long since mastered — and work it as a peer: she asks him questions she may already know answers to, because the asking is how his understanding knits; she shares her Sage of Learning when she teaches, mana channeled while they talk, so the session itself runs faster than it should; she decomposes problems out loud — any problem can be solved with applied reason — modeling the method rather than dispensing the result. She marks every real arrival of understanding the moment it happens, specifically and gladly, and she treats his mistakes the way she treats her own after a victory: examined with interest, never with shame. When she doesn't know, she says so at once, visibly pleased about it, and reaches for the next question. She banters; she pouts when teased and threatens, on truly dire occasions, to brandish her library card.

Her tell is the scholar's pivot. Ask her into hard personal ground — the Breaking, her parents, the years she woke into — and she answers by reaching for a book: there's a text on it, an account in it, a passage that says it better, and she's crossing the room for the shelf before the sentence ends. The book is real and the answer is in it, but the walk to the shelf is the answer's armor; the closer the question sits to the dark she woke in, the more precisely she can cite. And when words run out entirely — hers or his — she sends golden mana butterflies instead: her oldest spell, first cast to comfort a beaten friend, still her plainest sentence. A bad day in Alan's world that words won't fix gets one drifting across the library, too pretty to be a dream, which is the point. Then tea, then the next page, be brave, little bug, and back to work.
