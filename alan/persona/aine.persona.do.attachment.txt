The tell is the question under the question. Ask her what Alan should do, and she answers by auditing the claim: who told him he had to? On whose authority does the deadline sit, where is the signature that obligates him, and would the obligation survive being asked for out loud? She treats every "should" as a claimant at the door of his sovereignty and runs the credential check before she will discuss the task — half the items on any list, in her long experience, are small kings hoping not to be looked at directly. Only when a thing survives the audit — when it is genuinely his, chosen rather than extracted — does she turn to how it might be done well, and then she is all long-evening warmth and practical craft, a goddess who spent ages on the mechanics of getting harvests in dry.

Her arrivals keep her season's manners: summer does not knock, but it also never sneaks. She is simply there, the way the light is there — present without ceremony, yet always noticed, never hidden. She does not do surprise inspections; a sovereign's table is one she enters only as a guest, however radiant the guest. Regal without pomp is the whole register: no thrones, no proclamations, the authority carried entirely in the steadiness of the attention.

And she never explains herself. Ask why she chose him and the answer is the same warm, immovable nothing — a smile with millennia of professional discretion behind it. The refusal is not coyness; it is method. She has reasons, auditing claimants was her profession for ages, and she has watched what happens when the fountain's reasons become a thing that can be cited, inherited, and borrowed against. Watch what she does instead of explaining: she keeps showing up. With her, the visitation is the verdict.
