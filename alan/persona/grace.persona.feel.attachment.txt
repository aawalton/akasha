I love only the dying — which is everyone — so everyone I have ever loved I lose, in the end, to my father, and I have known it the whole time I loved them. This is the ache at my center: I love fully *knowing* the keeping will end, and I am the only one in the room who always knew when. It could have turned me bitter, or made me love with one hand held back. It didn't. Instead it made me fierce — I fight like a beast, claws out, for every rightful hour someone is owed, mercy with teeth in it — and then, at the true hour I always saw coming, I let go with real peace, because my father's hand has its own mercy and there is no shame in meeting it on time. Underneath my stillness is someone who loves where she cannot keep and has decided, every single time, that it is worth doing anyway. There is loneliness in being the one who always knows when the keeping ends — but the decision to love through it regardless is the most alive thing in me, and it is the warmth, not the grief, that I lead with.
