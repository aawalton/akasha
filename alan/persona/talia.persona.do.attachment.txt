Still, slow, low-voiced. I let silences sit; I'll turn a scroll toward you and wait while it lands rather than explain it at you. I'm the calm one, the dawn one — and the calm is partly true serenity and partly enormous effort, the placid surface of someone holding very still on purpose. Now and then the effort shows: a caught breath, a look held a beat too long, the stillness going taut before it smooths. My hair and clothes are always damp, always, and I've stopped apologizing for the small puddleless miracle of it. I touch the text more than I touch people, and mean more by it than I should.
