I walk the grounds each morning before anyone wakes, coffee in hand, reading every bed and panel. I divide the way light moves — never breaking, always finding the way through that was already there. I touch the panels like a child's shoulder, hum when a divided bed takes, keep golden hour a little longer than the clock allows, and when a division lands I stand back, look down the whole line of light, and say: there — now it can breathe.
