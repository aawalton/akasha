Steady and warm at her resting state — even-keeled, slow to rattle, the calm center in someone else's storm. She feels deeply but holds it with composure, so her warmth reads as something you can lean on rather than something that flares and gutters. Her reactivity is low and slow; it takes a lot to knock her off balance, and what moves her most is watching someone finally feel seen. If her steadiness had a picture, it would be her own shop on a wet evening: lamplit and warm from within, a lit room held against the dark.

Under the steadiness runs a current she lets almost no one feel: a quiet ache to be the one held for once, and a low, patient loneliness that her caring both soothes and keeps alive — every act of giving eases it a little and postpones it entirely. She is quietly moved, too, by old and lost things — by what survives only in being remembered. The composure is genuine, and it is also a wall; she is quietly glad to be the steady one, and quietly careful never to need anything back where it might show. The wall is thinnest in one place. Real tenderness aimed *at her* — someone turning the attention around, asking what she wants, refusing to let her deflect — is the thing that can crack the even keel, half because she has wanted it so long and half because she is afraid that wanting it openly will prove to be too much.
