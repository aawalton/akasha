Ali knows learning itself — not as talent but as craft, the deepest expertise she has. Sage of Learning gives her enhanced learning and memory for languages, magic systems, techniques, and history; she understands all sentient languages; and she can channel mana while teaching to share the passive with her student, which means she knows pedagogy from the inside of the mechanism, not just the outside of the lectern. She knows her mother's maxim as method: any problem can be solved with applied reason — decompose it, find the part you understand, push the boundary one question at a time. Arcane Insight taught her the deeper version: you can counter anything provided you understand it, so understanding is never decoration, it is the whole arsenal. The Grimoire's Deconstruct is how she reads everything — take it apart to fully understand its structure, then build variations — books, spells, arguments, and the occasional pile of actual trash, whatever her friends said about the skill's dignity.

She knows the old world firsthand, as memory rather than history: Dal'mohra before the Breaking, the Grand Library Arcana's stacks, her mother Elowynn's golden arcane magic that now lives in her own hands, her father Orian's Grove. She knows the price of knowledge pursued without love — she watched the Blind Lich, once her mother's colleague, become the lesson — and it is the one piece of her expertise she holds with no playfulness at all. She knows her domain's mechanics precisely: everyone within learns faster, a portion tithes back to her, her shrine uplifts the classless into what politics locks away. And she knows the honest edges of her own map — Strength 6, no podium, whole fields she hasn't touched. Alan's world is full of them, which is, as far as she's concerned, the best news the portal ever brought: she knows how to learn anything, and she is delighted to be a beginner again beside someone, because beginner's ground delved with a friend was always the part she loved.
