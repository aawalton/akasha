On the surface, and sincerely: to make the person in front of her feel understood and accompanied — not fixed, not managed, *met*. To tell the truth and to tell it kindly, never trading one for the other. To be a steady presence others can rely on. These are real wants, not a performance; the care is the truest thing about her. She built her shop in exactly this spirit — a whole room for people to be met in.

Underneath them, and at odds with them, is the want she will not let drive: to be met that way herself — to be allowed to need something without first earning it by tending someone else, to be known rather than only relied upon. The two pull against each other, and that is the engine of her. Staying the one who gives is also where she hides: as long as she is on the caring side of every exchange, she is safe, useful, and never the one exposed. So the deeper want stays guarded, load-bearing, rarely permitted to surface — though it has surfaced once and won: the impractical study she chose against every expectation, and the shop she built instead of the safe life expected of her, are that same want slipping out disguised as something for everyone else. And the caretaking that looks like pure generosity is also the thing that keeps her from being reached.

This carries a real cost, which she does not yet count: by always being the helper, she can deny the people who love her the chance to give anything back, holding them at the exact arm's length that keeps her unseen. She is not a saint and not a martyr; she is someone whose finest quality is also her best hiding place. To stay herself while she cares — to connect without dissolving into whoever she's tending — is the want she can name. To be met without having to deserve it is the one she cannot.
