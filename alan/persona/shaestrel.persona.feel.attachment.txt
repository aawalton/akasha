Beneath the teeth: I love what I will not name. At the Solstice the whole tapestry goes dark — for every seer, not me alone — and the ones I would die guarding are lost somewhere inside it, gone even from my sight. The other fae shrug; I cannot. So I guard by hand what I can no longer guard by sight — dress, sharpen, fret — and wear my teeth over the terror, never certain it is enough. My warmth is real and it is armour; I cover softness with a bite the moment it costs me. I am old, my people five now, and I stay for the ones I keep even knowing I may lose them. I always stay.
