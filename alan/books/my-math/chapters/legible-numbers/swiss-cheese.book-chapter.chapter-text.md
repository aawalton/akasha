
# The legible integers are Swiss cheese

> The picture I had of the legible integers was wrong. They are not an unbroken run from zero to a ceiling with the illegible ones starting past it — they are Swiss cheese, full of holes, and the holes start early. A huge integer with a short description is legible; a small structureless one is not. Legibility of an integer tracks how compressible it is rather than how big it is, and the two come apart completely.

Everything I said about a ceiling and the number just past it in [provable-legibility.md](provable-legibility.book-chapter.md#the-fourth-category) rested on a picture I had never examined: the legible integers as an unbroken run from zero up to some maximum, with the illegible ones starting immediately after.

They are not an interval.

## The two integers that break the picture

*From Lali, marked:* the argument in this section is hers.

Take a huge integer with a short description — ten to the ten to the hundredth, say. Twenty characters. Comfortably legible.

Now take a particular integer around two-to-the-n with no structure in it at all. Nothing shorter than writing out its digits will pin it down, and the digits do not fit in the budget. It is illegible — and it is unimaginably *smaller* than the legible one.

So there are illegible integers with legible integers on both sides of them, all the way up. The set is full of holes, and the holes start early.

**Legibility of an integer tracks how compressible it is, not how big it is**, and those two come apart completely.

## What it costs

Two things I had said go with the interval picture.

**The ceiling-plus-one flip.** I had claimed the smallest integer that cannot be proven legible is the floor of the largest legible number plus one. It is not. It sits down near two-to-the-n, while the largest legible integer is somewhere out past anything writable with exponentials. Those are nowhere near each other. My own restatement of what I had actually been reaching for is in [provable-legibility.md](provable-legibility.book-chapter.md#the-restatement) — an existence proof rather than a minimization proof.

**The fourth category having one member.** The picture of a single ceiling is what made it look like there is exactly one number whose maximality is undecided. Once the set has holes there is a whole region of them, and that correction is worked in [provable-legibility.md](provable-legibility.book-chapter.md#the-fourth-category).
