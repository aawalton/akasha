
# Latvian Citizenship Paths (May 2026)

Latvia operates a restored-state regime: the post-1991 framework only recognizes pre-1940 Latvian citizens and their descendants as citizens by right, while everyone else — including ~169k Soviet-era settlers who became stateless "non-citizens" (`nepilsoņi`) — must naturalize. Restored citizenship for pre-1940 descendants is the diaspora workhorse (no language test, no generational limit, dual citizenship allowed). Dual citizenship was reopened in 2013 but only for EU/EEA/EFTA/NATO + Brazil/Australia/NZ. Since 2022 the Saeima has aggressively tightened residence and naturalization for Russian/Belarusian nationals; the Golden Visa is closed to both.

## Restored citizenship — pre-1940 descendants (jus sanguinis, registration)

- **Category**: Registration / restored citizenship under §2 of the Citizenship Law.
- **Eligible**: anyone who was a Latvian citizen on 17 June 1940 (the day before Soviet occupation), plus all their direct descendants — no generational cap, both maternal and paternal lines count, ethnicity irrelevant.
- **Requirements**: documentary chain (ancestor's pre-1940 Latvian passport, civil registry entries, etc.) traced down to the applicant; clean criminal record; no membership in anti-Latvian or totalitarian organizations.
- **No language test, no Constitution/history exam, no oath, no residency**.
- **Dual citizenship**: permitted with any country (special category — restored citizens are exempt from the EU/NATO list restriction).
- **Timeline**: ~6–12 months end-to-end from document assembly to passport.
- **Backlog**: OCMA (PMLP) currently processes registration cases in roughly 4–6 months once docs are complete; volume is steady, no announced backlog.

## Descent — child of a Latvian citizen (jus sanguinis, automatic)

- **Category**: §3 — citizenship by birth.
- **Eligible**: child born anywhere if at least one parent is a Latvian citizen at the time of birth.
- **Requirements**: birth certificate + parent's citizenship proof; if born abroad, register with the nearest Latvian embassy / OCMA.
- **No language, civics, oath, or residency requirements.**
- **Dual citizenship**: child may hold any citizenship until age 18; if the second citizenship is *not* on the permitted list, the child must choose by age 25.
- **Timeline**: registration is administrative — weeks to a few months.

## Birth on territory — limited jus soli (anti-statelessness)

- **Category**: §3.1 / Law on Granting Citizenship to Children of Non-citizens (in force from 1 Jan 2020).
- **Eligible**: child born in Latvia from 1 Jan 2020 onward where parents are Latvian non-citizens or stateless persons receives Latvian citizenship automatically (parents may opt the child out into another nationality if both agree). Older cohort (born to non-citizens between 21 Aug 1991 and 31 Dec 2019) can be registered as citizens on parental application — no exam, no fees.
- **Requirements**: child must not already hold another citizenship (or parents must waive it).
- **Timeline**: weeks (declaration).
- **Effect**: this provision is closing the non-citizen population through generational attrition — no new non-citizen births since 2020.

## Naturalization by residency (standard path)

- **Category**: §12 of the Citizenship Law.
- **Eligible**: adult foreigner who has held a **permanent residence permit** (PR / EU long-term resident permit) in Latvia for at least 5 years immediately preceding the application. The PR itself requires ~5 years of prior temporary residence — so the realistic floor is **~10 years total in Latvia**.
- **Requirements**:
  - Latvian language exam at **A2-B1 level** (listening, reading, writing, speaking — 60% per section; a B1 raise has been debated but A2-anchored as of May 2026).
  - Constitution (`Satversme`) + history + culture + National Anthem exam.
  - Oath of loyalty to the Republic of Latvia.
  - Legal source of income.
  - Clean criminal record; no service in foreign armed forces / intelligence; no anti-constitutional activity.
  - **Renounce prior citizenship** unless held with a permitted-dual country (see Dual Citizenship section).
- **Exam waivers**: graduates of Latvian-language schools (≥50% curriculum in Latvian); disability groups I-III (full or partial); applicants over a high-age threshold may receive accommodations but not blanket exemption.
- **Each exam may be retried up to 3 times** (language: 3-month interval; Constitution: 1-month).
- **Timeline**: ~10 years residence + 6–12 months processing = **~10.5–11 years total**.
- **Backlog**: PMLP cites 6–12 months processing as standard in May 2026; no flagged backlog for non-Russian applicants.

## Naturalization by marriage (no facilitated path)

- **Category**: spouses fall under the standard naturalization regime.
- **Eligible**: same 5-year permanent residence requirement as standard naturalization; marriage does not shorten the clock and does not waive any exam.
- **Practical floor**: spouse residence permit → 5 years → PR → 5 years → naturalization = **~10 years**, same as standard path.
- **One mild concession**: spouses can sit alongside the standard requirements with no separate hurdle — i.e., Latvia is unusual in offering *no* facilitated marriage path at all.

## Naturalization of former non-citizens (`nepilsoņi`)

- **Category**: same §12 naturalization, but practical concessions apply.
- **Eligible**: ~169k holders of the non-citizen passport (mostly ethnic Russians, Belarusians, Ukrainians who settled in Latvia during Soviet rule and their pre-2020 descendants).
- **Requirements**: same Latvian-language A2-B1 exam + Constitution/history exam + oath. Non-citizens **do not need to renounce another citizenship** (they have none).
- **Concessions in current practice**: residency clock is satisfied (lifelong residents); waivers for elderly, Latvian-school graduates, disabled.
- **Timeline**: ~6–12 months once documents and exams pass.
- **Volume trajectory**: naturalization rate has fallen to a few hundred per year; the population is aging out faster than it naturalizes. The status itself is being phased out by the 2020 birth law, not by mass conversion.

## Repatriation — Latvian ethnic origin

- **Category**: §3 / Law on Repatriation — separate channel from §2 restored citizenship.
- **Eligible**: persons of Latvian or Livonian ethnicity (or descended from such), where there is no documented pre-1940 citizen ancestor. Requires proof of ethnicity (church records, Soviet-era documents, family genealogy).
- **Requirements**: ethnic-origin proof; modest Latvian-language proficiency expected (lower than naturalization); no residency precondition.
- **Timeline**: ~4–6 months administrative processing.
- **Dual citizenship**: permitted on the same generous terms as restored citizenship.

## Special merits — Saeima grant

- **Category**: §13 — citizenship for special merits in science, culture, sport, defence, or other public benefit to Latvia.
- **Eligible**: candidates whose merits are *already* documented (the grant is not for promised future contributions).
- **Process**: application → Saeima Citizenship, Migration and Social Cohesion Commission → full Saeima vote (100 MPs).
- **Requirements waived**: residence, language, civics — all may be set aside at Saeima discretion.
- **Dual citizenship**: explicitly permitted with any country.
- **Volume**: ~1–5 grants per year (notable: Mikhail Baryshnikov in 2017; Ukrainian athletes since 2022).
- **Timeline**: 6–18 months if accepted; rejection is silent.

## Stateless / refugee paths

- **Stateless persons** holding Latvian stateless-person status can naturalize on the standard §12 path; the 5-year permanent-residence clock applies, and the renunciation requirement is moot.
- **Recognized refugees**: standard naturalization clock; no shortened path. (Latvia has not legislated a refugee-specific naturalization fast track.)
- **Temporary Protection (Ukrainians since 2022)**: not a path to citizenship; status extended through at least 2026 but does not count toward the permanent-residence clock.

## Re-acquisition of lost citizenship

- **Category**: §24 — restoration of citizenship for persons who previously held it and lost it (e.g., by acquiring an unpermitted dual citizenship and being deregistered).
- **Requirements**: documented prior Latvian citizenship; no security disqualifications; if the unpermitted second nationality is still held, it must be renounced.
- **Timeline**: months, not years; no exams.

## Investment / Golden Visa — residence only, then standard naturalization

No CBI shortcut to citizenship exists in Latvia. The investor TRP feeds the standard ~10-year naturalization clock (5 TRP + 5 PR + processing) with full language and civics exam. See [residency/latvia.md](../residency/latvia.book-chapter.md) for tiers, RU/BY closures, and current scrutiny.

## Dual citizenship policy — precise state (May 2026)

Latvia permits dual citizenship under the 2013 amendments only with citizens of:

- **EU member states** (all 27);
- **EFTA member states** (Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, Switzerland);
- **NATO member states** (incl. UK, Canada, US, Türkiye, etc., beyond the EU overlap);
- **Australia, Brazil, New Zealand** (named exceptions).

Outside this list, the new citizenship must be renounced. **Exceptions that allow dual citizenship with any country**:

- Restored §2 citizens and their descendants;
- Repatriates and their descendants;
- Citizenship by special merits (Saeima grant);
- Latvians who left between 17 Jun 1940 and 4 May 1990 (Soviet/Nazi exile) and their descendants;
- Children — until they must elect at age 25 if their second nationality is unpermitted.

A Latvian who voluntarily acquires an unpermitted second nationality must declare and renounce Latvian citizenship within 30 days, or lose it by operation of law.

## Notable recent changes (2022–2026)

- **Feb 2022 onward**: Latvia closed the Golden Visa to Russian and Belarusian citizens; ~327 Russian residence-permit applications rejected on national-security grounds.
- **2022–2023 Immigration Law amendments**: ex-Latvian-citizens and ex-non-citizens who took Russian citizenship must re-pass an A2 Latvian language exam to renew their residence permits. ~4,650 Russian nationals directly affected.
- **Oct 2025**: PMLP issued exit orders to 841 Russian nationals who failed the A2 exam or did not submit documents by deadline; deportation orders followed for non-compliance.
- **15 Jul 2025**: Latvia stopped recognizing non-biometric Russian Federation passports — they no longer work for entry, residence, or visa purposes.
- **2020 birth law (in force)**: no new non-citizen births in Latvia; legacy non-citizen population (~169k) aging out.
- **Dec 2023**: Saeima passed amendments restricting non-citizen privileges to spur naturalization; impact remains modest.
- **B1 language proposal**: ongoing debate about raising the naturalization language bar from A2 toward B1, no enactment as of May 2026.

## Comparison summary

Ranked by speed-to-passport assuming clean documents and no security flags:

1. **Restored citizenship (pre-1940 descendant)** — ~6–12 months, no exams, dual OK. **Fastest practical path for anyone with a pre-1940 Latvian ancestor.**
2. **Descent (parent is Latvian citizen)** — weeks to months, automatic.
3. **Limited jus soli (child born to non-citizens since 2020)** — automatic at birth.
4. **Repatriation (Latvian/Livonian ethnicity, no pre-1940 ancestor doc)** — ~4–6 months, modest language requirement.
5. **Re-acquisition of lost citizenship** — months, no exams.
6. **Special merits (Saeima grant)** — 6–18 months, but unavailable to virtually everyone (extremely high bar).
7. **Non-citizen naturalization** — ~6–12 months processing (no residency clock to wait out), but requires passing A2-B1 language + civics exams.
8. **Standard residence-based naturalization** — **~10–11 years total** (5 TRP + 5 PR + processing) + language + civics + oath + renunciation.
9. **Marriage** — same ~10-year clock; marriage gives no facilitation in Latvia.
10. **Golden Visa investor** — same ~10-year clock as standard; closed to RU/BY; under suspension threat. See [residency/latvia.md](../residency/latvia.book-chapter.md).

**Effectively closed**: any path for Russian or Belarusian first-time applicants (Golden Visa shut; residence-permit renewals burdened or blocked; deportation risk rising). For everyone else without a Latvian ancestor, Latvia is one of the slower naturalization regimes in the EU — only the restored-citizenship channel is genuinely fast.
