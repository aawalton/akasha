
San Marino is a small republic (~33,000 population, ~61 km²) enclaved within Italy, outside the EU and outside Schengen formally but with an entirely open border with Italy — once you clear Italian (Schengen) passport control on arrival, you reach San Marino with no further controls. Time spent in San Marino effectively counts toward the Schengen 90/180-day limit because there is no exit border. San Marino uses the euro under a monetary agreement with the EU, and corporate / personal tax rates are lower than Italy's — most notably via two "Atypical Residency" regimes with substitute tax rates of 6% (pensioners) and 7% (foreign-income earners). Residency permits (Permesso di soggiorno) are issued by the Department of Foreign Affairs (Segreteria di Stato per gli Affari Esteri); decisions are typically made within 60–120 days. The April 2025 amendments tightened the pensioner Atypical Residency thresholds materially.

## Residency permits (Permesso di soggiorno)

### Atypical Residency for pensioners (Residenza Atipica)

The flagship HNW retiree program. As of the April 28, 2025 amendment, applicants must meet **both** conditions simultaneously: annual gross income of at least **€120,000** (up from the prior €50,000 threshold, which was already raised to €120,000 in late 2024) AND **€300,000+ in movable financial assets** (liquid funds or investment securities — real estate, trusts, and other illiquid holdings do not qualify) held in a San Marino bank for the entire duration of the residency. Income may include pension, rental income, dividends, and financial returns; Italian public-sector pensioners (INPDAP and similar) are excluded from the preferential rate. Pension income is taxed at a 6% substitute rate paid in a single installment by June 30 each year, and the rate applies for 10 years (potentially extending through permanent residency). Application fee €1,000, plus a preliminary property purchase or rental contract. Decision within 120 days. Open to pensioners from the EU, Switzerland, or third countries designated by the Congress of State.

### Atypical Residency with reduced tax regime (foreign-income earners)

For individuals who have never previously been resident in San Marino and earn income abroad. **7% substitute tax** on net foreign income, subject to a minimum of €10,000 and maximum of €100,000 annual tax, paid by June 30 each year. There is no minimum investment requirement codified — the gating is the substitute-tax floor (effectively requires ~€143,000+ in annual foreign income to reach the €10,000 minimum tax) and the property purchase or rental contract for residence registration. Application fee €1,000. Functions as San Marino's de-facto HNW / remote-earner program in lieu of a formal digital nomad visa.

### Elective Residency (investor / passive)

Investment-based residence, comparable to Italy's elective residence but with explicit asset thresholds. Three alternative qualifying investments: (a) purchase of San Marino real estate worth **€500,000+**, (b) purchase of real estate worth **€350,000+ with €150,000+ in renovations**, or (c) **€600,000 in interest-free Sammarinese government bonds** held for 10 years. Application fee €10,000 (in addition to the standard €1,000 administrative fee) and mandatory health insurance. Decision within 60 days. Elective residency converts to ordinary (Registry) residency after 10 years.

### Economic / Investor Residency (entrepreneur)

For business owners launching or running a Sammarinese company. Requires holding 51%+ of company capital stock; €75,000 guarantee deposit with the state in the first two years, rising to €150,000 thereafter; obligation to hire Sammarinese employees from the official employment lists; mandatory healthcare coverage (€350/month per capita) and €50,000+ insurance coverage per capita. A simplified pathway exists for companies hiring 5+ employees (8+ for industrial projects), purchasing property ≥€300,000, and providing 24-month healthcare coverage worth €30,000. Priority sectors: technology, green economy, tourism, pharmaceuticals, creative industries, hi-tech. Standard corporate tax of 17% applies.

### Work residence (Permesso di soggiorno per lavoro)

Employer-sponsored. Requires a valid job offer or employment contract from a San Marino-based employer and, in some cases, evidence the role cannot be filled by a Sammarinese resident (labor market test). Two sub-categories: continuous (indefinite contracts) and seasonal (up to 10 months). Most foreign workers in San Marino are Italian cross-border commuters (see below) who do not need this permit.

### Family reunification (Ricongiungimento familiare)

Available for spouses and dependent children of Sammarinese citizens and of long-term legal residents. Standard supporting documentation (proof of relationship, accommodation, income).

### Student residence

For enrollment in primary, secondary, or university programs (University of the Republic of San Marino). Permit issued for at least one year, renewable for the duration of studies.

### Registry Residency (Residenza)

Ordinary residency rather than a permit category per se — granted to spouses and children of citizens, to anyone with 5+ years of continuous documented residence, and to professionals in healthcare, banking, or security roles meeting specific criteria.

## Permanent residency

After **10 years** of continuous legal residence (per current guidance from San Marino legal advisors), an Atypical or Elective resident may transition to ordinary (Registry) residency, which is effectively permanent and is the prerequisite tier for the 20-year naturalization clock. Older sources cite 5 years for permanent residency — the current consolidated guidance is 10 years for the conversion from Atypical/Elective into Registry residency.

## Digital nomad / remote workers

San Marino does not currently operate a dedicated "digital nomad visa" or formal "Economic Residence Permit" under that name as of May 2026. The Atypical Residency with reduced tax regime (7% substitute tax on foreign income) is the closest functional equivalent and is in practice used by foreign remote earners with substantial income. The €10,000 minimum substitute-tax floor — implying ~€143,000+ in annual foreign income — keeps it gated to HNW remote workers, not budget nomads. There is no €40k-style income threshold or simplified online application analogous to Italy's, Spain's, or Portugal's nomad visas.

## Cross-border employment from Italy

Roughly **8,000 cross-border workers commute daily from Italy** to jobs in San Marino. They do not need a Sammarinese residence permit — bilateral agreements with Italy regulate entry, stay, and employment for Italian nationals, and residents of nearby Italian municipalities benefit from simplified arrangements. For US citizens who hold Italian residency (or who can secure Italian residency via Italy's various paths), Italian residence plus cross-border employment in San Marino is the lowest-friction way to access the Sammarinese labor market without going through full Sammarinese residency.

## Italian-mobility interface (de facto Schengen)

San Marino is not in Schengen but has no border controls with Italy. For a US citizen, this means: (a) entry to San Marino requires only the same visa-free / ETIAS authorization that grants entry to Italy and Schengen; (b) days in San Marino effectively count toward the Schengen 90/180-day limit because you transit Italian territory both directions; (c) a Sammarinese residence permit does **not** grant Schengen residence rights — to live in Italy or other Schengen states you still need their own permits; (d) the open border makes Sammarinese residents de facto fully mobile within Italy for shopping, services, and daily life, even though formal cross-border employment requires the bilateral arrangement.

## Practical assessment

For a US citizen seeking residency only, San Marino is a niche but viable option for HNW pensioners (Atypical Residency for pensioners, 6% pension tax, requires €120k income and €300k locally-held assets) or HNW foreign-income earners (7% substitute-tax Atypical Residency, effectively €143k+ annual foreign income). The Elective Residency property/bonds path is open to anyone with €500k–€600k of capital to commit. Processing is fast (60–120 days) and the open border with Italy means you live a fundamentally Italian daily life with a lower tax bill. For citizenship the timeline is 20 years (10 with a Sammarinese spouse), with a discretionary Grand and General Council vote at the end — so this is a residency play, not a passport play.
