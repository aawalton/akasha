
## Jus soli — birth on Canadian territory

### Unrestricted jus soli (Citizenship Act s. 3(1)(a))
- **Eligibility**: any child born in Canada (including Canadian airspace and territorial waters) is a citizen at birth, regardless of parents' immigration status. Sole exception: children of accredited foreign diplomats or international-organization personnel of equivalent status.
- **Timeline**: at birth.
- **Backlog**: none — automatic.
- **Recent changes (2024–2026)**: none enacted. Periodic political debate on "birth tourism" reform has produced no legislation as of May 2026.
- **Dual citizenship**: unrestricted (Canada formally permits multiple citizenships since the 1977 Act).

## Jus sanguinis — citizenship by descent

### First-generation-born-abroad descent (s. 3(1)(b))
- **Eligibility**: child born abroad to a parent who was either born in Canada or naturalized in Canada — automatic citizenship at birth, register with IRCC for a citizenship certificate.
- **Timeline**: at birth; certificate processing ~4–12 months.
- **Dual citizenship**: unrestricted.

### Second-and-later-generation descent under Bill C-3 (in force 15 Dec 2025)
- **Eligibility**: a Canadian parent who was themselves born or adopted abroad can now pass citizenship to children born abroad — provided the parent demonstrates **1,095 days (3 years) of cumulative physical presence in Canada before the child's birth or adoption** ("substantial connection" test). Removes the first-generation limit imposed by the 2009 Harper-era reform.
- **Timeline**: automatic at birth for children born on/after 15 Dec 2025 if parent meets the 1,095-day test; retroactive automatic grant for those born before 15 Dec 2025 who would have been citizens but for the first-generation limit or earlier-statute outdated provisions.
- **Backlog**: IRCC has flagged a near-term surge in citizenship-certificate (proof) applications under Bill C-3.
- **Recent changes**: Bill C-3 received royal assent 20 Nov 2025, in force 15 Dec 2025. Replaces the failed Bill C-71 (died on the order paper at prorogation) and resolves the Ontario Superior Court's Dec 2023 ruling that the first-generation limit was unconstitutional.
- **Dual citizenship**: unrestricted.

## Standard naturalization (Citizenship Act s. 5(1))

### Adult naturalization
- **Eligibility**: hold permanent-resident status; physically present in Canada **1,095 days (3 years) of the 5 years immediately preceding application** (PR-time counts 1:1; pre-PR temporary-resident or protected-person time counts at 0.5×, capped at 365 days); file Canadian taxes for at least 3 of those 5 years; if age 18–54, prove CLB-4 English or French and pass the 20-question knowledge test (15/20 to pass, three attempts); take the Oath of Citizenship; no serious criminality.
- **Timeline**: ~3 years PR-presence + processing.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: ~12–13 months current grant processing (IRCC's 80th-percentile service standard); ~321,000 applicants in queue per May 2026 IRCC dashboard. Down from the 2023 peak of ~38 months.
- **Recent changes (2024–2026)**: backlog cleared back inside the 12-month standard during 2025; March 2026 IRCC clarification of test rules; no statutory change to substantive requirements.
- **Dual citizenship**: unrestricted.

## Express Entry / PNP / Quebec — PR pathway shape

- **Not a separate citizenship route** — these are the PR-acquisition mechanisms (Federal Skilled Worker, Canadian Experience Class, Federal Skilled Trades, Provincial Nominee Program, PSTQ) that produce the permanent-resident status feeding standard naturalization above. Once PR is held, the 1,095-day clock runs identically regardless of which program issued the PR. See `../residency/canada.md` for the program mechanics.

## Resumption of former Canadian citizenship (s. 11)

### Resumption by application
- **Eligibility**: previously a Canadian citizen, lost or renounced status; must re-acquire PR status, then be physically present in Canada **at least 365 days during the 2 years preceding application**; file taxes for the prior year if required; no criminality bar.
- **Timeline**: 1 year PR-presence + ~12–24 months processing.
- **Recent changes**: no substantive change since the 2015 amendments. The 2005 amendment continues to relieve minors who lost citizenship through a parent's act from the PR-reacquisition step.
- **Dual citizenship**: unrestricted.

## Lost-Canadian remediation (Bill C-3, in force 15 Dec 2025)

### Automatic restoration / direct grant
- **Eligibility**: persons who lost or never obtained citizenship because of outdated statutory rules (pre-1977 dual-citizenship loss, the 2009 first-generation limit, the various "Lost Canadian" cohorts from prior amendments). Bill C-3 automatically restores citizenship to remaining Lost Canadians and their descendants born before 15 Dec 2025; for those affected only by the first-generation limit who were born before that date, the grant is automatic and the certificate is on application.
- **Timeline**: instant for the automatic cohort; certificate-of-citizenship processing 4–12 months.
- **Backlog**: IRCC anticipates a surge — millions of US-resident descendants potentially newly eligible.
- **Recent changes**: this is the central 2025 reform; supersedes the 2009 and 2015 partial remediations (which together restored ~20,000).
- **Dual citizenship**: unrestricted.

## Distinguished service / Governor-in-Council discretionary grant (s. 5(4))

### Discretionary grant by the Minister, on GiC direction
- **Eligibility**: GiC may direct the Minister, in their discretion, to grant citizenship to any person, on one of three grounds: alleviate **statelessness**; relieve **special and unusual hardship**; or **reward services of exceptional value to Canada**. All other Act requirements (PR, presence, language, knowledge) can be waived.
- **Timeline**: not published — IRCC does not publish service-standard processing times because these are non-routine, case-by-case files. Historically months to several years.
- **Backlog**: not published.
- **Recent changes**: none — provision unchanged.
- **Dual citizenship**: unrestricted.

## Adoption-based citizenship (s. 5.1 direct grant)

### Direct grant for foreign-born adopted minor
- **Eligibility**: child under 18 adopted by a Canadian citizen; adoption must be in the child's best interests, create a genuine parent-child relationship, comply with the adoption laws of both jurisdictions, not be primarily for immigration purposes, and not circumvent international-adoption rules. For adoptions on/after 15 Dec 2025 where the adoptive Canadian parent was themselves born or adopted abroad, the parent must meet the same 1,095-day-in-Canada substantial-connection test as the descent route.
- **Timeline**: ~12–24 months processing.
- **Recent changes (2024–2026)**: Bill C-3 (15 Dec 2025) imposes the 1,095-day test on second-generation adoptive parents.
- **Dual citizenship**: unrestricted.

## Stateless-persons / anti-statelessness protection

### Stateless born to a Canadian parent (s. 5(5))
- **Eligibility**: born outside Canada to a Canadian-citizen parent, stateless, under 23, with at least 3 years of physical presence in Canada in the 4 years before applying, and never convicted of certain national-security offences.
- **Timeline**: ~12–24 months.
- **Dual citizenship**: unrestricted.

### Stateless with no Canadian parent — s. 5(4) only
- **Eligibility**: no dedicated non-discretionary route; statelessness is one ground for the s. 5(4) discretionary grant above. Standard naturalization (PR + 1,095 days) remains the only non-discretionary path for stateless persons without a Canadian parent.
- **Recent changes**: 2017 amendment explicitly named statelessness as a s. 5(4) ground, but did not create an entitlement.
