
Monaco is a sovereign city-state of ~38,000 residents under Prince Albert II, **not in the EU** but inside the **Schengen area via two bilateral treaties with France**. There is **no formal Golden Visa / residency-by-investment** program in the statutory sense — but the practical entry bar (a Monégasque bank account holding €500,000–€1,000,000 plus a Monaco lease or property) functions as a de facto investment program. The principal draw is **tax residency**: Monaco levies **no personal income tax, no capital gains tax, no wealth tax, and no inheritance tax for direct heirs**. The Section des Résidents of the Sûreté Publique processes all applications. Issuance is governed by Sovereign Ordinances under the Franco-Monégasque establishment convention of 1963.

## Residence-card track (Carte de Séjour)

Every long-stay resident — French, EEA, or third-country — holds a **Carte de Séjour** ("Resident Card", historically called *Carte de Résident*). The card progresses through three statutory stages on a continuous-residence clock; each stage automatically renews into the next provided the underlying requirements (housing, means, presence) are maintained.

### Carte de Séjour Temporaire — 1-year card
Issued on first admission. Valid **1 year**, renewable annually for the first three renewals (years 1, 2, 3). Holder must continue to demonstrate housing, sufficient means, and physical presence in Monaco (typically ≥ 6 months/year). All initial-applicant requirements (below) are re-verified at each renewal in years 1–3.

### Carte de Séjour Ordinaire — 3-year card
Granted at year 3 to residents who have held a temporary card without lapse. Valid **3 years**, renewable once. Lighter renewal burden — confirms continued housing, means, and absence-of-issues. Continues counting toward citizenship.

### Carte de Séjour Privilégiée — 10-year card
Granted at **year 10** of continuous ordinary residence. Valid **10 years**, renewable indefinitely. Reserved for residents who have made Monaco their genuine center of life. This is the same 10-year clock used by Article 5 of the nationality law, though the privileged card is **not** an automatic ticket to naturalization — the Sovereign Prince retains full discretion.

## Initial-application requirements

All first-time applicants must satisfy four pillars at the Section des Résidents interview:

### 1. Accommodation in Monaco
A **lease** (≥ 12 months, registered with the Direction des Services Fiscaux) or **owned property** in Monaco. The dwelling must match household size — single applicants need a studio or 1-bed minimum; family applicants need adequate rooms. Sub-leases and hotel addresses are not accepted. Studios start at **€2,300–€3,500/month**, 1-beds at **€4,000–€6,500/month**, 2-beds at **€6,100–€16,000/month** (Carré d'Or peaks above). A 3-month security deposit and 10 % agency fee apply at lease signing.

### 2. Financial self-sufficiency
Proof of means through one of three channels: (a) a **Monégasque bank reference letter** evidencing a substantial deposit — banks set their own threshold, **typically €500,000 and increasingly €1,000,000+ in 2025–2026** for new non-business applicants; (b) a Monaco employment contract; (c) an established Monaco business in the applicant's name. The bank-deposit route is the dominant path for passive applicants.

### 3. Clean criminal record
Police clearance certificates (legalized / apostilled) from every country lived in for ≥ 6 months in the prior 5 years.

### 4. In-person interview at the Section des Résidents
Held **3–7 weeks** after file submission. Interviewer covers identity, family, education, employment history, source of funds, and intent to make Monaco the center of life. All originals are presented; the file is then sent for final review.

## Track variants by nationality

### French nationals
Free entry under the 1963 Franco-Monégasque convention. Still apply for a Carte de Séjour but no visa needed. **Crucial tax caveat:** under Article 7 of the 1963 treaty, French nationals domiciled in Monaco remain liable to French income tax **as if they lived in France**, except for two grandfathered categories (resident in Monaco since before 13 October 1957 with 5 years' presence by 13 October 1962, or born in Monaco with continuous residence). For a French passport-holder, Monaco offers no income-tax benefit — only social and lifestyle benefits.

### EU / EEA / Swiss nationals
No French Type D visa required. Apply directly to the Section des Résidents on arrival. Processing typically **6–10 weeks** from interview to card. Same financial / housing / clean-record pillars.

### Third-country nationals (including US citizens)
Must first obtain a **French long-stay visa Type D, mention "Monaco"**, from the French consulate in the country of legal residence. Only after the Type D is issued may the Carte de Séjour application proceed in Monaco. End-to-end timeline: **3–6 months from visa application to physical card** for a clean file.

## Entry routes by activity

### Passive resident (private means)
The default route for tax-driven applicants. No employment or business required; satisfied entirely by the bank-deposit + housing combination. This is the path most US applicants follow.

### Salaried employee
Monaco employer files a work-permit request; the employee then applies for the Carte de Séjour. Employer sponsorship plus housing satisfies the means test.

### Business creation / self-employment
Set up a Monégasque company (SARL, SAM, or sole proprietorship) with approval from the Direction de l'Expansion Économique. Approval is discretionary and considers nature of business, premises, and financial substance. Once approved, the Carte de Séjour follows on the basis of the business activity.

### Family reunification
Spouse and dependent children of a Carte de Séjour holder receive their own cards on the principal's file. Marriage or birth certificates (legalized) required.

### Student
Carte de Séjour for studies at the International University of Monaco or other authorized institution. Proof of enrollment plus means (typically through parents). Years on a student card do **not** count toward the 10-year naturalization clock under the standard reading.

### Digital nomad
No dedicated program. Not needed in practice — a passive-means applicant with foreign-source online income is fully accommodated under the standard track, and Monaco's zero-income-tax regime makes a remote-work visa structurally redundant.

## Tax residency

The tax draw is what makes Monaco residency a top global option:

- **No personal income tax** on residents (excluding French nationals — see above).
- **No capital gains tax** on individuals.
- **No wealth tax.**
- **No property tax** on principal residences (only a small annual rental tax / VAT on leases).
- **No inheritance / gift tax** between direct heirs (spouse and lineal descendants/ascendants). Higher rates apply to collaterals and unrelated heirs.
- **VAT** harmonized with France at 20 %.
- **Corporate income tax** at 25 % applies only to companies deriving > 25 % of turnover outside Monaco, or those engaged in industrial/commercial activity — many holding structures escape it.
- **Substance requirement** to maintain tax residency: spend > 183 days/year in Monaco *or* demonstrate Monaco as the center of vital interests. Authorities increasingly cross-check with utility records, school enrollment, and travel data.

## Path to citizenship

Carte de Séjour residence accrues toward the **10-year clock for naturalization by sovereign ordinance** — but acquisition is fully discretionary, requires renunciation of US citizenship, and yields only ~8–10 grants per year nationwide. See `citizenship/monaco.md`. Most foreign residents remain on the Carte de Séjour Privilégiée indefinitely without ever pursuing nationality.

## Processing time

End-to-end for a US-citizen passive applicant: **3–6 months** from file opening to physical card, assuming the bank account is opened and lease signed early. The Section des Résidents itself runs **2–5 months** from filing to card; the upstream tasks (French Type D visa, Monaco bank onboarding, lease execution) account for most of the calendar wall-clock.

## Recent changes (2024–2026)

- **Bank-deposit threshold inflation** — multiple Monégasque banks moved minimums from €500,000 toward **€1,000,000** for new private-banking residency relationships during 2024–2025. Threshold is bank policy, not law, and varies by applicant profile.
- **Rental market tightening** — the supply of large apartments fell again in 2025, pushing prime rents to record highs and lengthening the lease-hunt phase of an application.
- **No statutory change** to the Carte de Séjour stages or the 1963 Franco-Monégasque framework. The 2018-era enhanced source-of-funds scrutiny remains the standard.
- **MONEYVAL / AML review** — Monaco's 2022–2024 MONEYVAL evaluation produced ongoing tightening of bank onboarding, lengthening source-of-funds documentation requests.

## Practical assessment — realistic cost floor for a US citizen

- **Bank deposit (parked, not spent):** €500,000 floor, **€1,000,000 realistic** in 2026 for a clean private-banking onboarding.
- **Annual housing (renting):** ~€28,000–€42,000 minimum for a studio; ~€48,000–€78,000 for a 1-bed; family-sized at six figures. Lease + deposit + agency fees add ~4 months' rent up front.
- **Annual housing (buying):** apartments start above **€1 million** and average above **€52,000/m²**; a small purchased pied-à-terre runs **€2–5 million**.
- **Setup costs:** Type D visa fees + apostilles + legal/relocation advisor (~€10,000–€30,000) + Monaco bank account-opening costs.
- **Realistic year-1 commitment for a US single applicant:** **€1.05M deposit + €60–80k housing + ~€30k setup** = ~**€1.15M of liquid capital deployed**, with the deposit recoverable on departure.
- The benefit case requires either (a) a high enough taxable income that the US-tax exposure remains manageable via FEIE/FTC and the wealth/inheritance/capital-gains shields produce real savings, or (b) a renunciation-of-US-citizenship strategy. Under (a) Monaco only partially benefits a US citizen because the US taxes citizens on worldwide income; under (b) Monaco becomes one of the strongest jurisdictions globally.
