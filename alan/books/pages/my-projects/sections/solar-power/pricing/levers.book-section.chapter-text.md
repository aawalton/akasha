
# Levers and Knobs

Things that move the price for a specific Utah installation. Ordered roughly by magnitude of impact.

## High-Impact Levers (>10% price swing)

- **Sales channel.** Door-to-door / in-home rep adds the full customer-acquisition cost ($0.48–$0.69/W per [NREL soft-cost work](https://docs.nrel.gov/docs/fy25osti/92536.pdf)). Online marketplaces (EnergySage) drop bidders below this cost structure. Channel selection alone is **~20% of installed price**.
- **Cash vs financed.** Financed adds the 15–30% dealer fee ([CFPB Aug 2024](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_solar-financing-issue-spotlight_2024-08.pdf)). Lease / PPA worse still. Cash is cleanest.
- **Number of bids.** Three+ competitive bids reduces quoted price ~20% / $3k–$10k vs single-installer quote ([EnergySage](https://www.energysage.com/solar/why-compare-solar-quotes/)).
- **Federal ITC eligibility.** 30% off the total system cost, but the residential ITC for cash/loan systems was scheduled to **expire Dec 31, 2025** under the July 2025 budget bill ([Utah OED](https://energy.utah.gov/homepage/tax-credits/renewable-energy-systems-tax-credit/)). For a 2026 install: re-verify status before signing. If still available via TPO/lease structure only, weigh the dealer-fee tax against the credit value.
- **Battery inclusion and sizing.** Adding a 13.5 kWh Powerwall 3 adds $12k–$15k to the project — roughly **45% on top of a $34k PV system**. Skip if the goal is bill savings; include if grid resilience matters more than payback.

## Medium-Impact Levers (3–10% swing)

- **System size.** Price per watt falls with larger systems (fixed permitting, design, mobilization costs amortize). A 12 kW system typically prices $0.05–$0.15/W lower than a 6 kW system from the same installer.
- **Roof complexity.** Single south-facing plane = simplest layout. Multi-plane / east-west splits = more rails, more flashings, more design time. Steep pitch (>40°) or high roof (>2 stories) adds labor and safety overhead. Each obstruction (vent, chimney, dormer) eats usable area and adds layout cost.
- **Roof material.** Composition shingle is the cheap baseline. **Tile roofs add 15–25%** (specialized flashing, slower install, breakage risk). Standing-seam metal is moderate (clamp mounts, no penetration). Spanish tile and slate are most expensive.
- **Roof age.** If the roof has <10 years of remaining life, most installers will require replacement before install or refuse the job — adds $10k–$30k roof replacement cost. Even a "do it later" install means paying to remove/reinstall panels in 5–10 years.
- **Service panel upgrade.** 100A → 200A panel swap: $2,000–$5,000. Verify panel rating first; rule out before getting bids.
- **Microinverter vs string.** Microinverters add ~$0.15–0.30/W ([Boston Solar](https://www.bostonsolar.us/solar-blog-resource-center/blog/string-inverters-vs-microinverters-which-solar-inverter-is-better-for-new-englan/)). Worth it for shaded / multi-plane roofs; overkill for clean single-plane.
- **Panel choice — tier 1 vs tier 2 / efficiency class.** BNEF Tier 1 modules cost 10–30% more than tier 2 ([PVclick](https://pvclick.com/en/tier-1-solar-panels-complete-guide-for-distributors-and-installers/)). Higher-efficiency panels (>22%) cost more per watt but fit more capacity on the same roof; only matters when roof area is the constraint.

## Lower-Impact Levers (1–3% swing)

- **Local permitting / interconnection cost.** Varies by jurisdiction; Utah/Provo is moderate. Rocky Mountain Power's net billing tariff (Schedule 137) is the relevant interconnection path for most Utah Valley customers ([EnergySage Utah incentives](https://www.energysage.com/local-data/solar-rebates-incentives/ut/)).
- **State incentives.** Utah RESTC residential state credit **ended for systems installed 2024+** ([Utah OED](https://energy.utah.gov/homepage/tax-credits/renewable-energy-systems-tax-credit/)). Property-tax exemption for the solar value-add is still in effect. No meaningful state lever remains.
- **Time of year / installer backlog.** Winter / shoulder-season pricing typically beats summer peak by 3–5% as installers fight for queue. December–February is the value window.
- **Tariffs and trade policy.** US module prices moved 15%+ from April–August 2024 on tariff news ([pv-magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2024/06/10/solar-panel-import-tariffs-increase-us-module-prices-by-up-to-286/)). Modules are a small fraction of installed cost (~10%), so 15% tariff moves translate to ~1.5% on the installed price — material but not dominant. Watch for AD/CVD final determinations before signing a price lock.

## Knobs You Cannot Move

- **Labor rates.** US residential install labor is ~$0.49/W median vs $0.18/W Germany ([RMI](https://rmi.org/blog_2013_12_05_can_usa_solar_cost_compete_with_germany/)). This is structural — union exposure varies, electrician shortage, lack of standardized racking. You're stuck with the US labor curve.
- **Permitting overhead.** US permitting / inspection / interconnection adds $0.30–$0.50/W of pure soft cost vs Germany's $0.05 ([NREL via search](https://docs.nrel.gov/docs/fy25osti/92536.pdf)). Provo / Utah County is mid-pack — neither the best (SolarAPP+ jurisdictions clear in days) nor the worst.

## Action Priority for a Provo Quote

1. Verify the existing service panel is 200A and not at capacity — kills the largest hidden-cost surprise.
2. Inspect roof: <10 yr remaining → replace first or postpone solar.
3. Decide cash vs financed before requesting quotes; insist on the cash price line.
4. Get 3 quotes minimum via EnergySage; one or two local installers off-marketplace for comparison.
5. Decline battery on first install unless grid resilience is a stated goal.
6. Default to string inverter unless the roof has shading or multi-plane complexity.
7. Confirm 30% federal ITC status before signing — credit eligibility is the single biggest swing remaining in 2026.
