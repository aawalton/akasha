
# France

France offers many paths — standard residency-based naturalization by decree, declaration-based routes (marriage, siblings, possession d'état, Article 21-14 descendants), the famous Foreign Legion route, automatic acquisition at 18 for children born and raised in France, and "double jus soli" for those with a parent born in France. Dual citizenship is fully permitted (France has no general prohibition; loss requires explicit renunciation). The Loi du 26 janvier 2024 (Law 2024-42) and its implementing decree no. 2025-648 of 15 July 2025 raised the language bar from B1 to B2 for all naturalization applications filed from 1 January 2026, made the new digital civic exam (40 MCQ, 80% pass) mandatory, and a May 2025 Retailleau circular (in force 1 January 2026) tightened "stable resources" to 5 years of French-sourced earnings. Statutory source: [Code civil, articles 17 à 33-2 (Légifrance)](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/section_lc/LEGITEXT000006070721/LEGISCTA000006117764/).

## Birth on territory — automatic at 18

- **Category**: jus soli (Art. 21-7 Code civil).
- **Eligibility**: born in France to foreign parents; habitually resident in France with at least 5 years' residence (continuous or not) since age 11. Acquisition is automatic at majority unless declined between 17½ and 19. ([Service Public — Article 21-7](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F295))
- **Earlier acquisition by declaration**: from age 13 (with parents' consent, 5 years' residence since age 8); from age 16 (own declaration, 5 years since age 11).
- **Timeline**: at 18 automatically; declaration route 13–18.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.
- **Recent changes**: a 2025 Mayotte-specific tightening (loi du 9 avril 2025, upheld by Conseil constitutionnel n°2025-881 DC of 7 May 2025) requires one parent's regular residence at the child's birth — applies only to Mayotte; mainland rule unchanged. ([Verfassungsblog — Dismantling Jus Soli](https://verfassungsblog.de/dismantling-jus-soli/))

## Double jus soli — French at birth

- **Category**: jus soli (Art. 19-3 Code civil).
- **Eligibility**: born in France to at least one parent also born in France (including pre-independence French territories under certain rules). Citizenship from birth, no residency requirement. ([Wikipedia — French nationality law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_nationality_law))
- **Timeline**: at birth.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.

## Descent (jus sanguinis) — by filiation

- **Category**: descent (Art. 18 Code civil).
- **Eligibility**: at least one parent French at the time of the child's birth. No generational limit on its face — but Art. 30-3 imposes a **50-year possession d'état rule**: if neither the applicant nor the French-line parent has had any French ties (passport renewal, consular registration, voting) for 50 years, the right is lost.
- **Timeline**: at birth; certificate of nationality (CNF) via consulate or Service central d'état civil in Nantes — currently 12–24 months.
- **Backlog**: Nantes CNF processing has been 18–24 months through 2024–2026.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.

## Possession d'état de Français (Art. 21-13)

- **Category**: declaration — for those who lived as French without formally being so.
- **Eligibility**: continuously treated as French by authorities (French ID, passport issued in error, registered as French) for 10+ years. Declaration filed at TJ (tribunal judiciaire). ([Légifrance — Code civil Art. 21-13](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000006419930/))
- **Timeline**: ~12–24 months after filing.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.

## Descendants of French emigrants (Art. 21-14)

- **Category**: declaration for first- and second-generation descendants.
- **Eligibility**: descended (parent or grandparent) from a French national; demonstrate manifest cultural, professional, economic, or family ties to France. No residency required. Filed at the French consulate of place of residence. ([Légifrance — Art. 21-14](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000006419939))
- **Timeline**: 18–36 months, consulate-dependent.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.

## Naturalization by decree — standard residency

- **Category**: discretionary naturalization (Art. 21-15 to 21-25-1).
- **Eligibility** (from 1 Jan 2026):
  - **Residency**: 5 years' habitual residence with valid title, "centre of material interests" in France.
  - **Language**: **B2 oral and written** (raised from B1) via TCF IRN, TEF IRN, or recognized diploma. ([Décret 2025-648 du 15 juillet 2025](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/id/JORFTEXT000051900519))
  - **Civic exam**: mandatory digital MCQ, 40 questions, 80% pass — replaces the old assimilation interview. ([Ministère de l'Intérieur — examen civique](https://www.interieur.gouv.fr/actualites/communiques-de-presse/a-partir-du-1er-janvier-2026-reussite-a-lexamen-civique-sera-necessaire-pour-obtenir-carte-de-sejour))
  - **Stable, sufficient resources**: 5 years' French-sourced earnings, CDI valid 12+ months OR 24 months of chained CDDs; benchmarked to post-tax SMIC (~€1,443/month). Foreign income, dividends, pensions, social benefits excluded. ([Circulaire Retailleau, mai 2025](https://www.vie-publique.fr/en-bref/298439-circulaire-retailleau-sur-la-naturalisation-des-etrangers))
  - **Fiscal compliance**: 3 years of French tax returns clean.
  - **Criminal record**: no conviction ≥6 months unsuspended (French or foreign). No terrorism, drug, or human-trafficking convictions.
  - **Integration**: signed commitment to Republican principles (laïcité, equality, no polygamy, etc.) — new under 2024 law.
  - **Good morals**: discretionary; Retailleau circular instructs prefectures to refuse on weak labor-market attachment.
- **Timeline**: total ~5 + 2 = **~7 years minimum** from arrival; legal max 18 months from receipt (12 if 10+ years' residence).
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: 18–36 months prefecture+Ministry, with extremes to 5 years. Refusal rates rising under Retailleau guidance.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.

## Naturalization by decree — reduced 2-year residency

- **Category**: same as above with reduced "stage" (Art. 21-18).
- **Eligibility**: 2 years' residence if the applicant has **successfully completed 2+ years of higher education in a French institution and holds a French diploma**. Other doors: rendered "exceptional services" to France, or accomplishment requiring exceptional integration effort, or status of "francophone" contributor under a French-cultural-area provision.
- **Total timeline**: ~2 years residence + 18–24 months processing = **~4 years**.
- **Backlog**: same Ministry queue; file quality matters more than path.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.

## Naturalization by decree — no residency required (services exceptionnels)

- **Category**: residency waived (Art. 21-19).
- **Eligibility**: exceptional service to France (Olympic medal, major scientific contribution, defense-industrial value); discretionary. No B2 / civic exam waivers — language and civic still required unless specifically dispensed.
- **Timeline**: months once dossier accepted; rare.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.

## Declaration by marriage (Art. 21-2)

- **Category**: declaration, not discretionary.
- **Eligibility**: married to a French citizen for **4 years** (5 if the couple did not reside continuously in France); 3 years if a French common child exists or the spouse served in French public/military service abroad. Spouse must have been French at marriage and have retained nationality. Demonstrated community of life. **B2 French (oral and written) from 1 Jan 2026**. Civic exam *not* statutorily required for this declaration route (decree omits it). Clean criminal record required. ([Service Public — F2726](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F2726))
- **Timeline**: 4 years marriage + ~12–24 months processing. Government may oppose for 2 years post-declaration.
- **Backlog**: 12–24 months at the prefecture / Ministry.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.

## Foreign Legion — "français par le sang versé"

- **Category**: service-based (Art. 21-14-1 Code civil; Code du service national).
- **Eligibility (standard)**: 3 years' honorable service in the French Foreign Legion under real identity; clean record; "honour and fidelity"; commanding officer recommendation. Naturalization by decree but with the residency stage waived.
- **Eligibility (wounded)**: any legionnaire wounded in operations may claim French nationality immediately by declaration — the "français par le sang versé" provision. No language, civic, or resource threshold; effective at the act of declaration before TJ.
- **Timeline**: 3 years (standard) or instant (wounded).
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.
- **Notable**: uniquely fast service-based path in the EU; one of the few non-discretionary fast-tracks.

## Siblings of French citizens raised in France (Art. 21-13-2)

- **Category**: declaration.
- **Eligibility**: sibling acquired French nationality under Art. 21-7 or 21-11 (jus soli); applicant entered France before age 6, habitually resided since, completed mandatory schooling in French state-controlled establishments. Declaration filed at majority. ([Légibase — Art. 21-13-2](https://etat-civil.collectivites.legibase.fr/actualites/veille-juridique/la-procedure-de-naturalisation-pour-les-etrangers-dont-un-frere-ou-une))
- **Timeline**: ~12–18 months processing post-18.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.

## Adopted children

- **Plenary adoption (Art. 20)**: child of a French adoptive parent is **French from birth** — no procedure, no residency. ([Service Public — F3070](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F3070))
- **Simple adoption (Art. 21-12)**: child may declare French nationality before majority if resident in France (residence requirement waived if French adoptive parent is not habitually resident in France).
- **Other Art. 21-12 routes**: minors in care of French national / DASE / French institution for 5+ years.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.

## Refugees — accelerated naturalization

- **Category**: naturalization by decree with residency stage waived (Art. 21-19, 6°).
- **Eligibility**: OFPRA-recognized refugee. **No 5-year residency requirement** — can file immediately upon grant of status. B2 language and civic exam still apply from 1 Jan 2026 (no exemption from these gates, though refugees are exempt from the civic exam for residence permits). Republican-principles commitment required.
- **Timeline**: 18–36 months from filing.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.

## Stateless persons

- **Category**: standard naturalization — **no accelerated route**.
- **Eligibility**: same 5-year residency, B2, civic exam, resources, and morals requirements as a third-country national. France's 2024 reform left this gap unchanged. ([Statelessness Index — France](https://index.statelessness.eu/country/france))
- **Timeline**: ~7 years end-to-end.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.

## Reintegration (réintégration) — recovering lost French nationality

- **By declaration (Art. 24-2)**: for those who lost French nationality through marriage to a foreigner or voluntary acquisition of another nationality. No residency required; need clear ties to France. Declaration before TJ or consulate.
- **By decree (Art. 24-1)**: any age, no minimum residency duration — but applicant must be resident in France (centre of material interests + family) at the date of the decree. Must establish prior possession of French nationality. ([Service Public — F2214](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F2214))
- **Timeline**: 12–18 months by declaration; up to 18 months by decree.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.

## Investment / CBI

- **No formal CBI program**. France has no fast-track passport-for-investment route. For France's "Talent" residency passport and other residency programs, see [../residency/france.md](../residency/france.book-chapter.md).

## Recent / pending changes — May 2026 state

- **Loi du 26 janvier 2024 (Law 2024-42)**: enacted; raised assimilation bar, mandated Republican-principles commitment, set the legislative basis for B2 and civic exam.
- **Décret n° 2025-648 du 15 juillet 2025**: enacted; **B2 mandatory and civic exam mandatory from 1 January 2026** for all naturalization-by-decree applications. Files complete and receivable before 31 Dec 2025 grandfather under the old B1/interview rules. ([Arrêté du 10 octobre 2025 — examen civique](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/id/JORFTEXT000052381620))
- **Circulaire Retailleau, mai 2025** (in force 1 Jan 2026): five-year French-earnings rule, foreign-income exclusion, stricter fiscal scrutiny.
- **Loi du 9 avril 2025 (Mayotte)**: tightened jus soli in Mayotte only; upheld by Conseil constitutionnel n°2025-881 DC.
- **Proposition Marleix (Sept 2025)**: tightening proposal floated; **not enacted** as of May 2026 — remains in Assembly procedure with no published consolidated text.

## Comparison summary

Ranked by realistic speed (fastest → slowest) given typical applicants:

1. **Plenary adoption by French parent** — instant (French from birth).
2. **Double jus soli (born in France to France-born parent)** — instant.
3. **Foreign Legion (wounded, "sang versé")** — instant declaration.
4. **Descent (Art. 18) for those with active French ties** — at birth; CNF 12–24 months.
5. **Foreign Legion (3 years honorable service)** — ~3–4 years total.
6. **Marriage declaration** — 4 years marriage + 12–24 months → ~5–6 years.
7. **Reintegration by declaration** — 12–18 months.
8. **University-graduate decree (2-year stage)** — ~3.5–4 years.
9. **Art. 21-7 automatic at 18** — child must already meet 5y-since-11 residence.
10. **Refugee accelerated decree** — 18–36 months from refugee grant.
11. **Standard naturalization by decree** — **~7 years minimum** (5y residence + 18–24m processing), assuming B2 + civic exam + stable French earnings.
12. **Possession d'état / Art. 21-14** — viable but slow, 12–36 months.
13. **Stateless persons** — no acceleration; standard ~7 years.
14. **CBI / investment** — **does not exist**.

For Alan: realistically the fastest non-service path absent French ancestry is **marriage** (4 years) or **French masters + 2-year stage** (~4 years). All naturalization paths from 1 Jan 2026 require **B2 French** and the **digital civic exam (80% MCQ)**; resource thresholds now demand French-sourced income. Dual citizenship is unrestricted.
