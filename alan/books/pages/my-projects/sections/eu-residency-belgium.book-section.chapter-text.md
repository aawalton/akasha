
Belgium has no formal "Golden Visa" program in the Mediterranean sense — no fixed €/property tier, no fast-track passive-investor permit. Long-stay residence for non-EU nationals is granted via a **D-visa** routed to one of a handful of statutory grounds. The federal Immigration Office (DVZ/OE) decides residence; the three Regions (Flanders, Brussels-Capital, Wallonia) decide the **Professional Card** for self-employment. Time on any qualifying long-stay permit feeds the **EU long-term resident** clock (5 years) and the **CBN Art. 12bis** citizenship clock (5 or 10 years). The De Wever federal government installed Jan 2025 has signaled stricter migration policy; no statutory tightening of the routes below had taken effect as of May 2026.

## Self-employment / freelancer

### Professional Card + D-visa (self-employed)

- **Category**: Self-employed residence permit for non-EU nationals carrying out a self-employed activity in Belgium. The Professional Card is the regional work authorization; the D-visa is the federal residence authorization. Both are required and must be applied for jointly at the Belgian consulate.
- **Eligibility / threshold**:
  - Business plan demonstrating economic viability and benefit to the host Region (job creation, innovation, or contribution to the regional economy).
  - **Flanders**: minimum start-up capital **€22,838** (indexed) held in applicant's own account; serious financial plan.
  - **Brussels-Capital** and **Wallonia**: no published capital floor, but in practice ~**€18,600–€30,000** seed capital plus a credible business plan is expected.
  - Income to support self + family: **≥120% of social-integration income** (≈ **€2,131/month net for a family**, 2025 figure).
  - Affiliation with a Belgian social-insurance fund for the self-employed; registration in the Crossroads Bank for Enterprises (KBO/BCE).
  - Clean criminal record (extract <6 months old); medical certificate; health insurance.
- **Validity / renewal**: Initial Professional Card issued on a **probationary basis for 2 years**; renewals up to a max of **5 years per card**, indefinitely renewable while the activity continues. Residence permit (electronic A-card) initially matches the Professional Card term.
- **Physical presence**: No statutory days-per-year minimum, but the residence permit can be revoked if the holder establishes main residence outside Belgium (>6 months absence in a 12-month window is the practical risk threshold).
- **Citizenship clock**: **Full-rate** — counts at 100% toward both the EU long-term-resident 5-year clock and the CBN Art. 12bis 5-year (or 10-year) declaration clock, provided continuous legal main residence is maintained.
- **Recent reforms (2024–2026)**: Regional fees and contribution fees re-indexed annually; no structural reform of the Professional Card regime. **CBN Art. 12bis registration fee raised from €150 → €1,000 on 29 July 2025**, indexed from 1 Jan 2026 — affects the *citizenship* step, not the Professional Card itself.
- **Processing time**: **3–6 months** for the Professional Card (regional decision) running in parallel with the D-visa decision (typically 4–6 months). Total elapsed time from filing to electronic A-card: **~6–9 months**.

## Investor / business-investment route

### Business-investment D-visa (no statutory "Golden Visa")

- **Category**: Functionally an investor permit, but legally just a self-employment / company-director variant of the Professional Card. Belgium has **no** real-estate-backed or fund-subscription "Golden Visa". The phrase "Belgian Golden Visa" used by relocation marketers refers to this pathway.
- **Eligibility / threshold**: Incorporate a Belgian company (BV/SRL or SA/NV — minimum-capital floor abolished, but practical injection of **€200,000–€350,000** working capital is expected) with substance (registered office, employees, real activity). Typical envelope cited by practitioners is **€350,000–€500,000** across the first 5 years, including capital injection, operating costs, and follow-on investment. **No one-time lock**, no fund subscription, no real-estate route — the capital must finance an operating business.
- **Validity / renewal**: 1-year residence permit, renewable annually while the company is solvent; permanent residency available after 3–5 years of continuous residence.
- **Physical presence**: Same as Professional Card — no statutory days/year minimum, but main residence must be in Belgium.
- **Citizenship clock**: **Full-rate** under CBN Art. 12bis (5- or 10-year declaration), feeding into both the 5-year EU long-term-resident clock and the citizenship clock. Belgium has **no direct citizenship-by-investment** route.
- **Recent reforms (2024–2026)**: De Wever government (Jan 2025) flagged migration tightening; **capital-gains tax (10%) and exit-tax measures scheduled to enter force in 2026** affect investor tax planning but not visa eligibility.
- **Processing time**: 6–9 months from filing to A-card.

## Retirement / passive-income (financially independent)

### D-visa "private means" / financially independent person

- **Category**: Long-stay D-visa granted on the discretionary ground of **"stable, regular, and adequate means of subsistence"** (Art. 9 / Art. 13 of the 15 Dec 1980 Aliens Act). Belgium has **no codified "retirement visa" / "FIP visa"** statute — the Immigration Office grants this case-by-case. Reliable but discretionary.
- **Eligibility / threshold**:
  - Passive / non-Belgian income — pension, annuity, dividends, rental income — sufficient to cover the applicant without working in Belgium. The visa **prohibits employment** in Belgium.
  - Statutory benchmark: **≥110% of GAMMI** (Guaranteed Average Monthly Minimum Income) = **€2,408.79 net/month** (indexed 1 Apr 2026); renewal benchmark **≥ living wage** (€1,288.46/month, indexed 1 May 2024). Consulates in practice expect **€1,500–€2,000/month** baseline, scaling with family size.
  - Belgium-wide health insurance (Schengen-covering during initial period; mutuelle/ziekenfonds registration after arrival).
  - Proof of accommodation in Belgium (lease or property).
  - Clean criminal record + medical certificate.
- **Validity / renewal**: Initial permit typically **1 year**, renewable annually for 4 more years; permanent residency after **5 years**.
- **Physical presence**: Must maintain Belgium as main residence (no >6 month single absence; aggregate >10 month absence over 5 years risks loss of LTR eligibility).
- **Citizenship clock**: **Full-rate** for the EU long-term-resident 5-year clock and the CBN Art. 12bis **10-year track** (slow-track declaration with looser proofs — suits retirees who can't meet the 5-year track's 468-day work-history requirement). The 5-year track is technically open but the 468-day work-history requirement is incompatible with the no-work condition of this visa.
- **Recent reforms (2024–2026)**: Income thresholds reindexed (110% GAMMI raised to €2,408.79 on 1 Apr 2026). De Wever government has not codified the program; remains discretionary.
- **Processing time**: 4–6 months for D-visa; A-card issued within 8 days of municipal registration after arrival.

## Family reunification

### Article 10/10bis Aliens Act — family reunification

- **Category**: Residence via spouse, registered partner, minor child, or (limited cases) parent of an unaccompanied minor refugee. Sponsor must be Belgian, EU long-term resident, or hold a Belgian residence permit valid for >3 months.
- **Eligibility / threshold**: Sponsor must show **stable, regular, sufficient income ≥120% of GAMMI** (~€2,131/month for a family); health insurance; adequate housing meeting regional standards.
- **Validity / renewal**: A-card initially 1 year, renewable annually; permanent residency (B/D card) after 5 years.
- **Physical presence**: Main residence in Belgium; relationship must remain genuine and cohabitating.
- **Citizenship clock**: **Full-rate** toward both the EU LTR 5-year clock and CBN Art. 12bis (5-year track via marriage / cohabitation declaration, or 10-year general track). **Marriage declaration** route requires 5 years residence + 3 years marriage to a Belgian.
- **Recent reforms (2024–2026)**: From 2026, family-reunification entrants sign an **integration contract**; first renewal requires **A1** language proof (A2 for permanent residency / citizenship). D-visa filing fee €180; Immigration Office contribution fee indexed annually.
- **Processing time**: **6 months** for Belgian-citizen sponsor; up to **9 months** (extendable) for third-country sponsor.

## Long-term study / research

### Researcher single permit (Directive (EU) 2016/801)

- **Category**: Combined work + residence ("single permit") for non-EU researchers with a hosting agreement from a recognized Belgian research organization. PhD candidates with formal employment contracts qualify as researchers (not students).
- **Eligibility / threshold**: Hosting agreement with an accredited institution; sufficient resources; health insurance; doctoral or equivalent qualification.
- **Validity / renewal**: Matches research project duration (typically 1–4 years), renewable. Spouse / dependents may join under the directive.
- **Physical presence**: Main residence in Belgium during the research period.
- **Citizenship clock**: **Full-rate** as employed status — the researcher single permit counts at **100%** toward both the EU LTR 5-year clock and the CBN Art. 12bis 5-year track (468-day work history satisfied by the research contract itself). In **Flanders**, completed PhD years count as **2 years of work experience** toward permanent residency.
- **Recent reforms (2024–2026)**: D-visa filing fee €180 (Feb 2026); no structural reform.
- **Processing time**: **~90 days** post-application under the single-permit directive.

### Student residence permit (PhD without employment contract)

- **Category**: Long-stay residence as a student. Distinct from the researcher route — covers PhD candidates funded by scholarship rather than employment.
- **Eligibility / threshold**: Admission to a recognized higher-education institution; **~€776/month** proof of subsistence (indexed); health insurance.
- **Validity / renewal**: Renewable annually for the duration of studies.
- **Physical presence**: Active enrollment required.
- **Citizenship clock**: **Half-rate** — student years count at **50%** toward the EU long-term-resident 5-year clock (so 4 years of PhD on a student permit = 2 years of LTR credit, requiring 3 more years on a non-student permit to reach 5). For **CBN Art. 12bis citizenship**, the 5-year declaration track counts student years at full rate only if the applicant also meets the 468-day work-history requirement during the 5-year window.
- **Recent reforms (2024–2026)**: Subsistence threshold reindexed; no structural change.
- **Processing time**: 4–6 weeks once academic admission is confirmed.

## Excluded programs

- **EU Blue Card / single-permit (employee)**: employer-sponsored worker track — excluded per filter.
- **Posted-worker / intra-corporate transferee**: employer-sponsored — excluded.
- **Naturalization by Parliament (Art. 19 CBN, "exceptional merit")**: endorsement-based discretionary route requiring world-class scientific, sporting, or cultural distinction — excluded.
- **Digital nomad visa**: Belgium **does not offer one**. Remote work on a Schengen tourist visa is prohibited. Closest substitute is the Professional Card (already included above).
- **Real-estate / property-purchase Golden Visa**: **does not exist** in Belgium. No fixed-tier passive-investor program in the €250k–€500k Mediterranean mold; the "Belgian Golden Visa" marketed by some advisors is the business-investment Professional Card route (already covered).
