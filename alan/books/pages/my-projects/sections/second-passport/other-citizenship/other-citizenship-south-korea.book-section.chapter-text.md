
## Jus sanguinis — citizenship by descent (Nationality Act art. 2)

### Born to a Korean parent
- **Eligibility**: a child is Korean at birth if either parent (mother or father) holds Korean nationality at the time of birth. Pure jus sanguinis; place of birth is irrelevant. The 1997 amendment switched from patrilineal to bilineal descent.
- **Timeline**: at birth; report to a Korean diplomatic mission within 30 days for family-register registration.
- **Dual citizenship**: permitted at birth — child may retain both nationalities by filing a "foreign nationality non-exercise pledge" (외국국적불행사 서약) before age 22 (males with military-service obligation: by age 18 or after completing/exempt from service). See the dual-citizenship section below for the regime.

### Anti-statelessness jus soli (art. 2(1)(2)–(3))
- **Eligibility**: a child born in Korea is Korean if (a) both parents are unknown or stateless, or (b) the child is found abandoned on Korean territory and parents cannot be identified. There is no general jus soli.
- **Recent changes (2024–2026)**: Korea pledged under the Global Compact on Migration to "introduce a birth registration system for foreign children" by 31 Dec 2026 — a registration mechanism, not a citizenship extension.

## General naturalization — 일반귀화 (art. 5)

### Standard 5-year residence route
- **Eligibility**: continuous lawful residence in Korea for **5 years or more**; hold F-2 / F-5 permanent or long-term residence at time of application; age 19+; good conduct (no serious criminal record); financial means — annual income at least the prior year's GNI per capita, or assets ≥ KRW 60 million, or stable employment; demonstrate basic Korean language and civics knowledge via either the naturalization interview or completion of KIIP (Korea Immigration & Integration Program) Stage 5 — TOPIK level 4 is the de facto language benchmark.
- **Timeline**: application review **12–18 months** typical; total clock from arrival ≈ 6–7 years (5 yrs residence + interview/review + 1-yr renunciation).
- **Recent changes (2024–2026)**: 2023 Ministry of Justice proposal to ease pathways for children of long-term residents continues to advance; no statutory cut to the 5-year base for adult general applicants.
- **Dual citizenship**: not permitted under the standard general route — applicant must renounce foreign nationality within **1 year** of acquiring Korean nationality. See dual-citizenship section for the limited carve-outs.

## Simplified naturalization — 간이귀화 (art. 6)

### 3-year route — Korean parentage / birth in Korea / former Korean national
- **Eligibility**: continuous residence **3 years**; plus one of (a) a parent who was/is Korean, (b) born in Korea to a parent who was also born in Korea, or (c) was previously a Korean national. Age 19+; KRW 30 million assets or current/imminent employment; same language/civics standard as general.
- **Timeline**: 12–18 months review.

### Spouse routes (art. 6(2))
- **Eligibility**: married to a Korean national and either (a) **2 years** of residence after a 3-year marriage, or (b) **1 year** of residence after a 1-year marriage. Same conduct/means/language standards.
- **Dual citizenship**: marriage migrants are one of the carve-out categories — may take the foreign-nationality non-exercise pledge instead of renouncing.

## Special naturalization — 특별귀화 (art. 7)

### Distinguished service to Korea
- **Eligibility**: foreigners who have rendered special meritorious service to the Republic of Korea. Residence period waived; financial-means and age tests waived; language test discretionary.
- **Dual citizenship**: permitted (non-exercise pledge alternative).

### "Outstanding talent" route — OASIS-9 / Top-Tier nexus
- **Eligibility**: exceptional ability in science, economics, culture, sports, or other fields and likely to contribute to Korea's national interest. Process anchored in the OASIS-9 program for outstanding-talent recommendations from sponsoring ministries. No residence minimum.
- **Recent changes (2024–2026)**: April 2025 launch of the "Top-Tier Visa" (F-2 senior-engineer route — advanced degree from a top-100 university, 8+ years' experience, ≥ KRW 140M annual income in semiconductors/displays/batteries/biotech/robotics/defense) creates an explicit feeder to F-5 in 3 years and, by extension, to special naturalization for the very top of the cohort. K-STAR STEM track (≈400 invitees/year) added 2025.
- **Dual citizenship**: permitted.

### Adoption / minor child of a Korean parent
- **Eligibility**: minor adopted by a Korean national (after adoption recognized in Korea); minor whose Korean parent acquired/recovered Korean nationality. Residence and adulthood tests waived.

## Recovery / restoration of nationality — 국적회복 (art. 9)

### Former Korean nationals
- **Eligibility**: a person who previously held Korean nationality but lost or renounced it (e.g., by acquiring US citizenship) may apply to reacquire Korean nationality. No fixed residence minimum; conduct review applies; not available to those who lost nationality to evade military service.
- **Timeline**: ≈ 6 months from filing to decision; applicant must remain in Korea pending decision.
- **Dual citizenship**: in principle, applicant must renounce foreign nationality within 1 year — **except**:
  - Those aged **65 or older** at the time of recovery who enter Korea with intent to reside permanently. Practical sequencing: obtain F-4 (overseas-Korean) visa, take up residence, then file nationality recovery and the non-exercise pledge.
  - "Outstanding talent" recoveries.
  - Those whose foreign nationality cannot be renounced for reasons beyond their control.

## Dual citizenship — the 2010 framework and 2024–2026 status

### The 2010 reform (in force 1 Jan 2011)
- The pre-2010 Nationality Act required single nationality; the 2010 amendment created a **"foreign nationality non-exercise pledge"** (외국국적불행사 서약) regime: rather than renouncing the foreign passport, a person in an eligible category pledges not to exercise foreign-national rights inside Korea. With the pledge filed, Korea tolerates the second nationality.
- **Eligible categories**:
  1. Dual-national at birth (Korean + foreign by jus sanguinis or US-style jus soli) — must elect by age 22 (military-obligated males earlier).
  2. Marriage migrants naturalizing under art. 6(2).
  3. Special-naturalization grantees (distinguished service / outstanding talent).
  4. Adopted minors of Korean nationals.
  5. Former Koreans recovering nationality at age 65+ with permanent-residence intent.
- **Not eligible**: standard adult general naturalization (art. 5) and standard simplified naturalization without a qualifying tie — these still require renunciation of foreign nationality within 1 year.

### 2024–2026 status — no major liberalizing amendment enacted
- Despite continued public and ministerial discussion of broader dual-citizenship reform (driven by the demographic crunch and an aging diaspora), **no statutory broadening of the 2010 categories has passed** as of May 2026. The structure remains the 2010/2011 framework as refined by 2011–2016 amendments.
- **Judicial tightening**: Dec 2024 / Feb 2025 Seoul Administrative Court decisions reject dual-citizenship retention applications tied to US "birth tourism," signaling that courts will police the existing eligibility categories rather than expand them.
- **Administrative liberalization**: 2025 Ministry of Justice agenda items — area-specific regional visas, K-CORE/E-7-M shortened F-2 conversion for population-decline regions, Top-Tier and K-STAR talent tracks — all enlarge the upstream residency funnel but route candidates into the existing naturalization categories rather than create new dual-citizenship paths.

### Renunciation requirement, in detail
- **Default rule**: a foreigner who acquires Korean nationality must, within 1 year, either (a) renounce foreign nationality (file evidence with the Ministry of Justice) or (b) file the foreign-nationality non-exercise pledge if in an eligible category.
- **Pledge breach**: the Minister of Justice may order a pledge-holder who materially violates the pledge to select a single nationality within 6 months.
- **Loss for default**: failure to renounce or pledge within 1 year results in loss of Korean nationality.
