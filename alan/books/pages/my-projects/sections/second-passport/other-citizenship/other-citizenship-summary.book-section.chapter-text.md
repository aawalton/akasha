> Non-EU citizenship paths for a US-citizen adult applicant (married, three kids; family relocates together as accompanying dependents) with no foreign ancestry, no second non-US citizenship, after stripping closed, ancestry, renunciation, marriage-to-citizen, family-reunification-to-existing-citizen/PR, distinguished-service, functionally-inapplicable, high-capital (>€500k or CHF 400k/yr+), program-level-discretionary (invitation-gated points systems), narrow-discretionary-self-employment, individually-discretionary-where-no-rule-based-alternative-exists, and no-residency-path routes (May 2026). 15 non-EU countries surveyed; 2 retain at least one viable path AND a viable residency on-ramp under the affordability filter. Detail per-country files live alongside (e.g., uruguay.md, mexico.md).


Paths below require relocation and physical residence; none permit a passive clock. All retain dual US citizenship. Scope: stable, mostly Anglophone or otherwise high-quality jurisdictions outside the EU. For the residency on-ramp by country, see [../residency/summary.md](../residency/summary.book-chapter.md).

## Uruguay

- **Family residence (3y)** — 3y residence + Spanish at hearing (informal, not a test) + means of livelihood + good conduct; for applicants with constituted family (married couple, concubinato ≥5y, or dependent child residing in Uruguay). **A foreign married couple both holding Uruguay residence qualifies** — no Uruguayan-citizen spouse required. ~3.5–4y to Carta de Ciudadanía. **For the user's situation (married, 3 kids relocating together) this is the standout non-EU path.**
- **Solo residence (5y)** — 5y residence + same conditions; ~5.5–6y to Carta — fallback if family residence doesn't qualify.
- Carta clock runs from **filing date of the residencia permanente application**, not card-issuance date. Voting rights activate 3y after Carta; passport application is immediate. Note: Uruguay distinguishes **natural** (jus soli / jus sanguinis) from **legal** (naturalized) citizenship — legal citizens cannot become president/VP, lose Uruguayan status if they naturalize elsewhere, and the passport's birthplace anomaly (Apr→Jul 2025 reform reversed) means it lists country-of-birth in *Nacionalidad* again. Dual permitted in practice for legal citizens until they themselves naturalize in a third country.
- Residency on-ramp: **Residencia Permanente** — direct PR (no temp stage), income-based (~USD 1,500/mo single, ~USD 2,500/mo couple).

## Mexico

- **Standard residence (5y)** — 5y legal residence (Temporary Resident time + Permanent Resident time both count) + Spanish at SRE interview (informal image-card description, no DELE) + 10-question Mexican history/culture exam (8/10 pass mark, redesigned in 2018 to be substantially harder) + good conduct + **18-of-24-months physical presence** in the rolling window before filing. ~6.0–6.5y to Carta de Naturalización via the standard 4y Temporary Resident → 1y Permanent Resident ladder + 12–18mo for DGAJ to issue the Carta.
- No applicable reduced track for this family at arrival: the 2y track requires a Mexican spouse, a Mexican-born child, or Latin American / Iberian nationality (Art. 20.II). If a child is later born in Mexico (jus soli applies), both parents reset to the 2y track from the child's birth date — incidental accelerator, not a planning route.
- Dual citizenship with the US is permitted since the 1998 reform (Constitution Art. 30/32/37 + new Ley de Nacionalidad). Naturalized Mexicans can lose status by 5y continuous absence or fraud (Art. 37(B)); Mexicans-by-birth cannot (Art. 37(A)). Naturalized Mexicans are barred from President/VP, Cabinet, Supreme Court, Congress (by-birth-only offices).
- Residency on-ramp: **Temporary Resident** (~USD 4,400/mo income or ~USD 72k savings) → **Permanent Resident** by *arraigo* after 4y. Direct PR at consular filing requires ~USD 7,400/mo or ~USD 300k savings and is typically limited to "retired" applicants.
- **Versus Uruguay**: Mexico is materially slower (6.0–6.5y vs 3.5–4y under Uruguay's family-residence track for a foreign married couple), enforces a stricter physical-presence floor (18 of 24 months vs ~183 d/yr in Uruguay), and has a dramatically weaker rule-of-law profile (CPI 2025 Mexico **27 / 141st**, last among OECD; Uruguay **75 / 12th**, #1 in LATAM). Mexico passes the filter on the affordability + dual + no-renunciation + rule-based axes but is dominated on the headline comparison. Optionality: Mexico City f4 cost ~USD 3,700–4,100/mo (Numbeo, May 2026) is competitive with Montevideo; jus soli for any child born in Mexico is an option Uruguay also provides.

## Excluded by scope

- **Iceland** — citizenship path exists and permits dual (7y continuous domicile + Ríkisborgarapróf A1–A2 + Alþingi parliamentary grant fallback). Fails at the **residency on-ramp under the narrow-discretionary filter**: the only surviving non-employer route is the Business Operation Permit, which the Directorate of Immigration rejects for most generic freelancer / portable-income applicants — it requires a registered Icelandic business with demonstrable local value (employees, physical presence, Icelandic-market activity), not a fit for portable self-employment. Long-term Remote-Worker Visa is 6mo non-renewable and explicitly non-PR-feeding. No usable residency on-ramp under the active filter.
- **Norway** — citizenship path exists and permits dual since 1 Jan 2020 (Standard 8 of 11y + B1 oral + civics; high-income shortcut 6 of 10y if ≥3G income). Fails at the **residency on-ramp under the individually-discretionary filter**: the Self-employed sole proprietorship permit is the only on-ramp surviving the no-employer / no-endorsement / no-capital filter, but UDI conducts a discretionary business-viability review with no rule-based alternative (Norway has no Golden Visa, no investor program, no employer-free passive track). Under a strict "no individual discretion where no rule-based alternative exists" filter, Norway falls. Reconsider if you're willing to accept UDI discretion as more rule-bound than the Iceland Business Operation Permit case.
- **Australia, Canada** — citizenship paths exist and permit dual (Australia 4y residence + 12mo PR + citizenship test; Canada 1,095 days physical presence + CLB-4 + knowledge test). Both **fail at the residency on-ramp under the program-level-discretionary filter**: the only non-employer / non-state-nominated / non-endorsement tracks are invitation-gated points systems (Australia 189 Skilled Independent, Canada Express Entry FSW no-employer) whose final selection is controlled by the immigration department's policy choices each draw round rather than by objective applicant criteria. Australia 189 invitations deprioritized since 2022–23 in favor of state-nominated 190/491; Canada FSW general-draw CRS cutoff 510–547 in 2024–26 is practically unattainable for a US-citizen Anglophone with no Canadian study/work and no French. No usable citizenship path under the active filter.
- **Switzerland, New Zealand** — citizenship paths exist and permit dual, but the only residency on-ramp surviving the no-employer / no-endorsement filter is the high-capital track (Switzerland Lump-sum tax CHF 400k/yr+ federal + cantonal floors; New Zealand Active Investor Plus NZD 5M / NZD 10M), which is unaffordable. No usable citizenship path under the affordability filter.
- **UK** — citizenship paths (Standard 5+1, BNO, UK Ancestry, Spouse) exist and permit dual, but every residency on-ramp for a US-citizen adult with no UK family or Commonwealth ancestry requires either employer sponsorship (Skilled Worker, Scale-up) or designated-body endorsement (Global Talent, Innovator Founder). Tier 1 Investor closed 2022. No viable residency path under the active filter → no usable citizenship path.
- **Andorra, Japan, Liechtenstein, Monaco, San Marino, South Korea** — citizenship routes either require renouncing US citizenship (Andorra, Japan, Liechtenstein default, Monaco default, South Korea standard), are gated by an opaque 2/3 parliamentary/council vote after 20–30y residence (Liechtenstein, San Marino — San Marino's 2026 dual-legalization reform does not shorten this), or are statistically near-unobtainable (Monaco ~0.05%/yr grant rate, ~12–20y empirical wait). No usable citizenship path for a US citizen unwilling to renounce.
