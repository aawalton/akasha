
# Slovak Citizenship Paths (May 2026)

Slovakia operates under Act No. 40/1993 Coll. on State Citizenship. A 2010 Fico-era amendment (Act 250/2010, in force 17 Jul 2010) **automatically stripped Slovak citizenship from anyone who voluntarily acquired a foreign nationality** without prior notification of stay-based exemption — the most hostile dual-citizenship regime in the EU. Act 73/2022 (in force 1 Apr 2022) partially walked this back, exempting acquisitions by long-term residents (≥5 years) abroad and dramatically loosening the diaspora descent route. The 2024–2026 Fico-2 government has not re-tightened the 2022 rules; a draft 2026 procedural reform aims to consolidate the two-track residence/citizenship workflow. The ethnic-Slovak diaspora path via Slovak Living Abroad Certificate (Osvedčenie Slováka žijúceho v zahraničí) remains the only no-residence path open to those without a directly-born-in-Slovakia ancestor.

## Birth on Territory (Jus Soli) — Anti-Statelessness Only

- Slovakia is fundamentally jus sanguinis. Birth on Slovak soil alone confers nothing.
- §5: a child born in Slovakia acquires citizenship at birth if neither parent transmits any nationality and the child would otherwise be stateless (per 1961 UN Convention, ratified by SK in 2004).
- Foundlings (§5(2)): child found in SK whose parents are unknown is presumed Slovak unless rebutted within 6 months.
- Timeline: at birth. Backlog: not applicable.

## Descent (Jus Sanguinis) at Birth — Automatic

- §2: child born to at least one Slovak-citizen parent acquires Slovak citizenship at birth, regardless of birthplace.
- No registration deadline. Confirm via Certificate of Slovak Citizenship (osvedčenie o štátnom občianstve) at any embassy.
- Processing: typically 6–18 months for the certificate.

## Descent for Adults — §7(1)(b) "Citizenship by Descent" (Direct Ancestor Born in SK)

- Category: **discretionary grant**, but with most naturalization requirements waived. This is the post-2022-reform main diaspora route.
- Eligibility: at least one direct ancestor up to the **third generation** (parent, grandparent, or great-grandparent) who was **(i) born in the territory of present-day Slovakia AND (ii) was a Czechoslovak (or Slovak) citizen**. Both conditions are required — ethnic-only is not enough at this tier.
- 2022 reform clarified that great-grandparents are explicitly accepted (previously many were rejected under §7(2) ambiguity).
- Documentary proof: birth certificates in direct lineage from applicant to ancestor; ancestor's proof of Czechoslovak citizenship (domovský list, military records, old passport, naturalization records of country of emigration showing prior CS citizenship).
- **No language exam. No residency requirement. No civics test.** Clean criminal record still required.
- Dual citizenship: explicitly permitted (acquisition by descent is one of the carve-outs from the 2010 stripping rule).
- Filed at any Slovak embassy/consulate.
- Timeline: statutory maximum 24 months from filing with the Ministry of Interior. **Practical 2025–2026 backlog: 12–24 months at the Ministry**, plus 6–12 months upstream document gathering. Application fee EUR 700 at consulate (EUR 100 at residency office).
- **The Ethnicity Clarification**: ethnicity itself is **not** the criterion — the law is birth-in-territory + CS-citizenship of the ancestor. Hungarian, Roma, Rusyn, German, and Jewish persons whose ancestor was born in pre-1993 Slovak territory and held CS citizenship qualify equally.

## Slovak Living Abroad Certificate Route — §7(1)(b) via §7(2)/SLAC + 3-Year Stay

- Category: discretionary grant for ethnic Slovaks who cannot satisfy the direct-ancestor-born-in-SK test (e.g., ancestor born in Vojvodina, Carpathian Ruthenia, US, Argentina but identifying as Slovak).
- Step 1: obtain **Slovak Living Abroad Certificate** (Osvedčenie Slováka žijúceho v zahraničí, "SLAC") from Office for Slovaks Living Abroad (ÚSŽZ) in Bratislava.
  - Requires proof of Slovak ethnicity/heritage — ancestor self-identified as Slovak by nationality (even if state citizen elsewhere), and applicant retains some active connection (language, culture, association membership).
  - No strict generational limit by law; practical evidentiary ceiling at great-great-grandparents.
  - Processing: 60 days statutory; practically 3–6 months. No fee.
- Step 2: reside in Slovakia for **3 years of continuous permanent residence** holding the SLAC (SLAC itself grants a residence permit pathway).
- Step 3: apply for citizenship under §7. Language exam waived for SLAC holders.
- Fee: EUR 560 (SLAC-based application).
- **Total: ~3.5–4 years end-to-end** (SLAC issuance + 3 years residence + ~12 months citizenship decision).
- Alternative: SLAC-holder can also qualify without the 3-year residence by demonstrating significant benefit to the Slovak diaspora — discretionary and rare.

## Naturalization by Residency (Standard) — §7(1)(a)

- **Residency**: **8 years of continuous permanent residence** immediately preceding the application. In practice: ~5 years on temporary residence → upgrade to permanent residence → 3 additional years on permanent residence.
- **Language**: Slovak language proficiency demonstrated **orally and in writing**, verified by commission interview. Tasks: read a 500-word Slovak press article aloud, summarize it in writing, answer general-knowledge questions about Slovak history/culture/geography. Standardized across regions since 2022.
- **Civics**: same interview covers Slovak constitutional system, basic history, geography.
- **Clean record**: no criminal prosecution pending; no extradition or administrative expulsion proceedings; no current asylum-withdrawal proceeding. Clean record in SK and country of origin.
- **Livelihood**: documented stable income / employment / business activity; no welfare dependency. Tax compliance for residency period.
- **Integration**: housing proof, employment / family ties evidence.
- **Fee**: EUR 700.
- **Timeline**: statutory 24 months from filing. Practical 12–24 months. Plus the parallel-track residence-permit dependency creates additional delay (Foreign Police + Ministry of Interior must both progress).
- **Total elapsed: ~9–11 years from arrival.**

## Naturalization by Marriage — §7(2)(a): Facilitated 5-Year Residence

- Marriage to a Slovak citizen reduces the residence requirement to **5 years of common household in Slovakia immediately preceding the application**, with the marriage ongoing for at least those 5 years.
- All other requirements identical to standard naturalization: language, civics, clean record, livelihood. **No fee reduction.**
- **Total elapsed**: ~5–7 years from arrival in SK (assuming temporary residence accepted from marriage onset). The marriage itself does not waive prior temporary-residence buildup.
- Dual citizenship: marriage-based acquisition is one of the carve-outs from the 2010 stripping rule — Slovak spouse acquiring foreign nationality through their non-Slovak partner does not lose Slovak citizenship.

## Investment / CBI — None

- Slovakia has **no citizenship-by-investment program** and never has. For residency-program details (including absence of a golden visa), see [../residency/slovakia.md](../residency/slovakia.book-chapter.md).

## Distinguished Contribution — §7(2)(b)

- Discretionary grant by Ministry of Interior, government-proposal track for cases with significant contribution to SK in **economic, scientific, technical, social, cultural, or sporting** fields, or otherwise in the national interest.
- Waives the 8-year residency requirement entirely. Language exam typically waived.
- No published quota; case-by-case ministerial discretion. Used historically for Olympic athletes, defectors of value, prominent scientists.
- Timeline: 6–24 months once a government sponsor is engaged.

## Service-Based — None Defined

- No general military or government-service shortcut. §7(2)(b) discretionary path is the only de facto channel for distinguished state service.

## Refugees and Stateless Persons — §7(1)(a) with Reduced Term

- **Recognized refugees** (asylum holders): may apply after **4 years** of continuous residence post-asylum grant.
- **Stateless persons**: may apply after **3 years** of continuous residence.
- Language and civics requirements still apply, but evaluated with health/age/education-appropriate flexibility.
- Total elapsed: ~4–6 years from protection grant.

## Re-acquisition of Lost Citizenship — §9 / 2022 Long-Residence Exemption

- Two distinct sub-paths, both opened or expanded by Act 73/2022:
- **Sub-path A — 2010 Stripping Reversal (the headline 2022 fix)**: persons who lost Slovak citizenship between **17 Jul 2010 and 31 Mar 2022** by voluntarily acquiring a foreign nationality may re-acquire **without the 8-year residence requirement**, provided they had **legal residence in the foreign country for at least 5 years prior to acquiring that nationality** and can prove it documentarily. This is the political walk-back of the 2010 Fico law.
- **Sub-path B — Former-citizen general re-acquisition**: anyone who lost Slovak citizenship by release-on-request or by foreign-citizenship acquisition (any era) may re-acquire if they currently hold a Slovak residence permit. The 8-year continuous residence is waived; language and civics still applied at the Ministry's discretion.
- Dual citizenship: re-acquisition under the 2022 sub-path A is the **only context** in which the 2010 stripping rule is directly neutralized for past losses. Going forward, the same 5-year-foreign-residence rule (now codified in §9a) preserves dual citizenship prospectively.
- Timeline: 12–24 months Ministry processing.
- **Practical impact**: this is the path for Slovak emigrants in the US, Canada, Australia, etc. who naturalized after 2010 and lost SK citizenship; if they had been resident abroad ≥5 years before naturalizing, they get SK citizenship back with no language exam burden if they document carefully.

## Dual Citizenship Policy (May 2026, Enacted State)

The 2010 stripping rule (§9(1)(b) of the Act, inserted by Act 250/2010) **remains on the books** but is now riddled with carve-outs after Act 73/2022:

- **Slovak loses SK citizenship by acquiring foreign nationality UNLESS** one of these applies:
  - Acquired by **birth** (jus sanguinis from birth).
  - Acquired by **marriage** to the foreign-citizen spouse.
  - Acquired by **adoption** as a minor.
  - Acquired after **≥5 years of legal residence** in the foreign country (the 2022 amendment, the main exemption).
- Required: notify the SK Ministry of Interior within 6 months of foreign acquisition to claim the exemption. Failure to notify can still trigger loss in principle, though enforcement is weak in practice.
- Inbound side: SK does **not** require renunciation of prior nationality when granting citizenship by descent, marriage, or §7(2)(b) discretion. Standard naturalization applicants face no formal renunciation requirement either, though documentation often expects it.
- **Net 2026 position**: Slovakia tolerates dual citizenship in most realistic acquisition scenarios but is the only EU member that retains an explicit stripping clause for non-exempt foreign naturalizations. Anyone planning to naturalize abroad should pre-establish ≥5 years of foreign residence and document the notification.

## Comparison Summary

Ranked fastest-to-citizenship for a typical applicant:

| Rank | Path | Total time | Likelihood |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | §7(1)(b) descent (ancestor born in SK + CS citizen, up to great-grandparent) | 12–24 months (mostly document gathering + Ministry queue) | High if ancestor verifiable |
| 2 | §9 re-acquisition (lost 2010–2022, had 5y foreign residence) | 12–24 months | High if previously held |
| 3 | §7(2)(b) distinguished contribution | 6–24 months | Very rare; needs sponsor |
| 4 | Refugee naturalization (4y) / stateless (3y) | ~4–6 years | Conditional on protection status |
| 5 | SLAC + 3-year residence (ethnic-Slovak diaspora without territorial-birth ancestor) | ~3.5–4 years | High for documented ethnic Slovaks |
| 6 | Marriage facilitated (5y residence + 5y marriage) | ~5–7 years | Marriage-dependent |
| 7 | Standard naturalization (8y residence) | ~9–11 years | Achievable but long; language gate |
| — | CBI | N/A | Does not exist |
| — | Jus soli | N/A | Anti-statelessness only |

For Future-Alan: if any ancestor born in pre-1993 Slovak territory who held Czechoslovak citizenship is documentable in the family tree to great-grandparent (3rd generation), **§7(1)(b) is the strongest play** — no language, no residency, dual citizenship explicit, 12–24 months. Failing that, the **SLAC + 3-year residence** route is the fastest discretionary path for documented ethnic Slovaks. Standard naturalization is a hard 9–11-year slog with a Slovak-language oral+written commission interview.

## Recent Changes (2022–2026)

- **1 Apr 2022**: Act 73/2022 enters force. Major reforms: (i) **3rd-generation descent path** explicitly opened; (ii) **5-year foreign-residence exemption** to the 2010 stripping rule, prospective and retroactive (2010–2022 losers can re-acquire); (iii) procedural simplifications for SLAC-based applications.
- **2024 (Fico-2 government, Smer-SD return)**: no rollback of the 2022 dual-citizenship liberalization. Public statements from Interior Minister Šutaj Eštok signaled openness to further diaspora outreach. No restrictive amendments enacted as of May 2026.
- **Draft 2026 procedural reform** (in legislative pipeline, expected effect ~July 2026): consolidates the parallel Foreign-Police-residence-permit and Ministry-of-Interior-citizenship two-track process into a single intake to reduce the cross-dependency backlog. Does not change substantive eligibility.
- **2010 (historical context)**: Act 250/2010 under Fico-1 introduced the automatic-stripping rule, primarily as a retaliation against Hungary's simultaneous extension of Hungarian citizenship to ethnic Hungarians abroad (which would have given dual SK-HU citizenship to ~500k Slovaks of Hungarian ethnicity). The 2022 reform did not repeal §9(1)(b) — it added exemptions around it.
