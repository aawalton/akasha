
# Ireland — Paths to Citizenship (May 2026)

Ireland's regime is unusual in three ways: no language test for naturalization (English/Irish proficiency is not assessed), the **Foreign Births Register** (FBR) is the world's most-used diaspora citizenship path (grandparent-line, indefinitely extensible if each generation registers in order), and **everyone born in Northern Ireland** has a treaty-protected right to Irish citizenship under the Good Friday Agreement. Dual citizenship is fully allowed at all levels. The IIP "golden visa" closed February 2023 (Ireland never offered direct CBI); see [residency/ireland.md](../residency/ireland.book-chapter.md).

## Birth in Ireland (Jus Soli — Conditional Since 2005)

- **Pre-2005 (full jus soli):** Anyone born on the island of Ireland (Republic or Northern Ireland) before 1 January 2005 is automatically an Irish citizen regardless of parental status.
- **Post-2005 (conditional, per 27th Amendment, 2004 referendum):** Born in Ireland on/after 1 Jan 2005 → Irish only if at least one parent was (a) an Irish citizen, (b) a British citizen, (c) entitled to live in either jurisdiction without restriction, or (d) a non-EEA national legally resident on the island for **3 of the 4 years** before the birth (asylum-seeker time excluded).
- **Timeline:** Automatic at birth if conditions met.
- **Backlog:** None — register the birth, apply for passport.

## Descent — Parent Born in Ireland (Automatic, No FBR)

- **Eligibility:** Born outside Ireland with at least one parent who was born on the island of Ireland (Republic or NI). You are Irish from birth automatically.
- **Documentation:** Apply directly for an Irish passport with parent's Irish birth certificate, your birth certificate, and ID. No FBR registration required.
- **Timeline:** Automatic at birth; passport processing 4–8 weeks (first-time abroad applications longer).
- **Backlog:** Passport service operating near-normal in 2026.

## Descent — Foreign Births Register (Grandparent and Beyond)

The diaspora workhorse. Indefinitely extensible across generations *only* if each generation registers on the FBR **before the next generation is born**.

- **Eligibility:**
  - **Grandparent born in Ireland**, parent not born in Ireland and not on FBR at your birth → eligible.
  - **Parent born abroad but registered on FBR before your birth** → eligible (extends the chain).
  - Great-grandparent only? Eligible **only if** your grandparent (born abroad) was on the FBR before your parent's birth, AND your parent was on the FBR before your birth. Otherwise no FBR right; fall back to "Irish Associations" (discretionary).
- **Process:** Apply online via Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA), submit certified birth/marriage certificates establishing the chain, posted to Dublin.
- **Citizenship effective date:** Date of FBR entry — **not** retroactive to birth. Children born to you before your FBR registration are NOT Irish through you.
- **Timeline / backlog (May 2026):** 9–18 months from receipt of complete application; DFA processes in strict date order. Incomplete chains are the leading cause of delay.
- **Cost:** EUR 278 adult, EUR 153 minor.

## Northern Ireland — Good Friday Agreement Right

- **Eligibility:** Anyone born in Northern Ireland is entitled to identify as Irish, British, or both. Pre-2005 NI births are automatically Irish (unconditional). Post-2005 NI births qualify on the same conditional jus-soli rules as the Republic (parent Irish/British, settled, or 3-of-4-years lawful residence).
- **Process:** Apply for an Irish passport with NI birth certificate — citizenship is asserted, not granted.
- **Timeline:** Immediate on application; passport in weeks.
- **Note:** This right is treaty-protected by the 1998 Belfast/Good Friday Agreement and survives any future constitutional change in NI's status.

## Naturalization by Residency (Standard)

- **Eligibility:**
  - **5 years reckonable residence in the previous 9 years**, including **1 continuous year immediately before application** (max 70 days abroad in that final year, max 6 weeks in extenuating circumstances).
  - Reckonable stamps: Stamp 1, 1G, 4, 5. **Stamp 2 (student) and Stamp 3 do not count**; time as an asylum-seeker awaiting decision does not count.
  - Good character (criminal record in Ireland and abroad, Revenue/tax compliance, immigration compliance, financial solvency — broad ministerial discretion).
  - Intend to continue residing in Ireland.
  - Of full age (18+) or minor born in Ireland.
  - **No language test.** No civics test.
- **Timeline to citizenship:** ~5 years residence + processing.
- **Backlog (May 2026):** 12–18 months processing typical; ISD cleared a record 33,000 decisions in 2025 and held large ceremonies in Killarney in April 2026 (~5,000 new citizens). E-vetting and reduced documentation requirements have shortened wait times vs the 2022–2023 peak (then nearly 2 years).
- **Fees:** EUR 175 application; EUR 950 on grant (EUR 200 minor; widow/widower of Irish citizen waived in some cases).
- **Oath:** Declaration of fidelity to the nation and loyalty to the State at a public ceremony.

## Naturalization by Marriage / Civil Partnership

- **Eligibility:**
  - Married to or in civil partnership with an Irish citizen for **3 years**, AND
  - **3 years reckonable residence** on the island of Ireland in the previous 5 years, including 1 continuous year immediately before application.
  - Couple living together at time of application (formal declaration required).
  - Good character; intention to continue residing.
- **Timeline:** ~3 years (marriage + residence overlap fully) + processing.
- **Backlog:** Same 12–18 months as standard naturalization.
- **Fees:** EUR 175 + EUR 950.

## Irish Descent / Irish Associations (Discretionary — INA 1956 §§15, 16)

Ministerial discretion can waive any/all statutory conditions (residency, fees, even good character) for applicants with Irish blood, affinity, adoption, or civil-partner ties to an Irish citizen — including great-grandparent descent where the FBR chain was broken.

- **Eligibility:** Demonstrate Irish descent or "Irish associations" (blood/affinity/adoption/civil partnership to an Irish citizen or someone entitled to be one). Minister's absolute discretion.
- **Timeline:** Multi-year; not a reliable path. Applications routinely refused without reasons.
- **Backlog:** Long; treated as residual / exceptional.
- **Practical use:** Mostly invoked alongside another weak claim; not a standalone strategy.

## Investment / CBI — IIP Closed

The Immigrant Investor Programme closed to new applications on 15 February 2023; Ireland never offered direct citizenship-by-investment. Details (existing-holder status, replacement) in [residency/ireland.md](../residency/ireland.book-chapter.md). Existing approved investors remain eligible to naturalize via the standard 5-year residency route.

## Defence Forces Service

- **Current law (May 2026):** No statutory fast-track for military service. Defence Forces members follow the standard 5-year naturalization route.
- **Proposed (Feb 2026):** The Strategic Framework for the Transformation of the Defence Forces proposes: 3–5 years of service → naturalize without the 5-year residency requirement, EUR 1,000 fee waived. Status "undergoing further evaluation." Not enacted as of May 2026 — track for change.
- **Existing discretionary path:** "Distinguished services to the nation" honorary citizenship (INA §12) — extremely rare (Tip O'Neill, Jean Kennedy Smith, etc.); reserved for heads of state, public benefactors. Not a planning path.

## Refugees and International Protection

- **Pre-8 Dec 2025:** 3 years reckonable residence post-protection grant.
- **Post-8 Dec 2025 (current law):** **5 years reckonable residence** (aligned with standard naturalization). Applications lodged before 8 Dec 2025 remain on the old 3-year rule.
- **New criteria (from Dec 2025):** Good character bar tightened; new self-sufficiency requirement.
- **Asylum-seeker time excluded** — clock starts only at protection grant, not arrival.
- **Backlog:** 12–18 months processing.

## Stateless Persons Born in Ireland

- **Eligibility (INA 1956 §6(3)):** Person born in Ireland who is "not otherwise entitled to citizenship of any country" is Irish from birth.
- **Process:** Apply for a certificate of nationality from the Minister for Justice. No dedicated statelessness-determination procedure exists in Irish law (a known gap; reform pending).
- **Timeline:** Case-by-case; cert issued where stateless status established.

## Resumption / Re-acquisition (INA 1956 §19)

- **Eligibility:** Former Irish citizens who renounced (typically to acquire a citizenship that prohibited dual nationality) may apply to resume. Subject to ministerial discretion; considers residence, ties to the State, and reasons for resumption.
- **Note:** Resumption of FBR-derived citizenship after renunciation is generally straightforward; resumption after deprivation is not available.
- **Timeline:** Months to a year.

## Comparison Summary

### Ranked by speed (assuming eligibility)

1. **NI-born / parent born in Ireland** — automatic; passport in weeks. Fastest.
2. **Foreign Births Register (grandparent line)** — 9–18 months once application submitted; no residency required.
3. **Pre-2005 birth in Ireland** — automatic; passport in weeks.
4. **Resumption** — months, if eligible.
5. **Marriage** — 3 years marriage + 3 years residence + 12–18 months processing = ~4–5 years.
6. **Standard naturalization** — 5 years residence + 12–18 months = ~6–7 years.
7. **Refugee/International Protection (post-Dec 2025)** — 5 years post-grant + processing = ~6–7 years.
8. **Irish Associations (great-grandparent etc.)** — discretionary, multi-year, unreliable.
9. **Defence Forces fast-track** — not yet enacted (May 2026); track for change.

### Ranked by likelihood of success (no Irish ancestry, US citizen)

1. **Naturalization by residency** (5 years on Stamp 1/4) — primary realistic path.
2. **Naturalization by marriage** (if married to Irish citizen) — 3 years.
3. **Irish Associations** — only if a remote blood/affinity tie exists; unreliable.
4. **All other paths** — closed (IIP) or require birth/ancestry not present.

### Effectively closed paths

- **IIP / golden visa** — closed Feb 2023, no replacement.
- **Unconditional jus soli** — abolished for births on/after 1 Jan 2005.
- **Honorary citizenship** — not a planning path.

### Dual citizenship

- **Fully allowed.** Ireland imposes no restriction on holding other nationalities. The US likewise allows dual citizenship. No renunciation required either direction.

### Recent / flag-worthy changes

- **8 Dec 2025:** International Protection residency lengthened 3→5 years (Minister O'Callaghan migration package).
- **Feb 2026:** Defence Forces fast-track citizenship proposal published; not enacted.
- **April 2026:** Killarney ceremonies — ~5,000 new citizens; backlog visibly clearing.
- **15 Feb 2023:** IIP closed with one day's notice.
- **2025:** E-vetting and reduced documentation rollout cut processing-time backlog.
- **FBR pipeline (2024–2026):** 12–18 month steady state; was over 2 years during 2022–2023 peak.
