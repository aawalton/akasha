
# Greek Citizenship Paths (May 2026)

Greece runs a jus-sanguinis regime under the Greek Citizenship Code (Law 3284/2004), reformed by Law 4735/2020 which introduced the **Panhellenic Citizenship Test** (PEGP / ΠΕΓΠ) — a written Greek-language **B1** exam plus a history/geography/civics section — replacing the old discretionary committee interview for the language/integration check. Naturalization is **7 years** of legal residence (3 for EU citizens, recognized refugees, omogeneis, or spouses of a Greek citizen with a common child); backlogs in Athens and Thessaloniki are historically multi-year (4–5 years post-application). Dual citizenship is **unconditionally permitted**. Substantial diaspora (omogeneis) paths exist for ethnic Greeks abroad, especially Pontian Greeks from the former USSR and ethnic Greeks from Albania. For Greece's Golden Visa and other residency programs, see [../residency/greece.md](../residency/greece.book-chapter.md).

## Birth on Territory (Conditional Jus Soli)

- **Basis**: Art. 1A Greek Citizenship Code (Law 3284/2004 as amended by Law 4332/2015).
- **Eligibility — Track A (birth-time)**: child born in Greece **acquires citizenship at birth** if at least one parent has **5 consecutive years** of prior legal residence in Greece (parents must jointly declare and register the child at the municipality within 3 years; 10 years required if neither parent was a legal resident at the 5-year mark).
- **Eligibility — Track B (school-completion)**: child born in Greece or who entered before age 6, with **6 successful years of Greek schooling**, may acquire citizenship by declaration of a parent while the child is a minor; the parent must hold a valid residence permit.
- **Stateless-child protection**: child born on Greek territory who would otherwise be stateless acquires Greek citizenship (Art. 1(2)).
- **Timeline**: declaration plus municipal registration; effective date is birth date for Track A. Processing typically 6–18 months.

## Descent — Greek Parent (jus sanguinis, Art. 1)

- **Basis**: Art. 1 Citizenship Code — any person born to at least one Greek-citizen parent acquires Greek citizenship automatically at birth, anywhere in the world.
- **Eligibility tripwires by birth date**:
  - Born **on/after 8 May 1984**: either parent transmits automatically.
  - Born **before 8 May 1984** to a **Greek mother + foreign father**: child does **not** acquire automatically; must declare intention to acquire under Art. 14 (no language test, no residency).
  - Born **before 18 July 1982** to a Greek father in civil (non-religious) marriage with a foreigner: must declare intention.
- **Requirements**: parent's Greek citizenship documented at consulate; ancestor registered in the Greek municipal records (Δημοτολόγιο / Demotologion).
- **Timeline**: consular registration **6–24 months**; passport follows.
- **Cost**: ~€100 in consular and translation fees.

## Descent — Greek Origin Beyond the Parent (Art. 14 / 15)

- **Basis**: Art. 14–15 Citizenship Code; declaration by a person of Greek descent (omogenis) born abroad whose Greek ancestry runs through grandparent or great-grandparent.
- **Eligibility**: documented **unbroken chain** of Greek nationality (each ancestor was a Greek citizen at the time of the child's birth, even if later lost); the founding ancestor was registered in a Greek municipality. **No generation cap in statute**, but grandparent and great-parent claims are routine while great-great-grandparent claims become evidentiarily difficult. No residency, no language test, no Panhellenic Test.
- **Timeline**: consular application; **1–3 years** in practice depending on consulate workload and document completeness.
- **Notes**: this is the workhorse diaspora path for the Greek-American, Greek-Australian, and Greek-Canadian communities.

## Special Naturalization — Omogeneis with Special ID (Art. 23)

- **Basis**: Art. 23 Citizenship Code (formerly Law 2790/2000 and Law 3838/2010 amendments) — fast-track for ethnic Greeks (homogeneis) holding the **Special Identity Card for Co-Ethnic Greeks** (ΕΔΤΟ).
- **Eligibility**: ethnic Greek origin documented from the former USSR (Pontian Greeks), **Albania** (Northern Epirus / Vorioepirotes), Turkey, Egypt, or other historic Greek diasporas; possession of the Special ID Card; loyalty oath. **No Panhellenic Test required**; integration is presumed via ethnic origin.
- **Timeline**: **historically 1–3 years**; in practice the Albanian-omogeneis cohort received the Special ID in 2001 and ~45,000 naturalized within three years of the 2008 amendment.
- **Notes**: the largest pool is ~189,000 Albanian-Greeks issued Special IDs since 2001 plus Pontian Greeks who resettled from the former USSR in the 1990s. Distinct from the standard 7-year naturalization track and from the descent track.

## Standard Naturalization by Residency (Art. 5 / 5A — 7 Years)

- **Basis**: Art. 5 Citizenship Code as amended by Law 4735/2020 (Panhellenic Test) and Law 4332/2015.
- **Eligibility**:
  - **7 years of legal residence** in Greece (long-term residence permit, EU Blue Card, or specified permit categories); residence permit valid at the time of application.
  - Adult (≥18), legal capacity.
  - Clean criminal record (no felony conviction; no convictions ≥1 year for specified offenses including narcotics, trafficking, money laundering, terrorism, organized crime).
  - No outstanding deportation order.
  - **Panhellenic Citizenship Test (PEGP)**: written exam, ≥70% overall (≥66.6% on Greek-language B1 section, ≥50% on history/geography/civics section); held twice yearly (typically March and November).
  - Interview before the Citizenship Committee (post-PEGP) on civic integration and ties to Greece.
  - Sufficient income (tax-declared); social-security integration.
  - Loyalty oath to the Hellenic Republic.
- **Timeline to citizenship**: **7 years residence + 3–5 years processing**, so ~**10–12 years total** in current practice.
- **Cost**: €700 application fee (standard), €200 for renewals/re-submissions.

## Reduced Naturalization — 3 Years (EU / Refugee / Spouse-with-Child / Omogenis)

- **Basis**: Art. 5(1)(e) Citizenship Code.
- **Eligibility (any of)**:
  - **EU citizen** with 3 years of legal residence in Greece.
  - **Recognized refugee or stateless person** with 3 years of legal residence (counted from recognition, not from arrival).
  - **Spouse of a Greek citizen** with 3 years of marriage **and** a **common child** (biological or legally adopted) plus 3 years of legal residence in Greece.
  - **Omogenis** holding the Special ID card meeting Art. 23 conditions (see above).
- **All other Art. 5 conditions apply**: clean record, Panhellenic Test, interview, oath.
- **Timeline**: 3 years residence + 2–4 years processing = **5–7 years total** in practice.

## Naturalization by Marriage (No Common Child) — Standard 7-Year Track

- **Basis**: Greek law **does not grant citizenship by marriage alone**. A foreign spouse of a Greek citizen with **no common child** must complete the **standard 7-year naturalization track** (Art. 5) like any other third-country national.
- **Common-child shortcut**: 3-year reduced track applies only if a common child exists (biological or adopted) — see above.
- **Cohabitation evidence helps**: the Citizenship Committee considers marriage and family ties favorably in the integration interview.
- **Timeline**: ~10–12 years total without a common child; ~5–7 years with one.

## Investment / Golden Visa

- **No fast-track passport-for-investment route exists.** Greece's Golden Visa is residency only; Golden Visa years count as legal residence under Art. 5, but the 7-year naturalization clock requires substantive physical residence plus the Panhellenic Test. See [../residency/greece.md](../residency/greece.book-chapter.md) for the Golden Visa program details.

## Honorary / Distinguished Services (Art. 13)

- **Basis**: Art. 13 Citizenship Code — naturalization by **Presidential Decree** on justified proposal by the Minister of Interior for "special services to Greece" or where naturalization serves the national interest.
- **Eligibility**: discretionary; no residency, language, or test requirements. Granted for cultural, scientific, humanitarian, or athletic contributions.
- **Examples**: Tom Hanks and Rita Wilson (2020) — humanitarian response to 2018 Attica wildfires.
- **Timeline**: case-by-case; typically months once proposed. Extremely rare — a handful per year.

## Military Service Path

- Greece does **not** offer a discrete "military-service naturalization track" for foreigners; only Greek citizens (including naturalized ones) are subject to conscription. Foreigners may volunteer for limited capacities but this **does not** trigger expedited citizenship. Distinguished military service may be recognized under Art. 13 (honorary).

## Mount Athos Monks (Automatic)

- **Basis**: Constitution of Greece, Art. 105(1); Charter of Mount Athos — all persons admitted as monks or novices to the **Athonite monastic community** automatically acquire Greek citizenship upon admission, regardless of prior nationality.
- **Eligibility**: male, Eastern Orthodox, accepted by one of the 20 ruling monasteries on the autonomous peninsula.
- **Timeline**: effective upon admission/tonsure; documentation through the monastery and the Holy Community to the civil registry.
- **Notes**: a unique constitutional carve-out tied to the autonomous religious status of Mount Athos. ~2,000 monks total; the community is closed to women (avaton).

## Refugee / Stateless Path

- **Basis**: Art. 5(1)(e) — 3-year reduced track for recognized refugees and stateless persons (above).
- **Stateless-child-born-on-territory**: automatic at birth under Art. 1(2).
- **Pre-recognition years (asylum-seeker status) do not count** toward the 3-year clock.
- **Timeline**: 3 years from recognition + 2–4 years processing ≈ 5–7 years; in practice 8–10+ years from arrival because asylum recognition itself is slow.

## Re-acquisition of Lost Citizenship (Art. 22, 25)

- **Basis**: Art. 22 (former Greek citizens by birth or naturalization who lost it) and Art. 25 (specific re-acquisition cases including children of Greek mothers).
- **Eligibility**: documented prior Greek citizenship; clean record; loyalty oath; in some cases a residency requirement (for those who lost via foreign naturalization pre-1984).
- **Notable junta/post-monarchy case**: members of the **former royal family** stripped under **Law 2215/1994** (the "Evangelos Venizelos Law") applied 19 Dec 2024; the Minister of Interior reinstated citizenship the next day under that same statute, requiring adoption of the surname "**De Grèce / Ντε Γκρες**" and a republican-constitution allegiance pledge. Ten members received citizenship.
- **Holocaust survivors / descendants**: Greeks of Jewish origin born in Greece before 9 May 1945, and their direct-line descendants, who lost citizenship may reacquire under simplified procedure.
- **Timeline**: typically 6 months to 2 years by ministerial / cabinet decision.

## Children of Naturalized Parents

- **Basis**: Art. 11 Citizenship Code — minor children of a person who naturalizes acquire Greek citizenship automatically if they reside in Greece with the naturalizing parent.
- **Timeline**: effective on the parent's oath; municipal registration follows.

## Comparison Summary

Ranked by **speed** for a typical applicant (May 2026):

1. **Mount Athos monk** — immediate upon admission. Unique constitutional carve-out; gender- and faith-restricted; not a realistic plan.
2. **Descent via Greek parent (Art. 1)** — ~6–24 months consular processing. Automatic at birth; declaration if pre-1984 maternal line. Best path if eligible.
3. **Descent via grandparent / great-grandparent (Art. 14–15)** — ~1–3 years. No residency, no language, no test. Workhorse diaspora path.
4. **Omogenis with Special ID (Art. 23)** — ~1–3 years. Only for documented ethnic Greeks from former USSR / Albania / Turkey / Egypt cohorts; no Panhellenic Test.
5. **Honorary (Art. 13)** — months once proposed; functionally inaccessible without national-interest contribution.
6. **Re-acquisition (Art. 22, 25)** — 6 months to 2 years; only if previously Greek.
7. **Reduced naturalization (3-year track)** — **5–7 years total** (3 residence + 2–4 processing). Requires EU citizenship **or** refugee status **or** spouse-with-common-child **or** omogenis status; Panhellenic Test required.
8. **Standard naturalization (7-year track)** — **10–12 years total** (7 residence + 3–5 processing). Athens/Thessaloniki backlogs historically 4–5 years post-application per Generation 2.0; the Panhellenic Test is the principal hard gate.
9. **Marriage without common child** — same as standard 7-year track; marriage alone confers nothing.
10. **Golden Visa → citizenship** — ~**10–12 years**; visa requires no physical presence, but the 7-year naturalization clock effectively does. Not a citizenship shortcut. See [../residency/greece.md](../residency/greece.book-chapter.md).
11. **Refugee** — 5–7 years from recognition + processing; 8–10+ years from arrival.

**Likelihood**: descent and omogeneis paths are near-certain with documentation. Standard naturalization is the principal gate — Panhellenic Test (B1 Greek) is non-trivial, and Athens/Thessaloniki backlogs persist at multi-year scales. Marriage gives no shortcut without a common child.

**Dual citizenship**: **unconditionally permitted** under Greek law — no renunciation requirement, no notification, no cap. Naturalized Greeks (and descent applicants) keep their original nationality.

**Flag-worthy recent changes**:
- **Law 4735/2020** (in effect since 2021): Panhellenic Citizenship Test (PEGP) replaces the old Citizenship Committee interview as the language/integration gate. B1 Greek required.
- **Dec 2024**: Former royal family citizenship reinstated under Law 2215/1994 with required surname "De Grèce."
- **2026 status**: backlogs still pronounced; no signal of structural overhaul of the multi-year processing wait.
