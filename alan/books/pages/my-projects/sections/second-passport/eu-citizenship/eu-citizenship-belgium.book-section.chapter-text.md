
# Belgian Citizenship Paths (May 2026)

Belgium's regime is governed by the **Code of Belgian Nationality (CBN, 1984, last major reform 2013)**. The dominant adult path is a **declaration of acquisition** under Article 12bis (5-year track with integration proofs, or 10-year track with looser proofs). Parliamentary **naturalization** survives only for "exceptional merit" cases. **Dual citizenship has been fully allowed since 28 April 2008** (non-retroactive). Language can be Dutch, French, or German — regional/communal authorities determine which. As of **29 July 2025**, the registration fee jumped from €150 to **€1,000** (indexed annually from 1 Jan 2026). A B1-language + civics-exam reform package is queued for 2026–2027 under the De Wever government.

## Birth in Belgium (jus soli — conditional)

- **Category:** Birth on territory.
- **Requirements:** Belgium has only **second-generation jus soli**, not third. A child born in Belgium acquires Belgian nationality at birth if at least one parent was also born in Belgium and that parent had legal main residence in Belgium for **5 of the 10 years** preceding the birth (Art. 11 CBN). A third-generation rule (child born in Belgium to a parent born in Belgium) is automatic via the parent's own nationality, not a separate path.
- **Stateless children** born in Belgium acquire nationality automatically if they would otherwise remain stateless.
- **Timeline:** Automatic at birth; declared at the commune.
- **Dual citizenship:** Allowed.

## Descent (jus sanguinis)

- **Category:** Descent.
- **Requirements:** Automatic citizenship at birth if at least one parent is Belgian at the time of birth (Art. 8 CBN).
- **Generational limit (born abroad):** If the **Belgian parent was also born abroad**, the child does **not** automatically acquire Belgian nationality unless the parent files a declaration of attribution at a Belgian embassy/commune **within 5 years of the child's birth** (Art. 8 §1, 2° b CBN). Miss the 5-year window → the right is lost (the child must later naturalize as an adult). Effectively a **two-generation cap** for Belgians-abroad.
- **Timeline:** Same-day registration if filed on time.
- **Dual citizenship:** Allowed since 2008.

## Declaration after 5 years of legal residence (Art. 12bis §1, 2°)

- **Category:** Naturalization by declaration — the **main fast track**.
- **Requirements:**
  - Age 18+, legal main residence in Belgium for **5 uninterrupted years**.
  - **Language:** A2 (oral + written) in Dutch, French, or German (B1 reform queued, not yet in force May 2026).
  - **Social integration** — any one of: recognized diploma; 400-hour vocational training; completed regional integration course (*inburgering* / *parcours d'intégration*); or 5 years of uninterrupted work.
  - **Economic participation** — at least **468 days of work** (or social-security-contribution equivalent) in the past 5 years.
  - Clean criminal record (no serious offences).
- **Timeline:** 5 years pre-residence + ~5–6 months processing. **Royal Prosecutor has 4 months to object** from acknowledgement of receipt; silence = automatic grant under the "120-day rule".
- **Backlog (May 2026):** Brussels-area communes 5–8 months end-to-end (post-July-2025 fee hike caused an application rush; some communes report 6–12 month backlogs for in-person interviews).
- **Fee:** €1,000 (since 29 July 2025).
- **Dual citizenship:** Allowed; no renunciation required.

## Declaration after 10 years of legal residence (Art. 12bis §1, 5°)

- **Category:** Naturalization by declaration — slow track with looser proofs.
- **Requirements:** Age 18+, **10 uninterrupted years** of legal main residence; **knowledge of one national language (A2)**; proof of "participation in the life of the host community" (looser than the 5-year track — no diploma, training, or work-history requirement, just demonstrable social ties). No 468-day work-history rule.
- **Timeline:** 10 years pre-residence + ~5–6 months processing.
- **Use case:** Long-term residents (pensioners, homemakers, freelancers without formal Belgian work history) who can't meet 5-year track's integration proofs.
- **Fee:** €1,000.
- **Dual citizenship:** Allowed.

## Declaration via marriage / cohabitation (Art. 12bis §1, 3°)

- **Category:** Marriage / partnership.
- **Requirements:** Age 18+; resident in Belgium for **5 years** AND married to (or in registered legal cohabitation with) a Belgian for **at least 3 years and currently cohabiting**; A2 language; social integration (same options as 5-year track). **No separate economic-participation requirement.** Marriage abroad alone confers nothing — there is no acquisition by marriage per se since 1985; only the declaration route.
- **Timeline:** 5 years residence + 3 years marriage (typically overlapping) + ~5–6 months processing.
- **Variant — Belgian spouse abroad:** No separate "5 years abroad" track exists in current CBN; cohabitation must be in Belgium. (Sources sometimes cite an older repealed provision.)
- **Fee:** €1,000.
- **Dual citizenship:** Allowed.

## Declaration for parent of Belgian minor (Art. 12bis §1, 4°)

- **Category:** Family.
- **Requirements:** Age 18+; legal residence in Belgium for **5 years**; parent of a Belgian minor child; A2 language; social integration. No economic-participation requirement.
- **Timeline:** 5 years + ~5–6 months processing.
- **Fee:** €1,000.
- **Dual citizenship:** Allowed.

## Declaration for persons with a disability or aged 65+ (Art. 12bis §1, 6°)

- **Category:** Special category.
- **Requirements:** Age 65+ OR permanent disability/invalidity preventing work; **5 years** legal residence; no language or integration test (presumed met or waived).
- **Timeline:** 5 years + processing.
- **Fee:** €1,000.

## Naturalization by Parliament — "exceptional merit" (Art. 19 CBN)

- **Category:** Discretionary, parliamentary.
- **Requirements:** Age 18+; **permanent residence permit** (no fixed residency duration); **exceptional merit in science, sport, or culture** that "contributes to Belgium's international influence"; for science, a **doctorate is required**. Must explain why declaration is unavailable.
- **Process:** File with Chamber of Representatives Naturalization Committee → committee report → Chamber vote → law published in *Moniteur belge / Belgisch Staatsblad*.
- **Timeline:** Undefined by law; in practice **12–36 months**.
- **Volume:** ~0.4% of all 2023 grants. **Effectively closed to ordinary applicants** — used for Olympic athletes, prominent academics, world-class artists.
- **Fee:** €1,000.

## Recovery of lost Belgian nationality (Art. 24 CBN — *déclaration de recouvrement*)

- **Category:** Re-acquisition.
- **Requirements:** Previously held Belgian nationality and lost it (other than by deprivation for serious offence); age 18+; **legal main residence in Belgium for 12 months** immediately preceding the declaration. No language/integration test.
- **Timeline:** 12 months residence + ~4 months processing.
- **Fee:** **Free** (the only exception to the €1,000 fee).
- **Note:** The 2008 dual-citizenship reform was **not retroactive** — Belgians who lost nationality by voluntarily acquiring a foreign one before 28 April 2008 must use this recovery procedure. CJEU caselaw (*Tjebbes*, *Wiener Landesregierung*) has nudged Belgian courts toward more generous recovery rulings since 2022–2024.

## Refugees and stateless persons

- **Recognized refugees:** Use the **standard 5-year or 10-year declaration**, but the **asylum-procedure waiting time counts** toward residence (restored July 2018). Effectively reduces lead time by 1–3 years.
- **Stateless persons:** Eligible for **parliamentary naturalization after 2 years** of legal residence (the only remaining "short" residence path in Art. 19). Or use the standard 5/10-year declaration.

## Children — automatic / attribution paths

- **Adoption** by a Belgian (Art. 9): automatic if adopter is Belgian at adoption.
- **Declaration of attribution by parent** (Art. 11bis): Foreign minor born in Belgium to a foreign parent — parent declares before child turns 12 if parent has 10 years' Belgian residence.
- **Declaration by child** (Art. 12): Foreign minor adopted by or born to a foreigner who later becomes Belgian — automatic if under 18 and living with the new-Belgian parent.

## Investment / citizenship-by-investment

- **Belgium has no CBI program.** Investor visas (professional card, self-employed residence) lead only to residence; the path to citizenship still runs through the 5- or 10-year declaration. No fast-track exists for investors.

## Military / service-based

- **No formal military-service path.** Distinguished military or civil service occasionally cited under Art. 19 "exceptional merit" but rarely used.

## Notable changes 2023–2026

- **2024:** CBN amended to waive the handwritten-declaration requirement for illiterate applicants.
- **18 July 2025 / effective 29 July 2025:** Programme law raised the registration fee from €150 to **€1,000**; annual indexation begins 1 Jan 2026. Triggered a rush of pre-deadline applications across Brussels and Antwerp communes.
- **January 2025 coalition agreement (De Wever government):** Committed to (a) mandatory **citizenship exam** (civics + language), (b) raising language requirement to **B1**, (c) further tightening of integration proofs. Implementation expected 2026–2028; not yet in force May 2026.
- **2022–2024 jurisprudence:** Brussels Family Court has expanded retroactive recovery rights for those who lost Belgian nationality pre-2008, aligning with CJEU *Tjebbes* / *Wiener Landesregierung* doctrine.

## Dual-citizenship policy

- Fully allowed since **28 April 2008** for all acquisition and retention scenarios. No renunciation required when becoming Belgian; no loss when a Belgian voluntarily acquires another nationality (post-2008). Pre-2008 losses are not automatically restored — recovery declaration required.

## Comparison summary

Ranked by total elapsed time and likelihood for a typical applicant with no Belgian ancestry:

| Rank | Path | Total time | Likelihood |
| ---- | ---- | ---------- | ---------- |
| 1 | Descent (Belgian parent) | At birth | High if eligible |
| 2 | Recovery of lost nationality | ~16 months | High if eligible |
| 3 | Stateless — parliamentary naturalization | ~2.5–3 years | High if stateless |
| 4 | Declaration via marriage (5 yr res + 3 yr marriage) | ~5.5 years | High (with Belgian spouse) |
| 5 | Declaration — 5-year track | ~5.5 years | High (with integration + work) |
| 6 | Declaration — 10-year track | ~10.5 years | High (no work history needed) |
| 7 | Parliamentary naturalization — exceptional merit | 1–3 years post-PR | Very low (~0.4%) |
| 8 | CBI / investor fast track | N/A | Closed |

**Fastest practical path for an outsider without ancestry or Belgian spouse:** the 5-year declaration (Art. 12bis §1, 2°) — ~5.5 years total, ~€1,000 fee, A2 language, dual citizenship retained.
