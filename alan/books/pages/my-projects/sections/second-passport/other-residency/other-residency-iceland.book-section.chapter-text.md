
Iceland is **not in the EU** but is bound by **EEA + EFTA + Schengen + Nordic Passport Union**. This creates a hard two-track system: **EEA/EFTA/Nordic citizens** (and Swiss) register their domicile and need no residence or work permit; **third-country nationals (TCNs)** including US citizens must hold a residence permit issued by Útlendingastofnun (Directorate of Immigration) before any stay beyond 90 Schengen days. There is **no golden visa / residency-by-investment** program.

## EEA / EFTA / Nordic track (no permit)

### Registration of domicile (EEA / EFTA / Swiss citizens)
EU, EEA, EFTA, and Swiss citizens may live and work in Iceland without a residence or work permit. Stays beyond 3 months require registration with Registers Iceland (Þjóðskrá) for an Icelandic ID (kennitala) and legal domicile, on one of four grounds: employment, study, self-sufficiency (own funds + health insurance), or family member of an EEA/EFTA citizen. Counts toward permanent residence and the 7-year naturalization clock.

### Nordic citizens
Citizens of Denmark, Norway, Sweden, Finland, and the Faroe Islands may move and reside freely under the Nordic Passport Union — registration, but no permit. Shortened citizenship clocks (see `citizenship/iceland.md`).

## Third-country residence permits

All TCN tracks below feed the permanent-residence and naturalization clocks unless explicitly noted. First-time issue typically takes **8–10 months**; renewals up to 90 days. Apply before entering Iceland or before existing permit expires.

### Residence permit based on work — expert knowledge (specialist)
For roles requiring **university-level education or recognised specialist training**. Employer-sponsored. Issued for up to **4 years** (raised from 2 under the 2023–2025 Foreign Nationals Act amendments). Spouse and children gain family-reunification rights **and** the spouse may work without a separate work permit. Path-to-citizenship: yes.

### Residence permit based on work — shortage of labour
Employer must show the role cannot be filled from the domestic or EEA/EFTA labour market (advertise + document). Initial 1-year permits, renewable. Family reunification rights added under the recent amendments. Path-to-citizenship: yes.

### Residence permit for athletes
For professional athletes engaged by an Icelandic club. Now issued for up to **2 years** (raised from 1). Path-to-citizenship: yes.

### Residence permit on the basis of a collaboration or service contract
For specialists posted under a service contract; same family-reunification expansion as the expert-knowledge track. Path-to-citizenship: yes (if continuously renewed).

### Residence permit for students
For non-EEA/EFTA students admitted to a recognised Icelandic university. Requires proof of admission, housing, and sufficient financial means. Part-time work allowed up to **15 hours/week**. Post-graduation extension: up to **3 years** to seek expert-knowledge employment (raised from 6 months). Family reunification now included for all students. Path-to-permanent-residence: yes; counts toward citizenship clock at full rate.

### Residence permit for family reunification
For spouses, registered cohabitants (≥ 12 months prior cohabitation), children under 18, and dependent parents 67+ of Icelandic citizens or holders of qualifying residence permits. Sponsor must show housing and sufficient income. Recent expansion: children born in Iceland may receive permits even when parents' permits don't normally carry family-reunification rights. Path-to-citizenship: yes.

### Residence permit for self-employed / business operation
There is **no general self-employment or entrepreneur visa**. Self-employment is possible only as an adjunct to an already-issued residence permit, or via a narrow business-operation permit requiring a registered Icelandic business with the Tax Authority, a viable business plan, sufficient capital, and evidence of real local value. In practice the Directorate expects an Icelandic employer to sponsor a work permit — the self-employed route is rarely granted to general freelancers / digital nomads.

### Residence permit for au pair
Non-EEA/EFTA only, ages **18–25**. Host family must have at least one adult with Icelandic citizenship or permanent residence and at least one child. Minimum pocket money **ISK 60,000 per 4 weeks**; food and a separate lockable room provided. Application fee **ISK 120,000**. Issued for max **1 year, non-renewable**. **Does not** count toward permanent residence; does **not** lead to citizenship.

### Residence permit for cultural / religious workers
For invited unpaid positions with a religious society or the Evangelical Lutheran Church of Iceland, and similar cultural-exchange categories. Limited duration; path-to-citizenship limited.

### Residence permit for humanitarian / subsidiary-protection grounds
Refugee and subsidiary-protection permits feed the **5-year** citizenship route (see citizenship file).

## Long-term remote-worker visa (digital nomad)

A **long-term visa** (not a residence permit) for non-EEA/EFTA nationals exempt from Schengen short-stay visa, working remotely for a non-Icelandic employer or for non-Icelandic clients via IT.

- **Income threshold:** **ISK 1,000,000 / month** (≈ €6,900 / US$8,130) single; **ISK 1,300,000 / month** with spouse or cohabitant — one of the highest digital-nomad thresholds globally.
- **Duration:** up to **6 months, non-renewable**. Stay cannot be extended in Iceland; the visa-holder must leave at the end.
- **Processing:** 3–4 weeks from complete application.
- **Path-to-residency / citizenship:** **No.** Does not count toward permanent residence or naturalization clocks.

## Investor / golden visa

**Iceland has no golden visa, residency-by-investment, or citizenship-by-investment program** as of May 2026. Investors must use a standard track — typically the business-operation permit (active company, not passive investment) or the expert-knowledge work permit.

## Permanent residence permit (ófrímerkt dvalarleyfi)

- **Eligibility:** 4 years' continuous residence on a permit category that qualifies as a basis for permanent residence (excludes au-pair, student during studies, remote-worker visa).
- **Absences:** total absences ≤ 90 days per year of permit. Stays > 18 months over the 4-year window require prior written permission.
- **Language:** complete a course in Icelandic for foreigners — minimum **150 lessons** at an accredited institution with ≥ 85% attendance, **or** pass an Icelandic-for-foreigners assessment test certified by the Ministry of Education and Children.
- **Means:** demonstrate self-sufficient lawful support throughout the qualifying period.
- **Status:** must apply while holding a valid current permit.
- After PR: no further renewals required; PR holders may travel and work freely. Naturalization remains 7 years' total domicile (4 with Nordic citizenship; reduced for spouse / cohabitant / refugee / stateless / restoration / child-of-citizen tracks).

## Recent changes (2024–2026)

- **Foreign Nationals Act amendments (2023–2025).** Expert-knowledge permits now up to 4 years (was 2). Athlete permits 2 years (was 1). Post-graduation job-search extension 3 years (was 6 months). Spouses of expert-knowledge holders may work without a separate work permit. Family reunification rights added to student, shortage-of-labour, and service-contract permits.
- **2026 Directorate workflow.** Digital filing rolled out; first-residence processing still reported at 8–10 months under volume pressure. Expedited work-based processing cannot complete in under 8–10 weeks.
- **Long-term remote-work visa.** Income thresholds unchanged from 2020 launch (ISK 1m / 1.3m); no path-to-PR added.
