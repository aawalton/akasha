
Liechtenstein is in EFTA, EEA, and Schengen but not the EU. Despite EEA free-movement rules, Liechtenstein negotiated a permanent sectoral adaptation (EEA Annex VIII) allowing it to cap residence permits by annual quota even for EEA citizens — the only EEA state to do so. The result is the most quota-constrained immigration system in Europe by a wide margin: roughly 89 annual EEA permits, 17 Swiss permits, and a discretionary handful for third-country nationals, against ~40,000 residents. Half of EEA permits are awarded by lottery. There is no Liechtenstein "Golden Visa" or formal investor program.

## EEA framework

### EEA residence permit (Aufenthaltsbewilligung B)

Annual quota for EEA/EU nationals: 72 permits for gainful employment + 16 for residence without employment ("residence without work") + 5 short-stay permits — roughly 89 EEA-track permits total per year. Each pool is split 50/50 between two lottery draws (spring + autumn) and direct government allocation based on economic interest. Lottery applicants apply via the Migration and Passport Office (Ausländer- und Passamt); winners must take up the permit within 6 months. EEA citizens have a right to apply but no right to the permit — the quota and lottery override free movement under the EEA sectoral adaptation.

### Swiss reciprocal arrangement

Swiss citizens get a parallel but smaller quota: 12 employment + 5 residence-without-work + short-stay slots — ~17 permits per year — allocated by government decision (no lottery). Swiss citizens who keep their Swiss residence may work in Liechtenstein without a Liechtenstein permit (cross-border arrangement) under a separate bilateral track.

### Third-country nationals

No standing quota for non-EEA, non-Swiss citizens. Permits are granted only by discretionary government decision, must serve a Liechtenstein public interest (highly-skilled workers, key investors, exceptional cases), and are rare — typically a small single-digit number per year. A US citizen with no Liechtenstein connection has effectively no standard third-country residence track.

## Residence-without-work (independent means)

The 16-permit EEA "residence without work" pool is the closest thing Liechtenstein has to an independent-means / wealthy-individual track. Requires EEA citizenship (US citizens cannot apply directly — only by holding an EEA passport first), proof of stable independent income of roughly CHF 100,000+/year from sources outside Liechtenstein, secured Liechtenstein housing, full private health insurance, clean record, and a commitment not to take gainful employment in Liechtenstein. Tax residence in Liechtenstein follows. Subject to the same lottery/government-allocation split as the employment pool. No equivalent third-country path.

## Work permits

### Employer-sponsored employment permit

Embedded in the 72-permit EEA employment quota above. Requires a job offer from a Liechtenstein employer, employer sponsorship through the quota, and either lottery draw or direct government allocation. EEA labor-market test is light; quota is the binding constraint. Third-country nationals require additional government approval and are extremely rare outside specialist roles.

### Cross-border worker permit (Grenzgängerbewilligung)

The dominant labor pattern: roughly half of Liechtenstein's workforce commutes daily from Austria, Switzerland, or Germany. EEA cross-border workers obtain a Grenzgängerbewilligung via employer registration — quota-light and far easier than the residence permit. Swiss citizens resident in Switzerland need no permit at all to work in Liechtenstein. Cross-border status confers no Liechtenstein residence rights and does not count toward naturalization residence.

## Family reunification

Spouses and minor children of Liechtenstein residents (citizens, settlement-permit holders, EEA permit-holders meeting income and housing thresholds) may join through family reunification, outside the main quota lanes. Adequate housing, financial means, and health insurance required. EEA family members of EEA permit-holders enjoy stronger derived rights than third-country family members of third-country residents.

## Settlement permit (Niederlassungsbewilligung C)

The permanent-residence tier. EEA citizens may apply after 5 years of continuous lawful residence on a B permit; third-country nationals typically require 10 years (and a Liechtenstein integration / "citizenship test"-style assessment). Confers indefinite residence without annual renewal, subject to continued good conduct and non-reliance on welfare. Holders may switch employers and reside freely. Settlement is not citizenship — the 30-year facilitated-naturalization clock runs independently.

## Student permits

Limited; Liechtenstein has only the University of Liechtenstein and Private University in Triesen. Permits are issued for the duration of studies, do not count toward naturalization residence on the same terms as B permits, and do not convert automatically to work permits at graduation — graduates re-enter the standard quota lanes.

## Digital nomad

No formal digital-nomad visa. Remote workers must use one of the standard tracks (residence-without-work for high earners with EEA citizenship; cross-border commuting from Austria/Switzerland; or — rarely — discretionary third-country approval).

## Investor / Golden Visa

None. Liechtenstein has no citizenship-by-investment program and no residence-by-investment program. Online listings advertising "Liechtenstein CBI" or "golden visa" are misleading or refer to the residence-without-work pool (EEA-only, lottery-constrained, not investment-based).

## Path from residence to citizenship

Naturalization is the bottleneck, not residence. After 5 years of B-permit residence an EEA citizen may obtain settlement (C) — but ordinary naturalization still requires a 30-year wait via the facilitated route (years under age 20 count double), or 10 years via the communal-vote route that almost never approves applicants who have not lived in the commune for 30 years. The only sub-30-year naturalization paths are marriage to a Liechtensteiner and being born/raised in Liechtenstein — both detailed in the citizenship file. Renunciation of prior nationality is required at naturalization.

## Recent changes (2024–2026)

No quota or framework changes in this window. The EEA sectoral adaptation remains in force; the lottery continues to run twice yearly (spring + autumn). The 2020 dual-citizenship referendum result still blocks reform on the naturalization side. Austria's December 2025 cross-border worker permit creates a new third-country path *into Austria* for residents of bordering states but does not change Liechtenstein's own rules.
