
# Hungarian Citizenship Paths

Hungary's citizenship regime is governed by Act LV of 1993 on Hungarian Citizenship (1993. évi LV. törvény), materially expanded by Act XLIV of 2010 — the Fidesz government's flagship "simplified naturalization" amendment, in force 1 January 2011 — which grants citizenship to descendants of pre-1920 Hungarian citizens who speak Hungarian, with no residency requirement. Over 1.1 million people, mostly ethnic Hungarians in Romania, Serbia, Ukraine, and Slovakia, have been naturalized via this path; the politically charged "outsourcing of citizenship" remains the defining feature of Hungary's regime. Hungary fully allows dual/multiple citizenship; no renunciation required for any path. For the 2024 Guest Investor Program (residency-by-investment, not CBI), see [residency/hungary.md](../residency/hungary.book-chapter.md).

## Jus Soli — Anti-Statelessness Only (Act LV §3(3))

- **Category**: Birth on territory (residual, anti-statelessness only).
- **Eligibility**: (a) Foundling found in Hungary with unknown parents, OR (b) child born in Hungary to two stateless parents both domiciled (lakóhely) in Hungary. Pure birth on territory to foreign parents does **not** confer citizenship. Children born in Hungary to non-stateless foreign parents who fail to transmit nationality are **not** covered — a known gap criticized by the European Network on Statelessness.
- **Timeline**: At birth (automatic presumption).
- **Backlog**: N/A.
- **Dual citizenship**: Allowed.

## Jus Sanguinis — At Birth (Act LV §3(1))

- **Category**: Descent, automatic.
- **Eligibility**: At least one parent was a Hungarian citizen at the time of birth. Applies regardless of birthplace, no generational limit through citizen parents. Establishment of citizenship requires registration with the Hungarian birth registry (anyakönyv) via a consulate.
- **Timeline**: At birth; consular birth registration typically 3–9 months.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: 6–12 months at most consulates; longer (12–18 months) at high-volume posts (NY, LA, Toronto, Tel Aviv).
- **Dual citizenship**: Allowed.

## Simplified Naturalization — Ethnic Hungarians (Act LV §4(3), expanded by Act XLIV of 2010)

The dominant diaspora path. The single most-used route to a Hungarian passport since 2011.

- **Category**: Preferential naturalization (egyszerűsített honosítás) for descendants of Hungarian citizens or persons of Hungarian origin.
- **Eligibility** (all required):
  - **Ancestry**: Applicant descends from a person who was a Hungarian citizen — most commonly a pre-1920 Kingdom of Hungary citizen (i.e., from the Austro-Hungarian-era territories including Transylvania, Vojvodina, Sub-Carpathia, Upper Hungary/Slovakia, Burgenland, Međimurje, Prekmurje), or a Hungarian citizen during 1941–1945. **No generation limit.** Alternatively, applicant can substantiate Hungarian origin (Hungarian-language schooling, ethnic-Hungarian church/civil records, parental declaration).
  - **Language**: Demonstrate Hungarian proficiency at the consular interview — conversational competence, no formal certificate or B2/C1 level required; far below the standard-naturalization bar.
  - **Clean criminal record** (Hungary and home country).
  - **No threat to public order or national security**.
- **Not required**: residency in Hungary, livelihood in Hungary, place of abode in Hungary, constitutional-knowledge exam, renunciation of prior nationality.
- **Documents**: Applicant's birth/marriage certificates, full chain to the Hungarian-citizen ancestor, ancestor's Hungarian birth/baptism/military/citizenship records (anyakönyvi kivonat, illetőség records, magyar honosság igazolás).
- **Where filed**: Hungarian consulate, embassy, or any Hungarian government office; oath taken at consulate or town hall.
- **Timeline**: Total 12–24 months (document gathering 3–12 months; Ministry of Interior decision 6–12 months; oath scheduling 1–3 months).
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: Ministry of Interior (Belügyminisztérium) decision typically 8–14 months after consular submission; North American consulates 9–18 months end-to-end. Volume has tapered from the 2011–2015 peak but remains the largest naturalization channel.
- **Dual citizenship**: Allowed; no renunciation. (Slovakia historically stripped Slovak citizenship from Slovak-Hungarians who acquired Hungarian citizenship — that is Slovak law, not Hungarian.)
- **Sources**: Act XLIV of 2010; 1993. évi LV. törvény §4(3); MFA simplified-naturalization pages.

## Re-acquisition by Declaration — Loss 1947–1990 (Act LV §5/A)

- **Category**: Declaration (nyilatkozat) — fastest formal path for those who lost citizenship under communist-era emigration laws.
- **Eligibility**: Person who lost Hungarian citizenship by emigration/expatriation between 15 September 1947 and 2 May 1990, **and** their descendants. No language test, no residency, no exam.
- **Timeline**: 3–9 months from declaration to certificate.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: 4–8 months at MoI; consular intake adds 1–3 months.
- **Dual citizenship**: Allowed.

## Standard Naturalization — 8 Years Residency (Act LV §4(1))

- **Category**: Honosítás — general route for unrelated foreign nationals.
- **Eligibility** (all required):
  - 8 years continuous lawful residence in Hungary on a Hungarian address card (lakcímkártya).
  - Clean criminal record in Hungary.
  - Stable livelihood and place of abode in Hungary.
  - Naturalization not prejudicial to public order / national security.
  - **Pass the Hungarian constitutional / civics exam** (alkotmányos alapismeretek vizsga) in Hungarian — 40 topics, half culture/history, half constitutional law; requires roughly B2-level Hungarian.
  - Exemptions from the exam: age 60+, permanent disability, dependence preventing study, attainment of a Hungarian-language diploma (secondary or higher).
- **Renunciation**: Not required.
- **Timeline**: 8 years residency + 12–24 months processing = ~9–10 years total.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: MoI decision 14–22 months from filing; demand modest (~3,000–5,000 grants/year through this channel).
- **Dual citizenship**: Allowed.

## Reduced Naturalization — 5 Years (Act LV §4(2))

- **Category**: Preferential — reduced residency for closer ties.
- **Eligibility**: Same as standard, but residency reduced to 5 years if applicant: (a) was born in Hungary, OR (b) established Hungarian residency before age 18, OR (c) is stateless. Civics exam and language still required (with the same exemptions).
- **Timeline**: 5 years residency + 12–24 months processing.
- **Backlog**: Same as standard.
- **Dual citizenship**: Allowed.

## Reduced Naturalization — 3 Years (Act LV §4(2))

- **Category**: Preferential — strongest family/protection ties.
- **Eligibility** (residency 3 years continuous, plus all standard criteria):
  - Spouse of a Hungarian citizen, **and** the marriage has subsisted for at least 3 years (or the spouse died). Cohabitation in Hungary required.
  - Parent of a Hungarian-citizen minor (under 18) residing with the parent in Hungary.
  - Person adopted by a Hungarian citizen.
  - **Recognized refugee** under Hungarian law.
  - **Recognized stateless person** under Hungarian law.
  - Person whose Hungarian citizenship was previously withdrawn but is not eligible for declaration-based re-acquisition.
- Civics exam and language still required (subject to standard exemptions).
- **Timeline**: 3 years residency + 12–24 months processing = ~4–5 years total.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: Similar to standard, 14–22 months at MoI.
- **Dual citizenship**: Allowed.

## Naturalization by Marriage Living Abroad (Act LV §4(3))

- **Category**: Simplified naturalization by marriage — no Hungarian residency.
- **Eligibility**: Married to a Hungarian citizen and (a) marriage has lasted 10+ years, OR (b) marriage has lasted 5+ years and the couple has a common child. Hungarian language proficiency required (consular-interview standard). Clean criminal record, no security risk.
- **Timeline**: Marriage tenure + 12–18 months processing.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: 9–15 months at MoI.
- **Dual citizenship**: Allowed.

## Exceptional / Discretionary Naturalization (Act LV §4(7))

- **Category**: Presidential-discretion grant for overriding national interest.
- **Eligibility**: Minister of Interior proposes, President of the Republic decides. Standard requirements (residency, language, civics, livelihood) may be waived in any combination when the naturalization is of overriding interest to Hungary — typically distinguished economic, scientific, cultural, sporting, or diplomatic contribution. The recipient still must have a clean criminal record and pose no security risk. (Used historically for elite athletes, e.g., handballers and fencers, and select cultural figures.)
- **Timeline**: 6–18 months; no public quota, opaque.
- **Backlog**: Case-by-case; not a route an applicant can rely on.
- **Dual citizenship**: Allowed.

## Refugee / Stateless Paths

- Recognized **refugees** and recognized **stateless persons** qualify for 3-year reduced naturalization (above). They additionally benefit from waivers on documentary requirements when home-country documents are unobtainable. Civics exam and language still required (with statutory exemptions for older applicants and those with Hungarian-language education).

## Comparison Summary

Ranked by realistic total time to passport, May 2026:

1. **Jus sanguinis at birth** — instant, consular registration 6–12 months. Trivial if a parent is Hungarian.
2. **Re-acquisition by declaration (1947–1990 emigrants and descendants)** — 4–9 months. Narrow eligibility but very fast.
3. **Simplified naturalization (ethnic Hungarians, Act XLIV of 2010)** — 12–24 months. **By far the highest-volume and most accessible path** for anyone with a pre-1920 Kingdom-of-Hungary ancestor and conversational Hungarian. No residency, no civics exam.
4. **Marriage abroad** — depends on marriage tenure (5 or 10 years); processing 9–15 months.
5. **3-year reduced naturalization** (spouse-in-HU / refugee / stateless / parent of HU minor) — ~4–5 years.
6. **5-year reduced naturalization** (born in HU / minor-age residency / stateless) — ~6–7 years.
7. **Standard 8-year naturalization** — ~9–10 years. Heavy: B2 Hungarian, 40-topic civics exam in Hungarian.
8. **Guest Investor Program → naturalization** — ~8–10 years, EUR 250k–1M plus standard naturalization burdens. See [residency/hungary.md](../residency/hungary.book-chapter.md).
9. **Exceptional / discretionary (Presidential)** — fastest in absolute terms but not a route an ordinary applicant can pursue.

Effectively closed / nonexistent: pure jus soli (anti-statelessness only); direct citizenship-by-investment (does not exist — the 2013–2017 residency-bond scheme was citizenship-adjacent but the 2024 Guest Investor Program is strictly residency).

Flag-worthy: the simplified-naturalization path remains the politically dominant feature of Hungarian citizenship and is the obvious route for anyone with documentable Kingdom-of-Hungary ancestry plus working Hungarian.
