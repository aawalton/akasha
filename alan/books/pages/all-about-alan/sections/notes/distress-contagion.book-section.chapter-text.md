
# Distress contagion — a conditioned threat forecast

> Distress contagion as a conditioned threat forecast — others' distress cues fire Alan's own distress immediately, not as transmitted empathy but as an operantly conditioned prediction that his own distress is coming; intermittently reinforced before age six, extinction-resistant, running at two levels (a person-level Bayesian threat estimate plus an independent cue-level reflex).

When people around Alan are distressed, his nervous system picks up on it immediately and gets distressed. The standard reading of that response — affective empathy, her feeling arriving in him — is not what is happening. The mechanism is operant conditioning. Verbatim: "I have learned at a nervous system level that signs of distress in others are likely to soon cause distress in me, so the distress kicks in immediately."

The signal is a threat forecast about *his* near future, not a transmitted copy of the other person's feeling. A distress cue in someone nearby has historically preceded distress in him, so the cue now fires his distress directly — the way any conditioned threat cue fires before the threat lands.

## The forecast explains the freeze

The reframe accounts for the behavior the empathy reading cannot. A care response approaches the distressed person; a threat response freezes. Alan freezes: he goes still and quiet and focuses on regulating himself — triage, not absence — which is "not at all what Jen needs in those moments." His system responds to her distress as an incoming threat to him, because that is what the signal is: a forecast, not a shared feeling. The behavioral consequence for the emotional-support component of love's bundle is treated in [love-decomposition.md → the walk](love-decomposition.book-chapter.md#sorting-the-components--the-walk).

## The alarm has no payload

Detection works — he feels the distress immediately. But the signal carries no content about the other person's interior: no why, no cause, no how-to-relieve. That emptiness is consistent with the conditioning reading. A threat forecast only has to predict *his* distress; it has no reason to encode hers. An alarm is not a model of the fire.

## Intermittently reinforced, learned before six

He cannot trace the training experiences: "by the time I was six it was already in place." Whatever the schedule was, it was intermittent, and that makes the conditioning stronger, not weaker. Verbatim: "Even worse than reliably, it unreliably results in my distress, which creates a stronger operant conditioning distress signal."

That is a variable-ratio schedule — the extinction-resistant kind. Safe exposures don't update it, because the schedule the reflex was trained on already includes long safe runs; a stretch of distress cues with no consequence looks exactly like training data. This stacks with the [aphantasia extinction asymmetry](trauma-encoding.book-chapter.md): no memory replay means no self-exposure between live encounters, and the variable-ratio schedule means even live safe encounters are weak evidence. Two independent mechanisms point the same way — the reflex stays fresh.

## Two levels — an estimate and a reflex

The conditioning operates at two levels, and they dissociate.

- **Level 1 — person-level.** A threat estimate over interactions with a specific person, behaving like a Bayesian updater approximating a variable rate. The denominator is interactions in general, not just the distressing ones; the estimate decays when the trigger rate runs low and jumps on a new trigger.
- **Level 2 — cue-level.** The distress-cue → distress reflex, independent of the person-level estimate. Verbatim: "even when our interactions haven't been distressing for long enough that I forget, when she is in distress, I still get distressed."

The dissociation is what forces two levels: the cue-level reflex fires even when the person-level estimate has decayed to low. Calm months lower his expectation of distress *from Jen*; they do not touch the reflex that fires *at her distress cues*.

## Revision to the empathy-paradox reading

[empathy-alexithymia-paradox.md](empathy-alexithymia-paradox.book-chapter.md) records increased in-moment empathy alongside total alexithymia, resolved as: the in-moment nervous-system response to others' emotions is intact and amplified. The hardware observation stands. For distress cues specifically, this note revises the interpretation: the in-moment response is real and immediate, but it is self-referential — a conditioned forecast of his own incoming distress — not other-modeling. That is why it produces freeze-and-regulate rather than approach-and-tend, and why it arrives with no information about the other person to act on.
