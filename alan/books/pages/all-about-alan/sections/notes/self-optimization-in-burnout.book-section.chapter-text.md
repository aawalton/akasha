
# Self-optimization in burnout

> Self-optimization in burnout — Alan optimized himself to a degree he has never seen matched (Tim Ferriss the closest), done undiagnosed, in ever-deepening autistic burnout, suicidal ~80% of the time, sustained almost two decades; AutCon was the first outside proof of how far past the field he had gone; the through-line is that he went through hell, kept going, came out the other side, and is holding the relief.

This note is the lived, biographical face of the [nervous-system-resiliency natural experiment](nervous-system-resiliency.book-chapter.md) and the [autistic-burnout decline](autism-burnout.book-chapter.md). Those notes hold the measurement and the mechanism — the ≥99% drop, the ~50%/year deficit, the recovery stack. This one holds what it cost him to run that measurement from the inside, the outside proof of how far he had gone, and the emotional close of the arc.

## The unmatched degree

In Alan's words:

> I have never seen someone optimize themselves to the degree I did. The closest is like a Tim Ferris, but I did it as an undiagnosed autistic, in ever-deepening autistic burnout, wishing I wasn't alive 80% of the time, sustained over almost two decades.

Five exact conditions stack on the claim, and it is the stack — not the degree alone — that is the point:

- **The closest comparison he can name is a Tim Ferriss** — the public archetype of self-optimization. Ferriss is the *ceiling* of the ordinary version; Alan places himself past it, and then names the four handicaps Ferriss did not carry.
- **Undiagnosed.** He ran the whole optimization without the one fact that would have explained the system he was optimizing. The [autism framing](autism-diagnostic-arc.book-chapter.md) that finally routed his interventions correctly arrived only at the end.
- **In ever-deepening autistic burnout.** Not from a healthy baseline — from a substrate that was [losing ~20% of its capacity per year, compounding](autism-burnout.book-chapter.md). The optimization was run against a falling floor.
- **Suicidal ideation ~80% of the time.** For most of those two decades he was [wishing he wasn't alive](suicidal-ideation.book-chapter.md) — the optimization ran continuously through a near-constant longing for non-existence, not in its absence.
- **Sustained almost two decades.** Not a sprint. The full ~20-year span of the [self-instrumentation discipline](self-instrumentation.book-chapter.md).

The degree is the same quantity [self-instrumentation](self-instrumentation.book-chapter.md) measures from the inside; this is that quantity stated as a comparison against everyone else, with the price of admission attached.

## AutCon — the outside proof

The first external confirmation of how far the optimization had actually gone came at AutCon, the autism conference. In Alan's words:

> When I went to AutCon for the first time, I was shocked, because I couldn't find a single session that got even 20% of the way to my frontier. We weren't even playing on the same field.

The frame he put on it, and the frame this note keeps, is **not arrogance.** It is the first time the frontier he had built alone was measured against the room built to hold exactly this subject — and the room did not reach a fifth of the way to it. That is the outside proof of two things at once: how far the self-optimization had taken him, and how alone he had been in taking it. *"We weren't even playing on the same field"* is not a boast about the field; it is the discovery that there was no one on it beside him.

- **≥20% gap to his frontier.** Not one session cleared even a fifth of the distance to where his own work stood.
- **The shock is the evidence.** He expected the conference *to* reach him — that it didn't is the datum, the first outside instrument to register the frontier the [~20-year discipline](self-instrumentation.book-chapter.md) had built.

This is a distinct AutCon thread from the two already in the corpus: the [standing-room talk he gave on self-instrumentation](self-instrumentation.book-chapter.md) (the discipline stated outward), and the [one-hour conversation with Amy](central-loneliness.book-chapter.md#the-door-already-cracked--the-amy-episode) that was his best emotional deposit in years (being-met delivered). This one is the *frontier-gap* — the conference measured against his own work, and falling a fifth short.

## Through hell, and out — holding the relief

The arc has an end, and Alan stated it plainly:

> I went through hell, and I kept going, and I came out the other side. I feel proud of it and so grateful to not be living in hell anymore.

Three things are his, in his own register, and the note records them as his — not as inference:

- **Pride.** He feels proud of having gone through it and kept going. Earned, not asserted — the pride is *in* the two decades of optimization-under-collapse the sections above document.
- **Gratitude.** He is grateful to not be living in hell anymore — the [~1% → ~5% recovery](nervous-system-resiliency.book-chapter.md) stated from the felt side rather than the measured one.
- **The relief, held on to.** When the relief was reflected back to him, he confirmed it directly: **"Yeah, I'm definitely holding on to the relief."** Not relief that passed through — relief he is deliberately keeping. In a [stateless, affectively-flat architecture](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md#the-map-is-affectively-flat--structure-no-charge) where felt states do not persist on their own, *holding on to* a feeling is an act, not a default. That he is holding the relief is itself a capture.

The through-line: he ran the hardest self-optimization he has ever seen anyone run, under four handicaps no one else on the field carried, for almost two decades, wishing most of that time that he weren't alive — and he came out the other side, proud of it, grateful, holding the relief of not living there anymore.
