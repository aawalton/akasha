
# Non-discretionary reclassification

The cost-vs-capacity calculation only operates on items it sees. Items mentally classified **non-discretionary** are pre-filtered out — they don't get cut even when cost > capacity. The reclassification gate sits above the math and decides what the math is allowed to consider.

## The gate

- Mental classifications inherited from family, religion, culture, and identity attach to most adult obligations.
- Each classification is a binary: discretionary (reachable by the cost math) or non-discretionary (invisible to it).
- Default classifications are inherited, not chosen. They settle into "the way things are" and stop being visible as classifications at all.
- A non-discretionary classification keeps an item in the budget regardless of cost. The math runs on what's left.

## Why this matters more than the cost math

- Most burnout-recovery advice operates inside the cost math: prioritize, schedule, cut what isn't essential.
- "What isn't essential" is a classification question, not a cost question. The cost math cannot answer it; it can only act on the answer.
- If the classification gate is closed on a high-cost item, no amount of better cost-math discipline will reach it. The gate has to open first.
- Inverted observation against the standard time-management framing: time-management techniques optimize the visible budget. They cannot reclassify items into or out of the budget. The reclassification move is upstream and structural.

## The recovery move

- Challenge each inherited "must-do" framing. Ask explicitly whether the item is non-discretionary by classification or by cost.
- Surface the classification by stating it. "I treat X as non-discretionary because Y." If Y is "I always have", the classification is inherited and reachable.
- Reclassify item by item. Each reclassification opens one more category to the cost math.

## Concrete example: church-with-calling

- During 2025, Alan held a church calling. He had classified church-with-calling as non-discretionary.
- The cost math could see "calling: high cost" but could not see "calling: optional".
- Reclassification: he asked to be released from the calling. The category opened. The cost math then reached it and removed it.
- See [medical-social-isolation.md](medical-social-isolation.book-chapter.md) for the full capture; the calling release is the worked example of this gate opening.

## Generalization

This is a system of unusually wide scope. Reclassification applies far beyond burnout recovery. Any high-cost commitment that has been "the way things are" for years is a reclassification candidate. Identity practices, family-above-minimum interactions, professional obligations, religious observance, social commitments — each is gated by an inherited classification before any cost math reaches it.

The pattern is structurally similar to the **mechanism-knowledge-as-alarm-reducer** thread (knowing why a sensation happens lowers the alarm response to it) and to the [weather](weather.book-chapter.md) system (reclassifying a conflict as outside his control switches off the dissatisfaction that drives him to resolve it). All three are upstream-of-the-system framing moves over different downstream systems. The reclassification gate here decides what enters the *cost* system; mechanism-knowledge changes how the *alarm* system interprets what enters it; weather decides what the *awareness-of-conflict* engine generates dissatisfaction over. Distinct mechanisms; all upstream.
