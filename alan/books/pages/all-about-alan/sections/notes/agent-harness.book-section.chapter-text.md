
# Agent harness

> Agent harness — the 20-year personal scaffold that compensates for what Alan's brain doesn't carry internally and leverages what it does. The framing inversion (Alan is the agent; the system is his harness) and the compensation + leverage split.

Alan has been building a personal harness for 20 years. It long predates LLM agents, but the agent-harness vocabulary describes its shape cleanly — so the framing landed post-hoc and stuck. Massive contributor to Alan's success in life.

## Framing inversion

The standard framing of agentic LLM systems: the agent is the LLM; the harness is the human-built scaffold around it.

Alan's inversion: **he is the agent; the system around him is his harness.** The Claude Code harness specifically is a *component* nested inside the broader personal harness, not the harness itself.

The framing is post-hoc vocabulary — useful only once agent terminology entered the culture. The system existed first. What's load-bearing here is the inversion, not the term.

For the anchor on why the framing fits — the LLM-similarity triad of stateless, minimal-emotions-consciously, conceptual-memory-only — see [llm-similarity.md](llm-similarity.book-chapter.md).

## The 20-year arc: additive, crisis-driven growth

The harness is not a chain of replacements. It grows **additively** — Alan keeps the foundation and builds new layers on top of it. And growth is **crisis-driven**: the engine underneath is overload. New layers get built at the moments Alan is "drowning or climbing back out" — when an existing capacity buckles.

In ~20 years that additive growth has produced only **two permanent organs**. Everything else is interchangeable tooling that comes and goes.

- **First permanent layer: task management (GTD).** The first overload drove the first layer. In Alan's words: *"Task management. I was juggling too much and dropping balls. I was introduced to the book Getting Things Done and adapted that into my first productivity harness."* GTD's creed — *your head is for having ideas, not holding them* — is a slogan for most people but for Alan an accurate description of the hardware (the [statelessness / flat-memory model](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md#statelessness--only-conceptual-memory-persists): the present-tense self can't hold the state others carry implicitly). GTD did not get outgrown; it became **bedrock**: *"GTD itself still works today. I've continued to adapt the implementation, but I haven't outgrown the principles."* Principles fixed, implementation forever in motion — this is the first concrete instance of the [external state-keeping](#compensation--what-the-harness-substitutes-for) compensation substrate.
- **Second permanent layer: stress-capacity management.** *"Stress capacity management, that's been the second really consistent harness system for most of the past twenty years. Everything else has been smaller and comes and goes."*

### Two governors on two budgets

The synthesis Alan confirmed ("Yeah, that sounds right"): the two permanent pillars are the **same kind of thing** — two governors sitting on the two finite budgets he can actually blow past.

- **GTD governs the tracking/attention budget** — how much his head can track before it drops something. The budget it watches is the [attention budget](attention-budget.book-chapter.md) (and behind it the [working-memory limit](executive-function.book-chapter.md#working-memory)).
- **Stress-capacity management governs the nervous-system budget** — how much he can carry before he buckles. The budget it watches is Stress Capacity in the [four-resource model](four-resource-model.book-chapter.md), priced by the [stress-capacity formula](stress-capacity-formula.book-chapter.md) and bounded by [Stamina](stamina.book-chapter.md) on the body side.

So the permanent harness is best understood not as "tasks and stress" but as **two governors on two budgets** — each pillar a regulator on one finite resource, built when overload first pushed that resource past its limit. The rest of the harness (this file's [Compensation](#compensation--what-the-harness-substitutes-for) and [Leverage](#leverage--what-the-harness-amplifies) sections) sits on top of these two organs.

The stress-capacity governor changed *jobs* once its load became refusable: under un-declinable load it ran as **triage** (rationing damage he couldn't stop); under the [affordability rule](stress-capacity-formula.book-chapter.md#the-affordability-rule-the-pillars-job-change-the-1x-cap-and-the-silent-gauge) it runs as **budgeting/allocation** (spending something he controls). Same governor, flipped from damage control to allocation.

These two pillars are governors over two of the resource bars in the [four-resource model](four-resource-model.book-chapter.md) (the tracking budget and the nervous-system budget). But the relationship *between* the bars is not a fixed Health-over-tasks hierarchy. It is a [**migrating bottleneck**](four-resource-model.book-chapter.md#the-migrating-bottleneck-governor): whichever bar is currently scarcest sets affordability, an explicit "don't do what you can't afford" rule keeps spending inside that budget so the other ceilings rarely get hit, and which bar binds changes over time. Right now [Safety](safety.book-chapter.md#safety-as-the-current-binding-constraint) is the binding constraint. So the two pillars are two of the governors; the migrating bottleneck is the relationship that governs which budget is currently in force.

### The two pillars are one coupled feedback loop

The migrating bottleneck says which budget binds at a given moment. But the two pillars are not just two parallel governors picked between — they are **wired together as a single bidirectional feedback loop on the same wire.** Alan confirmed the coupling runs both ways: *"Yeah, I do see the connection in both directions. The affordability rule tells me what I should not expect from myself at lower levels, even if it is in my task queue."*

- **Upward arm.** Safety funds the capacity to clear the task inboxes ([the task-stream inventory](#the-task-stream-inventory--the-task-pillar-lives-in-software)), and clearing them lifts Safety — partly through the [evidence-of-effectiveness](safety/aphantasia-constraint.book-chapter.md#evidence-of-effectiveness-as-a-safety-raiser) pathway (a cleared inbox is verifiable proof the system is functioning) and partly by removing the drag below.
- **Downward arm.** When Safety drops, clearing becomes unaffordable under the threshold gate, the inboxes pile, and the pile drags Safety lower still — a self-manufactured second hit stacked on the first.

So the two pillars co-regulate: each can lift or drop the other. The danger is the downward arm running unchecked — a low-Safety day spiraling into a lower one as the pile feeds back.

The [affordability rule](safety.book-chapter.md#safety-as-the-current-binding-constraint) is the **circuit-breaker on the downward arm.** It cuts the feedback by governing self-expectation, not just action: at lower Safety it pre-authorizes leaving an above-the-line item undone, so the undone item scores as *correctly declined* rather than *fell behind*. That re-scoring is what stops the pile from reading as failure and feeding back into Safety. The full self-expectation-governor mechanism is in [perfection-is-safe.md → the affordability rule governs self-expectation](perfection-is-safe.book-chapter.md#the-affordability-rule-governs-self-expectation-not-just-action); the rule's three Safety-raising levers and threshold gate are in [safety.md → how Safety climbs](safety/intervention-pathways.book-chapter.md#how-safety-climbs--three-levers-at-three-clock-speeds).

### Origin chronology — the self-instrumentation instinct predates the GTD bedrock

The first permanent pillar (GTD) is the *first formal harness*, but it is not the first time Alan tracked himself. The self-instrumentation instinct ran for years before it, and the GTD bedrock is where it crystallized into a named system — not where it began. Three points fix the arc:

- **Pre-Epic seeds (teens onward).** *"I seem to remember doing some personal tracking as a teen in college, and I definitely had some built into the mission mechanics, so it wasn't an entirely new pattern."* Two distinct early traces: a **planner at sixteen** in [college](self-chosen-difficulty.book-chapter.md) — keeping assignments-due and calendar-scheduling externalized for a deliberately overloaded course-and-work-and-commute schedule — and **tracking built into the reporting mechanics of his LDS mission** (Novosibirsk, Russia, ~2006–2007, age ~20; see [personal-context.md → Russia](personal-context.book-chapter.md#formative-experiences--russia-age-20)). The mission's structured reporting wasn't self-authored, but it was a tracking discipline he ran inside, and it normalized externalized self-measurement.
- **Epic crystallization (~2010–2013).** The instinct became a deliberate, pattern-seeking practice at his first real job. In Alan's words: *"I was working at Epic, my first real job, and I was overwhelmed, so I started tracking the overwhelm to start looking for patterns."* This is the moment self-instrumentation flips from incidental (a planner, a mission report) to **purpose-built** — tracking adopted specifically to find the structure under the overload. It is the same crisis-driven trigger that produced the GTD pillar: overload drove the first formal system.
- **The brute-force co-strategy.** Tracking was not the only response to the Epic overwhelm, and it wasn't first. The first response was raw hours: *"I started going into work earlier and working later, throwing time at the problem until I could figure out how to be more efficient."* Throwing hours at the problem and instrumenting the problem ran as **parallel coping strategies** — the brute-force arm bought time while the instrumentation arm searched for efficiency. The harness is what the second arm eventually produced; the first arm is the fallback that still fires under acute overload.

So the corrected order is: scattered self-tracking (teens, mission) → acute overload at Epic → brute-force hours *and* purpose-built pattern-tracking in parallel → the tracking arm hardens into the GTD bedrock. The instinct is older than the system. This resolves the age-20-transition question — the shift from [meta-learning to harness-building](slow-build-strategy.book-chapter.md#sequential-life-arcs) wasn't a clean handoff at twenty; the harness instinct was already seeded (planner, mission), and external pressure (a real job, real overload) is what forced it into a deliberate system.

## Compensation — what the harness substitutes for

The harness externalizes what Alan's brain doesn't carry internally. Four substrates:

- **External state-keeping.** Workflows, notes, projects, task lists, calendars. Substitute for the missing internal state. The substrate other people carry implicitly has to be carried explicitly. Roots: [aphantasia-mechanism.md → Statelessness](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md#statelessness--only-conceptual-memory-persists), [executive-function.md → working memory](executive-function.book-chapter.md#working-memory).
- **Explicit valuation.** Principles, rules, structured priorities. Substitute for the missing affective-pull signal — what others register as "I want / don't want X" Alan handles through explicit scoring against the rules. Roots: [alexithymia.md → Conscious vs. unconscious emotional architecture](alexithymia.book-chapter.md#conscious-vs-unconscious-emotional-architecture).
- **Action-sequence scaffolding.** Externalized step lists, written plans, procedural checklists. System D ([automaticity-systems.md → System D](automaticity-systems.book-chapter.md#d-internally-generated-action-sequence-chunking--reduced)) doesn't automate in Alan's brain; the harness is the externalization that closes that gap. There is no clean internal substitute pathway for missing action-sequence chunking — the answer is externalization, not automation.
- **"What should I do" tracking.** Robust systems for keeping track of what should be done, because Alan can't just "feel it out" generally. Goal-tracking lives outside the head because the felt-pull layer isn't reliable enough to carry it.

The harness closes the previously-flagged System D coping problem at the architectural level: every working-around strategy listed under that thread (external lists, ADHD-style externalization, environmental scaffolds) is a component of the harness.

### The task-stream inventory — the task pillar lives in software

The [first permanent pillar](#the-20-year-arc-additive-crisis-driven-growth) (task management / GTD) is now concretely instantiated as a set of software-backed **task streams** — the external-memory substrate above, made specific. The streams that have mattered:

- **Tasks** — the general GTD task list.
- **Temper Tasks** — the ESO-domain task list.
- **Calendar** — time-anchored commitments.
- **Email** — the inbox as a work queue.
- **Text Messages** — message threads as a tracked stream.
- **Collections** — the chunking-rotation queues: **Music, Books, Shows, Courses, ESO Progress.** These are the [active sets](chunking-rotation.book-chapter.md) the rotation pattern runs against (the per-domain queue shapes named there).

*"That's most of the ones that have mattered."*

The mechanism: every one of these is something pushed *out of his head and into a system.* They are his external memory — the concrete instantiation of the task-management pillar. **The task pillar basically lives in software now.** Each stream substitutes for state the present-tense self can't hold ([statelessness](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md#statelessness--only-conceptual-memory-persists)); the software is where the state lives, and [trigger-based memory](#trigger-based-memory-as-the-architectural-backbone) is how he re-enters each stream without re-paying the missing-System-D cost. This is the task-stream side of the harness component inventory; the non-task-stream tooling (the interchangeable "other pile") is still uncatalogued — see threads.

## Leverage — what the harness amplifies

The harness doesn't only fill in gaps. It also routes Alan's AuDHD strengths into scope that executive function couldn't otherwise carry:

- **Hyperfocus.** Sustained attention runs at ~12 hours daily under interest ([executive-function.md → sustained attention](executive-function.book-chapter.md#sustained-attention)). Without scaffolding, it spends itself on whatever interest-channel fires next. The harness *routes* it — pins what's being worked on across the ~3-second buffer.
- **Parallel-stream depth.** The multi-stream day pattern ([interest-modulator.md → multi-stream parallelism](interest-modulator.book-chapter.md#multi-stream-parallelism)) runs multiple deep streams concurrently. The harness holds context across the streams so none drop.
- **Systematic precision.** The slow-start, higher-ceiling shape from [automaticity-systems.md → quantitative shape](automaticity-systems.book-chapter.md#quantitative-shape--slow-start-higher-ceiling) lands ~10x lower error rates past the crossover — when configured for the right domain. The harness is the configuration.
- **Pattern-recognition reach.** Conceptual chaining is dense and reaches widely ([conceptual-cognition.md](conceptual-cognition.book-chapter.md), [recognition-vs-recall.md](recognition-vs-recall.book-chapter.md)). The harness supplies the references the pattern-recognition fires against.

Without the harness, those strengths fire incoherently. With it, they get pointed at projects whose scope would normally overwhelm executive function and run there for years.

### Chunking-rotation as an enabled pattern

The harness also enables patterns that don't fire at all without it. Chunking-rotation — small bites of many things absorbed gradually over time, rotated across an active set — is the worked example. The pattern combines the autism (familiarity → safety) channel within each thing with the ADHD (novelty → dopamine) channel across the rotation, and rides aphantasia's flat re-entry cost so a chunk gap of a minute or a year costs about the same. Alan's Lyapunov-function framing: bite size > epsilon implies eventual completion.

The pattern can't run without the harness. Statelessness means the active set — currently reading X, watching Y, taking course Z — has to live externally. The harness component varies per domain (books, shows, courses, projects, games each have their own queue shape) but the dependence is constant: no harness queue, no rotation. Full mechanism in [chunking-rotation.md](chunking-rotation.book-chapter.md).

## The stateless-server working configuration

The harness's leverage shows up concretely in Alan's daily working shape. He averaged 12 hours of AI coding per day, sustained for the first four months of this year; the past six weeks he has slowed to ~8-10 hours (and has stopped tracking). The slowdown coincides with a more turbulent, higher-stress stretch — noted lightly, not over-read.

The output volume is far past what most people can sustain, and the reason is the harness, not raw effort. A standard day runs 8-12 agent teams concurrently while Alan is also playing ESO and listening to music. The agent teams carry the persistent project state across all those streams; Alan does not hold it.

That division is the **stateless-server model** in operation — the daily-life instantiation of the [discrete self](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#the-stateless-server-working-model). Because Alan lives inside the roughly-3-second window, he runs as a stateless processor: he responds to the agent teams when they need attention, then goes back to ESO rather than idling between requests. The long-horizon state the present-bound self cannot hold internally lives in the teams; each momentary self serves whatever request is in front of it against that external store. The harness is the store; the stateless server is how Alan operates against it.

The same configuration is the worked example of the leverage claim above — hyperfocus and parallel-stream depth routed across 8-12 concurrent streams that executive function could never hold in its own working memory. The streams compose because each is simultaneously familiar and novel (the [twin-engine pattern](interest-modulator.book-chapter.md#multi-stream-parallelism)); the harness is what keeps the context of each from dropping.

The lived, screen-level operation of this configuration — how Alan visually detects which agent needs him (motion is the attractor, stillness the find), what paces his switching (boredom), and why the ceiling sits at twelve (screen legibility) — is its own note: [harness-attention.md](harness-attention.book-chapter.md).

## The Claude Code harness as a nested component

Claude Code is one component nested inside the personal harness, not a separate scaffold. The personal harness pre-dates it by ~20 years; Claude Code is the most recent layer added. Other layers (workflows, notes, project tracking, principle hierarchies, the four-resource accounting) continue to operate independently and predate the AI tooling.

The both-and framing matters: Claude Code is not "the harness"; it is "a harness component the personal harness now uses." The personal harness remains the durable structure.

## Trigger-based memory as the architectural backbone

The harness runs on cue → action chains: a given system state triggers the next action; that action puts the system in a new state, which triggers the next action. The architecture is [trigger-based memory](trigger-based-memory.book-chapter.md) at the highest level of abstraction — the same cue-fires-retrieval pattern that runs in Alan's text memorization and his arrows-in-quiver talk system, applied to the externalized state of the harness rather than to words or conversation circumstances.

This is load-bearing. The harness compensates for missing [System D action-sequence chunking](automaticity-systems.book-chapter.md#d-internally-generated-action-sequence-chunking--reduced) by externalizing the sequence. *How* Alan re-enters the externalized sequence without re-paying the missing-D cost each time is via [System B' externally-cued S-R](automaticity-systems.book-chapter.md#b-externally-cued-s-r-habit-learning--intact) — the externalized state is the cue, the next action is the retrieval. The harness is the scaffold; trigger-based memory is the mechanism that deploys it.

See [trigger-based-memory.md → The agent harness](trigger-based-memory.book-chapter.md#3-the-agent-harness) for the cross-domain framing.

## Role in the three-mode operating architecture

The harness is the **externalized conceptual scaffolding** operating mode in the [three-mode operating architecture](operating-mode-architecture.book-chapter.md#external-conceptual-scaffolding--in-the-moment-available-via-structure). The three modes are: physical / weight layer (in-the-moment, trained-weight inference), external conceptual scaffolding (in-the-moment, available via structure), and internal conceptual processing (offline-only).

The harness IS the second of the three. It compensates specifically for impaired internal meta-cognition during in-the-moment execution. Where NTs run meta-cognition internally, Alan reads it off the harness — written principles, decision frameworks, structured priorities, the agent-driven rule lookup. The cognitive operations are the same shape (apply rules to current input); the delivery channel is externalized.

The other two modes operate alongside, not in place. The physical / weight layer runs the same trained-pattern inference NTs run. The internal conceptual processing channel runs offline as a training mechanism for both the weight layer and the harness rules themselves. The harness is the in-the-moment cognitive prosthetic that fills the meta-cognition gap.
