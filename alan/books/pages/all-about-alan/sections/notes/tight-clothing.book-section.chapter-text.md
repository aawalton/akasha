
# Tight clothing

> Tight clothing as a system — tight (not explicit compression) across undergarments, shirts, pants, socks. Default-tight 24/7 with partial loose-break under low Mana. Two-fold mechanism (proprioceptive grounding + touch-stimuli cost reduction). 10-25% nervous-system load reduction.

A coherent system across the full kit: keep tight-fitted clothing on by default, all the time, to ground proprioception and saturate the touch baseline so partial cues stop draining cost. Promoted from a starter sub-system in [sub-systems.md](sub-systems.book-chapter.md) once the bundle was probed in detail. Closest tactile analogue to the [auditory-sensitivity bundle](auditory-sensitivity-bundle.book-chapter.md) — same asymmetric-cost adaptation shape, different modality.

## Tight, not explicit compression

The starter capture called this "compression clothing." That framing is wrong. The category is **tight clothing**, not explicit or medical-grade compression gear — tight-fitted everyday garments across undergarments, shirts, pants, and socks. No graduated-pressure spec, no medical-device intent. Just tight as a default property of the clothing kit.

## Protocol summary

- **Default-tight, 24/7.** Every garment in the rotation is tight-fitted. Default state for both waking and sleeping hours. Inverts the typical sensory-tool framing where the tool is worn strategically — for tight clothing, it's always on.
- **Partial loose-break under low capacity.** Occasionally swap to loose for relief. Trigger is low-capacity days, especially low [Mana](mana.book-chapter.md). The break is partial — usually pants and shirt swap to loose; undergarments and socks tend to stay tight.
- **Not deterministic.** Doesn't fire on every low-Mana day. The trigger raises the probability; it doesn't force the swap.

## Two-fold mechanism

The bundle does two things at once.

- **Proprioceptive grounding.** Consistent touch stimulation tells him where his body is. Compensates for the underlying [proprioception deficit](sensory-experience.book-chapter.md) — without consistent tactile input, he doesn't intrinsically know where his limbs are. Tight clothing supplies that input continuously.
- **Touch-stimuli cost reduction.** Tight clothing also reduces ambient touch-stimulus drain. Saturating the touch baseline means partial cues — clothing-shifting-on-skin, fabric grazing one spot, an unexpected seam — stop pulling attention. Same shape as [iOS dark noise](auditory-sensitivity-bundle.book-chapter.md): saturate the floor uniformly so the residual stops standing out.

Two mechanisms, one tool. The grounding mechanism was the starter framing; the cost-reduction mechanism is the cycle-10 addition.

## Persistence vs habituation

The saturation effect does **not** habituate. Stays present indefinitely on the same kit.

It *can* drop out of conscious awareness when Alan is focused on something else — but the cost-reduction layer keeps operating in the background. Same shape as the [meditative breathing](meditative-breathing.book-chapter.md) protocol running for hours without conscious tracking: the mechanism doesn't require attention to function; it requires the input to be present.

## Magnitude

Roughly **10-25% nervous-system load reduction** vs. forced-loose days. Estimated from comparison to days he's had to wear loose clothing.

Less precise than the auditory bundle's anchor (50%-of-historical-baseline pre-AirPods, dropped to a rare residual today — see [auditory-sensitivity-bundle.md → magnitude](auditory-sensitivity-bundle.book-chapter.md#magnitude)), but substantial. The range is wide enough to flag as a quantification thread.

## Loose-break scope and trigger

The break, when it fires:

- **Trigger.** Low-capacity day, especially low Mana. Mana is the named correlate; whether Safety also modulates the trigger jointly with Mana isn't yet pinned down (see [safety.md → Safety modulates sensory cost](safety/sensory-cost-modulation.book-chapter.md#safety-modulates-sensory-cost) for the parallel auditory finding).
- **Partial scope.** Pants and shirt swap to loose; undergarments and socks tend to stay tight. The deepest layers — the ones in continuous skin contact — keep providing the saturation floor even when the outer layers come off.
- **Not deterministic.** Some low-Mana days don't trigger the swap. The trigger is probabilistic.

The partial-scope detail matters: even the relief move keeps the saturation floor running. Loose pants don't break the touch baseline because the undergarments do.

## Asymmetric-cost adaptation: tactile worked example

The default-max-with-manual-relief shape from the [auditory bundle](auditory-sensitivity-bundle.book-chapter.md#default-mode-and-manual-relief) applies here too:

- **Default to the protective extreme** (tight, all the time).
- **Manual relief downward** (partial swap to loose) under load.
- **Asymmetric cost.** Under-protection (loose by default) costs more than over-protection (tight by default), so the default sits at the protective end and override moves toward less protection.

This is the **second confirmed modality** for the same flip — the [four-tier sensory-regulation hierarchy](sensory-regulation-tools.book-chapter.md) (automatic > flexible > on/off > nothing) inverts under asymmetric cost, and that inversion now appears across two modalities, not just auditory. The pattern is starting to generalize. Whether it lifts to a mechanism note in its own right is a candidate flagged in `/abby`'s backlog.

## Stack with weighted blanket: different mechanism, not redundant saturation

A [weighted blanket](weighted-blanket.book-chapter.md) at night does not stack with tight clothing as a redundant saturation layer. The cycle-11 weighted-blanket capture corrected the prior framing: the weighted blanket runs on movement restriction and heat-sink mechanisms, not grounding, so it's not adding more of the same touch-saturation that tight clothing provides. Different tactile tool, different mechanism class.

Two consequences:

- **The weighted blanket isn't subject to a saturation cap from tight clothing.** When it works, it works because it stilled the body or absorbed excess heat — neither of which tight clothing covers.
- **The weighted blanket has its own cost shape (default-off, coin-flip hit rate, chest-weight blocks breathing) that doesn't match tight clothing's (default-on, high reliability).** Two tactile tools, opposite cost shapes. See [weighted-blanket.md → why default-off is correct](weighted-blanket.book-chapter.md#why-default-off-is-correct).
