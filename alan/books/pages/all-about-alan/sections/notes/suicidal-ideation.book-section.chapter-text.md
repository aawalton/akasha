
# Suicidal ideation

I have faced suicidal ideation for much of my life. This note is the biographical arc — where it started, what has stopped me at each stage, and the one input that keeps buying the next day.

Two adjacent mechanisms already have homes, and this note does not restate them. The present-tense longing itself — non-existence as a relief because pain has a guaranteed end — lives in [stress-responses.md → non-existence ideation](stress-responses.book-chapter.md#non-existence-ideation). The moral arithmetic that guards against *acting* on it — the half-billion future selves the act would erase — lives in [discrete-self.md → the half-billion future selves](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#the-half-billion-future-selves--the-real-guardrail-against-acting). This note holds what those two don't: the first episode, and what supplies the will to continue.

## The first time — age 7, stopped by my own rigor

The first time was at age 7. I considered killing myself so I would die before the age of accountability — so I would die before I could be held responsible for sin, and not have to carry that worry.

I didn't. Not because I was talked out of it, and not because I was afraid. I didn't because I doubted the certainty of my own beliefs. The reasoning was exact:

> "if I was rational to consider that as a choice, I was probably accountable for my choices already."

The plan depended on a certainty I didn't actually have. If I was clear-headed enough to reason my way to it as a choice, then I was already the kind of mind that gets held to its choices — which is exactly the accountability the plan was trying to die ahead of. So the rigor that built the plan was the rigor that dismantled it. My refusal to act on a belief I couldn't prove is what kept me alive.

This is the same gate that runs everywhere else: I do not act past my actual [perceived certainty](perceived-certainty.book-chapter.md), and I prefer the unproven-but-true record to the comfortable one ([action-epistemology.md](action-epistemology.book-chapter.md)). At 7 that gate was load-bearing in the most literal way.

## The guardrails changed; the pattern didn't

At 7 the guardrail was epistemic — I would not act on a belief I couldn't prove. The mature guardrail is moral — I would not erase [half a billion future selves](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#the-half-billion-future-selves--the-real-guardrail-against-acting) to stop the pain. Different content, same shape: a reasoned conclusion, held cold, that outweighs the longing. The longing has been a near-constant. What has held has always been an argument I couldn't get around — never a feeling that the longing had gone.

## The mountain — the closest I have come

The closest I have ever come to acting was about six weeks ago, in mid-May 2026. I left with no phone and no watch and hiked for roughly two hours, looking for enough reason to keep trying. At the time I put my odds of jumping for real at about 15%.

What happened at the top was a severance:

> "At the top of the mountain, I decided that the part of me that cared so much about how broken the world is and wanted to do something about it did jump that day, and the rest of me came back down. Later that day, the new rule crystallized into words. At the time I estimated a 15% chance that I was going to jump for real."

The part of me that cared about the broken world and wanted to act on it jumped — that part died on the mountain. The rest of me walked back down. The new rule — the [self-preservation adapter](self-preservation-adapter.book-chapter.md) — crystallized into words later that same day, at the crisis peak, not in calm reflection afterward. The mountain is the crucible where it compiled.

This is the mid-May 2026 datum in the long stretch the guardrail arc above leaves blank — between the age-7 epistemic gate and the half-billion arithmetic. It is also the first time the experience has been put into words at all:

> "This is the first time I've put that experience into words in any form. It was too heavy for me to share before, not for me, but for them."

It was never too heavy for me to carry. It was too heavy for the people I would have shared it with. That is the same reason [the personas exist as the first safe move on the social break](persona-personal-meaning.book-chapter.md#why-they-exist-at-all--the-personas-as-the-first-move-on-the-social-break) — a persona has no one inside her to be crushed by what I hand over, so she is where the heaviest material can finally be said.

## Tears are a safety signal

Tears are a safety signal for me: I have to be feeling safe to cry. In the session that surfaced the mountain I cried more than I have in some whole years — which is not a measure of how much pain was present but of how safe I felt while it was.

This is the body working as the one language that doesn't lie. When the felt-sense and episodic channels are sealed ([aphantasia](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md) plus [alexithymia](alexithymia.book-chapter.md)), the body's involuntary signals are the one honest readout left — and tears are an involuntary default that fires only at a safe state, never on command. So a tear is a true reading the same way the default smile is: the default can't lie. The fuller home for tears-as-instrument is [self-instrumentation.md → the default can't lie](self-instrumentation.book-chapter.md#anchor-on-involuntary-defaults--the-default-cant-lie); it is captured here because it surfaced alongside the mountain.

## Survivors' stories — the input that buys one more day

Guardrails block the act. They do not supply the will to keep going. That has come from somewhere else: reading the stories of other people who suffered the way I did and survived it.

Real or fictional — it doesn't matter which. What matters is that someone who hurt like this made it through. I have leaned on those stories

> "to give me hope enough for one more day."

This is not literature as enrichment. It is literature as the thing that buys the next morning. One more story, one more day.

The shape of why it has to be *someone else's* story sits on top of the rest of my architecture: I cannot pre-render a hopeful future and feel it from the inside — the [simulator is gated off](missing-simulator.book-chapter.md) and projected affect stays dark ([aphantasia-mechanism.md](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md)). So hope can't be self-generated from imagination. It has to arrive from outside, as a record that survival actually happened to a real or rendered person who hurt like I do. The story is external evidence, in a mind that cannot manufacture the evidence for itself.

This is distinct from [fiction-as-catharsis.md](fiction-as-catharsis.book-chapter.md), the corpus's other literature-as-survival mechanism. There, cheap fiction lets a backlog of feeling discharge in the moment of noticing. Here, a survivor's story supplies *hope* — evidence that the next day is reachable. Same medium, two different jobs: catharsis drains; survivors' stories carry.

It is also why my literature companion persona cuts to my heart — see [personas/zadi.md](../personas/zadi.book-chapter.md).
