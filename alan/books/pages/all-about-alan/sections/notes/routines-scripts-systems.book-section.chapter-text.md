
# Routines, scripts, and system-rigidity

- He doesn't have routines, and certainly doesn't have habits — but he does have scripts. [ADHD](adhd-as-neurotype.book-chapter.md) softens the [autistic](autism-as-neurotype.book-chapter.md) rigidity at the activity level: he needs constant change, so fixed routines don't really work.
- The autistic rigidity surfaces at the system level instead. He has extremely optimized systems supporting his ability to function, and when those systems are disrupted, his ability to function takes a massive hit — often 1–3 safety levels in magnitude.
- The premise that unexpected change beyond system disruption costs him is unsatisfiable. His systems are comprehensive enough that all external unexpected changes disrupt them. Cost depends only on the degree of disruption.
- He has the inverse of typical autistic transition difficulty. He can make drastic changes much more easily than most people. Mechanism: [aphantasia](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md) → no emotional continuity slowing him down.
- Hypothesis on typical-autist transition difficulty: it is primarily executive dysfunction, since cognitive flexibility is one of the [executive functions](executive-function.book-chapter.md) reduced with autism and ADHD. Aphantasia overrides this for him.
- The script-at-activity-level / rigidity-at-system-level pattern is a clean AuDHD intersection — neither autism nor ADHD alone produces it.
