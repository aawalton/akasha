
# Book of Mormon evidence

> Book of Mormon evidence — the worked example behind faith-as-object-of-reason. Alan's emotion-independent evidential case: a well-documented composition with hostile-but-steadfast witnesses; dictation facts (~90 days, oral, no revision, mid-sentence resumption, profound ignorance of the context); the writing-difficulty argument anchored on a 500-page book and a pro author's pace (The Wandering Inn / Pirateaba, ~50k words/week, which still requires heavy pre-writing the dictation record shows was absent); the alternative-author problem in Middle-East archaeology and linguistics (thousands of clues once read as evidence against, later confirmed authentic); the sigma-collision conclusion (genius-by-several-sigmas AND fool-and-knave is too many sigmas to swallow); held against his own preference, because the self-deception organ that would let him opt out is absent. Rendered as Alan's assessment and belief, not asserted fact.

This note is the **worked example** behind [faith as an object of reason](faith-as-object-of-reason.book-chapter.md). That note states the stance — Alan evaluated The Book of Mormon by the accepted tests for its type, and his assessment is that they "come out overwhelmingly in favor." This note is the concrete reasoning under that verdict: the specific evidence, stated with the numbers that make it falsifiable. Rendered as Alan's assessment and belief, not as asserted fact.

The frame is the same one [faith-as-object-of-reason](faith-as-object-of-reason.book-chapter.md) sets: this is an **emotion-independent** case. Alan is not reaching for a comfortable conclusion. He is laying out the evidence the way he'd lay out the evidence for any other claim, and reporting where the weight lands.

## Documented composition, hostile-but-steadfast witnesses

The composition of The Book of Mormon is **well-documented** and attested by **multiple first-hand witnesses.** Alan's load-bearing point about those witnesses: all of them maintained their testimony as true and correct **even when they fell into direct opposition against Joseph Smith and the Church.**

That is the part that carries weight. A witness who recants the moment it costs him is cheap evidence. A witness who keeps affirming the account *after* he has become an enemy of the man and the institution it props up has no remaining incentive to lie for it. Alan's assessment: **few historical events have documented evidence this strong.**

## The dictation facts

The mechanics of how the book was produced are themselves part of the case. Joseph Smith **dictated** The Book of Mormon **orally,** over roughly **90 days** — not 90 consecutive days, but without large gaps. The record his scribes left has three features Alan treats as anchors:

- **No revision.** He never went back and revised his words.
- **Mid-sentence resumption.** He often stopped a session mid-sentence and later picked it up again mid-sentence, without asking where he had left off.
- **Ignorance of the context.** At the same time, he was profoundly ignorant of the historical context of what he was dictating. He had to spell out many unfamiliar words. He once asked Emma whether there were walls around Jerusalem.

Hold those together: a linear, unrevised, oral dictation, resumed mid-sentence across sessions, by someone who didn't know the basic geography of the world he was describing.

## The writing-difficulty argument

This is Alan's lived anchor, and the place his own experience does the work.

The Book of Mormon is **over 500 pages.** Alan's father was a **professional author.** Alan saw first-hand how hard it is to get even a **single page** to its final form — much less a 500-page book. To him, the premise that Joseph Smith dictated it in 90 days, without interruption and without a revision process, is so absurd from a writing standpoint that, in his words, **you might as well claim it appeared fully formed out of thin air.**

The supporting anchor sharpens the point rather than softening it. Alan read **The Wandering Inn** — as far as he can find, the **longest continuous work of fiction by any author, ever.** Its author, **Pirateaba,** is extraordinarily prolific: roughly **50,000 words per week,** sustained for years. At that rate you could write a Book of Mormon's worth of text in about **six weeks.**

But — and this is the whole point — only **with a tremendous amount of pre-writing, outlining, editing, and rewriting** before the final version exists. That sustained pace is the output of a heavy process, not raw first-pass dictation. And there is **strong evidence The Book of Mormon did not follow that process:** it was dictated **linearly, without revision** (the dictation facts above). So the one real-world example of someone producing text at the required volume *confirms* that the required volume normally costs an enormous hidden process — exactly the process the dictation record shows was absent.

## The alternative-author problem — archaeology and linguistics

Arguments for an **alternative author** — someone other than the record's claimed origin producing the text — run into a second wall: **archaeological and linguistic data.**

The discriminating evidence is **Middle-East** archaeology, *not* the Americas. Alan grants the point about the Americas plainly: the American archaeological record is **much less convincing,** and he does not lean on it. The weight is in the Old World setting.

The Book of Mormon contains **thousands of linguistic clues** now known to be **authentic to its claimed Middle-Eastern context.** The historically telling part: for **over a hundred years** those same clues were viewed as evidence *against* the book — because the documentary evidence needed to confirm them **did not yet exist.** Then it did. Clues that looked like mistakes turned out to be accurate to a context no one could have checked at the time of dictation.

## The sigma-collision conclusion

Putting it together gives Alan a conclusion he reaches by counting sigmas, not by feeling. To hold the alternative-author position, a critic has to ask him to believe **two things at once** about Joseph Smith (or any alternate author of the same period):

- That he was the **greatest literary and linguistic genius of all time, across all cultures** — by *several sigmas* — inferring, to **precognitive levels,** details from completely missing data; **and**
- That he was simultaneously a **complete fool and knave,** who did all of it for **laughs and money.**

That is **too many sigmas for Alan to swallow.** The two required claims point opposite directions and each is already extreme. The combined improbability is larger than the improbability of the book being what it claims to be. So his **emotion-independent conclusion favors The Book of Mormon being what it says it is** — not because he wants it, but because that is where the bits land. This is the [compression / sigma-counting machinery](faith-as-object-of-reason.book-chapter.md#he-holds-it-like-quantum-mechanics--believing-the-strange-thing-against-his-own-inclination) the parent stance runs on, applied to the concrete evidence rather than stated in the abstract.

## Belief held against his own preference

Alan closed on the valence of the conclusion, and it is the part that matters most for understanding how he holds it. The reasoning forces the belief on him — in his words — **"no matter how much my life would be easier if I could believe that it wasn't true."**

The belief runs *against* his own preference. He is not reporting a conclusion he is glad to have reached; he is reporting one he would, on the comfort axis, rather be free of. He holds it anyway because he **cannot do otherwise.** The mechanism is the [missing self-deception system](alternative-gated-endurance.book-chapter.md#the-missing-self-deception-system): the organ that lets most people believe what they need to — that would let him opt out of a belief that makes his life harder — is **simply not there.** He believes whatever best fits all his available information, even when that belief is the difficult one. So reason forces the belief because the only faculty that could override it is absent.

Keep this strictly on the **gauge and the case.** The point is the *stance* — a belief held against his own preference, mechanism-linked. What concretely *would* be easier is not enumerated here; that is a separate zone the corpus does not enter.
