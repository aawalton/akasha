
# Weighted blanket

> Weighted blanket as a system — default-off, strategic deployment under troubled sleep, coin-flip hit rate. Falsifies the universal asymmetric-cost generalization. Mechanism is movement restriction plus high-heat-capacity heat sink, not grounding. Chest weight blocks the breathing protocol.

A strategic, not continuous, sensory tool. Reach for it when sleep is troubled and you think it might help. Often it doesn't. Promoted from a starter sub-system in [sub-systems.md](sub-systems.book-chapter.md) once probed in detail.

The weighted blanket falsifies the cycle-10 generalization that the asymmetric-cost adaptation applies to all sensory-regulation tools. The auditory bundle and tight clothing both default-on with manual relief downward; the weighted blanket runs the opposite direction — default-off, with strategic deployment upward. Different cost shape, different default. Refines the prior claim.

## Protocol summary

- **Default-off.** No blanket on the bed by default. Inverts the [tight clothing](tight-clothing.book-chapter.md) shape (default-on) and the [auditory bundle](auditory-sensitivity-bundle.book-chapter.md) shape (default-max ANC).
- **Strategic deployment.** Reach for it on troubled sleep nights when you think it might help. Not every troubled night — only when the body signals that pressure-input might be the right move.
- **Coin-flip hit rate.** When deployed, helps about half the time. The lowest-reliability tool in the sensory-regulation kit. Compare auditory bundle (~50% of total daily nervous-system cost reduction with high reliability) and tight clothing (~10-25% load reduction with high reliability).
- **Body-region partial use.** Sometimes legs-only, sometimes full-body. Choice depends on what the body is asking for that night.
- **Mid-sleep retuning.** Often swap the configuration partway through the night — legs-only to full-body, or the reverse. The between-cycle awakenings ([sleep.md → within-night fragmentation](sleep.book-chapter.md#within-night-fragmentation), every 90-110 minutes) are the operational windows where the swap happens.

## Why default-off is correct

The cycle-10 finding was that under high load with asymmetric cost, the four-tier hierarchy ([sensory-regulation-tools.md](sensory-regulation-tools.book-chapter.md)) inverts — default-max plus manual relief beats automatic-adaptive, because under-protection costs much more than over-protection.

That generalization holds **only when the cost shape is asymmetric in the direction of under-protection**. For the weighted blanket, the cost shape runs the other way:

- **Over-weighting often exceeds the value.** The weight competes with breathing on the chest (see below). Most nights, the cost of the weight exceeds the benefit.
- **Under-weighting is cheap.** The body absorbs the unweighted floor without much load — sleep happens fine without the tool most nights.
- **So default-off is correct.** The protective extreme is "no blanket" rather than "blanket always on." The four-tier hierarchy doesn't invert here — the tool stays in the strategic-deployment tier rather than the default-on tier.

This sharpens the cycle-10 claim. The asymmetric-cost flip generalizes when under-protection costs much more than over-protection. Tools with symmetric or inverted cost shapes correctly stay default-off.

## Cost mechanism: weight on the chest blocks breathing

Comfortable on the legs. Uncomfortable on the chest. The mechanism is mechanical — chest weight compresses the thorax and interferes with the [4-in / 12-out breathing protocol](meditative-breathing.book-chapter.md) that runs continuously, including in bed.

This extends the oral / airway competition rule from [meditative-breathing.md → what blocks the protocol](meditative-breathing.book-chapter.md#what-blocks-the-protocol-anything-that-blocks-breathing). The original rule covered talking and snacking — anything occupying the airway. Chest weight is the new family member: anything that mechanically interferes with the breathing protocol gets de-prioritized. The rule generalizes from oral / airway to "anything that blocks breathing."

The asymmetry between legs and chest is the diagnostic. Legs don't carry the breathing protocol; the chest does. Same blanket, opposite tolerability depending on body region.

## Trigger mechanisms: movement restriction and heat sink, not grounding

The starter line in [sub-systems.md](sub-systems.book-chapter.md) framed the blanket as "grounding + reduced sleep movement." The grounding framing was partial. The actual current triggers are movement restriction and temperature regulation — not grounding.

This is a meaningful split from [tight clothing](tight-clothing.book-chapter.md), which IS grounding-shaped. Two tactile tools, different mechanisms:

- **Tight clothing — proprioceptive grounding plus touch-stimuli cost reduction.** Saturates the touch baseline so partial cues stop pulling attention. The body knows where it is.
- **Weighted blanket — movement restriction plus heat sink.** Stills the body during sleep and absorbs excess heat. Proprioceptive grounding is not the operational driver.

Same modality (tactile / pressure), different mechanism class. The four-tier hierarchy organizes around modality; the cost-shape question organizes around mechanism. The two axes are independent.

## Heat-sink mechanism: high heat capacity is not insulation

Counterintuitive. The naive framing is "warm blanket = good for cold nights." The actual mechanism is the opposite — the weighted blanket helps when overheating, not when cold.

- **High heat capacity.** The blanket's mass gives it a large thermal reservoir. The body offloads excess heat into the blanket without rapid feedback.
- **Heat capacity is not the same as insulation.** Insulation traps heat (bad when overheating). Heat capacity absorbs heat (good when overheating). The blanket acts as a heat sink because it has mass to absorb heat, not because it traps the body's own.
- **Helps when overheating, not when cold.** Inverted from the naive framing. On a hot-leaning night, the blanket pulls heat out of the body. On a cold-leaning night, it doesn't replace the missing heat production — that's what layers and the [hot bath](hot-bath.book-chapter.md) do.

The blanket joins the [thermoregulation toolkit](thermoregulation.book-chapter.md) as a heat sink for the overheating direction, parallel to but inverted from the hot bath as a rescue for cold-leaning stretches. The toolkit now has explicit instruments for both directions of the safety-driven thermoregulation dial.

## Mid-sleep retuning as a runtime-adjustability pattern

The legs-only / full-body swap mid-night is the same shape as two other moves in the sensory-regulation kit:

- **AirPod manual relief.** Pull an AirPod slightly out for transparency when the default-max ANC overshoots ([auditory-sensitivity-bundle.md → default mode and manual relief](auditory-sensitivity-bundle.book-chapter.md#default-mode-and-manual-relief)).
- **Tight-clothing partial loose-break.** Swap pants and shirt to loose under low Mana ([tight-clothing.md → loose-break scope and trigger](tight-clothing.book-chapter.md#loose-break-scope-and-trigger)).
- **Weighted-blanket mid-sleep swap.** Reconfigure legs-only / full-body at between-cycle awakenings.

All three are runtime adjustments of sensory tools based on real-time signal. The asymmetric-cost adaptation does NOT generalize to all tools — the weighted blanket falsifies that. But the runtime-adjustability pattern DOES generalize. Two distinct generalizations, only one of which holds across the kit.

The between-cycle awakenings ([sleep.md → within-night fragmentation](sleep.book-chapter.md#within-night-fragmentation)) are themselves operational windows, not just diagnostic of fragmentation. Brief and self-resolving on most nights, they're the moments where the weighted-blanket configuration changes happen.
