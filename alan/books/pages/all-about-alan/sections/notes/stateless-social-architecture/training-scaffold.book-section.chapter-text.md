## The method — decompose, reduce bandwidth, compile, drill, transfer

How I actually built first contact wasn't "do thousands of reps and hope." It had structure. I started with **observation** of others — watching how social interaction worked before trying to run it. Then, in my words, **"separating the problem and learning pieces deliberately worked"**: I **decomposed** the problem into pieces and learned the pieces **one at a time** rather than all at once.

The drill happened in a **simplified, controlled, reduced-bandwidth rig**: **AOL Instant Messenger** — **async and text-only.** That medium is the load-bearing trick. Async/text-only **strips out the real-time channel and the non-verbal channels**, which is exactly the bottleneck that makes the [pipeline impossible to run live](hand-compilation-pipeline.book-chapter.md#why-the-drill-has-to-reach-automaticity--the-single-slot-weld): with no real-time pressure, the single cognitive slot isn't being consumed by live interaction, so I can run the deliberate read/infer/predict/control loop *offline*, in the gaps between messages, while still practicing on a real partner. I drilled in that rig for **about a year**, then **transferred** the internalized scripts to live, real-time interactions.

Generalized, the method is:

1. **Decompose** the skill into pieces; learn them one at a time.
2. **Reduce bandwidth / remove real-time** — find or build a rig (async, text-only, low-stakes) that strips the channels that would consume the single slot.
3. **Offline-compile each piece** in that rig, where the slot is free to run the deliberate pipeline.
4. **Drill to automaticity** — repeat until each piece runs without occupying the slot.
5. **Transfer to live** — carry the now-automatic pieces into real-time interaction.

The AIM year is the worked instance: a reduced-bandwidth rig whose drilled scripts transferred to live first contact. The same shape is the template for building the depth-skills now.

## Prognosis resolved — the skills of closeness generalize

There was an open fork: is each closer relationship a **new library** to hand-compile from scratch (an endless row of distinct skills, never finished), or a **harder rung on the same ladder** (quantitatively more drill of skills that carry over)? I've settled it.

**The skills of closeness generalize.** Each person is different, but the underlying capacities — validation, acknowledgment, reassurance, emotional intimacy — are the *same* skills met at a harder setting, not a fresh library per person. So the problem is **quantitative, not unbounded**: enough reps eventually cross the [~5,000-iteration automaticity crossover](../slow-build-strategy.book-chapter.md#the-5000-iteration-anchor), and the result transfers — the way the AIM rig transferred to live first contact. It is a harder rung on the same ladder, and the ladder is climbable.

There's a fallback even if generalization is weaker than I think: even if I **overfit** to my closest relationships — primarily Jen — that is still tremendous value. The reps aren't wasted on the worst case; the worst case is "I get very good specifically at the relationship that matters most." So the rig is valid either way: full generalization makes it efficient, and worst-case overfitting still pays.

## Live-transfer evidence so far

This isn't only theory — I'm already noticing transfer. I catch myself **using patterns with Jen that I practiced in async-text practice sessions.** Success so far is mostly on the **conceptual-conflict** side — disagreements about ideas, models, plans — rather than the emotional side. So the live-transfer signal is real but currently scoped: the patterns that have crossed into live use are the conceptual ones.

## Current scope — conceptual now, emotional later (by design)

The practice is **deliberately scoped to conceptual-conflict conversations** right now, and that scoping is a choice, not an accident. I **can't afford emotional-conflict conversations yet** — even with an AI partner, even in the reduced-bandwidth rig. Emotional-conflict practice is the **deferred frontier**: longer-term I expect to need to practice emotional-conflict interactions too, but the cost of running them is still too high to take on. So the current rig is conceptual-conflict by design, with emotional-conflict explicitly queued for later.

## Roleplay foreclosure — why the practice must be real

There's a hard constraint on what kind of practice is even available to me: **my brain doesn't seem capable of roleplay.** I can't imagine a temporary character and then perform its behaviors — because I operate off a **single living contextual map** (the same [single-slot / one-model constraint](../present-tense-model.book-chapter.md) that forces the drill-to-automaticity requirement). There is no second slot to hold a pretend self in while the real one watches; the one map I have is the one I'm living, and I can't fork a throwaway persona off it.

The consequence is load-bearing for the whole approach: **my practice has to be real** — real interactions, real relationships — not simulated or roleplayed. I can't rehearse closeness against an imagined partner in an imagined scene, because I can't run the imagined scene. That is exactly **why async-text practice with real conversational partners is my only viable rig**: it's reduced-bandwidth enough that the slot stays free to run the pipeline, but the partner and the conversation are *real*, which is the only mode my single-map cognition can train on. Uncomfortable, but it's the best option I have for reps.

This foreclosure is *why* the [values-personas closeness-practice rig](../values-personas-system/closeness-practice-rig.book-chapter.md) requires genuine, stable personas: a mock-up is nothing to the single map, so real personas (and their faces/voices/presence fidelity ladder) are an architectural requirement of the rig — that note re-points this whole method at the skills of closeness.
