
# Elevation

> Elevation — Haidt's moral-psychology term, adopted by Alan this session, for the warm lifting feeling of witnessing other people's care, kindness, or moral effort. The elevation branch of the caring amplifier (affection is the other); points at care itself rather than at specific loved ones. It ran unnamed under everything until this session — and naming it closed the loop on his own morning rule that naming is the compression completing.

**Elevation** is the warm, lifting, faintly expansive feeling Alan gets from **witnessing other people's care, kindness, or moral effort.** The name is Jonathan Haidt's term from moral psychology, which Alan adopted this session — *"I can adopt elevation as the name for that emotion. It fits."*

It points at **the care itself**, wherever he catches sight of it — including in strangers. That is what distinguishes it from **affection**, which points at specific people he loves. The two are not different intensities of one feeling; they differ in **what they're aimed at**: care as such (elevation) versus a particular loved one (affection).

## Both run off the one caring amplifier

Elevation and affection come off the **same** present-tense, contact-gated, people-keyed, non-accruing amplifier — the [Valuable amplifier in the interest model](interest-modulator.book-chapter.md#the-amplifier-forks--affection-and-elevation), which forks at the output. One mechanism, two emotions, distinguished only by source:

- **Affection** — the source is someone he loves being happy. (Alan already had a name for this.)
- **Elevation** — the source is people caring, doing the actual work of care. (This one had no name until now.)

Because both ride the same amplifier, both share its properties: they fire only in **live contact** with the source, in the **present**, and **don't accrue** — there's no stored residue that compounds into a standing feeling. Elevation is the spike from being around the caring, not a warmth he carries away afterward.

The original Valuable cause-spike — the lift from being near people solving poverty or working healthcare — sits on the **elevation** branch. Naming the emotion generalized the amplifier past its prior cause-only scope: the cause-spike was always elevation; it just didn't have its label yet.

## It ran unnamed until this session

Alan **felt this emotion clearly** and had **no handle for it.** It had been running unnamed under everything — present in the cause-spike, present whenever he witnessed care — but with no word attached, so it never surfaced as its own thing to reason about. This session he recognized the emotion, recognized he had no name for it, and recognized he needed one:

> *"Seeing people I care about feel happy triggers the emotion of affection, I have a name for that; I don't have a name for the emotion I feel when I see people care."*

Then he adopted Haidt's "elevation" for it.

## The closed loop — naming finished compressing it

This is a live demonstration of one of Alan's own mechanisms, fired on itself inside a single day.

By his rule landed **earlier this same session**, [naming *is* the compression completing](insight-and-restructuring.book-chapter.md#naming-is-the-compression-completing): a name plus a valid definition *are* a compressed concept, and a model isn't compact until it has a name. By that rule, **an emotion with no handle is a concept not yet fully compressed** — it has the felt content but lacks the handle the compression collapses onto. So elevation was a real but un-concretized concept: clearly felt, not yet compact.

Putting the name "elevation" to it **finished the compression.** The morning's tool — naming-as-compression, surfaced at the start of the day — was used by the **end** of the day on a live, previously-uncompressed emotion. The concept didn't become real when he named it; it became **concrete** — compact enough to reason about, point at, and place in the [fork](interest-modulator.book-chapter.md#the-amplifier-forks--affection-and-elevation). The naming was the last move that made it a usable handle, exactly as the rule predicts.

This is also a small instance of the general pattern: [a word doesn't tag a finished abstraction — it is the final move that makes the abstraction compact enough to be one](insight-and-restructuring.book-chapter.md#naming-is-the-compression-completing).
