
# Cultivating local B-tier relationships

> Cultivating local B-tier relationships — the pattern, repeated across the audits, where the household's B-grade dependencies cluster in two places (local independents and Costco), almost every other dependency is D, and the cluster is the result of deliberate cultivation rather than luck. Names the deliberate-cultivation strategy, the Costco-as-corporate-exception observation, the fragility / scale-limit / geographic-lock-in caveats, and the two parallel cultivation strategies (local where local works; self-reliance where it doesn't).

A pattern that surfaces repeatedly in the audits: the household's better dependencies cluster in two specific places, almost everything else is D, and the clustering is not accidental. Composes with [grading-scale.md](grading-scale.book-chapter.md), [trust-criterion.md](trust-criterion.book-chapter.md), and [alternatives.md](alternatives.book-chapter.md).

## Observation

Across the audits so far, the household's B-grade dependencies are concentrated in two clusters.

1. **Local independents.** Dr. Robinson at Grandview Family Medicine (see [healthcare.md](healthcare.book-chapter.md#dr-robinson-at-grandview-family-medicine)), the pediatric dentist (see [healthcare.md](healthcare.book-chapter.md#pediatric-dentist-under-the-ninth-east-dental-umbrella)), Edgemont Auto (see [transportation.md](transportation.book-chapter.md#edgemont-auto)). Person-to-person scale. The institutional grade may be lower — Grandview Family Medicine itself is C — but the practitioner-level relationship is B.
2. **Costco.** Costco Pharmacy (see [healthcare.md](healthcare.book-chapter.md#costco-pharmacy)), Costco Gas (see [transportation.md](transportation.book-chapter.md#costco-gas)), and the Costco membership itself per [grading-scale.md → B](grading-scale.book-chapter.md#b--trusted-with-reservations). One large-scale corporate dependency that consistently earns B across multiple product surfaces, with the supplement-testing reservation as the gating concern that prevents A.

Almost every other dependency in the audits so far is D.

## The pattern is deliberate

The household did not end up with B-grade local providers by accident. Selecting a primary-care doctor by reputation, choosing a local mechanic over a dealership, picking a pediatric dentist who matches the kids' needs — these are deliberate choices. They produce B-grade relationships from a population that, on the default selection, would mostly grade C-or-D.

Treating local-relationship cultivation as a deliberate strategy — not a happy accident — is the move. The B-grade cluster is **earned by selection effort**, and the same effort applied to currently-unaudited domains is likely to produce the same cluster shape there.

## Costco as the corporate exception

Costco is the one corporate-scale dependency that earns B across multiple surfaces. The household has compounded leverage on Costco trust by routing multiple categories through it — pharmacy, fuel, groceries, even the credit-card relationship via the Citi cobrand. The structural alignment that makes Costco different from other corporate-scale dependencies — membership-revenue model, wage-and-benefit policy, demonstrated pricing discipline — has held across product lines, which is what makes the leverage strategy work.

The cobrand entanglement is the cost. Per the [banking audit → Citi](banking.book-chapter.md#citi) entry, the Costco membership trust at B creates a Citi-card dependency at D, and the Costco-Pharmacy chain inherits the same upstream cascade per [healthcare.md → Costco-Pharmacy cascade](healthcare.book-chapter.md#open-audit-gaps).

## Caveats to local-individual trust

Three real limits keep this from being a universal strategy.

**Fragility.** Individual-level trust is structurally fragile. The practitioner can leave practice, retire, sell the business, or change behavior with no institutional control. Same caveat already documented at [healthcare.md → Dr. Robinson](healthcare.book-chapter.md#dr-robinson-at-grandview-family-medicine) and [transportation.md → Edgemont Auto](transportation.book-chapter.md#edgemont-auto). Diversifying within local relationships — maintaining secondary-provider awareness rather than full single-threading — is the hedge.

**Scale limits.** Local relationships don't scale to every category. There is no local independent for cellular carriers, no local independent for cloud compute, no local independent for the kind of infrastructure SaaS audited in [software-and-saas.md](software-and-saas.book-chapter.md). The pattern works where it works.

**Geographic lock-in.** The entire local-relationship cluster is anchored to Provo. Any move would reset most of the B-grade dependencies to "needs reselection." This is itself a hidden cost in the "invisible constraint" of jurisdictional change — moving carries a reselection tax across the local-relationship cluster that the third-party-org framing doesn't capture.

## Two parallel cultivation strategies

The split follows from the scale-limit caveat above.

1. **Where local works** — actively cultivate B-tier local relationships across every domain that supports them. Apply selection effort up front; accept the fragility caveat with secondary-provider awareness; treat the cluster as a deliberately maintained asset rather than a found one.
2. **Where local doesn't work** — accept D-grade temporarily while building the self-reliance alternative. Medication-independence (per [healthcare.md → strategy](healthcare.book-chapter.md#strategy--medication-independence-direction)), household local-compute via the Mac Studio (per [anthropic-remediation.md](anthropic-remediation.book-chapter.md)), rooftop solar (per [transportation.md → strategy](transportation.book-chapter.md#strategy--vehicle-replacement-plus-transport-autonomy)), and home food production are all instances. Self-reliance becomes the alternative to local-relationship cultivation in the categories where local-relationship cultivation isn't structurally available.

Both strategies share the underlying shape: they replace a D-grade dependency on a profit-pressured organization with something the household actually trusts — either a person whose alignment I can read directly, or a capability the household owns outright.

## Applications

- [healthcare.md](healthcare.book-chapter.md) — the densest B-grade cluster in the audit so far (Costco Pharmacy, Dr. Robinson, pediatric dentist). Surfaced this pattern initially.
- [transportation.md](transportation.book-chapter.md) — adds Edgemont Auto and Costco Gas to the same two clusters.
- [banking.md](banking.book-chapter.md) — the contrast case. The banking audit's highest grade is Vanguard at B (institutional, not person-to-person); the banking domain has no local-independent B available. Demonstrates the scale-limit caveat in practice — financial-infrastructure dependencies don't have a local-independent tier the way healthcare and transportation do.
- [food.md](food.book-chapter.md) — the food-domain instance of both clusters at once: Costco groceries (the largest Costco-spend surface) compounds the corporate-exception, and the home garden plus 8 fruit trees are the concrete "where local doesn't work" self-reliance build — still pre-productivity at <1% of household food. The specialty / direct-producer path (CSAs, direct-meat shares) is the food domain's not-yet-cultivated local-independent tier.
