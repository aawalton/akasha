
# The mission break

> The mission break — the onset of Alan's nervous-system damage on his LDS mission (Novosibirsk, Russia, ~2006–2007, age ~20). The symptom (shaking by default), the interpretation (demonic opposition), the inverted response (override for ~nine months), and the break (could not continue). The same event the burnout corpus uses as its peak-capacity calibration anchor; this note supplies the narration the corpus calibrated on but never told.

This is the event where Alan's nervous-system damage began — the onset of the second of the [two pillars](safety/progression.book-chapter.md#the-two-pillars--what-changed-between-then-and-now) that fell in the mission era. The corpus already uses this break as a calibration anchor (peak-capacity endpoint for the [18-year compound decline](autism-burnout.book-chapter.md)) but never narrates it. This note is the narration: symptom → interpretation → inverted response → break.

It is a **different break** from the [2024 firing / identity collapse](autism-diagnostic-arc.book-chapter.md#the-breaking). That later break was the ~80% identity collapse after he was fired from his startup. This one is the mission, ~2006–2007, age ~20 — the *start* of the decline the 2024 break sat at the bottom of. Do not merge them.

## The symptom — shaking by default

On his mission (Novosibirsk, Russia, ~2006–2007, age ~19–20), Alan's body flipped to shaking by default. A constant nervous-system tremor, the resting state rather than a reaction to a moment. This is the literal physical symptom of the damage onset.

## The interpretation — demonic opposition

He read the shaking as demonic opposition. In his words:

> I'm embarassed by it now, but at the time, demonic posession, was my best explanation. Opposition to scare me off from doing something good.

This was the most coherent explanation his inputs allowed. The inputs: a devout nineteen-year-old, no neurological framework in reach, and a worldview in which opposition is real and shows up precisely when you are about to do something good. Given those inputs, "the adversary is trying to frighten me off something righteous" fit the data better than any alternative he had. The meaning-engine produced the best explanation its inputs permitted — and the inputs did not include "your nervous system is breaking."

## The inverted response — override for ~nine months

The interpretation did not only mislabel the symptom. It **inverted the correct response.** If the tremor is the adversary trying to scare him off something good, then backing off = letting evil win. So instead of heeding the warning, he overrode it — pushed an already-failing nervous system forward for roughly **nine more months.**

A correct reading ("this is damage; stop") and his actual reading ("this is opposition; push harder") point in opposite directions on the same signal. The misinterpretation flipped what he was supposed to do about the symptom, and that override is what deepened the damage. The signal was a warning; he treated it as a test to pass.

## The three phases of the nine months

The nine months split into three phases — and the split resolves the corpus's two floating clocks (below) into one arithmetic: 3 + 6 = 9.

- **First three months — the MTC (Missionary Training Center).** Learning Russian and how to be a missionary. Not too bad. He already knew the facility — he had worked the MTC dishroom as his part-time job before, so he was familiar with parts of it and saw friends a few times who were still working there. The one hard part was the **drop in autonomy** — the first direct datapoint for the loss of [pillar one (autonomy)](safety/progression.book-chapter.md#the-two-pillars--what-changed-between-then-and-now). In his words:

> The first three weren't too bad, that was the missionary training center time when I was learning Russian and how to be a missionary. I had worked at the MTC as my part-time job in the dishroom primarily, so I was familiar with some of the facility and even saw friends a few times who were still working there. The drop in autonomy was hard though.

- **The following six months — the field.** Three months of tracting (door-to-door), then three months of street contacting. The load: **100 people a day, more than 10,000 rejections.** In his words:

> The following six months were brutal… 3 months of tracting and then 3 months of street contacting. 100 people a day, >10,000 rejections.

This six-month field window is the narration of the corpus's existing peak-capacity anchor — *six months of daily Level-6 social load (door-knocking strangers + street outreach in a foreign language) before his body broke.* It is recorded in [social-experience.md](social-experience.book-chapter.md) and [autism-burnout.md](autism-burnout.book-chapter.md); the 100-a-day / >10,000-rejection figures are the concrete texture of that load. Link, don't restate.

## The break — could not continue

The override held until his body broke and he could not continue. The same break the corpus calibrates on, read here as its endpoint rather than its anchor.

At the end of the nine months, the symptoms did not worsen evenly — they all crossed **new qualitative thresholds together.** In his words:

> My symptoms got worse all along the way and at the end of nine months, everything hit new qualitative thresholds together. My shaking was full body and harder to control. I had migraines. I had severe stomach cramping. I had crippling social anxiety, couldn't handle leaving the apartment. Looking back I can see severe sensory sensitivity, but I didn't have a name for that at the time. All my joints swelled to the point it was hard to walk. I was constantly exhausted.

The inventory at the break, as bodily fact:

- **Shaking** — now full-body and harder to control (the baseline tremor of the symptom section, crossed into a new threshold).
- **Migraines.**
- **Severe stomach cramping.**
- **Crippling social anxiety** — could not handle leaving the apartment.
- **Severe sensory sensitivity** — visible only in retrospect; he had no name for it at the time.
- **All joints swollen** — to the point walking was hard.
- **Constant exhaustion.**

These crossed their thresholds simultaneously at the break. None were in the corpus before this narration; this is the first record of what the break consisted of physically.

## How the mission ended — he did not go home

The break did not end the mission. Most people in that state would have gone home; Alan did not. In his words:

> I didn't end my mission there, though I think many would have. I got in touch with the mission president's wife who was the coordinator for health issues. She connected me to a church psychologist operating out of Germany over the phone. He had me take a stress assessment questionnaire, and I scored at the 99th percentile (literally could not have scored higher).

The concrete anchors: the **mission president's wife** was the health-issues coordinator; she routed him to a **church psychologist operating from Germany, by phone**; the **stress assessment questionnaire** put him at the **99th percentile — he could not have scored higher.**

### Why leaving was never a real option — the floor under the override

Leaving was never weighed and rejected. It never made the menu. In his words: *"I didn't view leaving as a real option, so I went back to my belief 'The only way out is through'."* He reached **back** to that belief — a handhold set long before the crisis, not learned from it. ("The only way out is through" is documented at [perfection-is-safe.md → the belief, and its currency](perfection-is-safe.book-chapter.md#the-belief-and-its-currency).)

Under that handhold was a floor. In his words: *"I think this traced back to my deeper beliefs **at the time** that my needs were not important and that I always had to meet the expectations of others."* Two beliefs, both flagged by Alan as **past** ("at the time"):

1. My needs are not important.
2. I always have to meet the expectations of others.

These are *why* leaving wasn't a real option. Going home would have meant setting his own need above an expectation — the one move the two beliefs forbade. The override was not willpower over a hard choice; it was the absence of a choice, manufactured by a belief structure that priced his own need at zero. Both beliefs (and their childhood origin) are documented at their canonical home — the childhood-needs belief excavated in [emotional-archaeology.md → the worked example](emotional-archaeology.book-chapter.md#the-worked-example--the-childhood-needs-belief), with the wound in [journey-as-reward.md → the biographical root](journey-as-reward.book-chapter.md#the-biographical-root--why-the-wanting-rule-exists). The corpus frames them as past and ~90% revised; this note adds only the tie that they were the floor under the mission override.

### The recovery seed — where the breathing practice was born

The Germany psychologist did not stop at the questionnaire. He taught Alan the main systems of the body, how they integrate with the autonomic nervous system, and that the **respiratory system is the semi-voluntary bridge into the autonomic** — controlled breathing is the one voluntary handle on the otherwise involuntary. This phone call, in an emergency, at age ~20, when he could not leave the room, is the **seed of the [meditative-breathing](meditative-breathing.book-chapter.md) / autonomic-regulation practice that now anchors hours of every day, twenty years on.** The substance of the practice and its origin live there; see [meditative-breathing.md → origin](meditative-breathing.book-chapter.md#origin--the-germany-phone-call).

## Two clocks on the same break — now reconciled

The corpus carries two durations for this event, and the [three-phase structure](#the-three-phases-of-the-nine-months) above reconciles them. They were never in conflict — they count different sub-windows of the same nine months:

- **Six months** — the [social-experience](social-experience.book-chapter.md) / [autism-burnout](autism-burnout.book-chapter.md) anchor: *six months of daily Level-6 social load (door-knocking strangers + street outreach in a foreign language) before his body broke.* This is the **field window** — the brutal tracting + street-contacting half (3 + 3). It is the peak-capacity endpoint against which the burnout low (<90 min to break ~18 months ago) is compared.
- **~Nine months** — this note's measure: the full window over which he overrode the shaking-by-default tremor, **MTC + field** (3 + 6).

So the arithmetic is now plain: **3 (MTC) + 6 (field) = 9.** The six-month anchor is the field half of the nine-month override window. The durations stop floating; this is the structure Alan stated, not precision invented beyond his words.
