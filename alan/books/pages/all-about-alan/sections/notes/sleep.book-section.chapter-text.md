
# Sleep

Third pillar of the always-running recovery stack alongside [meditative-breathing.md](meditative-breathing.book-chapter.md) and the hot bath. Recovery rate anchored at 1 stress-capacity hour per hour slept (see [recovery-rates.md](recovery-rates.book-chapter.md)).

## Protocol summary

- **Hours.** 9-10 hours per night through 2025, the recovery year. The number wasn't chosen by experiment — Alan slept "as much as I could" and 9-10 is what landed. No deficit-closing target, no external anchor. Every extra hour is noticeable at the margin.
- **Natural wake.** Almost always wakes when done sleeping. No alarm "for a long, long time." The 9-10hr ceiling is set by the body finishing, not by morning obligations cutting it short.

## Onset is bimodal

Once in bed, two modes only:

- **Fast onset.** Asleep within ~10 minutes.
- **Slow onset.** 1-3 hours.

There is no middle band.

The mode correlates with stress (~60%), but the deeper predictor is **resource state**: when the [Health bar](health.book-chapter.md) is high *and* not much stress capacity has been spent that day → fast onset. When either is low → 1-3hr range.

Resource state is the dominant lever, but not the only one. The standard sleep-hygiene knobs (cut screens, dim lights, fixed schedule, cool room, no caffeine after noon) don't move onset for Alan — inverted observation against the conventional advice. What *does* move it, beyond resource state, is an **attentional** knob: riding a low-grade focal anchor until it breaks down lets him slip from focus mode into the diffuse mode sleep requires — the canonical home is [sleep-onset-attention.md](sleep-onset-attention.book-chapter.md). Resource state and the attentional knob are the two he has clearly identified; there are a bunch of other identified factors as well.

## Duration: opposite-signature mechanisms

Two distinct mechanisms cut sleep duration, and they do it from opposite ends of the night:

- **Stress shortens duration via early wake (back of night).** Wake-up time moves earlier; bedtime is unchanged.
- **Vyvanse shortens duration via late onset (front of night).** Falling-asleep time moves later; wake time is unchanged.

Anchors (cleaned):

- **Pre-Vyvanse, low stress:** 9-10 hours.
- **Pre-Vyvanse, high stress:** 6.5 hours (drops further from the low-stress baseline).
- **Post-Vyvanse, low stress:** 8-9 hours. Roughly 1 hour of front-of-night loss vs. the pre-Vyvanse low-stress baseline.
- **Post-Vyvanse, high stress:** 6-7 hours. Both mechanisms compose — front-of-night loss from Vyvanse plus back-of-night loss from stress.

The two signatures are diagnostic. If the night was short *and* bedtime was late, the mechanism was Vyvanse. If the night was short *and* wake was early, the mechanism was stress.

## Within-night fragmentation

- Mid-night wakes between every sleep cycle (~90-110 minutes).
- Brief; "usually" resolve back to sleep within seconds with no active effort.
- Not load-bearing on recovery rate — the 1-stress-capacity-hour-per-hour-slept anchor in [recovery-rates.md](recovery-rates.book-chapter.md) holds because the cycle wakes don't fragment recovery.
- **Operational window for retuning sensory tools.** The brief wakes are also when the [weighted blanket](weighted-blanket.book-chapter.md) configuration gets swapped — legs-only to full-body, or the reverse. Diagnostic of fragmentation in one frame; operational handle for runtime adjustment in another.

## Where Alan sleeps — multiple places, two blocks

Sleep is the closest thing to a panacea Alan has found, so he chases it hard — which means sleeping wherever it lands, not in one fixed bed. **Most nights he sleeps in at least two different places at different times, in two blocks.** He almost always falls asleep 2-4 hours before Jen does.

This carries a marital cost. To get the sleep, Alan moved out of the shared bedroom (now Jen's bedroom); some nights he starts in her room, she wakes him when she comes to bed for a little connection, then he transfers downstairs. The full arrangement, the bind it creates with sex-initiation, and the proposed cuddling-at-the-transition fix are in [sexuality-knot/sleep-and-the-bedroom.md](sexuality-knot/sleep-and-the-bedroom.book-chapter.md).

## Naps

- Valued. Alan loves naps when he can manage them.
- Vyvanse blocks daytime sleep most days.
- Same drug squeezes both ends — late onset at night, no nap during the day.

## In-bed wind-down stack

Once in bed, two simultaneous practices run until tired enough to sleep:

- **Reading.** Backlit screen, dark mode, lowest brightness setting. Counter to standard sleep-hygiene advice ("no screens before bed"); consistent with the broader finding that environmental levers don't move onset for Alan — only resource state does. Same low-brightness dark-mode pattern shows up across the [visual-sensitivity bundle](visual-sensitivity-bundle.book-chapter.md) — screen brightness mode tracks time of day uniformly, in bed and elsewhere.
- **Meditative breathing.** Continues the 4-in / 12-out protocol that has run all day. The in-bed phase is one of the contexts the breathing stacks under (see [meditative-breathing.md → continuous, not episodic](meditative-breathing.book-chapter.md#continuous-not-episodic)).

## Discipline: low-effort, no pull-to-stay-up

"I always want to sleep, it's just a matter of whether I succeed in falling asleep when I want to."

The motivational pull-to-stay-up that most modern adults have is absent. The bottleneck is onset success, not will. Always-want-to-sleep is itself an autistic-burnout signature — chronic under-recovery means the want-to-sleep signal is always on (see [autism-burnout.md](autism-burnout.book-chapter.md)).

## Stack interactions

- **Hot bath: incidental onset support, not structural.** Bath fires when prerequisites align (high stress + sufficient Mana), which clusters in the afternoon and evening simply because mornings rarely accumulate enough stress to trigger it ([hot-bath.md → time-of-day pattern](hot-bath.book-chapter.md#time-of-day-pattern)). When a bath happens to land in the evening it tends to ease onset — probabilistic, "no guarantees." Evening landing is the side effect; the real driver is stress accumulation crossing the trigger threshold, not the clock.
- **Meditative breathing** stacks all day and continues in bed.
- **Weighted blanket: strategic mid-sleep tool.** Default-off; reach for it when sleep is troubled and pressure-input or heat-sinking might help. Coin-flip hit rate. Configuration (legs-only vs. full-body) gets retuned at the between-cycle awakenings above. See [weighted-blanket.md](weighted-blanket.book-chapter.md).
- **Sleep itself** is the third pillar of the always-running recovery stack — see [employment-cessation.md → composition](employment-cessation.book-chapter.md#composition-with-the-rest-of-the-recovery-stack).
