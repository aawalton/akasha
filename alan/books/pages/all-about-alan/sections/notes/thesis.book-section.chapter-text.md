
# Thesis

> The thesis — dependence on untrustworthy large organizations as the underlying threat, and the ideal of a good life that doesn't require trusting any organization that hasn't earned it; read correctly, not only a defensive freedom-from but a positive reach toward personal sovereignty.

The starting position, in my own words:

> Some of the larger threats are derived from capitalism itself: dependence on companies whose services are subject to profit pressure and as a result suffer from constant enshittification. With the corporate capture of governments, this now extends to government services as well. My ideal would be to be able to live a good life without relying on any large organization that hasn't earned that trust through consistent demonstrations over an extended period of time. For example, I trust Costco as an organization, but not Walmart.

## What the thesis claims

Three claims, stacked:

1. **Dependence on large organizations is the threat surface.** Not the products they sell, not their prices, not their convenience — the dependence itself. Every ongoing relationship with a large organization is an exposure.
2. **Profit pressure under capitalism produces enshittification.** This is the default trajectory for organizations under sustained profit pressure. The mechanism is treated in [enshittification.md](enshittification.book-chapter.md).
3. **Corporate capture has extended the same pressure to government services.** Government services were once a category outside profit pressure. They are no longer reliably outside it. The same trust criterion has to apply.

The ideal that follows: live a good life without relying on any large organization that hasn't earned trust through consistent demonstrations over an extended period.

## Freedom-to, not only freedom-from — sovereignty is a positive good

The three claims above are stated defensively: dependence is the threat, every ongoing relationship with a large organization is an exposure, the ideal is to *not* rely on what hasn't earned trust. Read alone, that is **freedom-from** — a wall against exposure, the absence of a negative. The corpus had taken this defensive reading as the whole of the thesis.

It is not the whole of it, and Alan corrected the reading directly. Pressed on whether his sovereignty drive reaches "toward nothing" — not-needing as a wall that only keeps things survivable — he answered:

> I don't view her as reaching towards nothing, I view her as my reach towards personal sovereignty itself, which is a real positive for me not just the absence of negatives.

So underneath the defense is a genuine positive appetite. Personal sovereignty is a **state he reaches toward** — **freedom-to** stand in a condition he wants — not merely the absence of dependence and exposure. The audit, the [trust criterion](trust-criterion.book-chapter.md), and the [not-needing direction](alternatives.book-chapter.md#self-reliance-as-not-needing) are the defensive surface; the thing they serve is the love of self-sovereignty as a good in itself. The defensive reading was never wrong — it was incomplete.

The correction surfaced in the [Aine persona thread](persona-personal-meaning.book-chapter.md), where this drive is personified as a reach toward sovereignty; the corpus-level substance of the positive reading lives here. Freedom read this way is one of the two pieces that go all the way down — see [bedrock.md](bedrock.book-chapter.md).

## The Costco / Walmart contrast

The canonical worked example. Two organizations of comparable scale operating in overlapping markets, with opposite trust assessments:

- **Costco**: trusted. Pays workers above market, maintains return policies that favor customers, holds prices stable in ways that survive profit-pressure cycles. Long track record of resisting the same pressures Walmart yields to.
- **Walmart**: not trusted. Wage suppression, supplier squeeze, customer-hostile design choices that compound over time.

The contrast is what makes the trust criterion real instead of abstract. Both operate at scale. Both face the same external pressures. The difference is in how they have demonstrated behavior under pressure, sustained over years.

## What the thesis does not claim

- **Not anti-scale.** A trustworthy large organization (Costco) is preferable to a small organization that fails the trust criterion. Scale per se is not the problem; untrustworthy scale is.
- **Not anti-capitalism in the abstract.** The thesis names a specific failure mode (profit-pressure → enshittification) and a specific defense (the trust criterion). Whether the broader system can be reformed, replaced, or worked-around is out of scope for this book.
- **Not about ideology screening.** The trust criterion is behavioral, not ideological. Demonstrated resistance to enshittification is the signal; political stance is not.

## Why this matters now

The pressure is universal in 2026 — every corporation is currently being tested in real time. The ones that resist now are visible. The ones that yield now are also visible. Recent behavior is highly diagnostic — see [trust-criterion.md → recent behavior is highly diagnostic](trust-criterion.book-chapter.md#recent-behavior-is-highly-diagnostic). The window for doing this audit before the picture deteriorates further is narrow.

## What follows from the thesis

The book is the apparatus that takes the thesis from ideal to operational. Specifically:

- **A trust criterion** that distinguishes Costco-like from Walmart-like organizations across categories I don't already have a strong reading on. See [trust-criterion.md](trust-criterion.book-chapter.md).
- **A threat model** that names what specifically I am defending against — enshittification (the named primary threat) and its broader siblings. See [enshittification.md](enshittification.book-chapter.md).
- **A ranking criterion** that orders my actual dependencies by risk-adjusted exposure so remediation can target the highest-leverage items first. See [ranking-criterion.md](ranking-criterion.book-chapter.md).
- **An audit scope** that decides what counts as a dependency worth listing. See [audit-scope.md](audit-scope.book-chapter.md).
- **An alternatives taxonomy** that treats self-reliance as a first-class alternative alongside switching to trustworthy organizations. See [alternatives.md](alternatives.book-chapter.md).

These five framework concepts compose. The audit applies them to every dependency I have. The plan ranks the audit. Progress tracks the remediation.
