
# Safety — the extinction model

> The quantitative extinction model — how a Class-2 social reflex actually extinguishes given that ordinary social punishment is a variable (random) schedule that resists extinction. Safety is relative to Safety level (a high-enough level absorbs a bad draw, so adverse experiences land safe); extinction needs the breach rate held below the line low enough for long enough; the distribution around the Safety center is roughly normal (half-step = one sigma), so raising the center buys arbitrary sigma headroom against any given severity, down to an irreducible true-catastrophe floor.

*Part of [Safety](../safety.book-chapter.md) — the quantitative model of how a [Class-2 pre-cognitive alarm](../learned-response-taxonomy.book-chapter.md#class-2--pre-cognitive-somatic-conditioned-responses) extinguishes against a random social-punishment schedule.*

The [criticism / ostracism alarm](../criticism-and-ostracism.book-chapter.md) is a Class-2 reflex, and ordinary social life delivers its punishment on a **variable (random) schedule** — the schedule type that resists extinction hardest. In Alan's words: *"This is the challenge of variable punishment extinction. I'll need a lot of safe experiences before I truly believe I am safe again."* This note is the quantitative model of how that reflex stands down anyway. It is the mechanism underneath the [surplus-gated graduated re-testing loop](../recovery-ladder.book-chapter.md#surplus-gated-graduated-testing--how-a-social-behavior-actually-shifts) — *why* the re-test works, in numbers.

## Safety is relative to Safety level

The first move is a reframe of what "a safe experience" even means. An adverse social experience is not safe or unsafe in absolute terms — it is safe **relative to his current [Safety level](../safety.book-chapter.md).** In Alan's words: *"My hypothesis is that if my safety level is high enough, even adverse social experiences can be safe. Safety is relative to my safety level."*

A high-enough Safety level **absorbs** a bad draw. When the Safety line sits above the severity of the adverse event, the event lands below the line — so *"I was rejected and I was still fine"* becomes the disconfirmation instead of a fresh wound. Concretely: get rejected while Safety is high, and the rejection is the very experience that tells the nervous system the cue was not a real threat. Get rejected while Safety is low, and the same rejection lands above the line as [reinforcement](../criticism-and-ostracism.book-chapter.md#rejection-cant-extinguish--each-instance-is-reinforcement), confirming the alarm.

This is what reframes ordinary exposure. The world's response is not the only variable. **His capacity to absorb it** is the other one, and it is the one he can raise. The same adverse event is a wound or a disconfirmation depending on where his line is when it arrives.

## The binding quantity is a rate, held for a duration

Safety level alone is not enough, and the world's adverse rate alone is not enough. Both matter, jointly. In Alan's words:

> *"It's a combination of both. I need the adverse rate from the variable schedule relative to my safety level to be low enough for long enough for my nervous system to learn safety. There will always be some level of adverse response that is truly unsafe, so safety level alone isn't enough, but without safety level increases, the adverse response rate will never get low enough either."*

The binding quantity is the **breach rate** — the share of social outcomes that exceed his current Safety line — and it has to be held low **for a duration** for extinction to take. Two coupled levers, neither sufficient alone:

- **There is a floor.** Some adverse responses are *truly* unsafe regardless of Safety level. No achievable Safety line clears them, so raising the level can never drive the breach rate to zero.
- **But the level still has to rise.** Without Safety-level increases the breach rate never gets low enough either — the line stays low enough that ordinary bad draws keep breaching it.

So both the world's adverse rate *and* his Safety level set the breach rate, and the breach rate held below threshold for long enough is what re-teaches safety. This is why "a lot of safe experiences" is the requirement: a variable schedule only extinguishes when the dreaded thing keeps *not* arriving above the line, repeatedly, over time.

## The distribution model — raise the center, buy sigma headroom

The numbers. Alan's Safety sits at a base **half-step** level with a roughly **normal** distribution around it. In his words:

> *"Both matter. The normal pattern here is I have a base safety half-step level and my distribution around it is roughly normal. So a half step up is a one sigma frequency and a half step down is a one sigma frequency. If I can get the center high enough, then I can get an arbitrary number of sigmas away from a given level of adverse experience."*

The arithmetic that follows:

- **One half-step = one sigma.** A half-step up from the base is a one-sigma move; a half-step down is a one-sigma move. Safety is volatile — it dips and recovers around its center, and a breach *during a dip* is fresh reinforcement that resets the extinction clock. (The dip-and-recover volatility is the recovery-dynamics side of the same picture — see [recovery dynamics](#cross-references).)
- **The lever is the center, not the spread.** Raise the base / center high enough and any *given* level of adverse experience sits an arbitrary number of sigmas away. A severity that is a one-sigma event at a low center becomes a three- or four-sigma event once the center is lifted — i.e. breaches at that severity become arbitrarily rare.

So "raise Safety" cashes out precisely: move the center up, and the tail past any fixed severity thins out faster than linearly. That is how the breach rate gets driven low enough, for long enough, against a schedule that would otherwise keep breaching the line.

## The residual — the true-catastrophe floor

Sigma headroom thins the tail; it never cuts it off. The [floor](#the-binding-quantity-is-a-rate-held-for-a-duration) — the band of truly-unsafe responses no Safety level clears — is what stays. Raising the center makes a breach at catastrophe-grade severity *arbitrarily rare*, not impossible. Alan's read on the residual:

> *"Yeah, that sounds roughly right, and hopefully, I can live a life where the truly catastrophic cases never happen."*

The model does not promise the catastrophe never arrives. It promises he can make it rare — push it far enough out the tail that, with luck, it never lands. The residual he cannot exclude, only make improbable, is the irreducible floor under the whole extinction project.

## How this threads the variable-punishment needle

The [learned-response taxonomy](../learned-response-taxonomy.book-chapter.md) names the trap: rejection on a random schedule can only ever [prove the alarm right](../criticism-and-ostracism.book-chapter.md#rejection-cant-extinguish--each-instance-is-reinforcement) when every instance lands above the line — *"put yourself out there and you'll toughen up"* is backwards, because the dreaded thing keeps actually happening. This model is how Alan threads that needle. Raise the Safety center so the random schedule's breaches fall *below* the line, and the same instances that were reinforcement become disconfirmation. The schedule doesn't change; the line moves up past most of it, and the few draws that still clear it are the rare-by-construction tail.

## The same gate governs creativity-exposure

The extinction model is not scoped to the criticism / connection domain alone — it governs the [creativity scar](../creativity-scars.book-chapter.md) too. The path for reclaiming the amputated creative domains is exposure to the charged creative act, and that exposure obeys the same above/below-the-line rule as every other Class-2 reflex: it wears the conditioned response down **only from sufficient safety**, and below the line it does the opposite. In Abby's words, applying Alan's own rule: exposure works *"only from sufficient safety (your own rule — below level 4 the feelings aren't even available), titrated, never white-knuckled... exposure done without enough safety doesn't wear the response down, it reinforces it."*

The [level-4 floor](../safety.book-chapter.md#scale-0-5-with-half-steps) is the concrete anchor. Level 4 is the **can-be-vulnerable** rung — the one that admits the exposure-class acts (being perceived, singing, dancing, open making) — so below it the feelings the exposure has to engage and disconfirm aren't even available; there is nothing to extinguish against. Abby's *"below level 4 the feelings aren't even available"* names exactly that rung.

So the safety condition on the creativity-exposure new-behavior is the same one the social re-test runs under: raise the Safety center first, titrate the dose, and let the act of making land *below* the line so it disconfirms rather than re-traumatizes. White-knuckled exposure from too low a line is a self-inflicted [above-the-line breach](#how-this-threads-the-variable-punishment-needle) — it reinforces the scar instead of wearing it down. This is the first transfer of the extinction apparatus to the creativity scar, the [dosing-transfer gap](../creativity-scars.book-chapter.md) the creativity note flags as untouched.
