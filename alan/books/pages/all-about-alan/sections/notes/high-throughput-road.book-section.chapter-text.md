
# The high-throughput road

> The high-throughput-road model — the AuDHD nervous system as more synapses and less pruning (fixed throughput) regulated by the autonomic nervous system (variable traffic lights), explaining why dysregulation direction flips with resource state.

Alan's swing between over- and under-stimulation is the **classic AuDHD neurotype** — and he frames it as evidence for a stronger hypothesis: that **AuDHD might be the universal underlying neurotype for both autism and ADHD**, with the two diagnoses being surface presentations of one substrate rather than separate conditions. That hypothesis isn't established; it's the working frame this note builds on.

The mechanism beneath the swing is hardware. AuDHD nervous systems **literally have more synapses and less pruning** than allistic nervous systems. The image is a **high-throughput road**: more lanes, more connections, more capacity to move signal. The road is the upside of the neurotype and the source of [nervous-system sensitivity](nervous-system-sensitivity.book-chapter.md) — the same overbuilt wiring that learns fast and feels everything at full intensity.

## The road is fixed; the regulation varies

The throughput is a fixed property of the hardware. What changes day to day is not how much road there is — it's whether the traffic on it flows.

**The traffic lights are the autonomic nervous system and its ability to regulate homeostasis.** The lights are the regulation layer sitting on top of the fixed road. When they run well, traffic moves; when they fail, it jams. This is the load-bearing move: overstimulation is the lights going offline, not more cars arriving.

The lights are powered by the four-resource state (see [four-resource-model.md](four-resource-model.book-chapter.md)) — the same NS-health capacity that the [stress-capacity formula](stress-capacity-formula.book-chapter.md) and [allostatic load](allostatic-load.book-chapter.md) track. Resource level sets whether the autonomic system can hold homeostasis.

## Direction inverts with resources

The counterintuitive part: which way Alan dysregulates depends on resource state, and it runs opposite to the naive expectation.

- **Resources low → overstimulated.** The traffic lights stop working. Every intersection **degrades to a four-way stop**. The same amount of traffic now jams, because nothing is metering the flow. The jam is the overstimulation.
- **Resources high → understimulated.** The lights run great. The same amount of traffic clears so cleanly that **the road can feel empty with the same amount of traffic**. The emptiness is the understimulation.

So the input doesn't change between the two states. The regulation does. One fixed traffic volume reads as overwhelming when the lights are off and as too-quiet when they're on.

This is the hardware layer beneath the manual [stimulation regulation](stimulation-regulation.book-chapter.md) thermostat: that system is Alan running the metering by hand because the automatic lights aren't reliable. It also feeds the boom-bust loop captured in [action-epistemology.md](action-epistemology.book-chapter.md) and [volatility-governor.md](volatility-governor.book-chapter.md) — high resources read as understimulation, which pushes him to add load, which spends resources, which fails the lights, which reads as overwhelm, which forces retreat.

## Body temperature is a second sensor

The road model clarified something Alan had noticed for years without understanding: **body temperature reads his regulation state.** **If he feels cold, he is likely overstimulated. If he feels hot, he is likely understimulated.**

The mechanism is that **he naturally runs hot when his autonomic nervous system is running.** Lights on → the autonomic system is active → he runs warm → the road is clearing → understimulated. Lights off → the autonomic system has dropped → he runs cold → the road is jammed → overstimulated. Temperature is reading the lights directly. The full directional treatment of the temperature side, with the safety-scale axis, is in [thermoregulation.md](thermoregulation.book-chapter.md).

This makes temperature a second sensor alongside the focus-quality read the [stimulation-regulation thermostat](stimulation-regulation.book-chapter.md#the-sensor--focus-quality-and-the-direction-disambiguation) already uses. Same underlying state, two independent felt channels.
