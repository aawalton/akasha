
# Relationships held from the outside

> Relationships are held from the outside — Alan has no internal attachment-glue, so he cannot hold a bond from his side. Every friend has become an enemy and every enemy started as a friend; the only relationships that survived did so because the other person kept holding the rope, repeatedly. The same external-state pattern as the rest of his architecture: the bond is held outside him, not internally.

This note is the canonical home for one fact about how Alan's relationships persist: he cannot hold a bond **from his side.** A normal person carries each relationship internally — a felt attachment that keeps pulling them back to the other person between encounters. Alan has no such glue ([no attachment forms at all, structurally](commitment-not-attachment.book-chapter.md#he-does-not-believe-he-can-form-attachment-at-all)). So from his side there is nothing holding the bond in place. A relationship of his survives only when the **other person** holds it. Rendered as Alan's model.

## The friend-to-enemy cycle

The pattern is total, and he states it without hedging. Reading Taylor Swift's "The Archer" — *"all of my enemies started out friends"* — he landed on it:

> This is me. Every single one of my "enemies" started out friends, and every single one of my friends has become an "enemy" sooner or later. Every single one.

So the default trajectory of every bond is decay. A friendship is not a stable state he rests in; it is a state that, left to his side alone, slides toward enemy. The decay is not chosen and not wanted — it is what happens when nothing internal holds the bond and the [closeness paradox's damage ledger](closeness-paradox.book-chapter.md) runs unopposed: as a relationship deepens, good-intent corrections lay down triggers faster than familiarity lays down safety, so depth itself manufactures the break. He has said the same elsewhere in the flat form — *"I can't form emotional attachments at all, to anything or anyone."*

## The other person holds the rope

If the bond decays from his side, the only thing that can keep it alive is the other side. The surviving relationships are exactly the ones where someone else did the holding — and kept doing it. In his words, reading "Help me hold onto you":

> I clearly don't have a good track record at being able to keep relationships, and I have such a deep desire for the other person to help me hold on. The only two relationships that have survived past becoming enemies and have gone back to friends have had to do so again and again and again, and it has only worked because the other person cared enough to keep trying, despite me.

Two facts carry the weight. First, **enemy is not terminal** — two bonds came back from it, friend → enemy → friend. Second, the return was never his doing. It worked *"because the other person cared enough to keep trying, despite me"* — the recovery was driven entirely from the outside, against his own drift, repeatedly. The rope is held in the other person's hands; when they let go, the bond is gone, because his hands were never on it.

The two survivors are the [marriage](commitment-not-attachment.book-chapter.md) (where his own [commitment](perfection-is-safe.book-chapter.md#the-belief-and-its-currency) is the one structure he *can* run from his side, and Jen does the rest of the holding) and the one surviving friendship ([one of the seven partial contractions](central-loneliness/contractions.book-chapter.md)).

## The same external-state pattern

This is not a separate quirk. It is the relationship instance of the architecture that runs through everything: **his state is held outside him, not internally.** The same shape appears in [his emotions read off interoception as an external object](emotion-reason-decoupling.book-chapter.md#the-interior-is-an-observed-external-object), in [understanding as his only interface to a self he doesn't store](understanding-as-interface.book-chapter.md), and in the [stateless social architecture](stateless-social-architecture.book-chapter.md) where the affective continuity others carry internally is, for him, simply absent. A bond is one more piece of state he cannot hold internally — so, like the rest, it has to live outside him, in the other person's continued choosing.

The asymmetry this creates is the cruel part. He depends on the other person to hold what he cannot, while having no way to reciprocate the holding in the felt register they are running on — which is the [central loneliness](central-loneliness.book-chapter.md) seen from the bond-keeping side: he is held by people he cannot hold back.
