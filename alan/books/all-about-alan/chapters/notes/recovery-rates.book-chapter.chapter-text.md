
# Recovery rates

- Deep meditative breathing (4 count in, 12 count out, timed to heartbeat): 1 hour = 1 stress-capacity hour. Anchored by breath count as well: 200 breaths = 1 stress-capacity hour. Alan's primary mechanism for paying stress-capacity cost. See [meditative-breathing.md](meditative-breathing.book-chapter.md) for protocol and daily throughput.
- Rest: 1 hour = 1 stress-capacity hour.
- Sleep: 1 hour = 1 stress-capacity hour. Mid-cycle wakes don't fragment the rate — see [sleep.md](sleep.book-chapter.md) for protocol, onset, and duration mechanisms.
- Very hot bath (hot enough to sweat): 1 hour = 3 stress-capacity hours. Mechanism: autonomic shift (SNS withdrawal → PNS rebound) and HPA-axis recalibration with repeated exposure, not sweat-mediated cortisol excretion as originally hypothesized. See [hot-bath.md](hot-bath.book-chapter.md) for protocol, stop triggers, and the mechanism correction.
- Nuropod: 1 hour = 3 stress-capacity hours. It carries a second, separate function on the Safety bar rather than this one — worn in social settings it acts as an artificial boost of 1.5 levels for cost-multiplier purposes. See [safety.md → Safety is a funded level](safety.book-chapter.md#safety-is-a-funded-level-not-a-per-alarm-switch).

## Surplus / deficit balance

The compounding direction of nervous-system capacity (the [Health bar's *capacity*](health.book-chapter.md)) is set by the sign of `recovery rate − stressor rate`:

- **Deficit** (stressors > recovery): capacity compounds downward over time. Pre-autism-diagnosis, Alan ran a sustained ~50% deficit (stressors at ~150% of recovery) for 18 years. Even with 4-12 hours daily of meditative breathing plus other interventions, the recovery rate was outpaced. Compound rate ~20% per year downward — see [autism-burnout.md](autism-burnout.book-chapter.md).
- **Surplus** (recovery > stressors): capacity compounds upward. Sixteen months of sustained surplus has produced ~5x growth in nervous-system-health (~1% → ~5%) — a ~3x per-year compounding rate.

Removing baseline overstimulation (one of the autism-framing's interventions) shifted the balance not by adding new recovery rate but by lowering the stressor rate enough that the existing recovery practices crossed from deficit into surplus.
