
# Sensory experience

- Sound is expensive. He estimates sound is roughly 5x louder for him than for most people. Pre-AirPods Pro, ambient sound without ear protection accounted for ~50% of total daily nervous-system cost (measured at 38 when he first realized he was autistic). The 50% baseline no longer holds today — Safety recovery has dropped the cost on the same input. Full protocol and current state in [auditory-sensitivity-bundle.md](auditory-sensitivity-bundle.book-chapter.md); cross-resource modulation by Safety in [safety.md → Safety modulates sensory cost](safety/sensory-cost-modulation.book-chapter.md#safety-modulates-sensory-cost).
- Light is also "loud". Dark mode for everything; sunglasses outdoors; turns the light off when entering a room. His wife has the inverse profile (light = positive stim + executive-function support), so they often compromise on the lighting. Full system and cohabitation negotiation in [visual-sensitivity-bundle.md](visual-sensitivity-bundle.book-chapter.md), the canonical home for the light load.
- Touch: severe sensory issues. Dry hands specifically — defended by a baseline-maintained lotion regimen with ~5-minute post-handwash re-application; full system in [lotion-regimen.md](lotion-regimen.book-chapter.md). His own skin touching his own skin is irritating via the input-doubling mechanism (the tactile signal lands twice on a system with high per-input cost); barrier-based defense in [skin-on-skin-prevention.md](skin-on-skin-prevention.book-chapter.md). Tight clothing across the full kit saturates the touch baseline so partial cues stop draining cost — full system in [tight-clothing.md](tight-clothing.book-chapter.md).
- Taste: less of an issue now; major issue as a kid. He would find safe foods and eat them continuously for months. Still does that some, but can also eat at restaurants normally. Exception: smoke-flavored food. Enjoyment is only a small part of why he eats — food is roughly **10% pleasure, mostly fuel** — so taste rarely drives a choice; the full food-as-fuel frame and its [ARFID](autism-comorbidities.book-chapter.md) edge are in [safe-foods.md → food as fuel](safe-foods.book-chapter.md#food-as-fuel--the-frame-underneath).
- Interoception: all the channels work, but the signals run quiet. "All the interoception channels do work, they're just generally quiet and easy to ignore." He barely notices hunger or thirst and finds heat and cold hard to sense — not because the channels are broken, but because the signals arrive at low amplitude and don't insist on attention. Deliberate attention recovers them sometimes: "if I'm quiet enough and my environment is quiet enough. External stimuli can drown it out." The interoceptive channels compete in the same [attention budget](attention-budget.book-chapter.md) as the external senses and usually lose — sound alone arrives ~5x louder — so the [destimulation room](destimulation.book-chapter.md) doubles as an interoception amplifier: strip the external field and the body signals become audible again. A salience problem, not a capability problem; the [unreadable salivation reflex](missing-simulator.book-chapter.md#a-measurement-caveat--channel-readability-may-confound-the-survivor-map) is the same sub-salience read. The dial also runs the amplifying direction — attention pointed at a quiet signal raises its felt amplitude, which is how listening for loneliness made it louder ([solitude experiment](solitude-experiment.book-chapter.md)). (This is the *input* side — sensing the body's own state. Distinct from the *output* deficit on heat production captured in [thermoregulation.md](thermoregulation.book-chapter.md); the two are independent dimensions and can co-occur.) The eating consequence of this quiet, self-extinguishing hunger channel — that an engineered points trigger has to stand in for the missing drive — is the keystone of how he eats, developed in [points-as-hunger-substitute.md](points-as-hunger-substitute.book-chapter.md).
- Proprioception: particularly bad. He realized about a year ago that he doesn't intrinsically know where his limbs are most of the time, which led to significant childhood clumsiness. Took almost a major's worth of ballroom-dance classes in college; that taught him to control his body consciously and got him past the clumsiness. He still frequently runs into things.
- Sensory-seeking is its own behavior category, not a sense. Characterized by what the behavior gives you, not by the senses themselves. Tight clothing → grounding + awareness mechanism (consistent touch stimulation tells him where his body is, doesn't habituate; full system in [tight-clothing.md](tight-clothing.book-chapter.md)). Weighted blankets → reduced sleep movement → longer sleep — a strategic, not continuous, tool whose mechanism is movement restriction plus heat-sink, not grounding (full system in [weighted-blanket.md](weighted-blanket.book-chapter.md)).
