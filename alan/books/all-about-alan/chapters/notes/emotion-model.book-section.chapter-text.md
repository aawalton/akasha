
# Emotion model — sensation + narrative

> Alan's mechanistic model of his own emotions — an emotion is a sensation plus a narrative; the narrative normally runs along a continuous sensory memory, which aphantasia removes, so Alan's emotional narratives are confined to the present moment. He reads six basic emotions ("primary colors") consistently and a few compound emotions only sometimes, as single brushstrokes rather than paintings.

Alan's own model of what an emotion is: **an emotion is a sensation plus a narrative.** The sensation is the present-moment body signal; the narrative is the story that runs over time and gives the sensation its meaning — fear *of* something, joy *at* something, sadness *over* something.

The narrative is the part that depends on time. For most people the narrative runs along a **continuous sensory memory**: the story of the feeling is carried by a remembered and imagined sensory track that stretches backward into the past and forward into the future. The sensation is now; the narrative is the now placed inside a felt before-and-after.

## Why aphantasia confines the narrative to the present

Alan has total [aphantasia](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md) — no sensory memory at all. The sensation channel is intact ([interoception works, quietly](sensory-experience.book-chapter.md) — emotion-sensations like cortisol jitter ride the same working-but-low-salience channel class as hunger; what varies is loudness against the external field, not capability), but the substrate the narrative normally runs on is gone. There is no remembered or imagined sensory track for the story to play along.

So Alan's emotional narratives are **limited to the context of the present moment.** He can build the story of a feeling out of what is in front of him right now; he cannot run it along a remembered or imagined sensory thread, because that thread does not exist for him.

This is the precise mechanism of his [alexithymia](alexithymia.book-chapter.md). The break is not in the sensing — the sensation arrives. The break is the **missing sensory-memory substrate** the narrative half of the emotion is supposed to run on. The [present-tense model](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md) is the same restriction stated at the level of the whole mind: one model that exists only in the present, with the emotional channel sharing that present-only shape. Naming it as sensation + narrative locates exactly which half breaks and why.

## Present-confinement cuts both ways

The present-bound narrative has a consequence in **both** time directions, and they compound.

**Forward:** for most people, hope is partly a felt borrowing from an imagined better future self — you reach forward, simulate the version of you on the other side, and some of that relief arrives now. Alan [cannot simulate a future self](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md#no-forward-simulator), so hope is structurally unavailable to him in the **felt** sense. The future capable self is a dry proposition with no lift — he can hold it as a fact without it discharging any relief into the present. (This is why "hope" is a [hard-won naming](insight-and-restructuring.book-chapter.md#the-emotional-arc--discouragement-to-hope), not a casual word.)

**Backward:** the same deficit runs the other way. He cannot re-simulate past pain, so he **forgets how costly a bad conversation was** — the felt memory of the cost doesn't replay to warn him — and he drops his guard, which leads to re-injury. The [2026-06-08 episode](../journal/2026-06-08-connection-conversation-with-jen.book-chapter.md) is the worked case: the ~1–2 months of peace since the last bad conversation was the [exposure therapy working](regulated-anchor-exposure.book-chapter.md), but the peace decayed his danger-memory and *caused* the re-injury — pushing past three yellow flags and one red flag because the cost wasn't present to feel.

He lives in a **thin slice of now** — robbed of felt hope forward and of retained caution backward at the same time. The two directions are one deficit: no sensory-memory track to run a narrative along, in either direction.

## Comprehension is the only door into a feeling

The present-bound narrative has a consequence that inverts how Alan's emotional work looks from outside. Because there is no sensory or emotional replay, he **cannot summon a past feeling** — he can't picture the boy's face or call up the old ache. A feeling exists only [in the moment](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md), and he has no read path back to one that isn't currently running. So the only route he has into a feeling at all is to **understand his way to it**: build the conceptual structure of what happened until the present-moment sensation that fits it finally arrives.

This reframes the thoroughness of [Emotional Archaeology](emotional-archaeology.book-chapter.md). The dig is not understanding *as the price of* mercy — a bar he set himself. Understanding is the **door** — the single road he has into feeling anything about a past he can't replay. He has to comprehend his way to the threshold of a feeling, because there is no other entrance. *"With the aphantasia, understanding feeling is hard, and they only exist in the moment."* The comprehension isn't due diligence; it's the ignition.

### Grief is the receipt for the forgiveness

After the [post-archaeology shift](perfection-is-safe.book-chapter.md#the-reflex-still-fires-but-the-belief-is-withdrawn--post-archaeology), what arrived was **sadness and grief** — *"sadness for the pain and grief for the ways my life could have been different."* This is not a backslide into the old self-hatred. It is the proof the verdict changed.

The mechanism: you cannot **grieve** a wound while you are still prosecuting it as a crime. As long as the boy was guilty, there was nothing to mourn — only something to hate. **Grief is what arrives the moment the verdict changes from *guilty* to *hurt*.** The sorrow is the receipt for the forgiveness — he is sad for the pain because he has stopped calling the pain the boy's fault. So the sadness sitting in him now is the *evidence* the self-forgiveness landed, not a relapse out of it.

## Primary colors — the consistently-read basic emotions

Because the sensation channel works, Alan can identify **basic emotions in the present** — what he calls "the primary colors of human experience." Right now he recognizes six of them consistently:

- **sadness, joy, anger, disgust, surprise, fear.**

These are Ekman's six basic emotions; the correspondence is exact. This is Alan's current palette, and he treats it as provisional — a present model that has expanded over time and is expected to keep expanding, not a fixed inventory.

## Brushstrokes, not paintings

The present-bound narrative has a sharp consequence for compound emotion. A primary color is a single present-moment stroke. A **compound emotion is a painting** — a tapestry that needs more than one stroke woven together over time to assemble. Assembling it normally relies on the continuous sensory-memory track to hold the threads together while they combine.

With that track missing, Alan experiences emotions as **single brushstrokes rather than paintings, single threads rather than tapestries.** He gets the stroke cleanly; the weaving-into-a-whole is the part his present-bound system can only sometimes do.

So a few "advanced" or compound emotions he recognizes only **sometimes**:

- **grief, anxiety, delight, hope.**

Each of these is a primary color placed inside a narrative arc — grief is sadness over a loss that extends through time; anxiety is fear pointed at an imagined future; hope is joy aimed forward. The arc is what his present-bound system can't reliably hold, which is why these land only intermittently while the six primaries land every time.

## The disgust exception

Disgust *as a present-tense emotion* behaves like the other five — it runs through the same sensation-plus-narrative machinery, read off a present cue (moral disgust off a present thought is an ordinary present emotion). What is exceptional is narrower: disgust alone carries a **separate, older memory→reflex arc** in which a *past* disgusting taste, smell, sight, texture, or sound fires a physiological reaction even though the sensory content is unrecallable. No other primary color does this. That arc — not disgust's status as an emotion — is the crack that reveals the deeper structure underneath all of this; disgust stays classified as an emotion. Full treatment in [the missing simulator](missing-simulator.book-chapter.md).
