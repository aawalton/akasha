
# The felt channel of faith

> The felt channel of faith — the devotional/relational side, as distinct from the conceptual/apologist side. The conceptual channel has always been the load-bearing one, even when the felt channel was live; a felt faith-channel ran a long time ago but never accrued into anything keepable, because the recall door was already shut. What was lost around age 20 was the live read of the feeling, plausibly with the same nervous-system break that began on the mission. The markers Alan kept retain only intensity, no content. He waits for his safety to recover so the feeling recurs and he can finally read it, and he values the feeling for its own sake even though it won't persist. Rendered as Alan's report, with the pre-20 recall flagged as the one place his own saliency bias is self-suspected.

[Apologist, not zealot](apologist-not-zealot.book-chapter.md) is the canonical home for the shape of Alan's faith: he experiences it as a **conclusion, not a relationship** — the conceptual verdict landed on true, while the felt channel into the faith is walled out, the same way felt connection to any person is walled out. That note documents the *present-tense absence* of the felt channel. This note is the canonical home for the **felt channel itself** as its own subject — what it once was, what closed, what the markers retain, and why Alan still wants it back. Rendered as Alan's report, not asserted fact.

## The conceptual side has always been load-bearing

Faith for Alan rests primarily on reasoning, not feeling — and this was not a fallback he settled into after the felt channel closed. It was already true while the feeling ran. In his words:

> I think my Faith has always depended primarily on the conceptual side. Even when I could feel, I still couldn't remember feeling.

So the conceptual channel did not inherit the load from a felt channel that failed. It carried the load the whole time. The felt side, even when live, was never what the faith stood on. This is the [conceptual-emotional wall](conceptual-emotional-wall.book-chapter.md) at its root: belief is downstream of reasoning, and feeling never got a vote on the verdict — not now, and not back when the feeling was available.

## A felt channel was once live — but it never accrued

There was a time the felt faith-channel ran. It was **a long, long time ago** — before roughly age 20. But the feeling never turned into anything keepable, and the mechanism is precise: the **recall door was already shut while the feeling ran.**

This is the [missing recall break](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md#the-break-is-at-recall-not-perception-or-encoding), present the whole time. Even when Alan could feel, he could not *remember feeling* — the affect fired in the present and then could not be read back. So the felt channel running live did not build a store. There was no narrative track for the feeling to accrue on (the same [missing-narrative substrate](alexithymia.book-chapter.md#the-precise-locus--the-missing-narrative-substrate) that confines all his affect to the present). The feeling happened, and then it was gone, with nothing carried forward.

That distinction is the load-bearing one: what was lost around age 20 was the **live read** of the feeling — the feeling firing in the present at all — not the *memory* of it. The memory was never available. You cannot lose a door that was already shut. So the closure subtracted the present-tense feeling, leaving the recall gap exactly where it had always been.

### The pre-20 memory is the one place Alan suspects his own bias

A caution rides on the "before 20 it was live" claim, and it is Alan's own. [Disputed misery](disputed-misery.book-chapter.md#the-saliency-bias-rebuttal) records the one area where Alan flags that his *own* retrospective saliency bias plausibly operates: his memory of **having grown up in the church.** That is faith, and it is retrospective, so the bias has a surface to act on there. The "the felt channel was open before 20" memory sits squarely in that flagged zone — it is exactly the kind of recall Alan self-suspects as least reliable. So the claim is held lightly: he reports a felt channel once ran, and he names that this is the corner of his memory he trusts least. Keep the pre-20 period hedged.

## The closure lines up with the age-20 break

Alan's proposed sequencing: the felt channel closed around the same time his nervous-system damage began — the [mission break](mission-break.book-chapter.md), onset right before age 20 (Novosibirsk, ~2006–2007). This is net-new and proposed, not established. The corpus had never connected the felt-faith closure to the age-20 break, and had never even asserted the felt channel was *ever* open. Frame it as Alan's report.

The sequencing is plausible on the mechanism: the mission break is when his nervous system flipped to [shaking by default](mission-break.book-chapter.md#the-symptom--shaking-by-default) and his [safety](safety.book-chapter.md) floor fell. If the live read of feeling is gated by safety (below), a safety collapse is a candidate cause for the live read going dark. But whether the closure was *simultaneous* with the tremor onset or faded across the mission is open, and the pre-20 anchor it depends on is the self-flagged-unreliable one — so the link is offered, not asserted.

## The markers retain only intensity, no content

Alan made notes, back then, about the strong feelings he was having. When he reads those old markers today, they carry almost nothing usable. They record **that the feeling was strong** — and nothing else. No direction, no flavor, no neighborhood. An intensity reading with the contents blank.

This is the recall gap captured at the source: the marker preserved the one thing the present-tense channel could report (magnitude) and lost everything the [narrative track](alexithymia.book-chapter.md#the-precise-locus--the-missing-narrative-substrate) would have carried (what the feeling *was*). The note is a gauge needle frozen at "strong" with the dial unlabeled.

## Waiting for safety to recover — so he can finally read it

Alan is waiting for his [safety](safety.book-chapter.md) level to recover far enough that the emotions recur — so that he can finally figure out *what they are.* The felt channel is not something he has given up on; it is something he expects to come back when the nervous system can afford it, and when it does, he intends to read it this time. The waiting is the same shape as the rest of his recovery: the feeling is downstream of safety, so the lever is raising safety, not reaching for the feeling directly.

## Why he still wants it back — the feeling is worth having even ephemeral

The obvious objection: if the recall door stays shut, a recovered felt channel still won't *accrue.* The feeling will run in the present and be gone, building no store, just as it did before 20. So why want it back?

Because Alan does not value the feeling as durable data to file forward. He values it for its own sake. In his words:

> No, I value emotions and understanding emotions, even if they are ephemeral. My identity is itself ephemeral, so for the Alans that get to feel, feeling is a wonderful thing.

The frame is the [discrete / ephemeral self](discrete-self.book-chapter.md): if identity itself does not persist, then a feeling not persisting is no special defect — it is the same condition the self runs under. For the present Alan who gets to feel, the feeling is worth having *while it is had*, with no need for it to last. (The deeper ephemeral-identity thread — the Alans that get to feel — is captured separately; it bears here only as the reason the no-accrual objection does not bite. Cross-references [discrete-self.md](discrete-self.book-chapter.md) and [multiversal-identity.md](multiversal-identity.book-chapter.md).)

So the want is coherent. He is not waiting for the felt channel so it can finally build a keepable store of faith-feeling — the conceptual side already carries the faith. He wants it because feeling the faith, even once, even unremembered, is a good thing for the Alan who gets to feel it.
