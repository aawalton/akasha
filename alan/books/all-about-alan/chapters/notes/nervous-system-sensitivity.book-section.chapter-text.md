
# Mechanism root: nervous-system sensitivity

- His nervous system is much more sensitive than average.
- Consequence (positive): he learns incredibly quickly, because learning is proportional to intensity of nervous-system activation.
- Consequence (negative): he gets overwhelmed very easily by emotional, social, or sensory stimulation.
- Consequence (negative, distinct from overwhelm): the same activation-intensity dial encodes negative events strongly too — **threat encoding** that later fires on partial cue match. That counterbalancing side has its own canonical home in [traumatic nervous-system encoding](trauma-encoding.book-chapter.md).

The hardware source of the sensitivity is the overbuilt wiring of the AuDHD nervous system — more synapses and less pruning. The [high-throughput road](high-throughput-road.book-chapter.md) treats that wiring as the mechanism beneath both the fast learning and the easy overwhelm, and explains why the overwhelm direction flips with resource state: when resources are low the autonomic regulation fails and the same input overwhelms, when resources are high it clears and the same input under-stimulates. Overstimulation is regulation offline, not more input.

## Input doubling on skin-on-skin contact

When skin touches skin, the contact registers on both sides at once — both surfaces receive the tactile input. On a system with average per-input cost, doubling is a small effect. On a system running at the high per-input cost set by nervous-system sensitivity, the doubling is multiplicative load on an already-expensive input.

This is why Alan finds his own skin touching his own skin irritating. The irritation isn't aesthetic. The tactile signal is counted twice on a system that pays a high cost per count.

The input-doubling mechanism is the cost source defended against by [skin-on-skin prevention](skin-on-skin-prevention.book-chapter.md) — barriers (clothing, body pillows) and posture choices that prevent the two surfaces from contacting in the first place.
