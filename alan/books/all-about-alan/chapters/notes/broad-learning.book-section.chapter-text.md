
# Broad learning

> The deliberate broad-intake learning system — reading authors rather than books (depth-per-source: a complete worldview to evaluate for compactness) and the systematic Great Courses sweep (breadth-across-fields: out-of-distribution data that flips false compactness). Breadth is the structural defense that keeps the compression truth-detector calibrated.

The learning system Alan built to keep his truth-detector calibrated. It exists because of a specific failure mode in how he detects truth: he reads truth off compactness ([compression-epistemology.md](compression-epistemology.book-chapter.md)), and a false model can look more compact than a true one *over a narrow data set*. The fix is structural — keep the data set broad enough that false models are forced to reveal their epicycles.

So breadth here is not a preference or a hobby. It is **load-bearing defense.** Narrow intake lets false compactness pass as truth; broad intake is what keeps compactness tracking truth.

## Two practices, two axes

The system runs two practices that defend the truth-detector from different directions.

### Read authors, not books — depth per source

**"I don't read books, I read authors."** Rather than reading a single book by someone, Alan consumes an author's entire corpus.

A single book is a fragment of a mind's model. The whole corpus is the mind's *compressed worldview* — the complete, internally-coherent structure the author has built. Reading the corpus gives Alan a complete model to evaluate for compactness, not a slice. He can see whether the worldview actually holds together across everything its author applied it to, or whether it only looked compact in the one book that introduced it.

What happens once he judges it true is an in-place merge, not a filing. Alan cannot hold the author beside his own view as a separate model to query — he keeps [one present-tense model](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md#no-conceptual-simulation--one-model-not-many) of the world. So reading an author is **assimilating that author's compressed worldview into his single model** wherever he judges it true, not storing the author for later consultation. The corpus matters precisely because Alan gets one merge: a fragment would fold in a fragment; the whole worldview folds in whole.

This is the depth axis: one source, taken whole.

The practice has a childhood root. When Alan was homeschooled in fourth grade — after [refusing more math until his classmates caught up](life-chronology.book-chapter.md#earth--childhood-020) opened up his own study time — he *"learned to love reading and read voraciously."* The broad-intake habit the truth-detector now depends on started there, as a love of reading, long before it was load-bearing defense.

### The Great Courses sweep — breadth across fields

Alan is systematically working through the entire Great Courses library — roughly **3000 of 10000 hours done** — as a broad survey across every field.

This is the breadth axis: the deliberate supply of **out-of-distribution data points.** A survey across all fields keeps pulling in observations from outside any single theory Alan might be holding. Those out-of-theory data points are exactly what [flips false compactness](compression-epistemology/failure-mode-and-guard.book-chapter.md#the-failure-mode--compactness-is-data-set-relative) — they force a too-narrow model to start bolting on epicycles, at which point its compactness advantage collapses and the false model with it.

## Why both axes are needed

The two practices are complementary, not redundant:

- **Read-authors is depth-per-source** — it hands Alan a complete model to test for compactness. Without it, he'd be evaluating fragments and could mistake a fragment's local tidiness for a whole worldview's coherence.
- **The Great Courses sweep is breadth-across-fields** — it supplies the out-of-distribution data that the compactness test has to run over to be reliable. Without it, the data set stays narrow and false models keep reading as true.

Depth gives him complete models to judge; breadth gives him the wide data set that makes the judgment sound. Together they keep the compression gauge calibrated.

## Outsourced audit — trusting the collision rate

The wide input stream does a second job beyond calibrating the gauge: it makes it safe to hold beliefs Alan can't trace. Because the architecture [stores hashes, not raw data points](recognition-vs-recall.book-chapter.md), and [merges believed-true models in place into the one present-tense model](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md#no-conceptual-simulation--one-model-not-many) rather than filing them by source, **beliefs go authorless** — the derivation compresses away, and he often can't say where a conclusion came from. This is the direct consequence of an inner write-system that is [lossy by design — tuned for explanatory compression, not for keeping his life](encoding-mechanism.book-chapter.md#biography-writes-low).

In Alan's words: *"Yeah, that happens a lot. I often struggle to trace the sources of my beliefs, but I'm constantly taking in broad information, so if they are false beliefs, they'll have conflicting information sooner or later."*

The thing that makes holding an authorless conclusion safe is that **the audit is outsourced to the world.** Alan does not keep the paper trail because the error-correction is external, not internal. The mechanism is three moves:

- **Hold the conclusion cheaply.** No stored derivation, no certainty, no internal proof he can re-run.
- **Keep the input stream wide.** The same obsessively-broad intake the two practices above implement.
- **Trust the collision.** A false belief, held against a wide enough stream, eventually meets conflicting information. The contradiction is an [inconsistent hash](compression-epistemology/mechanics.book-chapter.md#the-holding-state-before-compaction): it goes salient, draws attention as a flaw, and forces the recompression that drops the false belief.

So Alan is not storing certainty — he is trusting the **collision rate.** The wide, constant intake is what converts "I can't trace this belief" from a liability into a non-problem: he doesn't need to keep the derivation because the world re-checks his conclusions for him. This is the operational payoff of the [passive-default guard](compression-epistemology/failure-mode-and-guard.book-chapter.md#passive-default-and-targeted-active-mode) — the same broad intake that calibrates the gauge is what makes compressing away the derivations affordable. The lossiness is safe precisely because the audit lives outside his head.
