
# Employment cessation

Stopping paid employment is the largest single coping move in the recovery stack. At the most-aligned-imaginable role, employment was **80% of total stress-capacity cost**. Cutting it removes more cost than every other system combined.

## The 80% anchor

- Even after years of optimizing toward lower-cost roles, employment was 80% of Alan's total stress-capacity cost.
- 80% was the floor he could reach, not the ceiling of typical roles. The roles he ended up in were already much-better-than-average for his neurotype.
- Inverted observation against the standard "find a role that fits you" advice: the most-aligned imaginable role still ran at 80% of total cost. Role optimization can lower the multiplier; it cannot make employment cheap.

## The ratchet that forced the cut

- For most of the prior decade, Alan optimized year over year for lower-cost roles.
- Capacity dropped faster than role-cost optimization could keep up. Each new role fit better than the last; each new role still cost more than the prior role had cost when Alan started it.
- Years prior to 2025: cut everything outside work to preserve work. Income > zero income, while it was still sustainable.
- The diagnostic `cost > capacity even at the most aligned imaginable role` was already past threshold before the cut happened.

## Trigger: involuntary

- The cessation itself was triggered by being fired, not by a decision to stop.
- The decision had been made by the math months earlier; the firing was the precipitating event that forced the implementation date.
- Generalizable pattern: cost-vs-capacity diagnostics often run past threshold long before the implementation event arrives. The diagnostic is the real signal; the event is timing.

## Financial bridge as precondition

- 6 months of savings covered the immediate gap.
- A subsequent stock sale cleared the runway permanently — roughly 18 years of expenses at current pacing.
- Stated later, on 2026-08-06: *"We have 8-18 years depending on market outcomes"*, and *"we're living on a lump sum runway that isn't self-sustaining"* — *"we have to fill the gap somehow at some point."* The length is a range rather than a figure, and the runway is not read as permanent. The two exits, the deferral trigger at under five years remaining, and the never-an-employee-again bar are in [finances.md → the runway](finances.book-chapter.md#the-runway).
- Honest framing: the **diagnostic** (cost > capacity even at the most aligned imaginable role) is universal across burnt-out neurodivergent adults. The **implementation** (be able to stop) is conditional on resources.
- For readers without the runway: the diagnostic still holds. The implementation has to be solved separately — disability income, partner income, family support, restructured work hours, or staged reduction. This file does not solve that problem; it names it.

## Declinability, not slack, was the precondition for the affordability rule

The [affordability rule](safety.book-chapter.md#safety-as-the-current-binding-constraint) — *don't do what you can't afford* — was impossible for most of Alan's working life. Not because he lacked the capacity to bank a surplus, but because he carried external constraints he could neither afford nor decline. In his words:

> "No, the reason it wasn't affordable before is because I had external constraints that I couldn't afford and also couldn't decline, such as employment. It wasn't until after I retired that I was able to adopt this rule."

The mechanism: employment was the un-declinable, unaffordable load. The rule only exists where he can say no. So retirement did not add capacity so much as it made the load **refusable** — and the rule lives only where load is refusable. This is the deeper reason the cut was load-bearing: it didn't just remove [80% of the cost](#the-80-anchor), it opened the one gate (declinability) the entire downstream coping discipline depends on.

## Composition with the rest of the recovery stack

- Employment cessation is the **base layer**. The other systems compose on top of it.
- [Medical social isolation](medical-social-isolation.book-chapter.md) — the next layer down — is only affordable once employment cost is gone.
- [Meditative breathing](meditative-breathing.book-chapter.md) was already running 8-12 hours per day under employment; the recovery stack didn't need to be invented after the cut. It needed room to outpace the load.
- [Sleep](sleep.book-chapter.md) — 9-10 hours per night through 2025. One of the three pillars of the always-running recovery stack; once employment cost was gone, the duration ceiling stopped being clipped by morning obligations.
- [Hot bath](hot-bath.book-chapter.md) — the third pillar at the rate level (3x stress-capacity recovery; see [recovery-rates.md](recovery-rates.book-chapter.md)). Load-driven rather than scheduled; fires when prerequisites align.
- The recovery model — surplus once recovery rate exceeds stressor rate — is in [recovery-rates.md → surplus / deficit balance](recovery-rates.book-chapter.md#surplus--deficit-balance).
