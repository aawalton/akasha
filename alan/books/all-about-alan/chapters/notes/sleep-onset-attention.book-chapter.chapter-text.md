
# Sleep-onset attention

> How Alan falls asleep — riding a low-grade focal anchor (a count, a book, the hypnagogic image-steering) until it breaks down, and reading the breakdown as the gauge that diffuse mode has arrived and focus is no longer needed. An attentional stance, not a felt practice; the focal-regulation rule's one deliberate exception.

How I cross from awake to asleep. It is an attentional operation, not a body-feeling one — I do something with *where my attention is*, and I read a signal off the result. This is the attentional knob on [sleep onset](sleep.book-chapter.md#onset-is-bimodal) that resource state alone doesn't capture.

## Sleep requires the slip into diffuse mode

Sleep happens when I let myself drop out of focus mode into diffuse mode. *Any* focus holds me at the surface — not just the specific case of [steering the hypnagogic images](hypnagogic-imagery.book-chapter.md). In my words: *"any focus mode activity keeps me awake, thinking about something, reading, actively listening."* Thinking about a problem, reading, actively listening to something — each one keeps me conscious. The thing that has to happen for sleep is the release of focus, the slip into [diffuse mode](compression-epistemology/mechanics.book-chapter.md#generating-a-candidate--two-channels-semi-conscious).

The [hypnagogic note's](hypnagogic-imagery.book-chapter.md) finding — that steering the images blocks the slip and surrendering to them lets it happen — is the specific case of this general rule. Steering is focus; surrender is the release. Same mechanism, narrower window.

## It's attention, not a body-feeling

This is an attentional-stance operation with no felt-sense component. I am not noticing calm in my body or relaxing into a sensation — there is nothing somatic to ride. That is consistent with the [aphantasia constraint on intervention design](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md): "notice the calm in your body" levers don't work for me, because the felt-sense channel they reach for isn't available. Sleep-onset runs on the one channel I do have direct access to — where attention sits — not on an interoceptive read.

## The technique — ride a low-grade focal anchor until it breaks down

I don't drop into diffuse mode directly. I ride a **low-grade focal anchor** and let it give out under me. Three instances of the one shape:

- **The count.** *"counting is similar to reading, I use counting as a low-intensity focus mode and when I start losing the count, I know I'm [slipping] into diffuse mode."* (He said "sleeping into"; the mechanism is slipping into diffuse mode.)
- **The book.** *"My most common pattern currently is reading until I'm having trouble paying attention to the words and then putting my book down and letting myself drift off if I can."* The book is the anchor; trouble holding the words is it breaking down.
- **The image-steering.** In the hypnagogic window, the slight [voluntary control over the imagery](hypnagogic-imagery.book-chapter.md#what-the-imagery-is-like) is the anchor; the pictures starting to flow on their own is it breaking down.

A count, a page of a book, a steered image — three different anchors, one move. Each is a weak focal grip I hold *until it gives out*.

## The breakdown is the gauge

The breakdown is not a failure. It is the **reading.** Losing the count, losing the words, the images starting to flow on their own — each is a needle telling me diffuse mode has arrived and it's safe to let go.

So I am not focusing myself *to* sleep. I am using focus as a **gauge for the exact moment focus is no longer needed.** The anchor's job is not to put me under; its job is to break at the threshold and tell me I've crossed it. When it breaks, I stop — put the book down, drop the count — and let the diffuse mode I've just detected take me the rest of the way.

This is the same "read a felt state as a signal, not a verdict" move as [wanting-as-a-gauge](wanting-as-gauge.book-chapter.md): there, not-wanting is the read-out of a low resource bar; here, the anchor breaking is the read-out that diffuse mode has arrived. Both treat an internal state I can't read directly by instrumenting something I *can* read.

There's a second job the breakdown does. Riding the anchor until it gives out is a way to **break the jam** — the [focus/diffuse alternation stuck in the focus position](focus-diffuse-alternation.book-chapter.md), which is what [rumination](rumination.book-chapter.md) is. The failure of the anchor tips me into the diffuse side I couldn't reach by force; the breakdown-as-gauge is also the jam-breaking moment.

## Why low-intensity specifically — rumination

The anchor has to be *low*-grade for a reason. *"I use a low-intensity focus because if I do nothing I'm prone to rumination, which is high intensity focus."* If I leave attention idle, it fills with rumination on its own — and rumination is itself high-intensity focus, which keeps me awake the same way reading or active listening does.

So the low-grade anchor is protective on both sides. Weak enough to break down at the threshold — a high-intensity focus wouldn't give out, it would hold me up. Present enough to crowd out rumination — doing nothing isn't an option, because nothing isn't what attention does when left alone. The count occupies the slot rumination would otherwise take, but occupies it loosely enough to release when the time comes.

## The focal-rule exception

My regulation system runs on **focal** anchoring, not diffuse. The [counting-breath protocol](meditative-breathing.book-chapter.md#counting-is-structural) is the standing example: counting is what makes the breathing meditative, and I run it on a numerical anchor precisely because the standard diffuse observe/watch/feel mindfulness [collapses on aphantasia](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md#counting-substitutes-for-visualization-based-mindfulness) — there's no imagery for attention to rest on, so a focal anchor is the only kind that works for me.

Sleep-onset is the one place I have to do the opposite — let the focal anchor break and let diffuse mode take over. It is the exception that proves the focal rule. Everywhere else, the focal grip is the tool and I hold it. Here, the focal grip is the *gauge*, and the whole point is to feel it slip.

The focal/diffuse axis (Barbara Oakley) is already mine — I use it for [insight-on-waking](compression-epistemology/mechanics.book-chapter.md#generating-a-candidate--two-channels-semi-conscious), where the diffuse channel hands me a pre-built candidate right as I wake up, in this same liminal zone. Sleep-onset is the entry to that zone; insight-on-waking is the exit. Same two-mode vocabulary, the crossing run in both directions.

## The gauge cousins — two sensors on the same crossing

Reading the focal grip's release is one of two sensors I run at the sleep boundary. The other is [vision as a sleep-onset detector](hypnagogic-imagery.book-chapter.md#vision-as-a-sleep-onset-detector): "I know I'm falling asleep when I start seeing things." The two are distinct. Vision reads the **gate opening** — the extra sensory channels fading in. The anchor-breakdown reads the **focal grip releasing** — my attention letting go. Two different needles on the same crossing, one watching the gate, one watching my own attention.

Both belong to a broader pattern: instrumenting an internal state I can't read directly by reading an external proxy — a "manual closed-loop thermostat." The work-stream version is the [boredom thermostat](harness-attention.book-chapter.md#the-thermostat--boredom-converts-to-more-work-in-flight), where boredom is the sensor calling for more work-in-flight; the arousal version is [stimulation regulation](stimulation-regulation.book-chapter.md). The master pattern across all of these isn't built yet — it's flagged for structural work in the ledger.
