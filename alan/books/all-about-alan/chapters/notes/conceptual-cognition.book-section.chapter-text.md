
# Conceptual cognition

> Conceptual cognition — pattern recognition, detail orientation, systems thinking, encyclopedic recall; meaning attaches to the concept of an event, not the remembered experience of it (destiny needs no memory).

- Mechanism: non-habituation × aphantasia. All mental process that would otherwise be occupied by sensory or emotional memory and imagination has been repurposed for conceptual memory and imagination, which functions. (Full mechanism in [conceptual-reallocation.md](conceptual-reallocation.book-chapter.md).)
- Conceptual detail orientation is extremely strong. Concrete examples: linear-algebra A+ in college (curve-skewing; matrix multiplications without errors); job-interview programming-language spec gap that no other candidate or employee had caught; "Human Encyclopedia, Dictionary, and Thesaurus" college award; full Pokémon recall as a kid (151 — name, number, skills including level acquired, height, weight, evolution tree); enough about dinosaurs to pick apart Jurassic Park inaccuracies.
- Sensory and emotional details disappear almost as soon as they arrive (aphantasia). Tends to focus on the big picture and abstractions, missing the non-conceptual details.
- Bottom-up at first encounter, despite being perceived as top-down. Reads all of the docs for a new programming language before he starts using it. Doesn't remember the details, but the important abstractions stick, and he gets the benefit of recognition when he sees the concepts again.
- Recognition vs. Recall distinction (see [recognition-vs-recall.md](recognition-vs-recall.book-chapter.md)). Recall is broken; Recognition works. Hash-function metaphor: his brain can hash the input in front of him and compare to stored hashes, but cannot reconstruct from a hash. Concrete: face recognition is excellent, face imagination is impossible.
- Systems thinking is total: "Systems are more real to me than my senses."
- The profile shows up early. In preschool (ages ~4–5) Alan had already learned **math up through algebra** and had his **first experiences with computers** — what he later read as "foreshadowing much of the focus of my later life." At age six, tested for first-grade readiness a year young, he **scored at fourth grade or above in every category** (capped only by knowing his times tables to four). These are the earliest instances of the conceptual-detail profile above. Captured in the [life chronology](life-chronology.book-chapter.md#earth--childhood-020).

## The 80/20 split — conceptual over emotional-physical, and the map-building it forces

Trace the chain from root cause to behavior.

Root: no experiential memory, no imagination (aphantasia — see the Mechanism line above, and [conceptual-reallocation.md](conceptual-reallocation.book-chapter.md) for the full trade). Without a channel to replay or picture emotional/physical experience, that side of living never gets folded into the conceptual side the way it does for most people. In his words:

> "the lack of experiential memory or imagination causes my experience of life to be 80% conceptual and only 20% emotional/physical, with a sharp disconnect between the two (not integrated like they are for most people)"

Two consequences follow:

1. **The split.** Lived experience runs roughly 80% conceptual, 20% emotional/physical, by his own estimate.
2. **The disconnect is sharp, not gradual.** The two tracks don't blend. The 20% doesn't season the 80% the way it does for someone whose concept of an event and felt memory of it update each other continuously.

Because the emotional/physical channel is both thin and disconnected, it can't carry the work of understanding the world. The conceptual channel has to do that alone — and it does, at a cost:

> "I have put a ridiculous amount of time and effort into building robust conceptual maps of everything, because that's the only way I can understand the world."

The maps aren't a hobby. They're the only comprehension mechanism available to him, so building one is not optional wherever understanding is needed. And because the map is the whole mechanism, interest tracks its edge — the **conceptual frontier**, the region a map doesn't cover yet or covers thinly. That is where attention goes.

## Inventor / explorer — reinventing the path

The abstraction-seeing has a behavioral signature: Alan is an inventor and explorer, not a follower of footsteps. Because he sees the abstractions directly and "understands them quickly and often differently than others," he does not need anyone else's route to a destination. In his words:

> "This makes me a great inventor/explorer. I don't need to follow the footsteps of others to get somewhere, I actually prefer to reinvent the path myself."

The preference is the point — not just that he *can* find his own way, but that he'd *rather*. Seeing a problem as its abstraction rather than as a sequence of remembered steps means the path others took is one option among many he can see, and usually not the one he picks. This is the downstream behavior of the same trade that produces the cognition above: detail drops out, the abstraction is what remains, and the abstraction is enough to navigate from without a borrowed map. It is the gift the [conceptual tier of the disability model](models-of-disability.book-chapter.md#the-conceptual-gift-concretely--abstraction-seeing) is protecting.

### The double conflict — ~57.6% outside both circles

Reinventing the path isn't just a preference — it's arithmetic. Most of what he focuses on sits on his own **self-focus frontier** (the [map-building frontier](#the-8020-split--conceptual-over-emotional-physical-and-the-map-building-it-forces) applied to himself): at least 80% of his total conceptual frontier, by attention share.

That frontier fails two independent checks:

- **Check against other people's patterns.** Common patterns transfer to him at most 10% of the time — so at least 90% of what he's focused on has no borrowed pattern that applies. That's 90% outside the circle of someone else's understanding and alignment (Jen's, specifically, in the source conversation).
- **Check against social convention.** When he invents his own solution instead, it's comfortably compatible with standard social convention at most 20% of the time — so at least 80% of his inventions sit outside standard social alignment too.

Chain the three rates — frontier share × outside-pattern-transfer × outside-convention:

> "my self-focus frontier is at least 80% of the my total conceptual frontier in terms of attention… common patterns… transfer to me at most 10% of the time… When I invent my own solutions they are comfortably compatible with standard social conventions at most 20% of the time… Therefore, the majority (~80% * ~90% * ~80% = ~57.6%) of the things I am focused on are not just outside of Jen's circle of understanding and alignment, they are also outside of standard social alignment"

80% × 90% × 80% ≈ 57.6%. That's the majority case, not the exception: most of what he focuses on can't be checked against either circle — not against someone who knows him, not against the culture. The borrowed map was never going to cover this territory, which is why reinventing the path above is structural, not stylistic.

## Reading the shape of a feeling over its surface — the music instance

The same shape-over-surface reading runs on emotional content, not just technical content. When Alan listens to a song, he reads the **structure** of the feeling it describes, and the surface specifics fall away — so two songs with different particulars but an identical underlying shape register to him as the **same** thing. Reading Em Beihold's "Numb Little Bug":

> Some of the superficial details are wrong of course, I've never driven in LA, but the heart, the feeling, the reaching out and hoping against hope that there are other people who feel the same.

The wrong surface detail (he has never driven in LA) does not block the match, because the detail was never what he was reading. The match is on the abstraction underneath — *reaching out, hoping others feel the same.* This is the same detail-drops-out / abstraction-remains trade as the technical cognition above, applied to felt content: he hashes the **concept** of the feeling and compares it to his own, indifferent to the scenery the songwriter dressed it in. It is also why music reaches him through meaning rather than sound — the [conceptual door music uses](music.book-chapter.md#meaning-is-the-door-not-the-sound) is this same shape-reading, pointed at a song.

## Meaning attaches to the concept, not the remembered experience — destiny needs no memory

There is a sharper edge to the shape-over-surface reading, and it runs on the **temporal** axis rather than the detail axis. For most people, an event's weight is carried partly by the *remembered experience* of it — the episode replayed and re-felt. That channel does not exist for me: [aphantasia](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md) removes the felt recall and there is [no episodic memory](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md) to replay it from. So the weight has to attach somewhere else, and it attaches to the **concept** of the event — the idea of it — not to any stored experience of it.

The clean instance is Taylor Swift's "invisible string" — destiny read out of improbability, the unlikely path that had to run exactly as it did to arrive here. That lands for me **conceptually**: I don't need to have re-lived the events to feel the weight of how unlikely they were. The improbability is a fact about the idea, and the idea is enough. I named my own marriage as part of this — "incredibly unlikely in many ways" — and its unlikeliness lands the same way, as a concept I can hold rather than an experience I replay.

State it plainly: **destiny needs no memory.** A song about fate-from-improbability reaches me even though I cannot recall the improbable events, because the meaning was never in the recall. This is the same detail-drops-out / abstraction-remains trade as the [shape-over-surface music instance above](#reading-the-shape-of-a-feeling-over-its-surface--the-music-instance), now run against lived events on the time axis: the experience falls away, the concept of the event is what carries the weight. It is why the destiny-and-improbability songs work as a [distinct medicine for the loneliness](music.book-chapter.md#different-artists-deliver-different-medicine-for-the-same-loneliness) — they trade in exactly the currency a memoryless, aphantasic mind still holds.
