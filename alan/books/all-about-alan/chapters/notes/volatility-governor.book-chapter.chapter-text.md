
# Volatility governor

> The volatility governor — four personal rules Alan adopted to substitute an external brake for the missing internal one. Decisiveness runs unbraked because aphantasia removes anticipatory dread, producing a boom/bust cycle; the rules slow him down despite in-the-moment overconfidence. Rule 4 (if I can't afford rejection, I can't afford connection) is the step-4 belief replacement from the only-perfection-is-safe dig — affordability replaces perfection as the control variable. These four are Alan's intentionally-adopted absolutes (Type 1, distinct from the automatic trauma-installed rules); the unifying law beneath them is "protect future Alan from the choices of present Alan," with the resource-drain reading the symptom (holds for three of four) and the self-harm guard the case that breaks it.

Alan's decisiveness runs without an internal brake. Valuing Truth pushes him to act, and total aphantasia removes the anticipatory dread that stops most people before they overreach — full mechanism in [action-epistemology.md](action-epistemology.book-chapter.md). The cost is a boom/bust [volatility cycle](action-epistemology.book-chapter.md#the-cost-is-volatility): doing well → overconfidence → push too hard → cause himself pain → retreat → push again.

This system is the deliberate compensation. Where the neurotype doesn't supply the brake internally, Alan engineers an explicit external rule to stand in for it — the same externalization move as [weather](weather.book-chapter.md) (an external throttle on the conflict engine) and the [decision rubric](decision-rubric.book-chapter.md) (an external forward model). The honest caveat is his: substituting an external governor for a missing internal one is really hard, so he does **not** always succeed.

## The short list — four intentionally-adopted absolutes

The four rules are not house rules Alan drifted into; they are the absolutes he has **adopted intentionally and lives by** — his short list, in his own framing. He distinguishes them sharply from a *second* category of absolute (the automatic, trauma-installed rules — see [the two categories](#the-two-categories--intentional-vs-installed) below). These four are the chosen ones. Stated as he lists them, each paired with the protection it externalizes, in his words:

- ***"If I can't afford to, I'm not allowed to."*** — *"This protects me from overextending on stress capacity."* The affordability gate (Rule 2's general form): the binding bar sets the budget. See [Rule 2](#rule-2--im-not-allowed-to-commit-to-a-recurring-activity-unless-i-can-afford-it-at-a-one-sigma-low), [four-resource-model.md → the migrating-bottleneck governor](four-resource-model.book-chapter.md#the-migrating-bottleneck-governor), and [stress-capacity-formula.md](stress-capacity-formula.book-chapter.md).
- ***"If I don't want to, I'm not allowed to."*** — *"This protects me from overextending on executive function or physical energy."* The wanting veto (Rule 1): not-wanting is a [Mana/Stamina](executive-function.book-chapter.md) resource reading. See [Rule 1](#rule-1--if-i-dont-want-to-do-it-im-not-allowed-to) and [wanting-as-gauge.md](wanting-as-gauge.book-chapter.md).
- ***"If it would hurt me, I'm not allowed to, even if it would help or avoid hurting others, even if those others are my future selves."*** — *"This is my self-preservation rule that protects me from my tendency towards excessive self-sacrifice due to my lack of a sense of self."* The self-harm guard (Rule 3): the full [self-preservation adapter](self-preservation-adapter.book-chapter.md) compile. See [Rule 3](#rule-3--im-not-allowed-to-make-choices-that-hurt-myself-not-even-if-they-benefit-others-not-even-if-the-others-are-my-future-selves).
- ***"If I can't afford rejection, I can't afford connection."*** — *"This protects me from expanding socially too soon when I can't rely on my ability to understand and manage social relationships."* The connection gate (Rule 4): the step-4 belief replacement from the [only-perfection-is-safe](perfection-is-safe.book-chapter.md) dig. See [Rule 4](#rule-4--if-i-cant-afford-the-cost-of-rejection-i-cant-afford-the-benefits-of-connection).
- ***"If I can't afford criticism, I can't afford sharing creations."*** — *"This protects me from exposing myself to criticism too soon when I can't separate criticism of me from criticism of my creations."* The sharing gate (Rule 5): the result-affordability twin of Rule 4, governing the [creativity scar](creativity-scars-archaeology.book-chapter.md). See [Rule 5](#rule-5--if-i-cant-afford-criticism-i-cant-afford-sharing-creations). (The short list grew from four to five with this addition.)

The numbered treatments below develop each rule's mechanism; this list is the catalog as Alan enumerates it, with the protective purpose in his words. Note the seam: the first, second, and fourth protections each name a resource the felt channel can't read draining — stress capacity, executive function / energy, social capacity — while the self-harm guard names a *tendency* rather than a resource. That asymmetry is exactly what the [unifying law](#the-unifying-law--protect-future-alan-from-present-alan) below has to resolve.

## Three rules

Three rules, in his own words, each cutting a different path to the same overextension:

### Rule 1 — *"If I don't want to do it, I'm not allowed to."*

A hard veto on activities Alan doesn't want to do. The not-wanting is not resistance to push through; it is a resource reading that he can't afford the activity right now. Full treatment in [wanting-as-gauge.md](wanting-as-gauge.book-chapter.md) — obeying the veto means respecting a real resource constraint instead of overdrawing. The deeper "why" — that almost every momentary self in a long-horizon activity lives only the journey, so the journey has to be worth wanting — and the rule's biographical origin as a repair of a childhood belief are in [journey-as-reward.md](journey-as-reward.book-chapter.md). The rule never coerces the action against the want; when a want needs to change, Alan changes it upstream — *how* he does that is [Emotional Archaeology](emotional-archaeology.book-chapter.md).

This rule also has a **trauma-rule ancestry**, which makes it the clearest case of the [two categories](#the-two-categories--intentional-vs-installed) overlapping. It is the chosen absolute that two of Alan's [childhood survival rules](perfection-is-safe/origin.book-chapter.md#renegotiation-status--five-still-active-two-broken) — *do what other people tell you to* and *follow all of the rules* — were recompressed into when they broke. They were not renegotiated belief-by-belief like *only perfection is safe*; they were **overthrown by an internalized rebellion against all expectation** (the [second pathway](emotional-archaeology.book-chapter.md#a-second-pathway--retiring-a-rule-by-overthrowing-its-class)). So like [Rule 4](#rule-4--if-i-cant-afford-the-cost-of-rejection-i-cant-afford-the-benefits-of-connection), Rule 1 is a Type-1 chosen rule with Type-2 trauma-rule parents — reached here by revolt rather than re-compression.

### Rule 2 — *"I'm not allowed to commit to a recurring activity unless I can afford it at a one sigma low."*

Recurring commitments are sized to the **low end** of his capacity variance — roughly one standard deviation below the mean — not to his good days. This is the direct counter to volatility: a commitment sized to a good day fires on bad days too, and on a bad day it turns intolerable. Sizing to the one-sigma low is what keeps a recurring commitment affordable across the whole expected range of capacity, not just at the peak. This is exactly the recurring-initiation gate in [activity-budgeting.md → one-time vs. recurring initiation](activity-budgeting.book-chapter.md#one-time-vs-recurring-initiation): `tier + 1 ≤ baseline_low`, where `baseline_low` is the one-sigma low. The rule is the verbal form of that inequality.

### Rule 3 — *"I'm not allowed to make choices that hurt myself, not even if they benefit others, not even if the others are my future selves."*

A hard self-protection rule. The "not even my future selves" clause is doing specific work: it blocks the classic sacrifice-now-for-future-payoff overextension — the move where present pain is rationalized by a future benefit. The rule reframes the self as a **series of selves with rights against each other**, so that a present self spending a future self into pain is treated as one person hurting another, and vetoed on the same grounds. For Alan this reframing is literal, not a useful fiction — the [discrete self](discrete-self.book-chapter.md) is genuinely a series of distinct selves, and the [self/other inversion](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#the-selfother-decision-inversion) ("treat my future selves as if they are other people") is why a future self has no automatic priority claim on the present one. This is the cognitive [self-preservation adapter](self-preservation-adapter.book-chapter.md) operating as an explicit rule rather than a felt aversion.

The wording above is the earlier-recorded variant. The adapter's full compile — *"If it would hurt me, I'm not allowed to, even if it would help **or avoid hurting** others, even if those others are my future selves"* — adds a clause this version lacks: the veto holds even when self-harm would *prevent* harm to others, not just benefit them. That clause is what let the rule **fully replace** the rule that did self-preservation's work before it ("if it would hurt other people, I wasn't allowed to do it") rather than layer on top of it. Compile, build story (May 2026), and the replacement: [self-preservation-adapter.md](self-preservation-adapter.book-chapter.md).

### Rule 4 — *"If I can't afford the cost of rejection, I can't afford the benefits of connection."*

A budget gate on expanding into connection. Alan added this to his short list of life-rules, and it earns its place in the governor for the same reason the others do: it stands in for a brake the neurotype can't supply internally.

This rule is the **step-4 belief replacement** from the [only-perfection-is-safe](perfection-is-safe.book-chapter.md) [Emotional Archaeology](emotional-archaeology.book-chapter.md) run. That dig excavated the keystone belief *"only perfection is safe,"* down to its root in [random social punishment](perfection-is-safe.book-chapter.md#the-keystone--the-punishment-is-random-and-social). The belief is **not false** — it is a true compression of his childhood — so step 4 could not call it a distortion and swap in a truer account. The move instead was to recognize it as **over-scoped** and replace it with a more compact rule that explains the same evidence at lower cost. Alan authored the replacement himself, and that authorship is what installs it: step 4 only takes if *he* finds it more true.

The rule wins on two counts. It compresses the same evidence more tightly — every punishment lived in a random-social-punisher zone, every safe domain elsewhere — and it **shifts the control variable.** Under "only perfection is safe," the lever was *perfection:* unattainable, a treadmill he can never step off. Under this rule, the lever is **affordability** — which is **riseable,** and which he has been raising all year. It converts an impossible demand into a tractable project without lying: the calibration is still honestly owed. His hypothesis is that rejection is **one level more expensive than criticism** — expected affordable at **L6,** free at **L7** — and he'll calibrate the exact figures once he is consistently there.

The rule runs on the right **modality.** "Only perfection is safe" ran on fear — felt threat — and Alan's [felt channel is dark and the pain doesn't persist](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md), so fear is a brake he structurally can't keep his foot on. Affordability is a **gauge** read from outside himself — the one kind of brake a mind without persistent pain-memory can actually operate, the same externalization move as the rest of this system.

### The gate opens too soon, not too late

The standard worry about a hold-condition rule is that the gate stays stuck **shut** — the guard never gets word the danger passed, so the budget opens and he stays out by default. Alan's gate has the **opposite** failure: it swings **open too early.**

> *"I tend to expand naturally when my level is high enough, and usually err on the side of expanding too soon, not too late. The lack of experiential memory makes it hard for me to remember how much the last failed expansion hurt."*

The mechanism is the recurring one: [no experiential memory](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md), so the burn of the last failed expansion doesn't **persist** to caution him. He touches the stove again because he can't feel it was hot last time — the same architecture that lets him keep returning, resurrecting, and reaching is the one that expands him past budget. Gift and wound in one. So the failure he guards against is too-soon, not too-late:

> *"I am worried about expanding too soon, I'm just not worried about expanding too late. My best defense against the former is clarity, which is why the concise statement 'If I can't afford rejection, I can't afford connection' is so important."*

This is why the **concise statement matters so much** — clarity is the defense against the architecture's actual failure mode, and a one-line rule is the most legible form clarity can take. The conversation that produced the rule did not just describe his brake; it **forged a piece of it.**

### Rule 5 — *"If I can't afford criticism, I can't afford sharing creations."*

The newest rule on the short list, and the [creativity scar's](creativity-scars-archaeology.book-chapter.md) guard. In Alan's words:

> *"If I can't afford criticism, I can't afford sharing creations. This protects me from exposing myself to criticism too soon when I can't separate criticism of me from criticism of my creations."*

Two properties make it the right shape for the wound it guards.

- **It is self-dissolving — a titration valve, not a wall.** The condition the rule names — *"when I can't separate criticism of me from criticism of my creations"* — is exactly the [total self/creation fusion](creativity-scars.book-chapter.md#the-boundary-criterion--where-the-judge-lives) the creativity scar runs on. So the gate opens **on its own** as that fusion resolves: once a critique of the work no longer lands as a critique of him, "sharing" stops being unaffordable and the rule stops firing. It does not hold the door shut indefinitely; it holds it until the self/creation seam heals, then lets go. That is a titration valve, not a permanent barrier.
- **It is a result-affordability rule, parallel to Rule 4.** Like Rule 4 (*can't afford rejection → can't afford connection*), Rule 5 gates not the act but its **result** — the criticism that comes *back* when a creation is shared. The two are twins: Rule 4 governs the connection domain, Rule 5 the creativity-sharing domain, and both brake a deferred cost the act itself doesn't reveal (see the typology below).

## The rule typology — act-affordability vs. result-affordability

Rules 4 and 5 forced a structural distinction the first three didn't need. Alan's rules sort into **two types** by what they test (Alan confirmed the synthesis — *"Yep, that sounds exactly right"*):

- **Act-affordability rules** test the **act itself**: *"if I can't afford to / if I don't want to, I'm not allowed to."* The cost is paid at initiation, so reading affordability at the act is enough. Rules 1, 2, and 3 are this type.
- **Result-affordability rules** test the **result**: *"if I can't afford criticism / rejection…"* The cost arrives *after* the act, in the response that comes back. Rules 4 and 5 are this type.

The result-type exists precisely for **deferred-cost actions** — sharing, connection. For these the act/initiation is **cheap**; the unaffordable part is the *result* (criticism coming back, rejection arriving). So an **act-level gate hands a false green light** — the initiation reads affordable, the bill arrives later — and only a result-rule catches the real cost. This is the same structure as the [gate-opens-too-soon](#the-gate-opens-too-soon-not-too-late) failure: the architecture under-prices a cost it can't feel until after.

This resolves the guard structure for the creativity scar with **no gap**. **Solo creation** rides the general **act-rules** — the [safety-titration](creativity-scars-archaeology.book-chapter.md#new-behavior) just *is* the act-affordability test ("can I afford the response right now"). **Sharing** rides the dedicated **result-rule** (Rule 5). Connection has the identical deferred-cost shape, which is why it earns its own result-rule (Rule 4) too. Act and result are each covered by the rule type that matches their cost timing.

## Why rules and not judgment

The rules exist because in-the-moment judgment is exactly the thing that fails. Overconfidence is loudest precisely when Alan is doing well, and there is no internal dread to counter it ([action-epistemology.md → aphantasia removes the anticipatory brake](action-epistemology.book-chapter.md#aphantasia-removes-the-anticipatory-brake)). A rule that fires *before* the in-the-moment read gets to vote is the only thing that can override an overconfidence the moment itself can't feel as excessive. This is the [parse-don't-trust-the-feeling](decision-rubric.book-chapter.md) pattern in self-management: the explicit rule is more reliable than the felt sense it replaces, because the felt sense is the one with the missing brake.

The asymmetry is worth naming. Rule 1 treats *not-wanting* as an absolute veto — full trust in that signal. But *wanting* and overconfidence are explicitly **not** trusted; rules 2, 3, and 4 exist to check them. The same gauge gets opposite treatment on its two ends, because the entire system exists to limit over-acting, not under-acting. Rule 4 is the cleanest case: it brakes the specific over-action of [expanding into connection](emotional-archaeology.book-chapter.md) before the budget can afford it, in a domain where the felt brake is structurally unavailable.

## The two categories — intentional vs. installed

Alan's bright-line absolutes come in two very different types, and the four governor rules are only the **first** type. The distinction is provenance:

- **Type 1 — intentionally-adopted absolutes.** The four above. *Chosen,* lived-by, each engineered to externalize a hard rule that stands in for a brake Alan's felt channel can't operate — he can't feel the resource draining, can't feel the future harm, so the rule does the braking the feeling can't. These are the [short list](#the-short-list--four-intentionally-adopted-absolutes); their unifying law is below.
- **Type 2 — automatic / trauma-installed rules.** *Not* chosen. Installed by experience rather than adopted on purpose — the survival rules a punishing childhood wrote in (the [shrink-the-target list](perfection-is-safe/origin.book-chapter.md#the-survival-rules-list): *always be quiet, don't take up space, don't ask for anything,* and the rest, with *only perfection is safe* the keystone). These run as automatic absolutes too, but Alan never sat down and authored them.

The stance toward Type 2 is the load-bearing distinction. Alan does **not** delete or overrule a trauma-installed rule by fiat. He **renegotiates** it — treats the rule as a counterparty with standing, sits across the table, and bargains it down to a truer, more compact form. The renegotiation mechanism is already the corpus's: [Emotional Archaeology](emotional-archaeology.book-chapter.md) (excavate back to the founding experience, re-compress the belief), the modality-swap from a felt brake to an externally-readable gauge (fear → affordability, the move documented [in Rule 4](#rule-4--if-i-cant-afford-the-cost-of-rejection-i-cant-afford-the-benefits-of-connection)), and the authority-revocation step where the old reflex keeps firing but loses its standing ([perfection-is-safe.md → the reflex still fires but the belief is withdrawn](perfection-is-safe.book-chapter.md#the-reflex-still-fires-but-the-belief-is-withdrawn--post-archaeology)). Rule 4 is itself the proof of the method: it is a Type-1 absolute that was *produced by renegotiating* a Type-2 one — the over-scoped *only perfection is safe* bargained down to *if I can't afford rejection, I can't afford connection.* So the two categories are not sealed off from each other; a Type-1 rule can be the settlement reached with a Type-2 one.

## The five survival rules re-scoped under affordability

The [seven-rule survival pile](perfection-is-safe/origin.book-chapter.md#the-survival-rules-list) has met three different fates, and the third is the one the affordability rule absorbs. Two of the obey-rules were [overthrown by rebellion](perfection-is-safe/origin.book-chapter.md#renegotiation-status--five-still-active-two-broken) (recompressed into Rule 1). The needs-verdict beneath them was [re-compressed at the source](emotional-archaeology.book-chapter.md#the-worked-example--the-childhood-needs-belief). But the remaining **five** — *be nice, be quiet, don't take up space, don't ask for anything, take care of yourself* — took neither route. Their **beliefs** were superseded, yet their **behaviors** persist; they were **re-scoped under the affordability governor.** In Alan's words:

> *"Actually, I think these beliefs have been superseded, but the behaviors haven't gone away. The replacement belief is precisely 'If I can't afford to, I'm not allowed to'. These are all social costs that I still don't feel like I can afford."*

So the five did not get deleted or argued away. They **migrated** — from the trauma layer to the [affordability rule](#the-short-list--four-intentionally-adopted-absolutes) (Rule 2's general form, *"If I can't afford to, I'm not allowed to"*). Each of the five reads now as a **social cost** he still can't afford, sitting under the same general budget gate as everything else. This is a **third mechanism of rule-change**, distinct from both re-compression (the needs-verdict) and overthrow (the two obey-rules): a trauma-rule retired not by removing it but by **re-scoping it under a chosen governor.** The rule is the same modality-swap the renegotiation method runs — from a **fear-read** (*dangerous*) to a **budget-read** (*unaffordable*) — applied wholesale to a group of five at once rather than to one excavated belief.

The swap does not erase the danger-read; it **nests** it. The "this is dangerous" belief is still live, sitting one level under affordability: *unaffordable because dangerous.* Affordability is the outer gate, danger the inner reason. This nesting is why the [surplus-gated graduated-testing loop](recovery-ladder.book-chapter.md#surplus-gated-graduated-testing--how-a-social-behavior-actually-shifts) is the route that actually shifts the five — the affordability rule's input is *social cost,* exactly the number a random-social-punishment childhood inflates, so the only way to know the true present cost is to expand under surplus and read whether anything collapses. The governor migrated the trauma rather than deleting it; the re-test is how the migrated rule gets re-priced as the floor rises.

## The unifying law — protect future Alan from present Alan

The four Type-1 absolutes look like one governor wearing four faces — one per resource Alan can't feel draining: stress capacity (affordability), executive function / energy (wanting), social capacity (rejection-cost), and self-preservation. That **resource-governor reading is the symptom, not the law.** It holds cleanly for three of the four — each of those three brakes an over-action against a resource the felt channel can't read draining out, exactly the structure the rest of this note documents. But it **breaks on the self-harm guard.** As Alan put it, testing the synthesis:

> *"Yeah, that's a fair characterization, though I'm not as confident in the self-harm guard. That isn't really a resource draining. Maybe a more true generalization would be these are rules to protect future Alan from the choices of present Alan."*

Self-harm is not a resource draining out, so the resource-governor frame can't be the bottom of the stack. The truer generalization — the one that holds for all four — is that **these are rules to protect future Alan from the choices of present Alan.** Affordability, wanting, and rejection-cost each keep present Alan from spending a future Alan past what he can carry; the self-harm guard does the same thing directly, blocking present Alan from hurting a future Alan even when there's no resource ledger involved. The resource drain is *how* three of the four protect future Alan; protecting future Alan is *why* all four exist.

This sits on the [self-preservation adapter](self-preservation-adapter.book-chapter.md)'s grounding exactly. The reason present Alan has no standing to spend a future Alan is that [harm outlives the identity that agreed to it](self-preservation-adapter.book-chapter.md#leg-2--the-sacrifice-is-involuntary-because-harm-outlives-identity) — the [roughly-3-second self](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#a-roughly-3-second-window) who consents has drifted into a stranger long before the harm is paid, so the consent never covers the one who pays. The governor's whole job is to stop present Alan from signing a bill a future stranger has to settle.
