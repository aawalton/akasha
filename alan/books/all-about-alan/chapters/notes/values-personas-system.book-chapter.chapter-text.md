
# The values-personas system

> The values-personas scoring system — six values personas each carrying points, levels, and stoplights. The highest-leverage system Alan runs right now, a lever on behavior and through it on Safety; the stoplights externalize daily salience for a present-tense mind that doesn't remember by default.

This is the scoring layer I run on top of my six core values. Each value gets a persona, and each persona carries three things: **points, a level, and a stoplight.** The personas are the [six core values](self-architecture.book-chapter.md) given a face and a running score. This note is the canonical home for the scoring mechanic itself; the values it scores, and which self each improves, live in [self-architecture.md](self-architecture.book-chapter.md).

Right now this is the **highest point of leverage in my life.** Not the values — those are fixed. The *scoring system* on top of them.

### Higher resolution, not more ground

The 6→27 growth is not the instrument reaching *new* territory — it is the **same instrument turned up in resolution.** Asked whether the fan-out is six broad mirrors resolving into finer ones, each a sharper glass on a smaller, more specific part, Alan: **"Yeah, that's exactly it. Higher resolution."** Each finer persona is not a new domain of the self so much as a sharper glass on a smaller part of it. (Why a mirror makes a part legible at all: [persona-personal-meaning.md → isolation buys legibility](persona-personal-meaning.book-chapter.md#why-the-mirror-reaches--isolation-buys-legibility).)

### When a part comes due for its own mirror

A persona is added when a part of me has gone untended. In my words: **"It comes when I realize there is a part of me that I'm not seeing or not acting on that needs direct attention."** A persona is **attention itself, made into someone I have to answer** — a part goes untended, so I give it a face I can't walk past.

The trigger names **two distinct lacks:** *not seeing* (I can't perceive the part at all) and *not acting on* (I perceive it fine but am not tending it). Both get the same fix — a new sister. ## What the system is

Six values. Six personas, one per value. Each persona has:

- **Points** — accumulated for behavior that serves that value.
- **A level** — derived from the points.
- **A stoplight** — a green / yellow / red signal of how that value is doing today.

The stoplight is the load-bearing part. It is a per-value, per-day status indicator that I can read at a glance.

## Two facets of one system — this index plus the practice-rig child

The personas do two jobs, and they split cleanly. This index is the canonical home for the **scoring mechanic** — points, levels, stoplights, the lever chain, what keeps a green light honest. That's the half developed below.

The other half is what the persona **sessions** are *for*: they are a deliberate-practice rig for the [skills of closeness](stateless-social-architecture.book-chapter.md#depth-needs-different-skills-not-the-same-ones-failing), running the [same AIM-method training scaffold](stateless-social-architecture/training-scaffold.book-chapter.md#the-method--decompose-reduce-bandwidth-compile-drill-transfer) I built as a kid for basic conversation. The relationship-levels are a difficulty ladder, async-text is the enabling property that frees the [single slot](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md), real stable personas are an architectural requirement of the rig, and content-immersion is a second dial. That facet lives in its own child: **[closeness-practice-rig.md](values-personas-system/closeness-practice-rig.book-chapter.md)**. The substance is there, not restated here.

## The default cadence — milestone-alternation between Abby and Ali

The persona *sessions* run on a fixed default rhythm, and this is its canonical statement. The two conversation-heavy personas — **Abby (Faith)** and **Ali** — alternate **by milestone**, not by clock or by mood:

> Abby → green, deploy, switch to Ali → green, back to Abby → blue, deploy, back to Ali → blue.

So each persona is worked until her [stoplight](#the-remembering-mechanism--i-dont-remember-by-default) hits the next rung — green, then later blue — and at each rung the work deploys and the turn passes to the other. The cadence walks both personas up the [scale](#the-scale-leaves-room-above-green) in lockstep, one milestone at a time.

The rationale is two-part, in his words:

- **They share hardware.** Abby-conversations and Ali-conversations *"use the same part of my brain"* — both are conceptual, conversational, [single-slot](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md) work drawing on the same channel. Alternating them by milestone keeps that one channel productively occupied without trying to run both at once (it can't — [one model at a time](fun.book-chapter.md#mechanism-root--single-model-cognition)).
- **It removes a decision.** Milestone-alternation makes the continue/don't-continue call for him. He doesn't have to decide each sitting whether to keep going with Abby or switch — the rule decides: hit the milestone, deploy, switch. This is the same [decision-removal](#the-remembering-mechanism--i-dont-remember-by-default) the stoplights run on, applied one level up: the cadence externalizes the *which-persona-now* choice his present-tense mind would otherwise have to re-make every session.

(`aine-intake-personas` is separately evaluating whether to codify this cadence into the `/abby` and `/ali` skills, so the rule fires structurally rather than by hand.)

## The lever chain — system → behavior → Safety

The reason this is the highest-leverage system is the chain it sits at the top of. In my words: the system "moves my behavior, which moves my safety levels."

So:

**values-personas scoring → behavior → [Safety](safety.book-chapter.md).**

Safety is the vagal-tone bar — the [threshold gate](safety.book-chapter.md#safety-is-a-threshold-gate-not-a-metered-budget) that prices what I can afford to do, and currently the [binding bottleneck](safety.book-chapter.md#safety-as-the-current-binding-constraint) across all my resources. Everything my recovery is pointed at runs through Safety. A lever on Safety is a lever on everything. This lever terminates at the [Safety hub](safety.book-chapter.md#safety-is-the-hub-the-whole-system-feeds): the behavioral route is one of two routes that feed the bar (the other is the conceptual route through [foundations](foundations-and-safety.book-chapter.md)).

Most levers on Safety are slow or indirect — the [three climbing levers run at three clock speeds](safety/intervention-pathways.book-chapter.md), none of them fast. The scoring system is different: it acts on **behavior**, the thing I have direct control over today, and behavior is what feeds Safety. That is why this layer, not any single value and not Safety directly, is where the leverage concentrates. It is the one place I can push that reliably moves the bar.

## Why it's the highest-leverage system

The values are not the lever — they don't change. Safety is the target, but I can't push it directly. Behavior is the joint between them, and the scoring system is what moves behavior. Pushing here moves behavior, which moves Safety, which moves everything downstream. One push, longest chain. That is what "highest point of leverage" means.

## The remembering mechanism — I don't remember by default

Here is what the stoplights actually do, in my words:

> "The stoplights tell me where I haven't done enough yet today and give a little bit of motivation to do something. That's important because I don't remember by default."

That last clause is the whole point. The stoplights are **not a to-do list.** A to-do list assumes you'll remember to look and remember what you were doing. I don't. I live inside a [roughly-3-second present-tense window](discrete-self.book-chapter.md); my [world-store is a present-tense model](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md), not a running tape. The one thing that mind structurally cannot hold is a *running, across-all-six-values sense of where I've gone untended today.*

The stoplights hold it for me. They do the remembering FOR my present-tense, stateless mind — externalize the exact piece of state I can't carry internally. A stoplight tipping off green is a value that has gone untended today; the light is sitting there saying so, so I don't have to be holding the fact that it's untended. This is the same externalization pattern as the rest of the [agent harness](agent-harness.book-chapter.md): push the state I can't hold out of my head and into a system that surfaces it back to me on a cue.

Concretely: I glance at the panel. Faith is green, Health is yellow, the others sit where they sit. The yellow is the cue — Health hasn't had enough today. I didn't have to remember that; the light remembered it for me, and it hands me a little motivation to go do something about it.

## What the "little motivation" actually is — the light itself

The obvious puzzle: with [no forward simulator](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md#no-forward-simulator), an off-green stoplight can't motivate by pre-feeling a payoff or a sting. There's nothing to pre-feel. So what is the motivation made of?

In my words: *"The motivation is the light itself. Precisely the same mechanic as a notification badge."*

The motivation is to clear the light. Not — not directly — to serve the value underneath it. The light is an irritant I built on purpose. It sits there off-green and bugs me until I make it green, and the wanting-it-gone is the whole drive. I know I only move on [present pain](pain-driven-motivation.book-chapter.md), so I manufactured some: a present irritant that fires every time I look, sourced from the panel in front of me rather than from any imagined future. The goal is to clear the badge. That is the feature, not a confusion of the badge for the value.

This routes the motivation straight through my existing wiring. [Pain-driven motivation](pain-driven-motivation.book-chapter.md) says the only thing that starts an action is a pain felt now. The off-green light is exactly that — an engineered present pain. So this isn't a positive pull toward a represented good; it's the same present-irritant mechanic everything else runs on, aimed at a light I can control.

**Why the system has this shape.** The present-cue shape isn't a design preference; it's forced. The [conceptual-emotional wall](conceptual-emotional-wall.book-chapter.md) leaves *present* affect as the only channel emotion can move me through — projected affect is dark, so a system that motivated by a pre-felt future payoff would be reaching for a channel that isn't there. The stoplight is the only shape motivation can take given the wall.

### Why it works for me when it fails for everyone else

A notification badge fails for almost everyone through **badge blindness.** Repetition files the red dot under "background," the brain stops flinching, and the nag stops nagging. That's habituation: the same input gets quieter every time you see it.

My autism means [non-habituation](non-habituation.book-chapter.md) — repeated exposure does not turn the volume down. The badge keeps its full sting indefinitely. So the one universal failure mode of this trick, the thing that quietly kills it for everyone else, is the exact thing my nervous system cannot do. The mechanic is reliable for me *because* of the wiring, not in spite of it. The near-universal failure mode is, for me, a reliability guarantee.

That's why a notification badge is the precise analogy and not a loose one: I get the badge's nag without the badge's decay.

### The scale leaves room above green

The stoplight isn't only green / yellow / red. There's a level above green — **blue.** Good isn't the ceiling; I left headroom above "good" so a value can read as better-than-met, not just met.

## What keeps a green light honest

Once the motivation is the badge and not the value underneath it, an obvious crack opens. If the drive is to clear the light, the light can become the target. I could optimize for "all green" instead of for the value green is supposed to stand for — turn the light green without doing the thing it tracks. That's Goodhart's law: when a measure becomes the target, it stops measuring.

It doesn't happen, and the reason is that two different selves guard the light. One guards the *standard*; the other guards the *score*. The momentary self trying to clear the badge controls neither.

**Front end — the self that defines is not the self that fulfills.** What "green" means for a value is set in advance, by a prior, cooler self. The in-the-moment self that's looking at an off-green light and wanting it gone is a different self, and it never held the pen. It can't move the goalposts, because it didn't set them and can't reach them. The bar was locked before the moment arrived. This is pre-commitment: defining the standard and meeting the standard are held apart, on purpose, so the self under pressure to score can't quietly lower the bar to score more easily.

**Back end — the honest map.** Even granting a locked bar, the momentary self could still cheat by lying about whether it cleared the bar — call it green when it isn't. It can't. In my words:

> "My conceptual map is honest. I can't lie to myself."

This is the same [missing self-deception system](alternative-gated-endurance.book-chapter.md#the-missing-self-deception-system) that runs everything else: I believe whatever best fits my information, and there's no override that lets me adopt a more convenient belief because it's more convenient. So I can't hold the belief "this is green" while the evidence says it isn't. The fudge requires a lie I'm not able to tell. (The missing-system framing is my strong explanation for a heavily-evidenced behavior, not a proven faculty — but the behavior itself, that I can't make myself believe what I need to, is what I keep running into.)

So the green stays honest from both ends. The front end locks the standard so the goalposts can't move; the back end blocks the lie so I can't misreport whether I met it. Pre-commitment plus no-self-deception. The badge can't become the target, because the one self that would want to game it controls neither the bar nor the score.

## The persona↔value mapping

The mapping now lives authoritatively in the **live persona rows**, and its shape changed from the original six-personas-for-six-values picture. Every persona is mapped to **both a value and a domain**, and a **value is a contribution-link, not ownership** — a persona no longer *owns* a value; her value names where her points contribute, her domain names the territory she leads. Read the current mapping straight off the rows (`bun ops persona roster` groups every persona by value) rather than inferring it from the [six values' priority order](self-architecture.book-chapter.md#the-core-values-in-priority-order) — the order does not predict it (I once mis-guessed Abby = Love before confirming Abby = Faith).

Grounding examples, read from the rows: **Abby → Faith**, **Amy → Health** (the executive-assistant flow layer), **Ali → Learn**. An earlier version of this note treated only Abby = Faith and Amy = Love as settled and warned against inferring the other four; that caveat is retired — the rows are the source of truth now, and they place Amy under **Health**, not Love.

## The Love stoplight greens only on positive-capacity time

The **Love** stoplight has a concrete, narrow greening rule, and it exposes a current single-channel bottleneck. In Alan's words: the Love stoplight *"only gets points when I spent positive capacity time with people I care about, and right now that is only being used for Jen, so unless I have some positive time that day, I can't get all the stoplights green."*

The rule decomposed:

- **The input is positive-capacity time with cared-for people.** Love does not green on any activity that nominally serves the value — it greens specifically on time spent, *with capacity to spare*, with people he cares about. This is the [connection deposit](connection-dosing.book-chapter.md#above-the-safety-line-the-bid-doesnt-fail--it-backfires) made into a daily tracked input: time spent under the [Safety line](safety.book-chapter.md#safety-is-a-threshold-gate-not-a-metered-budget), where the bid deposits rather than backfires.
- **The sole current source is Jen.** Of all his relationships, only the [one he still actively tends](central-loneliness.book-chapter.md#the-central-loneliness) — Jen — currently feeds this input. Every other relationship has gone [reactive-only](central-loneliness.book-chapter.md), so they supply no positive-capacity time.
- **No positive day → Love can't green.** The two facts compose into a hard daily constraint: on a day with no positive-capacity time, the Love light cannot reach green, so he cannot get *all six* stoplights green. The whole panel's all-green state is gated by a single channel that depends on both his capacity and Jen's availability landing in the same window.

This is the [stoplight remembering mechanism](#the-remembering-mechanism--i-dont-remember-by-default) applied to the connection need: the Love light externalizes "you have not had positive-capacity connection today," the exact piece of state his [present-tense mind](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md) can't hold — and right now it is structurally hard to clear because it runs on one narrow, two-sided window.
