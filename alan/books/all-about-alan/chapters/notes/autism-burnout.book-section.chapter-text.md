
# Autistic burnout

- Description in Alan's words: he overdrew on his nervous system's capacity by an average of 50% per year for 18 years. As expected, this did massive damage.
- Natural experiments allow him to measure the impact: a 99% reduction in capacity to tolerate allostatic load, roughly 20% decline per year compounding annually. The 100x figure is the well-calibrated overall stress-resilience drop, estimated from comparison of pre/post breaking experiences and tolerable-capacity-deficit estimation; the 99%-reduction mental model has felt well-calibrated against the aggregate data. The same 99% read as a *measurement with recovery headroom* — age-anchored (20 → 38), recovered to ~5% (~10% with medication), and anchored to what he did at 1–2% capacity — is the subject of [nervous-system-resiliency.md](nervous-system-resiliency.book-chapter.md).
- Concrete natural-experiment anchor: at age 20, Alan sustained six months of daily Level-6 social load (mission in Novosibirsk, Russia — door-knocking and street outreach in a foreign language) before breaking; at the low point ~18 months ago, less than 90 minutes of comparable load reached the same break threshold. The 90-minute figure is *aggregate cost*, not isolated Tier-6 cost — the breaking event included surrounding Tier-3/4/5 loads, and Tier 6 was likely only ~10% of the total. Reading the comparison as an isolated Tier-6 measurement inflates the apparent drop by roughly an order of magnitude; the calibrated overall drop is the 100x figure above. Full treatment in [social-experience.md](social-experience.book-chapter.md).
- He survived by improving his coping mechanisms by more than 20% per year — until the final year, when an unexpected 50% increase in load broke him completely.
- **16 months into an estimated 3-5 year recovery (as of 2026-05-11)** — the corpus's authoritative recovery-duration figure. It counts from the *surplus-positive turn*, roughly two months after the break, so it runs about two months behind the *time since the low point* (the break was ~18 months ago at the same snapshot, per the concrete anchor above). The two are different clocks — recovery-duration (16) vs. time-since-break (18) — not a drifting figure; other notes cite this 16-month figure rather than restate a divergent number. The **5-year upper anchor** is also the time limit on the [solitude experiment](solitude-experiment.book-chapter.md): Alan holds solitude — stops trying to expand into connection — "for as long as it takes," bounded by this recovery window. The recovery duration and the maintain-solitude duration are the same clock.

## Recovery trajectory

- **Mechanism (corrected).** The 18-year compound decline was a *sustained deficit*, not absent recovery. Alan ran heavy intervention throughout — 4 to 12 hours per day of meditative breathing alone, plus other recovery practices, escalated over time. The interventions worked; they were just outpaced. Stressors ran ~150% of recovery, leaving a ~50% net deficit per cycle — compounding downward at ~20% per year.
- **Sign-flipped recovery.** The same shape works in reverse. Sixteen months of sustained surplus (recovery rate above stressor rate) has produced compounding upward capacity.
- **Quantitative trajectory.** Nervous-system health (Alan's 0-100% capacity metric on the Health bar — see [health.md](health.book-chapter.md)) went from a low point of ~1% to ~5% currently. Roughly 5x in 16 months — a ~3x per-year compounding rate. The parallel [Safety](safety.book-chapter.md) baseline shifted from 3.0-4.0 to 4.0-5.0 over the past four months; current envelope captured in [autism-diagnostic-arc.md → current capacity envelope](autism-diagnostic-arc.book-chapter.md#current-capacity-envelope).
- **What the autism framing changed.** It did not only supply new interventions. It explained *why the prior interventions were insufficient*: chronic baseline overstimulation was preventing the recovery rate from keeping up. Removing that baseline let the same intervention budget cross from deficit into surplus.
- **Trigger of the autism framing → recovery shift.** Captured under [autism-diagnostic-arc.md](autism-diagnostic-arc.book-chapter.md).
- **Purpose re-grounding (2026-06-11).** The recovery months now stand on a derived reason rather than an implicit one: NS recovery was self-improvement via life-improvement, because the nervous system was the self's biggest bottleneck. "I think I was doing the right thing but without having clarified the right reason." See [self-improvement.md → the recovery months, re-grounded](self-improvement.book-chapter.md#the-recovery-months-re-grounded).
- **Capacity-masking during the burnout years (2026-06-11).** "For much of my life any loneliness has been suppressed by my capacity level being too low to even feel the gap." Recovery can therefore *unmask* signals the collapse hid — rising felt loneliness as capacity returns may be a real deficit becoming feelable, not a new problem. The decomposition lives in [solitude-experiment.md](solitude-experiment.book-chapter.md).

### The needs-unmasking hierarchy

Needs suppress and unmask in cost order. Asked whether he expects more suppressed signals to unmask as recovery continues:

> I don't think so. People have always been the highest cost for me, so needs around people make sense as the last ones to unmask. I definitely hit low levels where more basic needs (hunger, thirst, bathroom breaks) would disappear as well. I haven't encountered any additional needs that seem on the same level of necessity.

At the deepest capacity lows, even basic interoceptive needs — hunger, thirst, bathroom — dropped below feeling. People-needs, always his highest-cost tier, unmask last; he expects connection to be the final tier, with no other needs of comparable necessity encountered. Capacity gates which needs are felt at all — the same gating shape as attention on the interoceptive channels ([sensory-experience.md](sensory-experience.book-chapter.md)), where the quiet signals exist only when something lets them reach awareness.

## The recovery stack: cuts plus always-running practices

The 2025 surplus was produced by composing a **cut stack** on top of an **always-running recovery stack**. The recovery stack was already running at full intensity through the burnout years — cutting load is what let it outpace stressors instead of barely keeping up.

**Cuts (2025 onward):**

- [Employment cessation](employment-cessation.book-chapter.md). 80% of total stress-capacity cost. Largest single move.
- [Medical social isolation](medical-social-isolation.book-chapter.md). Family-minimum + church only, all of 2025. Affordable only after employment is gone.
- [Non-discretionary reclassification](non-discretionary-reclassification.book-chapter.md) is the framing layer above both — items not classified discretionary never reach the cost math.

**Always-running recovery practices** (continued from the burnout years; Alan kept these running to survive work, and the same stack now produces surplus instead of barely-keeping-up):

- [Meditative breathing](meditative-breathing.book-chapter.md). 8-12 hours per day, continuous, stacked under most other activities.
- [Sleep](sleep.book-chapter.md). 9-10 hours per day for most of 2025. Chronic under-recovery shows up here as an always-on want-to-sleep signal — the motivational pull-to-stay-up that most modern adults have is absent.
- Hot baths. Roughly daily. See [recovery-rates.md](recovery-rates.book-chapter.md) for the 3x rate anchor.

The recovery rate is what it always was; the stressor rate dropped enough to flip the sign of the balance. See [recovery-rates.md → surplus / deficit balance](recovery-rates.book-chapter.md#surplus--deficit-balance).

## Physiological-only inversion

Twenty years of self-observation: **psychological interventions consistently produce no measurable effect for Alan; only physiological interventions move the needle.** The recovery stack is somatic — sleep, baths, breathing — not cognitive.

- Inverted observation against the standard mental-health framing, which treats psychological interventions (CBT, reframing, talk therapy) as the primary modality and physiological interventions as supportive.
- The mechanism understanding from the autism framing did not *provide* recovery rate. It routed interventions correctly — toward the somatic stack that actually works for him — and identified the load (chronic baseline overstimulation) that needed cutting. Both are upstream moves; the work itself remains physiological.
- Likely tied to [non-habituation](non-habituation.book-chapter.md) and reduced System-1 conversion: psychological interventions that rely on internalizing a new frame until it fires automatically run into the same wall masking and theory-of-mind compensation hit — the new frame stays System 2 indefinitely and never converts to a low-cost automatic response. Physiological interventions don't need System-1 conversion; they act directly on the autonomic substrate.
