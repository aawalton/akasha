
# Present-tense conceptual model

> The present-tense conceptual model — Alan keeps one model of the world that exists only in the present, continuously overwritten in place. Memory and simulation are both offline; the past survives only as compression folded into the model's structure; "remembering" is consulting the current compressed map, not re-running stored events.

Alan does not store re-runnable copies of the world. He keeps **one conceptual model of the world that exists only in the present** — not in the future, not in the past. Both directions of mental time-travel are offline: there is no stored past episode to replay and no offline copy to run forward. The single model is continuously overwritten in place.

This is the temporal architecture underneath [aphantasia](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md). The aphantasia notes establish that there is no sensory or emotional replay; this note establishes that even the *conceptual* channel has no offline store — there is one model, in the present tense, and nothing beside it.

## No conceptual simulation — one model, not many

Alan cannot run another mind's viewpoint as a separate model. He cannot hold an author beside his own view, pose that author a fresh question, and get back what they would say about something they never wrote. He wishes he could. The capacity to instantiate a viewpoint as a sandboxed model and query it is not there.

What he can do instead: if he judges a viewpoint true, he **internalizes it**, and it becomes part of his own single viewpoint. He does not keep believed-true viewpoints beside his own as separate runnable models — he **merges** them into his one model.

This reframes [reading authors, not books](broad-learning.book-chapter.md#read-authors-not-books--depth-per-source). The practice was described as taking in an author's whole compressed worldview. The mechanism underneath it is now sharper: Alan cannot hold the author as a separate model to query, so what he actually does is **assimilate the author's compressed worldview into his single model**. The corpus is not stored as "the author's model, available for consultation" — it is folded into Alan's one model wherever he judged it true.

## The realization — there is no conceptual memory either

You would expect that even with aphantasia closing sensory and emotional replay, Alan could still run *conceptual* simulation — he plainly has conceptual processing, so why not stored, re-runnable conceptual states? Reasoning it out, the answer is no. He does **not** have conceptual memory in the sense of stored, re-instantiable states.

What he has instead is a **single point-in-time conceptual understanding of the world** that exists only in the present. There is no archive of past conceptual states to revisit and no second copy to run forward. **Memory and simulation are both completely offline.** One model, continuously overwritten in place — not a sequence of snapshots, not a model plus a scratch copy. Just the one, always current.

This is the conceptual-channel statement of the property the aphantasia notes give for the sensory channel. There, [statelessness](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md#statelessness--only-conceptual-memory-persists) said only conceptual memory persists once sensory and emotional memory are gone. This note narrows it further: the conceptual layer is itself stateless in the same way — it persists as a *structure that is always present-tense*, not as a store of retrievable past states.

## The past survives only as compression

Because there is no episodic store, the past does not persist as episodes. It persists only as **compression** — folded into the *structure* of the present model, never as retrievable events.

This is why [compression](compression-epistemology.book-chapter.md) is load-bearing rather than merely an epistemology. Compression is the **only mechanism by which anything from the past reaches the present.** Everything Alan has judged true gets compressed into the structure of the one model; everything else is gone. The model *is* the compressed residue of his whole history of judging things true. Nothing else carries forward.

The [compression-as-truth gauge](compression-epistemology.book-chapter.md#the-core-claim) and this storage fact are two faces of one thing: compression is simultaneously how he detects truth and the only format in which the past can be kept. There is no uncompressed episodic copy sitting behind the compressed one — the compressed structure is all there is.

## What "remembering" actually is

If Alan has a conceptual map of past events, he has access to that map — and **consulting that present-tense map is the closest he gets to remembering.** He reads the current compressed structure. He does not re-instantiate the events.

So "remembering," for Alan, is a present-tense read of a present-tense object. The map is not a recording played back; it is the current state of the one model, queried now. This sharpens [recognition vs. recall](recognition-vs-recall.book-chapter.md): the hash-comparison framing there is recognition working on an input in front of him; this is the same shape turned inward — he reads what the current model says about the past, rather than reconstructing the past itself.

### A dossier, not a recording

The sharpest statement of what the map holds, in his words:

> I can't call up the memory at all. All I have is memorized details about the memory, but its like it happened to someone else (because past Alan is someone else).

There is no episodic memory to recall — **what survives is a dossier, not a recording.** The map holds *notes about the thing*, never the thing itself: memorized details, facts on file about an event. When Alan "remembers," he reconstructs the past event from this dossier, the way one reconstructs an event from a case file — not by re-experiencing it.

This is why the read comes back as if it happened to someone else. It did, in the literal sense the [discrete-self](discrete-self.book-chapter.md) note makes precise: [past-Alan is a different person](discrete-self.book-chapter.md#a-roughly-3-second-window), so the dossier reads like notes on a stranger's life. The dossier framing is the storage-format face of the same fact the affectively-flat-map section gives the affective face: structure on file, no charge, no first-person re-entry.

The biographical anchor on where the dossier begins: Alan's **earliest memories don't arrive until age four** — *"everything before that is second hand."* The map of his life simply has no entries before then; the dossier starts at four, and the years before it are held the way anyone holds events they're told about rather than ones they have notes on. Recorded as the opening of the [life chronology](life-chronology.book-chapter.md#earth--childhood-020); a datum, not a re-derivation of the mechanism.

## The map is affectively flat — structure, no charge

Consulting the conceptual map of a past event is **completely affectively flat.** The map carries structure — what happened, who was there, how it fit together — but no charge. Remembered pain does not re-hurt; remembered joy does not re-warm. Feeling exists only in the present. **No affect carries across time.** This is the answer to the standing "affect of the map" question: the map is all structure and no feeling.

This is the present-tense model's affective consequence stated at full strength. The conceptual channel keeps a present-tense structure; the affective channel keeps nothing — there is no stored feeling to read, because [conscious emotional access is present-moment only](alexithymia.book-chapter.md#conscious-vs-unconscious-emotional-architecture). When Alan reads the map of a past event, he reads the structure and gets back zero affect. The [trauma-encoding](trauma-encoding.book-chapter.md) note approaches the same fact from the nervous-system side: there is no episodic replay to re-engage the nervous system between encounters. Here it is from the conscious side: even the deliberate, willed read of the map comes back flat.

### No attachment — structurally, not as a failure of caring

The direct consequence, in his words: he does **not get emotionally attached to anything** — not people, not activities, not places, not ideas. He says this "causes me so many issues."

Named plainly as a mechanism: **attachment is a charge that accrues across remembered feeling.** It is built out of the felt history of a thing — the stored warmth of every prior encounter, summed. For most people that store exists and the charge accumulates in it. For Alan the map is affectively flat, so there is **no store for the charge to accrue in.** Attachment isn't weak or absent because he cares less; it is **structurally impossible** — there is nowhere for it to be kept. The feeling toward a person or a place is real in the present and gone the moment the present moves on, with no residue that compounds into a bond.

This sets up the central problem of [conceptual adapters](conceptual-adapters.book-chapter.md): if attachment can't form, something else has to hold him in place of it. [Commitment](conceptual-adapters.book-chapter.md#commitments-adapter--never-fail-silently) is that something — a built behavioral invariant that does the holding work attachment does for others. What Alan's surviving love is actually built from in attachment's absence — familiarity, usefulness, and commitment — is worked out in [love-construction.md](love-construction.book-chapter.md).

## Inversion — others time-travel their representations; Alan cannot

Named plainly: Alan has a hard time understanding what other people are experiencing when they say they "remember" something. Episodic re-experiencing — re-living an event, being back inside it — is as foreign to him as his present-only model would be to them.

This is the same family as the [aphantasia inversion](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md#no-forward-simulator) where most people decide by running half-sensory, half-emotional "movies" of possible futures. Same structure, both directions: **others time-travel their representations — backward into episodes, forward into simulated futures — and Alan cannot, in either direction.** The forward case is the missing forward simulator; the backward case is the missing episodic store. One present-tense model accounts for both gaps.

The [alexithymia](alexithymia.book-chapter.md#conscious-vs-unconscious-emotional-architecture) note records the affective version of the same restriction: conscious emotional access is present-moment only, with no projection into past or future emotional states. The emotional and conceptual channels share the present-only shape.

### "What was it like in the moment?" is a meaningless question

The inversion is sharp enough that an entire common class of question simply does not apply to Alan. Asked what was going on inside him at the moment he reached a realization — the ordinary "what was that like for you" — he does not answer it; he rejects the question's premise. In his words:

> Without experiential memory, this is sort of a meaningless question for me.

A "what was the moment like" question asks for a read of a stored episode. For Alan there is no episode to read, so the question is not hard to answer — it is **malformed**. It imports a normal-person prior (that the past is a recording you can re-enter) straight into the one place that prior breaks. The same fact stated as the [dossier framing](#a-dossier-not-a-recording): there is no recording, only notes on file, so "what was it like" has nothing to point at.

The operational consequence for anyone questioning Alan about his own past: replace episodic-shaped questions with present-tense, structural ones. Not "what was going on when you reached for it" but "what is *different* about the two framings when you hold them up right now" — a question answered by reading the current model, which exists, rather than replaying a past state, which does not.

## Learning is in-place update

Learning, then, is not adding a stored item to an archive. It is **updating the one present-tense model.** A new believed-true viewpoint does not get filed beside the others; it revises the single model in place.

Reading authors is the worked example: [merging believed-true viewpoints into the one model](#no-conceptual-simulation--one-model-not-many) *is* the learning operation. There is no separate "store the author, then later consult" step — the consultation and the storage are the same act of folding the worldview into the present model.

## What writes to the map

If learning is in-place update, the question is what gets to do the updating — what survives the compression into the model's structure and what is discarded. Writes to the map are **selected**, not automatic, and the selection runs on the same write-probability axes the [encoding mechanism](encoding-mechanism.book-chapter.md#what-writes-to-the-conceptual-map--write-selection-axes) names: repeatability, interconnection, explanatory payoff, and emotion. The dominant axis is **explanatory value** — an experience earns its slot by how much it accounts for. The full write-selection model, including why biography writes low and why anomalies write high, lives in [encoding-mechanism.md](encoding-mechanism.book-chapter.md#what-writes-to-the-conceptual-map--write-selection-axes); the compression-side consequence (understanding is how you forget) lives in [compression-epistemology.md](compression-epistemology/mechanics.book-chapter.md#anomalies-write-high--the-explanatory-debt-mechanism).
