
# Four-resource model

The bars are RPG-style resource bars I track over my life-systems — a stat sheet. The framing is explicit: *"Yeah, stress is Health. I guess Money is another resource bar."* So Health is the stress-capacity bar, Money is a resource bar of its own, and Safety is a bar too. The four below are the nervous-system / energy bars; Money sits alongside them as a non-NS bar, and the [agent harness](agent-harness.book-chapter.md) is the regulator that governs the whole set.

I track four resource bars in my own experience. Each is a continuous variable with consciously discernable thresholds; each is at a different measurement-maturity level depending on its history as a bottleneck.

| Resource | Operational meaning | Measurement level | Doc |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Health | Stress capacity (0-100%). **Health and Stress Level are the same bar** — one is the capacity readout, one the instantaneous load; both are "Health." | 5 (numeric) | [health.md](health.book-chapter.md) |
| Mana | Executive function — neurotransmitters + energy in the brain | 3 (stoplight; "not very numeric still") | [mana.md](mana.book-chapter.md) |
| Stamina | Physical energy — energy resources in the body | 3 (stoplight; "not very numeric still") | [stamina.md](stamina.book-chapter.md) |
| Safety | Vagal tone — long-term parasympathetic regulation | 4 (numbered; level-only, no numeric scale) | [safety.md](safety.book-chapter.md) |
| Money | Non-NS resource bar; sits alongside the four above | — | — |

Why the measurement levels are uneven — a strong numeric gauge costs years of being the bottleneck, and Health is the only bar that has paid that cost; Safety being level-only after ~a year as the binding bar is on schedule, and a [gate-bar may never need a numeric scale at all](measurement-maturity.book-chapter.md#a-numeric-gauge-costs-years-so-a-gate-bar-stays-level-only).

The measurement-maturity framework is in [measurement-maturity.md](measurement-maturity.book-chapter.md). Levels run 0-5: 0 = don't know it exists, 1 = know it exists, 2 = enough/not enough, 3 = stoplight (distribution-anchored), 4 = numbered (absolute), 5 = numeric.

## A fifth resource: connection

[Connection debt](connection-debt.book-chapter.md) is effectively a fifth resource bar. It tracks the [connection need](connection.book-chapter.md) the way the four above track their domains. It is shaped differently — a payable debt stock (sleep-debt-like: non-compounding, no interest) rather than a capacity buffer you draw against — so it sits alongside the model rather than inside the four-bar frame. The "four-resource model" name predates it; reconciling the naming is open (see [resource bars](../../../all-about-alan-topic/resource-bars.all-about-alan-topic.md)).

Connection couples back into the four bars more directly than the others couple to each other: paying it down costs Health/Mana/Stamina/Safety *and* pauses recovery, so NS-health capacity (Health and Safety) is the master variable setting how much connection is affordable — see [connection dosing](connection-dosing.book-chapter.md).

## Hierarchical relationships

The four resources aren't independent. Three of them are time-integrated stacks of stress measurement at different scales:

- **Stress Level** (instantaneous; 0-100, anchors per [health.md](health.book-chapter.md)) — the input.
- **Stress Capacity** (Health bar) — time-integral of Stress Level, with adjustments for direct interventions.
- **Safety Level** (Safety bar) — time-integral of Stress Capacity, drives the cost multiplier.

Mana and Stamina sit alongside this stack as separate resources tracking brain and body energy respectively. Their interactions with the stress stack aren't yet captured; see [interest-modulator.md → Four-resource model integration](interest-modulator.book-chapter.md).

## The migrating-bottleneck governor

The bars don't sit in a fixed hierarchy. The tempting model — Health/stress-capacity as a permanent governor sitting over everything, with the [task pillar](agent-harness.book-chapter.md) (GTD) operating inside whatever Health funds — is wrong. The governing relationship is not fixed; it is set by **which bar is the current bottleneck, and the bottleneck migrates over time.**

In Alan's words: *"The relationship has changed over time, it depends on where the bottleneck is. Currently Safety Level is my primary bottleneck, since I'm following the rules that I'm not allowed to do things I can't afford, which means I rarely hit my resource limits, and what I can afford is set by the Safety Level."*

The mechanism:

- **The current bottleneck bar sets affordability.** Whichever resource is scarcest right now defines the budget — what can be afforded is set by the binding bar.
- **An explicit rule keeps operation inside that budget: don't do what you can't afford.** Because he follows this rule, he operates inside the binding bar's budget and therefore *rarely hits his other resource ceilings at all.* The non-binding bars stay clear not because they're abundant but because the rule never lets spending reach them.
- **The bottleneck migrates.** Which bar binds has changed over the years. Right now [Safety](safety.book-chapter.md) is the binding constraint, so Safety sets the budget — this is also why Safety is the most measurement-mature NS bar (see the table above): sustained time as the bottleneck is what advances a bar's measurement level.

State the inversion plainly: it is **not** a fixed Health-over-tasks hierarchy. It is a **migrating bottleneck where the current scarcest resource governs the rest.** The two named [harness pillars](agent-harness.book-chapter.md#two-governors-on-two-budgets) (task management, stress-capacity management) are two of the bars' governors, but the relationship *between* the bars is the migrating bottleneck, not a static rank order. Right now the affordability budget is priced in Safety; see [safety.md → Safety as the current binding constraint](safety.book-chapter.md#safety-as-the-current-binding-constraint).

### Mana was a real former bottleneck — the migration ran EF → Safety

The lineage is not a clean Health → Safety skip. **For a stretch, executive function ([Mana](mana.book-chapter.md)) genuinely was the binding constraint** — the scarcest bar, governing the rest, the way Safety governs now. This is a real relocation of the load-bearing point, not a measurement artifact: EF held the bottleneck for a period, then was retired, and the throne passed to Safety. The master-variable order runs Health → **EF/Mana** → Safety, with EF a dated era of its own, not a bar skipped over.

**Three things retired EF as the bottleneck.** Alan holds them apart on a *noticeable* vs. *largest* axis — the most noticeable is not the one that mattered most:

- **Nervous-system recovery** — the *slowest* of the three, and the one he expects to matter **most**. The quiet, high-magnitude mover.
- **The harness** — externalizing the executive function he can't hold internally (state, valuation, action-sequencing) onto the screen. Standing compensation that also did dated work relieving the binding EF constraint; see [executive-function.md → the harness as a dated event](executive-function.book-chapter.md#the-harness-as-a-dated-event-not-only-standing-compensation).
- **Vyvanse** — the *most noticeable*, because it is fast and sharp, but **not** the one he expects to matter most.

Alan's anchor, asked whether the harness alone retired EF and handed the throne to Safety: *"Close, the harness helps, but general nervous system recovery also has an impact."* On the two axes: the most noticeable (Vyvanse, fast and sharp) is not the largest (nervous-system recovery, slowest). This is the same magnitude-not-noticeability discipline that runs across his whole psychology — see [importance-over-urgency.md](importance-over-urgency.book-chapter.md). The retirement is reconciled into [mana.md](mana.book-chapter.md), which no longer reads as "Mana hasn't been the primary bottleneck."

## Cost mechanics

Stress Level drives Stress Capacity drain at a multiplier determined by Safety differential. See [stress-capacity-formula.md](stress-capacity-formula.book-chapter.md).

Concrete sensory drains on Stress Capacity (sensory loads that tax the bar even when no specific stressor is active):

- **Auditory load.** See [auditory-sensitivity-bundle.md](auditory-sensitivity-bundle.book-chapter.md). Pre-AirPods, ambient sound without ear protection accounted for ~50% of total daily nervous-system cost. The 50% baseline no longer holds today — Safety recovery has dropped the cost on the same input (see cross-resource dynamics below).
- **High ambient light without sunglasses.** A continuous Stress Capacity drain — the felt cost is regulatory load on this bar, not pain. Inverted from typical light-sensitivity framing. See [visual-sensitivity-bundle.md](visual-sensitivity-bundle.book-chapter.md). Tool quality matters: the [four-tier sensory-regulation hierarchy](sensory-regulation-tools.book-chapter.md) determines how much of the load actually reaches Stress Capacity vs. being capped by the regulator.
- **Tactile load.** Ambient touch input — clothing-shifting-on-skin, fabric grazing one spot, partial cues — drains Stress Capacity unless the touch baseline is saturated. [Tight clothing](tight-clothing.book-chapter.md) saturates the floor and reduces the drain by ~10-25% vs. forced-loose days. **Mana modulates tactile tolerance directly** (parallel to Safety modulating auditory tolerance): the loose-break trigger fires on low-Mana days, suggesting Mana caps tactile tolerance the way Safety caps auditory tolerance. See [cross-resource dynamics](#cross-resource-dynamics) below.
- **Touch and proprioception** through the lotion regimen (see [sub-systems.md](sub-systems.book-chapter.md)).
- **Talking.** Pauses the meditative-breathing recovery protocol (see [meditative-breathing.md](meditative-breathing.book-chapter.md) and [social-experience.md → composition of total social cost](social-experience.book-chapter.md#composition-of-total-social-cost)).

## Reading the bars: wanting as an undifferentiated gauge

The four bars don't surface as four separate read-outs. Consciously, low capacity shows up as a single signal — not wanting to do something — that reports *a* bar is low without saying which. [Wanting-as-gauge](wanting-as-gauge.book-chapter.md) is the system that decodes it: a differential diagnosis across interoception and contextual reasoning matches the present state against each resource's signature (cortisol jitteriness for Health, global-vs-local EF drop for Mana, fatigue for Stamina, a downward activity-tier probe for Safety) to localize which bar crossed its threshold. The gauge is the input to [Rule 1 of the volatility governor](volatility-governor.book-chapter.md).

## Cross-resource dynamics

The four resources aren't independent. The first confirmed coupling — captured in cycle 7 — runs **Health → Mana, Stamina**: low Health directly reduces both Mana and Stamina ceilings. The second — captured in cycle 9 — runs **Safety → sensory tolerance**: the same sensory input costs less Stress Capacity at higher Safety. Worked example is the auditory drop from a 50% pre-AirPods baseline to a rare residual today, on the same input ([auditory-sensitivity-bundle.md → magnitude](auditory-sensitivity-bundle.book-chapter.md#magnitude); also captured in [safety.md → Safety modulates sensory cost](safety/sensory-cost-modulation.book-chapter.md#safety-modulates-sensory-cost)). Likely applies to visual cost too, though that side is inferred — not yet measured.

A third — captured in cycle 10 — runs **Mana → tactile tolerance**: tight-clothing's loose-break trigger surfaces on low-Mana days, not on Safety state directly ([tight-clothing.md → loose-break scope and trigger](tight-clothing.book-chapter.md#loose-break-scope-and-trigger)). The cycle-9 framing routed sensory tolerance entirely through Safety; cycle 10 surfaces the finer-grained finding that Mana also modulates sensory tolerance, at least for tactile. The cross-resource pathway from energy state to sensory tolerance is broader than Safety alone. Whether the loose-break trigger is Mana-only or Mana+Safety joint is still open (see [how the bars pull on each other](../../../all-about-alan-topic/how-the-bars-pull-on-each-other.all-about-alan-topic.md)).

The Health → Mana/Stamina coupling produces a self-defeating window at [Level -2 of the Health scale](health.book-chapter.md#self-defeating-window-at-level--2): Health can be too low to recover Health, because the recovery interventions that pay Health back ([hot bath](hot-bath.book-chapter.md), exercise) cost Mana and Stamina. The bath in particular is the worked example — it's the cheaper-than-exercise path through the same autonomic-shift mechanism, but Mana is its limiting resource on duration ([hot-bath.md → resource cost](hot-bath.book-chapter.md#resource-cost)), and at low Health there isn't enough Mana to spend.

The coupling has a structural shape worth naming: **the recovery move and the resource it recovers can be on opposite sides of the same gate.** When Health gates Mana, and the bath costs Mana to recover Health, the gate closes from the wrong side at the moment the bath is most needed.
