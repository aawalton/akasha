
# Connection conversation with Jen

> Raw first-person journal entry from Alan recounting a hard conversation with Jen on 2026-06-08 about social connection as a homeostasis need and loneliness as allostatic load. Captures the full lived sequence — the mild-cold backdrop, three missed yellow flags before the conversation, the car-ride modality compromise, the four-resource-bars mental model (Companionship/Coexistence, Belonging, Support, Knowing/Being Known), Jen's wrestling read as criticism triggering fight-or-flight, her own deferred-needs pain around his recovery levels, and the feasibility-vs-affordability reframe that left her with more clarity. Preserved faithfully, not distilled.

For background context, I've been coming in and out of feeling sick with a mild cold. Yesterday was a bit worse, so I think that reduced my ability to function at a high level, which was bad timing on my part for trying to do something hard.

In the morning I gave time for my thoughts to settle and then went looking for Jen around 10:30 am, found her in the shower. I think I asked how she was doing, and she mentioned that she was sore from swimming then launched into the full context story of her experience helping Katara (our daughter) get through a difficult challenge at her swim lessons this morning. I successfully listened while not forgetting what I wanted to talk through, but apparently I didn't backchannel effectively enough and Jen felt tolerated more than listened to (she said later). That was a second yellow flag I missed, if I had trouble with the listening conversation, I really shouldn't have pushed for the larger conversation that day.

Once she finished the story after about 30 min later, I brought up that I was interested in having a conversation with me sharing sometime today if she had time and capacity for it. She had a scheduled appointment to pick up a massive lot of Legos (250 lbs, cleaned and sorted) that she had found for me on Facebook Marketplace. I had mentioned I was interested in trying Lego Art as an activity to try to start working through my creativity scars. Since that was a fixed point already, she suggested we talk on the way up. We knew that was a little risky, since these types of conversations tend to go better on the phone where I can walk and she can keep her hands busy doing something else (usually coloring), but we decided to try it anyways with an experiment of having me sit in the back seat in hopes that would break the stress feedback loop from the conversation. I think this was the third yellow flag that I missed, would have been better to wait until we could use our best modality.

For the conversation itself, I started with my new understanding that social connection is a homeostasis need which led to my hypothesis that loneliness could be a significant source of allostatic load on my nervous system. This was new for Jen so it hit her instinctive resistance against ideas that aren't familiar and agreed with, so she starting wrestling with it, which is her normal process. My brain interpreted the wrestling as criticism, and I started getting a fight or flight response, which I'm sure fed back into her stress in my tone, even though she couldn't see my body language. This was the first red flag that I continued past.

After that first part, Jen suggested we try the version where she just listens until I finish communicating and then she saves the questions for the end. I think that was helpful as a mitigation, but it was likely less effective than it could have been because we were both already stressed and didn't take time to drop the stress back down before trying again. Jen had one question that was urgent enough that she forgot the goal of waiting to the end and asked it anyways, then caught herself after.

I tried to communicate my mental model of four resources bars for communication (Companionship was the one she had the question about, pushing back on it since for her Companionship means something more like Intimacy rather than Company, so she proposed Coexistance as an alternative word there). I then tried to communicate my belief that Companionship, Belonging, and Support were all at least yellow, while Knowing/Being Know was in the red. I tried to communicate that having a clearer mental model here is helpful because it helps me know which interventions to target and which are targeting the wrong things.

I think Jen has a deep related pain of her own that this conversation triggered, and I think that blocked her from being able to focus on listening and understanding me, since she was stuck on her own trigger. Her trigger was that since we didn't know what recovery would look like for me, she has been deferring some of her own deep needs until I've recovered enough to be able to handle them. We had a model a few years ago about how certain behaviors for me kick in at different levels, starting with the people who are closest to me and moving outwards, and also progressing from reactive to proactive. We had some times with me at earlier levels where I was able to start meeting some of her needs, so she was hopeful that when I was at higher levels consistently, I would be able to meet those needs consistently. As a result, every time my level has gone up, she's been watching for me to start meeting those needs consistently and I haven't been, so she's been losing hope.

We eventually clarified that the previous model was based entirely around feasibility. Am I physically capable of the behaviors regardless of the cost. Since then, we've introduced rules that "If it isn't affordable, I'm not allowed to do it" and "If I don't want to, I'm not allowed to". My belief is that the levels where behaviors become affordable and I start to want to do things are higher than the levels where I am physically capable of doing those same things, which gives me reason to believe there is still hope that I will naturally start to do those things in the future. I'm not sure how much that gave her hope that she could anchor to though. She did say by the end of the conversation that she felt like the conversation was helpful and she had more clarity at the end around her pain point.

After we came home, I went back to my office to focus on recovery and mostly just existed. Its now the next day and after sleeping I have enough capacity to try to work through this.
