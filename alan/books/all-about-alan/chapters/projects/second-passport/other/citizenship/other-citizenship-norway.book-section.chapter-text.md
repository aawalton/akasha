
Norway is **not in the EU** but is in **EFTA + EEA + Schengen + Nordic Passport Union**. Norway fully permits dual citizenship since 1 January 2020 (the last Nordic country to do so). The Norwegian Nationality Act and the Directorate of Immigration (UDI) govern naturalization; the King-in-Council retains a discretionary grant for special national interest.

## Acquisition at birth

### Jus sanguinis (automatic)
Any child born **anywhere in the world** on or after 1 September 2006 to at least one Norwegian parent automatically acquires Norwegian citizenship at birth, regardless of the parents' marital status or place of birth. Pre-2006 rules followed older legitimacy/registration filters; descent claims for those cohorts require civil-records review.

### Retention requirement (age 22)
Children born abroad with Norwegian citizenship who do **not** accumulate either 2 years' residence in Norway **or** 7 years' residence across the Nordic countries by age 22 must affirmatively apply to retain Norwegian citizenship before turning 22. **This rule was not abolished in 2020** — only the requirement to renounce prior foreign citizenship was removed. Late applications are accepted only where the missed deadline was not the applicant's fault or where loss would be unreasonable.

### Anti-statelessness pathway
Norway has **no automatic jus soli**, even for otherwise-stateless children. A 28 October 2016 Ministry of Justice instruction lets stateless children born in Norway acquire citizenship: 3 years' continuous residence in Norway before applying, with no minimum stay for children under 2, and immediate acquisition if the parents hold permanent residence. The statutory text remains misaligned with the 1961 Statelessness Convention; the instruction is the operative safeguard.

## Naturalization (UDI)

### Standard naturalization (8-year track)
- **8 of the last 11 years** of legal residence in Norway (raised from 7 to 8 effective 1 January 2022). Permanent residence permit required at the time of decision.
- **Norwegian oral B1** (CEFR) — raised from A2 effective 1 October 2022. Either Norsktest or equivalent (Bergenstesten).
- **Civics test** (samfunnskunnskapsprøven, 26/38 to pass) in Norwegian; alternatively, statsborgerprøven (24/36 to pass). Both cover history, geography, democracy, welfare, working life.
- **Self-support** during the 12 months before application.
- **Good conduct** — clean record; convictions trigger quarantine periods scaled to sentence.
- **Continuous residence** — absences capped (typically ≤2 months/year for full credit; partial credit for longer absences).
- Fee NOK 6,500 (adult).

### High-income shortcut (6-year track)
If the applicant's annual taxable income meets **3G** (three times the National Insurance basic amount — NOK 366,675 for 2024, NOK 384,348 for 2025; rises annually with G), the residence requirement drops to **6 of the last 10 years**. Language, civics, conduct, and PR conditions still apply.

### Marriage / cohabitation with a Norwegian
Combined "marriage + residence" must total **7 years** with at least 3 of those years lived in Norway, **and** the applicant must have been married to or registered cohabitant with the Norwegian for at least 2 years at decision. Time spent married while living abroad together counts toward the 7. Effective minimum Norway-residence under the marriage track is 3 years.

### Nordic citizens (declaration / simplified)
Citizens of Denmark, Sweden, Finland, or Iceland aged 12+ who have lived in Norway for the last 2 years and intend to remain may acquire Norwegian citizenship by simplified application — **no language test, no civics test**. Demonstrable understanding of Norwegian or Sami (employer/school letter) suffices.

### Refugees and persons granted protection
**7 years** of legal residence in Norway (one year less than the standard track). All other conditions (B1, civics, conduct, self-support) apply, with limited exemptions for documented hardship.

### Stateless persons
**3 years** of legal residence in Norway with a temporary permit — no permanent-residence prerequisite. Language requirement is the lower A2 level (exception preserved when B1 was introduced in 2022); civics, conduct, and self-support still apply.

### Children of applicants and adopted children
Children of a parent acquiring Norwegian citizenship are generally included in the parent's grant. Children adopted by Norwegian citizens under the Hague Convention acquire citizenship automatically; other adoptees apply through UDI.

## Re-acquisition / restoration

### Notification (lost-before-2020 cohort)
Former Norwegian citizens who lost Norwegian nationality before 1 January 2020 by acquiring a foreign citizenship may re-acquire by **notification** — no language exam, no residence requirement. Filed with UDI; fee NOK 3,200.

### Application route
Former Norwegians who do not qualify for notification can apply for restoration after 2 years' residence with a permit valid for at least one year. Conduct conditions apply (no qualifying criminal charges or convictions since loss).

### Nordic former-citizens
Former Norwegians who hold another Nordic citizenship and re-establish domicile in Norway may re-acquire by notification with **no minimum residence**.

## Discretionary grant (King-in-Council)

The Nationality Act allows the government in exceptional cases to grant citizenship bypassing standard requirements where there is "merit to the state or special national interest." Operationally rare — used for elite scientists, athletes, or persons of unusual national value. Not a route an ordinary applicant should plan around; the Storting itself does not issue parliamentary grants (unlike Iceland's Alþingi route).

## Dual citizenship

Norway permits dual (and multiple) citizenship **without restriction** since 1 January 2020. Naturalizing in Norway no longer requires renouncing existing citizenships. A Norwegian who acquires foreign citizenship after 1 January 2020 does not lose Norwegian citizenship. The 2020 reform generated a 26,000-application surge in the first months and pushed UDI's backlog from ~14,000 to ~40,000 cases.

## Recent changes (2024–2026)

- **Processing-time blowout (2024–2026).** UDI's guided wait climbed from 9–10 months in 2024 to ~25–30 months by Q1 2026 across citizenship and family-immigration queues. UDI explicitly labels its published times "guides, not promises."
- **B1 spoken Norwegian (Oct 2022, still in force 2026).** Raised from A2; the lower bar persists for stateless applicants.
- **8-year residence (Jan 2022).** Raised from 7 to 8 of the last 11 years. The 1-year reduction for refugees was preserved (7 years).
- **Family-immigration tightening (proposed 2026).** Government proposals to narrow parent/sibling reunification for adults with their own families and add Norwegian-language/self-support gates on spouses.
- **Digital UDI portal** for end-to-end filing; ID-porten authentication required.
