
# United Kingdom — Paths to Citizenship (May 2026)

The UK left the EU on 31 January 2020. The Labour government's May 2025 white paper, *Restoring control over the immigration system*, reframes British citizenship as something to be "earned through long-term contribution" — proposing a standard 10-year ILR clock (up from 5), English language at CEFR B2 across the board (up from B1), and a contribution-based "earned settlement" model that can reduce that 10-year baseline. Implementation is rolling: some changes (HPI/Global Talent reforms, Skilled Worker thresholds) already in force; the 10-year ILR is still under consultation as of May 2026. Dual citizenship is fully permitted. The BNO Hong Kong "5+1" route remains open and was expanded 9 Feb 2026.

## Birth in the UK (Jus Soli — Conditional Since 1983)

- **Pre-1983 (full jus soli):** Anyone born in the UK before 1 January 1983 is a British citizen automatically (under transitional provisions of BNA 1981), regardless of parental status.
- **Post-1983 (conditional):** Born in the UK on/after 1 Jan 1983 → British only if at least one parent was (a) a British citizen at the time, or (b) "settled" in the UK (ILR / permanent residence / settled status). Pure birth-on-soil is not enough.
- **Conditional registration:** A child born in the UK to non-settled parents can register as British under s.1(3) BNA if either parent later becomes British or settled while the child is still a minor; or under s.1(4) if the child lives in the UK for the first 10 years of life (regardless of parental status).
- **Backlog:** None — passport processing weeks.

## Descent (Jus Sanguinis — One-Generation Rule)

The default rule is one-generation transmission only.

- **Born abroad to a "British citizen otherwise than by descent" (BCOTBD) parent:** Automatically British "by descent" at birth. (BCOTBD = a Briton who acquired citizenship by birth in the UK, registration, or naturalization — not by descent.)
- **Born abroad to a "British citizen by descent" parent:** Generally NOT automatically British. The chain is broken at one generation.
- **Special transmission (s.3(2) BNA):** A child born abroad to a British-by-descent parent can be registered as British if that parent had themselves lived in the UK for a continuous 3-year period at some point before the child's birth (≤270 days absence in those 3 years).
- **Special transmission (s.3(5) BNA):** Same child can be registered if the family lives in the UK or a qualifying British territory for 3 continuous years while the child is a minor.
- **Historical-unfairness registration:** May 2025 guidance update — applicants who missed out on entitlement due to "historical legislative unfairness, an act or omission of a public authority or exceptional circumstances" may be eligible for special registration consideration (covers a range of pre-1983 gender-discrimination and unmarried-father cases).
- **Timeline:** Registration ~6 months; passport weeks thereafter.

## Naturalization as an Adult (Standard 5+1)

The primary route for non-ancestral migrants. **The 10-year ILR proposal in the May 2025 white paper would re-baseline this; not yet enacted as of May 2026.**

- **Eligibility:**
  - Hold ILR / Indefinite Leave to Remain / Settled Status for at least **12 months** before applying (so 5 years residence to ILR + 1 year ILR = 6 years minimum on the standard 5-year route).
  - **5 years continuous residence** in the UK in the qualifying period, with ≤450 days outside in total and ≤90 days outside in the final 12 months.
  - Pass the **Life in the UK Test** (24 multiple-choice questions on history, civics, values; 75% pass mark).
  - **English language at CEFR B1** speaking/listening (B2 expected from spring 2026 per the white paper; tightening already announced for several work routes from 8 January 2026).
  - "Good character" (clean criminal record, tax/immigration compliance).
  - Intent to continue residing in the UK.
- **Timeline to citizenship:** 5 years residence + 1 year ILR + ~6 months processing = ~6.5 years on current rules. Under the 10-year ILR proposal: 10 years + 1 year ILR ≈ 11+ years (with possible reductions for "contribution" — work, NI payments, no benefits, clean record, community contribution).
- **Backlog:** ~6 months processing typical; many local councils book citizenship ceremonies within 3 months of approval.
- **Fees:** £1,605 (adult) for naturalization + £19.20 Life in UK test + £80 ceremony.

## Naturalization by Marriage (3+0)

- **Eligibility:**
  - Married to or civil partner of a British citizen.
  - **3 years continuous residence** in the UK (≤270 days outside total, ≤90 in final 12 months).
  - Hold ILR / Settled Status at time of application (no 12-month wait after ILR, unlike standard route).
  - Life in UK Test + English B1 (B2 expected).
  - Good character.
- **Timeline:** Spouse visa 5 years → ILR → immediate naturalization = ~5 years from arrival.

## BNO (Hong Kong) Route — 5+1

Special status created in response to the 2020 National Security Law; opened 31 January 2021.

- **Eligibility:** Hong Kong residents holding British National (Overseas) status (and from 9 Feb 2026, adult children who were under 18 on 1 July 1997 can apply independently). Family members can accompany.
- **Path:** 5 years on BNO visa → ILR (180-day-absence-per-year rule, Life in UK, B1 English) → 12 months ILR → naturalization. Total 6 years.
- **Backlog:** Stable; processing 12 weeks visa, 6 months ILR, 6 months naturalization.
- **Recent change:** 9 Feb 2026 expansion to "TBN" adult children of BNO status holders.

## UK Ancestry Visa → Naturalization (5+1, Commonwealth Only)

- **Eligibility:** Commonwealth citizen aged 17+ with at least one grandparent born in the UK, Channel Islands, Isle of Man, or pre-1922 Ireland. Must be able and intending to work. **US citizens are NOT eligible** (US is not a Commonwealth member).
- **Path:** 5-year ancestry visa → ILR (≤180 days absent in any 12 months, continuous employment evidence, Life in UK, B1 English) → 12 months ILR → naturalization. Total ~6 years.
- **Backlog:** Standard work-route processing; no special delays.
- **White-paper risk:** The proposed 10-year general ILR clock could extend this route too; consultation pending.

## Registration Paths

- **Children born in the UK (s.1(3), s.1(4) BNA):** Register a child if a parent later becomes British/settled, OR if child has lived in UK first 10 years of life. Fee £1,214; routinely waived for looked-after children and financial hardship.
- **Stateless minors (Sch 2 BNA):** Children born in the UK who cannot acquire any other nationality can register as British (paragraph 3A requires demonstrating no entitlement from either parent's nationality, ages 5–17). Right falls away at 18.
- **BOTC → British citizen registration (s.4A BNA):** A British Overseas Territories citizen who is not already a British citizen may register as a British citizen. Most BOTCs are already British citizens automatically under the British Overseas Territories Act 2002 (effective 21 May 2002) — this path covers post-2002 BOTC naturalizations.
- **Windrush Scheme:** Free British citizenship for Commonwealth nationals who arrived in the UK before 1973 (or their children born in the UK / who arrived before 18). No fee. Includes registration, naturalization, and documentation correction.
- **Discretionary minor registration (s.3(1)):** Home Secretary may register any child under 18 as British citizen in their absolute discretion (typically used for long-residence children not otherwise eligible).

## Resumption (Former British Citizens)

- **s.13 BNA 1981:** Former British citizens who **renounced** (typically to acquire another country's citizenship which prohibited dual nationality) may resume British citizenship by registration.
- **As of right (once):** Available without restriction if the renunciation was necessary to acquire or retain another nationality. **One-time entitlement** — second renunciation/resumption requires Home Secretary discretion.
- **Backlog:** ~6 months processing.
- **Note:** Resumption is not available after deprivation (compulsory loss for treason, fraud, terrorism, etc.).

## Discretionary Grants — "Exceptional Circumstances" / Distinguished Service

- The Home Secretary retains discretion under s.6(1) BNA to waive certain naturalization conditions (residence, language, Life in UK) for "exceptional circumstances" — most commonly used for distinguished service to the UK, scientific/cultural achievement, or hardship cases.
- Not a planning path; case-by-case, rare grants.

## British Overseas Territories Citizenship (BOTC)

- **Acquisition:** BOTC by birth (in a territory to a BOTC/settled parent), descent (one-generation rule), or naturalization (5 years residence in a territory + 1 year with belonger/permanent-resident status — 3 years if married to a BOTC).
- **Automatic British citizenship:** Since 21 May 2002 (British Overseas Territories Act 2002), every BOTC is also a British citizen automatically (except those whose only connection is to the Sovereign Base Areas of Akrotiri and Dhekelia).
- **Practical use:** BOTC routes (e.g., Bermuda, Cayman, Gibraltar, BVI naturalization) provide a parallel path to UK citizenship with territory-specific requirements; can be faster or slower than direct UK route depending on territory.
- **Other historical statuses:** British Overseas Citizen (BOC), British Subject, British Protected Person, BN(O) — generally non-transmissible residual statuses; most do not give right of abode in the UK without further registration.

## Comparison Summary

### Ranked by speed (assuming eligibility)

1. **Pre-1983 UK birth / BCOTBD parent abroad** — automatic; passport in weeks.
2. **Resumption** — ~6 months processing.
3. **Marriage (with British spouse already on spouse visa route)** — ~5 years arrival → ILR → naturalization.
4. **BNO 5+1** — 6 years.
5. **UK Ancestry 5+1 (Commonwealth only)** — 6 years.
6. **Standard naturalization (5+1)** — 6–6.5 years on current rules.
7. **Under proposed 10-year ILR** — 11+ years for standard route (unless "contribution" reductions earned).

### Ranked by likelihood (US citizen, no UK ancestry)

1. **Skilled Worker / Global Talent / HPI → ILR → naturalization** — primary realistic path (see residency/uk.md).
2. **Marriage to British citizen** — 5 years.
3. **Self-sponsorship via UK company purchase + Skilled Worker** — 6 years if executed cleanly.
4. **Investor route** — closed Feb 2022, no replacement.
5. **UK Ancestry** — not available to US citizens.
6. **Resumption** — only if previously held UK citizenship.

### Dual citizenship

- **Fully allowed.** The UK imposes no restriction on holding multiple nationalities, and naturalization does not require renunciation of prior citizenship. The US likewise allows dual citizenship.
- **Diplomatic limitation:** The UK cannot provide consular assistance in the country of the other nationality.

### Recent / flag-worthy changes (2024–2026)

- **May 2025 white paper:** Standard ILR clock proposed 5 → 10 years; English B1 → B2 across the board; "earned settlement" contribution model; higher settlement fees signaled. Under consultation; not yet enacted as of May 2026.
- **8 January 2026:** English language threshold tightened for HPI and other work routes (B2).
- **9 February 2026:** BNO route expanded to TBN adult children of BNO status holders.
- **22 July 2025:** Skilled Worker salary threshold raised to £41,700 (general); RQF 6+ requirement reinstated; care worker overseas sponsorship closed (transitional rules to 22 Jul 2028).
- **17 February 2022:** Tier 1 Investor closed (extensions allowed until 17 Feb 2026, ILR applications until 17 Feb 2028).
- **April 2024:** Family visa minimum income £18,600 → £29,000; planned £38,700 hike paused under Labour pending MAC review.
- **May 2025 Home Office guidance:** Special registration consideration for historical-unfairness cases.
