
## Overview

Switzerland is **not** an EU or EEA member but is part of **EFTA** and the **Schengen Area**. EU/EFTA nationals enter under the bilateral Agreement on the Free Movement of Persons (AFMP) with a near-automatic right to a B permit on proof of employment, self-employment, study, or sufficient means. **Third-country nationals (including US citizens)** face strict annual quotas, an "economic interest" test, and a labor-market priority rule (Swiss + EU/EFTA candidates take precedence). Permits are administered cantonally; the federal SEM ratifies third-country admissions.

## The permit ladder

### L permit (short-term)
- Valid for the duration of a work contract under 12 months. Maximum 24 months (with one renewal).
- Tied to a specific employer and canton.
- Does **not** count toward the C-permit residence clock the same way as B-permit time.

### B permit (residence)
- Issued for contracts of 12+ months, study, family reunification, or recognized residence purposes.
- Third-country nationals: initial validity 1 year, annually renewable.
- EU/EFTA nationals: 5-year validity (workers with open-ended contracts).
- Tied to employer and canton in the early years; mobility loosens at renewal.

### C permit (settlement / permanent residence)
- Indefinite residence; near-full rights short of voting and elective office.
- **Standard track**: 10 years of B-permit residence for most third-country nationals.
- **Reciprocity track**: **5 years** for nationals of the **US, Canada, UK**, and certain other states with a Swiss reciprocity agreement. (EU/EFTA nationals: 5 years.)
- "Early" C permit (after 5 years instead of 10) also available to other third-country B holders who meet elevated integration criteria (language at B1 oral / A1 written, professional integration, no welfare reliance).
- Once held, the C permit becomes the prerequisite for naturalization.

### G permit (cross-border / frontier worker)
- For workers living abroad in an adjacent EU border zone and working in Switzerland with weekly return.
- Validity 5 years (EU/EFTA) tied to employment.
- 2026 telework framework allows up to 40% remote from the country of residence without losing tax/social-security status.
- **Does not provide Swiss residence** — it is a work-only permit. No path to C / citizenship from G.

## Work-based residence (third-country, e.g., US)

### Skilled worker (B / L)
- The dominant third-country path. Requires a Swiss employer who can demonstrate **economic interest**, the role is for a specialist / executive / highly qualified worker, and no suitable Swiss or EU/EFTA candidate was found (labor market test).
- Salary at standard local/sector wage.
- **2026 federal quota**: 8,500 third-country permits — 4,500 B and 4,000 L. Federal Council left quotas unchanged from 2025. Cantonal sub-quotas apply.
- 2024 utilization was around 74%, so quota exhaustion is not the typical bottleneck — the economic-interest and labor-priority tests are.
- Path to C permit: 5 years (US/Canada/UK reciprocity) or 10 years (other).

### Self-employed / entrepreneur (third-country)
- Must show the activity is in Switzerland's "overall economic interest": job creation for Swiss/EU/EFTA workers, capital investment, innovation, regional development.
- Business plan, projected balance sheet, evidence of seed capital, sufficient income to cover operating costs and personal living expenses.
- Counted against the same third-country quotas as employed workers.
- Bar is high in practice; rejections common where the business cannot show genuine new value to the Swiss economy.

## Wealth-based residence — the de facto Golden Visa analog

Switzerland has **no Golden Visa** (no residence-by-investment, no residence-by-donation, no real-estate program). The closest analog is the **lump-sum taxation** regime, which is a *fiscal* arrangement that anchors a residence permit application under "predominant fiscal / economic interest" cantonal provisions.

### Lump-sum taxation (forfait fiscal / Pauschalbesteuerung)
- Wealthy non-Swiss nationals are taxed on **deemed living expenses** rather than worldwide income and wealth. The taxable base is typically 7x annual housing rent / rental value, subject to floors.
- **2026 federal minimum taxable base**: CHF 435,000 (CHF 400,000 floor for EU/EFTA citizens). Cantons set their own (often higher) minimums.
- **Eligibility**: non-Swiss national, taking up first-time or post-10-year-absence Swiss residence, **no gainful employment in Switzerland** (foreign-sourced employment / passive income is fine).
- Available in most cantons. **Abolished at the cantonal level** in Zürich (2009), Schaffhausen (2012), Appenzell Ausserrhoden (2012), Basel-Stadt (2014), and Basel-Landschaft (2014). (Federal-level lump-sum still applies in those cantons but the cantonal/communal portion taxes worldwide income, making it impractical.)
- Most attractive cantons: Vaud (largest population of lump-sum residents, 1,400+), Valais, Geneva, Ticino, Graubünden, Zug, Bern, Schwyz, Fribourg, Lucerne.
- Pairs with a **B permit** under cantonal "particular economic / fiscal interest" provisions. Counts toward the C-permit clock (5 years for US/Canada/UK, 10 years otherwise). Lump-sum status is independent of the permit — losing one does not automatically lose the other but the cantonal interest finding may be revisited.

### High net worth / "particular economic interest" cantonal permits
Cantons may grant a B permit under economic-interest provisions to applicants who do not work in Switzerland and bring substantial fiscal contribution. In practice this category is **the wrapper** through which the lump-sum tax population obtains residence.

## Retirement / private means

### Retiree permit (over-55, no work)
For non-EU/EFTA retirees:
- Age **55+** at application.
- **No gainful employment** in Switzerland.
- Sufficient financial resources to support self (and any dependents) without state aid — practical floors around CHF 25,200/year for a single applicant and CHF 31,200 for couples, though cantons routinely expect substantially more.
- **Close personal ties to Switzerland** (prior stays, family, language, cultural integration). This is a discretionary threshold — pure financial sufficiency is not enough.
- Mandatory Swiss health and accident insurance.
- Resident in Switzerland 183+ days per year.
- Issued by cantonal authorities under SEM oversight. Counts toward C permit (5 years US/Canada/UK reciprocity, 10 otherwise).

## Family reunification

- **B permit holders** can sponsor a spouse and minor children if marriage/partnership is recognized in Switzerland, accommodation is adequate, and the family will not rely on social assistance.
- **C permit holders** have stronger family reunification rights with shorter time limits.
- **EU/EFTA** sponsors have broader rights (ascendants and descendants up to age 21 or dependent).
- **Third-country** sponsors typically limited to spouse and minor children.
- Sponsored family members generally have work rights (except parents/grandparents of L holders).

## Student permit

- B permit tied to admission at an accredited Swiss institution.
- Proof of financial means and accommodation.
- Limited work rights (typically 15 hrs/week during term).
- Student years count toward C-permit residence only after switching to a qualifying work permit; on its own the student period does **not** convert directly to a C permit.

## Posted workers / other

- **Posted workers** (employees of foreign firms sent to Switzerland) work under the AFMP rules (EU/EFTA) or the 90-day notification regime; no Swiss residence acquired.
- **G permit (cross-border)**: see above — no Swiss residence.
- **Digital nomad visa**: Switzerland has **no formal program**. EU/EFTA nationals may work remotely up to 90 days under freedom of movement; third-country nationals cannot legally reside as remote workers without one of the permits above.

## Recent changes (2024-2026)

- **2026 third-country quotas**: unchanged from 2025 (8,500 total = 4,500 B + 4,000 L). Federal Council confirmed late 2025.
- **G permit**: now issued as a biometric card; applications via Easy-Gov.swiss / cantonal portals. The 40% telework framework with all neighboring states became permanent.
- **Lump-sum taxation**: federal minimum base remains CHF 435,000 (CHF 400,000 EU/EFTA floor); no new cantonal abolitions since 2014.
- **EU/EFTA framework**: ongoing institutional package negotiation with the EU continues — no change to current AFMP rights as of May 2026.
