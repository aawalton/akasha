
Italy grants no investor-residency shortcut to citizenship. Every permit on this page feeds the standard **10-year legal-residence + B1 Italian + clean record + ~€8,263/yr income** naturalization clock under Art. 9 Law 91/1992. Anagrafe registration in a comune (not visa issuance) starts the clock. Processing of the naturalization decree itself adds **24-36 months** on top of the 10 years. Dual citizenship is permitted; no renunciation required for a US-citizen applicant.

## Active programs (within profile)

### Investor Visa ("Golden Visa") — included tiers

- **Capital**: **€250,000** in a qualifying Italian innovative startup, OR **€500,000** equity in an Italian limited company (S.p.A. or S.r.l.). Higher tiers (€1M philanthropy, €2M government bonds) are out-of-profile.
- **Process**: pre-approval model — applicant obtains a **Nulla Osta** from the Investor Visa Committee (Comitato Investitori) *before* wiring funds; investment must complete within 3 months of entry.
- **Validity**: 2-year residence permit, renewable for 3 years if the investment is maintained.
- **Physical presence**: **no minimum-stay requirement** for permit validity — uniquely permissive among EU golden visas. But naturalization separately requires actual continuous anagrafe residence.
- **Citizenship clock**: standard 10-year track. Permit allows but does not require presence; the clock only runs while anagrafe-registered and physically present per the absence rules below.
- **Processing**: typically **3-6 months** for Nulla Osta + visa.

### Elective Residency Visa (Residenza Elettiva)

- **Income**: legislative floor **~€31,000/yr** passive income for a single applicant; **+€11,400** for a spouse; +20% per minor child (consulates frequently apply 200-300% internal benchmarks, so plan for **€35-40k** single / **€50k+** couple). Income must originate **outside Italy** from non-employment sources (pensions, dividends, rentals, royalties). Savings alone do not qualify.
- **Critical limitation**: **no work permitted in Italy** — neither employment nor self-employment. Foreign self-employment income is in a grey zone; consulates have rejected applicants whose income visibly comes from active work. Pure passive sources are the safest fit.
- **Housing**: signed long-term rental or deeded property in Italy is mandatory before applying (no hotel bookings, no third-party hospitality).
- **Validity**: 1-year permit, renewable in 2-year increments.
- **Physical presence**: must transfer real residence to Italy; absences capped at 6 consecutive / 10 cumulative months over any 5-year window to preserve permit and PR clock.
- **Citizenship clock**: counts fully toward the 10-year naturalization clock.
- **Processing**: up to **90 days** at consulate.

### Self-Employment Visa (Lavoro Autonomo)

- **Quota gated**: subject to the **Decreto Flussi** annual quota — historically tiny (a few hundred autonomo slots nationwide, allocated by click-day in February). The 2026 quota opened 2025-10-23 with click-days **2026-02-16 and 2026-02-18**.
- **Eligibility**: regulated profession (with Italian professional-body registration) OR business owner / freelancer with proven business viability, Italian-side accommodation, minimum income ~**€8,400/yr**, and any required licences (Nulla Osta from the local Questura / Camera di Commercio).
- **Validity**: 2-year permit, renewable; converts to long-term EU residence after 5 years.
- **Physical presence**: real residence in Italy expected; standard absence caps apply.
- **Citizenship clock**: counts fully.
- **Practical fit**: low odds for an out-of-profile applicant in a non-regulated field — quota is the binding constraint, not eligibility. Treat as a fallback, not a primary route.

### Digital Nomad / Remote Worker Visa (DNV)

- **Launched**: published **2024-04-04** via Decree Law 79/2024 (effective immediately).
- **Income**: **~€28,000/yr** floor (3× the Italian healthcare exemption); consulate practice often demands **€32,400+**. Plus **€30,000 in savings** and ≥6 months prior remote-work / freelance experience. University degree or equivalent professional qualification required.
- **Work**: only remote work for non-Italian employers or non-Italian freelance clients; classified as "highly skilled".
- **Housing**: signed lease or deed required at application.
- **Validity**: 1-year permit, **renewable for 2-year periods** (annual income re-verification).
- **Physical presence**: real residence in Italy; standard absence caps.
- **Citizenship clock**: **counts fully** — PR eligible at 5 years (+ A2 Italian), naturalization at 10 years (+ B1). Confirmed PR-feeding.
- **Processing**: ~30-90 days at consulate; **best primary fit** for an applicant with portable self-employment income and no €500k+ to lock up.

### Family Reunification (Ricongiungimento Familiare)

- **Eligibility**: only available *after* the principal applicant has held an Italian permit ≥1 year — derivative, not a standalone entry route. Bring spouse, minor children, dependent adult children, and dependent parents (no other children in country of origin).
- **Income (sponsor)**: **≥€10,651/yr** (1.5× the 2026 social allowance of €7,101.12) + per-dependent increases.
- **Housing**: certificate of adequate accommodation from comune or ASL.
- **Validity**: family-reason permit tracks the sponsor's permit cadence.
- **Physical presence**: standard absence caps.
- **Citizenship clock**: counts fully for the joined family member from their own anagrafe-registration date.
- **Processing**: Nulla Osta up to **150 days**; visa within 6 months of Nulla Osta issuance.

### Long-Term Study Visa (Studio)

- **Eligibility**: acceptance at a recognized Italian university (bachelor's, master's, PhD) plus proof of funds (~€7,400/yr) and health insurance.
- **Validity**: matches program duration (annual renewal).
- **Physical presence**: must actually study; permit converts post-graduation to a 12-month job-search permit or a work / autonomo permit.
- **Citizenship clock**: time on a study permit **counts** toward the 10-year clock once anagrafe-registered (some agencies cite a halved 5-year path for graduates of Italian universities, but Article 9 text remains 10 — verify case-by-case at application time).
- **Processing**: ~1-3 months at consulate.

## Excluded (out of profile)

- **Investor Visa €1M philanthropy / €2M government bonds** — exceed €500k capital cap.
- **Lavoro Subordinato (employer-sponsored)** — requires Italian employer sponsorship; excluded by employer-sponsored filter.
- **EU Blue Card** — requires Italian employer; same exclusion.
- **Working Holiday / start-up visa endorsements** — endorsement-based; excluded.
- **Short-stay Schengen / tourist** — does not feed PR or citizenship clock.

## Recent reforms (2024-2026)

- **2024-04-04**: Digital Nomad / Remote Worker Visa launched (DL 79/2024). First Italian PR-feeding remote-work track.
- **Decreto Flussi 2026-2028**: total quota **164,850** for 2026 across all categories (lavoro subordinato dominates; autonomo is a sliver). Click-days **2026-02-16 / 2026-02-18**. Pre-fill portal opened 2025-10-23.
- **2025**: Rome administrative court ruling against consulates applying full €31k/yr Elective Residency floor *per minor child* — modest liberalization for family applicants.
- **Article 9 (10-year clock) unchanged** as of May 2026; B1 language and naturalization processing window (24-36 months) both still in force.

## Presence requirement (citizenship clock)

Italy is a **standard 183+ days/year** residency regime, not a strict ~10-month-floor regime. Three stacked rules govern the citizenship-clock floor:

- **Anagrafe (population registry) continuity is the gate.** Per TAR Lazio (8 May 2020) and TRGA Trento (14 Jan 2022, n. 3), "legal residence" under Art. 9 Law 91/1992 is anagrafe-registered residence — not mere physical presence. A `cancellazione per irreperibilità` (cancellation for unreachability) **resets the clock to zero** from re-registration; gaps do not aggregate. This is the load-bearing rule.
- **Civil Code Art. 43 + case law set the substantive floor.** `Residenza` = `dimora abituale` (habitual dwelling); the Cassazione interprets this as the place to which one returns "habitually and systematically" once temporary work / study / family commitments end. There is **no statutory day count**, but the Italian tax-residency primary test (TUIR Art. 2, post-Jan-2024 reform) sets **>183 days/yr including fractions** as the physical-presence threshold — and tax residency and anagrafe-registered residenza are formally aligned. Below 183 days/yr the applicant is presumed non-resident and at risk of both cancellation and a citizenship-clock challenge.
- **Permit / PR absence caps are the outer envelope.** Annual permits: no ≥6-month continuous absence. The 5-year PR-qualifying window: ≤6 consecutive months and ≤10 cumulative months out of Italy (Art. 9 Legislative Decree 286/1998). EU long-term resident status itself is lost after 12 consecutive months outside the EU.

**Defensible operating floor**: **≥183 days/yr physically in Italy, continuous anagrafe registration, no single absence ≥6 months, ≤10 cumulative months absent in any 5y PR window.** A summer trip back to the US of 8–10 weeks is well inside this envelope; many practitioners report families maintaining anagrafe-registered residence on roughly this profile.

**Comune-variability gotcha**: `verifiche anagrafiche` (vigili urbani home visits during the 45-day registration assessment phase) are uneven — large Comuni (Roma, Milano) generally verify, small Comuni often do not. **Failed home visits trigger a 3-strike sequence ending in cancellation** even mid-registration, so set up a credible doorbell / mailbox / neighbor-acknowledgement footprint before the first visit and respond to any `convocazione` (summons) within the stated window. Post-registration, an absent year + missed checks is the canonical irreperibilità path.

**Investor Visa is the one exception**: the permit itself imposes **no minimum stay** for the first 5 years — but anagrafe-registered residence + the 183-days floor still gate the citizenship clock. The Investor Visa is a citizenship-clock-feeding tool only if you actually live in Italy; otherwise it is a Schengen-mobility tool.
