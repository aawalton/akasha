
## Surviving programs

### Freiberufler — liberal-professions visa (§21 Abs. 5 AufenthG)

- **Who qualifies**: members of the *Katalogberufe* — IT consultants, engineers, architects, doctors, lawyers, tax advisors, journalists, translators, artists, scientists, teachers. Software/tech freelancers fit cleanly. US citizens may enter visa-free and file at the local Ausländerbehörde during the 90-day stay.
- **Capital / income**: no statutory minimum. Practice expects ~€9,000–12,000 in seed reserves plus a viable income forecast (~€2,500–3,500/mo to clear *Lebensunterhalt*). Older applicants (>45) must show pension cover by age 67 — monthly €1,612.53 for 12 yr or ~€232k in assets (rates effective 1 Jul 2025).
- **Documents**: qualification proof (degree / portfolio), letters of intent from German-resident clients (typically ≥2), Finanzamt tax-number application, business plan + 3-yr revenue forecast, private German health insurance, *Anmeldung* (address registration).
- **Validity**: initial residence permit up to 3 years (often 1 yr first cycle, then 2–3); renewable on continued viability.
- **Path to settlement**: Niederlassungserlaubnis after **5 years** for Freiberufler (§9 standard track — §21 Abs. 4 fast-track 3 yr only applies to §21 Abs. 1 Selbständige, not Abs. 5 liberal pros). Requires B1 German, civics ("Leben in Deutschland") test, 60 months pension contributions or equivalent private cover, secured livelihood.
- **Citizenship clock**: counts toward §10 StAG 5-yr naturalisation (post-StARModG, 27 Jun 2024). B1 + civics + secure livelihood; dual citizenship now allowed — no renunciation of US passport required.
- **Physical presence**: no statutory day count for the permit itself, but absences >6 months void the permit (§51 AufenthG). 183-day tax residency triggers German worldwide-income taxation.
- **Processing**: 6–10 weeks at the Ausländerbehörde; appointment backlogs in Berlin/Munich can push to 3–6 months.

### Selbständige Tätigkeit — entrepreneur visa (§21 Abs. 1 AufenthG)

- **Who qualifies**: founders/owners of a commercial business (GmbH, UG, sole trader-Gewerbe). Reviewed by the Ausländerbehörde with IHK chamber + Finanzamt input.
- **Test**: (a) economic interest or regional need, (b) positive economic effects expected, (c) secured financing. No fixed capital floor since the 2012 reform removed the old €250k/5-employee bar — but a credible plan typically shows €25k–€100k+ working capital and a multi-year P&L. *Under the €500k filter — passes.*
- **Validity**: up to 3 years, renewable.
- **Path to settlement**: Niederlassungserlaubnis after **3 years** under §21 Abs. 4 if the business is successfully established, livelihood secured, B1 German, civics knowledge.
- **Citizenship clock**: feeds §10 StAG 5-yr track.
- **Processing**: 2–4 months including IHK opinion.

### Opportunity Card — Chancenkarte (§20a AufenthG, launched 1 Jun 2024)

- **Who qualifies**: non-EU professionals seeking work in Germany. Either (i) recognised qualification (auto-eligible), or (ii) **≥6 points** under the new points grid (degree/training, language A1 German *or* B2 English, age, prior Germany ties, qualified work experience, spouse co-application).
- **Capital / income**: proof of funds ~€1,091/mo (€13,092/yr) via Sperrkonto, sponsor declaration, or work part-time (≤20 hr/wk + trial jobs allowed).
- **Validity**: up to 1 year; +2 yr extension if a qualifying job is found but no other title is available yet.
- **Conversion**: **does not auto-feed PR**. Holder must convert to Skilled Worker (§18a/b), EU Blue Card, or §21 self-employment once a qualifying offer/business materialises. Skilled Worker conversion is employer-sponsored and falls under the exclusion filter — so the Chancenkarte is **only useful as an on-ramp to §21 (self-employment)** for this applicant profile. Listed for completeness; the §21 routes above are the direct paths.
- **Citizenship clock**: time on the Chancenkarte counts toward the 5-yr naturalisation residence requirement.
- **Processing**: ~6–12 weeks at the German mission abroad or in-country.

### Financially Independent Residence Permit (§7 Abs. 1 S. 3 AufenthG) — "retiree route"

- **Who qualifies**: applicants whose purpose is settling in Germany on passive income / pension / investment returns without working in Germany. No age minimum in law, but in practice the Ausländerbehörde scrutinises non-retiree applicants harshly; framing as "early retirement on portfolio income" is the accepted shape.
- **Capital / income**: no statutory floor. Practice: demonstrable ~€1,200–1,500/mo plus rent (~€2,000–2,500/mo total in metro cities), evidenced via bank statements, brokerage statements, pension entitlements. Private German health insurance mandatory.
- **Validity**: usually 1 year initial, renewable annually on continued sufficiency.
- **Path to settlement**: Niederlassungserlaubnis after **5 years** (§9). B1 German, civics, no public assistance drawn. Counts toward §10 StAG 5-yr naturalisation.
- **Catch**: discretionary — §7(1) is the catch-all clause and Ausländerbehörden vary widely. Bavaria, Berlin, and Hamburg are friendlier than rural Länder. No central guarantee.

### Long-term study (Studium, §16b AufenthG)

- **Who qualifies**: admission to a German *Hochschule* (university or Fachhochschule) — many English-language Master's programmes have low/no tuition at public universities.
- **Capital / income**: Sperrkonto of €11,904/yr (€992/mo, rates effective Sep 2024).
- **Validity**: 2 yr initial, renewed to programme end. After graduation: 18-month *Aufenthaltserlaubnis zur Arbeitsplatzsuche* (§20 — job-seeker) or pivot to §21.
- **Path to settlement**: graduates of a German university qualify for Niederlassungserlaubnis after **2 years** of qualified employment post-degree (§18c Abs. 1 / §21 Abs. 2a). Study time itself does **not** count fully toward §10 StAG citizenship (only **half** of study time counts toward the 5-yr clock — confirm at file time).
- **Use case**: a late-career applicant could enrol in a 2-yr Master's, then pivot to §21 Freiberufler — total ~7 yr to citizenship.

### Family reunification (Familiennachzug, §§27–36 AufenthG)

- **Who qualifies**: spouse, registered partner, or minor child of a German citizen or a third-country national holding a residence/settlement/EU long-term permit.
- **Requirements**: A1 German for spouse (waived for spouses of Blue Card holders, German citizens in some cases, or where unreasonable); sufficient living space; sponsor's livelihood secured without state aid.
- **Validity**: matches sponsor's permit (typically 1–3 yr), renewable; work permitted from day 1.
- **Path to settlement**: Niederlassungserlaubnis after **3 years** if married to a German citizen and still cohabiting (§28 Abs. 2); 5 yr in other cases.
- **Use case**: only relevant if applicant has or acquires a German spouse — not a standalone route for the profile.

## Filtered out

- **EU Blue Card / Skilled Worker §18a–§18c** — employer-sponsored. Excluded per filter.
- **ICT Card (§19)** — intra-corporate transfer; employer-sponsored. Excluded.
- **Research visa (§18d)** — requires hosting agreement with a German research institution (sponsorship-equivalent). Excluded.
- **No CBI / golden-passport** — Germany has none.
- **No formal "digital nomad" visa** — the Freiberufler route serves this function for liberal professions; no separate scheme exists. Some Länder (notably Berlin) explicitly accept remote-client portfolios.
