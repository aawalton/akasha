
# Italy

Italy was historically the EU's most permissive jure sanguinis jurisdiction (unlimited generations, no language requirement, no residency requirement) — until the **Tajani Decree** (Decreto-Legge 36/2025 of 28 March 2025, converted into **Law 74/2025** published in Gazzetta Ufficiale n.118 on 23 May 2025, in force 24 May 2025) capped automatic descent at the parent/grandparent level and added "genuine link" conditions. Italy still allows dual citizenship without restriction. Naturalization is slow (10 years for non-EU, 4 for EU, 2–3 for marriage) and requires B1 Italian since the 2018 Salvini decree (DL 113/2018). The Constitutional Court rejected the first challenge to Law 74/2025 on 12 March 2026, confirming the generational cap stands. Legal sources: [Law 91/1992 consolidated text (GLOBALCIT)](https://data.globalcit.eu/NationalDB/docs/It%2091%201992%20amended%202018%20revGT%20consolidated.pdf), [Italian MFA — Citizenship](https://www.esteri.it/en/servizi-opportunita/italiani-all-estero/cittadinanza/), [IBA — Italy's 2025 reform](https://www.ibanet.org/from-ancestry-to-proximity-Italy-reform-2025).

## Birth on territory (jus soli) — conditional, at 18

- **Category**: birth; narrow.
- **Eligibility**: born on Italian soil to non-Italian parents AND continuously and legally resident in Italy from birth to age 18. Must submit a declaration of intent between the 18th and 19th birthday; the one-year window is strict and a single gap in legal residence forfeits the claim. Foundlings and children born to stateless/unknown parents acquire citizenship automatically at birth. ([Wikipedia — Italian nationality law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_nationality_law))
- **Timeline**: 18 years residency + months for the declaration to process.
- **Backlog**: declaration is administrative — weeks to months at the comune.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.

## Descent (jure sanguinis) — post-Tajani, parent/grandparent only

- **Category**: declaratory recognition (not a grant) of citizenship held since birth.
- **Eligibility (claims filed at/after 27 March 2025, 23:59 Rome time)**: applicant must have at least one **Italian-born parent or grandparent**. Further conditions: (a) **first generation abroad** (child of an Italian) — the Italian parent must have resided in Italy for ≥2 consecutive years before the applicant's birth and still held Italian citizenship at the birth; (b) **second generation abroad** (grandchild) — the Italian grandparent must have held Italian citizenship **exclusively** (never naturalized into another nationality) until death. The chain still requires unbroken Italian citizenship at each birth and no renunciation before the next link. No language or residency requirement on the applicant themselves. ([Italian MFA — Jure sanguinis](https://www.esteri.it/en/servizi-opportunita/italiani-all-estero/cittadinanza/), [IBA — Italy's 2025 reform](https://www.ibanet.org/from-ancestry-to-proximity-Italy-reform-2025))
- **Effectively closed for**: great-grandchildren and beyond (the historically most-used path for Italian-Americans, Italian-Argentines, Italian-Brazilians). Pre-2025 there was **no generational limit**.
- **Timeline**: declaratory — no waiting period, but document gathering, consular queue, and processing dominate.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: consulate route 4–9+ years for an initial appointment at New York, San Francisco, London, Buenos Aires, São Paulo; in-Italy comune route 90–180 days; **Law 11/2026 (January 2026)** created a centralized MFA office to absorb consular processing — implementation rolling out through 2026. ([My Italian Family — Processing timelines](https://www.myitalianfamily.com/resources/timelines-receive-italian-citizenship))
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.
- **Recent changes**: The single most consequential 2025 EU citizenship reform. See "Grandfathering" and "Minor-child transitional" below.

## Jure sanguinis — pre-Tajani grandfathering

- **Eligibility (pre-2025 rules still apply if ANY of)**: (a) a **complete application** was filed at a consulate or comune by 27 March 2025, 23:59 Rome time; (b) a **judicial petition** ("1948 case" or otherwise) was filed in Italian court by the same cutoff; (c) a **confirmed appointment** at a consulate was held by the cutoff. These cases retain the unlimited-generations rule and no "genuine link" conditions. ([Italian Citizenship Assistance — A-Z Guide](https://italiancitizenshipassistance.com/a-z-guide-to-the-2025-citizenship-changes/))
- **Backlog**: massive — most of the consular backlog is grandfathered files.

## Jure sanguinis — minor-child transitional declaration

- **Eligibility**: minor children (born abroad before 24 May 2025) of a parent who is or becomes Italian by jure sanguinis can be registered under the **pre-Tajani rules** if both parents file a declaration with the relevant consulate or comune. Original Law 74/2025 deadline was **31 May 2026**; a 2025 amendment extended this to **31 May 2029** for children born before 24 May 2025. Children born on/after 24 May 2025 follow the new generational cap from birth and the one-year birth-registration window. ([Italian Citizenship Assistance — Minor children](https://italiancitizenshipassistance.com/clarifying-the-new-citizenship-rules-for-minors/), [ItamCap — Law 74/2025 minors](https://itamcap.com/blog/major-changes-to-the-transmission-of-italian-citizenship-to-minors-what-you-need-to-know-about-law/))

## Jure sanguinis — "1948 case" (maternal line before 1948)

- **Category**: judicial (Tribunale di Roma, since 22 June 2022 jurisdictionally split to the applicant's ancestor's place of origin under DL 13/2022).
- **Eligibility**: applicant's chain of descent crosses an Italian-citizen woman who passed citizenship to a child born **before 1 January 1948** (when the Italian Constitution's gender-equality principles took effect). Pre-1948 Law 555/1912 did not allow maternal transmission; the Court of Cassation (Cass. 4466/2009) retroactively extended equality, but only through judicial petition — the consular/comune route still refuses these cases administratively.
- **Timeline**: 1–3 years to judgment; faster post-2022 jurisdictional reform than pre-2022 Rome-only docket.
- **Backlog**: caseload remains heavy; in late 2025 the Italian Supreme Court's Sezioni Unite postponed a unifying ruling on whether the post-2025 generational cap applies to 1948 cases — a 2026 hearing is expected, and the outcome will determine whether new 1948 filings survive Law 74/2025 unmodified. ([My Lawyer in Italy — 1948 Rule](https://www.mylawyerinitaly.com/legal-services/italian-dual-citizenship/1948-rule/))
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.

## Naturalization by residency — non-EU, 10 years

- **Category**: discretionary grant by Presidential Decree, Law 91/1992 art. 9(1)(f).
- **Eligibility**: ≥10 years of legal continuous residence in Italy; **B1 Italian** (CEFR) since DL 113/2018; clean criminal record (Italian and prior-country); proven income (last 3 tax years ≥€8,263.31/year single, ≥€11,362.05 with dependents); no ongoing expulsion/security proceedings; oath within 6 months of approval. The March 2025 Constitutional Court ruling exempted applicants with serious disability/illness/advanced age from the B1 requirement. ([Italian Citizenship Assistance — B1 exemption](https://italiancitizenshipassistance.com/exemption-from-language-test-for-citizenship-by-marriage-and-residency/))
- **Timeline**: 10 years residency + 24–36 months statutory processing → ~12–13 years total.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: statutory limit is 24 months extendable to 36; real-world 2–4 years post-application. ([Bocca di Tri — Residency](https://www.boccadutri.com/italian-citizenship-by-residency-or-naturalization/))
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed (Italy does not require renunciation).

## Naturalization by residency — EU citizens, 4 years

- **Eligibility**: same as 10-year path but residency reduced to **4 years** for EU/EEA/Swiss citizens. B1 Italian, income, criminal record all still apply.
- **Timeline**: 4 years + 24–36 months → ~6–7 years total.
- **Backlog**: same as 10-year path.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.

## Naturalization by descent — Italian ancestry, 3 years (Article 9(1)(a))

- **Category**: discretionary grant for descendants of Italian citizens who **don't qualify** under the now-restricted jure sanguinis (e.g., great-grandchildren post-Tajani, or someone whose grandparent naturalized abroad before the descendant's birth).
- **Eligibility**: foreigner whose **parent or grandparent** was Italian by birth, with **≥3 years of legal residence in Italy**; B1 Italian; income; clean record. ([Italian MFA — Article 9](https://www.esteri.it/en/servizi-consolari-e-visti/italiani-all-estero/cittadinanza/concessione-della-cittadinanza-ex-art-9-tra-cui-servizio-prestato-alle-dipendenze-dello-stato/))
- **Timeline**: 3 years residency + 24–36 months processing → ~5–6 years total.
- **Backlog**: same processing queue as other Article 9 grants.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.
- **Notable post-Tajani role**: this is the natural fallback for great-grandchildren who lost direct jure sanguinis recognition in March 2025 — but it requires actually moving to Italy for 3 years, which most pre-2025 applicants were not planning.

## Naturalization by residency — born in Italy, 3 years

- **Eligibility**: foreigner **born on Italian soil** with ≥3 years continuous legal residence as an adult (separate from the at-18 jus soli path). B1 Italian, income, criminal record.
- **Timeline**: 3 years + 24–36 months → ~5–6 years total.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.

## Naturalization by residency — stateless persons & recognized refugees, 5 years

- **Eligibility**: stateless persons (per the 1954 Convention) and recognized Convention refugees (per Law 91/1992 art. 16(2)) with ≥5 years legal residence in Italy. B1 Italian, income, criminal record. Refugees are treated as stateless for this purpose. ([asylumineurope.org — Italy naturalisation](https://asylumineurope.org/reports/country/italy/content-international-protection/status-and-residence/naturalisation/))
- **Timeline**: 5 years + 24–36 months → ~7–8 years total.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.

## Naturalization by marriage — 2 or 3 years (Article 5)

- **Category**: declaratory; faster than residency-based but subject to security review.
- **Eligibility**: married to an Italian citizen (or in a civil union since 2016) for **2 years if resident in Italy** OR **3 years if resident abroad**; halved if the couple has minor children (biological or adopted). **B1 Italian since DL 113/2018**. Marriage must be valid and ongoing at application **and at grant**; divorce or separation between filing and decree blocks the grant. Clean criminal record (specific bars for crimes against the State). Fee €250.
- **Timeline**: 2–3 years marriage + 24–36 months statutory processing → ~4–6 years total.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: statutory 36 months from filing; real-world 2–3 years depending on prefecture. ([CTZN Group — Marriage 2026](https://thectzngroup.com/italian-citizenship-by-marriage-2026-requirements-timeline-and-process/))
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.

## Special category — Istria, Fiume, Dalmatia (Law 124/2006)

- **Eligibility**: persons who were Italian nationals residing 1940–1947 in Istria, Fiume (Rijeka), or Dalmatia and lost citizenship under the 1947 Treaty of Paris, plus their **descendants** (no generational limit); also those residing in zone B of the Free Territory of Trieste until 1977 (lost under the 1975 Treaty of Osimo) and descendants. Descendants must demonstrate **knowledge of Italian language and culture**. ([Italian MFA — Law 124/2006](https://www.esteri.it/en/servizi-consolari-e-visti/italiani-all-estero/cittadinanza/riconoscimento-della-cittadinanza-ex-lege-n-124-2006-ai-connazionali-di-istria-fiume-e-dalmazia-e-ai-loro-discendenti/))
- **Timeline**: declaratory; case-by-case, often 1–2 years at consulate.
- **Status under Law 74/2025**: this is a separate special-law track and **not** subject to the new generational cap.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.

## Special category — former Austro-Hungarian territories (Law 379/2000) — CLOSED

- **Eligibility (historical)**: persons born and residing in territories annexed to Italy from Austria-Hungary in 1920 (Trentino-Alto Adige, parts of Friuli-Venezia-Giulia, Veneto) who emigrated **25 December 1867 – 16 July 1920**, plus descendants.
- **Status**: filing window closed **20 December 2010**. No re-opening. Descendants of ancestors who emigrated *after* 16 July 1920 from these regions are handled under standard jure sanguinis (subject to Tajani). ([Italian MFA — Laws 379/2000 and 124/2006](https://www.esteri.it/en/servizi-consolari-e-visti/italiani-all-estero/cittadinanza/riconoscimento-della-cittadinanza-ai-sensi-delle-leggi-379-2000-e-124-2006/))

## Service-based — State employment (Article 9(1)(c))

- **Eligibility**: foreigner whose **parent or grandparent** was Italian by birth AND who serves the Italian State (including abroad) for ≥5 years. Declaration of intent required.
- **Timeline**: at the 5-year service mark + processing.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.

## Extraordinary merit (Article 9(2))

- **Category**: discretionary Presidential grant; no formal application.
- **Eligibility**: extraordinary contribution to Italy in arts, sciences, sport, economy, or international promotion of Italian image. Nominated by a public institution; no residency, language, or income gates.
- **Timeline**: indeterminate; rare.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.

## Reacquisition of lost citizenship (Article 13 / Article 17)

- **Eligibility**: former Italian citizens (typically those who lost citizenship by naturalizing into another country before **15 August 1992**, when Italy began permitting dual nationality) can reacquire via: (a) **Article 13** — declaration plus 1 year of established residence in Italy; (b) Italian State service; (c) military service. Law 74/2025 added a special **Article 17 declaration window from 1 July 2025 to 31 December 2027** allowing simple-declaration reacquisition without the residency step for those who lost citizenship before 16 August 1992. ([Italian Citizenship Assistance — Reacquisition](https://italiancitizenshipassistance.com/new-rules-for-reacquisition-of-italian-citizenship/), [Consolato NY — Reacquisition](https://consnewyork.esteri.it/en/servizi-consolari-e-visti/servizi-per-il-cittadino-straniero/cittadinanza/reacquisition-of-italian-citizenship/))
- **Timeline**: Article 13 — ~1 year residency + months; Article 17 declaration window — months.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed.

## Comparison summary

**By speed (fastest first, post-2025 rules):**
1. **Article 17 reacquisition declaration** (former citizens, window through 31 Dec 2027) — months.
2. **Jure sanguinis (parent/grandparent, in-Italy comune)** — 90–180 days; eligible only if you fit the narrow new tests.
3. **Pre-Tajani grandfathered jure sanguinis (in-Italy comune)** — 90–180 days; eligibility frozen at 27 March 2025.
4. **Law 124/2006 (Istria/Fiume/Dalmatia descendants)** — 1–2 years; small eligible population.
5. **At-18 jus soli declaration** — months at the comune; eligibility set 18 years earlier.
6. **Marriage, resident in Italy** — ~4 years (2 + ~2 processing).
7. **EU-citizen naturalization** — ~6–7 years (4 + 2–3).
8. **Italian-descent naturalization (3-year)** — ~5–6 years; the new fallback for great-grandchildren post-Tajani.
9. **Refugee/stateless naturalization** — ~7–8 years.
10. **Standard non-EU naturalization** (including Golden Visa — see [residency/italy.md](../residency/italy.book-chapter.md)) — ~12–13 years.

**Most likely paths for non-Italian foreigners with no ancestry**: marriage (if applicable) > EU naturalization (if EU passport) > 10-year residency. **For Italian-descended Americans/Argentines/Brazilians**: pre-Tajani grandfathering if already in queue; otherwise jure sanguinis only if Italian parent/grandparent (with the new conditions); otherwise the 3-year residency-with-ancestry path or the 1948 judicial route (pending the Sezioni Unite ruling). **Effectively closed paths**: Law 379/2000 (Austro-Hungarian, closed Dec 2010); jure sanguinis through great-grandparents and earlier (closed 27 March 2025 except for files-in-flight and pending 1948 judicial review).
