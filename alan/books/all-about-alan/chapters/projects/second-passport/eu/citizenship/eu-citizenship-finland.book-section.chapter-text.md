
# Finland

Finland's citizenship regime is governed by the Nationality Act (Kansalaisuuslaki 359/2003, in force since 1 June 2003) and administered by Migri (Finnish Immigration Service). Dual citizenship has been allowed since 2003 with no renunciation requirement. The right-leaning Orpo government (since June 2023) has executed a multi-phase tightening: residency extended from 5 to 8 years (1 Oct 2024), strict welfare-dependency bar and 100% digital filing (17 Dec 2025), permanent-residence period from 4 to 6 years (8 Jan 2026), and a citizenship test bill submitted to Parliament 16 Apr 2026 targeting early 2027. The Nordic-citizen application path remains unusually fast at 2 years.

## Birth in Finland (limited jus soli)

- **Category:** Birth on territory — anti-statelessness only.
- **Eligibility:** Child born in Finland who would otherwise be stateless and has no entitlement to claim any foreign nationality. Also covers children of refugees/protected persons unable to safely register with their home authorities.
- **Timeline:** At birth (automatic).
- **Notes:** No general jus soli. Vast majority of Finnish citizenship at birth flows via descent below.

## Descent (jus sanguinis)

- **Category:** Birth by descent.
- **Eligibility (automatic at birth, born on/after 1 Jun 2003):**
  - Mother is a Finnish citizen at time of birth — automatic regardless of birthplace or marital status.
  - Father is a Finnish citizen AND (a) child is born in Finland and paternity is established, OR (b) parents are married, OR (c) child is born abroad to married Finnish father.
- **Eligibility (declaration, born abroad to unmarried Finnish father, paternity established):** Submit citizenship declaration; no residence requirement.
- **Timeline:** Automatic (birth) or a few months (declaration processing).
- **Cost/burden:** Declaration fee + paternity documentation.
- **Notes:** Pre-1 Jun 2003 cohorts follow older Nationality Act rules (paternal-line restrictions for children of unmarried Finnish fathers born abroad). Sufficient-connection rule: dual citizens lose Finnish citizenship at age 22 unless they have a sufficient connection to Finland (e.g., residence, birth in Finland, application to retain).

## Naturalization by residency (standard application)

- **Category:** Standard naturalization (Migri citizenship application).
- **Eligibility (effective 1 Oct 2024, further tightened 17 Dec 2025):**
  - **Residence:** 8 years continuous residence (raised from 5) — measured backward from application date; absences during the period reduce the count.
  - **Age:** 18+ (or married).
  - **Identity:** Reliably established.
  - **Integrity:** No criminal record, no pending charges, no unpaid taxes/fines/child support (waiting periods apply per offense severity).
  - **Language:** Satisfactory Finnish OR Swedish OR Finnish Sign Language — typically B1 (intermediate) via the National Certificate of Language Proficiency (YKI) or equivalent.
  - **Livelihood:** Reliable, established source of income. Application rejected if applicant received unemployment benefit or social assistance for more than 3 months total in the past 2 years (exemptions only for minors and over-65s).
  - **Filing:** As of 17 Dec 2025, all applications must be filed via the Enter Finland e-portal — paper, postal, and walk-in submissions abolished.
- **Timeline to citizenship:** 8 years residence + 8–23 months processing → ~9–10 years total.
- **Backlog (May 2026):** ~21,900 applications pending; Migri targeting backlog clearance by end-2026. Pre-2024 applications prioritized; post-1 Jan 2024 applications begin processing thereafter. Post-digital-switch end-to-end target is ~6 months once steady state is reached.
- **Dual citizenship:** Allowed without renunciation since 2003.

## Naturalization by residency — 5-year exception track

- **Category:** Reduced-residence naturalization (still an "application", not a declaration).
- **Eligibility:** All standard requirements above, except residence is **5 years** (continuous) if any one applies:
  - Demonstrated language skills at the required level (Finnish/Swedish/FSL) — the standard exception most applicants qualify for.
  - Spouse of a Finnish citizen with 3+ years of cohabitation.
  - Stateless person or refugee status.
- **Timeline to citizenship:** 5 years residence + processing → ~6–7 years total.
- **Notes:** The "5 vs 8" split means the 2024 reform's bite falls hardest on applicants who do not reach B1 — the language-skill track preserves the old 5-year norm for most integrated applicants. Yle reported that for most applicants, 5 years remains the practical pathway.

## Naturalization by declaration — Nordic citizens (fast track)

- **Category:** Declaration (kansalaisuusilmoitus), simplified process.
- **Eligibility:** Citizen of Denmark, Iceland, Norway, or Sweden; 18+; resided in Finland for past 5 years; no imprisonment sentence in past 5 years. Acquired their Nordic citizenship other than by naturalization (otherwise the application route applies).
- **Timeline to citizenship:** ~5 years residence + ~few months processing.
- **Alternative for Nordic citizens — application route:** Nordic citizens may instead apply (not declare) with only **2 years** of Finnish residence, subject to standard application criteria. This is the fastest practical naturalization path in Finland.

## Naturalization by declaration — child of Finnish parent, ages 18–22

- **Category:** Declaration for "second-generation Finns abroad".
- **Eligibility:** Aged 18–22; one parent was/is a Finnish citizen; applicant has been domiciled in Finland for at least 10 years total, including continuous residence for the 2 years immediately preceding the declaration.
- **Timeline:** Few months of processing; no further residence accrual needed at filing time.
- **Notes:** Window closes at 23rd birthday. Designed for adult children who grew up in Finland but did not acquire citizenship at birth (e.g., pre-2003 paternal-line cases).

## Naturalization by declaration — former Finnish citizens (reacquisition)

- **Category:** Declaration.
- **Eligibility:** Lost Finnish citizenship previously; no residence in Finland required. Excludes those who lost citizenship via revocation (false information, treason/high treason/terrorism, or revocation of maternity/paternity).
- **Timeline:** A few months of processing.
- **Notes:** The simplest route for diaspora Finns; bypasses both residence and language requirements.

## Naturalization by marriage

- **Category:** Not a separate path — folded into the 5-year exception track above.
- **Eligibility:** Spouse of Finnish citizen; 3+ years cohabitation; 5 years Finnish residence; otherwise standard application criteria (language, livelihood, integrity).
- **Timeline:** 5 years residence + processing → ~6–7 years.
- **Notes:** No "facilitated" marriage path — Finland never granted marriage-only citizenship.

## Citizenship for a child (co-applicant or declaration)

- **Category:** Derivative.
- **Eligibility:**
  - Under 18, included as co-applicant on a parent's naturalization application.
  - Under 18, child of a Finnish father by declaration (where paternity established after birth).
- **Timeline:** Tied to parent's application or short declaration processing.

## Investment / CBI

- **Status:** None. Finland has no citizenship-by-investment program. No "golden passport" or fast-track investor route exists or is proposed.

## Ingrian Finnish returnees (legacy, closed)

- **Status:** Application window closed 1 July 2016 — no new returnee residence-permit applications accepted. Approx. 30,000 Ingrian Finns moved to Finland under the program (1990–2016). Anyone who completed the residence-permit path before closure can naturalize via the standard routes above; descendants without an existing permit no longer have a dedicated returnee track. Former Finnish citizens in this group can still use the reacquisition-by-declaration path.

## Refugees and stateless persons

- **Category:** Reduced-residence naturalization application.
- **Eligibility:** Recognized refugee status or stateless status → 5-year residence track (same as the language-exception track). All other standard requirements apply (language, integrity, livelihood).
- **Timeline:** 5 years + processing → ~6–7 years.

## Service-based

- **Status:** None. No special citizenship path for military service. Conscription applies to male Finnish citizens age 18+ as a duty of existing citizenship, not a route to acquiring it.

## Re-acquisition of lost citizenship

- See "Naturalization by declaration — former Finnish citizens" above. Declaration suffices; no residence; some exclusions for revocation cases.

## Comparison summary

Ranked by total elapsed time to citizenship (fastest first), with likelihood of success for a typical applicant:

1. **Descent (jus sanguinis) — automatic at birth.** Highest likelihood if Finnish parent. Zero residence.
2. **Reacquisition declaration (former Finnish citizens).** Months. Very high likelihood if eligible — no residence/language gate.
3. **Declaration ages 18–22 with 10 yrs domicile.** Months at filing; assumes the prior 10-year domicile was accrued. High likelihood inside the age window.
4. **Nordic-citizen application (2 yrs residence).** ~2.5–3 years total. Fastest naturalization route. High likelihood for DK/IS/NO/SE citizens.
5. **Nordic-citizen declaration (5 yrs residence).** ~5–5.5 years. Higher residency burden but lower documentation burden than the application.
6. **5-year exception track (language B1 / spouse 3+ yrs / stateless / refugee).** ~6–7 years. The realistic standard route for most integrated immigrants — Yle's reporting confirms 5 years remains the practical norm for those clearing B1.
7. **Standard 8-year application (no language exception).** ~9–10 years. Post-2024 default; in practice avoided by clearing B1.
8. **Birth-in-Finland (anti-statelessness).** Automatic for the narrow eligible group.
9. **Investment / service / Ingrian returnee.** Effectively closed or never existed.
