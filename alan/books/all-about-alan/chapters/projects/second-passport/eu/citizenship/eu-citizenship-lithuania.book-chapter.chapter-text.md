
# Lithuanian Citizenship Paths (May 2026)

Lithuania operates predominantly *jus sanguinis* with a narrow stateless-prevention *jus soli*. Article 12 of the Constitution bars dual citizenship "except in individual cases provided by law" — the largest such case being descendants of pre-1940 Lithuanian citizens (which makes the **Litvak-diaspora restoration route** the dominant practical fast path for outsiders). Two constitutional referendums to broaden dual citizenship have failed on turnout: 2019 and again on 12 May 2024 (74% yes, but short of the absolute-majority-of-electorate threshold). As of May 2026 there is no constitutional path forward; the Seimas is pursuing an ordinary-law workaround restricted to EU/NATO/OECD states.

## Citizenship by Descent (jus sanguinis, current-generation)

- **Eligibility**: At least one parent is a Lithuanian citizen at the time of birth, regardless of birthplace. Children born in Lithuania to one Lithuanian and one foreign parent qualify automatically.
- **Timeline**: Automatic at birth; passport issuance ~1–3 months at a consulate or the Migration Department.
- **Dual citizenship**: **Permitted** — a child who acquires Lithuanian citizenship and another citizenship at birth retains both. This is the constitutional carve-out for "birthright" Lithuanians.

## Restored / Reinstated Citizenship (descendants of pre-1940 citizens — the Litvak path)

- **Eligibility**: Lineal descendant (child, grandchild, or great-grandchild — three generations, no further) of a person who held citizenship of the Republic of Lithuania between **16 Feb 1918 and 15 June 1940** and who left Lithuania **before 11 March 1990** to a country other than a former Soviet republic, or who was deported/exiled. The 23 June 2016 amendments (in force 1 July 2016) removed the political-reason-for-leaving requirement, opening the route broadly to Litvak Jewish diaspora descendants in Israel, the US, South Africa, and elsewhere.
- **Requirements**: Documentary proof of ancestor's pre-1940 Lithuanian citizenship (pre-1940 passport, military service record, birth/identity document, school/work/residence record) and an unbroken lineage chain. **No residency, no language exam, no Constitution exam, no oath, no income test, no criminal-record bar above ordinary standards.**
- **Timeline**: 6–12 months once documents are assembled. Complex archival cases (typical for Litvak applicants where pre-Holocaust records require LCSA / Vilnius archives research) extend to 1–2 years.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: Migration Department running 9–18 months on descent claims; presidential decree stage (final formal step for restoration) often adds 2–6 months and has no statutory deadline.
- **Dual citizenship**: **Permitted** — this is the largest constitutional exception. Applicants retain their existing nationality.
- **Why it matters**: Roughly 220,000 Litvaks lived in Lithuania before WWII; surviving diaspora descendants worldwide (estimated several hundred thousand) are eligible, and this is the **fastest realistic EU passport route for them**.

## Naturalization by Residency (standard)

- **Eligibility** (Law on Citizenship Art. 18):
  - 10 years of continuous legal residence in Lithuania immediately before applying.
  - Hold a **permanent residence permit** at application and at decision.
  - Pass the **state Lithuanian language exam** (oral + written, ~A2/B1 level).
  - Pass the **Constitution of Lithuania exam** (20-question written test).
  - Legal source of subsistence.
  - Clean criminal record (no serious or recent convictions).
  - Renounce existing citizenship (or prove a renunciation request was filed within 3 months of the citizenship grant — tightened from 6 months under the 2026 reform).
  - Take the **oath of allegiance** to the Republic.
- **Exemptions from language + Constitution exams**: applicants aged 65+; persons with 0–55% work-capacity rating; pensioners with special needs; persons with serious chronic mental disorders.
- **Timeline**: 10 years residence + 9–12 months statutory processing + presidential decree (no deadline) = ~11+ years total minimum.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: MIGRIS-stage decisions running 2–4 years in practice (vs. the 9–12 month nominal target), driven by heightened security screening for Russian/Belarusian applicants and the unbounded presidential-decree stage; the LRT investigation in 2025 reported applicants stuck "in limbo at the final stage" indefinitely.
- **Dual citizenship**: **Not allowed.** Renunciation enforced.

## Naturalization by Marriage (facilitated)

- **Eligibility**: Married to a Lithuanian citizen and resided **together in Lithuania for at least 7 years** on a permanent-residence basis. Same language exam, Constitution exam, subsistence, clean-record, oath, and renunciation requirements as standard naturalization.
- **Reduced 5-year variant**: foreign spouse of a former deportee, political prisoner, or child born to such persons in exile; or spouse who lived ≥1 year with a Lithuanian-citizen spouse who then died and has continued to reside in Lithuania for 5 years.
- **Timeline**: 7 years (or 5 in the reduced cases) + processing/decree = ~8–11+ years.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: Same MIGRIS + presidential queue as standard naturalization.
- **Dual citizenship**: **Not allowed** — renunciation required.

## Citizenship by Birth on Territory (jus soli — anti-statelessness only)

- **Eligibility**: A child born or found in Lithuania who would otherwise be stateless acquires Lithuanian citizenship. Covers (a) children of unknown parents found in Lithuania, (b) children born in Lithuania to stateless parents permanently resident there, (c) children where one parent is a stateless permanent resident and the other is unknown — provided the child does not acquire another citizenship.
- **Timeline**: From birth; passport issuance follows civil registration.
- **Dual citizenship**: Not a factor (no competing nationality by definition).

## Citizenship for Ethnic Lithuanians (facilitated naturalization)

- **Eligibility**: A person who claims **ethnic Lithuanian descent** but does not qualify under the pre-1940-descent restoration route may obtain a "right to citizenship" certificate, then naturalize without the standard 10-year residency requirement. Must demonstrate the ethnic origin (typically through ancestor records) and meet language, Constitution, subsistence, clean-record, oath, and renunciation requirements.
- **Timeline**: ~6–18 months once eligibility is confirmed, plus presidential decree.
- **Dual citizenship**: **Not allowed** under this path — renunciation required (distinct from the pre-1940 restoration route which preserves dual).

## Citizenship by Exception (presidential discretionary grant)

- **Eligibility**: The President may grant citizenship "by way of exception" to a foreign national for **outstanding merit** to Lithuania — significant contributions to culture, sports, science, the economy, or national security. No residency, language, Constitution exam, or oath threshold is fixed; in practice a connection (often ~5 years residence and some language) is expected.
- **Dual citizenship**: **Permitted** — this is a constitutional carve-out. The grantee may retain prior citizenship.
- **Frequency**: Very rare. Historically used for foreign basketball players representing Lithuania, certain scientists, and other prominent figures. Single-digit grants per year.
- **Timeline**: Months once initiated; entirely at presidential discretion.

## Re-acquisition of Lost Citizenship (former citizens, not pre-1940 descendants)

- **Eligibility**: Persons who lost Lithuanian citizenship (typically by acquiring another citizenship post-1990 in a non-dual-eligible context) may apply to re-acquire. Standard requirements apply but the residency bar is relaxed; renunciation of the second citizenship is generally required unless the applicant falls under a Constitution-Article-12 exception.
- **Restoration of birthright (separate concept)**: Those who held Lithuanian citizenship at birth and lost it as minors (e.g., due to parental naturalization elsewhere) may have a smoother path back, sometimes with retained dual.
- **Timeline**: ~1–2 years.
- **Dual citizenship**: Depends on whether the second citizenship falls within an Article-12 exception (e.g., acquired by birth, EU/NATO state under the proposed Seimas bill — see Recent Changes).

## Refugee / Stateless Naturalization

- **Eligibility**: Recognized refugees and stateless persons follow the standard naturalization rules, but the residency period is **reduced to 5 years** of continuous lawful residence on a permanent-residence basis. Language, Constitution, subsistence, oath, clean-record requirements still apply. Stateless persons have no renunciation step.
- **Timeline**: ~6 years total (5 + processing + decree).
- **Dual citizenship**: Not at issue for the stateless; for refugees, renunciation is required unless impossible (in which case it may be waived).

## Citizenship by Investment (CBI)

- **Status**: **None.** Lithuania has no citizenship-by-investment program. For investor-residence permits (which feed only the standard 10-year naturalization clock), see [residency/lithuania.md](../residency/lithuania.book-chapter.md).

## Service-Based / Military

- **Status**: No dedicated military-service shortcut. Distinguished military or other contributions can route through the **Citizenship by Exception** path above, but there is no general expedited path for service members.

## Notable Recent Changes (2024–2026)

- **12 May 2024 — Constitutional referendum failed**: 74.13% voted yes (1.026M of ~2.385M registered), but the absolute-majority-of-electorate threshold (~1.193M) was not met. Second failure after 2019 referendum. Article 12 unchanged; broad dual citizenship for naturalized citizens remains constitutionally barred.
- **13 May 2024 onwards — Seimas workaround bill**: MP Dalia Asanavičiūtė filed legislation to allow Lithuanian citizens in **EU and NATO member states** to retain Lithuanian citizenship after acquiring those countries' nationalities, framed as fitting within Article 12's "individual cases" exception. As of May 2026 the bill remains under legislative consideration with no enactment; constitutional-court referral expected if passed.
- **2025–2026 — No major statutory overhaul**. The Migration Department clarified procedural points on dual-citizenship proof and digitized integration assessments (test results transmitted electronically from 2026).
- **2026 — Tightened renunciation proof window**: deadline for proving entitlement to retain dual citizenship reduced from **6 months to 3 months**, with non-compliance triggering revocation procedures.
- **2026 — Extended security screening**: Russian and Belarusian applicants face significantly extended processing on top of the standard MIGRIS queue.
- **Persistent presidential bottleneck**: LRT reporting (2025–2026) emphasizes there is no statutory deadline for the President's citizenship decree, leaving complete files in indefinite limbo at the final stage.

## Comparison Summary

**By total elapsed time (fastest first):**

1. **Birthright (parent is Lithuanian citizen)** — automatic at birth; weeks to issue passport.
2. **Pre-1940 descent restoration (Litvak path)** — 6–18 months typical, up to 2 years for archival cases. **The only fast outsider route with retained dual citizenship.**
3. **Citizenship by exception (presidential merit)** — months; vanishingly few grants per year.
4. **Refugee/stateless naturalization** — ~6 years.
5. **Ethnic Lithuanian facilitated naturalization** — 6–18 months once eligibility confirmed (but requires renunciation).
6. **Marriage naturalization** — ~8–11 years.
7. **Standard naturalization** — ~11+ years.
8. **Investment / CBI** — does not exist; routes through 10-year residency only.

**By likelihood of approval (highest first):** birthright > pre-1940 descent (documents permitting) > ethnic Lithuanian > refugee/stateless > marriage naturalization > standard naturalization > re-acquisition > exception (single-digit grants/yr).

**Effectively closed paths**: jus soli (anti-statelessness only), citizenship-by-investment, broad dual citizenship for naturalized adults (constitutionally barred and twice rejected at referendum). **The only realistic non-residency path for an outsider is pre-1940 descent — and for Litvak diaspora it is highly viable.**
