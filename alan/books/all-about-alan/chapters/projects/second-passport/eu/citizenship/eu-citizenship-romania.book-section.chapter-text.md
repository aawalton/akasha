
# Romanian Citizenship Paths

Romania's citizenship regime is governed by Law 21/1991 on Romanian Citizenship (as amended), with the most recent major overhaul via Law 14/2025 (in force 15 March 2025). The defining feature is **Article 11 reacquisition** for persons who lost Romanian citizenship pre-1989 and their descendants up to the 3rd degree (great-grandchildren) — the dominant diaspora workhorse, with over one million Moldovans alone having recovered Romanian citizenship through it by 2024. ANC (Autoritatea Națională pentru Cetățenie) processing typically runs 2–3+ years despite a statutory 2-year deadline. Romania entered Schengen by air/sea in March 2024 and by land in January 2025; dual citizenship is fully allowed for all paths.

## Jus Soli — Foundlings Only (Law 21/1991 art. 5)

- **Category**: Birth on territory, residual / anti-statelessness only.
- **Eligibility**: A child found on Romanian territory is presumed Romanian citizen until proven otherwise (foundling rule). Pure birth on territory to foreign parents does **not** confer citizenship. **No safeguard for otherwise-stateless children born to non-stateless foreign parents** — a documented gap criticized by the European Network on Statelessness.
- **Timeline**: At birth (presumption).
- **Backlog**: N/A.
- **Dual citizenship**: Allowed.

## Jus Sanguinis — At Birth (Law 21/1991 art. 5)

- **Category**: Descent, automatic.
- **Eligibility**: At least one parent was a Romanian citizen at the time of birth. Applies regardless of birthplace. **No generational limit** through citizen parents. Establishment requires transcribing the foreign birth certificate into the Romanian civil registry via a consulate or directly at SPCLEP in Romania.
- **Timeline**: At birth; consular transcription 6–18 months in practice.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: 6–12 months at most consulates; 12–24 months at high-volume posts (Tel Aviv, Madrid, Rome, London, NYC).
- **Dual citizenship**: Allowed.

## Article 11 — Reacquisition for Former Citizens and Descendants (Law 21/1991 art. 11)

The defining Romanian citizenship route. By volume the largest single naturalization channel in the EU; >1M Moldovans have used it, plus large diasporas in Israel, Ukraine, Western Europe, and the Americas.

- **Category**: Reacquisition / restoration (redobândire) of citizenship lost without applicant's consent (territorial losses 1940 — Bessarabia, Northern Bukovina, Hertsa; communist-era expatriation; postwar expulsions).
- **Eligibility** (all required):
  - **Lineage**: Applicant is (a) a former Romanian citizen who lost citizenship "for reasons not imputable to them," OR (b) a descendant up to the 3rd degree — child, grandchild, **or great-grandchild** — of such a former Romanian citizen. The historical anchor is most commonly an ancestor who was a Romanian citizen in pre-1940 Greater Romania (Bessarabia, Bukovina, Cadrilater, Transylvania, Banat, Old Kingdom) and lost it via the 1940 territorial losses or the 1948 communist citizenship law.
  - **Ancestry documentation**: Birth/baptism/marriage/death records proving the unbroken chain plus an ancestor's pre-1989 Romanian citizenship (or Romanian record in pre-1940 territory). The **2022–2023 tightening** added explicit archive-evidence requirements (state archive certificates from Chișinău, Cernăuți, București, Sibiu, etc.) and witness rules for files lacking primary documents — driven by anti-fraud pressure following Moldovan applicant volume.
  - **Clean criminal record** (Romania and country of residence); no actions against the rule of law / national security / public order.
  - **Loyalty declaration** to the Romanian state.
  - **Language**: B1 Romanian per Law 14/2025 — **but former Romanian citizens themselves are exempt** (statutory exemption for prior holders). Descendants who never held citizenship are subject to the B1 requirement, with a transitional grace period to **14 March 2026** to submit the certificate; that deadline has now passed, so as of May 2026 all new descendant filings must include B1 proof at submission. Applicants 65+ at the time of application are exempt regardless.
  - **No livelihood, no Romanian residency required.**
- **Includes**: Minor children of the applicant can be included on the parent's file.
- **Where filed**: ANC headquarters Bucharest or any of its territorial offices (Iași, Suceava, Galați, Timișoara, Cluj); diaspora applicants typically file via the Romanian Embassy in Chișinău (Moldova) or via designated consulates abroad.
- **Timeline**: 2.5–5+ years end-to-end (document gathering 6–18 months; ANC decision 2–4 years; oath ceremony 2–6 months).
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: Statutory deadline is **2 years from registration** (extended from 5 months by 2018 amendment) with up to a 6-month justified extension; in practice ANC routinely overruns, with effective processing of **3 years typical, 4–5 years not unusual**. The administrative-litigation route (`contencios administrativ`) can compel ANC to resolve a file within 30–90 days once 6 months have elapsed since submission — increasingly used by Moldovan applicants and their legal counsel. Articles 10/11 files have statutory priority over Article 8 (standard naturalization).
- **Dual citizenship**: Allowed; no renunciation required.
- **Sources**: Law 21/1991 art. 11; Law 14/2025; ANC administrative practice; legal500.com 2025 analysis.

## Article 10 — Reacquisition for Former Citizens (No Generational Extension)

- **Category**: Reacquisition — former citizens who lost it for reasons other than the historical territorial/communist loss covered by art. 11, OR who lost it post-1989, plus their descendants up to the 2nd degree.
- **Eligibility**:
  - Was a Romanian citizen and lost it (any reason except revocation for disloyal acts), OR is a child or grandchild (no great-grandchildren) of such a person.
  - In practice the law treats art. 10 and art. 11 reacquisition similarly. Ethnic origin is **not** required under either; the ancestor's ethnicity (Romanian, Hungarian, Jewish, German, Ukrainian, Roma, etc.) is irrelevant — only the prior Romanian citizenship status matters.
  - Clean record, loyalty declaration, B1 Romanian (with the same exemptions as art. 11 — former citizens exempt; descendants subject; 65+ exempt).
  - No residency or livelihood requirement.
- **Timeline**: 2.5–5 years (same ANC pipeline as art. 11).
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: Same ANC queue as art. 11 — 2–4 years typical.
- **Dual citizenship**: Allowed.

## Article 8 — Standard Naturalization by Residency (Law 21/1991 art. 8(1))

- **Category**: Naturalization — general route for unrelated foreign nationals.
- **Eligibility** (all required):
  - **8 years continuous lawful residence in Romania** on a permanent residence permit (or 5 years for EU/EEA/Swiss citizens and recognized refugees — see below).
  - Conduct and attitude proving loyalty to the Romanian state.
  - Age 18+.
  - Legal means of livelihood in Romania at least at the level of the gross minimum wage.
  - Clean criminal record (Romania and abroad) — no convictions for offences making the applicant "unworthy of Romanian citizenship."
  - **B1 Romanian language proficiency** (Common European Framework) — mandatory for all naturalization applicants under Law 14/2025 with no exemption except age 65+ and former citizens (irrelevant here).
  - **Knowledge of the Romanian Constitution and the national anthem** ("Deșteaptă-te, române!"), assessed at interview — applicants must be able to recite or sing at least one verse and answer basic constitutional questions.
  - Knowledge of Romanian culture and civilization.
- **Renunciation**: Not required.
- **Oath**: Taken before the ANC president or vice-president after the citizenship order is issued. Citizenship is conferred on the date of the oath, not the date of the order.
- **Timeline**: 8 years residence + 2–3 years ANC processing = ~10–11 years total.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: ANC art. 8 decisions typically 2–3 years after filing; art. 10/11 files take priority, so art. 8 files often run longer than the statutory 2-year cap.
- **Dual citizenship**: Allowed.

## Article 8(2) — Reduced Naturalization (4 / 5 Years)

- **Category**: Naturalization with reduced residency for closer ties.
- **Eligibility**: Standard art. 8 requirements (livelihood, language, Constitution, anthem, clean record, loyalty) **plus** one of:
  - **5 years** if married to a Romanian citizen — residency runs **from the marriage date**, cohabiting in Romania throughout. Authorities scrutinize cohabitation evidence and interview both spouses.
  - **4 years** if the applicant is an EU/EEA/Swiss citizen.
  - **4 years** if the applicant holds **refugee** status in Romania (running from refugee recognition).
  - **4 years** if the applicant is an internationally renowned personality.
  - Residency can additionally be **reduced by up to half** (down to 4 years from the 8-year base, or 2.5 from 5) for applicants demonstrating significant economic, educational, cultural, social, or voluntary contribution to Romania.
- **Timeline**: Reduced residency + 2–3 years ANC processing.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: ANC ~2–3 years post-filing.
- **Dual citizenship**: Allowed.

## Article 8¹ / 8² — Distinguished-Contribution Citizenship (Law 14/2025 additions)

- **Category**: Discretionary naturalization for exceptional contribution; **no residency requirement**.
- **Eligibility**: Foreign nationals or stateless persons who have significantly contributed to the protection and promotion of Romanian culture, civilization, and spirituality (art. 8¹) or who have promoted Romania's image internationally through exceptional achievements, particularly in sport (art. 8²). Confirmation by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (cultural contribution: teaching, research, Romanian-language publishing, diaspora support) or the relevant ministry. Standard clean-record and loyalty requirements still apply; language and Constitution requirements may be waived in the discretionary grant.
- **Timeline**: 6–18 months; opaque, ministerial discretion.
- **Backlog**: Case-by-case; not a route an ordinary applicant can pursue.
- **Dual citizenship**: Allowed.

## Refugee / Stateless Paths

- **Recognized refugees** in Romania qualify for the 4-year reduced naturalization under art. 8(2) (above), running from the date of refugee status recognition. B1 Romanian, Constitution/anthem knowledge, livelihood, and clean record still required.
- **Stateless persons** are **not** automatically given a reduced timeline — the 8-year general rule applies — though Romania can grant citizenship without residency under art. 8¹ for distinguished contribution. This is the documented statelessness-protection gap relative to refugees.

## Investment / CBI — Does Not Exist

- **Romania has no CBI program** and has never had one. A residency-by-investment ("Romania Golden Visa") proposal is in the legislative pipeline as of May 2026 — produces residency only, citizenship still requires the standard 8-year art. 8 track. See [../residency/romania.md](../residency/romania.book-chapter.md) for program details.

## Comparison Summary

Ranked by realistic total time to passport, May 2026:

1. **Jus sanguinis at birth** — instant; consular transcription 6–24 months. Trivial if a parent is Romanian.
2. **Article 11 reacquisition** (former citizens / descendants up to great-grandchildren of pre-1940 Romanian citizens) — **2.5–5 years end-to-end**; by far the highest-volume diaspora route. No Romanian residency, no language test for former citizens themselves (or for descendants 65+); descendants now need B1 at filing post-March 2026 grace period. The dominant Romanian path globally — used most by Moldovans, also by Ukrainians (Bukovina), Israelis, and Romanian-American diasporas.
3. **Article 10 reacquisition** (former citizens + children/grandchildren, no great-grandchildren) — 2.5–5 years; same ANC queue as art. 11, narrower lineage.
4. **Article 8¹/8² distinguished contribution** — 6–18 months; not a route an ordinary applicant can rely on.
5. **Article 8(2) 4-year naturalization** (EU citizens, recognized refugees, internationally renowned) — ~6–7 years total.
6. **Article 8(2) 5-year naturalization** (spouse of Romanian citizen) — ~7–8 years total, requires cohabitation in Romania throughout.
7. **Article 8 standard 8-year naturalization** — ~10–11 years total; heaviest burden (B1 Romanian, Constitution, anthem, livelihood, full interview).

Effectively closed / nonexistent: **CBI** (does not exist); **broad jus soli** (foundlings only). For the proposed residency-by-investment program, see [../residency/romania.md](../residency/romania.book-chapter.md). The statelessness-safeguard gap for children born in Romania to non-stateless foreign parents remains an open compliance issue.

Flag-worthy May-2026 changes:
- **Law 14/2025 (in force 15 March 2025)**: B1 Romanian language mandatory for all naturalization and (descendant) reacquisition applicants; transitional grace period closed **14 March 2026**, so all new files now need B1 proof at submission unless the applicant is a former citizen or aged 65+. New art. 8¹/8² distinguished-contribution channels added.
- **Article 11 documentation tightening (2022–2023 reforms, ongoing 2025 implementation)**: stricter archive-evidence and witness rules driven by Moldovan volume — expect to need state-archive certificates rather than just family documents.
- **ANC backlog**: statutory 2-year processing target is routinely overrun; expect 3 years for art. 10/11 and 2–3 years for art. 8. Administrative-litigation acceleration (`contencios administrativ`) is the standard counter-tool once 6 months have elapsed.
- **Schengen**: full Schengen entry by land January 2025 (air/sea March 2024) — Romanian passport is now a full Schengen-area document.
