
# Netherlands

The Netherlands runs a **restrictive renunciation regime**: standard naturalization (Article 8 RWN) requires giving up the prior nationality unless a statutory exception applies. The **option procedure** (Article 6 RWN) is the fast lane — 3 years for spouses, 1 year for former Dutch, automatic for several diaspora categories — and **always permits dual nationality**. The **Schoof / Wilders PVV-led cabinet (2024–2025)** pushed to extend residency from 5 to 10 years and raise language to B1; the **successor D66–VVD–CDA cabinet (sworn in Jan 2026)** dropped the 10-year plan and kept 5 years, but the B1 language hike and the renunciation requirement remain on the books.

## Jus Soli — Birth on Territory (Article 3(3) RWN, "third-generation")

- **Eligibility**: child born in NL/Aruba/Curaçao/Sint Maarten to a parent who *also* had main residence in the Kingdom at the time of *that parent's* birth (third-generation born-and-raised). Foundlings of unknown parentage also acquire Dutch nationality.
- **Dual citizenship**: yes — automatic at birth, no renunciation.
- **Timeline**: automatic.
- **Backlog**: none.

## Jus Sanguinis — Descent (Article 3(1) RWN)

- **Eligibility**: at least one parent was Dutch at the time of birth. Father–child link requires marriage to the Dutch father, pre-birth acknowledgement, or post-birth recognition with DNA. Generation cut-off via **Article 15(1)(c)**: a Dutch national born and resident outside NL/EU loses nationality after **13 years** abroad as a dual national if they don't renew a Dutch passport, ID card, or certificate of Dutch nationality (extended from 10 → 13 years on 1 April 2022 post-*Tjebbes*); descendants born after the parent's loss are not Dutch.
- **Pre-1985 mother gap**: covered via the **option procedure** (Article 6), not automatic — see below.
- **Dual citizenship**: yes, automatic.
- **Timeline**: automatic if eligible; consular declaration confirms.
- **Backlog**: passport/CoN issuance via Dutch consulates 4–12 weeks.

## Option Procedure — Article 6 RWN (Declaration-based, fast track)

The headline path for diaspora and integrated residents. **Declaration at municipality, decision in ~13 weeks, fee €241, no renunciation requirement, dual citizenship permitted.** Eligible categories include:

- **Born in NL + lifelong residence**: born in the Kingdom and main residence here since birth, age ≥ 18.
- **Spouse/registered partner of a Dutch citizen**: 3 years of marriage/partnership *and* 3 years of uninterrupted lawful residence in NL (the iconic "marriage option"). Living together abroad does **not** count under current law — the spouse must be physically resident in the Kingdom.
- **Former Dutch national**: lost Dutch nationality, now back in NL with a valid (preferably permanent) residence permit for **1 year**.
- **Long-resident from minority**: lived in the Kingdom continuously since age 4, age ≥ 18.
- **Born to Dutch mother pre-1985**: born before 1 Jan 1985 to a Dutch mother and non-Dutch father (and descendants of such people) — fixes the pre-1985 patrilineal-only rule.
- **Adopted by a Dutch parent abroad**: under specific conditions.
- **Stateless child born in the Kingdom**: 3 years lawful residence (no age limit if residence permit; otherwise ≤ 21 with 5 years' stable residence — Oct 2023 expansion).
- **Children**: under 18 can be co-opted on a parent's option declaration.

Standard option requires civic integration (A2 inburgering) for most adult categories *except* spouses-of-Dutch and former-Dutch — both have **no exam**. Timeline: declaration to swearing-in ~3–6 months.

## Naturalization by Residency — Article 8 RWN (Standard)

- **Eligibility**:
  - **5 years** uninterrupted lawful residence in the Kingdom (NL or Caribbean) with a valid permit — *not yet 10 years; the Wilders coalition's 10-year proposal was formally dropped by the D66–VVD–CDA government in January 2026*.
  - Permanent or non-temporary residence permit.
  - **Inburgeringsexamen** at **A2** (still A2 in May 2026; **B1 hike is enacted as policy direction but not yet in force for naturalization filings**) — Dutch language (reading, writing, listening, speaking) + Knowledge of Dutch Society (KNM) + Orientation on the Dutch Labour Market (ONA). In the Caribbean: A2 Dutch + Papiamentu (Aruba/Curaçao/Bonaire) or Dutch + English (Sint Maarten/Saba/Statia).
  - Sufficient income, not reliant on social assistance.
  - No serious criminal record in last 4 years (custodial sentences > 6 months, community service > 240 hours, or fines ≥ €810 are disqualifying).
  - **Renunciation of prior nationality** at the swearing-in ceremony, unless an exception applies (see Dual Citizenship section).
  - Declaration of solidarity at ceremony; loyalty oath.
- **Dual citizenship**: no by default — must renounce.
- **Timeline**: 5 years residence + **up to 12 months** statutory decision period (often 9–12 months in practice) + ~3 months to ceremony ≈ **6–6.5 years total**.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: IND statutory decision period 12 months; practical 9–12 months; complex files (foreign documents, criminal-record verification) push to 18 months. Fee: **€1,139** single applicant (€1,455 joint, €168 minor co-applicant).

## Reduced-Residency Exceptions to Article 8 (Article 8(2)–(4) RWN)

- **3 years if married to / partnered with a Dutch citizen and living in NL**: same 3-year residence as the option route but via naturalization (used when a category doesn't fit option, e.g., civic-exam exemption issues). Renunciation still required unless EU/EEA spouse exception (no — Netherlands does *not* exempt EU spouses).
- **3 years if stateless** at time of application: half-rate residence.
- **2 years residence reduction** if previously lived in NL for 10+ years as a minor.
- **No residence requirement** for: born in the Kingdom and resident here at filing (rare carve-out); adopted by Dutch parent as minor.

## Investment / Citizenship-by-Investment

- **None as a citizenship route**. The Netherlands has no CBI.
- The **residence-by-investment** scheme was **abolished on 17 April 2024** by the IND. See [residency/netherlands.md](../residency/netherlands.book-chapter.md) for the historical terms and existing-holder treatment.

## Refugees and Stateless Persons

- **Recognized refugees / asylum status holders**: standard 5-year Article 8 path **with renunciation waived** — refugees may keep their original citizenship.
- **Stateless adults**: 3-year reduced residence under Article 8(4), plus stateless-child option above. The new D66–VVD–CDA coalition agreement floats a **6-year** path for asylum holders (two temporary permits) but this is not yet enacted.
- **Timeline**: 5 years (4 if reduced) + 9–12 months processing.

## Service-Based — Distinguished Services / Royal Decree

- **Article 10 RWN**: the Crown may, on Cabinet recommendation, grant Dutch nationality to a foreigner for **special services to the Dutch state**, without meeting residency, language, or renunciation requirements. Extremely rare — used for high-profile athletes (Olympic team adds), exceptional cultural/scientific figures, occasionally diplomats' staff. No published criteria; lobbying-based; no public timeline.
- **No military-service-to-passport** path — KMar/Defence service requires Dutch nationality at entry.

## Reacquisition of Lost Dutch Nationality

Three mechanisms:
1. **Option (Article 6)** — former Dutch national + 1 year residence in NL with a valid permit. **Keeps other nationalities.** Fee €241, 13-week decision. (See option section.)
2. **Article 6 confirmation for *Tjebbes* / Article 15 victims** — those who lost via the 10/13-year clock can opt back if they apply within reasonable time and pass the **EU proportionality test** (Tjebbes 2019 ECJ ruling + 1 April 2022 RWN amendment).
3. **Article 8 naturalization from abroad** — discretionary, rarely used, normally requires return.

## Dutch Caribbean — Aruba / Curaçao / Sint Maarten / BES

- Residency in any of these Kingdom territories **counts identically** for both option and naturalization tracks — the requirement is "lived in the Kingdom of the Netherlands."
- Naturalization applications in the Caribbean start at the **Cabinet of the Governor**, language tests are bilingual (Dutch + Papiamentu or Dutch + English), and processing is generally **slower** than the European Netherlands (often 18–24 months).
- No CBI in any Kingdom territory; Caribbean investor permits confer residency only and run under the standard Kingdom-residency clock (see [residency/netherlands.md](../residency/netherlands.book-chapter.md)).

## Sephardic Jewish Descent

- **No dedicated Dutch path.** Sephardic communities settled in Amsterdam from 1492; their modern descendants apply through ordinary jus sanguinis if a Dutch parent exists, otherwise via standard Article 8.
- Descendants of *Dutch Sephardim* are typically eligible for **Portuguese** Sephardic citizenship (Law 30-A/2015, now requiring 3-year Portuguese residence post-2022 reform) — but voluntarily acquiring Portuguese citizenship would **trigger Article 15(1)(a) automatic loss of Dutch nationality** unless a renunciation exception applies.

## Comparison Summary

**Ranked by speed and likelihood (May 2026):**

1. **Jus sanguinis (descent)** — automatic at birth; dual citizenship allowed.
2. **Option — former Dutch national** — 1 year residence + ~3 months ≈ **15 months**; keeps other nationality.
3. **Option — born to pre-1985 Dutch mother** — declaration only (no residence) ≈ **3–6 months**; keeps other nationality.
4. **Option — spouse of Dutch citizen** — 3 years marriage + 3 years residence in NL ≈ **3+ years**; keeps other nationality, no civic exam.
5. **Article 8 — marriage / partner short track** — 3 years residence + ~12 months processing ≈ **4 years**; renunciation required.
6. **Article 8 — refugee** — 5 years residence + 12 months ≈ **6 years**; renunciation waived.
7. **Article 8 — standard residency** — 5 years residence + 12 months ≈ **6–6.5 years**; renunciation required.
8. **Article 10 — distinguished services Royal Decree** — variable, opaque, near-zero approval odds without elite profile.

**Effectively closed**: residence-by-investment (abolished 17 Apr 2024); no CBI ever; no service-to-passport; no Sephardic-specific path.

**Dual-citizenship stance**: restrictive. Renunciation is the default at naturalization. **Statutory exceptions**:
- Spouse/partner of a Dutch citizen at date of Royal Decree (most-used carve-out).
- Recognized refugees / asylum holders.
- Persons whose country of origin **refuses** to release them (Morocco, Iran, Argentina, Tunisia, Greece for males of conscription age, etc.).
- Persons for whom renunciation imposes **unreasonable hardship** (substantial financial loss: pension, inheritance, property; mandatory military service buy-out).
- Persons **born in the Kingdom** and resident at naturalization.
- Persons acquiring via **option (Article 6)** — option never requires renunciation.
- Article 10 Royal Decree grantees (de facto).

**Flag-worthy 2024–2026 changes (Wilders era and successor)**:
- **17 Apr 2024**: residence-by-investment scheme abolished.
- **Oct 2023**: stateless-children-without-permit option route added.
- **2025 draft amendment**: Schoof/Wilders cabinet proposed 5 → 10 years standard residency, A2 → B1 language, scrapping 3-year marriage-abroad route.
- **Jan 2026**: Schoof cabinet falls; new **D66–VVD–CDA cabinet drops the 10-year plan** but keeps the renunciation requirement and the B1 language direction (timing TBD).
- **B1 language**: enacted policy direction; not yet in force for filings as of May 2026. Watch the spring 2026 RWN amendment cycle.
- **1 Apr 2022**: Article 15 loss clock extended 10 → 13 years; Tjebbes proportionality test required.
