
# Cost Curve — Past 15 Years

The trajectory of US residential rooftop solar pricing, and the component subcurves underneath it.

## Total Residential Retail — US Median $/W

| Year | $/W (residential installed, US median) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2010 | ~$7.14 | [SolarReviews historical](https://www.solarreviews.com/blog/how-has-the-price-and-efficiency-of-solar-panels-changed-over-time) |
| 2014 | $3.75 (EnergySage marketplace launch median) | [pv-magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2025/04/29/u-s-residential-solar-falls-to-lowest-ever-2-50-per-watt-said-energysage/) |
| 2020 | ~$2.85 | [Statista residential](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1401509/cost-of-residential-solar-systems-united-states/) |
| 2022 (Q1) | $3.18 (NREL benchmark) | [Utility Dive / NREL](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/solar-costs-residential-utility-nrel-module-inverter/697937/) |
| 2023 (Q1) | $2.68 (NREL benchmark, -16% YoY) | [Utility Dive / NREL](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/solar-costs-residential-utility-nrel-module-inverter/697937/) |
| 2024 (H2) | $2.50 (EnergySage marketplace median) | [pv-magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2025/04/29/u-s-residential-solar-falls-to-lowest-ever-2-50-per-watt-said-energysage/) |
| 2024 (Q1) | $3.15 MMP / $2.74 MSP (NREL modeled) | [NREL Q1 2024](https://data.nrel.gov/submissions/307) |
| 2026 (Utah) | $2.65 (EnergySage Utah median) | [EnergySage Utah](https://www.energysage.com/local-data/solar-panel-cost/ut/) |

**Cumulative reduction:** ~65% from 2010 to 2024. The early-decade fall (2010 → 2015) was almost entirely module wholesale collapse; the late-decade fall (2015 → 2024) was modules continuing to deflate plus inverter and BOS price drops, partially offset by rising soft costs.

**Inflection points:**

- **2012–2016:** Section 201 tariff (Suniva/SolarWorld petition), but module wholesale was deflating fast enough that retail still fell.
- **2019–2021:** ITC step-down anxiety (was scheduled to ramp from 30% → 26% → 22% before the IRA extended it back to 30%).
- **2020–2022:** COVID supply-chain shock — module spot prices spiked, install backlogs grew, installed price flattened.
- **2022:** Inflation Reduction Act extended the 30% ITC through 2032 and added domestic-content adders. Installed price resumed declining.
- **2024:** AD/CVD investigation against Southeast Asian module imports + UFLPA enforcement → US module prices rose 15%+ Apr–Aug 2024.
- **2025:** "One Big Beautiful Bill" (July 2025) phased out the residential ITC for cash/loan systems after Dec 31, 2025. This is a discontinuity, not a smooth curve point — affordability for new 2026 buyers without TPO structuring is materially worse.

## Modules — Wholesale $/W

| Year | Wholesale $/W (utility-scale, FOB China) | US Market $/W |
|---|---|---|
| 2010 | ~$1.80 | ~$2.50+ |
| 2016 | ~$0.40 | ~$0.55 |
| 2020 | ~$0.20 | ~$0.30 |
| 2023 | ~$0.13 | ~$0.30 |
| 2024 Q3 | $0.096 (BNEF record low) | $0.25–$0.28 |
| 2025 | $0.08–$0.11 China FOB | $0.25–$0.28 US |

Source: [Solartech 2025 module prices](https://solartechonline.com/blog/solar-module-prices-guide-2025/), [BNEF 3Q 2024 PV outlook](https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-energy/3q-2024-global-pv-market-outlook/).

**The story:** wholesale modules dropped ~95% from 2010 to 2024. **US installed prices fell only ~65% over the same period** because hardware is a shrinking share of the total. Today, modules are ~10% of the installed price — a 50% wholesale price drop only moves installed price ~5%. The tariff stack puts US installers at ~3× the wholesale price the rest of the world pays.

## Inverters

- **String inverters:** wholesale ~$0.05–0.10/W today vs ~$0.40/W in 2010. The decline mirrors modules.
- **Microinverters:** Enphase IQ8 system-level cost $1.10–$2.00/W including all the per-panel hardware; this premium has stayed roughly flat in $/W terms since the IQ7 → IQ8 generation, but the value proposition improved (longer warranty, higher AC output) ([Electrical Trader](https://electricaltrader.com/blogs/news/string-inverters-vs-microinverters-cost-comparison)).
- **SolarEdge:** post-2022 financial troubles and Enphase competition compressed pricing; current SolarEdge Home Hub systems quote at ~$2.90/W installed.

## Batteries — Lithium-Ion Cells vs Residential Pack

Source: [BloombergNEF battery price survey 2024 / 2025](https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-transport/new-record-lows-for-battery-prices/).

| Year | Cell $/kWh (BNEF survey, volume-weighted) | Residential Pack Installed $/kWh |
|---|---|---|
| 2010 | $1,200+ | n/a (no consumer product) |
| 2015 | $373 | n/a |
| 2020 | $137 | ~$1,500 |
| 2023 | $144 | ~$1,200 |
| 2024 | $115 (-20% YoY, biggest drop since 2017) | $1,000–$1,500 |
| 2025 | $108 (-8% YoY) | $681–$1,600 (Powerwall low, Enphase high) |

**The story:** cell-level prices fell ~90% from 2010 to 2025. **Installed residential battery prices fell roughly 30%** over the same period. The gap — install labor, BMS, casing, dealer margin, customer-acquisition cost — has barely budged. Same soft-cost story as PV.

## Soft Costs — The Reason US Prices Are High

US residential soft costs are the dominant cost and have **not** fallen at the rate of hardware:

| Region | Soft cost $/W (residential, recent) | Install labor $/W (median) |
|---|---|---|
| US | $1.22 | $0.49 |
| Germany | $0.33 | $0.18 |
| Australia | comparable to Germany | similar |
| Italy | comparable to Germany | similar |

Source: [RMI USA vs Germany comparison](https://rmi.org/blog_2013_12_05_can_usa_solar_cost_compete_with_germany/), [NREL Documenting 15 Years of Reductions](https://docs.nrel.gov/docs/fy25osti/92536.pdf).

**Why the gap:**

- **Customer-acquisition cost.** $0.48–$0.69/W in the US ([NREL](https://docs.nrel.gov/docs/fy25osti/92536.pdf)). Germany / Australia have near-zero CAC because policy stability (FiT history, predictable incentives) drove organic demand; US demand is sold door-to-door.
- **Permitting overhead.** US permitting / inspection / interconnection averages weeks-to-months per project; Germany typically processes in days against a standardized national protocol. The US has 18,000+ local AHJs each with unique requirements.
- **Lack of standardization.** Each US installer designs racking, layout, electrical from scratch. Germany / Australia have de-facto standardized prefab racking kits.
- **Labor cost structure.** US licensed electrician rates are higher; install crews are smaller and less specialized.

**The implication for Future-Alan:** further hardware deflation will barely move the installed price. The remaining $1.00+/W of fat is in the channel — sales, permitting, overhead. Choosing the right installer (online quote, lean back office) captures more of that fat than choosing the right panel.

## Anchoring Bet

A reasonable expectation for the next 3–5 years, assuming no new policy shocks: US installed residential prices grind down toward **$2.20–$2.40/W** at the marketplace median (best channel), while NREL-modeled MMP for the broad market stays in the $2.80–$3.00/W range. Battery installed pricing follows cell-level deflation more slowly; expect Powerwall-class storage to reach **$600–$700/kWh installed** by 2028. None of this changes if the residential ITC stays expired — the cash-cost curve is independent of the credit; the after-credit cost just jumped 30% when the credit went away.
