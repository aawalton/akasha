
# Destimulation

> Destimulation — Alan's first-line response to acute sensory overload, a deliberately near-zero-sensory-input room (dark, quiet, cold) that drives the baseline sensory draw toward zero so regulation can come back online and the attention budget can recover.

When the [attention budget](attention-budget.book-chapter.md) collapses far enough that the fixed [baseline sensory draw](attention-budget.book-chapter.md#the-economics-one-fixed-cost-against-a-swinging-budget) is eating 50–100+% of it — acute overload, nothing discretionary left — the first thing Alan reaches for is **destimulation.**

Concretely: he has a closet in his basement office that is full blackout dark, quiet, and cold. It is cold because it has an exterior wall. He lies there, sometimes under a [weighted blanket](weighted-blanket.book-chapter.md), until he can handle stimulation again.

## Mechanism: drive the baseline draw toward zero

This is the physical floor of sensory-draw reduction. The [sensory-regulation tools](sensory-regulation-tools.book-chapter.md) shed one modality of input at a time — dampen sound, cut light, saturate touch. The closet does all of them at once, to the floor. Dark plus quiet plus cold is a deliberately near-zero-sensory-input room.

The point is the [attention budget](attention-budget.book-chapter.md). When the room strips ambient input toward zero, the fixed baseline sensory draw drops toward zero with it. That hands the discretionary remainder back, and it lets the autonomic [traffic lights](high-throughput-road.book-chapter.md) come back online — the regulation that clears the road and sets the budget in the first place. The closet doesn't add capacity; it removes the load that was consuming all of it, so the regulation can recover on its own.

A side effect: the room doubles as an **interoception amplifier**. Alan's [interoceptive channels work but run quiet](sensory-experience.book-chapter.md), losing the attention competition to louder external input; with the external field stripped to near zero, the body signals — hunger, thirst, the emotion-sensations — become audible again.

The [weighted blanket](weighted-blanket.book-chapter.md) layers on top. While the other inputs are stripped away, the blanket adds soothing deep-pressure proprioceptive input — one chosen input added back into an otherwise emptied room. Consistent with its [strategic, not continuous](weighted-blanket.book-chapter.md#protocol-summary) use elsewhere, it is *sometimes* under the blanket, not always.

## First-line, and the counterpart to the adding knobs

Destimulation is the first-line move under overload, ahead of the [stimulation-regulation](stimulation-regulation.book-chapter.md) knobs. Those knobs are two-directional — they add arousal (ESO, music, light up) as readily as they shed it. The closet is the far end of the shedding direction: the counterpart to the stimulation-*adding* knobs used when Alan is understimulated. When the budget has overflowed, the move is not to retune a knob but to take the whole load to the floor and let the system reset.

"Until I can handle stimulation again" is the exit condition, and it is a [recovery](recovery-rates.book-chapter.md) read, not a clock — Alan stays until the baseline draw has fallen back under the budget and discretionary attention returns. Stepping back into ambient input before that point would just re-jam the road.
