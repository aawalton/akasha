
# Measurement maturity

> Measurement maturity — the standard 0-5 progression of mental models for any continuous variable. Stoplight is distribution-anchored; numbered levels and numeric scales are absolute-anchored. Progression is bottleneck-driven.

Any continuous variable I track in my own experience progresses through a standard sequence of mental models as my visibility into it improves. This ladder is one instrument inside the broader discipline of [self-instrumentation](self-instrumentation.book-chapter.md) — measuring my own psychology and physiology as a semi-opaque system, where better measurement is often the best intervention. The underlying variable is continuous, with thresholds at points where the change becomes consciously discernable. The thresholds may live in physiology, in perception, or in both — color works this way, with no actual transitions in wavelength but real perceived transitions where the brain registers a color change. The framework doesn't commit to where the thresholds originate; it just tracks how cleanly I can read them.

## The levels

0. **Don't know it exists.** The variable is operating, but I have no awareness it's a thing.
1. **Know it exists.** I can name it, but can't yet read its state.
2. **Enough vs. not enough.** Binary — I can tell whether the resource is sufficient for what I'm doing, but no finer.
3. **Stoplight.** Colour bands rather than numbers. My main pattern runs four of them, green at the median — see [the band scheme](#the-band-scheme--four-colours-at-sigma-spacing) below. Anchored partly to my distribution. This ladder describes what a stoplight can *resolve*; what the mechanic is *for* — pointing me at what to do next, where aphantasia leaves no signal to do it — is [stoplight-mechanic.md](stoplight-mechanic.book-chapter.md).
4. **Numbered levels.** More than three qualitative levels with absolute anchors — operational capability descriptions at each integer that don't shift with my distribution.
5. **Numeric scale.** Decimal value with full anchors and inputs/outputs. The most precise level; treats the variable as a measurable quantity with absolute meaning.

## Distribution-anchored vs. absolute

Stoplight is fundamentally a different kind of measurement than numbered or numeric levels. Where a numbered level's anchors are fixed capability descriptions, a stoplight's are set in part by where my readings have been landing lately, so they move as the distribution moves. Several stoplight redefinitions can happen before a dimension graduates to absolute anchors.

### The band scheme — four colours at sigma spacing

This note previously recorded the bands as three, with yellow at the median, green about +1σ and red about −1σ. That is not the pattern I run:

> I actually think the distribution-anchored is median at green, not yellow these days as my main pattern. Yellow is caution, red and black are alarms.

So there are four bands rather than three, and two of them are alarms. Below green the spacing is by sigma:

> Essentially, I'm saying a one sigma down is caution, a two sigma down is alarm, and a three or four sigma down is crisis.

| Band | Position | Reads as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Green | median and above | where I have been living |
| Yellow | −1σ | caution |
| Red | −2σ | alarm |
| Black | −3σ to −4σ | crisis |

Asked whether pinning the two alarm bands to absolute anchors — leaving green and yellow free to float — would cost me anything I wanted from the scale, I kept them as they are: *"No, I think they stay."*

### The anchoring is a hybrid, and it moves slowly

The bands are not purely relative:

> The color ranges shift only slowly, after lots of evidence, so they give me a picture of how I'm doing, with a combination of absolute value and relative to ensure enough differentiation to be useful.

Three things in that. The mix is deliberate — absolute *and* relative together. The purpose it is tuned for is **differentiation**, so the scale keeps telling me something rather than parking me in one colour. And the movement is slow, gated on lots of evidence rather than on recent readings.

I have seen the drift bite:

> Yeah, and I've seen this happen before, but even then, I think communicating the actual state is the right thing.

*Abby's flag, not a finding: the case put to him was that a policy which widens the spread of his readings pushes −2σ further down in absolute terms, so the alarm bands retreat as life gets more turbulent. He granted having seen it and did not treat it as disqualifying. Whether the sigma is computed over a long baseline or a rolling recent window was asked and not answered — see the threads below.*

Numbered levels and numeric scales require absolute anchors. The Safety scale's "2 = can pay attention" and the Stress Level scale's "15 = light activity, 25 = athletic exertion" are operational capability descriptions, not relative to current distribution. Advancing past stoplight means finding fixed reference points to anchor to.

## Sub-zero territory

Sub-zero structure (negative-band states like Safety's -1 sympathetic and -2 parasympathetic flop) becomes readable only when three conditions all hold:

1. The dimension's resolution has advanced to numbered levels (level 4) or above.
2. The resource has accessible negative territory — not floored at zero.
3. I have experienced that territory.

Below stoplight resolution, descent into negative territory is real but unreadable as distinct sub-states. Even at numbered levels, sub-zero anchors only land if the negative band is reachable and has been reached.

Sub-zero readability is itself advancing from retrospect-only toward real-time. During the 2026-06-09 flop day, the full -2 flop periods were recognized in real time — "mostly my conceptual clarity of the levels and the states. I'm getting better and recognizing them automatically." The map functions as an instrument: building the conceptual model of the levels increased real-time resolution of states that previously resolved only retrospectively, and the recognition is becoming automatic via the conceptual route ([automaticity-systems.md → schema integration](automaticity-systems.book-chapter.md#c-schema-integration--intact)). ## Progression mechanism

Advancement up the levels is exposure-driven. When a dimension is the current limiting bottleneck, sustained time at that variable's edge produces finer distinctions in the experience, which then land as anchors at the next level. I don't push for measurement maturity in the abstract; whichever dimension is the current bottleneck gets advanced.

Two concrete examples:

- **Health** was the primary bottleneck for ~95% of 18 years, which is why Stress Level reached level 5 (numeric scale). The 0-100 stress-level scale predates the autism diagnosis by enough years to be the most mature reading I have.
- **Safety** went from level 0 (didn't know it existed) to its current level 4 (numbered levels with sub-zero structure) over the past year, as it became the primary bottleneck ~80% of the time. Several stoplight iterations preceded the level-4 anchoring. Safety is still the fundamental bottleneck.

## A numeric gauge costs years, so a gate-bar stays level-only

Building a strong numeric measurement system for a bar is expensive — it takes years of being at that bar's edge for the fine distinctions to land as anchors. In Alan's words: *"I think you might be miscalibrated for how hard it is to get to a strong numeric measurement system. For Health/Stress Capacity it took years."* The Health/Stress-Level gauge matured into a numeric scale, but only over a decade-plus of Health being the bottleneck.

Read against the [resource roster](four-resource-model.book-chapter.md), the maturity states are uneven by exactly this cost:

- **Health (= Stress Level — the same bar): numeric.** Matured into a numeric 0-100 / 0-100% scale, but it took years.
- **Mana and Stamina: not very numeric still.** Both at stoplight; neither has been the bottleneck long enough to fund a numeric gauge.
- **Safety: measurable only by level.** Numbered (level/capability anchors), no fine numeric scale yet.

The consequence is that Safety being level-only after ~a year as the binding bar is **on schedule, not an anomaly** — a numeric gauge costs years, and a year hasn't bought one. There's a second, reinforcing reason Safety may never need one: Safety is a [threshold gate, not a metered budget](safety.book-chapter.md#safety-is-a-threshold-gate-not-a-metered-budget). A gate only compares two levels and refuses anything above the line, so a numbered level is sufficient by construction — a numeric scale would buy nothing. A metered budget like Health, by contrast, has to track a running balance and so *earns* the cost of a numeric gauge.

## An instrument can fall idle when a cheaper governor holds the line

Measurement effort tracks the bottleneck, so an instrument can go quiet without the variable graduating or regressing. The Stress Level gauge is the worked case: Alan's most mature reading (level 5, numeric, ~20 years), yet *"I actually haven't been measuring that pillar for a few weeks, since the [affordability rule](stress-capacity-formula.book-chapter.md#the-affordability-rule-the-pillars-job-change-the-1x-cap-and-the-silent-gauge) has been enough."*

The bottleneck didn't vanish; the rule keeps him far enough from the limit that the needle doesn't move, so sampling it adds nothing. The system shifted from **feedback** (read actual stress against the gauge) to **feedforward** (trust the cost model and the 1x cap, don't sample). The constraint retired the instrument. This is distinct from the [progression-down question](../../../all-about-alan-topic/how-well-i-can-measure.all-about-alan-topic.md): the anchors are intact and the resolution is unchanged — the gauge is simply not being read, because a cheaper governor is holding the line in its place.
