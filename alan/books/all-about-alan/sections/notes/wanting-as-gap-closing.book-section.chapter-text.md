
# Wanting as gap-closing

> How a positive want fires — the trigger-based gap-closing model. Alan perceives something that triggers awareness of a gap between a current state and its ideal; the want is the desire to close that gap. He does not author the want, so he goes upstream to the one lever he can move — the triggers. The ideals the gap is measured against are either intrinsic (physical / emotional / conceptual, fired as desire or irritation) or explicit (defined in his conceptual map). Most explicit ideals are instrumental; self-improvement is the one terminal ideal.

This note is the canonical home for **how a positive want fires** in Alan. It is the layer beneath the two notes that treat *changing* a want — [Emotional Archaeology](emotional-archaeology.book-chapter.md) (the belief lever) and [the environment lever](environment-lever.book-chapter.md) — and the complement to [wanting-as-gauge.md](wanting-as-gauge.book-chapter.md), which treats *not*-wanting as a low-resource veto. Those notes act on a want; this one is the generative model that produces it in the first place.

## Open-field steering — I do what I want; if I don't, I rest

The [four intentional absolutes](volatility-governor.book-chapter.md) are guardrails at the edges, not the shape of life inside them. In Alan's words: *"you are implying that these four rules fully determine my life. Far from it. Present Alan still has a wide scope of options and is constantly making choices. The rules are guardrails around what choices are available."* The rules bound which choices are on the board; they do not pick among the ones that remain. Most of life is lived in the interior field, where nothing forbids and nothing compels.

So what steers in that open field? In his words: *"Easy enough, I do what I want to. If I don't want to do anything, I rest."* The steering input is **want**. Absence of want is not a problem to push through — it routes straight to rest, the same hard read [wanting-as-gauge.md](wanting-as-gauge.book-chapter.md) makes of not-wanting. The interior field is governed by wanting; the rest of this note is the mechanism of how that wanting arises.

## Wanting is trigger-based — the gap-closing model

Alan does not *author* a want by an act of will. The want fires on its own, triggered by a perception. In his words:

> "For me, wanting is trigger based. I perceive something that triggers awareness of a gap between the current state of something and the corresponding ideal. Wanting is my desire to close that gap. Much of my environmental design is built around controlling and choosing the triggers."

The structure has three parts in fixed order:

1. **Perception** — he perceives something.
2. **Gap awareness** — the perception triggers awareness of a gap between the **current state** of something and its **corresponding ideal**.
3. **The want** — the want *is* the desire to close that gap. It is not a separate decision laid on top of the gap; it is the gap registered as a pull.

Because the want is *read off* a perceived gap rather than authored, it is not a thing Alan can simply decide to have or not have — the same not-authored property that makes not-wanting a [gauge to obey rather than a preference to override](wanting-as-gauge.book-chapter.md). The want is downstream of the perception. So the only place to intervene is **upstream of the perception** — at the trigger.

## The one lever — control the triggers

If the want fires automatically from a perceived gap, the lever that *is* movable is which perceptions reach him. That is why *"much of my environmental design is built around controlling and choosing the triggers."* He cannot will a want into or out of existence; he can author what he will perceive, and let the wanting fire from there.

This is the rationale beneath [the environment lever](environment-lever.book-chapter.md). That note treats the physical environment as externalized memory, attention, and agenda; this note supplies *why* curating it works as want-control. A want is the desire to close a perceived gap, so:

- **Remove a trigger** and the gap is never perceived, so the want never fires ([environment-lever.md → remove things to reduce load](environment-lever.book-chapter.md#intervention-1--remove-things-to-reduce-load)).
- **Make something visible** and its gap is perceived on sight, so the want fires reliably ([environment-lever.md → make things visible](environment-lever.book-chapter.md#intervention-2--make-things-visible-to-direct-attention)).
- **Deny entry to high-entropy triggers** and the want-field stays the one he endorses rather than the one his surroundings author for him ([environment-lever.md → trigger curation](environment-lever.book-chapter.md#trigger-curation--removing-high-entropy-triggers)).

The gap-closing model is the *theory*; the environment lever is the *practice* the theory licenses. Trigger curation is rational precisely because wanting is trigger-based — the upstream point is the only point he can move.

## Where the ideals come from — intrinsic vs explicit

A gap is measured against an **ideal** — the reference point the current state falls short of. Alan's ideals come in two kinds:

> "I think the ideals are either intrinsic, I perceive something and that triggers a desire or irritation of some sort. Those can be physical (hungry), emotional (lonely), or conceptual (unnecessary complexity). The other case is ideals that are explicitly defined in my conceptual map, such as 'all of my stoplight metrics should be green or blue'."

### Intrinsic ideals — given, not authored

Intrinsic ideals are not built; they fire on their own as **desire or irritation**. They map one-to-one onto the [tripartite self](self-architecture.book-chapter.md) — one flavor per part of him:

- **Physical** — hunger. The Body self's gap.
- **Emotional** — loneliness. The Emotions self's gap.
- **Conceptual** — unnecessary complexity. The Mind self's gap.

The conceptual flavor is the one worth naming carefully: an ideal held by the **Mind** fires with the same gut-level pull as a body signal. Unnecessary complexity *irritates* him the way hunger does — it is not a calm preference for tidiness but a felt itch demanding to be closed. The conceptual self has its own appetites and aversions, and they register as bodily-grade signals, not as detached judgments. (This is consistent with [the split being real but not unlimited](self-architecture.book-chapter.md#the-split-is-real-but-not-unlimited--load-floods-across): the conceptual self is its own person with its own felt drives, not a dispassionate witness.)

### Explicit ideals — defined in the conceptual map

The other kind is **explicitly defined** — a target Alan wrote into his conceptual map, against which a current state can be checked. The worked example is the [values-personas system](values-personas-system.book-chapter.md): the ideal *"all of my stoplight metrics should be green or blue."* A yellow or red light is a perceived gap against that explicitly-defined ideal, and the want to close it is what the [notification-badge mechanic](values-personas-system.book-chapter.md#what-the-little-motivation-actually-is--the-light-itself) runs on. The explicit ideal supplies the reference point; the off-green light is the perceived gap; clearing it is the want.

So the two kinds differ in origin but run through the same gap-closing engine. An intrinsic ideal is given and fires as raw desire or irritation; an explicit ideal is authored into the map and fires when a perceived state is checked against it. Either way, a perception surfaces a gap against an ideal, and the want is the pull to close it.

## Instrumental vs terminal ideals

The ideals also split on *why* they are wanted. Most explicit ideals are **instrumental** — wanted not for their own sake but for what closing their gap produces. The stoplight ideal is the clean case: green is wanted because green means the intrinsic ideals underneath it (health, connection, truth, …) are being fed. The [lever chain runs scoring → behavior → Safety](values-personas-system.book-chapter.md#the-lever-chain--system--behavior--safety); the green light is instrumental to the resource state it tracks, not an end in itself.

The one explicitly-held **terminal** ideal — pursued for its own sake — is **self-improvement.** In Alan's words: *"self-improvement is an ideal that I pursue for its own sake."* And on *why* it is terminal: *"It's not that it isn't completable that makes it the terminal, it's the center of my identity and path, so everything else derives from that."* It is terminal not because its gap can never close, but because it is the **root** — the [atom every other belief and purpose compresses into](self-improvement.book-chapter.md) — so every other ideal derives from it rather than the reverse. The terminal-self-improvement-as-identity-center material is already developed at its canonical home; this note only places it in the ideals taxonomy. See [self-improvement.md](self-improvement.book-chapter.md) for the atom and [journey-as-reward.md](journey-as-reward.book-chapter.md) for the journey-not-destination corollary.
