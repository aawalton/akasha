
# Autism as neurotype (working definition)

- Autism is a neurotype — a difference in brain architecture that has been around as long as there have been humans. Brings significant advantages and significant disadvantages.
- "Superpowers + kryptonite" framing. Much of his life has been spent finding ways to use his superpowers to avoid his kryptonite.
- Would-you-take-a-pill question: no for autism (would change him so fundamentally he would no longer be himself). Yes in a heartbeat for the comorbidities of autism.
- Foreshadow: as much as autism itself profoundly influences the course of someone's life, the comorbidities often have an even more pronounced impact. (Full treatment in [autism-comorbidities.md](autism-comorbidities.book-chapter.md).)

## Heart of autism: nervous-system sensitivity + non-habituation

Two mechanism roots sit under autism: [nervous-system sensitivity](nervous-system-sensitivity.book-chapter.md) and [non-habituation](non-habituation.book-chapter.md). Almost all of the kryptonite — and much of the superpower side — radiates from these two. This is the autism-side parallel to ADHD, whose heart is a single trait, executive dysfunction ([adhd-as-neurotype.md](adhd-as-neurotype.book-chapter.md)).

The two roots compose. Sensitivity sets a high cost per input — and, on the same dial, fast learning, since learning is proportional to intensity of nervous-system activation. Non-habituation means that cost never amortizes: every exposure costs the same as the first. Together, every input is expensive and stays expensive permanently — and the fast-learn, easy-overwhelm, high-ceiling, no-plateau signatures all fall out of that one pairing.
