
# Being-known cost

> Being-known cost — being known modeled as a model-update operation the other person runs, its cost decomposition (mechanics + content), the inertia-mismatch dynamic between change-seeker and change-resister, and the throughput limit that turns fast identity change into an accruing being-known deficit.

Being known is not a state the other person occupies; it is an **operation they run.** To know me is for them to hold an internal model of me and to keep it current. Being known, then, is the other person **updating their internal model of me** as I change. That reframing is what makes the cost legible: every update has a price, and the price is paid by the knower.

This is the mechanism behind the [Connection tank](connection.book-chapter.md#four-factors--what-the-need-decomposes-into) — the being-deeply-known sub-need. The tank fills when someone's model of me is current; it drains as I change faster than their model tracks.

## Cost decomposition — mechanics plus content

The total cost to the knower of one model-update splits into two parts.

- **Mechanics cost** — the effort of revising the model at all. Always present. Even a perfectly welcome update takes work to take in, integrate, and store. This is the floor; no update is free.
- **Content cost** — paid **only when the new content is misaligned** with the knower's own beliefs or values. Updating their model of me to include something they disagree with, or that conflicts with how they see the world, costs more than updating it with something neutral or congenial. Aligned content pays mechanics only; misaligned content pays both.

The split matters because the two costs have different levers. Mechanics cost is reduced by throughput (below) and by channel choice (see [the channel dial](connection-expansion.book-chapter.md#the-channel-dial)). Content cost is reduced by **reaching alignment** — which is why real-time mutual processing is load-bearing rather than a tolerated expense (see [connection-expansion.md → real-time being-known](connection-expansion.book-chapter.md#real-time-being-known--the-audio-only-channel)).

## The inertia mismatch

How expensive the mechanics cost runs depends on the knower's **cognitive inertia** — how hard their self-and-world model resists revision.

- **Low inertia** (aphantasia, in my case) → seeks change, undergoes rapid identity change, and **generates** model-updates fast. The person being known is a high-output source of updates.
- **High inertia** (hyperphantasia) → resists change, integrates updates slowly and **expensively.** The person doing the knowing is a low-throughput, high-cost sink.

The pathological pairing is a **change-seeker partnered with a change-resister.** It puts the maximal update demand on the most update-resistant knower — and does so exactly when identity is changing fastest, so the demand peaks precisely when the channel is least able to carry it. This is a concrete instance, not a generic claim: it is the shape of the being-known load between me and Jen.

### Why Jen's cost runs doubly high — hyperfantasia

Jen is generally willing to do the costly work of knowing me. But the work costs *her* more than it would cost most knowers, for two compounding reasons, both downstream of one trait: **hyperfantasia.**

- **Stronger emotional memory → more inertia.** Vivid, durable emotional memory means her current state carries the charge of past states forward. Her self-and-world model is harder to shift because more of it is held in place by accumulated feeling. High inertia, by the mechanism above, makes every model-update expensive.
- **Vivid negative-future simulation → anticipatory anxiety.** She can pre-play negative future possibilities in full sensory and emotional detail. Taking in a hard update about me means living the futures it implies, which adds an anxiety cost on top of the mechanics cost.

Name the inversion plainly: this is the **mirror image of my own architecture.** I carry almost no [emotional-memory inertia](present-tense-model.book-chapter.md#the-map-is-affectively-flat--structure-no-charge) and cannot [simulate futures](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md#no-forward-simulator) at all, so I have neither her inertia nor that flavor of anticipatory anxiety. We sit at **opposite poles of the imagery axis** — total aphantasia ↔ hyperfantasia — and that maximal distance is exactly what makes *mutually* knowing each other expensive. The distance that drives knower-similarity cost (above) is, between us, at its maximum.

The same imagery-axis polarity drives the [disputed-misery memory dispute](disputed-misery.book-chapter.md): Jen's hyperfantasia gives her vivid, state-dependent emotional memory that reconstructs the shared past differently than Alan's contemporaneous record, so the polarity that makes mutual knowing expensive also makes the two of them disagree about what the worst 20 years were even like.

### The intersection limit and instrumental capture

Two limited tanks gate the channel, not one. My affordable-update output is bounded by my [connection dose](connection-dosing.book-chapter.md); her affordable-update intake is bounded by the hyperfantasia cost above. We must rate-limit these conversations to the **intersection of the two capacities** — throughput is gated by the smaller of two limited tanks, in whichever window both have headroom (the [joint-capacity window](connection-dosing.book-chapter.md)).

And the scarce high-capacity window typically gets spent on something other than being-known. It goes to **negotiating shared concerns that are blocking my current critical-path hypothesis** — getting *me* unblocked. So the very bandwidth that could close the being-known gap is consumed by instrumental problem-solving; being-known happens only adjacently, as a byproduct, never as the thing the window was spent on.

## Knower similarity drives the base cost

Inertia sets how *expensively* a knower updates. **Similarity** sets how *much* they have to update in the first place — and it is the larger lever on the mechanics cost.

To build a model of me, a knower starts from some prior. The closer their own mind is to mine, the more of that prior is already correct, and the less they have to build. A **highly similar knower barely has to update at all: they can use themselves as the model of me.** The mechanics cost collapses toward zero, because the model they need is mostly the one they already run on themselves.

This is why being known is normally **expensive**. Most people have a mind wildly unlike mine, so their default prior about me is wrong in most places, and they must construct a non-standard model from scratch — high mechanics cost, sustained. The cost isn't intrinsic to me; it's the *distance* between the knower's self-model and me.

Two levers therefore lower the base cost: a **near-twin** (low distance → little to build) or **expertise** (a knower skilled at building non-standard models cheaply). The supply consequence — who can close the gap cheaply vs. expensively, and the plan that exploits both — is worked out in [being-known-supply.md](being-known-supply.book-chapter.md).

## The danger geometry — stake in the accuracy of the model

Knower similarity sets how *much* a knower must update and inertia sets how *expensively*; a third axis sets how *dangerous* being known is. The danger scales with the knower's **stake in the accuracy of their model of me.**

This inverts the intuitive reading. Vulnerability is **safe with strangers**, for **two distinct reasons** that the intuitive reading collapses into one.

- **The best-behavior mask.** Strangers run a default best-behavior script on first contact — they mask and show their best behavior — which supplies short-horizon predictability and, on its own, makes them safe to be around early. This is also the channel that quiets the [uncertainty estimator](safety-estimator.book-chapter.md#the-mask-resolves-the-apparent-contradiction--un-modelable-yet-safe) for an un-modelable stranger: the mask buys safety the person-specific model hasn't earned.
- **No stake in my model.** The validity of my self-beliefs does not affect a stranger. A stranger has no reason to correct my model of myself; if I'm wrong about who I am, it costs them nothing, so they let it stand. This is the stake axis: danger scales with how much the other person's life depends on holding an accurate model of me.

The corpus has historically weighted the stake reason as primary and the mask as a softening detail. Alan's [middle-distance safety ranking](closeness-paradox.book-chapter.md#the-safety-ordering) — where strangers on best behavior outrank long-positive relationships, and the safest zone is the *middle* (modeled but not yet correcting) — implies the **mask carries more weight than that ordering of reasons grants.** The best-behavior window is doing first-class work, not trim. *(The two-reasons split and the stake account are Alan-stated; the relative-weight inference — that the mask outweighs what the note previously credited — is read off his ranking, not a separate claim he made.)* Danger is therefore **not a function of closeness per se;** it is a function of both how much stake the other person has in their model of me **and** whether the best-behavior mask is still up.

Jen's whole life-model has me **load-bearing.** So every update to my self-understanding forces a revision of *her* map of her own life — which compels her to [wrestle with the update](criticism-and-ostracism.book-chapter.md) rather than absorb it quietly, because absorbing a wrong update would mis-build the map she runs her life on. That wrestling is the exact cue that trips my [criticism alarm](criticism-and-ostracism.book-chapter.md). The danger geometry and the alarm meet here: her stake compels the pushback, and the pushback fires the survival alarm.

The cruel structure: **the people who most need to know me accurately are exactly the ones whose stake compels the pushback.** A near-twin therapist ([Amy](being-known-supply.book-chapter.md#the-committed-plan--therapy-with-amy-corry)) is cheap and willing partly because her life does not load-bear on me — low stake, low compelled-wrestling, low danger. The person with the highest stake (Jen) is the highest-danger channel for the same reason she is the most important one.

And Knowing/[Being-Known is the bar in the red](connection.book-chapter.md#a-homeostatic-requirement-not-a-luxury) — the [critical-path reach](recovery-ladder.book-chapter.md#connection-is-the-current-critical-path-reach). So the only channel that can fill the most-depleted resource is the same channel that trips the deepest survival alarm. That collision is what makes the [regulated-anchor exposure](regulated-anchor-exposure.book-chapter.md) work necessary rather than optional: there is no low-danger route to the resource that matters most.

## Throughput and the being-known deficit

Being-known is not transmitted in one batch. It moves in **partial syncs**, each bounded by what's affordable in that session — mechanics plus content for the updates that session carries. The channel has a **throughput**: a maximum rate of model-update it can pass per unit time.

When the **rate of identity change exceeds channel throughput**, the updates I generate outrun the updates the channel transmits. The untransmitted remainder accrues as a **being-known deficit** — a growing gap between who I am now and who the other person's model says I am.

This deficit is distinct from a batched teardown. A teardown is a single large shock — a long-hidden truth disclosed all at once, paid in one expensive session. The being-known deficit is **throughput-limited, not batch-shock:** it accrues continuously because the steady rate of change exceeds the steady rate of transmission, even when no single update is large. The fix for a teardown is one big affordable session; the fix for a throughput deficit is **raising throughput** (richer channel, more frequent sync, lower content cost via alignment) or slowing the rate of change — a different remedy entirely.

## Being-known is the footprint of the need, not the need

A precision correction sits underneath this whole cost model. Being-known is **not Alan's mammalian social need.** It is the clearest **footprint** of that need on the conceptual side of his being — the side he has easy access to. In his words: "I don't think being known is my mammalian social need. I think it is the clearest footprint of that need on the conceptual side of my being that I have easy access to. I am uncertain about the footprint on the physical/emotional side."

The model that follows from the correction: there is a single **need** — old, mammalian, running the same social hardware and software any [social obligate mammal](connection.book-chapter.md#a-homeostatic-requirement-not-a-luxury) runs, outside Alan's awareness and access. That need throws **two footprints.**

- **The conceptual footprint** — being-known. Alan can read it, and only this year, as he started to [understand himself](emotional-archaeology.book-chapter.md), did it become something he could begin to feed.
- **The physical/emotional footprint** — behind the [aphantasia wall](aphantasia-mechanism.book-chapter.md), where he cannot tell whether anything is landing.

Feeding the readable footprint may or may not reach the need itself — he can't see the far wall, and he refuses to assume it does. But it **reliably** does one thing: it sharpens his explanations of himself. "Better clarity means better explanations of myself, which means better grounded interventions, which means better results." Those explanations are the **inputs to every intervention he aims at the physical side.** So clarity is upstream of the body's work even when it never directly touches it — useful either way, and specifically useful as *the source the physical interventions draw from.*

The discipline he holds: he **won't stamp the need "met"** just because the footprint he can see is getting fed. There is a likely gap on the side he can't instrument, and an honest ledger leaves it open. Compressing "the readable part looks satisfied" into "the need is satisfied" would be a lie, so the book stays open in the dark on purpose. This is the same [truth-gate](self-improvement.book-chapter.md) turned on his own healing.
