
# Portuguese Citizenship Paths (May 2026)

Portugal runs Lei da Nacionalidade no. **37/81** as repeatedly amended; the **2026 reform** (Decree-Law 48/XVII, promulgated by President António José Seguro on **3 May 2026** after a Dec 2025 Constitutional Court strike-down and a 1 Apr 2026 re-vote) extends standard naturalization from **5 to 10 years** and CPLP/EU naturalization from **3 to 7 years**, restarts the residency clock from **first residence-card issuance** (not application date), tightens jus soli, adds a culture/history test alongside the **A2 CIPLE** language exam, and is in the brief window between promulgation and publication in the Diário da República as of 16 May 2026 (publication expected within ~30 days, in-force the day after). The **Sephardic Jewish path** (Law 30-A/2015) effectively closed: Law 1/2024 imposed a 3-year-residence requirement and the practical pipeline is winding down with no new claims accepted in 2026. The **Golden Visa** dropped its real-estate option in Oct 2023 (Mais Habitação / Law 56/2023); fund, R&D, cultural-donation, job-creation, and business routes remain. **Dual citizenship is fully and unconditionally permitted.** Sources: IRN, AIMA, Diário da República, Morais Leitão, Constitutional Court Decree 17/XVII ruling Dec 2025.

## Birth on Territory (Conditional Jus Soli — Art. 1)

- **Basis**: Art. 1(1)(e)–(f) Lei 37/81; tightened by the 2026 reform.
- **Pre-reform rule (applies to births before in-force date)**: child born in Portugal acquires citizenship at birth if **at least one parent has 1 year of legal residence** prior to birth (any permit type).
- **2026 reform rule (post-publication births)**: child born in Portugal acquires citizenship at birth if at least one parent has **5 years legal residence** (general) or **3 years** for CPLP-country parents, **plus** the child must be enrolled in Portuguese compulsory schooling. No transitional grandfathering for existing residents — the new threshold applies to any birth after the law enters into force.
- **Stateless-child protection**: child born in Portugal who would otherwise be stateless acquires Portuguese citizenship automatically (preserved across reforms).
- **Timeline**: civil-registry registration; effective at birth. Processing typically 3–9 months for the citizenship certificate.

## Descent — Portuguese Parent (Jus Sanguinis, Art. 1(1)(a)–(c))

- **Basis**: Art. 1(1)(a)–(c) Lei 37/81.
- **Eligibility**: any person born to at least one Portuguese parent acquires Portuguese citizenship **automatically at birth**, anywhere in the world; child must be registered in the Portuguese civil registry (Conservatória dos Registos Centrais via consulate).
- **Requirements**: parent's citizenship documented; no language, no residency, no test, no effective-ties requirement for the parent track.
- **Timeline**: consular birth registration **6–24 months** in practice (longer for high-volume consulates like Brazil/USA); passport follows within weeks.
- **Cost**: ~€175 registration fee plus translations.

## Descent — Portuguese Grandparent (Art. 1(1)(d))

- **Basis**: Art. 1(1)(d) Lei 37/81 — citizenship of origin for grandchildren of a Portuguese citizen by birth, **upon declaration of will** and proof of effective ties.
- **Eligibility**: grandparent was Portuguese at the time of applicant's birth (or remained Portuguese); declaration of will to acquire; **effective ties to the Portuguese community** (ligação efetiva). The 2020 amendment created a **presumption of effective ties** if the applicant has **A2 Portuguese language** proficiency (CIPLE, IILP, or recognized course); otherwise prove via regular visits, family in Portugal, property, cultural/community involvement.
- **No residency requirement**, no criminal-record bar of its own (general criminal-record limits do apply via Art. 9 grounds of opposition).
- **Timeline**: consular or IRN application; **12–36 months** in practice.
- **Note**: this is the workhorse diaspora path for the Lusophone-American, Brazilian-Portuguese, South African, and Indian Goan communities. **Not affected** by the 2026 residency-extension reform.

## Descent — Great-Grandparent (Naturalization Discount, Art. 6(7))

- **Basis**: Art. 6(7) Lei 37/81 — great-grandchildren of Portuguese citizens by birth qualify for **naturalization** (not citizenship of origin) on a reduced track if they take up residence in Portugal.
- **Eligibility**: documented great-grandparent who held Portuguese citizenship by birth; A2 Portuguese; effective ties; clean criminal record. Under pre-reform law, **5 years of legal residence** was the reduced threshold (same as standard); under the 2026 reform, this collapses into the new 7-year (CPLP/EU) or 10-year (others) residency baseline unless interpreted as a preserved fast-track — current commentary suggests great-grandchildren retain a discounted track but the exact threshold is under regulatory clarification.
- **Timeline**: residency window + 12–24 months processing.

## Standard Naturalization by Residency (Art. 6)

- **Basis**: Art. 6(1) Lei 37/81 as amended by 2026 reform.
- **Pre-2026-reform rule (applies to applications pending at entry into force)**: **5 years legal residence** (3 for CPLP/EU under prior practice), A2 Portuguese (CIPLE or recognized course), clean record (no conviction ≥3 years under Portuguese law), effective ties, sufficient means.
- **Post-reform rule (applications filed after entry into force)**:
  - **10 years legal residence** for third-country nationals (general baseline).
  - **7 years legal residence** for **CPLP-country nationals** (Brazil, Angola, Mozambique, Cabo Verde, Guinea-Bissau, São Tomé e Príncipe, Equatorial Guinea, Timor-Leste) and **EU/EEA citizens**.
  - **A2 Portuguese** (CIPLE certificate from CAPLE/Universidade de Lisboa, or IILP-recognized equivalent, or 150-hour PLA course). CPLP citizens may have language demonstration waived as native speakers.
  - **Portuguese culture/history/civics test** (new — implementing regulation pending).
  - **Effective ties to the Portuguese community** (Art. 9(b) grounds-of-opposition reframed as a positive criterion).
  - Clean record: no conviction ≥**3 years** prison sentence under Portuguese law (the 2-year threshold proposed in Decree 17/XVII was struck down as unconstitutional by the Constitutional Court Dec 2025; 3 years is the enacted version).
  - **Residency clock starts on first residence-card issuance**, not application date — eliminating the prior practice of counting AIMA wait time.
- **Timeline**: 7 or 10 years residence + **12–24 months** processing (legal limit is 4 months, rarely met); ~**8–12 years total** (CPLP) or **11–13 years total** (others) in practice.
- **Backlog**: AIMA/IRN have ~**40,000–60,000 pending citizenship cases** as of April 2026; most 2022–2025 primary applicants are now receiving 2026 appointments — clearing but not cleared.
- **Cost**: €250 application + ~€175 registration + language/test fees.

## Naturalization by Marriage / De Facto Union (Art. 3)

- **Basis**: Art. 3 Lei 37/81 — facilitated acquisition for spouses and de-facto partners of Portuguese citizens. **Not changed in core duration by 2026 reform** but tightened on integration evidence.
- **Eligibility**:
  - **3 years of marriage** or judicially recognized **de facto union** to a Portuguese citizen (parish-declaration de facto unions no longer suffice post-reform — court recognition required).
  - **Effective ties to the Portuguese community** — work, study, property, community involvement, or family ties. Waived if the couple has a **common child** or has been married/in union for **6+ years**.
  - Clean criminal record (Art. 9 grounds of opposition apply).
  - A2 Portuguese **not** statutorily required for marriage track, though language is a common effective-ties proof.
  - **No physical residence in Portugal required** under the statute (effective-ties can be demonstrated from abroad), though residing helps the file.
- **Timeline**: 3 years marriage + **12–36 months** IRN processing.
- **Note**: Marriage is the fastest naturalization path without prior ancestry; survives the 2026 reform with only integration tightening.

## Investment / Golden Visa

- **No citizenship-by-investment shortcut.** Portugal's Golden Visa (ARI) is a residence permit only; Golden Visa years count toward the standard Art. 6 residency clock (7 years CPLP/EU, 10 years others post-2026 reform). See [residency/portugal.md](../residency/portugal.book-chapter.md) for current investment routes, the closed real-estate tiers, and clock-start mechanics. Language A2 and culture test still required at naturalization.

## Sephardic Jewish Path (Law 30-A/2015 — Effectively Closed)

- **Basis**: Art. 6(7) extended by Law 30-A/2015; **tightened by Law 1/2024 (5 March 2024, in force 1 April 2024)** after the Roman Abramovich scandal exposed weak vetting (Abramovich was granted citizenship in April 2021 via the Porto Jewish Community certificate; the Porto rabbi was later charged with forgery and corruption in 2022).
- **Pre-2024 rule**: Sephardic-origin certificate from the Lisbon or Porto Jewish Community + clean record + no Portuguese residency needed. ~262,000 applied by end of 2022; ~75,000 grants.
- **Law 1/2024 changes**:
  - Adds a **3-year prior legal residence** requirement in Portugal (collapsing the path's main appeal — distance-friendliness).
  - Sephardic origin must be evidenced by **objective ties to Portugal** beyond a community certificate.
  - Creates an **evaluation commission** with academic Sephardic-studies experts to review every grant.
  - Transitional regime for pre-1 Apr 2024 applications: decided under prior rules; pending pipeline being worked down through 2025–2026.
- **2026 state**: New applications are effectively not viable — the 3-year residence + objective-ties bar means it now functions as a slower variant of standard naturalization, with no advantage. Multiple law firms describe the path as **closed for new claims** as of 2026. The 2026 reform package also signaled formal repeal but the precise statutory wind-down language is in regulatory pending.
- **Pending pipeline**: ~150,000+ unresolved applications filed before the 2024 cutoff; IRN clearing through 2026–2027. No new admissibility.

## CPLP Nationals — Accelerated Naturalization

- **Basis**: Art. 6(1) reduced threshold for CPLP-state nationals (Brazil, Angola, Mozambique, Cabo Verde, Guinea-Bissau, São Tomé e Príncipe, Equatorial Guinea, Timor-Leste).
- **Pre-reform**: 3 years legal residence.
- **2026-reform**: **7 years legal residence**, A2 language demonstration generally waived as native Portuguese speakers (except Equatorial Guinea / Timor-Leste, situational), culture/history test still applies, clean record.
- **Timeline**: 7 years residence + 12–24 months processing = **8–10 years total**.
- **Note**: CPLP citizens may use the **CPLP Mobility Agreement** (ratified 2021, in-force Portugal 2022) to obtain residence permits on simplified terms — the residence permit makes the citizenship clock start; the agreement does not itself shorten the citizenship clock.

## Macanese / Former-Portuguese-Territory Path

- **Basis**: Decree-Law 308-A/75 (post-decolonization nationality rules) and Art. 14 Lei 37/81; specific carve-outs for Macau preserved via Macau-handover agreements.
- **Eligibility (Macau-specific)**:
  - Born in Macau and dependencies **before 21 Nov 1981** (date Lei 37/81 took effect in Macau): entitled to Portuguese citizenship by birth.
  - Born in Macau **21 Nov 1981 – 19 Dec 1999** to a Portuguese father or mother: entitled by descent.
  - Born after **20 Dec 1999** (handover): standard descent rules apply.
  - Women foreign-born married to Portuguese nationals before 21 Nov 1981 and their descendants: entitled.
- **Mechanism**: register birth in the Portuguese civil registry; no language test, no residency, no effective-ties test for those qualifying directly.
- **Timeline**: 6–24 months registration.
- **Note**: Former African colonies (Angola, Mozambique, Cabo Verde, Guinea-Bissau, São Tomé) lost Portuguese citizenship en bloc at independence in 1975 under DL 308-A/75 unless specific criteria met — descendants now use the standard CPLP or descent routes.

## Refugee / Stateless / Subsidiary-Protection Path

- **Basis**: Art. 6(1) + Asylum Law 27/2008.
- **Pre-reform**: 5 years from recognition of protection status; same Art. 6 conditions otherwise.
- **2026-reform**: Folds into the **10-year general / 7-year CPLP-EU** threshold; **stateless persons** retain an aggregation rule (6 years counted from any legal residence period).
- **Stateless children born in Portugal**: automatic citizenship at birth (Art. 1).
- **Pre-recognition asylum-seeker years**: do **not** count.
- **Timeline**: 10 years from recognition + processing ≈ **11–13 years total** post-reform; 6 years for stateless under aggregation rule.

## Honorary / Distinguished Services (Art. 6(6))

- **Basis**: Art. 6(6) Lei 37/81 — discretionary government grant of citizenship for "relevant services rendered to the Portuguese State or the national community."
- **Eligibility**: discretionary cabinet decision; no residency, language, or test. Used sparingly (a handful per year) — cultural, scientific, humanitarian, athletic contributions.
- **Timeline**: months to years, case-by-case.
- **Note**: This was the formal route the Abramovich grant exploited under the Sephardic provision; tightened scrutiny since 2022.

## Children of Naturalized Parents (Art. 2)

- **Basis**: Art. 2 Lei 37/81 — minor children of a person naturalizing may acquire Portuguese citizenship by declaration of the naturalizing parent (or upon reaching age of majority by their own declaration).
- **Effect**: derivative; effective on parent's grant.
- **Timeline**: tied to parent's process plus civil-registry registration (3–9 months).

## Re-acquisition of Lost Citizenship (Art. 30–31)

- **Basis**: Art. 30–31 Lei 37/81 (current consolidated text; major liberalization via Law 1/2004 of 15 Jan 2004 which eliminated grounds of opposition and made re-acquisition ex lege where loss had not been registered).
- **Eligibility categories**:
  - Former Portuguese citizens who voluntarily acquired a foreign nationality and lost Portuguese citizenship before 1981 (when dual citizenship became permitted).
  - Women who lost citizenship via marriage to a foreigner under pre-1981 law.
  - Persons resident in Portugal <5 years on 25 April 1974 who lost citizenship under DL 308-A/75 transitional rules (former colonial subjects), provided they stayed in Portugal and did not serve a foreign state.
- **Mechanism**: declaration of will to re-acquire; civil-registry processing.
- **Timeline**: 6–24 months; no language or residency requirement.

## Service-Based (Military)

- Portugal does **not** offer a discrete "military service citizenship track" for foreigners. Portuguese Armed Forces recruit only Portuguese citizens (including dual-nationals and naturalized citizens). Distinguished foreign service to Portuguese national interests may qualify under Art. 6(6) honorary — extremely rare.

## Comparison Summary

Ranked by **speed and likelihood** (May 2026, post-reform):

1. **Descent via Portuguese parent (Art. 1(1)(a)–(c))** — 6–24 months consular. Automatic at birth; documentation gate only. **Best path if eligible**.
2. **Macanese pre-1999 birth (DL 308-A/75)** — 6–24 months registration; only for documented Macau-born cohorts.
3. **Re-acquisition (Art. 30)** — 6–24 months; narrow eligibility, near-certain if you fit.
4. **Descent via grandparent (Art. 1(1)(d))** — 12–36 months. A2 Portuguese or other effective-ties proof. **Workhorse diaspora path; unaffected by 2026 reform**.
5. **CPLP MOA + naturalization** — ~8–10 years total (7 years residence + 1–3 processing). Best path for Brazilians/Angolans/Mozambicans without ancestry.
6. **Marriage / de facto union (Art. 3)** — 3 years + 1–3 processing ≈ **4–6 years total**. Fastest naturalization path without ancestry. Survives 2026 reform with integration tightening.
7. **Great-grandparent naturalization (Art. 6(7))** — ~6–11 years depending on regulatory clarification post-reform; presumes residency.
8. **Standard naturalization, EU citizen** — ~8–10 years (7 residence + processing).
9. **Standard naturalization, third-country** — **11–13 years** post-reform; principal hard path.
10. **Golden Visa → naturalization** — ~11–13 years (8–10 if CPLP). Visa lets you keep your existing life elsewhere with low presence; citizenship clock unchanged.
11. **Refugee / stateless** — 11–13 years from protection grant (6 years stateless aggregation); slower in practice given asylum delays.
12. **Honorary (Art. 6(6))** — discretionary; functionally inaccessible without national-interest contribution.
13. **Sephardic Jewish (Law 30-A/2015)** — **effectively closed**. New applications not viable post-Law 1/2024 (3-year residence + objective-ties bar). Pipeline winding down through 2026–2027.

**Dual citizenship**: **unconditionally permitted** under Portuguese law since 1981 — no renunciation, no notification, no cap. Reacquisition explicitly recognizes prior loss to dual-citizenship rules.

**Flag-worthy recent changes**:
- **Mais Habitação / Law 56/2023 (Oct 2023)**: Golden Visa real-estate options eliminated; fund/R&D/cultural/jobs routes remain.
- **Law 1/2024 (1 Apr 2024)**: Sephardic path gutted by 3-year-residence + objective-ties + evaluation-commission requirements; functionally closed for new claims.
- **Constitutional Court ruling (15 Dec 2025)** on Decree 17/XVII: struck down four provisions including the 2-year-prison conviction bar and automatic-revocation rule; 10-year/7-year timeline survived.
- **2026 reform — Lei Orgânica 1/2026 (Decree 48/XVII)**: promulgated 3 May 2026; awaiting publication in Diário da República (expected by mid-May 2026; reportedly published 18 May / in-force 19 May per advance press). Extends naturalization to 10 years general / 7 years CPLP+EU; clock from first residence card; tighter jus soli; culture test alongside A2 language; pending IRN cases decided under prior 5-year rule.
- **Backlog**: 40k–60k IRN citizenship cases pending April 2026; AIMA absorbed SEF in 2023 and is still digesting both pipelines.
