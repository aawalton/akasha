
# Austria

Austria is one of the EU's most restrictive jurisdictions for naturalization: standard residency is 10 years, dual citizenship is generally prohibited (foreign nationality must be renounced before grant, and Austrians who voluntarily acquire another citizenship automatically lose Austrian unless pre-approved retention is granted), income and language gates are real, and Vienna's MA35 carries multi-year backlogs. The two paths that are both fast and dual-citizenship-friendly are §58c (descendants of Nazi-era persecuted persons) and §10(6) (extraordinary services). A coalition reform (ÖVP–SPÖ–NEOS) is in preparation in 2026 — expected to raise the language bar from B1 to B2 and add a mandatory ÖIF citizenship course — but no draft bill has been published as of May 2026. Legal source: [Staatsbürgerschaftsgesetz 1985 (StbG), consolidated text on RIS](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/GeltendeFassung.wxe?Abfrage=Bundesnormen&Gesetzesnummer=10005579).

## Birth on territory (jus soli)

- **Category**: birth; effectively does not apply.
- **Rule**: Austria is pure jus sanguinis. Birth on Austrian soil to non-Austrian parents confers nothing. Two narrow exceptions: (a) foundlings under 6 months are presumed Austrian; (b) children born stateless in Austria may apply between 18–21 if they have lived lawfully in Austria ≥10 years (5 continuous before application) — see "Stateless paths" below. Foreign-born-in-Austria residents get a reduced 6-year residency for standard naturalization instead of 10. ([Wikipedia: Austrian nationality law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_nationality_law))

## Descent (jus sanguinis) — by birth

- **Category**: descent / automatic at birth.
- **Eligibility**: at least one parent is Austrian at the time of the child's birth. If parents are married and either is Austrian → automatic. If unmarried and only the father is Austrian → father must acknowledge paternity (or paternity must be judicially established) within 8 weeks of birth; otherwise the child does not acquire Austrian citizenship by descent. No generational limit — descent flows indefinitely as long as the chain of births occurred while the parent held Austrian citizenship. ([oesterreich.gv.at — Acquiring citizenship by descent](https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/en/themen/menschen_aus_anderen_staaten/staatsbuergerschaft/Seite.260410))
- **Timeline**: at birth; consular certificate of citizenship takes weeks to several months.
- **Backlog**: depends on the consulate — typically a few months.
- **Dual citizenship**: allowed when acquired automatically at birth (e.g., child born abroad to Austrian + foreign parent). This is the largest carve-out from Austria's monocitizenship rule. ([oesterreich.gv.at — Dual citizenship](https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/en/themen/menschen_aus_anderen_staaten/staatsbuergerschaft/Seite.260430))

## §58c — Restitution for descendants of Nazi-era persecuted persons

- **Category**: special / declaration-based ("Anzeige"); the single most-used dual-citizenship-friendly path in the 2020s.
- **Eligibility**: applicant is the victim themselves, or any direct descendant (child, grandchild, great-grandchild, …) of an Austrian citizen or resident who left Austria before 15 May 1955 because of persecution (or fear of persecution) by NSDAP organs or Third Reich authorities, or because of persecution for defending the democratic Republic of Austria. Adopted minors count as descendants. Spouses and registered partners are not eligible — only blood/adopted-minor lineage. No residency, language, income, or civics requirement. ([BMEIA — Citizenship for Persecuted Persons and their Direct Descendants](https://www.bmeia.gv.at/en/austrian-embassy-london/service-for-citizens/citizenship-for-persecuted-persons-and-their-direct-descendants))
- **Timeline**: declaration filed at the competent Austrian embassy/consulate; statutory MA35/BMI processing target is 6 months, real-world 4–9 months once files reach Vienna. From first appointment to passport often 12–18 months total. ([BMEIA — §58c FAQ](https://www.bmeia.gv.at/en/consular-section-of-the-austrian-embassy-in-washington/service-for-citizens/citizenship-for-persecuted-persons-and-their-direct-descendants/frequently-asked-questions-faq))
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: consulate appointment queues (Tel Aviv, NYC, London, Berlin) remain multi-month for an initial slot; uptake remains very high. 2024: 8,783 §58c naturalizations (≈99.9% living abroad). 2025: 9,521 §58c naturalizations — 38.0% of all 2025 naturalizations. Top countries of residence: Israel, US, UK. Since the May 2022 extension, ≥37,000 people have reclaimed Austrian citizenship via this route. ([Statistik Austria — Naturalisations 2025](https://www.statistik.at/fileadmin/announcement/2026/02/20260218Einbuergerungen2025EN.pdf), [The Local — 2026 naturalisation profile](https://www.thelocal.at/20260218/austria-sees-a-rise-in-naturalisations-but-who-are-the-new-citizens-in-2026))
- **Dual citizenship**: explicitly allowed — applicant keeps existing nationality. ([oesterreich.gv.at — Dual citizenship](https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/en/themen/menschen_aus_anderen_staaten/staatsbuergerschaft/Seite.260430))
- **Recent changes**: §58c was extended on 1 September 2020 to cover all direct descendants (previously only the victim themselves); a clarifying amendment took effect 1 May 2022. No further substantive amendments since.

## Standard naturalization — 10-year residency

- **Category**: naturalization; the default path for foreigners without ancestry/marriage/refugee shortcuts.
- **Eligibility**: ≥10 years of legal, continuous residence in Austria (≥5 years on a settlement permit/Niederlassungsbewilligung); B1 German (CEFR); pass civics test on Austrian democratic system, history, and the relevant Bundesland; clean criminal record; secure livelihood proven by ≥36 months of stable income within the last 6 years (the last 6 months must be immediately before application — calibrated against the ASVG reference rate, ≈€1,273.99/month for a single person in 2025); no unpaid taxes/fees; no current asylum proceedings; release from prior citizenship before grant (with narrow exceptions, see Dual citizenship below). ([oesterreich.gv.at — General conditions for naturalisation](https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/en/themen/menschen_aus_anderen_staaten/staatsbuergerschaft/1/Seite.260421))
- **Timeline**: 10 years residency + 12–36 months processing. Vienna applicants currently see ~12 months just for the initial MA35 appointment, then 1–2 years to decision. Total real-world from arrival to passport: ~12–13 years.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: MA35 (Vienna) — initial-appointment wait ≈1 year, decision queue 1–2 years on top. Other Bundesländer (Tirol, Vorarlberg, etc.) are materially faster. ([Vienna Briefing — MA35 reform](https://viennabriefing.substack.com/p/vienna-ma35-immigration-citizenship-reform), [The Local — Citizenship is problem child](https://www.thelocal.at/20230112/citizenship-is-problem-child-how-viennas-immigration-office-ma35-is-changing))
- **Dual citizenship**: must renounce prior citizenship; written renunciation evidence required between conditional grant and final award. ([BMEIA — Dual Citizenship](https://www.bmeia.gv.at/en/travel-stay/living-abroad/citizenship-and-union-citizenship/dual-citizenship))
- **Recent changes**: 2026 coalition program proposes raising B1 → B2 and adding a mandatory ÖIF citizenship course; no draft bill yet. ([The Local — Planned citizenship reform](https://www.thelocal.at/20260107/what-austrias-planned-citizenship-reform-could-mean-for-foreign-residents), [RIHS — Government Program 2025–2029](https://www.rihs.law/en/news/planned-changes-in-austrian-citizenship-law-2025-key-updates-and-implications/))

## Accelerated naturalization — 6 years (sustained personal integration)

- **Category**: naturalization, §11a StbG (the "vorzeitige Verleihung" entitlement after 6 years).
- **Eligibility**: 6 years legal continuous residence (≤20% time abroad) PLUS one of: (a) B2 German; (b) B1 German + ≥3 years documented voluntary work for a charitable organization, OR ≥3 years professional activity in the health/social/education sectors, OR active office in a recognized social-partner organization (e.g., trade union, chamber); OR (c) EU/EEA/Swiss citizenship and 6 years residence; OR (d) born in Austria; OR (e) recognized refugee under the 1951 Convention. All standard general conditions (livelihood, integrity, civics test, no asylum proceedings) still apply. ([oesterreich.gv.at — Vorzeitige Verleihung nach sechs Jahren](https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/de/themen/menschen_aus_anderen_staaten/staatsbuergerschaft/1/Vorzeitige-Verleihung-nach-sechs-Jahren), [asylumineurope.org — Naturalisation Austria](https://asylumineurope.org/reports/country/austria/content-international-protection/status-and-residence/naturalisation/))
- **Timeline**: 6 years residency + 12–24 months processing → ~7–8 years total.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: same MA35 queue as the 10-year path in Vienna.
- **Dual citizenship**: must renounce prior — no carve-out for the accelerated path.

## Naturalization by marriage / registered partnership

- **Category**: naturalization with reduced residency, §11a(2) StbG. Austria has the most restrictive marriage-based citizenship rule in the EU.
- **Eligibility**: ≥6 years legal continuous residence in Austria AND ≥5 years married (or registered partnership) in joint household to an Austrian citizen at time of decision. All general conditions apply (B1 German, civics, livelihood, integrity, renunciation of prior citizenship). ([oesterreich.gv.at — Allgemeine Einbürgerungsvoraussetzungen](https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/en/themen/menschen_aus_anderen_staaten/staatsbuergerschaft/1/Seite.260421))
- **Timeline**: ≥6 years residency + 1–2 years processing → ~7–8 years total.
- **Backlog**: MA35 queue applies in Vienna.
- **Dual citizenship**: must renounce prior.

## §10(6) — Extraordinary services ("Citizenship by Investment / Exception")

- **Category**: discretionary grant by the Federal Government for services already rendered (or expected to be rendered) in the special interest of the Republic — economic, scientific, cultural, athletic.
- **Eligibility**: no residency, language, or civics requirement. Requires (a) a recommendation from the relevant Land government, (b) endorsement by the Federal Minister of the Interior, and (c) unanimous Council of Ministers approval. "Investment" is not a category in the law — passive instruments (bonds, real estate, fund units) do not qualify. Acceptable patterns: active operating investments (€2M–€10M+) generating Austrian jobs / tech transfer / export uplift / R&D footprint; world-class athletic, scientific, or cultural achievement attributable to Austria. ([CitizenX — Austria CBI guide](https://citizenx.com/insights/austria-citizenship-investment/), [CitizenX — Austria citizenship by exception](https://citizenx.com/insights/austria-citizenship-exception/))
- **Timeline**: 18–36 months from first government engagement to Council of Ministers vote; no statutory deadline.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: capacity is the bottleneck — 20–30 grants per year nationally across all §10(6) categories combined; no published queue. Pre-vetting via the BMI is months-long before a formal file opens.
- **Dual citizenship**: explicitly allowed — recipient keeps existing nationality (the second large carve-out from Austria's monocitizenship rule). ([BMEIA — Dual Citizenship](https://www.bmeia.gv.at/en/travel-stay/living-abroad/citizenship-and-union-citizenship/dual-citizenship))
- **Effective status**: active but de facto closed to ordinary applicants — no published menu, no broker channel, no guarantee even at €10M+. Treat as a unicorn path, not a program.

## Refugee / subsidiary protection

- **Category**: naturalization with reduced residency for recognized refugees.
- **Eligibility**: recognized refugees qualify for the §11a 6-year accelerated path if they hold B2 German, or B1 + documented integration (volunteering, social-sector employment, social-partner office). Asylum-procedure years count toward residence. Beneficiaries of subsidiary protection have a longer path — typically 10 years, with 6 years possible under the same enhanced-integration test. ([UNHCR Austria — Recognized refugees](https://help.unhcr.org/austria/rights-and-obligations/recognized-refugees-or-beneficiaries-of-subsidiary-protection/))
- **Timeline**: 6–10 years residency + MA35 processing.
- **Dual citizenship**: renunciation required, but waived where renunciation is impossible or unreasonable (the "Unzumutbarkeit" exception of §20(2) — covers most refugee origin countries that refuse to release nationals). ([Wikipedia: Austrian nationality law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_nationality_law))

## Stateless persons

- **Category**: naturalization with statutory entitlement.
- **Eligibility — born stateless in Austria**: apply between ages 18 and 21, lawful residence in Austria ≥10 years (≥5 continuous immediately before application), B1 German, civics test, integrity. ([Statelessness Index — Austria](https://index.statelessness.eu/country/austria))
- **Eligibility — stateless adults**: same 10-year general path, but several documentary requirements may be waived where statelessness is documented.
- **Timeline**: 10 years + processing.
- **Dual citizenship**: not applicable (no prior citizenship to renounce).

## Re-acquisition of lost Austrian citizenship

- **Category**: §10(4) / §58 — preferential grant for former Austrians.
- **Eligibility**: held Austrian citizenship continuously for ≥10 years before loss, not deprived for cause; OR lost it via marriage to a foreigner (apply within 5 years of marriage dissolution); OR were a minor whose loss followed a parent's loss. Applicant must be present in Austria for the grant and must release any current foreign citizenship — UNLESS a §28 retention permit is obtained in advance. ([BMEIA — Acquisition](https://www.bmeia.gv.at/en/austrian-embassy-london/service-for-citizens/citizenship-and-union-citizenship/acquisition), [Wikipedia: Austrian nationality law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_nationality_law))
- **Timeline**: 6–18 months.
- **Dual citizenship**: not automatic — requires §28 retention permit ("special interest of the Republic" or "personal reasons worthy of special consideration", or best-interest-of-the-child for minors).

## §28 — Retention permit for outgoing Austrians

- **Category**: not a path to citizenship; the mechanism by which Austrians lawfully avoid losing citizenship when naturalizing elsewhere.
- **Eligibility**: must be granted *before* the foreign naturalization completes. Grounds: (a) special interest of the Republic; (b) personal reasons worthy of special consideration (long ties, family in Austria, study/career); (c) best interest of a minor child. Discretionary; rejection is common. ([BMEIA — Loss, revocation and renunciation](https://www.bmeia.gv.at/en/travel-stay/living-abroad/citizenship-and-union-citizenship/loss-revocation-and-renunciation))

## Military / distinguished contribution

- **Category**: subsumed under §10(6) — Austria has no separate military, sports, or arts naturalization track. Olympic athletes, Nobel laureates, and similar exceptional individuals are processed under §10(6) and benefit from the same dual-citizenship carve-out.

## Ethnic-German repatriation

- **Category**: does not apply. Austria has no Aussiedler/repatriation equivalent to Germany's BVFG. Heimatvertriebene from the post-WWII expulsions were resolved historically; no living analogue exists today.

## Comparison summary

| Path | Total time | Dual citizenship | Practical viability |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Descent at birth | At birth | Allowed | Automatic if eligible |
| §58c (Nazi descendants) | 12–18 months | Allowed | High if lineage documents exist |
| §10(6) extraordinary services | 18–36 months | Allowed | Effectively closed (20–30/yr nationally) |
| Re-acquisition (former Austrian) | 6–18 months | Conditional (§28) | Moderate if §28 granted |
| Accelerated 6-year (B2 / EEA / refugee / born-in-AT) | ~7–8 years | Renounce | Moderate |
| Marriage | ~7–8 years | Renounce | Moderate |
| Standard 10-year | ~12–13 years | Renounce | Slow but viable |
| Stateless (born in AT) | 18–21 years old + 10y residence | N/A | Niche |
| Jus soli | N/A | N/A | Does not exist |

**Ranked by speed (practical)**: §58c ≈ re-acquisition ≈ §10(6) > descent-at-birth (only if already eligible) > 6-year accelerated ≈ marriage > 10-year standard > stateless.

**Ranked by likelihood-of-success for a typical applicant**: descent-at-birth (binary — you qualify or you don't) > §58c (binary on lineage; if documents exist, success rate is very high) > 6-year accelerated for EEA/refugees > 10-year standard > marriage (the 5+6 dual clock is the trap) > re-acquisition > §10(6) (effectively closed).

**Effectively closed or near-closed**: §10(6) for ordinary applicants (no money amount guarantees a grant); jus soli (does not exist); ethnic-German repatriation (does not exist).

**Watch-items**: the 2026 ÖVP–SPÖ–NEOS reform — if enacted, B1 → B2 hike and ÖIF civics course raise the bar on every naturalization path except §58c, descent-at-birth, and §10(6). No draft bill as of May 2026.
