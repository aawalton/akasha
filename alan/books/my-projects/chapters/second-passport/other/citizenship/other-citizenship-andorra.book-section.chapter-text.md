
## Overview

Andorra has among the world's most restrictive naturalization regimes. Standard naturalization requires 20 years of legal residence, plus a demanding exam in Catalan covering language, history, geography, and civics. Dual citizenship is **strictly prohibited** — applicants must renounce all other nationalities, and acquiring a foreign citizenship later is grounds for automatic loss of Andorran nationality. The Llei Qualificada de la Nacionalitat is currently undergoing a major reform presented in May 2025, with revised continuity rules and an enhanced integration exam in effect.

## Dual Citizenship Prohibition (read this first)

Andorra does **not** permit dual citizenship under any path. Key mechanics:

- Applicants must expressly renounce every other citizenship held. Documentary proof of renunciation must be presented before Andorran nationality is granted, or completed within five years of grant.
- Where the home country prohibits formal renunciation (e.g. US citizens face the expatriation tax under IRC §877A and Department of State CLN process), Andorran authorities still require the applicant to demonstrate they are no longer "functioning as" a citizen of that state.
- Voluntary acquisition of any foreign citizenship after naturalization is statutory ground for **involuntary loss** of Andorran citizenship.
- This makes Andorra fundamentally hostile to a "second-passport" strategy that preserves the original passport. The only path that yields Andorran citizenship while retaining a US passport is having an Andorran-citizen parent at birth (dual at birth, not by naturalization) — and even that status is fragile against later positive acts of foreign allegiance.

## Citizenship Paths

### Jus sanguinis at birth (Andorran parent)

- **Eligibility**: child of at least one Andorran-citizen parent at the time of birth, regardless of place of birth.
- **Timeline**: at birth, registered through the consulate or civil registry.
- **2024–2026 changes**: the May 2025 reform contemplates a limited descent extension reaching grandchildren where the Andorran grandparent resided in the principality for ≥10 years and the applicant has spent ≥15 years on the territory; status remains pending parliamentary approval.
- **Dual citizenship**: tolerated *at birth* in practice (the child has not yet "acquired" a foreign citizenship voluntarily), but any later voluntary act of foreign allegiance can trigger loss.

### Anti-statelessness jus soli

- **Eligibility**: child born in Andorra to unknown parents (foundlings) or to two stateless parents.
- **Timeline**: at birth.
- **Rationale**: codified in Article 2 of the Qualified Law on Nationality to meet international anti-statelessness norms.

### Birth in Andorra to permanent-resident parent (conditional jus soli)

- **Eligibility**: child born in Andorra where at least one parent has 10 years of legal residence at the time of birth — automatic citizenship at birth.
- **Provisional path**: if parents have not yet reached 10 years, the child receives *provisional* Andorran nationality and must confirm at age 18 (age of majority) to retain it.
- **Timeline**: at birth or at 18.

### Standard naturalization (20-year residency)

- **Eligibility**: 20 years of legal residence in Andorra; sufficient integration; clean criminal record in Andorra and country of origin; passing the Catalan-language and Andorran culture / history / geography exam; renunciation of prior citizenships.
- **Timeline**: 20 years minimum; application processed by Govern d'Andorra.
- **2024–2026 changes**: the May 2025 reform (parliamentary process ongoing) makes the 20 years *non-consecutive* — all periods of legal residence count, provided the applicant has resided continuously for the **5 years immediately prior to application**. The reform also strengthens the integration interview and adds explicit good-conduct / criminal-record requirements both in Andorra and in the country of origin. A five-year grace window after BOPA publication is provided for completing the new Catalan culture exam.
- **Dual citizenship**: prohibited. Renunciation required.

### Reduced 10-year naturalization (Andorran-school track)

- **Eligibility**: 10 years of legal residence where the applicant attended Andorran mandatory schooling (Andorran, Spanish, or French school system inside Andorra during minor years).
- **Timeline**: 10 years residence + application after attaining majority.
- **Rationale**: rewards integration through the local education systems.
- **Dual citizenship**: prohibited. Renunciation required.

### Marriage to an Andorran citizen

- **Eligibility**: marriage to an Andorran citizen + **3 years of marriage** + **3 years of legal residence** in Andorra (note: residence and marriage windows can overlap; the prevailing reading is 3 years married while resident in Andorra). Some sources state simply "3 years of residence as the spouse of an Andorran." Verify with Govern d'Andorra at application time.
- **Timeline**: ~3 years post-marriage.
- **Integration**: still requires proof of integration into Andorran society, Catalan language proficiency, and renunciation of prior citizenships.
- **Verification**: authorities may inspect for legitimacy of the marital relationship.
- **Dual citizenship**: prohibited. Renunciation required.

### Distinguished service (exceptional naturalization)

- **Eligibility**: extraordinary services rendered to Andorra in fields such as science, culture, sport, or diplomacy.
- **Mechanism**: by parliamentary act (Llei) of the Consell General — extremely rare, used historically only a handful of times.
- **Timeline**: case-by-case, opaque.
- **Dual citizenship**: prohibited in principle, though parliamentary acts have on rare occasion accommodated edge cases.

## Loss and re-acquisition

- **Voluntary loss**: by formal renunciation declared to the civil registry.
- **Involuntary loss**: voluntary acquisition of any foreign citizenship; failure to complete renunciation of prior citizenships within five years of acquiring Andorran nationality; conduct gravely prejudicial to Andorra (rare).
- **Children of Andorrans born abroad**: must maintain a recognized link with Andorra (registration, periodic residence, or affirmative confirmation at majority) to avoid eventual loss of provisional status.
- **Re-acquisition**: possible by petition for former citizens who lost nationality through acquisition of another, conditional on giving up the intervening citizenship and re-establishing residence; case-by-case, no fast track.

## Practical assessment

For a US citizen unwilling to renounce US citizenship, **Andorra citizenship is not a viable goal**. Andorra's value proposition is residency and tax — not the second passport. The naturalization tracks are designed to gate a very small population (descent, school-track integration, or long-term resident commitment), and the renunciation rule eliminates the standard HNW second-passport playbook entirely.
