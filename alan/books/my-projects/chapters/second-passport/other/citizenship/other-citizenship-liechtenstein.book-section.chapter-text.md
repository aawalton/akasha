
Liechtenstein has roughly 40,000 citizens and one of the most restrictive naturalization regimes in Europe — alongside Switzerland. Naturalization paths are governed by the Citizenship Act (Bürgerrechtsgesetz). Dual citizenship for naturalized foreigners was rejected in a 2020 national referendum and remains prohibited; renunciation of prior nationality is required for all naturalization paths. Communal admission (Gemeindebürgerrecht) is a separate but mandatory prerequisite for ordinary national citizenship.

## Citizenship by descent and birth

### Jus sanguinis at birth

A child receives Liechtenstein citizenship automatically at birth if at least one parent is a Liechtensteiner at the time of birth and the parents are married. For unmarried parents, citizenship passes automatically only through the Liechtensteiner mother; transmission through an unmarried Liechtensteiner father requires a paternity recognition. Place of birth is irrelevant.

### Anti-statelessness jus soli

A child born in Liechtenstein who would otherwise be stateless acquires Liechtenstein citizenship at birth. There is no general jus soli — birth on the territory to foreign parents does not by itself confer citizenship.

## Ordinary naturalization

### Standard naturalization via communal vote (Bürgerabstimmung)

The default adult path. Requires 10 years of ordinary residence in Liechtenstein, B1 oral and A2 written German (CEFR), integration into Liechtenstein society, demonstrated knowledge of Liechtenstein history/legal order, financial self-sufficiency (no welfare reliance), clean record, and renunciation of prior nationality. The applicant must first be admitted to a municipal citizenship (Gemeindebürgerrecht) by popular vote of the eligible voters in their commune — the Bürgerabstimmung. National citizenship is conferred by the Diet only after the communal vote succeeds. In practice the communal vote almost never approves applicants who have lived in the commune for fewer than 30 years, and there is no appeal; this path is widely described as effectively closed for ordinary applicants.

### Facilitated naturalization via 30 years residence

Since 2000, a foreigner with 30 years of ordinary residence in Liechtenstein (years lived in Liechtenstein before age 20 count double, capping the worst-case wait at effectively 15 years for those raised there) plus continuous residence in Liechtenstein for the last 5 years may apply for citizenship via an administrative procedure — no communal vote. Same B1 oral / A2 written German, integration, financial self-sufficiency, clean record, and renunciation requirements apply. This is the realistic "long-residence" route created specifically to bypass the communal-vote bottleneck.

## Facilitated naturalization (other)

### Marriage to a Liechtensteiner

A spouse of a Liechtensteiner may naturalize via facilitated procedure (no communal vote) when all four conditions are met: at least 5 years of marriage, at least 5 years of ordinary Liechtenstein residence, at least 10 years' total relationship-to-Liechtenstein (residence after marriage counts double, so 5 years of married-residence satisfies it), and the marriage is genuine and ongoing. B1/A2 German, integration, and renunciation still required. Sham-marriage scrutiny is active. This is by far the most accessible adult path.

### Born and raised in Liechtenstein

A foreigner born in Liechtenstein who has lived there continuously through childhood may naturalize via a facilitated procedure on reaching adulthood, on the same renunciation/language/integration terms.

### Distinguished service

The Diet may grant honorary citizenship for outstanding service to Liechtenstein. Extremely rare, used roughly a few times per generation.

## Re-acquisition and restoration

A former Liechtensteiner who lost citizenship (e.g., by acquiring another nationality and not protecting Liechtenstein citizenship at the time) may apply to be readmitted; conditions are case-by-case and typically require residence and the standard integration profile. There is no automatic restoration right for descendants of Liechtensteiners who lost citizenship generations ago.

## Communal admission (Gemeindebürgerrecht)

Liechtenstein citizenship is two-tier: every Liechtensteiner is first a citizen of a commune, and only through that of the country. For the ordinary (10-year) path the commune admits via popular vote (Bürgerabstimmung) before the State Diet grants national citizenship. For facilitated paths (30-year, marriage, born-and-raised, distinguished service) the government grants both communal and national citizenship administratively, with no commune-level vote. The 2020 referendum to relax these rules failed.

## Dual citizenship policy

Naturalizing foreigners must renounce their previous nationality — no exceptions for ordinary or facilitated paths. The two practical exceptions live elsewhere: (1) jus-sanguinis Liechtensteiners by birth may acquire a second nationality without losing Liechtenstein citizenship, and (2) a child born to a Liechtensteiner parent and a foreign parent may inherit both nationalities at birth. The 2020 popular vote rejecting dual citizenship for naturalized applicants makes any near-term liberalization unlikely.

## Recent changes (2024–2026)

No structural changes to the Citizenship Act in this window. The B1 oral / A2 written German standard is the post-2008 regime and remains in force. The 2020 referendum result continues to block dual-citizenship reform. No investment-based or "golden passport" route exists or is under public discussion; commercial "citizenship-by-investment" listings for Liechtenstein are misleading.
