
# Cypriot Citizenship Paths (May 2026)

Cyprus is a small EU member (since 2004) with a jus-sanguinis regime. Its Citizenship-by-Investment programme was shut down in November 2020 after the Al Jazeera "Cyprus Papers" exposé and EU infringement proceedings; the final Cabinet "exceptional economic interest" loophole was closed by parliament on 4 December 2025, and the Commission formally closed its infringement case on 11 March 2026. Diaspora descent (jus sanguinis with no generation cap) remains the cheap and reliable path; northern Cyprus (TRNC) is not recognized, but ethnic Turkish Cypriots with pre-1974 roots may register with the Republic and obtain a full EU passport. Dual citizenship is unconditionally permitted.

## Birth on Territory (jus soli) — Effectively Closed

- **Basis**: §109(1) Civil Registry Law 141(I)/2002 — citizenship at birth in Cyprus only if at least one parent is a Cypriot citizen (i.e., still descent-driven). No unconditional jus soli.
- **Stateless-child protection**: None. Cyprus and Romania are the only two EU states with no statutory protection for stateless children born on territory. UN ECOSOC formally called on Cyprus to fix this gap in 2024; no legislation has followed.
- **Practical timeline**: N/A — birthplace alone confers nothing.

## Descent — Cypriot Parent (jus sanguinis)

- **Basis**: §109 Civil Registry Law. Form **M121** ("consular birth certificate") for children born abroad after 16 Aug 1960 to at least one Cypriot citizen parent.
- **Eligibility tripwires by birth date**:
  - Born **before 16 Aug 1960** (independence): eligible if **father** was of Cypriot origin (form **M72**, or **M71** if UK/colonies citizen).
  - Born **16 Aug 1960 – 10 Jun 1999**: eligible only if **father** was a Cypriot citizen at birth (mother-line transmission did not exist until 1999).
  - Born **on or after 11 Jun 1999**: either parent transmits.
- **Other requirements**: parent's Cypriot citizenship documented; clean record; no residency, no language test.
- **Timeline / processing**: register at the Cypriot consulate; typically **6–18 months** to issue the M121 birth certificate; passport follows immediately.
- **Fees**: €20 government fee on M121.

## Descent — Cypriot Origin (Section 110 / Category B "ancestry" path)

- **Basis**: §110 Civil Registry Law — registration for adults of Cypriot origin who fall outside §109 (e.g., grandparent or great-grandparent line, or born before 1960 via female line, or descendants of the 1914–1960 British-citizenship cohort).
- **Forms**: **M123** (born on/after 16 Aug 1960 to non-citizen parents but with documented Cypriot lineage, including descent from a person made British under the 1914–1943 Annexation Orders or born in Cyprus 5 Nov 1914 – 16 Aug 1960); **M124** (born before 16 Aug 1960 of Cypriot origin not covered by M71/M72).
- **Eligibility**: documented descent from a person of Cypriot origin; loyalty declaration to the Republic; clean criminal record. **No residency requirement, no language test, no generation cap** in practice — great-grandparent claims are routinely approved.
- **Timeline**: typically **1–3 years** end-to-end (decision plus passport).
- **Notes**: this is the workhorse diaspora path — used heavily by the UK, US, Australia, South Africa, and Greek communities.

## Naturalization by Residency — Standard 7-Year Track

- **Basis**: §111 Civil Registry Law, form **M127**.
- **Eligibility**:
  - **7 years of legal residence within the prior 10 years**, plus **12 months continuous** immediately before application (the trailing year permits up to 90 days absence).
  - "Good character"; no serious criminal record.
  - Suitable housing and stable, sufficient financial means.
  - Intent to continue residing in Cyprus.
  - Greek language **B1** (CEFR) per the 30 Nov 2023 amendment (no language test pre-2023 for the standard track).
  - Loyalty oath; full background check.
- **Timeline to citizenship**: **8 years total** (7 + 1) from arrival.
- **Backlog (May 2026)**: official estimate **2–3 years** for standard applications; historically 3–4 years.
- **Cost**: €500 application fee + stamps.

## Naturalization by Residency — Fast-Track Highly Skilled (4 or 5 years)

- **Basis**: §111A Civil Registry Law as amended 30 Nov 2023 (effective March 2024).
- **Eligibility**:
  - Holding a "highly skilled worker" employment permit (employed by a Cyprus-registered foreign-interest / international-business company; minimum gross salary ~€2,500/month).
  - **4 years** lawful residence + Greek **B1** certificate, OR **5 years** + Greek **A2** certificate.
  - Clean record, stable income, suitable housing.
- **Timeline**: **4 or 5 years** to apply; **statutory 8-month processing cap** (the only track with a guaranteed clock).
- **Backlog**: applications since 2024; early decisions issuing within the 8-month window per Ministry of Interior.

## Naturalization by Marriage / Civil Partnership

- **Basis**: §110A Civil Registry Law, form **M125**.
- **Eligibility**:
  - **3 years of marriage** to a Cypriot citizen, AND
  - **2 years of cohabitation in Cyprus** during the marriage (≤90 days absence in the year before application), AND
  - Harmonious-living declaration co-signed by the Cypriot spouse.
  - Clean criminal record; no language test for the spouse track.
- **Special abroad clause**: couples living abroad may apply after 3 years of marriage with a written justification; if abroad ≥5 years and a child of the marriage exists, no justification needed.
- **Timeline**: file at year 3; decision typically **1–2 years**. Total ~**4–5 years** from marriage.
- **Cost**: €300 + 2× €8.54 stamps.

## Cypriot Origin — UK / Commonwealth / Irish Citizens (1-Year Registration)

- **Basis**: §110(2) Civil Registry Law — special legacy provision for UK, Irish, and Commonwealth citizens of Cypriot descent.
- **Eligibility**: documented Cypriot descent + **12 months legal residence in Cyprus** + loyalty declaration. No language test.
- **Timeline**: 1 year residence + ~1–2 years processing = ~**2–3 years total**. Cheaper and shorter than naturalization.

## Refugees and Recognized Stateless Persons

- **Basis**: §111 Civil Registry Law with refugee-specific computation (asylum guidance per Ministry of Interior).
- **Eligibility**: **8 years total legal residence** counted **from the date of refugee/stateless-status recognition** (not from arrival or asylum-seeker status — those years are excluded). Final 12 months continuous; B1 Greek; good character; resources.
- **Timeline**: **~8+ years** from recognition; in practice 10+ years from arrival because asylum-seeker years don't count.
- **Backlog**: 2–3 years processing on top of the residency clock; refugee files are slow.

## Honorary / Exceptional Service Citizenship

- **Basis**: §111A(1) Civil Registry Law (the *non-investor* honorary clause retained after the December 2025 amendment removed the economic-interest sub-clause).
- **Eligibility**: rendered "high service" to the Republic — Olympic medals for Cyprus, distinguished cultural / scientific contribution, humanitarian acts. Discretionary, terms set by Council of Ministers per case.
- **Timeline**: granted by Cabinet decision on case-by-case basis; no published clock. Extremely rare — typically a handful per year.
- **Notes**: this is the only remaining discretionary track. The parallel §111A economic-interest clause, which Brussels treated as the last CBI loophole, was repealed 4 Dec 2025.

## Re-acquisition of Lost Citizenship

- **Basis**: §113 Civil Registry Law — former citizens who renounced (or lost) Cypriot citizenship may apply to be re-registered.
- **Eligibility**: prior Cypriot citizenship documented; clean record; no continuing impediment (e.g., security grounds). No new residency requirement.
- **Timeline**: typically 6–18 months by Cabinet decision.
- **Notes**: rarely invoked because Cyprus permits dual citizenship, so renunciations are uncommon.

## Children of Cypriot Citizens Born After Naturalization

- **Basis**: §109 — minor children of a Cypriot citizen acquire citizenship automatically at birth (worldwide) or by registration if the parent naturalizes after the child is born (form M126; both parents must consent if both have parental authority).
- **Timeline**: immediate to ~6 months by registration.

## Turkish Cypriot Path (Northern Cyprus Residents)

- **Basis**: Republic of Cyprus considers all persons descended from pre-1974 inhabitants of the entire island to be Cypriot citizens; routes via §109/§110 still apply.
- **Eligibility**: Turkish Cypriots and their descendants who can document residence / lineage in the territory of the Republic at or before independence may register with the Republic and receive a full Cypriot (EU) passport — the TRNC passport itself is recognized only by Turkey and unusable in the EU.
- **Timeline**: typically 12–24 months once lineage is documented.
- **Notes**: politically sensitive; the Republic actively issues passports to Turkish Cypriots as part of its position that the island is one state.

## Citizenship by Investment — Closed

_See [../residency/cyprus.md](../residency/cyprus.book-chapter.md) for the closed Cyprus CBI programme (terminated 1 Nov 2020; final §111A Cabinet-discretion loophole repealed 4 Dec 2025; EU infringement case closed 11 Mar 2026) and the surviving €300k real-estate Permanent Residency Permit (PR-only; does not shortcut the 8-year naturalization clock)._

## Comparison Summary

Ranked by speed for a typical applicant (May 2026):

1. **Descent via Cypriot parent (M121)** — ~6–18 months. Effectively automatic if documentation is in order. Best path if eligible.
2. **Descent via Cypriot origin / ancestry (M123/M124)** — ~1–3 years. No residency or language; grandparent/great-grandparent claims accepted. Workhorse diaspora path.
3. **Marriage to a Cypriot citizen** — ~4–5 years from wedding (3 years marriage + 2 years cohabitation + ~1–2 years processing).
4. **UK/Commonwealth/Irish + Cypriot origin (§110(2))** — ~2–3 years (1 year residence + processing).
5. **Fast-track highly skilled + Greek B1** — **4 years residence + 8 months processing** ≈ ~4.7 years; with A2, ~5.7 years. Only track with a statutory processing deadline.
6. **Standard naturalization** — 7 years residence + 1 year continuous + 2–3 years backlog ≈ **~10–11 years** in practice.
7. **Refugee** — 8+ years from recognition + backlog ≈ 10+ years.
8. **Honorary / exceptional** — case-by-case, not a realistic plan.

(The closed CBI programme and the surviving €300k real-estate PR are described in [../residency/cyprus.md](../residency/cyprus.book-chapter.md); neither offers a current investment path to a Cyprus passport.)

Likelihood: descent paths (any flavor) are near-certain with documentation; naturalization tracks gated by Greek B1 and a slow Ministry of Interior. Honorary is functionally inaccessible.

**Dual citizenship**: unconditionally permitted under Cypriot law — no renunciation, no notification, no cap on additional nationalities held.
