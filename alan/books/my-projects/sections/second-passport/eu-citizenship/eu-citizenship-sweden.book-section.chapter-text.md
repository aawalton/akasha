
# Sweden

Sweden's citizenship regime is governed by the Citizenship Act (Lag om svenskt medborgarskap 2001:82, in force since 1 July 2001) and administered by Migrationsverket. Dual citizenship has been fully allowed since 2001 with no renunciation requirement. The Kristersson coalition (M+SD+KD+L, since Oct 2022) has executed a sweeping tightening: Parliament passed **Skärpta krav för svenskt medborgarskap** on 29 April 2026, with most provisions coming into force on **6 June 2026** — residency raised from 5 to 8 years, mandatory civics + language tests (medborgarskapsprov), "respectable lifestyle" (hederligt levnadssätt) expanded to overseas conduct, and a self-support floor of 3 income base amounts (~250,200 SEK/yr in 2026). As of **May 2026** the old 5-year rules technically still apply, but Migrationsverket assesses cases under whichever law is in force at the *decision* date — so any application not decided before 6 June 2026 will be judged under the new regime. The Nordic-citizen notification (anmälan) at 2 years remains the fastest practical route.

## Birth in Sweden (jus soli)

- **Category:** Birth on territory.
- **Status:** None. Sweden does not grant citizenship by birthplace. Anti-statelessness handled via the stateless-child notification path below.

## Descent (jus sanguinis)

- **Category:** Birth by descent — automatic.
- **Eligibility (born on/after 1 April 2015):** At least one parent is a Swedish citizen at the time of birth (or a deceased parent was Swedish at time of death). Applies regardless of birthplace, marital status, or parental gender.
- **Eligibility (born before 1 April 2015):** Automatic Swedish citizenship if (a) Swedish mother, OR (b) Swedish father AND parents married, OR (c) Swedish father AND child born in Sweden. Children born abroad to an unmarried Swedish father did *not* acquire citizenship automatically — must be claimed by paternal notification (see below).
- **Timeline:** Automatic at birth.
- **Sufficient-connection rule:** Swedes born and raised abroad lose citizenship at age 22 unless they have lived in Sweden, visited regularly, or applied to retain. The 2026 reform introduces a new declaratory recovery path for those who lost citizenship this way (see Re-acquisition).

## Paternal notification for pre-2015 births abroad

- **Category:** Declaration (anmälan).
- **Eligibility:** Father is Swedish citizen; child born outside marriage and outside Sweden before 1 April 2015. Submitted by father (or by adult child themselves before age 18, in some cases).
- **Timeline:** Months of processing; no residence requirement.
- **Notes:** This is one of the few anmälan routes preserved in the 6 June 2026 cutover.

## Naturalization by residency (standard application — pre-reform, in force May 2026)

- **Category:** Standard naturalization.
- **Eligibility (rules currently in force, applied if decision issued before 6 June 2026):**
  - **Residence:** 5 years continuous habitual residence (permanent residence permit / PUT or equivalent typically required).
  - **Age:** 18+.
  - **Identity:** Reliably established (the long-standing identity-documentation bottleneck for stateless / undocumented applicants).
  - **Conduct (hederligt levnadssätt):** Orderly life — no serious unpaid debts (Kronofogden), no recent crimes; waiting periods (karenstider) scale with offense severity.
  - **No language or civics test.**
  - **No formal self-support requirement.**
- **Timeline to citizenship:** 5 years residence + processing.
- **Backlog (May 2026):** Average decided-case processing rose to **1,021 days (~34 months)** in Jan–Feb 2026; backlog ~100,000+ pending; Migrationsverket targets 55,000 decisions in 2026 against ~60,000 new applications, so backlog grows ~5,000/yr. Citizenship team expanded from 200 to 300 staff. Pre-2022 cases prioritized in strict date order.
- **Critical timing risk:** Because decisions follow the law in force at decision date, an applicant who filed in 2023–2025 under the 5-year rule but whose case is not decided by 5 June 2026 is reassessed under the new 8-year regime. The Migrationsverket announcement of 6 May 2026 confirms this.

## Naturalization by residency (standard application — post-reform, from 6 June 2026)

- **Category:** Standard naturalization under Skärpta krav för svenskt medborgarskap.
- **Eligibility (from 6 June 2026):**
  - **Residence:** **8 years** habitual residence (up from 5).
  - **Conduct:** Hederligt levnadssätt — expanded to consider behavior **both in Sweden and abroad** (foreign criminal records, debts, conduct now in scope). Future conduct expectation retained.
  - **Self-support:** Own income at minimum **3 income base amounts/year** (250,200 SEK in 2026 = ~20,850 SEK/month gross). Welfare-dependent applicants disqualified.
  - **Language (svenska):** Demonstrated functional Swedish at the citizenship-test level via the new medborgarskapsprov reading/listening component. Implementation phased: language component live by **1 October 2027** at the latest (earlier if government orders).
  - **Civics (samhällskunskap):** Pass UHR-administered medborgarskapsprov civics component. First test sittings begin **August 2026**.
  - **Age band for tests:** 18–67 (some sources cite 16–66). Exemptions for old-age pensioners, permanent disability, certain pension recipients, full-time secondary/upper-secondary students, and other personal reasons.
- **Timeline to citizenship:** 8 years residence + processing.
- **Status (May 2026):** Enacted (Parliament 29 Apr 2026), in force 6 June 2026. Civics test starts Aug 2026; language test by Oct 2027.

## Naturalization by residency — reduced-residence categories

- **Category:** Naturalization application with reduced residence.
- **Eligibility (in force May 2026; reform may further tighten — final post-reform figures: 5 yrs for stateless adults, 7 yrs for refugees per the government's 2025 inquiry recommendations):**
  - **Stateless adults:** 4 years (pre-reform). Government has signaled 5 years post-reform.
  - **Refugees (recognized status):** 4 years (pre-reform). Post-reform: 7 years.
  - **Nordic citizens applying (not via notification):** 2 years habitual residence; same other standard criteria. Confirmed unchanged by reform.
- **Timeline:** 2–4 years residence + processing.
- **Notes:** All standard conduct/self-support/test criteria still apply post-reform.

## Naturalization by marriage / cohabitation (3+2 rule)

- **Category:** Reduced-residence application.
- **Eligibility:**
  - Spouse, registered partner, or sambo (cohabitant) of a Swedish citizen.
  - **3 years habitual residence in Sweden** AND **2 years cohabitation** with the Swedish partner.
  - Swedish partner must have held citizenship for at least 2 years if they were previously foreign/stateless.
  - "Special integration into Swedish society" — Migrationsverket weighs marriage duration, Swedish-language ability, and self-support.
- **Special exception:** Couples who have lived together abroad for **10+ years** with a Swedish partner, and the applicant resides in a country other than their country of citizenship, can naturalize with **no Swedish residence requirement**. Rare but useful for diaspora cases.
- **Timeline:** 3 years + processing (~5–6 years end-to-end given backlog).
- **Post-reform:** Marriage path retained but the standard conduct/self-support/test requirements layer on top from 6 June 2026.

## Notification (anmälan) — Nordic citizens (fastest practical path)

- **Category:** Declaration, not application.
- **Eligibility:** Citizen of Denmark, Finland, Iceland, or Norway; 18+; **2 years habitual residence in Sweden**; orderly conduct (no significant crime/debt).
- **Timeline:** ~2 years residence + a few months processing.
- **Preserved by reform:** From 6 June 2026, the Nordic adult notification path explicitly survives — one of only a handful of anmälan routes retained.
- **Notes:** This is the unambiguous fastest naturalization route for anyone with eligible Nordic citizenship. No language test, no civics test, no self-support floor, no 8-year wait.

## Notification — stateless children born in Sweden

- **Category:** Declaration by parent/guardian.
- **Eligibility:** Child stateless since birth, born in Sweden, has lived in Sweden for at least 3 years (2 years for stateless children specifically). Application before age 18 (some categories before 21).
- **Timeline:** Months of processing.
- **Preserved by reform:** Retained from 6 June 2026.

## Notification — young adults aged 18–21 born/raised in Sweden

- **Category:** Declaration.
- **Eligibility:** Born in Sweden and habitually resident since birth, OR habitually resident in Sweden for at least 5 years and currently between ages 18–21.
- **Timeline:** Months of processing.
- **Preserved by reform:** Retained from 6 June 2026.

## Re-acquisition of former Swedish citizenship

- **Category:** Application or declaration depending on circumstance.
- **Eligibility (pre-reform notification, in force May 2026):** Former Swedish citizen who lost citizenship; resided in Sweden as a Swede until age 18; currently habitually resident in Sweden for at least 2 years immediately before notification. Or via standard naturalization application from abroad.
- **Eligibility (new from 6 June 2026):** Persons who lost citizenship at age 22 under the foreign-born sufficient-connection rule may apply to regain it if Migrationsverket finds that the loss had **disproportionate consequences** for the applicant or family members in Sweden or another EU Member State. Discretionary.
- **Timeline:** Months to a year.
- **Notes:** No general ancestry/descent path for grandchildren etc. — Sweden does not recognize jure sanguinis claims beyond one generation.

## Investment / CBI

- **Status:** None. Sweden has no citizenship-by-investment program, no fast-track investor naturalization, and no proposals to create one. For residency-program details, see [../residency/sweden.md](../residency/sweden.book-chapter.md).

## Service-based

- **Status:** None. No military or public-service shortcut to citizenship.

## Distinguished contribution / discretionary

- **Category:** Government-granted naturalization.
- **Eligibility:** Migrationsverket / government may waive criteria for "particular reasons" (e.g., applicant of significant national interest, athletes, Nobel-level contribution). Rare and discretionary; not a reliable path.

## Citizenship revocation — proposed constitutional amendments (status as of May 2026)

- **Status (May 2026):** **Not yet law, under constitutional inquiry process.** Sweden's current Constitution (Regeringsformen / Instrument of Government) **prohibits revocation of citizenship for Swedish-born citizens** and bars stripping naturalized citizens in ways that produce statelessness. The Kristersson government's revocation agenda requires constitutional change, which requires Parliament to pass the amendment **twice with a general election in between**.
- **Inquiry commissioned:** May 2025 — examining revocation for (a) citizenship obtained by fraud/bribery, (b) serious crimes against the State (espionage, treason), and (c) ICC-jurisdiction crimes; separately probing whether crimes that "seriously threaten the vital interests of the State" can also trigger revocation. Inquiry report submitted late 2025.
- **December 2025 proposal:** Government tabled draft constitutional amendments aimed at stripping organized-crime leaders and serious offenders of citizenship where they hold dual nationality (statelessness bar preserved).
- **Stakeholder review (remiss):** Open until **1 June 2026** — i.e., still active as of May 2026.
- **Next steps:** Must be passed by Parliament once before the next Riksdag election (scheduled Sep 2026), and a second time by the new Parliament after the election. Earliest possible enactment: late 2026–2027. Until then, no naturalized Swede can have citizenship revoked except via existing narrow fraud-based grounds.

## Comparison summary

Ranked by total elapsed time to citizenship (fastest first), with likelihood of success for a typical applicant:

1. **Descent (jus sanguinis) — automatic at birth.** Highest likelihood if Swedish parent post-2015 (or under pre-2015 rules + paternal notification). Zero residence.
2. **Re-acquisition for diaspora age-22 loss (new from 6 June 2026).** Months; discretionary "disproportionate consequences" test — useful for Swedish-American / Swedish-Canadian families.
3. **Nordic-citizen notification (anmälan) — 2 yrs residence.** ~2.5 years total. Fastest naturalization route; preserved by the 2026 reform. High likelihood for DK/FI/IS/NO citizens.
4. **Notification for 18–21-year-olds born/raised in Sweden.** Months at filing; assumes the prior residence already accrued.
5. **Marriage / cohabitation (3+2 rule).** ~5–6 years end-to-end given backlog. Reform adds tests and self-support from 6 June 2026.
6. **Stateless adult application — 5 yrs (post-reform).** ~5 yrs + ~2–3 yrs processing.
7. **Refugee application — 7 yrs (post-reform).** ~7 yrs + ~2–3 yrs processing.
8. **Standard naturalization — 8 yrs (post-reform, from 6 June 2026).** ~10–11 years end-to-end with current backlog. Reality for almost all non-Nordic applicants applying from 2026 onward.
9. **Standard naturalization — 5 yrs (pre-reform, decisions before 6 June 2026 only).** ~5 yrs + ~34 mo backlog = ~7.5 yrs. Window closing fast — applications still in the queue past 5 June 2026 flip to the 8-yr regime.
10. **Discretionary / distinguished contribution.** Rare, government-discretion only.
11. **Investment / service / general ancestry beyond one generation.** None exist.
