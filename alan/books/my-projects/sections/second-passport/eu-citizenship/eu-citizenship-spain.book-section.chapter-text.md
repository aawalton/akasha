
# Spain — Paths to Citizenship (May 2026)

Spain's regime is jus-sanguinis-dominant with limited jus soli (statelessness only). Three structural traits dominate: a 2-year fast track for Ibero-American / Andorran / Filipino / Equatorial Guinean / Portuguese / Sephardic-by-history applicants (vs 10 years standard); asymmetric dual citizenship — freely allowed with those same countries plus France (treaty), formally forbidden everywhere else (renunciation required, though many origin countries ignore the declaration); and the **Democratic Memory Law (Law 20/2022)** as the single most-used current path, whose application window closed definitively **22 October 2025** (~1.2M consular filings, backlog projected to 2028). The **Sephardic path (Law 12/2015)** closed 1 October 2019 with pipeline cases continuing. For residency programs (including the terminated Golden Visa, Digital Nomad Visa, and Non-Lucrative Visa), see [../residency/spain.md](../residency/spain.book-chapter.md).

## Path 1 — Jus Sanguinis (Descent, Spaniard parent)

- **Category:** Automatic / declarative (Civil Code Art. 17.1.a)
- **Eligibility:** At least one parent Spanish at child's birth. No residency, no language, no exams.
- **Timeline:** Immediate at birth; registration at Civil Registry / consulate.
- **Backlog:** Consular registration weeks-to-months; severely strained in Latin America due to Democratic Memory Law surge.
- **Dual citizenship:** Permitted by birth (Spaniards "of origin" never lose nationality by acquiring another).

## Path 2 — Jus Soli (Birth on Territory, Limited)

- **Category:** Conditional jus soli (Civil Code Art. 17.1.b–d)
- **Eligibility:** Born in Spain AND (a) at least one parent also born in Spain; OR (b) both parents stateless or parents' law confers no nationality on child (statelessness prevention); OR (c) unknown parentage of child found in Spain.
- **Timeline:** Immediate / by declaration.
- **Backlog:** Minor — administrative only.
- **Dual citizenship:** Acquired by origin, no renunciation.

## Path 3 — Option for Children Born in Spain (1-Year Residency Track)

- **Category:** Option (Civil Code Art. 22.2.a)
- **Eligibility:** Born in Spain to foreign parents, 1 year legal residency. No DELE/CCSE for minors under 14.
- **Timeline:** Apply at age 1; resolution ~12–18 months.
- **Backlog:** Generally fast under the Ministry of Justice digital platform.
- **Dual citizenship:** Renunciation required at age of majority unless from privileged country.

## Path 4 — Naturalization by Residency (Standard, 10 years)

- **Category:** Residency (Civil Code Art. 22.1)
- **Eligibility:** 10 years legal, continuous, immediately prior residency; DELE A2 (Instituto Cervantes, ~€138; exempt for nationals of Spanish-speaking countries); CCSE civics/culture exam (~€85, 25 multiple-choice, 4-year validity); clean criminal record (Spain + origin country); **"buena conducta cívica"** (good civic conduct, discretionary assessment); proof of integration (income, ties, no prolonged absences >6 mo).
- **Timeline:** 10 years residency + 1–3 years application processing.
- **Backlog (May 2026):** Ministry of Justice digital platform (launched 2024–2025) cuts online resolutions to ~5–6 months in best cases; offline / complex files still 18–24 mo. 2025 saw 295,107 new residency applications — historic high.
- **Dual citizenship:** **Renunciation of prior nationality required** before Spanish notary at oath ceremony (jura). Origin country may or may not recognize the declaration (US, UK, Germany ignore it in practice — de facto dual results).

## Path 5 — Naturalization by Residency (2-Year Fast Track)

- **Category:** Residency, reduced (Civil Code Art. 22.1)
- **Eligibility:** As Path 4 but only **2 years legal residency** if applicant is a national of: any Ibero-American country (Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, Cuba, Dominican Republic, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Mexico, Nicaragua, Panama, Paraguay, Peru, Puerto Rico, Uruguay, Venezuela), **Andorra, Philippines, Equatorial Guinea, or Portugal**; OR Sephardic Jew (legacy path, see Path 9). DELE A2 typically waived for native Spanish speakers; CCSE still required.
- **Timeline:** 2 years residency + 6–24 months processing.
- **Backlog:** Same digital queue as Path 4.
- **Dual citizenship:** **Permitted** — no renunciation required for the listed countries. Cornerstone of Spain's asymmetric policy.

## Path 6 — Naturalization by Residency (1-Year Reduced Track)

- **Category:** Residency, special (Civil Code Art. 22.2)
- **Eligibility:** 1 year legal residency if: (a) born in Spanish territory; (b) failed to exercise jus optionis on time; (c) under legal guardianship of a Spanish citizen/institution for 2+ years; (d) **married to a Spaniard for ≥1 year and not legally/de facto separated, with shared registered residence (empadronamiento)**; (e) widow(er) of a Spaniard, married at time of death; (f) descendant born outside Spain of a Spaniard-by-origin parent/grandparent who lost nationality. DELE A2 + CCSE required (waivers for native speakers).
- **Timeline:** 1 year qualifying status + 1–2 years processing.
- **Backlog:** Same digital queue.
- **Dual citizenship:** Renunciation required unless privileged country (Path 5 list).

## Path 7 — Citizenship by Marriage (Subset of Path 6)

- **Category:** Residency by marriage (Civil Code Art. 22.2.d)
- **Eligibility:** Married to Spanish citizen for 1+ year, **and** 1 year legal residency in Spain after the residence permit issues, **and** marriage registered with the Spanish Civil Registry, **and** joint empadronamiento. Civil unions (pareja de hecho) do NOT qualify — must be formal marriage.
- **Timeline:** ~2–3 years total (residence permit → 1 year cohabitation → application → ~12 mo processing).
- **Backlog:** Generally faster than standard residency; suspicion-of-convenience interviews common.
- **Dual citizenship:** Renunciation required unless privileged country.

## Path 8 — Democratic Memory Law (Law 20/2022, "Ley de Nietos")

- **Category:** Special / declarative (Law 20/2022, in force 21 Oct 2022)
- **Eligibility:** Three sub-tracks: **(1)** persons born outside Spain to a Spanish-origin father/mother/grandfather/grandmother who lost or renounced nationality due to exile (Civil War / Franco regime, 1936–1975); **(2)** sons/daughters born abroad of Spanish women who lost nationality by marrying a foreigner pre-1978; **(3)** adult children of those who acquired Spanish nationality under the 2007 Historical Memory Law. **No residency, no DELE, no CCSE, no renunciation.**
- **Timeline:** Application + 1–3 years processing (consular bottleneck severe).
- **Backlog (May 2026):** Application window **closed 22 October 2025** (extended once by Royal Decree-Law 6/2024 from the original Oct 2024 deadline; no further extensions). ~1.2M applications filed at consulates; another ~1.3M still on appointment waitlists when window closed (status of those filings disputed — some consulates accepted, some rejected). Ministry of Foreign Affairs projects processing through **2028**. Pipeline cases continue to be adjudicated.
- **Dual citizenship:** Fully permitted (acquired as Spaniard "of origin"; no renunciation).

## Path 9 — Sephardic Jewish Origin (Law 12/2015, CLOSED)

- **Category:** Special / historical (Law 12/2015)
- **Eligibility (historical):** Documented Sephardic ancestry (Federation of Jewish Communities of Spain certificate, surname/family/cultural evidence) + special connection to Spain + DELE A2 + CCSE. No residency required.
- **Status (May 2026):** **CLOSED for new applications since 1 October 2019.** ~132,226 applications filed; pipeline cases continue under multiple deadline extensions for documentation cures (final extensions exhausted 2021–2022; residual humanitarian exceptions remain via the open-ended provision in the law). Sephardic applicants now route via **Carta de Naturaleza** (Path 13) on a discretionary basis.
- **Dual citizenship:** Permitted (no renunciation in the original law).

## Path 10 — Refugees (5-Year Reduced Residency)

- **Category:** Residency, reduced (Civil Code Art. 22.1)
- **Eligibility:** Formally recognized refugee status under Spanish asylum law + 5 years legal residency + DELE A2 + CCSE + good conduct.
- **Timeline:** 5 years + 1–2 years processing.
- **Dual citizenship:** Renunciation requirement may be waived in practice where origin-country contact is unsafe.

## Path 11 — Stateless Persons

- **Category:** Mixed (Civil Code Art. 17.1.c for children born in Spain; Art. 22 for adults with stateless status, often 10 years but reduced in practice).
- **Eligibility:** Recognized stateless status (Ministry of Interior certificate).
- **Dual citizenship:** N/A (no prior nationality to renounce).

## Path 12 — Re-acquisition of Lost Spanish Nationality

- **Category:** Recovery (Civil Code Art. 26)
- **Eligibility:** Former Spaniard who lost nationality; declare desire to recover before Civil Registry; renounce intervening nationality (waived for Spaniards-by-origin from privileged countries and for Democratic-Memory-eligible cases). Residency in Spain generally required unless emigrant/descendant of emigrant.
- **Timeline:** Weeks-to-months administrative.
- **Dual citizenship:** Asymmetric per general rules.

## Path 13 — Carta de Naturaleza (Discretionary, Royal Decree)

- **Category:** Discretionary / exceptional (Civil Code Art. 21.1)
- **Eligibility:** Granted by Council of Ministers via Royal Decree for "exceptional circumstances" — extraordinary merit (sport, science, culture), national interest, humanitarian grounds, distinguished service. No residency / language / exam requirements; no right of appeal.
- **Timeline:** Variable (months to years); rare, ~dozens/year.
- **Dual citizenship:** Decree may or may not waive renunciation depending on case; Sephardic exceptional cases retain dual.

## Comparison Summary

Ranked by speed for a typical applicant with the relevant qualifying factor:

1. **Jus sanguinis (Path 1)** — immediate, if Spaniard parent.
2. **Democratic Memory (Path 8)** — ~1–3 yrs, but **window closed Oct 2025**; only pipeline cases proceed.
3. **Marriage (Path 7)** — ~2–3 yrs total; reliable if marriage is genuine.
4. **Ibero-American / Andorra / Philippines / Equatorial Guinea / Portugal / Sephardic-by-history fast track (Path 5)** — ~3–4 yrs total; dual citizenship preserved.
5. **Carta de Naturaleza (Path 13)** — discretionary, rare, fast for the chosen few.
6. **Refugee (Path 10)** — ~6–7 yrs.
7. **Standard residency (Path 4)** — ~11–13 yrs; **renunciation required**.
8. **Closed paths:** Sephardic (Path 9, closed 2019). For the terminated Golden Visa and current residency replacement options, see [../residency/spain.md](../residency/spain.book-chapter.md).

Most-likely practical paths for a US citizen without Spanish ancestry: Path 7 (marriage) > Path 5 (after acquiring Ibero-American citizenship first, e.g., via Argentina or Mexico) > Path 4 (10 years + renunciation declaration that the US ignores → de facto dual).
