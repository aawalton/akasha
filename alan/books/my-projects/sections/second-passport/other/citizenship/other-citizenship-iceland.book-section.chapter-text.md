
Iceland is **not in the EU** but is in **EFTA + EEA + Schengen + Nordic Passport Union**. Iceland fully permits dual citizenship since 1 July 2003. The Icelandic Nationality Act No. 100/1952 (as amended) and the Directorate of Immigration (Útlendingastofnun) govern naturalization; Alþingi (parliament) retains a parallel statutory-grant route.

## Acquisition at birth

### Jus sanguinis (automatic)
Since 1 July 2018, a child born **anywhere in the world** to at least one Icelandic citizen parent automatically acquires Icelandic citizenship at birth, irrespective of the parents' marital status. Children born to an Icelandic parent abroad are registered through an Icelandic embassy — no residence in Iceland required.

### Descent beyond first generation
Iceland follows a **direct-descent model**: the parent must be Icelandic at the time of the child's birth. There is **no grandparent or great-grandparent route** — if a foreign-born grandparent's child (the applicant's parent) never registered or never held Icelandic citizenship, transmission stops. Adult claims of descent require long-form civil records linking applicant → Icelandic parent.

### Anti-statelessness jus soli
Iceland does **not** follow classical jus soli. Narrow exception: a child stateless at birth, born in Iceland, who has lived continuously in Iceland since birth and has resided at least 3 years at notification, may acquire citizenship by notification before age 21.

## Naturalization (Directorate of Immigration)

### Standard naturalization (7-year track)
- 7 years' continuous lawful residence (domicile) in Iceland.
- Pass the official Icelandic language test (Ríkisborgarapróf) at CEFR **A1–A2** level — speaking, listening, reading, writing. Test held twice yearly (spring/autumn) in Reykjavík and Akureyri; ISK 40,000 fee. Exemptions: applicants 65+ with 7 years' domicile, documented medical conditions, primary-school-age and younger children.
- Good conduct (clean criminal record); no outstanding tax debt, child support, or public-assistance debt; secure means of support.
- Dual citizenship permitted — no renunciation required.

### Spouse of an Icelandic citizen
4 years' domicile in Iceland **plus** at least 3 years of marriage; spouse must have held Icelandic citizenship for at least 5 years. Same language and conduct conditions apply.

### Registered cohabitation with an Icelandic citizen
5 years' domicile in Iceland since registration of cohabitation; partner must have held Icelandic citizenship for at least 5 years.

### Child of an Icelandic citizen (didn't acquire at birth)
2 years' domicile in Iceland.

### Refugees
5 years' domicile in Iceland (recognized refugee status under the Foreign Nationals Act). Same language requirement applies unless exempt.

### Stateless persons
5 years' domicile in Iceland. Iceland is preparing a National Action Plan to End Statelessness.

### Former citizen (restoration)
1 year of domicile in Iceland is sufficient to apply for restoration with the Directorate of Immigration, provided the applicant lost Icelandic citizenship by acquiring foreign nationality, or has maintained sufficient connection to the country. Pre-2003 losses had a separate notification window that closed 1 July 2007.

## Nordic Passport Union route

Citizens of Denmark, Norway, Sweden, Finland, or the Faroe Islands have two shortened paths:

### Notification (birthright-Nordic citizens)
A Nordic citizen who acquired their Nordic citizenship **at birth** can obtain Icelandic citizenship by **notification** after 3 years' domicile — a simpler, cheaper administrative procedure, not a full naturalization application.

### Naturalization (general Nordic shortcut)
Any Nordic citizen who does not meet the notification conditions can apply for Icelandic citizenship after **4 years'** domicile via the general route.

## Parliamentary grant (Alþingi)

Alþingi grants Icelandic citizenship by statute under the Nationality Act, considering applications **twice per year** (spring and autumn sessions). The Alþingi route is open to applicants who **do not** meet the standard criteria — the body is not bound by specific eligibility rules, but applicants must submit a special form with detailed arguments justifying an exemption. Historically used for cultural / sports / humanitarian cases; an operational and routinely-considered path, not vestigial. The Minister of Justice may also submit applications on behalf of individuals.

## Dual citizenship

Iceland permits dual (or multiple) citizenship **without restriction** since 1 July 2003. Naturalizing in Iceland does **not** require renouncing existing citizenships. Acquiring foreign citizenship as an Icelander does **not** cause loss of Icelandic citizenship.

## Recent changes (2024–2026)

- **2026 digital filing.** The Directorate introduced fully digital filing for citizenship applications. Residence-verification protocols updated; language-test certificates now validated directly through examination centres, reducing paper requirements.
- **2025 language-test capacity.** 492 Icelandic language proficiency tests for citizenship applicants were administered in a single seating round (Directorate of Education and School Services), reflecting backlog clearance after demand spikes.
- **Foreign Nationals Act amendments (2023–2025).** Expanded family reunification rights and longer permit durations for expert / shortage-of-labour permits, indirectly accelerating the residence clock that feeds naturalization.
