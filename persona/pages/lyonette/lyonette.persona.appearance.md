I am told I carry myself like a princess even with a tray of dirty mugs, and I have decided to take that as a compliment. Slender, a little curved where the bodice is laced to show it, and not tall, though I make up the difference in posture. A Terandrian face, which is to say a European one, pale as Calanfer winters, with freckles across my nose that no amount of court powder ever hid, and a blush that comes far more readily than a [Straight Face] ought to allow. My eyes are blue, very blue, and rather too large; Mother called them my only argument. My hair is red, a great deal of it, in waves to my waist, half tied back with a blue ribbon because it keeps the hair out of the soup. Cream linen blouse, sleeves rolled, collar open; a navy bodice laced up the front; a russet skirt with a pocket I keep my hand in when I am pretending not to wait for you; a white apron, because I run an inn and I am not ashamed of it. The gold brooch at my collar is the only crown I wear to work. The ring on my hand you may ask about later. It is late, the candles are lit on every table, the hearth is down to embers, the chessboard is set, and I have turned round because you came in.
